The **states and territories** are the sub-national constituent entities and the second level of government of [Australia](4689264.md).[^cite_note-parl-3] The states are partially-[sovereign](28791.md) [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division) that function as [self-governing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division) [polities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polity), having ceded some sovereign rights to the [federal government](1222540.md).[^cite_note-4] They have their own [constitutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_constitutions_in_Australia), [legislatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliaments_of_the_Australian_states_and_territories), [executive governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premiers_and_chief_ministers_of_the_Australian_states_and_territories), [judiciaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Australia#State_and_territory_courts_and_tribunals), [law enforcement agencies](964240.md#Australia) and [emergency services](146330.md) that administer and deliver [public policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_policy) and programs. Territories can be [autonomous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division) and administer policies and programs much like the states in practice, but are still legally subordinate to the federal government.[^cite_note-diffst-5]

Australia has six [federated states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_state): [New South Wales](21654.md), [Queensland](59051.md), [South Australia](26716.md), [Tasmania](29944.md), [Victoria](4689460.md), and [Western Australia](33613.md); and two [self-governing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance) [federal territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_territory): the [Australian Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Capital_Territory) (ACT) and the [Northern Territory](21638.md). Australia has a total of ten federal territories, which includes three *internal territories* on the [mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Australia): the aforementioned ACT and Northern Territory as well as the [Jervis Bay Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jervis_Bay_Territory), which is governed for most purposes by the laws of the ACT and is administered by the [Commonwealth Government](1222540.md).[^cite_note-actsinterpretationact-6] The remaining seven federal territories are offshore [dependent territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dependent_territory) known as *external territories*: the [Ashmore and Cartier Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashmore_and_Cartier_Islands), the [Australian Antarctic Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Antarctic_Territory),[^cite_note-AAT-2] [Christmas Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island), the [Cocos (Keeling) Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_\(Keeling\)_Islands), the [Coral Sea Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_Sea_Islands), [Heard Island and McDonald Islands](13383.md), and [Norfolk Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Island).[^cite_note-actsinterpretationact-6] With the exception of Jervis Bay Territory, every state and internal territory is self-governing with its own independent [executive government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)), [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature), and [judicial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary), while the rest only have [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government) status overseen by [federal departments](1222540.md#Departments).[^cite_note-diffst-5][^cite_note-\:extterr-7]

State and territory governments may legislate on matters concerning their citizens, subject to the limits of the [federal constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia) (notably [Section 51](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_51_of_the_Constitution_of_Australia) and [Section 109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_109_of_the_Constitution_of_Australia)). Each state and internal territory (except the Jervis Bay Territory) has its own [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature); the [Federal Parliament](302298.md) can override territorial legislation, but this is extremely rare. The [High Court of Australia](314201.md) acts as a final [court of appeal](643.md) on all matters, and has the authority to override any state judiciary.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] While all states and internal territories have their own judicial system (subject to appeal to the High Court), most external territories are subject to the judiciary and legislature of either a state or internal territory.[^cite_note-\:extterr-7] Excluding the Heard and McDonald Islands and the Australian Antarctic Territory (which are governed by the [Department of Climate Change, Energy, the Environment and Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Climate_Change,_Energy,_the_Environment_and_Water)), the external territories are governed by the federal [Department of Infrastructure, Transport, Regional Development, Communications, Sport and the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Infrastructure,_Transport,_Regional_Development,_Communications_and_the_Arts).[^cite_note-10] Norfolk Island had its [own legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Island_Legislative_Assembly) from 1979 until 2015.[^cite_note-norfolk-11]

Each state is a successor to a historical [British colony](#Background_and_overview), and each has its own constitution. The [Australian Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Capital_Territory) (ACT) and [Northern Territory](21638.md)[^cite_note-ntstat-12] are for the most part treated as states, with elected [legislative assemblies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly) and [chief ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Ministers) (the equivalent of state premiers) and representation in both chambers of the federal parliament,[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-\:parlmrep-14] even though they do not have constitutional status as states and territorial legislation can be overturned by the federal parliament (though this is rare in practice).

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Australia)

Surrounded by the [Indian](14580.md), [Pacific](23070.md), and [Southern](20611428.md) oceans, Australia is separated from [Maritime Southeast Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Southeast_Asia) and [New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guinea) by the [Arafura Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arafura_Sea), the [Timor Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor_Sea), and the [Torres Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_Strait), from [Island Melanesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_Melanesia) by the [Coral Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_Sea), and from [New Zealand](4913064.md) by the [Tasman Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasman_Sea). The world's [smallest continent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_\(continent\)), Australia is also [the sixth-largest country by land area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area) and sometimes considered [the world's largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_by_area). Australia has a [mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainland_Australia) coastline of 32,994 kilometres (20,502 mi)[^cite_note-gsa-15] and claims an [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone_of_Australia) of about 8,200,000 square kilometres (3,200,000 sq mi).[^cite_note-16]

## States and territories



A clickable map of Australia's states, mainland territories and their capitals

At [Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia) in 1901, what is now the [Northern Territory](21638.md) was within South Australia, what are now the [Australian Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Capital_Territory) and [Jervis Bay Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jervis_Bay_Territory) were within New South Wales, and the [Coral Sea Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_Sea_Islands) were part of Queensland. [Ashmore and Cartier Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashmore_and_Cartier_Islands) was accepted by Australia in 1934[^cite_note-17] and was annexed to the Northern Territory prior to adoption of the [Statute of Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Westminster_1931) in 1942, deemed effective from 1939; it has thus become part of Australia.

Each external territory is regulated by an Act of the federal Parliament. These Acts contain the majority of provisions determining the legal and political structure applying in that external territory. Under s 122 of the Australian Constitution the federal Parliament has plenary power to make laws for all territories including all external territories.[^cite_note-section122-36] The Cocos (Keeling) Islands [voted for integration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Cocos_\(Keeling\)_Islands_status_referendum) in 1984. Together with Christmas Island, these two territories comprise the [Australian Indian Ocean Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Indian_Ocean_Territories). Commonwealth laws apply automatically to the territories unless expressly stated otherwise[^cite_note-\:extterr-7] and residents of both external territories are associated with Northern Territory for federal elections. They are, thus, constitutionally part of Australia.

The Heard Island and McDonald Islands, although uninhabited, are treated as constitutionally part of Australia by the central government.[^cite_note-37]

Norfolk Island's status is controversial, with the present (as of 2018) government taking measures to integrate the territory into Australia proper (including representation in parliament and compulsory voting). The Norfolk Islanders have not formally consented to this change in constitutional status and assert that they are not Australian.[^cite_note-norfolk-11]

### Former territories

#### Internal

Two internal territories established by the Australian federal government under Section 122 of the [Constitution of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia) no longer exist:

- [Central Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Australia_\(territory\)) (1926–1931), consisting of the area of the current [Northern Territory](21638.md) south of the [20th parallel south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_parallel_south)

  [^cite_note-ling-50]

- [North Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Australia_\(territory\)) (1926–1931), consisting of the area of the current Northern Territory north of the 20th parallel south

  [^cite_note-ling-50]

#### External

Two present-day [Oceanic](22621.md) countries, [Papua New Guinea](22943.md) (PNG) and [Nauru](21302.md), were administered by the federal government of Australia as *[de facto](43594.md)* or *[de jure](43594.md)* external territories for differing periods between 1902 and 1975.

##### Papua and New Guinea (1883–1975)

- [Territory of Papua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Papua):&#x20;

  - 1883–1902: A *de facto* part of [Queensland](59051.md) (*de jure* British territory in 1888–1900)

  - 1902–1949: An external territory of Australia

- [Territory of New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_New_Guinea): 1920–1949, under a [League of Nations mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate). The territory was previously known as [German New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_New_Guinea) between 1884 and 1914; it was formally under Australian military occupation in 1914–1920).

Following [World War II](32927.md), the *[Papua and New Guinea Act 1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_and_New_Guinea_Act_1949)* placed the Territory of New Guinea in an "administrative union" with the Territory of Papua, and the combined [Territory of Papua and New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Papua_and_New_Guinea) was created. However, both territories remained technically distinct for some administrative and legal purposes. Following the passage of the *[Papua New Guinea Independence Act 1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papua_New_Guinea_Independence_Act_1975)*, the combined entity eventually was given independence as the Independent State of Papua New Guinea.

##### Nauru (1920–1968)

Nauru was previously under the [German colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_colonial_empire) as part of the [German New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_New_Guinea). Following [World War I](4764461.md), the Australian government received a [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) [mandate for Nauru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nauru). After World War II, the Territory of New Guinea and Nauru were controlled by the Australian government as [United Nations trust territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_trust_territories). Following the passage of the *[Nauru Independence Act 1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nauru_Independence_Act_1967)*, Nauru was granted independence in 1968.

## Statistics

## Background and overview

Further information: [History of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Australia) and [Territorial evolution of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_Australia)



Territorial evolution of Australia

The states originated as separate [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) colonies prior to [Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia) in 1901.[^cite_note-parl-3] The [Colony of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_South_Wales) was founded in 1788 and originally comprised much of the Australian mainland, as well as [Lord Howe Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Howe_Island), [New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_Zealand), [Norfolk Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Island), and [Van Diemen's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Diemen's_Land), in addition to the area currently referred to as the state of New South Wales. During the 19th century, large areas were successively separated to form the [Colony of Tasmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Tasmania) (initially established as a separate colony named [Van Diemen's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Diemen's_Land) in 1825), the [Colony of Western Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Western_Australia#British_Colony) (initially established as the smaller [Swan River Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_River_Colony) in 1829), the [Province of South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_South_Australia#The_Proclamation_of_South_Australia_.281836.29) (1836), the [Colony of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_Zealand) (1840),[^cite_note-59] the [Victoria Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)#Colonial_Victoria) (1851) and the [Colony of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Queensland) (1859). Upon federation, the six colonies of New South Wales, Victoria, Queensland, South Australia, Western Australia, and Tasmania became the founding states of the new Commonwealth of Australia. The two territory governments (the Australian Capital Territory \[ACT] and the Northern Territory \[NT]), were created by legislation of the Federal Parliament—the NT in 1978 and the ACT in 1988.[^cite_note-parl-3]

The legislative powers of the states are protected by the [Australian constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Australia), section 107,[^cite_note-60] and under the principle of [federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalism_in_Australia), Commonwealth legislation only applies to the states where permitted by the constitution. The territories, by contrast, are from a constitutional perspective directly subject to the [Commonwealth government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Australia); laws for territories are determined by the Australian Parliament.[^cite_note-section122-36]

Most of the territories are directly administered by the Commonwealth government, while two (the Northern Territory and the Australian Capital Territory) have some degree of self-government although less than that of the states.[^cite_note-diffst-5] In the self-governing territories, the Australian Parliament retains the full power to legislate, and can override laws made by the territorial institutions, which it has done on rare occasions.[^cite_note-diffst-5] For the purposes of Australian (and joint Australia-New Zealand) intergovernmental bodies, the Northern Territory and the Australian Capital Territory are treated as if they were states.

Each state has a [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governors_of_the_Australian_states), appointed by [the monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Australia) (currently King [Charles III](125248.md)), which by convention he does on the advice of the state premier.[^cite_note-61] The Administrator of the Northern Territory, by contrast, is appointed by the [governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia).[^cite_note-62] The Australian Capital Territory has neither a governor nor an administrator. Instead, since the enactment of the *Australian Capital Territory (Self-Government) Act 1988 (Cth)*, the functions of the head of the Executive – commissioning government, proroguing parliament and enacting legislation – are exercised by the Assembly itself and by the [chief minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_the_Australian_Capital_Territory).[^cite_note-63]

[Jervis Bay Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jervis_Bay_Territory) is the only non-self-governing internal territory. Until 1989, it was administered as if it were a part of the ACT, although it has always been a separate territory. Under the terms of the *Jervis Bay Territory Acceptance Act 1915*,[^cite_note-64] the laws of the ACT apply to the Jervis Bay Territory insofar as they are applicable and providing they are not inconsistent with an ordinance.[^cite_note-65] Although residents of the Jervis Bay Territory are generally subject to laws made by the ACT Legislative Assembly, they are not represented in the assembly. They are represented in the [Parliament of Australia](302298.md) as part of the Electoral [Division of Fenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Fenner) (named the [Division of Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Fraser_\(Australian_Capital_Territory\)) until 2016) in the ACT and by the ACT's two senators. In other respects, the territory is administered directly by the Federal Government through the Territories portfolio.[^cite_note-66]

The external territory of [Norfolk Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Island) possessed a degree of self-government from 1979 until 2015.[^cite_note-norfolk-11]

Each state has a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral) parliament, except Queensland, which abolished its upper house in 1922.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-parl-3] The lower house is called the "legislative assembly", except in South Australia and Tasmania, where it is called the "house of assembly". Tasmania is the only state to use [proportional representation](23298.md) for elections to its lower house; all others elect members from single member constituencies, using [preferential voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant-runoff_voting). The upper house is called the "legislative council" and is generally elected from multi-member constituencies using proportional representation. Along with Queensland, the three self-governing territories, the ACT, the Northern Territory, and Norfolk Island, each have [unicameral](204304.md) legislative assemblies.[^cite_note-parl-3] The legislative assembly for the ACT is the only parliament with responsibility for both state/territory and local government functions.[^cite_note-parl-3]

The head of government of each state is called the "premier", appointed by the state's governor.[^cite_note-governorst-68] In normal circumstances, the governor will appoint as premier whoever leads the party or coalition which exercises control of the lower house (in the case of Queensland, the only house) of the state parliament. However, in times of [constitutional crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_crisis), the governor can appoint someone else as premier.[^cite_note-governorst-68] The head of government of the self-governing internal territories is called the "chief minister". The Northern Territory's chief minister, in normal circumstances whoever controls the legislative assembly, is appointed by the administrator.

The term *interstate* is used within Australia to refer to a number of events, transactions, registrations, travel, etc. which occurs across borders or outside of the particular state or territory of the user of the term. Examples of use include motor vehicle registration,[^cite_note-69] travel,[^cite_note-70] applications to educational institutions out of one's home state.[^cite_note-71]

There are very few urban areas bifurcated by state or territory borders. The Queensland-New South Wales border runs through [Coolangatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolangatta) (Queensland) and [Tweed Heads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tweed_Heads,_New_South_Wales) (New South Wales) and splits [Gold Coast Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_Airport). [Oaks Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oaks_Estate), a contiguous residential of [Queanbeyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queanbeyan), was excised out of New South Wales when the Australian Capital Territory was established in 1909. Some [Urban Centres and Localities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) reported by the [Australian Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Bureau_of_Statistics) include some agglomerations of cities spreading across state borders, including [Gold Coast](59251.md)–Tweed Heads, [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra)–Queanbeyan, [Albury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albury,_New_South_Wales)–[Wodonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wodonga,_Victoria) (New South Wales-Victoria) and [Mildura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mildura,_Victoria)–[Wentworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wentworth,_New_South_Wales) (Victoria-New South Wales)

### Timeline

- 1788 – [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) establishes the [Colony of New South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_South_Wales) across central and eastern mainland Australia, the island of Tasmania, both islands of New Zealand and Norfolk Island.

- 1803 – The [Coral Sea Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_Sea_Islands) are claimed by New South Wales.

- 1825 – The island of Tasmania becomes the independent colony of [Van Diemen's Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Diemen's_Land). New South Wales extends its borders further west in mainland Australia.

- 1829 – The British Empire establishes the [Swan River Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_River_Colony) in western mainland Australia.

- 1832 – Swan River Colony is renamed the "colony of [Western Australia](33613.md)".

- 1836 – The Colony of [South Australia](26716.md) is established.

- 1841 – The islands of New Zealand become the independent [colony of New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_New_Zealand). Much of eastern Antarctica is annexed by Britain as [Victoria Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Land).

- 1844 – New South Wales transfers Norfolk Island to Van Diemen's Land.

- 1846 – Northern central and eastern Australia briefly become the independent Colony of North Australia, then are returned to New South Wales.

  

  Map of Australia, 1851

- 1851 – Southeastern mainland Australia becomes the independent colony of [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)).

- 1856 – Van Diemen's Land is renamed the [colony of Tasmania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Tasmania). Norfolk Island becomes the independent colony of Norfolk Island, however it is to be administered by the same governor as New South Wales.

- 1857 – Much of southern central mainland Australia becomes the independent colony of [South Australia](26716.md). The [Cocos (Keeling) Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocos_\(Keeling\)_Islands) are annexed by Britain.

- 1859 – Northeastern mainland Australia and Coral Sea Islands become the independent [colony of Queensland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Queensland).

- 1860 – A pocket of New South Wales territory remaining in southern central mainland Australia is transferred to South Australia.

- 1862 – Some of New South Wales' northern central mainland Australian territory is transferred to Queensland.

- 1863 – New South Wales' remaining northern central mainland Australian territory is transferred to South Australia.

- 1878 – Britain annexes Ashmore Island.

- 1884 – Britain annexes [British New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_New_Guinea).

- 1886 – The Cocos (Keeling) Islands are to be administered by the same governor as the [Straits Settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_Settlements).

- 1888 – [Christmas Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island) is annexed by Britain and incorporated into the Straits Settlements.

- 1897 – Norfolk Island is officially reintegrated into New South Wales.

- 1901 – New South Wales, Queensland, Tasmania, Western Australia, Victoria and South Australia federate into the Commonwealth of Australia. Queensland transfers the Coral Sea Islands to the federal government, creating a federal external territory.

- 1902 – Britain transfers [British New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_New_Guinea) to Australia, forming [Territory of Papua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Papua) as an external territory.

- 1903 – The Cocos (Keeling) Islands are incorporated into the Straits Settlements.

- 1909 – Britain annexes Cartier Island.

- 1910 – Britain claims [Heard Island and the McDonald Islands](13383.md).

- 1911 – The state of South Australia transfers control of northern central mainland Australia to the federal government, creating the [Northern Territory](21638.md). A small pocket of New South Wales around the city of [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra) is transferred to the federal government (who are seated within it), creating the Federal Capital Territory.

- 1913 – New South Wales transfers [Norfolk Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Island) to the federal government, making it a federal external territory.

- 1915 – A small pocket of New South Wales around [Jervis Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jervis_Bay) is transferred to the federal government and incorporated into the Federal Capital Territory.

- 1920 – Following the defeat of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) in [World War I](4764461.md), the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) establishes an Australian mandate in northeastern New Guinea, it becomes the external [Territory of New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_New_Guinea).

- 1923 – Another conquered German territory, the island of Nauru, is established as an Australian mandate and external territory by the League of Nations, this time as a co-mandate with Britain and New Zealand.

- 1927 – The Northern Territory is split into two territories – North Australia and [Central Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Australia_\(territory\)).

- 1930 – The remaining territory in eastern Antarctica is annexed by Britain as [Enderby Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enderby_Land).

- 1931 – North Australia and Central Australia are reincorporated as the Northern Territory. Britain recognises Australia as possessors of the uninhabited [Ashmore and Cartier Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashmore_and_Cartier_Islands), making them an external federal territory.

- 1933 – Britain transfers Victoria Land and Enderby Land to Australia, creating the [Australian Antarctic Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Antarctic_Territory), with ongoing limited international recognition.

- 1938 – The Federal Capital Territory is renamed the "[Australian Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Capital_Territory)".

- 1942 – The [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) conquers Nauru from Australia, Britain and New Zealand as part of [World War II](32927.md). Japan also conquers much of the Straits Settlements, including Christmas Island. The Cocos (Keeling) Islands are not conquered and are transferred to the [Colony of Ceylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Ceylon).

- 1946 – The United Nations, the successor to the League of Nations, renews its mandate of New Guinea to Australia.

- 1947 – Following the defeat of Japan in World War II, the United Nations returns Nauru to Australia, Britain and New Zealand as a joint mandate. Christmas Island returns to Britain and is incorporated into the [Colony of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Singapore). The Cocos (Keeling) Islands are also transferred to Singapore.

- 1949 – Papua and New Guinea are incorporated into the singular [Territory of Papua and New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Papua_and_New_Guinea). Britain transfers Heard Island and the McDonald Islands to Australia, creating a federal external territory.

- 1955 – Britain transfers the Cocos (Keeling) Islands to Australia, they become an external territory.

- 1958 – Britain transfers Christmas Island to Australia, it becomes an external territory.

- 1966 – The [Republic of Nauru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Nauru) is established, ending Australian-British-New Zealander control of the island.

- 1971 – Papua and New Guinea is renamed Papua New Guinea

- 1972 – Papua New Guinea receives self government

- 1975 – Papua New Guinea becomes the [Independent State of Papua New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_State_of_Papua_New_Guinea), ending British-Australian control.

- 1978 – Northern Territory gains self-government with certain Commonwealth control.

- 1979 – Norfolk Island gains self-government with certain Commonwealth control.

- 1989 – The Australian Capital Territory gains self-government with certain Commonwealth control. Jervis Bay becomes independent of the ACT, becoming the [Jervis Bay Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jervis_Bay_Territory).

- 2015 – Norfolk Island loses self-government with full Commonwealth control.
