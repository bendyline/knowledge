# Deadweight tonnage

Not to be confused with [vessel displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_\(ship\)).



The more heavily loaded a ship is, the lower it sits in the water. Maximum DWT is the amount of weight a ship can carry without riding dangerously low in the water.



Scale for a 6,000-tonne DWT ship

**Deadweight tonnage** (also known as **deadweight**; abbreviated to **DWT**, **D.W\.T.**, **d.w\.t.**, or **dwt**) or **tons deadweight** (DWT) is a measure of how much [weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weight) a ship can carry.[^cite_note-tm-1][^cite_note-hm-2][^cite_note-msd-3] It is the sum of the weights of [cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo), fuel, [fresh water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_water), [ballast water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing_ballast#Water_ballast), provisions, passengers, and [crew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew).[^cite_note-tm-1]

DWT is often used to specify a ship's maximum permissible deadweight (i.e. when it is fully loaded so that its [Plimsoll line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plimsoll_line) is at water level), although it may also denote the actual DWT of a ship not loaded to capacity.

## Definition

Deadweight tonnage is a measure of a vessel's weight carrying capacity, not including the empty weight of the ship. It is distinct from the [displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displacement_\(ship\)) (weight of water displaced), which includes the ship's own weight, or the volumetric measures of [gross tonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_tonnage) or [net tonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_tonnage) (and the legacy measures [gross register tonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_register_tonnage) and [net register tonnage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_register_tonnage)).

Deadweight tonnage was historically expressed in [long tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_ton),[^cite_note-4] but is now usually given internationally in [tonnes](31185.md) (metric tons).[^cite_note-5] In modern international shipping conventions such as the [International Convention for the Safety of Life at Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOLAS_Convention) and the [International Convention for the Prevention of Pollution From Ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MARPOL_73/78), deadweight is explicitly defined as the difference in tonnes between the displacement of a ship in water of a [specific gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specific_gravity) of 1.025 (corresponding to average density of [sea water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_water) of 1,025 kg/m<sup>3</sup> or 1,728 lb/cu yd) at the [draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_\(hull\)) corresponding to the assigned [summer freeboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_draft) and the [light displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_displacement) (lightweight) of the ship.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]
