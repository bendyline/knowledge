**Eid al-Fitr**[^cite_note-3] is the first of the two main [festivals in Islam](2035419.md), the other being [Eid al-Adha](358226.md). The holiday falls on the first day of [Shawwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawwal), the tenth month of the [Islamic calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] One of the most important Islamic celebrations, Eid al-Fitr is celebrated by Muslims worldwide as it marks the end of the [month-long, dawn-to-dusk fasting (*sawm*)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasting_in_Islam) during [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan).[^cite_note-Britannica-6][^cite_note-7] The holiday is known under various other names in different languages and countries around the world.[^cite_note-10]

Eid al-Fitr begins with a communal prayer and is followed by visits to relatives, giving gifts, and sharing meals.[^cite_note-11] It has a particular *[salah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah)* that consists of two *[rakats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakat)* generally performed in an open field or large hall. It may only be performed in congregation (*jamāʿat*) and features six additional *[Takbirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takbir)* (raising of the hands to the ears whilst reciting the [Takbir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takbir), saying "Allāhu ʾAkbar", meaning "God is the greatest"). In the [Hanafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi) school of [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam), there are three *Takbirs* at the start of the first *rakat* and three just before *[rukūʿ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruku)* in the second *rakat*.[^cite_note-12] Other Sunni schools usually have 12 *Takbirs*, similarly split in groups of seven and five. In [Shia Islam](26961.md), the *salat* has six *Takbirs* in the first *rakat* at the end of *[Tilawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilawa)*, before *rukūʿ*, and five in the second.[^cite_note-13] Depending on the juristic opinion of the locality, this *salat* is either *[farḍ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fard)* (فرض, obligatory) or *[mustaḥabb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustahabb)* (strongly recommended). After the *salat*, Muslims celebrate the Eid al-Fitr in various ways with [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_cuisine) being a central theme,[^cite_note-14] which also gives the holiday the nickname "Sweet Eid" or "Sugar Feast".[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-BBC-16]

In many parts of the world, Eid al-Fitr is also characterized by distinctive local customs that reflect regional cultures. Communities often mark the occasion with large family visits, public celebrations, and the sharing of traditional foods and sweets prepared specifically for the holiday. Markets and neighborhoods in several countries become especially lively as people buy new clothing, gifts, and festive meals, while charitable giving and community gatherings remain central elements of the celebration. Despite cultural differences, the festival commonly emphasizes social connection, generosity, and the strengthening of family and community ties.[^cite_note-17]

## History

According to Muslim tradition, [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) instituted the celebration of Eid al-Fitr.[^cite_note-18] According to a certain [hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith), these festivals were initiated in [Medina](36636.md) after the [migration of Muhammad from Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijra_\(Islam\)). [Anas ibn Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anas_ibn_Malik), a [companion of Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companion_of_Muhammad), narrated that when Muhammad arrived in Medina, he found people celebrating two specific days in which they entertained themselves with recreation. Muhammad then remarked that [God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/God_in_Islam) had fixed two mandatory days of festivity: Eid al-Fitr and [Eid al-Adha](358226.md).[^cite_note-19]

## General rituals

See also: [Zakat al-Fitr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakat_al-Fitr)

Eid al-Fitr begins at sunset on the night of the first sighting of the crescent moon. The night on which the moon is sighted is celebrated as *[Chand Raat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chand_Raat)* in countries like Bangladesh. If the moon is not observed immediately after the 29th day of the previous lunar month (either because clouds block its view or because the western sky is still too bright when the moon sets), then the holiday is celebrated the following day.[^cite_note-20] Eid al-Fitr is celebrated for one to three days, depending on the country.[^cite_note-aljazeera/June-21] It is [forbidden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haram) to fast on the Day of Eid, and a specific prayer is nominated for this day.[^cite_note-Deborah-22] As an obligatory act of charity, money is paid to the poor and the needy (*[zakat al-Fitr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakat_al-Fitr)*) before performing the 'Eid prayer.[^cite_note-23]

### Eid prayer

Main articles: [Eid prayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_prayers) and [Eidgah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eidgah)



Many Muslims often bring prayer rugs to the mosque on Eid al-Fitr.

The Eid prayer is performed by the congregation in an open area such as a field, community center, or mosque.[^cite_note-aljazeera/June-21] No [call to prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhan) is given for this Eid prayer, and it consists of only two [rakaʿāt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raka%CA%BF%C4%81t), with a variable amount of *[takbirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takbir)* and other prayer elements depending on the [branch of Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branches_of_Islam) observed. The Eid prayer is followed by the [khutbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khutbah) and then a [dua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dua) asking for God's forgiveness, mercy, peace and blessings for all living beings across the world. The sermon also instructs Muslims as to the performance of rituals of Eid, such as the *[zakāt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zak%C4%81t)*.[^cite_note-24] The sermon of Eid takes place after the Eid prayer, unlike [Friday prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_prayer) which comes first before prayer. Some imams believe that listening to the sermon at Eid is optional.[^cite_note-25] After the prayers, Muslims visit their relatives, friends, and acquaintances or hold large communal celebrations in homes, community centers, or rented halls.[^cite_note-aljazeera/June-21]

#### Sunni procedure

As ritual dictates, Sunnis praise God in a loud voice while going to the Eid prayer:

> *Allāhu Akbar, Allāhu Akbar. Lā ilāha illà l-Lāh. Allāhu Akbar, Allahu akbar, wa-li-l-Lāh al-ḥamd*

Recitation ceases when they get to the place of Eid or once the Imam commences activities.[^cite_note-26]

The prayer starts by doing [niyyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niyyah) "intention" for the prayer before the takbir is said by the [imam](15240.md). Next, the takbir al-[ihram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ihram) is performed, by saying takbir three times, raising hands to the ears and dropping them each time, except for the last when the hands are folded. The Imam then reads [al-Fatihah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Fatihah), followed by another surah. The congregation performs [ruku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruku) and [sujud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sujud). This completes the first *rak'ah*.[^cite_note-27]

The congregation rises and folds their hands for the second rak'ah, after which the imam recites al-Fatiha followed by another surah. After this, three takbirs are called out just before the ruku, each time raising hands to the ears and dropping them. For the fourth time, the congregation says Allahu Akbar and subsequently goes into the ruku. The rest of the prayer is completed regularly. This completes the Eid prayer. After the prayer, there is a [khutbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khutbah).[^cite_note-28]

#### Shia procedure

The prayer starts with the *Niyyat* followed by five *Takbirs*. During every *Takbir* of the first *rakat*, a special *Dua* is recited. Then, the Imam recites *Sūrat al-Fātiḥah* and *Surat Al-'A\`lá* and the congregation performs *Ruku* and *Sujud* as in other prayers. In the second *Rakat*, the same above steps (five *Takbeers, Sūrat al-Fātiḥah* and *Surat Al-'A\`lá, Ruku* and *Sujud*) are repeated. After the prayer, the khutbah starts.[^cite_note-29]

### Post-prayer celebration

See also: [Eid cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_cuisine)

During the Eid celebration, Muslims greet each other by saying *'Eid Mubarak'*, which is Arabic for "Blessed Eid". As it comes after a month of fasting, sweet dishes and foods are often prepared and consumed during the celebration. Muslims typically decorate their homes, and are also encouraged to forgive each other and seek forgiveness. In countries with large Muslim populations, it is normally a public holiday with most schools and businesses closed for the day. Practices differ by country and region.[^cite_note-auto4-30]

#### Turkey

It is also customary for young children to go around their neighborhood, door to door, and wish everyone a "Happy *Bayram*", for which they are awarded candy, chocolates, traditional sweets such as [baklava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baklava) and [Turkish delight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_delight), or a small amount of money at every door.[^cite_note-31]

late 19th or early 20th century Ottoman Eid card

Ramadan in the Ottoman era was passed with great happiness due to the existence of various kinds of entertainment. The streets would be filled with performances by musicians, magicians and other talented people that suitable for all ages, so children could enjoy it. Poetry had a huge influence on Ottoman classical music, in fact it served as a basis. At iftar, they had popular song to be played that called fasil music along with some instruments such as tambourine and flute. Next, mahya illuminations represent gratitude towards God with lamps of olive oil, barb, and ropes which would form certain sentences or images as desired. This decoration lightened up the darkness of the night with the beauty of its light.[^cite_note-33]

During Ramadan, many restaurants and cafes may have limited operating hours during the day and may be closed during fasting hours. In the middle of the night drummers circulate through towns and villages to wake sleepers so they can prepare Sahur, the big early-morning meal to be eaten before the fast begins again at sunrise. They tend to make their noise around 02\:30 and 03\:00 am, and they make sure everyone hears them.[^cite_note-34] Turkish people invite anyone to break the fast together in the order of inviting older relatives first, other relatives, neighbors who are quite close, and some areas that have village's leaders, teachers, and caretakers where the order has been determined by tradition.[^cite_note-35]

#### Afghanistan



An Afghan child eating a piece of candy received as a gift on Eid al-Fitr

In the predominantly [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) Muslim culture of Afghanistan, Eid al-Fitr holds significant importance and is celebrated widely for three days. It popularly involves special festivities for children and the youngest members of families.[^cite_note-auto-71] The most common greeting is *Akhtar mo Mubarak sha* (Eid Mubarak) in the [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto)-speaking community. Afghans start preparing for the Eid al-Fitr festival up to ten days prior by cleaning their homes (called *Khana Takani* in [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language)).[^cite_note-72] Afghans visit their local bazaars to buy new clothes, sweets, and snacks including *[Jalebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelabi)*, *Shor-Nakhod* (made with chickpeas), *Cake wa Kolcha* (a simple cake, similar to pound cake). The traditional *[Bolani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolani)* (vegetarian flatbreads) is a popular meal during Eid al-Fitr in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-auto-71]

On the day of Eid al-Fitr, Afghans will first offer their Eid prayers and then gather in their homes with their families, greeting one another by saying "*Eid Mubarak*" and usually adding "*Eidet Mobarak Roza wa Namazet Qabool Dakhel Hajiha wa Ghaziha,*" which means "Happy Eid to you; may your fasting and prayers be accepted by God, and may you be counted among those who will go to the Hajj-pilgrimage." Family elders will give money and gifts to children. It is also common practice to visit families and friends, which may be difficult to do at other times of the year. Children walk from home to home saying "*Khala Eidet Mubarak*" ("aunt happy Eid"), and they receive cookies or *Pala*. Young girls and women apply henna "tattoo"s on their hands and feet. The older women while applying it too, don't do very complicated designs. The boys and young men in some communities might apply it as well but, with very simple designs like a circle in their palms or just coloring the fingertips. The older men might do those simple designs as well, but it is not that common. Henna is a "woman's" thing in Afghanistan when it comes to doing designs on their hands and feet. At night, multiple campfires are set around houses, sometimes to the point that entire valleys may initially appear to be engulfed in flame. Celebratory fire with automatic rifles, particularly tracer rounds, can also be expected in high density.[^cite_note-73]

Eid prayers at [Badshahi Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badshahi_Mosque), in [Lahore](125315.md), Pakistan

In [Pakistan](23235.md), Eid al-Fitr is also referred to as 'the Lesser Eid' ([Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu): چھوٹی عید, *chhoṭī īd*, [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language): نکی عید, *nikkī īd*) or 'Sweet Eid' ([Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu): میٹھی عید, *mīṭhī īd*, [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language): مٹھی عید, *miṭṭhī īd*).[^cite_note-TheNation2020-06-30-01a-8] People are supposed to give obligatory charity on behalf of each of their family members to the needy or poor before Eid day or, at most, before the Eid prayer, allowing for all to share in the joy of Eid. At home, family members enjoy a special Eid breakfast with various types of sweets and desserts, including [Kheer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kheer) and the traditional dessert [sheer khurma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheer_khurma), which is made of vermicelli, milk, butter, dry fruits, and dates. Eid is especially enjoyed by the kids, as they receive money in cash called "[Eidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eidi_\(gift\))" as gift from their relatives and elders.[^cite_note-81] People tend to get fresh currency notes issued by [State Bank of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Bank_of_Pakistan) with which they give to the children.[^cite_note-82]

#### Sri Lanka

[Sri Lankan Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lankan_Muslims_\(ethnic_group\)) like to eat [watalappam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watalappam), [falooda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falooda), [samosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samosa), [gulab jamun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulab_jamun), [sheer kurma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheer_kurma), [oil cake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevum) and other national and regional dishes.[^cite_note-83]

#### Nepal

Eid is a national holiday in Nepal. [Nepalese Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_Muslims) often consume the popular Nepalese dessert [sewai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevai) (vermicelli pudding) to mark the occasion. It is commemorated throughout the country with large prayers, dinners and social celebrations.[^cite_note-84]

#### Maldives

Celebration in the [Maldives](19117.md) include cultural performances.[^cite_note-85]

### Southeast Asia

#### Indonesia

Main article: [Lebaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebaran)



Eid al-Fitr prayers on the slopes of Mount Sumbing and Sindoro, precisely at Garung Field, Butuh, Kalikajar District, [Wonosobo Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonosobo_Regency), [Central Java](453584.md), Indonesia.&#x20;

Eid is known in Indonesia as *Hari Raya Idul Fitri, Idul Fitri,* or more popularly as *[Lebaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebaran)*, and is a [national holiday](1200613.md).[^cite_note-investvine-86] People return to their home town or city (a homecoming exodus known as *[mudik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudik)*) to celebrate with their families and to ask forgiveness from parents, in-laws, and other elders.[^cite_note-87] Festivities start the night before with chanting the *[Takbir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takbir),* lighting lamps, and often celebrated with [fireworks](59493.md) and street parades.[^cite_note-auto3-32] On the day itself, before [Eid prayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eid_prayer) in the morning, *[zakat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakat)* alms are distributed in the mosques, prioritized to be given for the poor. People gather with family and neighbors in traditional clothing and have a special *Lebaran* meal, which are special dishes include [ketupat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketupat), [rendang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendang), [opor ayam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opor_ayam) and [gulai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulai). Many, usually children, are [given money in colorful envelopes.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_envelope)[^cite_note-isna00-88] Later, it is common for Muslims in Indonesia to visit the graves of relatives that have deceased to ritually clean the grave. Muslims also attend mass gathering in a special tradition called *Halal bi-Halal,* sometime during or several days after *Idul Fitri*.[^cite_note-89]

#### Malaysia, Singapore, and Brunei

See also: [Malay festivals and celebrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_festivals_and_celebrations) and [Hari Raya songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Raya_songs)



Rows of Pelita ([oil lamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_lamp)) which is used to illuminate homes and the streets during the season. Seen here in [Muar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muar_\(town\)), Johor, Malaysia

In Malaysia, Singapore, and [Brunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunei), Eid is more commonly known as *Hari Raya Aidilfitri* ([Jawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawi_script): هاري راي عيدالفطري), *Hari Raya Idul Fitri*, *Hari Raya Puasa*, *Hari Raya Fitrah*, *Hari Lebaran* and sometimes Aidilfitri. *Hari Raya* means 'Great Day'.[^cite_note-90]

It is customary for workers in the city to return to their home town to celebrate with their families and to ask forgiveness from parents, in-laws, and other elders. Forgiveness is often requested without citing any specific error to avoid arguments. This is known in Malaysia as *[balik kampung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balik_kampung)* (homecoming).[^cite_note-91]

The night before Hari Raya is filled with the sounds of takbir in the mosques or musallas. In many parts of Malaysia, especially in the rural areas, *pelita* or *panjut* or *lampu colok* (as known by [Malay-Singaporeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay-Singaporeans)) (oil lamps, similar to tiki torches) are lit up and placed outside and around homes, while [tiki torches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiki_torches) themselves are also a popular decoration for that holiday. Special dishes like [ketupat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketupat), [rendang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendang), [lemang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemang) (a type of [glutinous rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutinous_rice) cooked in bamboo) and Malay delicacies such as various *[kuih-muih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kue)* are served during this day. It is common to greet people with "Salam Aidilfitri" or "Selamat Hari Raya" which means "Happy Eid". Muslims also greet one another with "maaf zahir dan batin", which means "Forgive my physical and emotional (wrongdoings)".[^cite_note-92]

It is customary for [Muslim-Malaysians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Malaysia) to wear a traditional cultural clothing on Hari Raya. The [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)) variant (worn in Malaysia, Singapore, Brunei and [Southern Thailand](1453076.md)) is known as the [Baju Melayu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baju_Melayu), shirt worn with a *sarong* known as *kain samping* or songket and a headwear known as *songkok*. Malaysian women's clothing is referred to as [Baju Kurung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baju_Kurung) and [baju kebaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kebaya). It is a common practice however for the Malays in Singapore and Johor, Malaysia to refer to the *baju kurung* in reference to the type of outfit, worn by both men and women.[^cite_note-93]



Street festival during Eid in [Geylang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geylang), Singapore

In Malaysia, especially in the major cities, people take turns to set aside a time for *open house* when they stay at home to receive and entertain neighbours, family and other visitors. It is common to see non-Muslims made welcome during Eid at these *open houses*. They also celebrate by lighting traditional [bamboo cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_cannon) [firecrackers](418188.md) known as *meriam buluh*, using [kerosene](16992.md) in large hollow [bamboo](39029.md) tubes or Chinese imported crackers. The traditional bamboo cannon, *meriam buloh*, and fireworks are notoriously loud and can be very dangerous to operator, bystander and even nearby buildings. These are usually bamboo tubes 5–10 cm (2.0–3.9 in) in diameter and 4–7 m (13–23 ft) long, filled with either: water and several hundred grams of [calcium carbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_carbide), or heated kerosene, then ignited by match.

In Malaysia, children are given token sums of money, also known as "[duit raya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_envelope)", from their parents or elders.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

#### Brunei

In Brunei, a special centrepiece is made to coming guests by young ladies of the family called the *kepala meja* ('head of the table') where a decorated cake is presented so any man who wished to propose and take her in marriage would cut the cake in response. Here is also where special dishes unique to this country are served, such as [kelupis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelupis) with prawn or tahai (smoked [sardinella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardinella_gibbosa)) sambal and curry.[^cite_note-96]

#### Thailand

There are almost 3 million [Muslims in Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Thailand). In the southernmost provinces, wearing gold on Eid is a popular practice there along with neighbouring Myanmar. In some parts of the country, Muslim residences would open their homes with food specialities to visitors.[^cite_note-97]

#### Cambodia, Laos and Vietnam

The [Chams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chams) constituted the bulk of the Muslim population in both Cambodia, Laos and Vietnam, especially in Cambodia and Vietnam where they are located mainly, and the Eid is also known as "*Roya Haji*", "*Roya Haji Aidil Fitri*", "*Hari Raya Aidilfitri*", or "*Roya Eidul Fitri*", among the Muslims there.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

[Muslims of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Cambodia) Cambodia often open their homes to friends and neighbours to share food together.[^cite_note-100] Men, women and children dress in fine attire, while the preferred dress colour is white, symbolising the purity of the soul after a month of Ramadan.[^cite_note-101] It is customary during Eid for Cambodian Muslims to donate 3 kilograms of rice to the poor or disabled.[^cite_note-102]

Eid is also celebrated by the much smaller [Muslim community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Vietnam) of Vietnam, which makes up less than 0.1% of the population. The Muslim community in Vietnam are mostly of the same ethnic as the [Cham people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cham_people).[^cite_note-103]

Eid al-Fitr in [Laos](17752.md) is also celebrated by the Muslim community, including [Chams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chams), Pakistani, [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Malays), and expatriate Muslims. In cities like [Vientiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vientiane) and [Savannakhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannakhet), Muslims gather for Eid prayers, followed by communal meals and [Zakat al-Fitr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakat_al-Fitr). Traditional spiced rice dishes and grilled meats are shared, making the celebration a modest yet meaningful occasion that fosters unity and cultural diversity.[^cite_note-104]

#### Myanmar

During Ramadan, in small towns and big villages with significant Muslim populations, Burmese Muslim youth organize singing teams called Jago (meaning "wake up"). Jago teams usually do not use musical instruments apart from the occasional use of harmonica mouth organs.[^cite_note-105] The roving groups of singers will take the tunes of popular Hindi movie songs, replaced with Burmese lyrics and invocations about fasting, the principles of Islam, and the benefits of Salat.[^cite_note-106]

#### Philippines

Among [Muslim Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Filipino) in the [Philippines](23440.md), Eid al-Fitr is commonly known as *Hariraya*, *Buka*, *Hariraya Buka*, or *Hariraya Buka Puasa*. "Eid al-" is often replaced with *Eid'l* in the [country's local English variant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English).[^cite_note-Lidasan-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] It is also known as *Wakas ng Ramadan* (lit. "End of Ramadan"), *Araw ng Raya* ("Feast Day"), or *Pagtatapos ng Pag-aayuno* ("End of the Fast") in [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language). It was proclaimed a legal holiday for Muslim Filipinos in 1977 by Presidential Decree No. 1083. In 2002, this was upgraded to a [public national holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_Philippines) by Republic Act No. 9177. It is also sometimes known by its [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language) name "Hari Raya Puasa"; and by its [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) name "[Lebaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebaran)".[^cite_note-Tan-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-Raslan-112]



President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) interacts with participants of the 2016 Eid al-Fitr celebrations in [Davao City](489401.md), Philippines.

Its beginning is decided by the sighting of the crescent moon (*hilal*), followed by morning prayers in mosques or public plazas.[^cite_note-tl-113] When this occurs can sometimes differ depending on the regional government. In some places it is based on the physical sighting of the *hilal*; while in others it is determined by the [Regional Darul Ifta' of Bangsamoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Darul_Ifta'_of_Bangsamoro) (RDI-BARMM) or the [National Commission on Muslim Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Commission_on_Muslim_Filipinos) (NCMF), especially during cloudy days.[^cite_note-Lidasan-107][^cite_note-cp-114][^cite_note-Cabrera-115]

The sighting of the *hilal* is traditionally marked by the beating of drums in some regions. In modern times, this has evolved into a noise barrage known as "Mobile Takbir", where celebrants, especially youths, rev their motorcycles or honk their horns while driving through the streets. Guns are also sometimes fired. These practices have been discouraged by the [Grand Mufti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mufti) of [Bangsamoro](37260549.md) and local government officials as not being in accordance with Islamic teachings as well as being dangerous and causing accidents in the past.[^cite_note-Cabrera-115][^cite_note-Rosalado-116][^cite_note-Jocson-117]

Hariraya is characterized by the giving of gifts (known as *Eid*), food sharing (*salu-salo*), and visiting the elderly and the sick.[^cite_note-Jocson-117][^cite_note-118] Food, alms, and basic necessities are also donated to the poor, a practice known as *Fitrana* or *Zakat al-Fitr*. This is usually done a day before Eid al-Fitr.[^cite_note-Tan-110][^cite_note-119] Various traditional sweet delicacies of the different Muslim Filipino ethnic groups are served for breakfast, including [daral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daral_\(food\)), [dodol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodol), [browas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mam%C3%B3n), [tinagtag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokot-lokot), [panyalam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panyalam), [jampok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maruya_\(food\)), and so on.[^cite_note-120] Various activities also mark the celebrations, including dancing, boat races, horse races, and [carabao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carabao) fighting in cities and towns with significant Muslim populations. In [Metro Manila](72340.md), the celebrations are usually held at the [Manila Golden Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Golden_Mosque) and the [Quirino Grandstand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quirino_Grandstand).[^cite_note-tl-113] The celebration lasts for three days.[^cite_note-cp-114]

### East Asia

#### China mainland

See also: [Islam in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_China)



An ethnic [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) family celebrating Eid al-Fitr in [Ningxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningxia)

In [mainland China](55079.md), out of 56 officially recognized ethnic groups, Eid al-Fitr is celebrated by at least 10 ethnic groups that are predominantly Muslim. These groups are said to total 18 million according to official statistics, but some observers say the actual number may be much higher. It is also a [public holiday in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_People's_Republic_of_China) in certain regions, including two Province Prefecture Level regions, [Ningxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningxia) and [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang). All residents in these areas, regardless of religion, are entitled to either a one-day or three-day official holiday. Outside the Muslim-majority regions, only Muslims are entitled to a one-day holiday. In Xinjiang province, Eid al-Fitr is even celebrated by the [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) population. During the holiday, supplies of mutton, lamb and beef are distributed to households as part of a welfare program funded by government agencies, public and private institutions, and businesses. In [Yunnan](166410.md), Muslim populations are spread throughout the region. On Eid al-Fitr, however, some devotees may travel to [Sayyid 'Ajjal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_Ajjal_Shams_al-Din_Omar)'s grave after their communal prayers. There, they will conduct readings from the Quran and clean the tomb, reminiscent of the historic annual Chinese Qingming festival, in which people go to their ancestors' graves, sweep and clean the area, and make food offerings. Finally the accomplishments of the Sayyid 'Ajall will be related in story form, concluded by a special prayer service to honour the hundreds of thousands of Muslims killed during the [Panthay Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panthay_Rebellion), and the hundreds killed during the [Shadian incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadian_incident).[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123]

#### Taiwan

See also: [Islam in Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Taiwan)

The Eid al-Fitr prayer and celebration in Taiwan draws much attention from local media. Special features of the event are regularly carried out in the newspapers and aired on televisions. These phenomena gives a boost to the Islamic activities in Taiwan.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] Muslims, mostly [Indonesian](4885606.md) blue collar men and women, typically gather at [Taipei Main Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Main_Station) to perform the prayer.[^cite_note-126]

### Europe



[Pomaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomaks) dancing during Ramazan Bayram in a village centre in [Bulgaria](3415.md)

#### Albania and Kosovo

Albanian Muslims generally celebrate the day as with most other countries. The day is known as Fitër Bajrami or Bajrami i Madh in Albania. Worshipers attend a dawn prayer and a sermon, after which people visit each other, plan gatherings and give gifts to children.[^cite_note-127] [Magiritsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magiritsa) (Greek Easter soup) and [baklava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baklava) are popularly consumed.[^cite_note-auto1-54]

#### Bosnia and Herzegovina

In [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), Eid al-Fitr is locally known as Ramazanski bajram. It is a three-day public holiday. Like Albanians, worshipers attend a dawn prayer and a sermon, after which people visit each other, give gifts to children, and popularly consume [baklava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baklava).[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

#### Germany

In Germany, Eid al-Fitr is most commonly known as *Zuckerfest* ("Sugar Festival", a [calque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calque) of the Turkish *Şeker Bayramı*).[^cite_note-Zuckerfest-130] It is not a public holiday, but most states allow children to take the day off school and some businesses close for the day or allow Muslim employees to take the holiday.[^cite_note-In_Deutschland-131] Due to the history of [Turkish immigration to Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_in_Germany), Turkish traditions and terminology dominate in German Eid al-Fitr celebrations.[^cite_note-Zuckerfest-130]

#### Greece and Cyprus

Eid al-Fitr (i.e. Seker Bayram, Sugar Feast) ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Σεκέρ Μπαϊράμ or Ιντ αλ-φιτρ) is celebrated in Greece mainly in the [Western Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Thrace) region from the local [Muslim minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_minority_of_Greece) ([Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_of_Western_Thrace), [Pomaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomaks) and [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people_in_Greece)), along with the other two major celebrations, [Kurban Bayram](358226.md) (Sacrifice Feast) ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Κουρμπάν Μπαϊράμ or Ιντ αλ-αντχά) and [Hıdırellez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C4%B1d%C4%B1rellez); it is also celebrated by the Turks of Northern Cyprus. On the day of the Bayram, family gathers together, wear their best clothes, and celebrate with a common meal, after attending the morning prayer. The women prepare and offer sweets to family and visitors, while small children go around and pay their respects to the elderly by kissing their hands. The elder in turn reward them with candies, sweets, and small amounts of money.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-edu-133] Local Muslim shopkeepers close their shops this day, while [Muslim minority schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_minority_in_Greece#Education) have a 5-day holiday for the feast.[^cite_note-edu-133]

#### Lithuania, Belarus and Poland

[Lithuania](17675.md), [Belarus](3457.md) and Poland are homes to an indigenous Muslim people, the [Lipka Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipka_Tatars), though Lithuania and Poland have seen newer Muslims arriving, mainly the [Chechens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechens), [Volga Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_Tatars) and [Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars), as well as Muslims from various Asian and African states, altogether making up just 0,1%. In both countries, Eid al-Fitr is referred as "*Ramadan Bajram*" and celebrated by these Muslims of both states.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

#### Russia



Uraza Bayram celebration in [Kazan](202295.md), Tatarstan, Russia (2025).

In Russia where 14 million Muslims reside as of 2017,[^cite_note-137] Eid al-Fitr is often known as *Uraza Bayram* ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Ураза-байрам) and is a public holiday in the republics of [Adygea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adygea), [Bashkortostan](735530.md), [Dagestan](751099.md), [Ingushetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingushetia), [Kabardino-Balkaria](817725.md), [Karachay-Cherkessia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karachay-Cherkessia), [Tatarstan](67135.md) and [Chechnya](6095.md).[^cite_note-138] Most festive dishes consist of [mutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutton), but salads and various soups are also popular. As the Muslim population is diverse, traditional festive dishes differ between regions – for example in Tatarstan [pancakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancakes) are popularly baked.[^cite_note-139]

Russian Muslims go to festive worships at mosques in the morning of Eid al-Fitr, after which they often visit older relatives as a sign of respect. In the North Caucasian republics, children popularly go past various houses with a bag to get it filled with [candy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candy), specially stored by locals for the celebration. In Dagestan, eggs with bright stickers is a popular traditional dish served there during Eid al-Fitr. People generally dress more during this day – women choose bright dresses with [beads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bead) while older people would wear [papakhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papakha).[^cite_note-auto2-140] In many places in the country master classes are also hosted where families take part in activities such as [embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery) and [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay) making.[^cite_note-auto2-140]

#### Spain

In Spain, Eid al-Fitr (officially in Spanish: 'Fiesta del Eid Fitr'[^cite_note-141]) is officially recognised in Spanish law (Resolución de 17 de octubre de 2025: BOE-A-2025-21667[^cite_note-142]) and celebrated in the autonomous city of Melilla (Spanish: Ciudad de [Melilla](20226.md)) on the [North African coast](21714.md) along with [Eid al-Adha](358226.md) (officially named in la Ciudad de Melilla as 'Fiesta Sacrificio-Aid al Adha'[^cite_note-143]). Meanwhile, also on the North African coast, the autonomous city of Ceuta (Spanish: Ciudad de [Ceuta](6443.md)) celebrates only the latter, Eid al-Adha (officially named in la Ciudad de Ceuta as 'Fiesta Sacrificio-Eidul Adha'[^cite_note-144]) as a public holiday. Its second public holiday is instead dedicated to its patron saint, '[Our Lady of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Africa_\(title\))' (officially in Spanish: 'Nuestra Señora de África'[^cite_note-145]).

#### Ukraine

In Ukraine, Eid al-Fitr ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Рамазан-байрам) as well as [Eid al-Adha](358226.md) have been official state holidays since 2020.[^cite_note-146] During the festival, [Ukrainian Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Muslims) (most of whom are [Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars)) often gather with loved ones.[^cite_note-147]

#### United Kingdom

Although Eid al-Fitr is not a recognized public holiday in the United Kingdom,[^cite_note-148] many schools, businesses, and organisations allow for at least a day's leave to be taken for religious celebrations.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

In the United Kingdom, individuals take part in prayers and play games for Eid al-Fitr.[^cite_note-151] [Blackburn Rovers F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackburn_Rovers_F.C.) had an Eid prayer on their pitch in 2022.[^cite_note-152]

### Americas

#### United States



[Muslim U.S. soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslims_in_the_United_States_military) performing the Eid prayer

In New York City, alternate side parking (street cleaning) regulations are suspended on Eid. Beginning in 2016, New York City public schools also remain closed on Eid.[^cite_note-153] Other school districts close on Eid, including Irvington New Jersey's school district and Vermont's Burlington School District.[^cite_note-154]

The [United States Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service) (USPS) has issued several Eid postage stamps, across several years—starting in 2001—honoring "two of the most important festivals in the Islamic calendar: Eid al-Fitr and Eid al-Adha." Eid stamps were released in 2001–2002, 2006–2009, 2011, and 2013. They are also being issued as [Forever Stamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-denominated_postage). All Eid stamps to date show the work of [Mohamed Zakariya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Zakariya).[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159]

Mosques in North America offer Eid prayer. For Eid during the [COVID-19 pandemic in the United States](63136490.md), some mosques required temperature checks and for participants to socially distance. Other mosques gathered at public parks to celebrate. By 2021, some Muslims were returning to mosques to pray for the holiday.[^cite_note-160]

#### Trinidad and Tobago

The Muslim population of [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) popularly eat sawine/[vermicelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermicelli), a sweet dessert, to mark the celebration,[^cite_note-161] but the feast also consists of curries, [rotis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti), and chicken and [curry goat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry_goat).[^cite_note-162] Eid al-Fitr is a public holiday in Trinidad and Tobago, having been declared in 1962.[^cite_note-163]

#### Suriname, Guyana

Eid al-Fitr is a public holiday in [Suriname](26828.md) and [Guyana](17238691.md).[^cite_note-164]

#### Argentina

In Argentina, Eid ([Spanish](26825.md): *Fiesta del Fin del Ayuno*) is officially a non-working holiday for [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Argentina).[^cite_note-165]

### Oceania

#### Australia and New Zealand

Australian and New Zealand Muslims celebrate Eid al-Fitr in a variety of ways depending on their specific cultural background. Many local celebrations take place in community centers and mosques.[^cite_note-166]

## In the Gregorian calendar

See also: [Islamic calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar) and [Gregorian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_calendar)

[](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ae/Hijri_to_gregorian_calendar.svg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org\&utm_campaign=index\&utm_content=original)

Conversion of Hijri years 1343 to 1500 to the Gregorian calendar, with first days of al-Muharram (brown), Ramadan (grey) and Shawwal (black) bolded, and Eid al-Adha dotted – in [the SVG file,](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/a/ae/Hijri_to_gregorian_calendar.svg?utm_source=en.wikipedia.org\&utm_campaign=index\&utm_content=original) hover over a spot to show its dates and a line to show the month

Although the date of Eid al-Fitr is always the same in the Islamic calendar, the date in the Gregorian calendar falls approximately 11 days earlier each successive year, since the Islamic calendar is [lunar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_calendar) and the Gregorian calendar is [solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_calendar). Hence if the Eid falls in the first ten days of a Gregorian calendar year, there will be a second Eid in the last ten days of the same Gregorian calendar year, as happened in 2000 CE. The Gregorian date may vary between countries depending on the local visibility of the new moon. Some expatriate Muslim communities follow the dates as determined for the nearest Islamic country to their country of residence, while others follow the local dates of their home country.[^cite_note-167]
