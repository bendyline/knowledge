**Pregnancy** is the time during which one or more [offspring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offspring) [gestates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestation) inside a [woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman)'s [uterus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterus).[^cite_note-NIH2013Def-4][^cite_note-Mosby-10] A [multiple pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_birth) involves more than one offspring, such as with [twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin).[^cite_note-\:1-11]

[Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conception_\(biology\)) usually occurs following [vaginal intercourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_intercourse), but can also occur through [assisted reproductive technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_reproductive_technology) procedures.[^cite_note-She2016-12] A pregnancy may end in a [live birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_birth_\(human\)), a [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage), an [induced abortion](765.md#Induced), or a [stillbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stillbirth). [Childbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childbirth) typically occurs around 40 weeks from the start of the [last menstrual period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstruation#Onset_and_frequency) (LMP), a span known as the [*gestational age*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_age_\(obstetrics\));[^cite_note-NIH2013Def-4][^cite_note-Ab2011-5] this is just over nine months. Counting by [*fertilization age*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_fertilization#Fertilization_age), the length is about 38 weeks.[^cite_note-Ab2011-5][^cite_note-Mosby-10] [Implantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implantation_\(embryology\)) occurs on average 8–9 days after [fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_fertilization).[^cite_note-13] An *[embryo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryo)* is the term for the developing offspring during the first seven weeks following implantation (i.e. ten weeks' gestational age), after which the term *[fetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetus)* is used until the birth of a *[baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby)*.[^cite_note-Ab2011-5]

[Signs and symptoms of early pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signs_and_symptoms_of_pregnancy) may include [missed periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amenorrhea), [tender breasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_tenderness), [morning sickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_sickness) (nausea and vomiting), hunger, [implantation bleeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implantation_bleeding), and frequent urination.[^cite_note-NIH2013Sym-1] Pregnancy may be confirmed with a [pregnancy test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_test).[^cite_note-NIH2012Diag-6] Methods of [contraception](18978770.md) are used to avoid pregnancy.

Pregnancy is divided into three trimesters of approximately three months each. The [first trimester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_trimester) includes conception, which is when the sperm fertilizes the egg. The [fertilized egg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilized_egg) then travels down the [fallopian tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallopian_tube) and attaches to the inside of the [uterus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterus), where it begins to form the [embryo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embryo) and [placenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta). During the first trimester, the possibility of miscarriage (natural death of embryo or fetus) is at its highest. Around the middle of the second trimester, movement of the fetus may be felt. At 28 weeks, more than 90% of babies can [survive outside of the uterus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetal_viability) if [provided with high-quality medical care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neonatal_intensive_care_unit), though babies born at this time will likely experience serious health complications such as heart and respiratory problems and long-term intellectual and developmental disabilities.

[Prenatal care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_care) improves pregnancy outcomes.[^cite_note-NIH2013Prenatal-14] [Nutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutrition_and_pregnancy) during pregnancy is important to ensure healthy growth of the fetus.[^cite_note-Handbook-15] Prenatal care also include avoiding [recreational drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_drug) (including [tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking_and_pregnancy) and [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetal_alcohol_spectrum_disorder)), taking regular exercise, having [blood tests](336557.md), and regular [physical examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_examination).[^cite_note-NIH2013Prenatal-14] [Complications of pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complications_of_pregnancy) may include [disorders of high blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertensive_disease_of_pregnancy), [gestational diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_diabetes), [iron-deficiency anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron-deficiency_anemia), and [severe nausea and vomiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperemesis_gravidarum).[^cite_note-NIH2013Compli-3] In the ideal childbirth, labour begins on its own "at term".[^cite_note-ACOGfive-2-16] Babies born before 37 weeks are [preterm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preterm) and at higher risk of health problems such as [cerebral palsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_palsy).[^cite_note-NIH2013Def-4] Babies born between weeks 37 and 39 are considered "early term" while those born between weeks 39 and 41 are considered "full term".[^cite_note-NIH2013Def-4] Babies born between weeks 41 and 42 weeks are considered "late-term" while after 42 weeks they are considered "[post-term](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postterm_pregnancy)".[^cite_note-NIH2013Def-4] [Delivery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childbirth) before 39 weeks by [labour induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_induction) or [caesarean section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarean_section) is not recommended unless required for other medical reasons.[^cite_note-WHO2014-17]

## Terminology

Title page from an 18th-century book about pregnancy

[William Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hunter_\(anatomist\)), *Anatomia uteri humani gravidi tabulis illustrata*, 1774

Associated terms for pregnancy are *gravid* and *parous*. *Gravidus* and *gravid* come from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) word meaning "heavy" and a pregnant female is sometimes referred to as a *gravida*.[^cite_note-MFD-18] *[Gravidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravidity_and_parity)* refers to the number of times that a female has been pregnant. Similarly, the term *[parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravidity_and_parity#Parity_in_medicine)* is used for the number of times that a female carries a pregnancy to a [viable stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetal_viability).[^cite_note-Patient-19] [Twins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twins) and other multiple births are counted as one pregnancy and birth.

A woman who has never been pregnant is referred to as a *nulligravida.* A woman who is (or has been only) pregnant for the first time is referred to as a *primigravida*,[^cite_note-TMHP-20] and a woman in subsequent pregnancies as a *[multigravida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multigravida)* or as *multiparous.*[^cite_note-MFD-18][^cite_note-21] Therefore, during a second pregnancy a woman would be described as *gravida 2, para 1* and upon live delivery as *gravida 2, para 2.* In-progress pregnancies, [abortions](765.md), [miscarriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage) and/or [stillbirths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stillbirth) account for parity values being less than the gravida number. Women who have never carried a pregnancy more than 20 weeks are referred to as *nulliparous*.[^cite_note-22]

A pregnancy is considered *term* at 37 weeks of gestation. It is *preterm* if less than 37 weeks and *post-term* at or beyond 42 weeks of gestation. The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists have recommended further division to *early term* from 37 weeks up to 39 weeks, *full term* 39 weeks up to 41 weeks, and *late term* 41 weeks up to 42 weeks.[^cite_note-23] The terms *preterm* and *post-term* have largely replaced the earlier terms *premature* and *postmature*, which related historically to the infant's size and state of development rather than to the stage of pregnancy.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## Demographics

About 213 million pregnancies occurred in 2012, of which, 190 million (89%) were in the [developing world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_world) and 23 million (11%) were in the developed world.[^cite_note-Sed2014-8] The number of pregnancies in women aged between 15 and 44 is 133 per 1,000 women.[^cite_note-Sed2014-8] Pregnancy rates are 140 per 1000 women of childbearing age in the developing world and 94 per 1000 in the developed world.[^cite_note-Sed2014-8] The rate of pregnancy, as well as the ages at which it occurs, differ by country and region. It is influenced by a number of factors, such as cultural, social and religious norms; access to contraception; and rates of education. The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) (TFR) in 2024 was estimated to be highest in [Niger](21373.md) (6.64 children/woman) and lowest in [South Korea](27019.md) (1.12 children/woman).[^cite_note-26]

About 10% to 15% of recognized pregnancies end in [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage).[^cite_note-John2012-2] In 2016, [complications of pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complications_of_pregnancy) resulted in 230,600 [maternal deaths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_death), down from 377,000 deaths in 1990.[^cite_note-GBD2016-9] Common causes include [bleeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_bleeding), [infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postpartum_infections), [hypertensive diseases of pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertensive_disease_of_pregnancy), [obstructed labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstructed_labor), miscarriage, abortion, or [ectopic pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectopic_pregnancy).[^cite_note-GBD2016-9] Globally, 44% of pregnancies are [unplanned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unintended_pregnancy).[^cite_note-Bea2018-27] Over half (56%) of unplanned pregnancies are aborted.[^cite_note-Bea2018-27] In countries where [abortion is prohibited](697618.md), or only carried out in circumstances where the mother's life is at risk, 48% of unplanned pregnancies are [aborted illegally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_abortion). Compared to the rate in countries where abortion is legal, at 69%.[^cite_note-Bea2018-27] Among unintended pregnancies in the United States, 60% of the women used [birth control](18978770.md) to some extent during the month pregnancy *had* began.[^cite_note-28]

In the United States, a woman's educational attainment and her marital status are historically correlated with childbearing: the percentage of women unmarried at the time of first birth drops with increasing educational level. Three studies conducted between 2015 and 2018 indicate a large fraction (\~80%) of women without a [high school diploma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_diploma) or [ local equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Educational_Development) in the US are unmarried at the time of their first birth. By contrast, the same studies indicated fewer women with a bachelor's degree or higher (\~24%) have their first child while unmarried. However, this phenomenon also has a strong generational component: a 1996 study found 48.2% of US women without a bachelor's degree had their first child whilst unmarried, and only 4% of women with a bachelor's degree had their first child whilst unmarried. These studies indicate a rising trend for US women of all educational levels to be unmarried at the time of their first birth.[^cite_note-29]

### Teenage pregnancy

Main article: [Teenage pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_pregnancy)

[Teenage pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_pregnancy) is also known as [adolescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolescent) pregnancy.[^cite_note-WHO3-30] The [WHO](33583.md) defines adolescence as the period between the ages of 10 and 19 years.[^cite_note-WHO1-31] Adolescents face higher health risks than women who give birth at age 20 to 24 and their infants are at a higher risk for preterm birth, low birth weight, and other severe neonatal conditions. Their children continue to face greater challenges, both behavioral and physical, throughout their lives. Teenage pregnancies are also related to social issues, including [social stigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stigma), lower educational levels, and poverty.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-WHO3-30] Female adolescents are often in abusive relationships at the time of their conceiving.[^cite_note-33]

## Diagnosis

[Penile-vaginal sex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penile-vaginal_sex) is typically the cause of pregnancy, although myths regarding other sexual acts persist, particularly in populations that received poor sex education such as [abstinence-only](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstinence-only_sex_education).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Pregnancy can also be the result of [assisted reproductive technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_reproductive_technology).[^cite_note-36]

The beginning of pregnancy may be detected based on the woman's symptoms or by using [pregnancy tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_test). [Denial of pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denial_of_pregnancy) by the woman is a common condition with serious health implications. About 1 in 475 denials will last until around the 20th week of pregnancy. Denial persisting until delivery occurs in about 1 in 2,500 cases.[^cite_note-pmid21725094-37] Conversely, some non-pregnant women have a strong belief that they are pregnant along with some physical changes. This condition is known as a [false pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_pregnancy).[^cite_note-Gabbe2012-38]

- [implantation bleeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implantation_bleeding) that occurs at [implantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implantation_\(embryology\)) of the embryo in the uterus during the third or fourth week after last menstrual period

- [Goodell's sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodell's_sign) (softening of the vaginal portion of the cervix)

Other common symptoms include [constipation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constipation), [back pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Back_pain), [pelvic girdle pain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelvic_girdle_pain), [headaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headaches),[^cite_note-Common-45] and food cravings or food aversions.[^cite_note-\:5-42] Pregnant women may also experience [urinary tract infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urinary_tract_infection#Pregnant_women),[^cite_note-46] [increased urinary frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Increased_urinary_frequency),[^cite_note-47] worsened [sleep quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_quality), increased [dream recall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_recall), and [nightmares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightmare).[^cite_note-48] In later pregnancy, [hemorrhoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemorrhoids) are more common.[^cite_note-CE-Vazquez-49] Each person's pregnancy can be different and many women do not experience all of the common signs and symptoms.[^cite_note-\:3-50] The usual [signs and symptoms of pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signs_and_symptoms_of_pregnancy) do not significantly interfere with [activities of daily living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activities_of_daily_living) or pose a health-threat to the [mother](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother) or fetus.[^cite_note-\:3-50] Complications during pregnancy can cause other more severe symptoms, such as those associated with [anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemia).[^cite_note-51]

### Biomarkers

Further information: [Pregnancy test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_test)

Pregnancy detection can be accomplished using one or more various [pregnancy tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_test),[^cite_note-pregnancy-52] which detect hormones generated by the newly formed [placenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta), serving as [biomarkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomarker_\(medicine\)) of pregnancy.[^cite_note-53] Blood and urine tests can detect pregnancy by 11 and 14 days, respectively, after fertilization.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-American_Pregnancy_Association_2021-55] Blood pregnancy tests are more [sensitive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensitivity_and_specificity) than urine tests (giving fewer false negatives).[^cite_note-56] Home pregnancy tests are urine tests, and normally detect a pregnancy 12 to 15 days after fertilization.[^cite_note-Cole-57] A quantitative blood test can determine approximately the date the embryo was fertilized because [hCG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_chorionic_gonadotropin) levels double every 36 to 72 hours before 8 weeks' gestation.[^cite_note-Williams-58][^cite_note-American_Pregnancy_Association_2021-55] A single test of [progesterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progesterone) levels can also help determine how likely a fetus will survive in those with a [threatened miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threatened_miscarriage) (bleeding in early pregnancy), but only if the ultrasound result was inconclusive.[^cite_note-pmid23045257-59]

### Ultrasound

Main article: [Obstetric ultrasonography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetric_ultrasonography)

[Obstetric ultrasonography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetric_ultrasonography) can detect [fetal abnormalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fetal_abnormalities), detect [multiple pregnancies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_pregnancy), and improve gestational dating at 24 weeks.[^cite_note-60] The resultant estimated [gestational age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_age_\(obstetrics\)) and due date of the fetus are slightly more accurate than methods based on last menstrual period.[^cite_note-61] Ultrasound is used to measure the [nuchal fold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuchal_scan) in order to screen for [Down syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_syndrome).[^cite_note-62]

### Medical imaging



[CT scanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan) ([volume rendered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volume_rendering) in this case) confers a [radiation dose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorbed_dose) to the developing fetus.



A pregnant woman undergoing an [ultrasound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultrasound). Ultrasound is used to check on the growth and development of the fetus.

Main article: [Medical imaging in pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging_in_pregnancy)

[Medical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging) may be [indicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indication_\(medicine\)) in pregnancy because of [pregnancy complications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complications_of_pregnancy), disease, or routine [prenatal care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_care). [Medical ultrasonography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_ultrasonography) including [obstetric ultrasonography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetric_ultrasonography), and [magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging_in_pregnancy) without [contrast agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MRI_contrast_agent) are not associated with any risk for the mother or the fetus, and are the imaging techniques of choice for pregnant women.[^cite_note-acog-63] [Projectional radiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projectional_radiography), [CT scan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan) and [nuclear medicine imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_medicine#Diagnostic) result in some degree of [ionizing radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionizing_radiation) exposure, but in most cases the [absorbed doses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorbed_dose) are not associated with harm to the baby.[^cite_note-acog-63] At higher dosages or frequency, effects can include [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage), [birth defects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_defect) and [intellectual disability](18567040.md).[^cite_note-acog-63]

The [chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronology) of pregnancy is, unless otherwise specified, generally given as [gestational age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_age_\(obstetrics\)), where the starting point is the beginning of the woman's [last menstrual period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_menstrual_period) (LMP), or the corresponding age of the gestation as estimated by a more accurate method if available. This model means that the woman is counted as being "pregnant" two weeks before [conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conception_\(biology\)) and three weeks before [implantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implantation_\(human_embryo\)). Sometimes, timing may also use the [fertilization age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilization_age), which is the age of the embryo since conception.

### Start of gestational age

Main article: [Gestational age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_age)

The [American Congress of Obstetricians and Gynecologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Congress_of_Obstetricians_and_Gynecologists) recommends the following methods to calculate gestational age:[^cite_note-acog2012-66]

- Directly calculating the days since the beginning of the [last menstrual period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstrual_cycle).

- Early [obstetric ultrasound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetric_ultrasound), comparing the size of an [embryo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_embryo) or [fetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetus) to that of a [reference group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reference_group) of pregnancies of known gestational age (such as calculated from last menstrual periods), and using the mean gestational age of other embryos or fetuses of the same size. If the gestational age as calculated from an early ultrasound is contradictory to the one calculated directly from the last menstrual period, it is still the one from the early ultrasound that is used for the rest of the pregnancy.

  [^cite_note-acog2012-66]

- In case of [in vitro fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro_fertilization), calculating days since [oocyte retrieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oocyte_retrieval) or [co-incubation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-incubation) and adding 14 days.

  [^cite_note-67]

### Estimation of due date



Distribution of [gestational age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_age) at childbirth among singleton live births, given both when gestational age is estimated by first trimester ultrasound and directly by last menstrual period.

[^cite_note-hoffman2008-69]

&#x20;Roughly 80% of births occur between 37 and 41 weeks of gestational age.

Main article: [Estimated date of delivery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimated_date_of_delivery)

[Due date estimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estimated_date_of_delivery) basically follows two steps:

- Determination of which time point is to be used as [origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_\(number\)) for [gestational age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_age_\(obstetrics\)), as described in the section above.

- Adding the estimated gestational age at childbirth to the above time point. Childbirth on average occurs at a gestational age of 280 days (40 weeks), which is therefore often used as a standard estimation for individual pregnancies.

  [^cite_note-mayo-70]

  &#x20;However, alternative durations as well as more individualized methods have also been suggested.

The [American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_College_of_Obstetricians_and_Gynecologists) divides full term into three divisions:[^cite_note-71]

- Early-term: 37 weeks and 0 days through 38 weeks and 6 days

- Full-term: 39 weeks and 0 days through 40 weeks and 6 days

- Late-term: 41 weeks and 0 days through 41 weeks and 6 days

- Post-term: greater than or equal to 42 weeks and 0 days

*Naegele's rule* is a standard way of calculating the due date for a pregnancy when assuming a gestational age of 280 days at childbirth. The rule estimates the expected date of delivery (EDD) by adding a year, subtracting three months, and adding seven days to the origin of gestational age. Alternatively there are [mobile apps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_app), which essentially always give consistent estimations compared to each other and correct for [leap year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leap_year), while pregnancy wheels made of paper can differ from each other by 7 days and generally do not correct for leap year.[^cite_note-pmid24036402-72]

Furthermore, actual childbirth has only a certain probability of occurring within the limits of the estimated due date. A study of singleton live births came to the result that childbirth has a [standard deviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_deviation) of 14 days when gestational age is estimated by first trimester [ultrasound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetric_ultrasonography), and 16 days when estimated directly by last menstrual period.[^cite_note-hoffman2008-69]

## Physiology

### Capacity

Further information: [Pregnancy over age 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_over_age_50)

[Fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility) and [fecundity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fecundity) are the respective capacities to [fertilize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_fertilization) and establish a clinical pregnancy and have a live birth. [Infertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infertility) is an impaired ability to establish a clinical pregnancy and [sterility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterility_\(physiology\)) is the permanent inability to establish a clinical pregnancy.[^cite_note-Zegers-Hochschild_Adamson_Dyer_Racowsky_2017_pp._393–406-73]

The capacity for pregnancy depends on the [reproductive system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_reproductive_system), [its development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Development_of_the_reproductive_system) and [its variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variations_in_sex_characteristics), as well as on the condition of a person. Anyone who has a functioning [female reproductive system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_reproductive_system), regardless of [intersex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersex) or [transgender](19904525.md) identity, is capable of becoming pregnant.&#x20;

Some people are not capable of becoming pregnant, even with advanced [assisted reproductive technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_reproductive_technology). In some cases, someone might produce viable eggs, but might [not have a womb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterine_malformation) or none that can sufficiently gestate, in which case they will not be able to become pregnant or sustain the pregnancy. [Surrogacy](1514981.md) is their only option for having genetic children.[^cite_note-nhs.uk_2021-74]

### Initiation

See also: [Human fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_fertilization)



Fertilization and implantation in humans

Through an interplay of hormones that includes [follicle stimulating hormone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Follicle_stimulating_hormone) that stimulates [folliculogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folliculogenesis) and [oogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oogenesis) creates a mature [egg cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_cell), the female [gamete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamete). [Fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_fertilization) is the event where the egg cell fuses with the male gamete, [spermatozoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spermatozoon). After the point of fertilization, the fused product of the female and male gamete is referred to as a [zygote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygote) or fertilized egg. The fusion of female and male gametes usually occurs following the act of [sexual intercourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_intercourse). [Pregnancy rates for sexual intercourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_rate#Pregnancy_rate_for_sexual_intercourse) are highest during the [menstrual cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstrual_cycle) time from some 5 days before until 1 to 2 days after ovulation.[^cite_note-75] Fertilization can also occur by [assisted reproductive technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_reproductive_technology) such as [artificial insemination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_insemination) and [in vitro fertilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro_fertilisation).

Fertilization (conception) is sometimes used as the initiation of pregnancy, with the derived age being termed [fertilization age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilization_age). Fertilization usually occurs about two weeks before the *next* expected menstrual period.

A third point in time is also considered by some people to be the true beginning of a pregnancy: This is time of implantation, when the future fetus attaches to the lining of the uterus. This is about a week to ten days after fertilization.[^cite_note-Berger-76]

### Development of embryo and fetus

Main articles: [Human embryonic development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_embryonic_development), [Prenatal development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_development), and [Fetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetus)



The initial stages of [human embryogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_embryogenesis)

The sperm and the egg cell, which has been released from one of the female's two [ovaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovaries), unite in one of the two [fallopian tubes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallopian_tube). The fertilized egg, known as a [zygote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zygote), then moves toward the uterus, a journey that can take up to a week to complete. Cell division begins approximately 24 to 36 hours after the female and male cells unite. Cell division continues at a rapid rate and the cells then develop into what is known as a [blastocyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blastocyst). The blastocyst arrives at the uterus and attaches to the uterine wall, a process known as [implantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Implantation_\(human_embryo\)).

The development of the mass of cells that will become the infant is called [embryogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_embryogenesis) during the first approximately ten weeks of gestation. During this time, cells begin to differentiate into the various body systems. The basic outlines of the organ, body, and nervous systems are established. By the end of the embryonic stage, the beginnings of features such as fingers, eyes, mouth, and ears become visible. Also during this time, there is development of structures important to the support of the embryo, including the [placenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta) and [umbilical cord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbilical_cord). The placenta connects the developing embryo to the uterine wall to allow nutrient uptake, waste elimination, and gas exchange via the mother's blood supply. The umbilical cord is the connecting cord from the embryo or fetus to the placenta.

After about ten weeks of gestational age—which is the same as eight weeks after conception—the embryo becomes known as a [fetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetus).[^cite_note-MSD-77] At the beginning of the fetal stage, the risk of miscarriage decreases sharply.[^cite_note-sharply-78] At this stage, a fetus is about 30 mm (1.2 inches) in length, the heartbeat is seen via ultrasound, and the fetus makes involuntary motions.[^cite_note-Prechtl-79] During continued fetal development, the early body systems, and structures that were established in the embryonic stage continue to develop. Sex organs begin to appear during the third month of gestation. The fetus continues to grow in both weight and length, although the majority of the physical growth occurs in the last weeks of pregnancy.

Electrical [brain activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_activity) is first detected at the end of week 5 of gestation, but as in [brain-dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain-death) patients, it is primitive neural activity rather than the beginning of conscious brain activity. Synapses do not begin to form until week 17.[^cite_note-80] Neural connections between the [sensory cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sensory_cortex) and [thalamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalamus) develop as early as 24 weeks' gestational age, but the first evidence of their function does not occur until around 30 weeks, when minimal [consciousness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consciousness), [dreaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream), and the ability to feel pain emerges.[^cite_note-81]

Although the fetus begins to move during the first trimester, it is not until the second trimester that movement, known as [quickening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quickening), can be felt. This typically happens in the fourth month, more specifically in the 20th to 21st week, or by the 19th week if the woman has been pregnant before. It is common for some women not to feel the fetus move until much later. During the second trimester, when the body size changes, [maternity clothes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternity_clothes) may be worn.

### Maternal changes



The [uterus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterus) expands making up a larger and larger portion of the abdomen. During the final stages of gestation the uterus may drop to a lower position.

Main article: [Physiological changes in pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiological_changes_in_pregnancy)



Breast changes as seen during pregnancy. The areolae are larger and darker.

During pregnancy, a woman undergoes many normal [physiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiological) changes, including [behavioral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parental_brain), [cardiovascular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular), [hematologic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematologic), [metabolic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolic), [renal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renal), and [respiratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiration_\(physiology\)) changes. Increases in [blood sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_sugar_level), [breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_rate), and [cardiac output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_output) are all required. Levels of [progesterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progesterone) and [estrogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrogen) rise continually throughout pregnancy, suppressing the [hypothalamic axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothalamic%E2%80%93pituitary%E2%80%93gonadal_axis) and therefore the [menstrual cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menstrual_cycle). A full-term pregnancy at an early age (less than 25 years) reduces the risk of [breast](70547.md), [ovarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovarian_cancer), and [endometrial cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endometrial_cancer), and the risk declines further with each additional full-term pregnancy.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]



End of second trimester + 2 weeks (26 weeks of pregnancy)

The fetus is genetically different from its mother and can therefore be viewed as an unusually successful [allograft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allograft).[^cite_note-Mor-84] The main reason for this success is increased [immune tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_tolerance_in_pregnancy) during pregnancy,[^cite_note-85] which prevents the mother's body from mounting an [immune system response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_response) against certain triggers.[^cite_note-Mor-84] A [Rho(D) immune globulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rho\(D\)_immune_globulin) shot is recommended for women with RhD negative blood carrying a RhD positive fetus as a preventative measure against [Rhesus disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhesus_disease).[^cite_note-86]

During the first trimester, [minute ventilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minute_ventilation) increases by 40 percent.[^cite_note-pmid11316633-87] The womb will grow to the size of a [lemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemon) by eight weeks. Many [symptoms and discomforts of pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symptoms_and_discomforts_of_pregnancy), such as nausea and [tender breasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_tenderness), appear in the first trimester.[^cite_note-88] [Braxton Hicks contractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braxton_Hicks_contractions) are sporadic [uterine contractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterine_contraction) that may start around six weeks into a pregnancy but are usually not felt until the second or third trimester.[^cite_note-Hen2005-89]

Pregnant women have higher total [blood volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_volume) that increases throughout the duration of the pregnancy.[^cite_note-90] It is during the third trimester that maternal activity and sleep positions may affect [fetal development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetal_development) due to restricted [blood flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_flow). For instance, the enlarged uterus may impede blood flow by compressing the [vena cava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vena_cava) when lying flat, a condition that can be relieved by lying on the left side.[^cite_note-pmid21673002-91]

Most weight gain takes place during the third trimester. A pregnant woman's [navel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navel) may become convex ("popping" out) during this time. Her abdomen will expand and change in shape as the fetus turns in a downward position nearing childbirth.[^cite_note-92] [Head engagement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_engagement), also called "lightening" or "dropping", occurs as the fetal head descends into a [cephalic presentation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cephalic_presentation). While it relieves pressure on the upper abdomen and gives a renewed ease in breathing, it also severely reduces bladder capacity, resulting in a need to [void more frequently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urinary_frequency), and increases pressure on the pelvic floor and the rectum. It is not possible to predict when lightening will occur. In a first pregnancy it may happen a few weeks before the due date, though it may happen later or even not until labour begins, as is typical with subsequent pregnancies.[^cite_note-93]

### Childbirth

Main article: [Childbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childbirth)

Childbirth, referred to as labour and delivery in the medical field, is the process whereby an infant is born.[^cite_note-Williams-58]

A woman is considered to be in labour when she begins experiencing regular uterine contractions, accompanied by changes of her cervix—primarily effacement and dilation. While childbirth is widely experienced as painful, some women do report painless labours, while others find that concentrating on the birth helps to quicken labour and lessen the sensations. Most births are successful vaginal births, but sometimes complications arise and a woman may undergo a [cesarean section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesarean_section).

During the time immediately after birth, both the mother and the [baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby) are hormonally cued to bond, the mother through the release of [oxytocin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxytocin), a hormone also released during [breastfeeding](19347033.md). Studies show that skin-to-skin contact between a mother and her newborn immediately after birth is beneficial for both the mother and baby. A review done by the [World Health Organization](33583.md) found that skin-to-skin contact between mothers and babies after birth reduces crying, improves mother–infant interaction, and helps mothers to breastfeed successfully. They recommend that [neonates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neonates) be allowed to bond with the mother during their first two hours after birth, the period that they tend to be more alert than in the following hours of early life.[^cite_note-94]

In the ideal [childbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childbirth), labour begins on its own when a woman is "at term".[^cite_note-ACOGfive-2-16] Events before completion of 37 weeks are considered preterm.[^cite_note-preterm_definition-95] [Preterm birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preterm_birth) is associated with a range of complications and should be avoided if possible.[^cite_note-SaigalDoyle2008-97]

Sometimes if a woman's [water breaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupture_of_membranes) or she has [contractions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterine_contraction) before 39 weeks, birth is unavoidable.[^cite_note-term_definition-96] However, spontaneous birth after 37 weeks is considered term and is not associated with the same risks of a preterm birth.[^cite_note-Williams-58] Planned birth before 39 weeks by [caesarean section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarean_section) or [labour induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_induction), although "at term", results in an increased risk of complications.[^cite_note-ACOGfive-1-98] This is from factors including [underdeveloped lungs of newborns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transient_tachypnea_of_the_newborn), infection due to underdeveloped immune system, feeding problems due to underdeveloped brain, and [jaundice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaundice) from underdeveloped liver.[^cite_note-npr_July_2011-99]

Babies born between 39 and 41 weeks' gestation have better outcomes than babies born either before or after this range.[^cite_note-term_definition-96] This special time period is called "full term".[^cite_note-term_definition-96] Whenever possible, waiting for labour to begin on its own in this time period is best for the health of the mother and baby.[^cite_note-ACOGfive-2-16] The decision to perform an induction must be made after weighing the risks and benefits, but is safer after 39 weeks.[^cite_note-ACOGfive-2-16]

Events after 42 weeks are considered [postterm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postterm_pregnancy).[^cite_note-term_definition-96] When a pregnancy exceeds 42 weeks, the risk of complications for both the woman and the fetus increases significantly.[^cite_note-UpToDate-100][^cite_note-101] Therefore, in an otherwise uncomplicated pregnancy, obstetricians usually prefer to induce labour at some stage between 41 and 42 weeks.[^cite_note-102]

### Postnatal period

Main article: [Postpartum period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postpartum_period)

The [postpartum period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postpartum_period) also referred to as the *puerperium*, is the postnatal period that begins immediately after delivery and extends for about six weeks.[^cite_note-Williams-58] During this period, the mother's body begins the return to pre-pregnancy conditions that includes changes in hormone levels and uterus size.[^cite_note-Williams-58]

### Prenatal care

Main articles: [Prenatal care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_care) and [pre-conception counseling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-conception_counseling)

[Pre-conception counseling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-conception_counseling) is care that is provided to a woman or couple to discuss conception, pregnancy, current health issues and recommendations for the period before pregnancy.[^cite_note-Lyons-105]

[Prenatal medical care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_care) is the medical and nursing care recommended for women during pregnancy, time intervals and exact goals of each visit differ by country.[^cite_note-106] Women who are high risk have better outcomes if they are seen regularly and frequently by a medical professional than women who are low risk.[^cite_note-107] A woman can be labeled as high risk for different reasons including previous complications in pregnancy, complications in the current pregnancy, current medical diseases, or social issues.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]

The aim of good prenatal care is prevention, early identification, and treatment of any medical complications.[^cite_note-110] A basic prenatal visit consists of measurement of blood pressure, [fundal height](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundal_height), weight and fetal heart rate, checking for symptoms of labour, and guidance for what to expect next.[^cite_note-Lyons-105] Healthcare providers may [screen](3211372.md) for [domestic violence during pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_violence_and_pregnancy), particularly in regards to [reproductive coercion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_coercion).[^cite_note-111]

### Nutrition

Main article: [Nutrition and pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutrition_and_pregnancy)

[Nutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutrition) during pregnancy is important to ensure healthy growth of the fetus.[^cite_note-Handbook-15] Nutrition during pregnancy is different from the non-pregnant state.[^cite_note-Handbook-15] There are increased energy requirements and specific micronutrient requirements.[^cite_note-Handbook-15] Women benefit from education to encourage a balanced energy and protein intake during pregnancy.[^cite_note-112] Some women may need professional medical advice if their diet is affected by medical conditions, food allergies, or specific religious or ethical beliefs.[^cite_note-113] Further studies are needed to access the effect of dietary advice to prevent [gestational diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_diabetes), although low quality evidence suggests some benefit.[^cite_note-114] Adequate periconceptional (time before and right after conception) [folic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folic_acid) (also called folate or Vitamin B<sub>9</sub>) intake has been shown to decrease the risk of fetal neural tube defects, such as [spina bifida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spina_bifida).[^cite_note-pmid16303691-115] L-methylfolate, the bioavailable form of folate is also considered acceptable to take. L-methylfolate is best used by the 40% to 60% of the population with genetic polymorphisms that reduce or impair conversion of folic acid into its active form.[^cite_note-116] The neural tube develops during the first 28 days of pregnancy, a urine pregnancy test is not usually positive until 14 days post-conception, explaining the necessity to guarantee adequate folate intake before conception.[^cite_note-Cole-57][^cite_note-pmid11015508-117] Folate is abundant in [green leafy vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_vegetables), [legumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legume), and [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus).[^cite_note-118] In the United States and Canada, most wheat products (flour, noodles) are fortified with folic acid.[^cite_note-pmid18185493-119]

Weight gain during pregnancy

Measurement of the belly being performed by a pregnant woman during her pregnancy

The amount of healthy weight gain during a pregnancy varies.[^cite_note-AHRQ-weight-120] Weight gain is related to the weight of the baby, the placenta, extra circulatory fluid, larger tissues, and fat and protein stores.[^cite_note-Handbook-15] Most needed weight gain occurs later in pregnancy.[^cite_note-IQWiG-Weight-121]

The [Institute of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Medicine) recommends an overall pregnancy weight gain for those of normal weight ([body mass index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_mass_index) of 18.5–24.9), of 11.3–15.9 kg (25–35 pounds) having a singleton pregnancy.[^cite_note-122] Women who are underweight (BMI of less than 18.5), should gain between 12.7 and 18 kg (28–40 lb), while those who are [overweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overweight) (BMI of 25–29.9) are advised to gain between 6.8 and 11.3 kg (15–25 lb) and those who are [obese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obese) (BMI ≥ 30) should gain between 5–9 kg (11–20 lb).[^cite_note-pmid23262962-123] These values reference the expectations for a term pregnancy.

During pregnancy, insufficient or excessive weight gain can compromise the health of the mother and fetus.[^cite_note-IQWiG-Weight-121] The most effective intervention for weight gain in underweight women is not clear.[^cite_note-IQWiG-Weight-121] Being or becoming overweight in pregnancy increases the risk of complications for mother and fetus, including [cesarean section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarean_section), [gestational hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_hypertension), [pre-eclampsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-eclampsia), [macrosomia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrosomia) and [shoulder dystocia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoulder_dystocia).[^cite_note-AHRQ-weight-120] Excessive weight gain can make losing weight after the pregnancy difficult.[^cite_note-AHRQ-weight-120][^cite_note-NIHR-weight-124] Some of these complications are risk factors for [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke).[^cite_note-Bushnell-125]

Around 50% of women of childbearing age in developed countries like the United Kingdom are overweight or obese before pregnancy.[^cite_note-NIHR-weight-124] Diet modification is the most effective way to reduce weight gain and associated risks in pregnancy.[^cite_note-NIHR-weight-124]

### Medication

Main article: [Pharmaceutical drugs in pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical_drugs_in_pregnancy)

Drugs used during pregnancy can have temporary or permanent effects on the fetus.[^cite_note-Briggs-126] Anything (including drugs) that can cause permanent deformities in the fetus are labeled as [teratogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teratology).[^cite_note-127] In the U.S., drugs were classified into categories A, B, C, D and X based on the [Food and Drug Administration](11632.md) (FDA) rating system to provide therapeutic guidance based on potential benefits and fetal risks.[^cite_note-128] Drugs, including some [multivitamins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multivitamins), that have demonstrated no fetal risks after controlled studies in humans are classified as Category A.[^cite_note-Briggs-126] On the other hand, drugs like [thalidomide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thalidomide) with proven fetal risks that outweigh all benefits are classified as Category X.[^cite_note-Briggs-126]

### Recreational drugs

See also: [Neonatal withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neonatal_withdrawal)

The use of [recreational drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_drugs) in pregnancy can cause various [pregnancy complications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_complication).[^cite_note-Williams-58]

- [Alcoholic drinks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_drinks) consumed during pregnancy can cause one or more [fetal alcohol spectrum disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetal_alcohol_spectrum_disorder).

  [^cite_note-Williams-58]

  &#x20;According to the [CDC](6811.md), there is no known safe amount of alcohol during pregnancy and no safe time to drink during pregnancy, including before a woman knows that she is pregnant.

  [^cite_note-129]

- [Tobacco smoking during pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_smoking_and_pregnancy) can cause a wide range of behavioral, neurological, and physical difficulties.

  [^cite_note-Hackshaw-130]

  &#x20;Smoking during pregnancy causes twice the risk of [premature rupture of membranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premature_rupture_of_membranes), [placental abruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placental_abruption) and [placenta previa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta_previa).

  [^cite_note-CDC_preventing-131]

  &#x20;Smoking is associated with 30% higher odds of preterm birth.

  [^cite_note-CDC_tobacco_use-132]

- [Prenatal cocaine exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_cocaine_exposure) is associated with [premature birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preterm_birth), [birth defects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_defect) and [attention deficit disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention_deficit_disorder).

  [^cite_note-Williams-58]

- [Prenatal methamphetamine exposure](11038318.md#Methamphetamine_babies) can cause premature birth and [congenital abnormalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congenital_abnormalities).

  [^cite_note-Fact-133]

  &#x20;Short-term neonatal outcomes in [methamphetamine babies](11038318.md#Methamphetamine_babies) show small deficits in infant neurobehavioral function and growth restriction.

  [^cite_note-Grotta-134]

  &#x20;Long-term effects in terms of impaired brain development may also be caused by [methamphetamine](11038318.md) use.

  [^cite_note-Fact-133]

- [Cannabis in pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_in_pregnancy) has been shown to be [teratogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teratogenic) in large doses in animals, but has not shown any teratogenic effects in humans.

  [^cite_note-Williams-58]

### Exposure to toxins

Further information: [Environmental toxicants and fetal development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_toxicants_and_fetal_development)

A video describing research on [N95 respirator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N95_respirator) use during advanced pregnancy

Intrauterine exposure to [environmental toxins in pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_toxins_in_pregnancy) has the potential to cause adverse effects on [prenatal development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_development), and to cause [pregnancy complications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complications_of_pregnancy).[^cite_note-Williams-58] Air pollution has been associated with low birth weight infants.[^cite_note-135] Conditions of particular severity in pregnancy include [mercury poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_poisoning) and [lead poisoning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_poisoning).[^cite_note-Williams-58] To minimize exposure to environmental toxins, the American College of Nurse-Midwives recommends: checking whether the home has [lead paint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_paint), washing all fresh [fruits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit) and [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable) thoroughly and buying [organic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_food) produce, and avoiding cleaning products labeled "toxic" or any product with a warning on the label.[^cite_note-136]

Pregnant women can also be exposed to [toxins in the workplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_hazard), including airborne particles. The effects of wearing an [N95 filtering facepiece respirator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N95_respirator) are similar for pregnant women as for non-pregnant women, and wearing a respirator for one hour does not affect the fetal heart rate.[^cite_note-137]

### Death by violence

Pregnant women or those who have recently given birth in the U.S. are [more likely to be murdered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_pregnant_women) than to die from obstetric causes. These homicides are a combination of intimate partner violence and firearms. Health authorities have called the violence "a health emergency for pregnant women", but say that pregnancy-related homicides are preventable if healthcare providers identify those women at risk and offer assistance to them.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140]

### Sexual activity

Main article: [Sexual activity during pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_activity_during_pregnancy)

Most women can continue to engage in sexual activity, including [sexual intercourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_intercourse), throughout pregnancy.[^cite_note-Prenatal_Care_chapter-141] Research suggests that during pregnancy both sexual desire and frequency of sexual relations decrease during the first and third trimester, with a rise during the second trimester.[^cite_note-Bermudez-142][^cite_note-Fok-143][^cite_note-Reamy-144][^cite_note-145] Sex during pregnancy is low-risk except when the healthcare provider advises that sexual intercourse be avoided for particular medical reasons.[^cite_note-Prenatal_Care_chapter-141] For a healthy pregnant woman, there is no single safe or right way to have sex during pregnancy.[^cite_note-Prenatal_Care_chapter-141]

### Exercise



&#x20;A pregnant woman and her colleague returning from fishing, Gurara River bridge, [Kachia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachia), Nigeria

Regular [aerobic exercise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerobic_exercise) during pregnancy appears to improve (or maintain) physical fitness.[^cite_note-146] [Physical exercise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_exercise) during pregnancy appears to decrease the need for [C-section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-section)[^cite_note-147] and reduce time in labour,[^cite_note-\:2-148] and even vigorous exercise carries no significant risks to babies[^cite_note-Beetham2019-149] while providing significant health benefits to the mother. Studies show that performing light moderate intensity and strength exercises while pregnant does not harm the mother's cardiovascular system and may limit excessive weight gain.[^cite_note-150]

The American College of Sports and Medicine recommends pregnant women should participate in at least 150 minutes/week of moderate exercise.[^cite_note-151] These forms of exercise should avoid heavy lifting, hot temperatures, and high impact sports. The Clinical Practice Obstetrics Committee of Canada recommends that "All women without contraindications should be encouraged to participate in aerobic and strength-conditioning exercises as part of a healthy lifestyle during their pregnancy".[^cite_note-Davies-152] Although an upper level of safe exercise intensity has not been established, women who were regular exercisers before pregnancy and who have uncomplicated pregnancies should be able to engage in high intensity exercise programs without a higher risk of prematurity, lower birth weight, or gestational weight gain.[^cite_note-Beetham2019-149] In general, participation in a wide range of recreational activities appears to be safe, with the avoidance of those with a high risk of falling such as horseback riding or skiing or those that carry a risk of abdominal trauma, such as soccer or hockey.[^cite_note-pmid12547738-153]

[Bed rest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bed_rest), outside of research studies, is not recommended as there is potential harm and no evidence of benefit.[^cite_note-154]

#### High intensity exercise

During pregnancy, women can experience a loss of postural stability, pelvic incontinence, back pain, and fatigue, among other symptoms. Resistance training has been found to reduce pregnancy symptoms and reduce postpartum complications. Provided that women also regularly participate in low-impact training, strength training can improve pelvic girdle pain severity postpartum.[^cite_note-\:4-155] When incorporating exercises that focus on pelvic muscle strength, they can help reduce pain and stress urinary incontinence.[^cite_note-\:4-155]

Engaging in regular exercise and physical activity has been shown to be beneficial during pregnancy. Acute bouts of [high intensity interval training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_intensity_interval_training) can help decrease the risks of health complications associated with pregnancy, maintain a healthy body fat percentage during pregnancy, as well as improve overall well-being.[^cite_note-156] Pregnant women who participated in high intensity interval training have been shown to undergo physical improvements in body composition after intervention as well as show general improvement in cardiorespiratory fitness and exercise tolerance.[^cite_note-\:0-157] Taking part in this style of exercise, similarly to moderate intensity continuous training, has also been shown to improve glycemic response and insulin sensitivity.[^cite_note-158] There are specific concerns to be avoided with exercise during pregnancy such as overheating, fall-risk, and remaining in a supine position for an extended period of time. Inexperienced individuals new to high-intensity interval training could potentially increase their risk for negative conditions associated with hypertension, such as pre-eclampsia.[^cite_note-159]

### Sleep

Main article: [Pregnancy and sleep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_and_sleep)

It has been suggested that [shift work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shift_work) and exposure to bright light at night should be avoided at least during the last trimester of pregnancy to decrease the risk of psychological and behavioral problems in the newborn.[^cite_note-ReiterTan2013-160]

### Stress

Heightened maternal [stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_\(biology\)) during pregnancy has been consistently associated with alterations in fetal and infant brain development and an increased risk for later mental health problems ("[psychopathology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychopathology)"). Prenatal adversity (for example elevated maternal stress and [depressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(mood\)) and [anxiety symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety) during pregnancy) has been consistently associated with an increased risk for psychopathology in children. Maternal stress during pregnancy is therefore thought to influence fetal brain development and thereby contribute to increased vulnerability to later psychopathology.[^cite_note-\:6-161]

Research demonstrates that prenatal stress can fundamentally alter the brain's physical architecture (smaller overall volume, altered cortical thinning, functional connectivity, ...), leading to reduced volume and weakened connectivity in areas critical for emotion processing and regulation, as well as learning and memory. In contrast, also caregiving-focused interventions and higher natural caregiving quality have been associated with a positive impact on the brain structure. [Animal studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_studies) further show that enhanced maternal care or enriched environments can reverse the effects of prenatal adversity at the cellular level, supporting the biological plausibility of similar processes in humans.[^cite_note-\:6-161] Children of women who experienced high stress levels during pregnancy are slightly more likely to show [externalizing behavioral problems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Externalizing_behavior), such as impulsivity. These behavioral effects appear to be most pronounced during early childhood.[^cite_note-\:0-157]

Importantly, prenatal stress does not inevitable cause mental health problems. Not all children who are exposed to prenatal adversity develop psychiatric disorders. Evidence from both human and animal studies suggests that high-quality caregiving, cognitive and language stimulation, social support, and higher [socioeconomic status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socioeconomic_status) can act as protective or supportive factors. Improving outcomes for children exposed to prenatal stress mainly involves strengthening the early postnatal environment rather than attempting to eliminate all stress during pregnancy. Supportive environments in early postnatal life may promote brain development and help normalize developmental trajectories that were altered by prenatal stress, highlighting caregiving quality, cognitive and language input, social support, and socioeconomic stability as key factors. High-quality caregiving is consistently identified as especially important, with studies showing that associations between prenatal stress and adverse outcomes are not observed when maternal sensitivity is high, and that sensitive caregiving can reduce the impact of prenatal stress on neurocognitive and neuroendocrine pathways linked to later psychopathology. Beyond the parent–child relationship, broader support systems also matter: higher levels of social support and socioeconomic resources are associated with more adaptive development and can, in some contexts, reduce the negative effects of preterm birth or low birth weight on neurocognitive outcomes. Overall, promoting warm, responsive caregiving and ensuring families have adequate social and material support during the early years can meaningfully improve children's developmental trajectories, even when prenatal stress has occurred.[^cite_note-\:6-161]

Extreme stress caused by events such as natural disasters, genocide, slavery, forced family separation, or long-term exposure to war is thought to create widespread trauma that changes how people function physically, psychologically, and socially, and these changes may be passed on [across generations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgenerational_trauma). Research suggests that recognizing severe maternal stress early, during or after such events, and providing structured psychological support such as [psychological first aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_first_aid) or [cognitive behavioural therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_behavioral_therapy) can lower long-term risks for child development. When stress exposure is particularly high, ongoing monitoring, early developmental support, and sensitive, responsive caregiving are especially important to prevent lasting negative effects.[^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163]

### Dental care

See also: [Prenatal dental care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_dental_care)

The increased levels of [progesterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progesterone) and [estrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrogen) during pregnancy make [gingivitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gingivitis) more likely; the [gums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gums) become edematous, red in colour, and tend to bleed.[^cite_note-164] Also a [pyogenic granuloma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyogenic_granuloma) or "pregnancy tumor", is commonly seen on the labial surface of the papilla. Lesions can be treated by local debridement or deep incision depending on their size, and by following adequate [oral hygiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_hygiene) measures.[^cite_note-165] There have been suggestions that severe [periodontitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodontal_disease) may increase the risk of having [preterm birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preterm_birth) and [low birth weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_birth_weight); however, a Cochrane review found insufficient evidence to determine if [periodontitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodontitis) can develop adverse birth outcomes.[^cite_note-pmid28605006-166]

### Flying

In low risk pregnancies, most health care providers approve flying until about 36 weeks of gestational age.[^cite_note-167] Most airlines allow pregnant women to fly short distances at less than 36 weeks, and long distances at less than 32 weeks.[^cite_note-Jarvis-168] Many airlines require a doctor's note that approves flying, especially at over 28 weeks.[^cite_note-Jarvis-168] During flights, the risk of [deep vein thrombosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_vein_thrombosis) is decreased by getting up and walking occasionally, as well as by avoiding dehydration. The exposure to cosmic radiation is negligible for most travelers. For pregnant women, even the longest intercontinental flight would expose them less than 15% of both the [NCRPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_on_Radiation_Protection_and_Measurements) and [ICRP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Commission_on_Radiological_Protection) limit.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-Jarvis-168] [Full body scanners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_body_scanner) use non-ionizing radiation that does not penetrate the body for more than 1 mm, and are believed not to pose a risk in pregnancy.

### Pregnancy classes and birth plan

Main article: [Birthing classes and birth plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birthing_classes_and_birth_plans)

To prepare for the birth of the baby, health care providers recommend that parents attend antenatal classes during the third trimester of pregnancy. Classes include information about the process of labour and birth and the various kinds of births, including both vaginal and [caesarean delivery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarean_delivery), the use of forceps, and other interventions that may be needed to safely deliver the infant. Types of pain relief, including relaxation techniques, are discussed. Partners or others who may plan to support a woman during her labour and delivery learn how to assist in the birth.

It is also suggested that a birth plan be written at this time. A birth plan is a written statement that outlines the desires of the mother during labour and delivery of the baby. Discussing the birth plan with the midwife or other care provider gives parents a chance to ask questions and learn more about the process of labour.[^cite_note-170]

In 1991 the [WHO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO) launched the [Baby-Friendly Hospital Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby-Friendly_Hospital_Initiative), a global program that recognizes birthing centers and hospitals that offer optimal levels of care for giving birth. Facilities that have been certified as "Baby Friendly" accept visits from expecting parents to familiarize them with the facility and the staff.[^cite_note-171]

## Complications and diseases

Main article: [Complications of pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complications_of_pregnancy)

See also: [High-risk pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-risk_pregnancy)

Each year, ill health as a result of pregnancy is experienced (sometimes permanently) by more than 20 million women around the world.[^cite_note-WHO_Reproductive_Health_and_Research_Publications:_Making_Pregnancy_Safer-172] In 2016, complications of pregnancy resulted in 230,600 deaths down from 377,000 deaths in 1990.[^cite_note-GBD2016-9] Common causes include [bleeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_bleeding) (72,000), [infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postpartum_infections) (20,000), [hypertensive diseases of pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertensive_disease_of_pregnancy) (32,000), [obstructed labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstructed_labor) (10,000), and [pregnancy with abortive outcome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Pregnancy_with_abortive_outcome) (20,000), which includes [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage), [abortion](765.md), and [ectopic pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectopic_pregnancy).[^cite_note-GBD2016-9]

Signs are red plaques, papules, and itchiness around the belly button that then spreads all over the body except for the inside of hands and face.

### Miscarriage and stillbirth

Main articles: [Miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage) and [Stillbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stillbirth)

See also: [Miscarriage and grief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage_and_grief)

Miscarriage is the most common complication of early pregnancy. It is defined as the loss of an embryo or fetus before it is able to survive independently. The most common symptom of miscarriage is vaginal bleeding with or without pain. The miscarriage may be evidenced by a clot-like material passing through and out of the vagina.[^cite_note-178] About 80% of miscarriages occur in the first 12 weeks of pregnancy. The underlying cause in about half of cases involves chromosomal abnormalities.[^cite_note-179]

Stillbirth is defined as fetal death after 20 or 28 weeks of pregnancy, depending on the source. It results in a baby born without signs of life. Each year about 21,000 babies are stillborn in the U.S.[^cite_note-180] Sadness, anxiety, and guilt may occur after a miscarriage or a stillbirth. Emotional support may help with processing the loss.[^cite_note-181] Fathers may experience grief over the loss as well. A large study found that there is a need to increase the accessibility of support services available for fathers.[^cite_note-182]

### Diseases in pregnancy

Further information: [Pre-existing disease in pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-existing_disease_in_pregnancy)

A pregnant woman may have a [pre-existing disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-existing_disease_in_pregnancy), which is not directly caused by the pregnancy, but may cause [complications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complications_of_pregnancy) to develop that include a potential risk to the pregnancy; or a disease may develop during pregnancy.

- [Diabetes mellitus and pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_mellitus_and_pregnancy) deals with the interactions of [diabetes mellitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diabetes_mellitus) (not restricted to [gestational diabetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestational_diabetes)) and pregnancy. Risks for the child include miscarriage, growth restriction, growth acceleration, [large for gestational age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Large_for_gestational_age) (macrosomia), [polyhydramnios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyhydramnios) (too much [amniotic fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amniotic_fluid)), and birth defects.

- [Thyroid disease in pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid_disease_in_pregnancy) can, if uncorrected, cause adverse effects on fetal and maternal well-being. The deleterious effects of thyroid dysfunction can also extend beyond pregnancy and delivery to affect [neurointellectual development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_milestone) in the early life of the child. Demand for thyroid hormones is increased during pregnancy, which may cause a previously unnoticed thyroid disorder to worsen.

- Untreated [celiac disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celiac_disease) can cause a [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage), [intrauterine growth restriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intrauterine_growth_restriction), [small for gestational age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_for_gestational_age), [low birthweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_birthweight) and [preterm birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preterm_birth). Often [reproductive disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_system_disease) are the only manifestation of undiagnosed celiac disease and most cases are not recognized. Complications or failures of pregnancy cannot be explained simply by [malabsorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malabsorption), but by the [autoimmune response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoimmmune_response) elicited by the exposure to [gluten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluten), which causes damage to the [placenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta). A [gluten-free diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluten-free_diet) avoids or reduces the risk of developing reproductive disorders in pregnant women with celiac disease.

  [^cite_note-TersigniCastellani2014-183]

  [^cite_note-SacconeBerghella2015-184]

  &#x20;Also, pregnancy can be a trigger for the development of celiac disease in [genetically susceptible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_susceptibility) women who are consuming gluten.

  [^cite_note-Glutengovca-185]

- [Lupus in pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lupus_and_pregnancy) confers an increased rate of fetal death *in utero,* miscarriage, and of [neonatal lupus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neonatal_lupus).

- [Hypercoagulability in pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercoagulability_in_pregnancy) is the propensity of pregnant women to develop [thrombosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrombosis) (blood clots). Pregnancy itself is a factor of [hypercoagulability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercoagulability) (pregnancy-induced hypercoagulability), as a physiologically adaptive mechanism to prevent [postpartum bleeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postpartum_hemorrhage).

  [^cite_note-gresele-186]

  &#x20;However, in combination with an underlying hypercoagulable state, the risk of thrombosis or embolism may become substantial.

  [^cite_note-gresele-186]

## Birth control and abortion

### Birth control and education

Main article: [Birth control](18978770.md)

[Family planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning), as well as the availability and use of [contraception](18978770.md), along with increased [comprehensive sex education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_sex_education), has enabled many to prevent pregnancies when they are not desired. Schemes and funding to support education and the means to prevent pregnancies when they are not intended have been instrumental and are part of the third of the [Sustainable Development Goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Development_Goals) (SDGs) advanced by the [United Nations](31769.md).[^cite_note-Population_Division-187]

### Abortion

Main article: [Abortion](765.md)

An abortion is the termination of an embryo or fetus via medical method. It is usually done within the first trimester, sometimes in the second, and rarely in the third. Reasons for [pregnancies being undesired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unintended_pregnancy) are broad.[^cite_note-Zdanowicz_2019-188] Many jurisdictions restrict or prohibit abortion, with [rape](7980471.md) being the most legally permissible exception.[^cite_note-Center_for_Reproductive_Rights_2022-189]

### Assisted reproductive technology

Main article: [Assisted reproductive technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assisted_reproductive_technology)

Modern reproductive medicine offers many forms of assisted reproductive technology for couples who stay childless against their will, such as [fertility medication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_medication), [artificial insemination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_insemination), [*in vitro* fertilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_vitro_fertilization) and [surrogacy](1514981.md).

## Society and culture

### Legal protections

Further information: [Reproductive rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_rights) and [Reproductive justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reproductive_justice)

Many countries have various legal regulations in place to protect pregnant women and their children. Many countries have laws against [pregnancy discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_discrimination).[^cite_note-190]

The [Maternity Protection Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternity_Protection_Convention,_2000) ensures that pregnant women are exempt from activities such as night shifts or carrying heavy stocks. [Maternity leave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternity_leave) typically provides paid leave from work during roughly the last trimester of pregnancy and for some time after birth. Notable extreme cases include Norway (8 months with full pay) and the United States (no paid leave at all except in some states).

In the United States, some actions that result in miscarriage or stillbirth, such as beating a pregnant woman, are considered crimes. One law that does so is the federal [Unborn Victims of Violence Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unborn_Victims_of_Violence_Act). In 2014, the American state of [Tennessee](30395.md) passed a law which allows prosecutors to charge a woman with criminal assault if she uses illegal drugs during her pregnancy and her fetus or newborn is harmed as a result.[^cite_note-191]

However, protections are not universal. In [Singapore](27318.md), the *Employment of Foreign Manpower Act* forbids current and former [work permit](14666962.md) holders from becoming pregnant or giving birth in Singapore without prior permission.[^cite_note-yale-192][^cite_note-193] Violation of the Act is punishable by a fine of up to [S\$](476174.md)10,000 (US\$7300) and [deportation](412990.md),[^cite_note-yale-192][^cite_note-194] and until 2010, their employers would lose their \$5,000 security bond.[^cite_note-195]

### Racial disparities

There are significant racial imbalances in pregnancy and neonatal care systems.[^cite_note-Pereira-196] Midwifery guidance, treatment, and care have been related to better birth outcomes. Diminishing racial inequities in health is an increasingly large public health challenge in the United States. Despite the fact that average rates have decreased, data on neonatal mortality demonstrates that racial disparities have persisted and grown. The death rate for African American babies is nearly double that of white neonates. According to studies, [congenital defects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_defects), [SIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudden_infant_death_syndrome), [preterm birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preterm_birth), and [low birth weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_birth_weight) are all more common among African American babies.[^cite_note-Guerra-Reyes-197]

### Transgender people

Main article: [Transgender pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_pregnancy)

Transgender people have experienced significant advances in societal acceptance in recent years leaving many health professionals unprepared to provide quality care. A 2015 report suggests that "numbers of transgender individuals who are seeking family planning, fertility, and pregnancy services could certainly be quite large". Regardless of prior [hormone replacement therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender-affirming_hormone_therapy) treatments, the progression of pregnancy and birthing procedures for [transgender people who carry pregnancies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_pregnancy) are typically the same as those of [cisgender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisgender) women.[^cite_note-198] However, transgender people may be subjected to discrimination, which can include a variety of negative social, emotional, and medical experiences, as pregnancy is regarded as an exclusively female activity. According to a study by the [American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_College_of_Obstetricians_and_Gynecologists), there is a lack of awareness, services, and medical assistance available to pregnant trans men.[^cite_note-199]

### Culture

In most cultures, pregnant women have a special status in society and receive particularly gentle care.[^cite_note-isbn_9780759110441-200] At the same time, they are subject to expectations that may exert great psychological pressure, such as having to produce a son and heir. In many traditional societies, pregnancy must be preceded by marriage, on pain of ostracism of mother and [(illegitimate) child](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legitimacy_\(family_law\)).

Overall, pregnancy is accompanied by numerous customs that are often subject to ethnological research, often rooted in [traditional medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_medicine) or religion. The [baby shower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_shower) is an example of a modern custom. Contrary to [common misconception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_common_misconceptions), women historically in the [United States](3434750.md) were not expected to seclude themselves during pregnancy, as was popularized by *[Gone With the Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gone_with_the_Wind_\(film\))*.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202]

Pregnancy is an important topic in [sociology of the family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociology_of_the_family). The prospective child may preliminarily be placed into numerous [social roles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_role). The parents' relationship and the relation between parents and their surroundings are also affected.

A [belly cast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belly_cast) may be made during pregnancy as a keepsake.

#### Arts

Main article: [Pregnancy in art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_in_art)

Images of pregnant women, especially small [figurines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figurine), were made in traditional cultures in many places and periods, though it is rarely one of the most common types of image. These include ceramic figures from some [Pre-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian) cultures, and a few figures from most of the ancient Mediterranean cultures. Many of these seem to be connected with [fertility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_in_art). Identifying whether such figures are actually meant to show pregnancy is often a problem, as well as understanding their role in the culture concerned.

Among the oldest surviving examples of the depiction of pregnancy are prehistoric figurines found across much of [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) and collectively known as [Venus figurines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_figurines). Some of these appear to be pregnant.

Due to the important role of the [Mother of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_of_God) in [Christianity](5211.md), the Western visual arts have a long tradition of depictions of pregnancy, especially in the biblical scene of the [Visitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visitation_\(Christianity\)), and devotional images called a *[Madonna del Parto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_del_Parto)*.[^cite_note-203]



[The Visitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visitation_\(Christianity\)): [Mary](73513.md), pregnant with [Jesus](1095706.md), visiting pregnant [Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_\(biblical_figure\)), depicted as a statue at the [Church of the Visitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Visitation) in [Ein Karem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ein_Karem), Israel

The unhappy scene usually called *Diana and Callisto*, showing the moment of discovery of [Callisto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callisto_\(mythology\))'s forbidden pregnancy, is sometimes painted from the Renaissance onwards. Gradually, portraits of pregnant women began to appear, with a particular fashion for "pregnancy portraits" in elite portraiture of the years around 1600.

Pregnancy, and especially pregnancy of unmarried women, is also an important motif in literature. Notable examples include [Thomas Hardy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Hardy)'s 1891 novel *[Tess of the d'Urbervilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tess_of_the_d'Urbervilles)* and Goethe's 1808 play *[Faust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faust:_The_First_Part_of_the_Tragedy)*.
