[Criminal organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_organization) have been prevalent in [Italy](14532.md), especially in [the southern part of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Italy), for centuries and have affected the social and economic life of many Italian regions.[^cite_note-1] There are multiple major native mafia-like organizations that are heavily active in Italy. The most powerful of these organizations are the [Camorra](44945.md) from [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania), the ['Ndrangheta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Ndrangheta) from [Calabria](44772.md) and the [Cosa Nostra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosa_Nostra) from [Sicily](27619.md).

In addition to these three long-established organizations, there are also other significantly active organized crime syndicates in Italy that were founded in the 20th century: the [Sacra Corona Unita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacra_Corona_Unita), the [Società foggiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Societ%C3%A0_foggiana), and the [Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari) crime groups from [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia); the [Stidda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stidda) from Sicily; and the [Sinti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinti) crime groups, such as the [Casamonica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamonica_clan), the Spada and the Fasciani clan from [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

Four other Italian organized crime groups, namely the [Banda della Magliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_della_Magliana) of [Rome](25458.md), the [Mala del Brenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mala_del_Brenta) of [Veneto](43780.md), and the [Banda della Comasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_della_Comasina) and [Turatello Crew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Turatello), both based in [Milan](36511.md), held considerable influence at the height of their power but are now severely weakened by Italian law enforcement or even considered defunct or inactive. One other group, the [Basilischi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilischi) of [Basilicata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilicata) region, is currently active but is considered to have mostly fallen under the influence of the larger and more powerful 'Ndrangheta. The latest creation of Italian organized crime, [Mafia Capitale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia_Capitale) (which was partially a successor or continuation of Banda della Magliana, involving many former Banda della Magliana members and associates), was mostly disbanded by the police in 2014.[^cite_note-Rai_-_Radiotelevisione_Italiana-4]

The best-known Italian organized crime (IOC) group is the Mafia or Sicilian Mafia (referred to as *Cosa Nostra* by members). As the original group named "[Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia)", the Sicilian Mafia is the basis for the current colloquial usage of the term to refer to organized crime groups. It along with the Neapolitan Camorra and the Calabrian 'Ndrangheta are active throughout Italy, having presence also in other countries.[^cite_note-5]

Italian organized crime groups receipts have been estimated to reach 7–9% of Italy's GDP.[^cite_note-nytimes.com-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] A 2009 report identified 610 [*comuni*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comune) which have a strong Mafia presence, where 13 million [Italians](385155.md) live and 14.6% of the Italian GDP is produced.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] However, despite the ubiquity of organized crime in much of the country, Italy has only the 47th highest murder rate, at 0.013 per 1,000 people,[^cite_note-NationMaster.com-11] compared to 61 countries, and the 43rd highest number of rapes per 1,000 people, compared to 64 countries in the world, all relatively low figures among developed countries.

## Largest crime groups

### Sicilian Mafia

Main article: [Sicilian Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia)



1900 map of Mafia presence in Sicily. Towns with Mafia activity are marked with red dots, while towns marked with black dots indicate the absence of Mafia activity.

The word *mafia* originated in Sicily. The [Sicilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_language) noun *mafiusu* (in Italian: *mafioso*) roughly translates to mean "[swagger](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/swagger#English)", but can also be translated as "boldness, [bravado](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bravado)". In reference to a man, *mafiusu* in 19th-century Sicily was ambiguous, signifying a bully, arrogant but also fearless, enterprising and proud, according to scholar [Diego Gambetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Gambetta).[^cite_note-mafiusu-12] In reference to a woman, however, the feminine-form, *"mafiusa"*, means a beautiful or attractive female. The Sicilian word *mafie* refers to the caves near [Trapani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trapani) and [Marsala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsala),[^cite_note-Gambetta1996-13] which were often used as hiding places for refugees and criminals.

The genesis of the [Sicilian Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia) is hard to trace because mafiosi are very secretive and do not keep historical records of their own. They have been known to spread deliberate lies about their past and sometimes come to believe in their own myths.[^cite_note-14] The Mafia's genesis began in the 19th century as the product of Sicily's transition from [feudalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudalism) to [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) as well as its unification with mainland Italy. Under feudalism, the [nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_nobility) owned most of the land and enforced the law through their [private armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_army) and [manorial courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manorialism). After 1812, the [feudal barons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feudal_baron) steadily sold off or rented their lands to private citizens. [Primogeniture](136535.md) was abolished, land could no longer be seized to settle debts, and one fifth of the land became private property of the peasants.[^cite_note-sardell-15] After Italy annexed Sicily in 1860, it redistributed a large share of public and church land to private citizens. The result was a huge increase in the number of [landowners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_tenure) – from 2,000 in 1812 to 20,000 by 1861.[^cite_note-bandiera-16]

The early Mafia was deeply involved with citrus growers and cattle ranchers, as these industries were particularly vulnerable to thieves and vandals and thus badly needed protection. Citrus plantations had a fragile production system that made them quite vulnerable to sabotage.[^cite_note-Dickie2007p39-17] Likewise, cattle are very easy to steal. The Mafia was often more effective than the police at recovering stolen cattle; in the 1920s, it was noted that the Mafia's success rate at recovering stolen cattle was 95%, whereas the police managed only 10%.[^cite_note-18]

In the 1950s, Sicily experienced a substantial construction boom. Taking advantage of the opportunity, the Sicilian Mafia gained control of the building contracts and made millions of dollars.[^cite_note-19] It participated in the growing business of large-scale heroin trafficking, both in Italy and Europe and in US-connected trafficking; a famous example of this are the [French Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Connection) smuggling with Corsican criminals and the Italian-American Mafia.

The Sicilian Mafia has evolved into an international [organized crime](22625.md) group. It specializes in heroin trafficking, [political corruption](66220.md), and military [arms trafficking](42210.md) and is the most powerful and most active Italian [organized crime](22625.md) group in the United States, with estimates of more than 2,500 affiliates located there.[^cite_note-fbi-20] The Sicilian Mafia is also known to engage in arson, frauds, counterfeiting, and other racketeering crimes. It is estimated to have 3,500–4,000 core members with 100 clans, with around 50 in the city of Palermo alone.[^cite_note-21]

The Cosa Nostra has had influence in 'legitimate' power too, particularly under the corrupt [Christian Democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democracy_\(Italy\)) governments, from between the 1950s to the early 1990s. Its reach included many prominent lawyers, financiers, and professionals; it has also exerted power by bribing or pressuring politicians, judges, and administrators. It has lost influence on the heels of the [Maxi Trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxi_Trial), the campaign by magistrates [Giovanni Falcone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Falcone) and [Paolo Borsellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Borsellino) and other actions against corrupt politicians and judges; however it retains some influence.



[Salvatore Riina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Riina)

The Sicilian Mafia became infamous for aggressive assaults on Italian law enforcement officials during the reign of [Salvatore Riina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Riina), also known as "Toto Riina". In Sicily the term [Excellent Cadavers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excellent_Cadavers_\(term\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) is used to distinguish the assassination of prominent government officials from the common criminals and ordinary citizens killed by the Mafia. Some of their high ranking victims include police commissioners, mayors, judges, police colonels and generals, and Parliament members.

On 23 May 1992, the Sicilian Mafia struck Italian law enforcement. At approximately 6 pm, [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_people) Magistrate [Giovanni Falcone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Falcone), his wife, and three police body guards were killed by a massive bomb. Falcone, Director of Prosecutions (roughly, District Attorney) for the court of [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) and head of the special anti-Mafia investigative squad, had become the organization's most formidable enemy. His team was moving to prepare cases against most of the Mafia leadership. The bomb made a crater 10 meters (33 feet) in diameter in the road Falcone's caravan was traveling on. This became known as the [Capaci bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capaci_bombing).

Less than two months later, on 19 July 1992, the Mafia struck Falcone's replacement, Judge [Paolo Borsellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Borsellino), also in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo), Sicily. Borsellino and five bodyguards were killed outside the apartment of Borsellino's mother when a car packed with explosives was detonated by remote control as the judge approached the front door of his mother's apartment.

In 1993, the authorities arrested Salvatore Riina, believed at the time to be the [capo di tutti capi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capo_di_tutti_capi) and responsible directly or indirectly for scores (if not hundreds) of killings. Riina's arrest came after years of investigation – which some believe was delayed by Mafia influence within the [Carabinieri](45173.md). Control of the organization then fell to [Bernardo Provenzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Provenzano) who had come to reject Riina's strategy of war against the authorities, in favor of an approach of bribery, corruption, and influence-peddling. As a consequence, the rate of Mafia killings fell sharply, but its influence continued in the international drug and slave trades, as well as locally in construction and public contracts in Sicily. Provenzano was himself captured in 2006 after being wanted for 43 years.

In July, 2013, the Italian police conducted sweeping raids targeting top mafia crime bosses. In [Ostia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostia_\(Rome\)), a coastal community near the capital, police arrested 51 suspects for alleged crimes connected with Italy's [Sicilian Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia). Allegations included extortion, murder, international drug trafficking, and illegal control of the slot machine market.[^cite_note-22]

### Camorra

Main article: [Camorra](44945.md)



[Raffaele Cutolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaele_Cutolo)

The origins of the [Camorra](44945.md) are unclear. It may date to the 17th century,[^cite_note-23] however the first official use of *camorra* as a word dates from 1735, when a royal decree authorized the establishment of eight gambling houses in Naples. The Camorra's main businesses are drug trafficking, racketeering, counterfeiting and money laundering. It is also not unusual for Camorra clans to infiltrate the politics of their respective areas.

The Camorra also specializes in [cigarette smuggling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cigarette_smuggling) and receives payoffs from other criminal groups for any cigarette traffic through Italy. In the 1970s, the Sicilian Mafia convinced the Camorra to convert their cigarette smuggling routes into drug smuggling routes. Yet not all Camorra leaders agreed, sparking a war between the two factions and resulting in the murder of almost 400 men. Those opposed to drug trafficking lost the war.

The Camorra Mafia controls the drug trade in Europe and is organized on a system of specific management principles. From the 1990s to the early 2000s, the [Di Lauro clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Di_Lauro_clan) ran the then-largest open-air market in Europe, based in [Secondigliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondigliano). The former leader of the clan, [Paolo Di Lauro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Di_Lauro), who designed the system, has been imprisoned since 2005. His organization however earned about €200 million annually, solely from the drug trafficking business. The Di Lauro clan war against the [Scissionisti di Secondigliano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scissionisti_di_Secondigliano) inspired the current Italian television series *[Gomorrah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomorrah_\(TV_series\))*.[^cite_note-econ-24]

Outside Italy, the Camorra has a strong presence in Spain. There, the organization has established a massive business operation revolving around drug trafficking and [money laundering](19390.md). They are known to reinvest their profits into the creation of hotels, nightclubs, restaurants and companies around the country.[^cite_note-25]

According to Naples public prosecutor Giovanni Melillo, during a 2023 speech of the [Antimafia Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimafia_Commission), the most powerful groups of the Camorra in the present day are the [Mazzarella clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazzarella_clan) and the [Secondigliano Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondigliano_Alliance). The latter is an alliance of the [Licciardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licciardi_clan), [Contini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contini_clan) and [Mallardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallardo_clan) clans.[^cite_note-26]

### 'Ndrangheta

Main article: ['Ndrangheta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Ndrangheta)



[Antonio Macrì](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Macr%C3%AC)

Derived from the Greek word *andragathía* (meaning courage or loyalty), the ['Ndrangheta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Ndrangheta) formed in the 1890s in [Calabria](44772.md). The 'Ndrangheta consists of 160 cells and approximately 6,000 members, although worldwide estimates put core membership at around 10,000.[^cite_note-johnhooper-27] The group specializes in political corruption and cocaine trafficking.

The 'Ndrangheta cells are loosely connected family groups based on blood relationships and marriages.

Since the 1950s, the organization's influence has spread towards Northern Italy and worldwide. According to a 2013 "Threat Assessment on Italian Organized Crime" by [Europol](525329.md) and the [Guardia di Finanza](44013914.md), the 'Ndrangheta is among the richest (in 2008 their income was around 55 billion dollars) and most powerful organized crime groups in the world.[^cite_note-Italian_Organised_Crime-28]

The 'Ndrangheta is also known to engage in cocaine (controlling up to 80% of that flowing through Europe)[^cite_note-johnhooper-27] and heroin trafficking, murder, bombings, counterfeiting, illegal gambling, frauds, thefts, labor racketeering, loan sharking, illegal immigration, and rarely some kidnapping.

## Other crime groups

### Sacra Corona Unita

Main article: [Sacra Corona Unita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacra_Corona_Unita)

The [Sacra Corona Unita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacra_Corona_Unita) (SCU), or United Sacred Crown, is a Mafia-like criminal organization from the region of [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia) (in Italian *Puglia*) in Southern Italy, and is especially active in the areas of Brindisi and Lecce and not, as people tend to believe, in the region as a whole. The SCU was founded in the late 1970s as the Nuova Grande Camorra Pugliese, based in [Foggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foggia), by the Camorra member Raffaele Cutolo, who wanted to expand his operations into Apulia. It has also been suggested that elements of this group originated from the 'Ndrangheta, but it is not known if they were breakaways from it or the result of indirect co-operation with clans of the 'Ndrangheta.

A few years after the creation of the SCU, following the downfall of Cutolo, the organization began to operate independently from the Camorra under the leadership of [Giuseppe Rogoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Rogoli?action=edit\&redlink=1). Under his leadership the SCU mixed its Apulian interests and opportunities with 'Ndrangheta and Camorra traditions. Originally preying on the region's substantial wine and olive oil industries, the group moved into fraud, gunrunning, and drug trafficking, and made alliances with international criminal organizations such as the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_mafia) and [Albanian mafias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_mafia), the Colombian drug cartels, and some Asian organizations. The Sacra Corona Unita consists of about 50 clans with approximately 2,000 core members[^cite_note-fbi-20] and specializes in smuggling cigarettes, drugs, arms, and people.

Very few SCU members have been identified in the United States, however there are some links to individuals in Illinois, Florida, and possibly New York. The Sacra Corona Unita is also reported to be involved in money laundering, extortion, and political corruption and collects payoffs from other criminal groups for landing rights on the southeast coast of Italy. This territory is a natural gateway for smuggling to and from post-communist countries such as [Croatia](5573.md), [Montenegro](20760.md), and [Albania](738.md).

With the decreasing importance of the Adriatic corridor as a smuggling route (thanks to the normalization of the Balkans area) and a series of successful police and judicial operations against it in recent years, the Sacra Corona Unita has been considered, if not actually defeated, to be reduced to a fraction of its former power, which peaked around the mid-1990s.

#### Local rivals

The internal difficulties of the SCU aided the birth of antagonistic criminal groups such as:

- Remo Lecce Libera: formed by some leading criminal figures from Lecce, who claim to be independent from any criminal group other than the 'Ndrangheta. The term Remo indicates Remo Morello, a criminal from the Salento area, killed by criminals from the Campania region because he opposed any external interference;

- Nuova Famiglia Salentina: formed in 1986 by De Matteis Pantaleo, from Lecce and stemming from the Famiglia Salentina Libera born in the early 1980s as an autonomous criminal movement in the Salento area with no links with extra-regional Mafia expressions

- Rosa dei Venti: formed in 1990 by De Tommasi in the Lecce prison, following an internal division in the SCU.

### Società foggiana

Main article: [Società foggiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Societ%C3%A0_foggiana)

The Società foggiana – also known as *Mafia Foggiana* (*Foggian mafia*) and the *Fifth Mafia*[^cite_note-urlPuglia_crimewave_points_to_emergence_of_fifth_Italian_mafia_|_World_news_|_The_Guardian-30] – is a [mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia)-type criminal organization. They are operating in a large part of the [province of Foggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Foggia), including the city of [Foggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foggia) itself, and have significantly infiltrated other Italian regions.[^cite_note-31] Currently, the group is considered one of the most brutal and bloody of all organized crime groups in Italy.[^cite_note-32] There was about one [murder](20171.md) a week, one [robbery](54849.md) a day, and an [extortion](162449.md) attempt every 48 hours in Foggia province in 2017 and 2018.[^cite_note-33] These were wrongly reported as the work of the Sacra Corona Unita (the fourth mafia) by news media, unaware of the new independent mafia in Foggia province. "But that wasn't the case. We are witnessing what should be called a fifth mafia, independent of the Sacra Corona Unita" according to Giuseppe Volpe, a prosecutor and anti-mafia head of Bari.[^cite_note-34] The Società foggiana is known to have numerous alliances with Balkans criminal groups, in particular with the [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_mafia).[^cite_note-35]

The most powerful clans inside the Società Foggiana are:

### Bari crime groups



Domenico Strisciuglio, head of the Strisciuglio clan, considered the most powerful clan operating in Bari.

Bari crime groups also known as the "Baresi clans" are organized crime groups that operates in the city of [Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari) and in the surrounding area of the [Metropolitan City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Bari). Its mainly a confederation between clans, which are dedicated to [drug trafficking](218449.md), smuggling and extortion as their primary activities. These clans were founded in the 1980s and 1990s and currently have hegemony in the illegal activities of the city of Bari and its metropolitan area, having also infiltrated in the politics of the region.[^cite_note-37]

There are three main clans in the city of Bari: the [Strisciuglio clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strisciuglio_clan) headed by the boss *Domenico Strisciuglio*, operating mainly in the northern area, the [Parisi-Palermiti clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parisi-Palermiti_clan), headed by the bosses [Savino Parisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savino_Parisi) and Eugenio Palermiti, operating mainly in the Japigia district and the [Capriati clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capriati_clan), headed by Antonio Capriati, known as *Tonino,* operating mainly in the Borgo Antico.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

Other clans present in the city are: the Lorusso clan of the Libertà, Carrassi and San Pasquale districts, the Di Cosola clan operating in the Carbonara, Ceglie del Campo and Loseto districts, the Anemolo clan operating in Poggiofranco, the Misceo clan operating in the San Paolo district, the Fiore-Risoli clan of the Poggio Franco and San Pasquale neighborhoods, the Mercante-Diomede clan operating in the Carrassi, Libertà, Poggiofranco, San Paolo and San Pasquale neighborhoods, the Velutto clan operating in Carrassi, Picone and San Pasquale, and the Rafaschieri clan operating in Madonnella.[^cite_note-41]

### Stidda

Main article: [Stidda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stidda)

La [Stidda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stidda) (Sicilian for 'star') is the name given to the Sicilian organization founded by criminals Giuseppe Croce Benvenuto and Salvatore Calafato, both of [Palma di Montechiaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palma_di_Montechiaro), in the [Agrigento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrigento) province of Sicily. The Stidda's power bases are centered in the cities of [Gela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gela) and [Favara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Favara,_Sicily), [Caltanissetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltanissetta) and Agrigento provinces. The organization's groups and activities have flourished in the cities of Agrigento, [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania), [Syracuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily) and [Enna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enna) in the provinces of the same name, Niscemi and Riesi of Caltanissetta province, and Vittoria of Ragusa province, located mainly on the Southern and Eastern coasts of Sicily. The group also has members and is active in [Malta](19137.md).

The Stidda has extended its power and influence into the mainland Italy provinces of Milan, Genoa, and Turin. The members of the organization are called *Stiddari* in the Caltanissetta province, and *Stiddaroli* in the Agrigento province. Stidda members can be identified and sometimes introduced to each other by a tattoo of five greenish marks arranged in a circle, forming a star called "i punti della malavita" or "the points of the criminal life."

The Cosa Nostra wars of the late 1970s and early 1980s brought the Corleonesi clan and its vicious and ruthless leaders [Luciano Leggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Leggio), Salvatore "Toto" Riina, and [Bernardo Provenzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Provenzano) into power. This caused disorganization and disenchantment inside the traditional Cosa Nostra power base and values system, leaving the growing Stidda organization to counter Cosa Nostra's power, influence, and expansion in Southern and Eastern Sicily. Stidda membership was also reinforced by Cosa Nostra *men of honor,* such as those loyal to slain Capo [Giuseppe Di Cristina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Di_Cristina) of Riesi who had defected from Cosa Nostra's ranks during the bloodthirsty reign of the Corleonesi clan.

The organization also enlarged its membership by absorbing local thugs and criminals (*picciotti*) who operated at the far margins of organized crime. This allowed Stidda to gain more power and credibility in the Italian underworld. From 1978 to 1990, former Corleonesi clan leader and aspirant to the Cosa Nostra's "Capo di Tutti Capi" title, Salvatore Riina, waged a war within Cosa Nostra and against the Stidda. The war spread death and terror among mafiosi and the public, leaving over 500 dead in Cosa Nostra and over 1,000 in La Stidda, including Stidda captains Calogero Lauria and Vincenzo Spina.

With the 1993 capture of Salvatore Riina and the 2006 jailing of Bernardo Provenzano's, a new [Pax Mafiosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Mafiosa) arose – following a new, less violent and low-key approach to criminal activities. As a result, the Stidda cemented its power, influence and credibility among the longer-established criminal organizations in Italy and around the world, making itself a bonefied underworld player.

## Sinti origin

### Casamonica clan

Main article: [Casamonica clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casamonica_clan)

The Casamonica clan is a mafia criminal organization of [Sinti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinti) origin and operating in the Rome area and present, with their ramifications, in various other areas of [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio), [Abruzzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abruzzo) and [Molise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molise). Its criminal rise took place in the early 1970s, with criminal collusion with the [Banda della Magliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_della_Magliana), which provided it with initial contacts with [Camorra](44945.md), ['Ndrangheta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Ndrangheta) and [Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia). With the dismantling of the Banda della Magliana, the Clan increased its criminal power by inheriting its criminal dealings and contacts with [Camorra](44945.md) and ['Ndrangheta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Ndrangheta). The Casamonica clan wove together a criminal network of closely affiliated clans, made up of numerous Sinti families, such as Di Silvio, Spada, Spinelli, and others. These Sinti families illegally appropriated surnames of Italian origin and land that did not belong to them, building lavish and luxurious illegal villas with the profits from their criminal mafia activities.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

## Defunct or severely weakened groups

### Basilischi

Main article: [Basilischi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilischi)

The [Basilischi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilischi) is a [mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafia)-type criminal organization active in the [Basilicata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilicata) region, founded in [Potenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potenza) in 1994 with the approval of the ['Ndrangheta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Ndrangheta). Its status as a mafia group was only recognized on 21 December 2007, with a verdict from the Potenza Court that sentenced twenty-six defendants to a total of 242 years in prison.[^cite_note-history-45]

An ancient term, used as far back as [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) to refer to a lethal reptile of the time, the name is known to the general public, especially due to Lina Wetmuller's film *[I Basilischi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Basilischi)* (1963). It carries a double meaning, representing both a large and lazy human lizard and an inhabitant of [Basilicata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilicata). The name gained renewed fame through the second chapter of the *[Harry Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Potter)* saga, where the [Basilisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilisk) was a huge and deadly mythological serpent. The Basilischi adopted this name as a symbol of their origins and their dangerous nature.[^cite_note-history-45]

### Banda della Magliana

Main article: [Banda della Magliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_della_Magliana)



[Banda della Magliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_della_Magliana)

The [Banda della Magliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_della_Magliana) (English translation: Magliana gang) was an Italian criminal organization based in [Rome](25458.md) and active mostly throughout the late 1970s until the early 1990s. The gang's name refers to the neighborhood in Rome, the [Magliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magliana), from which most of its members came.

The Magliana gang was involved in criminal activities during the Italian "[years of lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Years_of_Lead_\(Italy\))" (or *anni di piombo*). The organization was tied to other Italian criminal organizations such as the Cosa Nostra, Camorra and the 'Ndrangheta. Most notably though, it was connected to [neo-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-fascism) [paramilitary](146730.md) and [terrorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) organizations, including the [Nuclei Armati Rivoluzionari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclei_Armati_Rivoluzionari) (NAR), the group responsible for the [1980 Bologna massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Bologna_massacre).

In addition to their involvement in traditional organized crime rackets, the Banda della Magliana is also believed to have worked for Italian political figures such as [Licio Gelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licio_Gelli), a grand-master of the illegal and underground [freemason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freemason) lodge known as [Propaganda Due](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_Due) (P2), which was purportedly connected to neo-fascist and far-right militant paramilitary groups.

### Banda della Comasina and the Turatello Crew

Main articles: [Francis Turatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Turatello) and [Banda della Comasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_della_Comasina)



[Renato Vallanzasca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renato_Vallanzasca)

The [Banda della Comasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_della_Comasina) (English translation: Comasina gang) was an organized crime group active mainly in [Milan](36511.md), the [Milan metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_metropolitan_area), and [Lombardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombardia) in the 1970s and 1980s, or *[anni di piombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anni_di_piombo)*. Their name is derived from the Milan neighborhood of [Comasina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comasina), the founding location of the organization. The group was led by the Milan [crime boss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_boss) [Renato Vallanzasca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renato_Vallanzasca), a powerful figure in the Milanese underworld in the 1970s. The group began as a smaller [robbery](54849.md) and [kidnapping](169376.md) [gang](80408.md), and continued to specialize in [armed robbery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_robbery), kidnapping, [carjacking](362652.md), and [truck hijacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truck_hijacking) even as they grew in power and expanded into other subtler areas of organized crime. The gang became notorious for brazenly setting up [roadblocks](585425.md) and robbing members of the Milan police force. As the Banda della Comasina rose in power, they expanded into other areas of organized crime, such as [arms trafficking](42210.md), [illegal gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_gambling), [drug trafficking](218449.md), [contract killing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_killing), [extortion](162449.md), [racketeering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racketeering), [bootlegging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rum-running), and [corruption](3608404.md).

The group's downfall was partially brought about by its brazen disregard for both subtlety and authority, as well as its continued reliance on kidnapping and armed robbery to make money. The gang's leader, Renato Vallanzasca, repeatedly escaped from police custody and continued to commit robberies and kidnappings of wealthy and powerful people, even while living as fugitive. In 1976, the group committed approximately 70 robberies and multiple kidnappings (many of which were never reported to police), including the kidnapping of a prominent [Bergamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergamo) businessman. Several of the robberies resulted in the murder of the robbery victims and responding officers, including four policemen, a doctor and a bank employee. That same year, Vallanzasca (still a fugitive at this point) and his gang kidnapped 16-year-old Emanuela Trapani, the daughter of a Milanese businessman, and held her captive for over a month and a half, from December 1976 to January 1977. They only released the girl upon payment of a one billion ransom in Italian currency.[^cite_note-int-47] Soon after, the gang killed two highway police officers who had stopped a car containing Vallanzasca and his gang members. Two other members of the Banda della Comasina, Carlo Carluccio and Antonio Furiato, were killed in separate gun battles with policemen, in Piazza Vetra in Milan and on the [Autostrada A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A4_\(Italy\)) motorway respectively.

Vallanzasca was eventually captured, and while in prison, he developed an alliance and friendship with his former rival, [Francis Turatello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Turatello), another recently incarcerated, powerful crime boss in Milan with strong connections to the [Sicilian Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia), [Camorra](44945.md), and [Italian-American](18293303.md) [Gambino crime family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambino_crime_family) (as well as possible ties to the [Banda della Magliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_della_Magliana) and Italian political terrorist groups). As the leader of the so-called Turatello Crew, Francis Turatello was a protégé of the Sicilian Mafia and an important ally in Milan, for both the Sicilian Mafia and [Nuova Camorra Organizzata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuova_Camorra_Organizzata). The Turatello Crew controlled various illegal rackets in the Milan underworld with the backing of the Sicilian Mafia and Camorra, controlling [prostitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prostitution) in Milan and, like the Banda della Comasina, participating in robbery and kidnapping.

The Turatello Crew and Banda della Comasina had been in the middle of a gang war with each other when their leaders, Vallanzasca and Turatello, were incarcerated in the same prison, reconciling and bonding there. The newly forged alliance between the two crime groups only increased their influence and power in the Milan underworld, allowing them to control much of Milan's organized crime, even while their leaders remained incarcerated.

In 1981, Turatello was assassinated in prison by order of Camorra boss [Raffaele Cutolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffaele_Cutolo), and the Turatello Crew collapsed while the Banda della Comasina lost an important ally. Soon after Turatello's assassination, Vallanzasca, still imprisoned, organized and participated in a prison revolt in which two [pentiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentiti) (former gangsters that collaborate with the Italian government) were brutally killed. Despite repeated escape attempts, Vallanzasca remains in prison, serving four consecutive life sentences plus 290 years, and the Banda della Comisina collapsed and disbanded in the early 1980s in his absence.

## Italian criminal groups in other countries

Italian organized crime groups, in particular the Sicilian Mafia and the Camorra, have been involved in heroin trafficking for decades. Two major investigations that targeted their drug trafficking schemes in the 1970s and 80s are known as the [French Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Connection) and [Pizza Connection Trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pizza_Connection_Trial).[^cite_note-48] These and other investigations have thoroughly documented their cooperation with other major drug trafficking organizations. Italian crime groups are also involved in illegal gambling, political corruption, extortion, kidnapping, frauds, counterfeiting, infiltration of legitimate businesses, murders, bombings, and weapons trafficking. Industry experts in Italy estimate that their worldwide criminal activity is worth more than US\$100 billion annually.[^cite_note-\:0-49][^cite_note-50]

The Italian crime groups (especially the Sicilian Mafia) have also connections with [Corsican gangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corsican_mafia). These collaboration were mostly important during the French Connection era. During the 1990s, the links between the Corsican mafia and the Sicilian mafia facilitated the establishment of some Sicilian gangsters in the [Lavezzi Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavezzi_Islands).

Those currently active in the United States are the [Sicilian Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia), [Camorra](44945.md), ['Ndrangheta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Ndrangheta), and [Sacra Corona Unita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacra_corona_unita) or "United Sacred Crown". The [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) (FBI) refers to them as "Italian Organized Crime" (IOC). These Italian crime groups frequently collaborate with the [Italian-American Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian-American_Mafia), which is itself an offshoot of the Sicilian Mafia.[^cite_note-\:0-49]

Thanks to its dominance in global drug trade, the 'Ndrangheta became the first and only Italian criminal organization present on all continents in the world. It has a strong presence in countries such as the [Netherlands](21148.md), [Belgium](3343.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Australia](4689264.md) and [Canada](5042916.md).[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

In particular the 'Ndrangheta and the Camorra consider the UK an area of interest for laundering money, using financial companies and business activities, while having large investments in [London](17867.md).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

Outside Italy, the Camorra is particularly present in Spain, having established a strong presence in the country since the 1980s. Among the most important clans operating in Spain are the [Polverino clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polverino_clan), the [Amato-Pagano clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scissionisti_di_Secondigliano) and the [Contini clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contini_clan). However, Camorra clans are also active in other European countries with varying degrees of presence. Its presence in the countries is mostly related to drug trafficking and money laundering.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56]

The 'Ndrangheta has carved out turf and formed close ties with organized crime groups in Latin American countries such as [Colombia](5222.md), [Brazil](3383.md), and [Argentina](18951905.md). The Camorra also maintains important drug import routes from South America since the 1980s. And the Sicilian Mafia had a presence in [Venezuela](32374.md), with the [Cuntrera-Caruana Mafia clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuntrera-Caruana_Mafia_clan) in particular having established a strong settlement.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

## Non-Italian criminal groups in Italy

There are numerous criminal organizations operating in Italy. They are mostly composed of non-Italians living in Italy, such as the [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_mafia), [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_mafia), [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_mafia)[^cite_note-59] and [Moroccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_mafia) mafias, the [Chinese Triads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triad_society), the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_mafia), [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_mafia) and [Nigerian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_organized_crime) gangs, among others.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] There is also a growing number of criminal groups originating from [Hispanic America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_America), such as the [Latin Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Kings_\(gang\)), operating most often in Lombardy.[^cite_note-64] The Albanian gangs historically operate mainly in Lazio, Lombardy and Apulia, but they are also expanding to other regions such as Emilia-Romagna and Liguria.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

Most of these organizations focus their ambitions on prostitution and drug trafficking, under the control and with permissions of the Italian organized crime groups.

### Albanian mafia

This section is an excerpt from [Albanian mafia § Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_mafia#Italy).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Albanian_mafia\&action=edit)]



Elvis Demçe, one of the main leaders of the Albanian mafia in Italy.

The geographic vicinity, the accessibility to the EU through Italy, and the ties with the Calabrian and [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia) criminality have all contributed to the expansion of the Albanian criminality on the Italian scenario.

Further factors relative to the economy have rendered the Albanian criminality even more competitive. In fact, some organisations which reached Italy in the early 1990s, infiltrated the Lombardy narco-traffic network run by the 'ndrangheta. Albanian mafia leaders have reached stable agreements with the Italian criminal organisations, also with the mafia, so have gained legitimacy in many illegal circuits, diversifying their activities to avoid reasons of conflict with similar host structures. So, Albanian drug trafficking has emerged in [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia), [Sicily](27619.md), [Calabria](44772.md) and [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campania), expanding its drug trafficking operations also in [Lazio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazio), [Lombardy](43807.md) and [Emilia-Romagna](162715.md).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] The Albanian mafia is highly involved in the drug trafficking in [Rome](25458.md), being considered by the investigations as one of the most important organizations active in the Italian capital. In towns on the outskirts of Rome, in particular in [Velletri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velletri), the Albanians were also considered for a time to be the main organization dedicated to drug trafficking.[^cite_note-70] One of the most notorious and important criminals of the Albanian mafia operating in and around Rome was [Elvis Demce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Demce?action=edit\&redlink=1), arrested in 2022 and sentenced to 18 years in jail.[^cite_note-71]

The Albanian mafia is very active in the drug business in the city of [Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari), where it established alliances with the city's crime groups, in particular with the [Strisciuglio clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strisciuglio_clan).[^cite_note-72] Albanian criminal groups are also responsible for trafficking weapons of war into Bari and its [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Bari). Among the figures of the Albanian mafia operating in Bari is Aleksander Hodaj, responsible for massive heroin trafficking between Albania and the city.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

Among the most important Albanian mobsters active in Italy are: Arben Zogu: boss of a drug trafficking crime group active in Rome.[^cite_note-75] Ismail Rebeshi: drug lord active in the city of [Viterbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viterbo) and in its [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Viterbo).[^cite_note-76] Ermal Arapaj: drug lord active in the region of Lazio.[^cite_note-77] Dorian Petoku: one of the most notorious Albanian drug traffickers active in Rome.[^cite_note-78] Janku Kristo Dhimitraq also known as "Janku Kristo", born in 1969, involved in a big drug trafficking scheme with the [Barese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barese) Strisciuglio clan.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

In July 2025, the Prosecutor’s Office of Verona, together with [SPAK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPAK) (the Albanian Special Anti-Corruption and Organized Crime Prosecutor’s Office) and the [Guardia di Finanza](44013914.md), carried out an investigation targeting the Albanian mafia involved in international cocaine trafficking. The group reinvested drug trafficking proceeds in real estate in the [province of Verona](990199.md), including a building in [Nogarole Rocca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nogarole_Rocca) with 30 rental units and another property valued at over 4 million euros. A preventive seizure was ordered on financial assets, company shares, and real estate linked to money laundering. Investigations revealed a Verona-based real estate company receiving large, unusual bank transfers from Albania, connected to two brothers leading the clan, one an international fugitive, the other imprisoned in Belgium for a murder linked to drug trafficking disputes. Italian accomplices, allegedly on the clan’s payroll, were also investigated. The operation involved joint efforts between Italian authorities and SPAK, with financial and telecommunications surveillance confirming complex money laundering activities, including the use of an Albanian fruit and vegetable company to conceal illicit funds.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

In February 2026, the Italian police launched the “Operazione Ura”, which uncovered connections between the Albanian mafia and the [Parisi-Palermiti clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parisi-Palermiti_clan) active in [Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari). The investigation focused on large quantities of heroin and cocaine moved since 2016 between the [Balkans](4829.md), [Northern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe), [South America](26769.md), and the region of [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia). Investigators reconstructed what was described as a “communion of interests” between the Albanian mafia, responsible at a transnational level for the commercialization and transfer of narcotics, and the Parisi-Palermiti clan. According to the investigation, members of the Parisi-Palermiti were responsible for the cutting and packaging of the drugs into blocks, which were then sold wholesale to other criminal organizations in the cities of Bari, [Brindisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brindisi), and [Lecce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecce) interested in receiving high-quality heroin and cocaine originating respectively from [Turkey](11125639.md) and South America.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

According to a report presented in April 2026, the VI regional anti-mafia commission highlights the growing role and increasing autonomy of the Albanian mafia in the region of [Liguria](71864.md), in [Northwest Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Italy). The analysis indicates that this criminal presence has evolved from carrying out subordinate tasks to establishing a more significant and consistent role, particularly in the logistical aspects of drug trafficking. Specifically, these groups are involved in the retrieval, storage, and distribution of narcotics, operating in connection with the strategic use of Ligurian ports. The report further notes that this development reflects a now “equal collaboration” with the still-dominant 'Ndrangheta, marking a shift in the balance of organized crime in the region.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

The Nigerian mafia has a strong presence throughout Italy. ### Romanian mafia

This section is an excerpt from [Romanian mafia § Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_mafia#Italy).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Romanian_mafia\&action=edit)]

One of the most notorious aspects of the Romanian mafia in Italy is its involvement in human trafficking. Many women and children from Romania are trafficked into Italy, often subjected to sexual exploitation and forced into prostitution. These criminal networks play a significant role in the trafficking of women and minors into highly exploitative conditions.The Romanian mafia is also heavily involved in drug trafficking. Italy is a key point in the global drug trade, and Romanian groups act as intermediaries in the distribution of substances such as cocaine and heroin. They have established networks within Italy, working alongside other [Italian criminal organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_criminal_organization) to control the flow of drugs across the country. Additionally, Romanian crime groups are involved in thefts and robberies. Thefts can range from breaking into homes and cars to stealing valuable goods like jewelry. Robbery is often a major income source for these criminal groups, who use the profits to further fund their operations.[^cite_note-93]

Extortion is another key activity of the Romanian mafia in Italy. They target local businesses and sometimes even other Romanian immigrants, coercing them into paying for "protection." Those who resist or refuse to comply may face violent repercussions, reinforcing the mafia's control over certain territories. Romanian crime groups operates in a decentralized manner in Italy, with various cells or factions working independently, but still maintaining links to one another.[^cite_note-94]

Violence is a common tool used by these groups to intimidate victims, enforce control, and establish dominance over certain areas. While they may not have the same level of public visibility or political influence as some of Italy's other criminal organizations, Romanian mafia groups still collaborate with other criminal entities, particularly when it comes to drug trafficking and sexual exploitation. The Italian authorities have made efforts to crack down on the Romanian mafia in recent years, launching more investigations and carrying out numerous arrests.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

In Italy, most of the Romanian criminals are involved in theft and pimping, however, in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), they created an Italian-style mafia within their local community, extorting money from Romanian night-clubs that ran prostitutes, and would impose their own businesses and singers on weddings by force.[^cite_note-97]

Also in Turin, Romanian criminals created the 'Brigada Oarza' group, specialized in extortion, robbery and drug trafficking. In 2014, 15 members of the Brigada Oarza were convicted of mafia affiliation, being sentenced up to 15 years in prison. It was the first Italian sentence that condemned a group of Romanian people for mafia-style criminal association.[^cite_note-98] The leader of the Brigada Oarza was allegedly the former professional boxer Viorel Lovu.[^cite_note-99]

### Chinese triads

Main article: [Triad (organized crime)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triad_\(organized_crime\))

According to the expert on terrorist organizations and mafia-type organized crime Antonio De Bonis, there is a close relationship between the [Triad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triad_\(organized_crime\)) and the Camorra and the [Port of Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Naples) is the most important landing point for the activities managed by the Chinese groups in cooperation with the camorra. Among the illegal activities in which the two criminal organizations work together are human trafficking and illegal immigration aimed at the sexual and labor exploitation of Chinese people on Italian territory, drug trafficking and the laundering of illicit capital through the purchase of real estate, commercial establishments and businesses.[^cite_note-100]

In 2017, investigators uncovered a plan between the Camorra and Chinese gangs: these exported industrial waste from Italy to [China](5405.md) which guaranteed revenues of millions of euros for both organizations. Industrial waste left [Prato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prato) and arrived in [Hong Kong](13404.md). Among the clans involved in this alliance were the [Casalesi clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casalesi_clan), the [Fabbrocino clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Fabbrocino) and the Ascione clan.[^cite_note-101]

In 2018, Naizhong Zhang, a 58-year-old Chinese crime boss, was arrested in Rome for leading a powerful criminal organization that dominated Chinese goods transportation across much of Europe through violence and extortion. Based in [Prato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prato), where 50,000 Chinese nationals reside, Zhang imposed control over rival gangs after a deadly turf war and expanded into illegal activities such as gambling, drug trafficking, counterfeiting, and money lending. Authorities, led by the Florence anti-mafia unit, issued 33 arrest warrants and investigated 54 other individuals as part of the "China Truck" operation that began in 2011. Zhang flaunted his power with luxury cars, extravagant weddings, and public shows of respect from followers. His organization also operated illegal gambling dens in bars, brothels disguised as massage parlors, and invested criminal profits into ambitious projects, including purchasing part of a coal mine in China. Zhang was arrested along with his secretary and lover, Chen Xiaomian, who was found with €30,000 in cash.[^cite_note-102]

Zhang Dayong, known as Asheng, was executed in Rome, in April 2025, with a gunshot to the head and three to the chest. He was the right-hand man of Naizhong Zhang, the alleged head of the Chinese mafia in Europe, and served as his representative in Rome. Zhang Dayong had a history of violent offenses and was considered a key player in the organization's illegal activities in Italy, including loan sharking and running underground gambling operations. Although he officially ran a store in Rome's [Esquilino neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esquilino_\(rione_of_Rome\)), Asheng was in charge of the Chinese mafia's operations in the Italian capital. Investigators link him to past violence, including the enforcement of payments through beatings. He was also present at the lavish 2013 wedding of Zhang Naizhong's son, attended by major figures in the Chinese criminal underworld. Asheng reportedly lost favor within the organization after several missteps, including getting drunk and assaulting women at a brothel managed by a fellow gang member. Intercepted conversations reveal how his disrespect toward superiors and erratic behavior eventually sealed his fate. His murder is believed to be connected to the ongoing turf war among Chinese gangs, known as the "hanger war" of Prato, which has now spread to Rome. His death marks a significant escalation in the mafia conflict.[^cite_note-103]

## Fight against organized crime in Italy



A hearing of the [Maxi Trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxi_Trial)

In order to combat the phenomenon more effectively, some legislative measures on the subject began to be created starting from the 1980s, such as the introduction of the crimes of mafia-type association and political-mafia electoral exchange, of a special prison regime, with the introduction of article 41 bis in the law on the Italian penitentiary system, international judicial cooperation[^cite_note-104] and the creation of some ad hoc bodies such as the High Commissioner for the coordination of the fight against mafia delinquency (later abolished). In the 1990s, due to the work of some Italian magistrates such as [Giovanni Falcone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Falcone), [Paolo Borsellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Borsellino) and [Antonino Caponnetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonino_Caponnetto), the [Direzione Investigativa Antimafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direzione_Investigativa_Antimafia) and the [Direzione Nazionale Antimafia e Antiterrorismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direzione_Nazionale_Antimafia_e_Antiterrorismo?action=edit\&redlink=1) were created. Many of the provisions on the matter were then collected in Legislative Decree no. 59 of 6 September 2011.

The [Antimafia Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimafia_Pool) was a group of [investigating magistrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Examining_magistrate) at the Prosecuting Office of [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) ([Sicily](27619.md)) who closely worked together sharing information and developing new investigative and prosecutorial strategies against the [Sicilian Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia). An informal pool had been created by Judge [Rocco Chinnici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocco_Chinnici) in the early 1980s following the example of anti-terrorism judges in Northern Italy in the 1970s.[^cite_note-jamieson29-105] Most important, they assumed collective responsibility for carrying Mafia prosecutions forward: all the members of the pool signed prosecutorial orders to avoid exposing any one of them to particular risk, such as the one that had cost Judge [Gaetano Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaetano_Costa) his life. Costa had signed the indictments of 55 against the Mafia heroin-trafficking network of the Spatola-Inzerillo-Gambino clan after virtually all of the other prosecutors in his office had declined to do so – a fact that leaked out of the office and eventually cost him his life. He was murdered on 6 August 1980, on the orders of [Salvatore Inzerillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Inzerillo).[^cite_note-schneiders-106] In July 1983, [Rocco Chinnici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocco_Chinnici) was killed by the Mafia. His place as head of the ‘Office of Instruction’ (Ufficio istruzione), the investigative branch of the Prosecution Office of Palermo, was taken by [Antonino Caponnetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonino_Caponnetto), who formalized the pool. Next to [Giovanni Falcone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Falcone), the group included [Paolo Borsellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Borsellino), Giuseppe Di Lello and Leonardo Guarnotta.[^cite_note-schneiders-106][^cite_note-stille85-107] The group subsequently pooled together several investigations into the Mafia, which would result in the [Maxi Trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxi_Trial) against the Mafia starting in February 1986 and which lasted until December 1987.



The *[pentito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentito)* [Tommaso Buscetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommaso_Buscetta) (in sunglasses) is led into court at the [Maxi Trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxi_Trial)

The [Maxi Trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxi_Trial) ([Italian](14708.md): *Maxiprocesso*) was a criminal trial against the [Sicilian Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia) that took place in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo), [Sicily](27619.md). The trial lasted from 10 February 1986 (the first day of the [Corte d'Assise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corte_d'Assise)) to 30 January 1992 (the final day of the [Supreme Court of Cassation](3588041.md)), and was held in a [bunker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunker)-style courthouse specially constructed for this purpose inside the walls of the Ucciardone prison. Sicilian prosecutors indicted 475 mafiosi for a multitude of crimes relating to [Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia) activities, based primarily on testimonies given as evidence from former Mafia bosses turned informants, known as *[pentiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentito)*, in particular [Tommaso Buscetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommaso_Buscetta) and [Salvatore Contorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvatore_Contorno). Most were convicted, 338 people, sentenced to a total of 2,665 years, not including life sentences handed to 19 [bosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_boss); the convictions were upheld on 30 January 1992 by the [Supreme Court of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Italy), after the final stage of appeal. The importance of the trial was that the existence of Cosa Nostra was finally judicially confirmed.[^cite_note-schneiders-106] It is considered to be the most significant trial ever against the [Sicilian Mafia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sicilian_Mafia), as well as the largest trial in world history.[^cite_note-big-108] Throughout and after the trial, several judges and magistrates were killed by the Mafia, including the two who led it—[Giovanni Falcone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Falcone) and [Paolo Borsellino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Borsellino).

The [Spartacus Trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartacus_Trial) ([Italian](14708.md): *Processo Spartacus*) was a series of criminal trials, each specifically directed against the activities of the powerful [Casalesi clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casalesi_clan) of the [Camorra](44945.md). The trial was opened at the [Corte d'Assise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corte_d'Assise) of [Santa Maria Capua Vetere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_Capua_Vetere) in [Caserta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caserta). It was named after the historical gladiator, [Spartacus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartacus), who led a rebellion of slaves beginning in old [Capua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_Capua_Vetere) against the ancient [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire). The trial was initially chaired by its president, Catello Marano on 1 July 1998. It continued just over ten years, until its final verdict was eventually read on 19 June 2008.[^cite_note-cu300905-109][^cite_note-cor200608-110] In that 10-year legal trial, 36 members of the clan were charged with a string of murders and other crimes. The Casalesi clan had exploited and extorted from every business and economic opportunity, from waste disposal to construction, in creating a monopoly in the cement market for their own building businesses to the distribution of materials. Building business would have to pay for the contracts, buy material from the clan, and keep paying for protection. The clan also controlled elections.[^cite_note-smh210608-111] More than 1,300 people were investigated, 508 witnesses gave evidence and 626 were interviewed in the trial which saw the heaviest penalties ever for organized crime with a total of 700 years of imprisonment. Over the course of the initial trial and the appeal, five people involved in the case were murdered, including a court interpreter. A judge and two journalists were threatened with death.[^cite_note-smh210608-111][^cite_note-ind200608-112] In all, 115 people were prosecuted, 27 life sentences, plus 750 years in prison were handed out to the defendants. On 15 January 2010, Italy's Supreme Court confirmed the sentence.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114]
