**Gelendzhik** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Геленджик; [Adyghe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adyghe_language): Хъулъыжъий, romanized: *Ḣułəžij*) is a [resort town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort_town) in [Krasnodar Krai](474125.md), Russia, located on the [Gelendzhik Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelendzhik_Bay) of the [Black Sea](3386.md), between [Novorossiysk](1190336.md) (31 kilometers (19 mi) to the northwest) and [Tuapse](1623160.md) (93 kilometers (58 mi) to the southeast). Greater Gelendzhik sprawls for 102 kilometers (63 mi) along the coastline and covers an area of 122,754 hectares (303,330 acres), although only 1,926 hectares (4,760 acres) fall within the boundaries of Gelendzhik proper. Population: 80,204 ([2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census));[^cite_note-2021Census-7] 54,980 ([2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Russian_census));[^cite_note-2010Census-2] 50,012 ([2002 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Russian_census));[^cite_note-PopCensus-8] 47,711 ([1989 Soviet census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Soviet_census)).[^cite_note-Census1989-9]

## Etymology

The [toponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponym) *Gelendzhik* likely originates from the indigenous [Adyghe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adyghe_language) name for the area, Хъулъыжъий (*Khulyzhyy*), which translates as 'little pasture,' where хъулъы (*khulye*) means 'pasture' or 'clearing,' and жъий (*zhyy*) means 'little.'[^cite_note-10] Folk etymology derives the toponym from [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language) *gelin*, meaning 'bride,' with *-cik*, a diminutive and affectionate suffix, thus meaning 'little bride,' possibly because it was a major [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_Ottoman_Empire) centre, serving as the main port for the export of local girls to [harems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harem).[^cite_note-Oxford-11] In 2010, a sculpture titled 'White Bride' was installed on the Gelendzhik waterfront.[^cite_note-12]

## History

Gelendzhik is one of the most ancient settlements on the coast of the [Black Sea](3386.md).[^cite_note-enc-13] In antiquity, the Gelendzhik Bay was the site of a minor Greek outpost in the 6th century BC, mentioned as **Torikos** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Τορικος) in the *[Periplus of Pseudo-Scylax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periplus_of_Pseudo-Scylax)*.[^cite_note-enc-13] It is unknown to Hellenistic sources, but reappears in Roman ones under the name of **Pagrae** in 64 BC.[^cite_note-14] As a [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) colony, it was wiped out by the invading [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols).[^cite_note-enc-13]

During the [late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages), the [Genoese Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_Republic) had a notable influence on the region, while the [Ghisolfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghisolfi), a Genoese-Jewish family, had a prominent role in the trade and commerce in [Gazaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazaria_\(Genoese_colonies\)). During this period, the town was named **Maurolaca** or **Mauro Laco**, and was considered one of the most important [Genoese colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genoese_colonies) in the Black Sea.[^cite_note-15] From the late 15th century, it belonged to the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).[^cite_note-enc-13]

Before Russia secured the coast as a result of the [Treaty of Adrianople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Adrianople_\(1829\)) in 1829, a brisk slave trade had been carried on between the mountaineers and the Ottoman Empire. The tribes of [Shapsugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapsugs) and [Natukhajs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natukhajs) inhabited the area until the end of the [Caucasian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasian_War).[^cite_note-enc-13] In 1831, Russian troops under the command of E. A. Berkhman built the Gelendzhik fortress, which became an outpost as well as a naval base for the [Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet).[^cite_note-enc-13] However, during the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) of 1854–1856, it was destroyed and abandoned.[^cite_note-enc-13] The fortress was rebuilt in 1857, and in 1864, the *[stanitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanitsa)* of Gelendzhiksaya was built in its place.[^cite_note-enc-13] In 1915, it was given town status.[^cite_note-enc-13]

In the late 19th century, Gelendzhik developed as a [spa resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa_resort), and in the early 20th century, sanatoriums were built.[^cite_note-enc-13] A sanatorium for children with [bone tuberculosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_tuberculosis) was opened in 1913.[^cite_note-enc-13] According to the [1926 Soviet census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_Soviet_census), the population was 45.4% Russian, 25.0% Ukrainian, and 20.7% Greek.[^cite_note-16] During [World War II](32927.md), it became the main base for small ships of the Black Sea Fleet.[^cite_note-enc-13] In 1970, it became a resort of all-union significance in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-enc-13] The town is home to more than 180 sanatoriums, holiday hotels, rest houses, and tourist centres.[^cite_note-enc-13]

## Description



Residential complex in Gelendzhik

During the [Soviet period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_period), Gelendzhik was developed as a [resort town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort_town). It possesses sand beaches, three [waterparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterpark), two [chairlift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairlift) lines, and two Orthodox churches (from 1909 and 1913, respectively). The environs of Gelendzhik are noted for a chain of [waterfalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfall), [an outcrop of dolmens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolmens_of_Russia), two extremely ancient pine and [juniper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniper) groves, and the [Sail Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sail_Rock), located 17 kilometers (11 mi) from the town's central area. The coastal village of [Arkhipo-Osipovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkhipo-Osipovka), administrated from Gelendzhik, contains the terminus of the [Blue Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Stream) gas pipeline. An annual [hydroaviasalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroaviasalon) is held in Gelendzhik since 1996.

Revealed on 29 March 2001 between [Bietingen](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bietingen%20\(\(Gottmadingen\)\)) and [Schaffhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schaffhausen), Litolmore Trading Limited, a company which conducts business in mineral fertilizers, coal, oil, gas, fuel oil, aviation and diesel fuel, is a Gelendzhik firm.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

A 17,691 square metres (190,420 sq ft) [palace allegedly built for Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Putin's_Palace) is located on the Gelendzhik Bay not far from the city.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Administrative and municipal status



Gelendzhik town administration building

Within the [framework of administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Administrative_divisions), it is, together with twenty [rural localities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia), incorporated as the **[Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_federal_subject_significance) of Gelendzhik**—an administrative unit with the status equal to that of the [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Krasnodar_Krai).[^cite_note-Ref1481-1] As a [municipal division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Municipal_divisions), the Town of Gelendzhik is incorporated as **Gelendzhik Urban Okrug**.[^cite_note-Ref749-4]

## Climate

Gelendzhik has a borderline [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa*) and a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*). The climate of Gelendzhik is noticeably drier than Novorossiysk and Tuapse. Winters are mild and snowfall is light. Summers are hot with warm evenings. Sometimes Gelendzhik has experienced big floods as it happened in 2012 ([2012 Krasnodar Krai floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Krasnodar_Krai_floods)).
