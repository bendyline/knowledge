**Harakat al-Shabaab al-Mujahideen**[^cite_note-38] simply known as **al-Shabaab**[^cite_note-39] is a transnational [Wahhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabi)-[Salafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_jihadism) [Jihadist](7826589.md#Sunni_jihadism) militant organization.[^cite_note-Wahhabi1-22] It is involved in the ongoing [Somali Civil War](21454663.md), controlling a [non-contiguous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographic_contiguity#Non-contiguity) network of primarily [rural areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural_area) in southern Somalia that some consider a [quasi-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasi-state).[^cite_note-40] Since 2011, the group refers to the territory under its control as the [Islamic Emirate of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Somalia), although it has not formally declared itself as an emirate.[^cite_note-fs-41] Al-Shabaab has invoked [takfir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takfir) to rationalize its [terrorist attacks](566821.md) on [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Somalia) [civilians](145804.md) and [civil servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service).[^cite_note-Takfir1-23] It is, in a more limited capacity, active elsewhere in [East Africa](260987.md), and has pledged allegiance to the militant organization [al-Qaeda](1921.md). Al-Shabaab has been designated as a [terrorist organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_organization) by numerous countries, including the [United States](3434750.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), [Australia](4689264.md), and [Canada](5042916.md), as well as several [EAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Community) and [EU](9317.md) [member-states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_state). The militant group remains subject to [international sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions) imposed by the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md), the [European Union](9317.md), and other [jurisdictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction).&#x20;

Formed in the mid-2000s as a youth militia within the wider military wing of the [Islamic Courts Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Courts_Union), Al-Shabaab came to prominence during the 2006–2009 [Ethiopian invasion and occupation of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somalia_War_\(2006%E2%80%932009\)), during which it presented itself as a vehicle for the waging of armed resistance against the occupying Ethiopian army. In subsequent years, it gained popular support from [Somalis](1571696.md) and became a dominant force in south and central Somalia, defending large swathes of territory by fighting against the [African Union Mission to Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_to_Somalia) and the [Federal Government of Somalia](36817379.md), as well as the latter's [transitional predecessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Federal_Government_of_Somalia). Al-Shabaab gained international prominence due to its recruitment of foreign fighters.&#x20;

Between 2011 and 2013, a coalition of [African Union](10634933.md) forces, led by the Somali government, wrested a significant amount of territory from Al-Shabaab, including the capital city, [Mogadishu](60154.md). During the same period, the group was plagued by internal conflicts over its leadership and ideological direction, which intensified when, in February 2012, Al-Shabaab's leadership [pledged allegiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay%27ah) to al-Qaeda. It suffered further military losses in 2014, as a result of [Operation Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Indian_Ocean), and the killing of its [emir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir), [Ahmed Abdi Godane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Abdi_Godane). Several years thereafter, Al-Shabaab retreated from the major cities, but it remained influential in many rural areas, and it prioritized [guerrilla](12720.md) attacks over territorial acquisitions. It is responsible for many high-fatality attacks, including the [2013 Westgate attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westgate_shopping_mall_attack), [October 2017 Mogadishu bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14_October_2017_Mogadishu_bombings) and the [2022 Somali Ministry of Education bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Somali_Ministry_of_Education_bombings). Apart from its activities in Somalia, the group also operates in East African neighboring countries, extending its insurgency to Kenya's border regions with its [Jaysh Ayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaysh_Ayman) wing [^cite_note-jamestown-13] and carrying out a [major incursion into Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shabaab_invasion_of_Ethiopia) in 2022. Attendant to its recent resurgence, it was estimated to have increased its combat strength to between 7,000 and 18,000 fighters during 2022.[^cite_note-nordicmonitor.com-42][^cite_note-rs-36][^cite_note-Marchal_309–317-43][^cite_note-GCT-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]&#x20;

## Name

Al-Shabaab is also known as **Ash-Shabaab**,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBryden2006-47] as well as Jaysh al-Usra in Somalia (Arabic: جيش العسرة في الصومال).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] The term *Al-Shabaab* means "[The Youth](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D8%B4%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%A8#Arabic)" in Arabic. It has also referred to itself as the **[Islamic Emirate of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Wilayat_of_Somalia)** since 2011.[^cite_note-fs-41][^cite_note-50]&#x20;

The [Federal Government of Somalia](36817379.md) often refer to Al-Shabaab as the **Khawarij** (**[Kharijites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharijites)**).[^cite_note-KhawarijSources-5][^cite_note-51] The Somali government has instructed local media to refer to the militant group by the acronym **UGUS** (Somali: **Ururka Gumaadka Ummadda Soomaaliyeed**; English: **The Group that Massacres the Somali People**).[^cite_note-UGUS1-4] Journalists have faced pressure following the directive issued by Somalia's [National Intelligence and Security Agency (NISA)](40417792.md), while al-Shabaab has threatened those who use the term.[^cite_note-UGUS2-52]&#x20;

### Origins

Although it is unclear when Al-Shabaab was formed, it is understood to have originated as a youth militia within the military wing of the [Islamic Courts Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Courts_Union), an umbrella group which provided *de facto* governance in much of Somalia until the [country was invaded by Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Somalia_\(2006%E2%80%932009\)) in December 2006.[^cite_note-us-53][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEInternational_Crisis_Group2022-54][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55] From 2007 to 2008, Al-Shabaab established itself as an independent actor, gaining prominence as a vehicle of armed resistance against the Ethiopian [military occupation](816214.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEInternational_Crisis_Group2022-54][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55] Many of its early leaders and members previously belonged not only to the Islamic Courts Union but also to the older [Al-Itihaad al-Islamiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Itihaad_al-Islamiya), a group founded on the tenets of [Islamism](15012.md) and opposition to [clannism](1571696.md#Clans).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarnett2020-56] Many early Al-Shabaab leaders had also been trained as [mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mujahideen) in [Afghanistan](737.md) and practiced the [Hanafi school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanafi_school) of [jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_jurisprudence).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]&#x20;

### Ideology

Al-Shabaab adheres to a radical militant interpretation of the [Wahhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabism) ideology,[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-bbc_2017-60] and generally advocates a form of [jihadism](7826589.md) with transnational aims,[^cite_note-Marchal_309–317-43][^cite_note-GCT-44] linking local grievances to the plight of [Muslims](19541.md) worldwide.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEInternational_Crisis_Group2022-54] According to the [International Crisis Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Crisis_Group), Salafism has been a core unifying principle of Al-Shabaab, although this principle is not interpreted uniformly by the group's members and leaders.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEInternational_Crisis_Group2022-54] The ideology of Al-Shabaab represents a fusion of the transnational [Salafi Jihadist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_jihadism) trend in Somalia, which collaborated with the [Takfir wal-Hijra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takfir_wal-Hijra) group, with the hardline elements of Wahhabi groups in Somalia.[^cite_note-61] Certain elements of [Jama'at al-I'tisam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jama%27at_al-I%27tisam) supported the setting up of early al-Shabab training camps, and its most hardcore faction merged into Al-Shabaab in late 2004.[^cite_note-62]&#x20;

Following its pan-Islamist political outlook, incursions into Somalia by Ethiopia – and later by Kenya, the United States, and others – are viewed by Al-Shabaab as part of a wider American-sponsored [war against Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_against_Islam).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarnett2020-56][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVidinoPantucciKohlmann2010-63] For instance, Al-Shabaab denounced the [2006 Ethiopian invasion of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Somalia_\(2006%E2%80%932009\)) as a "Zionist-Crusader aggression",[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarnett2020-56] in which the United States "unleashed its 'hunting dogs' in Ethiopia and Kenya" by deploying "the world's crusader forces" to counter the rise of the [Islamic Courts Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Courts_Union).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVidinoPantucciKohlmann2010222-64] However, this globalist framework is not universal within the group,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchaefer2011-65][^cite_note-counterterrorism-66] an ideological fault-line which has sometimes fostered [factionalism](40362512.md) and internal conflict. Much of Al-Shabaab's Somali support base sees as its primary goal the establishment of a stable Islamic state inside Somalia,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVidinoPantucciKohlmann2010-63][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon2014-67] or, more ambitiously, inside so-called [Greater Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Somalia), uniting the ethnic Somali populations of Ethiopia, Kenya, and Djibouti.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPetrich2022-68] Other domestic supporters are concerned primarily with clan-related and local objectives, and are therefore prone to infighting and shifting alliances.[^cite_note-Asnctc-69]&#x20;

However, these ideological differences can accommodate broad opposition within the group to common adversaries – notably, opposition to external intervention in Somalia, often publicly expressed in quasi-[Qutbist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutbism) terms;[^cite_note-70] and opposition to the internationally recognised [Federal Government of Somalia](36817379.md) (FGS), which, lacking a basis in religious ([Sharia](28840.md)) law, is seen to lack legitimacy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEInternational_Crisis_Group2022-54] As part of its opposition to the Somali federal government, Al-Shabaab declares *takfir* against FGS and justifies attacks against FGS government officials and civilian supporters.[^cite_note-Takfir1-23] Al-Shabaab hardliners broadly adhere to a [Takfiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takfiri) interpretation of the principle of *[al-wala' wal-bara'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Wala%27_wal-Bara%27)* (lit. 'loyalty and disavowal'),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEInternational_Crisis_Group2022-54][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlkaff2013-71] insofar as it prescribes dissociation from non-Muslims and those perceived as [apostates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy_in_Islam).[^cite_note-Takfir1-23]&#x20;

A major component of Al-Shabaab's ideology is [Islamic expansionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_military_jurisprudence), which is incorporated into its [Pan-Islamist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Islamism) cause. [Ummah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummah) themes warning Somalis of the plots from international [NGOs](46539.md), alongside "[Christian Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades)" ([United States](3434750.md), [Ethiopia](187749.md), [AMISOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_to_Somalia)) and their collaborators, are a regular feature of the movement's propaganda. During the [Ethiopian invasion of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Somalia_\(2006%E2%80%932009\)), Al-Shabaab positioned itself as a militia under the Islamic *[Muqawwama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muqawamah)* (resistance coalition) of the [Islamic Courts Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Courts_Union), taking the most hardline stance against the invading "Christian crusaders". After the collapse of the ICU in 2007, Al-Shabaab launched its own independent insurgency, gaining popular support from Somalis for defending the country from [American imperialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_imperialism) and foreign occupation. [al-Qaeda](1921.md) began enhancing its co-operation and support to Al-Shabaab during this period, which enabled the movement to establish itself as the strongest military power in Southern Somalia. Al-Shabaab regards Somalia's [Federal Government](36817379.md) as an illegitimate "[apostate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostasy_in_Islam)" entity backed by foreign invaders.[^cite_note-Makhaus_8-72][^cite_note-73]&#x20;

The group has persecuted those individuals belonging to Somalia's small [Christian minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Somalia), whom it accused of aiding the agenda of foreign "[Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades)" to "[convert Somalis to Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_mission)".[^cite_note-economist-74] In 2009, Al-Shabaab destroyed a [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) shrine and its associated graves, asserting that over-embellishing burial sites into shrines is incompatible with *[Sharia](28840.md)*.[^cite_note-Alertnet.org-75][^cite_note-english.aljazeera.net-76] Al-Shabaab has clashed with the pro-[AMISOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_to_Somalia) Sufi militias of [Ahlu Sunna Waljama'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahlu_Sunna_Waljama%27a).[^cite_note-Somali_rage_at_grave_desecration-77][^cite_note-78] In addition, its statements have expressed [anti-Zionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Zionism) sentiments,[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] and the group claimed that its [2019 DusitD2 complex attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairobi_DusitD2_complex_attack) was retaliation against the declaration of [Jerusalem](16043.md) as the capital of Israel.[^cite_note-81] In 2025, Al-Shabaab condemned the Israeli recognition of [Somaliland](51425.md).[^cite_note-82]&#x20;

### Size and governance

In 2017, observers estimated that Al-Shabaab comprised between 7,000 and 9,000 fighters.[^cite_note-bbc_2017-60] In 2018, the [Council on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_on_Foreign_Relations) and [United States military](32212.md) revised this figure downwards, estimating 3,000 to 6,000 and 4,000 to 6,000, respectively.[^cite_note-83] Reflecting an apparent Al-Shabaab resurgence, the [United States Africa Command](9347800.md) estimated 5,000 to 10,000 fighters two years later, in 2020.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarrington2021-84][^cite_note-85] Most recently, an expert report submitted to the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) in early February 2022 estimated that Al-Shabaab's fighting force had grown to between 7,000 and 12,000 fighters.[^cite_note-nordicmonitor.com-42] In late 2022, President of Somalia [Hassan Sheikh Mohamud](36973989.md) stated the faction had around 15,000 to 18,000 fighters.[^cite_note-rs-36]&#x20;

The group is led by an executive *tanfid* council of 7 to 14 members—a configuration also seen in equivalent organisations such as al-Qaeda—supported by a consultative *shura*. It operates several internal departments known as *makhtab*, including *Jabhat* (military), *Sanaaca* (explosives), *Da'wa* (preaching), *Zakat* (taxation), *Wilayah* (local administration), *Garsoor* (Islamic courts), *Amniyat* ([intelligence](81014.md) agency) and *Jaysh al-Hisbah* (police force).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarrington2021-84][^cite_note-86] The organization appoints governors (*waali*), of whom there are ten, who oversee all civil services in Al-Shabaab-controlled areas, including welfare and roads.[^cite_note-87]&#x20;

### Membership

Especially in its early years, Al-Shabaab was sometimes described by its opponents as dominated by the prominent [Hawiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawiye) clan,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchaefer2011-65] who remain influential in the group.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBacon2022-88] According to a 2018 analysis by the Somali Hiraal Institute, five of the ten members of the executive shura council were Hawiye, as were about 94 of the top 220 officials.[^cite_note-Clanpolitics1-28] However, Al-Shabaab opposes clannism, and has tried to appeal to minority groups and to appoint leaders from a variety of ethnic groups and clans.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChonka2016b-89] It incorporates a relatively large contingent of [foreign fighters](70009576.md) (see [§ Foreign recruitment](#Foreign_recruitment)). Rank-and-file members, though sometimes recruited by force,[^cite_note-Asnctc-69] are also attracted by the regular pay that Al-Shabaab offers and by its political [propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChonka2016b-89] In the past, many young Al-Shabaab recruits were drawn from marginalised southern groups, such as the [Jareer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Bantus) [ethnic minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_group#Racial_and_ethnic_minorities).[^cite_note-MinorityInterests1-29] Many are [children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children_in_the_military).[^cite_note-90]&#x20;

In February 2012, [Fu'ad Qalaf Shongole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuad_Qalaf), an Al-Shabaab officer with responsibility for "awareness raising", encouraged a Somali gathering to send their unmarried daughters to fight [jihad](16203.md) with Al-Shabaab. Until then, Al-Shabaab had used only male fighters.[^cite_note-ASWGTJWAG-91] However, according to International Crisis Group, women rarely participate directly in military affairs, though they do play important roles in recruitment, intelligence, and explosives [smuggling](28645.md).[^cite_note-92]&#x20;

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of al-Shabaab-related events](47554620.md).

Al-Shabaab was founded in [Las Anod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Anod) in 2003.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] During that same year, the American [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) began covert operations targeting the [Islamic Courts Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Courts_Union) (ICU) by backing anti-ICU Somali warlords[^cite_note-us-53] with the aim of preventing the formation of a '[Taliban](30635.md) like' state in Somalia that could provide haven to [al-Qaeda](1921.md).[^cite_note-95] In 2005, Mogadishu was hit by a significant wave of unexplained [assassinations](2963.md) and disappearances. The Islamic Courts claimed that covert US government operations and warlords were targeting high ranking ICU officials. According to C. Barnes & H. Hassan, "It was in this context that a military force known as Al-Shabaab ('the Youth') emerged, related to but seemingly autonomous of the broad based Courts movement."[^cite_note-96] Contrary to many reports, Al-Shabaab was neither the armed wing of the Islamic Courts Union nor its most important military component. Al-Shabaab fighters operated as the youth wing of the Courts' militia and gained a fierce reputation during the war against the CIA-backed [Somali warlord alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_for_the_Restoration_of_Peace_and_Counter-Terrorism) in Mogadishu in early to mid-2006, distinguishing themselves within the ICU's military wing.[^cite_note-97]&#x20;

### 2006–2009: Ethiopian invasion

Main article: [Ethiopian invasion and occupation of Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Somalia_\(2006%E2%80%932009\))



Al-Shabaab insurgents marching through [Bay](185141.md) region during the Ethiopian [military occupation](816214.md) (15 July 2008)

Al-Shabaab rose to prominence and radicalized following the full scale Ethiopian invasion of Somalia during December 2006.[^cite_note-Celso-2015-98][^cite_note-as-99] At the time, Al-Shabaab was about six hundred fighters strong.[^cite_note-100] The invasion resulted in the deaths of many Islamic Courts Union affiliates, leaving a vacuum for the small group of several hundred youth that served as the ICU's Shabaab militia to gain prominence.[^cite_note-101] During the [military occupation](816214.md) the group garnered popular support from across many segments of Somali society, as Al-Shabaab was widely viewed as a genuine resistance movement against the Ethiopian military occupation; despite its inclinations towards hardline interpretations of Islam. Though the invasion had fractured the [Islamic Courts Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Courts_Union), it galvanized [non-ethnic religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_nationalism) [Islamic nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_nationalism) on which Al-Shabaab capitalized, especially for recruitment purposes, with thousands of new recruits drawn to the group.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-as-99]&#x20;

During 2008, Al-Shabaab began rapidly expanding and governing territory for the first time.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-as-99] The African Union reported Al-Shabaab was about 2,000 fighters strong during 2008.[^cite_note-na-104] Many ordinary citizens had been [radicalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radicalization) by the US-backed Ethiopian invasion, enabling Al-Shabaab to firmly embed itself in the regions social, economic and political environment.[^cite_note-105] According to Cocodia, "Al-Shabaab from an objective standpoint is the response of an unlawfully deposed regime employing all means possible to reclaim the authority that was wrenched from it." In the initial years, many Somalis perceived Al-Shabaab as disciplined, orderly, and fair, which earned the group significant legitimacy. However, this perception was later lost due to their arbitrary rulings.[^cite_note-us-53]&#x20;

Most people who had joined Al-Shabaab appeared to be motivated by the behavior of Ethiopian troops rather than an ideological commitment to [Al-Qaeda](1921.md).[^cite_note-\:46-106] Violence and [war crimes](48626.md) against civilians carried out by the Ethiopian military rallied many to support the organization, and over the following two years Al-Shabaab became battle hardened. In this period, the group laid the foundation for an enduring insurgency in Somalia, establishing networks and territorial bases concentrated in the rural south-central regions.[^cite_note-as-99][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEInternational_Crisis_Group2022-54]&#x20;

A critical juncture in transformation of Al-Shabaab was the assassination of the groups military leader [Aden Hashi Ayro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aden_Hashi_Farah_Ayro) in an American airstrike during May 2008. It resulted in several significant developments for Al-Shabaab, most prominently the accession of [Ahmed Godane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Abdi_Godane) to leadership of the organization. Godane moved Al-Shabaab in a far more violent direction, alienating many fighters and civilian supporters. The assassination of Ayro did nothing to prevent the groups expansion.[^cite_note-as-99] The withdrawal of Ethiopian troops in January 2009 significantly diminished the public support that Al-Shabaab had previously enjoyed as a resistance faction.[^cite_note-107] However, this move came too late to have a substantial impact on the group's transformation into a formidable oppositional force.[^cite_note-as-99]&#x20;

### 2009–10: Dominance in the South

See also: [Somali Civil War (2009–present)](21454663.md)

Having made important territorial gains from mid-2008, Al-Shabaab increasingly focused its attention on opposition to the Somali [Transitional Federal Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_Federal_Government_of_Somalia), as the Ethiopian-led war segued into the next phase of the protracted [Somali Civil War](21454663.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEElliotHolzer2009-108] By 2009–10, Al-Shabaab controlled most of central and southern Somalia (south of the semi-autonomous region of [Puntland](233569.md)), and it had formed administrative structures to govern territories under its control.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnzalone2013-109][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBryden2014-110] It had particular success building relationships with local leaders in order to build cross-clan coalitions, combining its principled anti-clannism with a "pragmatic clannism": a willingness to manipulate clan networks and exploit inter-clan politics.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIngiriis2018b-111][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEInternational_Crisis_Group2022-54][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchaefer2011-65][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChonka2016b-89]&#x20;

By 2009, Al-Shabaab started drastically altering its choice of targets and frequency of attacks. The use of [kidnappings](169376.md) and bombings in urban areas significantly grew in use.[^cite_note-as-99] The groups significant support from the [Somali diaspora](9662371.md) dwindled in response to the usage of terror tactics.[^cite_note-na-104]&#x20;

### 2011–13: Internal and external challenges



Over 200 Al-Shabaab fighters surrender to [AMISOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Mission_to_Somalia), September 2012.



Somali and Kenyan troops celebrate Al-Shabaab's retreat from [Kismayo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kismayo_Airport), 2012.

In subsequent years, however, Al-Shabaab's strong position was significantly weakened, as – in the context of a [famine](51329.md) in the region and, simultaneously, a military offensive against the group – the group experienced territorial and strategic setbacks in the military arena; an internal struggle over the group's direction and leadership; and, in response to both, a wave of high-profile [defections](383663.md).&#x20;

#### Territorial losses

The group's military fortunes turned with the failure of the August 2010 [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan) offensive – the inauguration of the [Battle of Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_\(2010%E2%80%932011\)) – which resulted in heavy Al-Shabaab losses.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55] By August 2011, Al-Shabaab had announced a "strategic withdrawal" from [Mogadishu](60154.md), although, from outlying districts, it continued to exert influence in the city and to launch deadly [guerrilla](12720.md) attacks against the AMISOM and the TFG.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnzalone2013-109] In October and November 2011, Kenya and Ethiopia – partnering with local militias – launched offensives against Al-Shabaab strongholds: Kenya's [Operation Linda Nchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Linda_Nchi) on the southern front, while Ethiopia approached from the west. The group lost territory to both the armies, notably losing [Baidoa](1648752.md) to Ethiopia in February 2012 and [losing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kismayo_\(2012\)) the port city (and revenue hub) of [Kismayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kismayo) to Kenya in October 2012.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnzalone2013-109][^cite_note-112] Military pressures on the group were sustained into 2013.&#x20;

#### Drought, al-Qaeda, and internal struggle

Some Al-Shabaab members viewed the 2010 Ramadan offensive as disastrously ill-conceived, and subsequent territorial losses galvanised internal opposition to the leadership of [Ahmed Abdi Godane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Abdi_Godane), who was publicly named the group's emir in December 2007.[^cite_note-113] As [a severe drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_East_Africa_drought) afflicted the region, critics, generally associated with the leadership of [Hassan Dahir Aweys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Dahir_Aweys) and [Mukhtar Robow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukhtar_Robow),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBryden2014-110][^cite_note-114] opposed Al-Shabaab's response to the resulting famine, particularly its obstruction of foreign [humanitarian aid](434679.md) to populations in its territories (see [below](#Humanitarian_access)).[^cite_note-Csfdafbtas-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] More broadly, they argued that the group's authoritarian style of governance, and use of violence, were causing the group to lose public support. Some suggested that these leadership missteps were the result of Godane's lack of clan roots, which they said led him to undervalue the lives both of civilians and of Al-Shabaab fighters.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55]&#x20;

These criticisms became intertwined with a broader and pre-existing dispute over the increasingly globalist flavour of Al-Shabaab's ideology – Godane was among the faction which viewed Somalia as only one battleground of global jihad.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVidinoPantucciKohlmann2010-63][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon2014-67] Godane's announcement in February 2012 of a merger with [al-Qaeda](1921.md) (see [§ al-Qaeda](#al-Qaeda)) thus also met opposition.[^cite_note-da-118][^cite_note-119] Other senior Al-Shabaab members met at a conference in Baidabo,[^cite_note-120] and outlined a policy programme which diverged from Godane's: they rejected Godane's proposal to change the group's name to al-Qaeda in East Africa, and resolved to focus on domestic issues rather than global jihad. They also resolved to establish a regional shura of Islamic clerics, independent of al-Qaeda control.[^cite_note-da-118][^cite_note-121] Godane's rival Aweys declared publicly that "Al-Shabaab and al-Qaeda are merely a small part of the larger Islamic group and al-Qaeda's ideology should not be viewed as the sole, righteous path for Islam."[^cite_note-122]&#x20;



Suspected Al-Shabaab militants in [Mogadishu](60154.md) during a joint operation between Somali forces and AMISOM, May 2014

In 2013, these internal rifts led to internecine violence as Godane effected what was virtually a [purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purge) of his critics.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBryden2014-110] Among those killed were [Ibrahim al-Afghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_al-Afghani) and three other senior commanders, executed in June;[^cite_note-sd-123] and [Omar Hammami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Hammami), killed in September.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019a-124] Journalist Simon Tisdall viewed the September 2013 [Westgate shopping mall shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westgate_shopping_mall_shooting) in [Nairobi](21482.md), Kenya as a reflection of the internal power struggle, with Godane's hardline globalist faction seeking to exert its authority.[^cite_note-125]&#x20;

#### Defections



Al-Shabaab fighters in the city of Barawe in 2013

From mid-to-late 2011, and into 2012, Al-Shabaab faced an increasing number of defections.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnzalone2013-109] It was not the first such wave of defections: in particular, in late 2009, after the Ethiopian departure from Somalia, several leaders had defected to the Somali government, citing complaints about Al-Shabaab's use of [suicide attacks](18835454.md) and executions;[^cite_note-126] its "false interpretations of Islam";[^cite_note-127] and its use of [extortion](162449.md) and attitude towards foreign humanitarian aid.[^cite_note-128] Such defections were viewed as strategically important to Al-Shabaab's adversaries, not only for their propaganda use but because former militants could provide intelligence about Al-Shabaab's combat strategy.[^cite_note-Dnsfs-129] However, according to the AMISOM and the Somali government, the volume of defections in the period around 2012 was unprecedented, and indicated that Al-Shabaab's cohesion and authority were deteriorating: in June 2012, the government said that around 500 Al-Shabaab militants had already defected to it, and that more were doing so every day.[^cite_note-Defecting_2012-130] Al-Shabaab brutalities, which alienated local populations, were again cited as central in motivating defections.[^cite_note-On_the_run_2012-131] On 5 September and 22 September 2012, two large contingents of Al-Shabaab fighters – around 200 each time – surrendered to the government coalition in Afmadow and Garsale respectively.[^cite_note-Dnsfs-129][^cite_note-Aasssaotmdnj-132] Another wave of defections and desertions followed Godane's 2013 purges – Aweys, for example, fled the group in mid-2013.[^cite_note-sd-123]&#x20;

### 2013–2017: Regrouping

Following its territorial losses, Al-Shabaab reverted to the tactics of [asymmetric warfare](46863.md), launching guerrilla attacks on the AMISOM and government positions and territories.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAnzalone2013-109] Southern commander Aweys had announced this new strategy in a media interview shortly after the withdrawal from Mogadishu in August 2011.[^cite_note-133] Al-Shabaab increasingly avoided direct military combat and large battles, in favour of "dictat\[ing] the conflict's pace by undertaking smaller ambushes at locations of its own choosing".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEInternational_Crisis_Group2022-54] The group undertook high-profile attacks outside Somalia such as the [Westgate shopping mall attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westgate_shopping_mall_attack) by four militants left 67 dead - the deadliest attack in Kenya since the [1998 US embassy bombings in Nairobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_United_States_embassy_bombings). That death-toll was exceeded two years later in the Garissa University College attack in which 148 people were killed in an attack targeting Christian students. Furthermore, two sophisticated attempts to target airliners were observed in 2016. In February a device in a laptop bag that had passed screening was detonated mid-flight, killing only the bomber, and in March another laptop bomb exploded during screening. Reacting to apparent advances in the group's bomb-making capabilities, the UN Security Council later prohibited the transfer of bomb components to Somalia. The group has also developed a strategy of maintaining a "semi-territorial presence" in key regions and parts of key cities: while it no longer had exclusive and military authority over substantial territory, it retained a sporadic presence, and therefore significant influence, in many places.[^cite_note-yn-134][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDoboš2016-135]&#x20;



Territorial control of Somalia as of April 2017

Nonetheless, by 2016, Al-Shabaab was viewed as significantly weakened by the military campaign against it, with earlier losses compounded in 2014–15 by [Operation Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Indian_Ocean), a joint endeavour by the AMISOM, the Somali government, and the United States.[^cite_note-yn-134][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJonesLiepmanChandler2016-136] The Somali government claimed in October 2014 that over 700 Al-Shabaab militants had surrendered since September, when it had extended an [amnesty](18940624.md) offer to them.[^cite_note-137] Reports of further senior defections continued into 2015.[^cite_note-Welsis-138][^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141] Moreover, an [American drone strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_military_intervention_in_Somalia_\(2007%E2%80%93present\)) killed Godane on 1 September 2014;[^cite_note-142] he was succeeded as Al-Shabaab leader by [Ahmad Diri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Diri), who took office the same week.[^cite_note-143] Other senior members were killed in armed clashes or by American drone strikes in 2014 and 2015.[^cite_note-Ucdskalis-144][^cite_note-Gsgwtdoaslyd-145][^cite_note-Ucosistal-146][^cite_note-Gctgfgkarl-147] According to some reports, since Godane's death, the group has placed less weight on global jihad than on local grievances.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBryden2014-110][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPetrich2022-68]&#x20;

### 2018–2022: Resurgence

In October 2017, more than 500 people were killed by [a suicide truck bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14_October_2017_Mogadishu_bombings) in Mogadishu, including many children, provoking domestic and international outrage. Al-Shabaab did not claim responsibility for the attack but was suspected of involvement.[^cite_note-148] In December 2019, [another suicide truck bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_2019_Mogadishu_bombing) marked the beginning of a series of Al-Shabaab attacks on the capital city, which continued into 2022.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151] Al-Shabaab also targeted American military personnel in [an attack on a Kenyan base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Simba_attack) in January 2020.[^cite_note-152] By 2020, the group's strategy of semi-territorialism allowed it operate freely in much of rural Somalia, with its primary base in the [Jubba River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jubba_River) Valley,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarnett2020-56] although air strikes against its leaders continued;[^cite_note-153] and it has recently won military successes against the government.[^cite_note-154] It had also expanded its operations in Puntland,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHorton2017-155][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFelbab-Brown2017-156] prompting a military offensive by Puntland forces in 2021.[^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158] As of July 2022, Al-Shabaab is generally considered to be "resurgent",[^cite_note-159] a situation partly enabled by a reduction in the number of American air strikes,[^cite_note-160] and possibly motivated by competition with [Islamic State in Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_in_Somalia), which has been conducting its own expansionary campaign.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGlobal_Interagency_Security_Forum2016-161]&#x20;

On 20 July 2022, [Al-Shabaab launched an invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shabaab_invasion_of_Ethiopia) into Ethiopia's [Somali Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Region).[^cite_note-lwj1-162][^cite_note-163] Taking advantage of the ongoing [Tigray War](65760352.md), the goal of the operation was to establish a presence for the group within southern [Ethiopia](187749.md). The incursion began with over a thousand Al-Shabaab fighters staging diversionary attacks on four [Ethiopian-Somali border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopia%E2%80%93Somalia_border) towns in order to allow a force of 500 to 800 fighters to penetrate the Ethiopian security zone and advance into the region, who then advanced 150 km into the region.[^cite_note-164] After two weeks of intense clashes and airstrikes, the ENDF and the Somali Region security forces began to reassert control.[^cite_note-165] A [battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion) of around 500 Al-Shabaab fighters succeeded in evading the Ethiopian army and reached its main target, the [Bale Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bale_Mountains).[^cite_note-166]&#x20;

### 2022–2024: Escalation

In August 2022, Somali President Hassan Sheikh Mohamud declared "total war" against Al-Shabaab during a televised address after the group carried out a deadly [hotel attack in Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2022_Mogadishu_attack) and also announced that the Somalia military had regained territory from Al-Shabaab in the central Galmudug state and the Southwest state.[^cite_note-167] By September 2022 Somali and the [ATMIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATMIS) offensive operations against Al-Shabaab, with U.S. support, would escalate to the highest point in five years.[^cite_note-atmissomali-168] The operation, which is considered a combined Somali-ATMIS offensive,[^cite_note-atmissomali-168] began in August 2022 and, with assistance from U.S. airstrikes, has been focused on disrupting Al-Shabaab activity in Somalia's central [Hiraan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiraan) region.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-atmissomali-168] Other Al-Shabaab-controlled areas in southern Somalia have been targeted by the Somalia military as well.[^cite_note-170] On 20 September 2022, as the military operation progressed, a statement was released by the Somali Ministry of Information which revealed that President Sheikh would not offer any other option than surrender for Al-Shabaab members.[^cite_note-171]&#x20;

On 29 October 2022, Al-Shabaab took responsibility for [a twin car bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Somali_Ministry_of_Education_bombings) that occurred in Mogadishu targeting the [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(Somalia\)). The attacks killed 121 people and injured 333, making it the deadliest at the time since 2017.[^cite_note-172]&#x20;

On 26 May 2023, Al-Shabaab claimed responsibility for [an attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Buulo_Mareer) that reportedly killed 137 soldiers in the [Buulo Mareer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buulo_Mareer) base, just 75 mi (121 km) south-west of the capital Mogadishu. Meanwhile, the Ugandan President [Yoweri Museveni](293409.md) stated that the number is closer to 54 African Union peacekeepers stationed in Somalia.[^cite_note-173]&#x20;

In December 2024, the UN Security Council approved a resolution to establish the [African Union Support and Stabilization Mission in Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Support_and_Stabilization_Mission_in_Somalia) (AUSSOM), a streamlined successor to the [African Union Transition Mission in Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Union_Transition_Mission_in_Somalia) (ATMIS), which ends in 2024. The AUSSOM will deploy up to 12,626 personnel to support Somalia's fight against the Al-Shabaab insurgency.[^cite_note-174]&#x20;

On 26 December 2024, the militant group of Al-Shabaab confirmed the death of Mohamed Mire Jama,[^cite_note-175] a senior leader of the group, who had been designated as a terrorist by the [United States](3434750.md),[^cite_note-176] in an American [drone strike](38212231.md) near [Kunyo Barrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunyo_Barrow) of the [Lower Shabelle](185139.md) region in the [South West State of Somalia](483484.md).[^cite_note-177]&#x20;

### 2025–present: Military offensive

Main article: [2025 Shabelle offensive](79290890.md)

In early 2025, Al-Shabaab launched a large-scale military [offensive](15077993.md), capturing dozens of towns and vast areas of territory. In its most ambitious operation in years, the group's ultimate objective is to seize [Mogadishu](60154.md) and overthrow the federal government. Probing attacks that began in January escalated into major offensives on multiple fronts, stretching government troops and auxiliary clan militias to their breaking point. By April the military situation had deteriorated to the extent that waves of rumors were circulating on [social media](5897742.md) of an impending insurgent assault on the capital. While this did not occur, an attack was carried out on the [motorcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcade) of Somali President [Hassan Sheikh Mohamud](36973989.md) while it was driving through the city. [Airstrikes](323167.md) by international actors and sporadic local [counterattacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterattack) have slowed the advance.[^cite_note-178]&#x20;

According to *[The Telegraph](23797577.md)*, as the group has gained major ground this year, Al-Shabaab has sought to implement a "[hearts and minds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearts_and_Minds_\(Vietnam_War\))" strategy, aiming to shed its reputation for repression and for carrying out suicide bombings that frequently kill civilians. Large-scale and indiscriminate [suicide attacks](18835454.md) in Mogadishu, once capable of killing hundreds, have largely ceased. Analysts have compared the campaign to the [Taliban's 2021 offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive) in [Afghanistan](737.md), noting that Al-Shabaab and the [Taliban](30635.md) are "very structurally, militarily and ideologically similar." The internationally recognized governments of both Somalia and Afghanistan have been described by observers as products of the [war on terror](13425800.md), plagued by [corruption](3608404.md), predatory governance, and low public legitimacy.[^cite_note-179]&#x20;

## Propaganda strategy



Drawings left by fighters on the walls of a building in [El Baraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Baraf)&#x20;

Although Al-Shabaab has disseminated its propaganda by various media, the bulk of its engagement with Somalis in rural areas is either face-to-face or by radio broadcast.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrobbelaar2022-180] Face-to-face, the group holds seminars on [Islamic jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiqh) and community meetings on such matters such as grain and livestock distribution.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrobbelaar2022-180] It operates its own radio station, [Radio Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_al-Andalus), mainly operated using [relay stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_relay_station) and other equipment seized from private radio stations, including the [BBC](37627.md).[^cite_note-media-181] Presenters broadcast in Somali, Arabic, [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language) and English.[^cite_note-media-181] In April 2010, Al-Shabaab banned the BBC and [Voice of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_America) broadcasts on Somali radio, objecting to what they alleged was Christian propaganda.[^cite_note-182] Also in 2010, and prior to its expulsion from Mogadishu the following year, Al-Shabaab launched a television news channel, [al-Kataib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Kataib_Media_Foundation) News.[^cite_note-183] The group has also been known to conduct [military parades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_parade) in its territories, as a show of force.[^cite_note-184]&#x20;

### Propaganda films

Al-Shabaab began creating [propaganda films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_film) early in its campaign against Ethiopian forces,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrobbelaar2022-180] produced by its dedicated media wing, [al-Kataib Media Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Kataib_Media_Foundation).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMenkhaus2014-185]&#x20;

Since 2009, Al-Shabaab's films have become noticeably more "professional", both in their production quality and in their messaging, reportedly with direct support from al-Qaeda's As-Sahab Media Foundation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMenkhaus2014-185] The early films were distributed primarily, and widely, online, and were primarily used as tools for recruitment, particularly among foreign jihadists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrobbelaar2022-180] More recent films show Al-Shabaab distributing humanitarian aid and participating in other community or religious activities – though others are much more gruesome, displaying the corpses of beheaded members alleged to have been spies.[^cite_note-media-181][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChonka2016a-186] Propaganda films are also occasionally used to attempt to mobilise jihadi activity abroad: in October 2013, one film encouraged British jihadists to follow the example set by [Lee Rigby's killers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Lee_Rigby),[^cite_note-aljazeera-187] while a February 2015 film called (without effect) for attacks on shopping malls in Canada, the United Kingdom, and the United States, including the [West Edmonton Mall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Edmonton_Mall) in [Alberta](717.md), Canada, and the [Mall of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mall_of_America) in [Bloomington, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomington,_Minnesota).[^cite_note-Nittwemvrs-188][^cite_note-189]&#x20;

### Internet and social media

Al-Shabaab was an early adopter among African insurgents of the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet), which it uses to distribute its propaganda videos and for various other propaganda functions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrobbelaar2022-180] Especially in its early years, it used online chatrooms and discussion forums, encouraging foreign fighters and even military commanders to post updates and field public questions about the state of the jihad.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMenkhaus2014-185] Particularly prominent was American-born Omar Shafik Hammami, also known as [Abu Mansoor al-Amriki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Mansoor_Al-Amriki), who for many years kept a [video blog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlog) about his life in Al-Shabaab and who, from 2009, also created and posted [raps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapping) about jihad.[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMair2017-191] Al-Shabaab also had an official website, which carried official statements and news – including sundry edicts and threats – and religious guidance. Since the site was closed in 2009, it has distributed its press releases and videos using other sympathetic websites, or, more commonly, using [social media](5897742.md) networks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrobbelaar2022-180][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMenkhaus2014-185]&#x20;

Indeed, although Al-Shabaab spokesmen occasionally grant interviews to "carefully selected" local and foreign journalists,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMenkhaus2014-185] social media networks have provided an important channel for interaction with the press, as well as for disseminating information and building support. Al-Shabaab has used [Facebook](7529378.md), particularly to communicate with the [Somali diaspora](9662371.md);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMenkhaus2014-185] and it has made prolific and innovative use of [Twitter](9988187.md) since late 2011,[^cite_note-bt-192] although its first accounts were active as early as 2009.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMolony2019-193] Online engagement with Al-Shabaab surged during Operation Linda Nchi, the Kenyan offensive of 2011–12, when Al-Shabaab used Twitter, under the handle @HSMPress, to urge Somalis to take up arms against the Kenyan forces[^cite_note-Aljazeera-194] and to portray its own military losses as tactical retreats.[^cite_note-195] In an extreme example of the latter in late 2011, Al-Shabaab photos purportedly showed several dozen AMISOM casualties – but, according to an [African Union](10634933.md) spokesperson, in fact showed Al-Shabaab's own casualties dressed in their adversaries' uniforms.[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197] What received most attention, however, were tweets Al-Shabaab posted mocking the [Kenya Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Defence_Forces) (KDF) and sparring with Major Emmanuel Chirchir, then the KDF's official spokesman.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMolony2019-193][^cite_note-NYT_HSMPress_story-198] Responding to Chirchir's claim that Kismayo had been captured by the KDF, Al-Shabaab tweeted that the KDF "boys are a grotesque parody of an army! \[Al-Shabaab] can outpace ur [world-class runners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kenyan_records_in_athletics) by far. Indeed, they 'Run like a Kenyan'".[^cite_note-HSMPress_Kenyan_running_tweet-199] Showing uncharacteristic levity, Al-Shabaab also suggested by a tweet that it meet a UN official for "a [caramel macchiato](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Caramel_macchiato\&action=edit\&redlink=1)".[^cite_note-HSMPress_caramel_macchiato_tweet-200]&#x20;

Most of Al-Shabaab's tweets are in English, suggesting that they are intended for a foreign audience.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoble2011-201] In 2011, officials in the United States, where Twitter is based, said they were considering having the account closed, but had legal and [free speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_speech) concerns.[^cite_note-NYT_official_concerns_about_Twitter_story-202] Chirchir himself tweeted that such a move would be counterproductive, as "Al-Shabaab needs to be engaged positively, and Twitter is the only avenue."[^cite_note-Chirchir_response_tweet-203] Nonetheless, in January 2013, Twitter suspended Al-Shabaab's English-language account. Observers guessed that the suspension was a response to the account having issued [death threats](13262036.md) against Kenyan [hostages](146794.md) and against the French spy [Denis Allex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulo_Marer_hostage_rescue_attempt), followed in the latter case by confirmation that the execution had taken place.[^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205] A new English-language account, opened in February 2013,[^cite_note-Aqgibotab-206] was closed in September. This suspension also followed an apparent violation of Twitter's rules: Al-Shabaab had recently used the account to claim responsibility for an unsuccessful [ambush](1905.md) of a convoy carrying Somali President [Hassan Sheikh Mohamud](36973989.md), and to warn that, "next time, you won't be as lucky".[^cite_note-Sastasa-207]&#x20;

Al-Shabaab relaunched its English-language Twitter account once again on 11 September 2013.[^cite_note-Asasbotas-208] Two weeks later, the group gained notoriety for [live-tweeting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liveblogging) the 2013 Westgate shopping mall attack, "justifying the attack, creating fictional threats, providing news on hostages and mocking the police and military response".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMair2017-191] The account, which then had 15,000 or more [followers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Following_\(social_networking\)), was [retweeted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retweet) several million times before it was shut down by Twitter.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrobbelaar2022-180][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMenkhaus2014-185] And after the @HSMPress Twitter account was deleted, the live updates continued from other, new accounts: over the course of the attack, which lasted several days, at least eight different Al-Shabaab-affiliated Twitter accounts were active.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGrobbelaar2022-180] Al-Shabaab had opened a new Twitter account by December that year, with an official telling [Al Jazeera](30204052.md) that, "The aim is to vigorously challenge [defamatory](28661.md) reports in the media by presenting an accurate portrayal of the current state of Jihad in Somalia and countering Western, state-sponsored propaganda machines".[^cite_note-bt-192] It has since tweeted, from various accounts, during other attacks;[^cite_note-209] Facebook, too, has had difficulty expeditiously removing graphic Al-Shabaab content when it appears on newly created accounts.[^cite_note-210] The group is adept in using intricately structured networks of amplifier accounts, leveraging algorithms and emerging technologies, to disseminate its messages.&#x20;

## Local governance strategy



Residents of Tortoro celebrate with [Lower Shabelle](185139.md) governor Abdulkadir Mohamed Sidi after the town's liberation from Al-Shabaab, June 2015.

In territories it holds, Al-Shabaab typically maintains "limited but effective" administrative control over resident populations,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEInternational_Crisis_Group2022-54] providing services – such as [dispute resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispute_resolution) through Sharia-based courts – which are sometimes compared favourably to those offered in government-held territories.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarnett2020-56][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESkjelderup2020-211][^cite_note-Kluijver-212] Somalis have been observed to travel out of government-controlled areas into Al-Shabaab territory to settle disputes, particularly involving clan dynamics, lacking trust in the official institutions.[^cite_note-213] Al-Shabaab claimed some credit for Somalia's excellent [crop yield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crop_yield) in early 2010, saying that [Somali grain production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Somalia) had flourished due to Al-Shabaab's reduction of food imports, and that the policy had [redistributed income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redistribution_of_income_and_wealth) to the poor, rural Somali farmers.[^cite_note-214] However, the group has also allegedly committed widespread [human rights](13831.md) abuses against the populations in its territories, including through a brutal interpretation and application of Islamic jurisprudence on [hudud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudud).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESkjelderup2014-215][^cite_note-216]&#x20;

### Humanitarian access

&#x20;Despite routinely expelling, attacking, and harassing aid workers, Al-Shabaab permits some agencies to work in areas under its control. At the height of its territorial control it implemented a system of aid agency regulation, taxation and surveillance. Where the agencies are allowed to operate, this is often due to the desire of Al-Shabaab to coopt and materially and politically benefit from the provision of aid and services.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJacksonAynte2013-217] Senior aid agency representatives often strongly rejected claims that they talked with Al-Shabaab, while aid workers working in Al-Shabaab-controlled areas often reported they directly negotiated with the group out of necessity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJackson2014-218] Complaints made against the group include its attacks on aid workers. According to the journalist [Jon Lee Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Lee_Anderson):

> The number of people in Somalia who are dependent on international food aid has tripled since 2007, to an estimated 3.6 million. But there is no permanent foreign expatriate presence in southern Somalia, because the Shabaab has declared war on the UN and on Western non-governmental organizations. International relief supplies are flown or shipped into the country and distributed, wherever possible, through local relief workers. Insurgents routinely attack and murder them, too; forty-two have been killed in the past two years alone.[^cite_note-MostFailedNYer-219]

In 2009, Godane imposed an Al-Shabaab ban on the UN [World Food Programme](33582.md) and Western agencies in Somalia. The ban was opposed by other senior members, including Robow and Aweys, but Godane overruled them.[^cite_note-Csfdafbtas-115][^cite_note-220]&#x20;

In response to the [2011 Eastern Africa drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Eastern_Africa_drought), which lasted until early 2012,[^cite_note-221] Al-Shabaab announced in July 2011 that it had withdrawn its restrictions on international humanitarian workers.[^cite_note-UN_News_July_13,_2011-222] The group also adapted its propaganda strategy to accommodate the changing circumstances. In some cases, group members employed humanitarian aid as a recruitment tool, using relief supplies as bribes and as an incentive to join the militants, whose numbers had decreased due to casualties and defections.[^cite_note-223] The group members dismissed the UN declaration of famine in various regions as grossly exaggerated and banned various organizations from providing aid to those regions.[^cite_note-224] In response, in August 2011, Somali Prime Minister [Abdiweli Mohamed Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdiweli_Mohamed_Ali) announced the establishment of a new 300-man security force, which, assisted by the AMISOM, was tasked with protecting aid convoys from Al-Shabaab and with securing [IDP](427408.md) camps while relief was being distributed.[^cite_note-Pmasuapf-225] Although fighting disrupted aid delivery in some areas, humanitarian access to Al-Shabaab-controlled areas improved, and a scaling up of relief operations in mid-November prompted the UN to downgrade the humanitarian situation in several regions from famine to emergency levels.[^cite_note-Rfrisbwba-226]&#x20;

### Ban on single-use plastic bags

In July 2018, Al-Shabaab announced a complete ban on [single-use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposable_product) [plastic bags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_bag) within its territory in a broadcast in which it stated that they "pose a serious threat to the well-being of humans and animals alike", and in the same announcement, it also imposed a complete ban on the logging of several species of rare trees.[^cite_note-227][^cite_note-Kluijver-212] Some have argued that, whilst these environmentally-conscious advances are welcome, they are overshadowed by the group's terrorist activities, whilst others have mocked the United States and other countries for taking less action on climate change than a terrorist group.[^cite_note-228]&#x20;

### Response to COVID-19

Al-Shabaab acknowledged the existence of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and announced the formation of a corona virus prevention and treatment committee.[^cite_note-229] In mid-June 2020, the group announced that it had set up a coronavirus treatment centre in [Jilib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jilib), about 380 km (236 mi) south of the capital, Mogadishu.[^cite_note-230]&#x20;

## Foreign recruitment

Al-Shabaab is noteworthy in the region for its extensive recruitment of foreign fighters (see [list below](#Foreign_leaders_and_members)).[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-Rspiaamgga-232] These foreign recruits include members of the Somali diaspora or ethnic [Somalis](1571696.md) overseas; citizens of other [East African](260987.md) countries; and citizens of countries further afield, including in the [Middle East](19323.md) and the [West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West).[^cite_note-ff-233][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVidinoPantucciKohlmann2010-63] Non-Somali fighters have occasionally orchestrated high-profile Al-Shabaab attacks, notably the [2010 Kampala bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Kampala_bombings) and [2019 DusitD2 complex attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairobi_DusitD2_complex_attack) in [Nairobi](21482.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconEllisMilton2021-234] However, of greater importance to Al-Shabaab are the combat experience and specialised skills of some, usually [Arab](2185.md), foreign fighters,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShinn2010-235] which have been linked to Al-Shabaab's increased sophistication in producing explosives,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019a-124] improved [sniper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sniper) capability,[^cite_note-ff-233] and increased propensity to use suicide attacks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55] Foreign fighters may also have access to networks which improve Al-Shabaab's capacity to recruit and operate in the region, and have made useful contributions to Al-Shabaab's propaganda campaign: early Western recruits helped produce Al-Shabaab's first English-language propaganda, and, thanks to its East African membership, Swahili remains the second most common language in Al-Shabaab publications.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019a-124][^cite_note-Al_Shabaab-236] Foreign fighters also have symbolic importance for Al-Shabaab propaganda: Al-Shabaab has intentionally played up the diversity of its recruits as a paean to its [pan-Islamism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Islamism) – that is, to demonstrate that it transcends clannism and nationalism, embodying a united transnational [ummah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ummah).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019a-124]&#x20;

### Early surge



[Abu Mansoor Al-Amriki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Mansoor_Al-Amriki), one of the earliest foreign fighters, seen planning an ambush against an [ENDF](9405.md) convoy alongside [Mukhtar Robow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukhtar_Robow), the current religious affairs minister of the [Federal Government](36817379.md), during the [Ethiopian military occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Somalia_\(2006%E2%80%932009\)) (15 July 2008)

Foreign recruitment peaked in the period between 2011 and 2013,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019a-124] which was also the period in which the phenomenon received the most attention. The 2006 Ethiopian invasion of Somalia attracted foreign volunteers to Al-Shabaab's cause,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVidinoPantucciKohlmann2010-63] and, as of 2010, Al-Shabaab included an estimated 200–300 foreign fighters, not including a further 1,000 ethnic Somalis from the diaspora.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShinn2010-235] In subsequent years, Al-Shabaab recruitment became a concern for the governments of both the United Kingdom and the United States. At a security conference in London in 2010, the former head of [MI5](100076.md), [Jonathan Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Evans_\(MI5_officer\)), said that "a significant number of U.K. residents" were training with Al-Shabaab;[^cite_note-237] the precise number of British recruits was estimated at between 50[^cite_note-238] and 100[^cite_note-Ttbfanwotamw-239] persons. There was also evidence that the group had received funding from Somali residents in Britain.[^cite_note-Burleigh-240] Between 2012 and 2013, several British citizens faced [control orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_order) or even criminal charges related to association with Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-241][^cite_note-242]&#x20;

Sweden, Denmark, Canada, and the United States similarly encountered evidence of citizens' affiliation with Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEVidinoPantucciKohlmann2010-63] In the United States, the [U.S. Senate Homeland Security Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Homeland_Security_and_Governmental_Affairs) heard as early as 2009 – from [Michael Leiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Leiter), director of the [National Counterterrorism Center](3231506.md) – that American and other foreign fighters were being recruited and trained by Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-counterterrorism-66] In 2011, the [House Committee on Homeland Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Committee_on_Homeland_Security) reported that more than 40 [Muslim Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Americans) and 20 Canadians had fought with Al-Shabaab, and that at least 15 of those volunteers had been killed in Somalia.[^cite_note-243] Later that year, a U.S. military official told the *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)* that about 30 U.S. citizens were Al-Shabaab fighters.[^cite_note-pk-244] This was a resumption of an earlier trend of Al-Shabaab recruitment among Americans, which previously had peaked in 2007–08.[^cite_note-nytimes2-245] Also in 2011, two [Somali Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Americans) in Minnesota were convicted of illegally financing Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-pk-244] By mid-2013, observers believed that recruitment of U.S. citizens had subsided.[^cite_note-Mrosayahsuc-246]&#x20;



American-born [Jehad Mostafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehad_Mostafa) is a senior Al-Shabaab commander.

In general, foreign activity in Al-Shabaab has decreased, first, and perhaps most importantly, because the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) became [the focal point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_fighters_in_the_Syrian_civil_war) for foreign jihadist recruitment networks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019a-124] Other factors, however, are more specific to Al-Shabaab. One factor was the group's internal struggle of 2011–2013: as tensions between Somali and non-Somali members increased, Godane scapegoated the foreigners as the cause of the group's disunity and, during his 2013 purge, executed various foreign recruits (see [above](#Drought,_al-Qaeda,_and_internal_struggle)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019a-124][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55] In addition to serving as a disincentive to foreign fighters, this shift reduced the influence of the group's remaining foreign leaders.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBryden2014-110] Moreover, whereas the ascendancy of Godane and his faction had partly internationalized Al-Shabaab, in subsequent years, Al-Shabaab became (re-)"domesticated": as it made territorial gains, its attention was increasingly consumed by local governance and the management of clan dynamics, activities in which foreign fighters – frequently mistrusted by local populations – lacked utility and influence.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55] As the conflict, and Al-Shabaab's priorities, became more localized and clan-based, they also held less appeal for foreign fighters themselves.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019a-124] Foreign fighters also attracted the counter-terrorism efforts of foreign countries, and were sometimes suspected of disloyalty and spying by their Somali peers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55]&#x20;

### Current status

As of 2020, estimates of the number of foreign recruits still active in Al-Shabaab ranged from 450 to 2,000.[^cite_note-ff-233] Most of those recruits are from East Africa – primarily Kenya,[^cite_note-Fjacliyk-247] but also Tanzania and Uganda, all countries in which Al-Shabaab recruitment networks are well entrenched.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEInternational_Crisis_Group2022-54][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019a-124] The flow of recruits from [Arab](2185.md) countries and the West has diminished substantially since 2014, except for in the case of [Yemen](350939.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconEllisMilton2021-234][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019a-124] Moreover, few foreign fighters hold strategically important roles in the group. Many are among the rank-and-file of the military wing.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019a-124][^cite_note-Anbotib-248] Those in leadership positions tend to have prominent, but strategically marginal or mid-level, roles – for example, in managing the group's public relations or external relations, or in supervising and training other foreign recruits.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconMuibu2019b-55] (Godane's nationalist critics, however, argue that earlier, under his leadership, foreign fighters did in fact gain significant influence over the group.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESolomon2014-67]) Since 2013, Al-Shabaab has also established two external military units, on Godane's initiative: one, in [Ethiopia](187749.md), has struggled, but the other, in the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes) region, has carried out several attacks in [Kenya](188171.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaconEllisMilton2021-234]&#x20;

## Relationship with other militant groups

### Hizbul Islam

Between 2010 and 2012, [Hizbul Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hizbul_Islam), a smaller group of Somali militants,[^cite_note-249] was merged with Al-Shabaab: after suffering military defeats against Al-Shabaab in southern towns, Hizbul Islam was officially absorbed by its former rival in December 2010.[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251] However, on 24 September 2012, Hizbul Islam announced that it would split from Al-Shabaab, claiming the union had only ever been nominal and that the factions' political philosophies had differed irreconcilably. Hizbul Islam said that it welcomed "negotiations with all groups for the interest of Somalia", and added that Al-Shabaab had been considerably "weakened" in recent years.[^cite_note-Gshigwasasiw-252][^cite_note-Shiswas-253]&#x20;

### Affiliates in Kenya

Al-Shabaab has Kenya-based affiliates, with [al-Hijra](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Al-Hijra\&action=edit\&redlink=1) as its official Kenyan wing from 2012, then with a unit established in 2013 known as [Jaysh Ayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaysh_Ayman), based primarily in the Boni Forest. While the former group was used to carry out minor attacks within Kenya, the Al-Shabaab leadership later switched its focus to Jaysh Ayman which proved more effective. Jaysh Ayman comprises both Somali and Kenyan fighters as well as fighters from overseas.&#x20;

### al-Qaeda

Al-Shabab members pledged allegiance to [al-Qaeda](1921.md) in 2012 and has reportedly forged ties with [al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Islamic_Maghreb), and [al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Arabian_Peninsula). Before 2012, al-Qaeda and Al-Shabaab had what academic [Daniel Byman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Byman) calls an "on-again, off-again" relationship, though both groups praised each other online in 2008.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEByman2012-254][^cite_note-255] During this early period, Al-Shabaab occasionally harboured al-Qaeda leaders in Somalia, in exchange for their technical assistance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEByman2012-254][^cite_note-qn-256] In September 2009, in a video entitled "At Your Service Osama", Al-Shabaab publicly declared allegiance to al-Qaeda leader [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden) – but this reflected an "unrequited courtship", largely ignored by al-Qaeda.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELahoud2012-257] However, after Osama bin Laden's death and replacement by [Ayman al-Zawahiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayman_al-Zawahiri), Al-Shabaab became an official al-Qaeda affiliate in February 2012. In a fifteen-minute video message released on 9 February, Al-Shabaab leader Godane pledged allegiance to al-Qaeda and Aymam al-Zawahiri on behalf of Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-AlQaeda-258][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas2013-259] Ayman al-Zawahiri confirmed the alliance,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELahoud2012-257] which he said would "please the believers and disturb the disbelievers" and "support the jihadi unity against the [Zio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism)-[Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) campaign and their assistants amongst the treacherous agent rulers."[^cite_note-260] The announcement was met with internal opposition among some factions of Al-Shabaab (see [§ Drought, al-Qaeda, and internal struggle](#Drought,_al-Qaeda,_and_internal_struggle)).&#x20;

By late 2012, the groups cooperated closely in various arenas, especially indoctrination and training, both in basic [infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry) skills and in advanced explosives and assassination – after the merger, a corps of al-Qaeda-trained militants moved to Somalia to train members of Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEByman2012-254] However, Al-Shabaab remained "largely independent".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEByman2012-254] Similarly, although Al-Shabaab has publicly exploited the al-Qaeda "brand", echoing al-Qaeda ideology in its recruitment videos,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEByman2012-254] it is unclear whether the alliance has affected Al-Shabaab's behaviour and aims on the ground.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarrington2021-84] A 2021 statistical analysis suggests that it has not significantly affected Al-Shabaab's military strategy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEOnatGulerHsuReyes2021-261] While an Al-Shabaab commander has claimed that the group will bring jihad first to East Africa "and afterward, with God's will, to America",[^cite_note-262] an analyst at the [Center for Strategic and International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Strategic_and_International_Studies) argues that the group is far more interested in establishing an Islamic State inside Somalia than in carrying out attacks in the West.[^cite_note-Atgstcus-263]&#x20;

### AQAP, AQIM and Boko Haram

In June 2012, General [Carter Ham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Ham) of the [U.S. Army](32087.md) said that Al-Shabaab, [al-Qaeda in Islamic Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_Islamic_Maghreb) (AQIM), and the Nigeria-based [Boko Haram](23769768.md) were attempting to coordinate their activities, including in funding, training, and explosives.[^cite_note-Atgstcus-263] In 2011, Boko Haram's [first suicide bomb attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Abuja_police_headquarters_bombing) had been preceded by a public statement warning that "our jihadists have arrived in [Nigeria](21383.md) from Somalia where they received real training on warfare from our brethren who made that country ungovernable", which analysts understood as an allusion to cooperation with Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-qn-256][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELulie2013-264] However, in the view of some observers, most consequential are Al-Shabaab's links to [al-Qaeda in the Arabian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Arabian_Peninsula) (AQAP).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMuibu2017-265][^cite_note-266] AQAP is based in Yemen, which has longstanding ties to Somalia, and analyst Katherine Zimmerman told the [U.S. Congress](31756.md) that AQAP "almost certainly provided the equipment or the expertise" for Al-Shabaab's [2016 laptop bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daallo_Airlines_Flight_159).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMuibu2017-265]&#x20;

We have been ignoring their wicked behaviors for some time to give them a chance to change, but they have continued their wrongheadedness. Our senior command has ordered our fighters to attack and eliminate the 'disease' of IS and uproot the tree that would be used to undermine the fruits of the [Jihad](16203.md).&#x20;

### Islamic State

In early 2015, the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) (ISIL) published online the first of a series of videos aimed at Al-Shabaab, calling on the group to switch allegiances from al-Qaeda to ISIL and its leader [Abu Bakr al-Baghdadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Bakr_al-Baghdadi).[^cite_note-268] These public appeals had reportedly been preceded by informal ISIL attempts to establish a relationship with Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWarnerWeiss2017-269] Yet Al-Shabaab publicly ignored the overtures.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWarnerWeiss2017-269] By September 2015, it had issued an internal memo, directed at pro-ISIL elements in its ranks, reaffirming the group's allegiance to al-Qaeda and banning any discussion about ISIL. The group also detained several fighters who had voiced support for ISIL.[^cite_note-270]&#x20;

In October 2015, senior Al-Shabaab commander [Abdul Qadir Mumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Qadir_Mumin) and approximately 20 of his followers in Puntland pledged allegiance to ISIL,[^cite_note-271] establishing what became [Islamic State in Somalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_in_Somalia) (ISIS).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeiss2019-267] Further defections in Al-Shabaab ranks occurred in the border region between [Somalia](27358.md) and northern [Kenya](188171.md),[^cite_note-272] leading the head of Al-Shabaab in the [Lower Shabelle](185139.md) region, Abu Abdalla, to announce that all pro-ISIL members should leave the group or be killed.[^cite_note-voa-273] The groups clashed violently on several occasions, most fiercely during 2015, and Al-Shabaab's internal security service continued to arrest, and sometimes execute, suspected ISIS supporters within Al-Shabaab's ranks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeiss2019-267]&#x20;

Violent conflict between the groups was reinvigorated in 2018 by ISIS provocation, and, in December, Al-Shabaab's spokesman announced an offensive – [code-named](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_name) Operation Disease Eradication – against ISIS.[^cite_note-274] The same week, Al-Shabaab's general command released an 8-page treatise rebuking ISIS's ideology and listing crimes it had committed under the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWeiss2019-267]&#x20;

## Sources of income and arms

In 2020, the Hiraal Institute estimated that Al-Shabaab collected at least \$15 million in revenue every month, implying revenue on a similar scale to the government's;[^cite_note-sc-275] and the UN estimates that the group's military budget was approximately \$21 million in 2019.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarrington2021-84] In the period after 2014, Al-Shabaab established its own Ministry of Finance,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDathan2017-276] and it has cultivated various revenue streams, among which it switches as its military position or political circumstances change.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyYusuf2021-277]&#x20;

### External support

During its early years, Al-Shabaab received substantial external support in donations from the Somali diaspora or interested international jihadists,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyYusuf2021-277] enabled by the weakness of the government of Somalia's [financial regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_regulation) framework and the ubiquitous use of the [hawala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawala) system for receipt of [remittances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance).[^cite_note-278] Although Al-Shabaab initially appeared as a promising alternative to the Islamic Courts Union, its popularity with diaspora donors declined after the Ethiopian withdrawal and as the group gained a reputation for brutality.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyYusuf2021-277] Concurrently, however, Al-Shabaab increased its ties to other jihadist groups, especially al-Qaeda, which have extensive capacity to generate income in the [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md) and the [Horn of Africa](62142.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyYusuf2021-277] For example, years before Al-Shabaab became an official al-Qaeda affiliate, Osama bin Laden publicly called on Muslims to send money to Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEByman2012-254] However, over the past decade, the counter-terrorism efforts of foreign governments have obstructed international funding sources.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyYusuf2021-277]&#x20;

According to authorities, the Somali state of Puntland is a key transit point for weapons shipments into Somalia from foreign countries – particularly frequent shipments of [small arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_arms_trade) and ammunition from Yemen, typically transported across the [Gulf of Aden](58716.md) in [skiffs](1913729.md), and occasionally larger shipments from the [Makran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makran) coast of Iran.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBahadur2020-279][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations_Security_Council2017-280][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations2019-281] Puntland authorities have also seized Yemeni explosives shipments, which they suspect are the result of cooperation between Al-Shabaab and the Yemen-based AQAP.[^cite_note-282]&#x20;

### Alleged Eritrean support

In December 2009, adopting [Resolution 1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1907), the UN Security Council imposed sanctions on [Eritrea](17238590.md), accusing the country of arming and financing militia groups, including Al-Shabaab, in southern Somalia's conflict zones.[^cite_note-Erunribsr-283] Reports in 2010 and 2011 by a UN International Monitoring Group alleged continuing Eritrean support to Somali rebels – including, in the latter year, about \$80,000 in monthly financial support and two air deliveries of weapons – but the Eritrean government emphatically denied the accusations, describing them as "concocted, baseless and unfounded".[^cite_note-Erunribsr-283][^cite_note-284] The [International Crisis Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Crisis_Group) added some credence to Eritrea's denials, suggesting that historical Eritrean support to Somali militants had been aimed at undermining Ethiopia during its 2006–9 invasion, and that Hizbul Islam was more likely a recipient than Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-285]&#x20;

On 5 July 2012, the U.S. government imposed sanctions on Eritrea's intelligence chief and a high-ranking military officer, who allegedly facilitated support to Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-wapo20120705-286] However, later that year, the UN Monitoring Group on Somalia and Eritrea reported that it had found no evidence of direct Eritrean support to Al-Shabaab in the preceding year.[^cite_note-287] Between then and 2017, successive annual reports of the Monitoring Group found no evidence for Eritrean involvement, while noting that Eritrea had not provided full access to investigators;[^cite_note-288][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations_Security_Council2017-280] nonetheless, the UN Security Council did not enact the group's recommendation to lift its sanctions on Eritrea until November 2018.[^cite_note-289]&#x20;

### Internal revenue

#### Trade and smuggling



The port city of [Kismayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kismayo), held by Al-Shabaab between 2009 and 2012, is a [charcoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charcoal) smuggling hub.&#x20;

As Al-Shabaab expanded its territorial control, it was able to augment its internal funding through illicit markets and local populations. The smuggling of [charcoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charcoal) has been an important source of income for the group since it first gained control of Somali port cities, especially Kismayo.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPetrich2022-68] Calculating that Al-Shabaab was earning between \$15 million and \$50 million annually from illegal trade, the UN banned Somali charcoal imports.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyYusuf2021-277] The group also profits from involvement in – or taxation of – the smuggling and sale of [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory), [sugar](27712.md), and, reportedly, [heroin](14034.md);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDathan2017-276][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPetrich2022-68] and it has been implicated in [illegal mining](49640132.md) and minerals exports in East Africa.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyYusuf2021-277] There is some evidence that Al-Shabaab has profited from [piracy](17015903.md). In 2011, the head of the UN's counter-piracy division, Colonel John Steed, warned that there was circumstantial evidence that Al-Shabaab increasingly sought to cooperate with [pirate gangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piracy_in_Somalia) and other criminal organizations.[^cite_note-pirate_links-290] Further reports suggested that Al-Shabaab members had extorted pirates, demanding [protection money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protection_racket) equivalent to 20% of the pirates' [ransom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransom) proceeds, but did not provide evidence that Al-Shabaab was itself involved in piracy.[^cite_note-Prceuwsm-291]&#x20;

#### Extortion and taxation

Also in the 2008–2011 period, Al-Shabaab established a sophisticated taxation system, framed as a form of [zakat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakat), in line with sharia, but often resembling extortion. Al-Shabaab taxes may apply to clans, traders, corporations, farmers, or [livestock](25160767.md) herders – in fact, livestock are sometimes stolen outright by Al-Shabaab militants, with the theft framed as a kind of pre-emptive [in-kind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_kind) taxation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyYusuf2021-277] Humanitarian aid agencies are also taxed or extorted, in exchange for permission to operate inside certain territories (see [§ Humanitarian access](#Humanitarian_access));[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyYusuf2021-277] and Al-Shabaab has been known to execute [kidnappings](169376.md) for ransom.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFelterMastersSergie2020-292] In recent years, facing territorial losses in urban areas, Al-Shabaab has had less recourse to previous revenue streams – notably the charcoal trade, through Kismayo, and the remittance economy – and therefore has relied more heavily on extracting zakat.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChonka2016b-89] The development of its security and intelligence services has allowed it to expand its tax base by imposing taxes even in areas outside its territorial control:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyYusuf2021-277] according to the Hiraal Institute, more than half of Al-Shabaab's revenue in 2019–20 came from Mogadishu, whose major companies are taxed at 2.5% of their annual profits.[^cite_note-sc-275] Indeed, a commander in the Somali army confessed to paying tax to Al-Shabaab despite being at war with it.[^cite_note-sc-275] The group has been known to punish non-payment severely, including by [blockading](293054.md) villages under threat of [starvation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starvation).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevyYusuf2021-277]&#x20;

#### Somali government

Either directly or indirectly through black markets, Al-Shabaab has access to arms intended for the Somali government. In 2018 and 2019, expert reports to the UN Security Council found that government military equipment enters the [black market](42210.md), where some of it is sold to Al-Shabaab militants. In some cases, senior government officials orchestrate large-scale diversions of government arms and ammunition; in others, low-ranking members of the security forces sell their weapons for subsistence.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations2019-281][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations2018-293] An earlier report in 2014 had alleged that government officials were actively involved in the direct supply of governments arms to Al-Shabaab.[^cite_note-294] The group has also been known to infiltrate government institutions, as became clear when regional government employees carried out Al-Shabaab's [24 July 2019 Mogadishu bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24_July_2019_Mogadishu_bombing), which killed Mogadishu Mayor [Abdirahmean Omar Osman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdirahman_Omar_Osman).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUnited_Nations2019-281]&#x20;

### Bounties

In 2012, with the support of the Somali government,[^cite_note-Arsasotcfoc-313] the United States began issuing [bounties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bounty_\(reward\)) for Al-Shabaab members under its [Rewards for Justice Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewards_for_Justice_Program). On 7 June, the [U.S. Department of State](31975.md) offered a total of \$33 million in bounties for information leading to the capture of any of seven senior commanders, including \$7 million for Godane, then the group's emir, and \$5 million for Robow, then his deputy.[^cite_note-Arsasotcfoc-313] In response, Al-Shabaab's Fu'ad Qalaf issued a mock bounty of his own, promising ten camels to anyone possessing information on the U.S. President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama), and a further, less valuable, reward of ten cocks and ten hens for information on [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton), the [U.S. Secretary of State](32293.md).[^cite_note-Arsasotcfoc-313] On an official state visit to Mogadishu, the top U.S. envoy [Johnnie Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnnie_Carson) dismissed the counter-offer as "absurd". He also announced that the U.S. would impose sanctions, including visa and travel bans and [asset freezes](5847461.md), on anyone attempting to thwart the ongoing [political transition](37022532.md) in Somalia.[^cite_note-Smsascrfboa-314]&#x20;

On 21 March 2013, the U.S. Department of State offered \$5 million apiece for information on two American senior Al-Shabaab commanders, Omar Shafik Hammami and [Jehad Mostafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehad_Mostafa).[^cite_note-Uspboamis-315] On 15 March 2014, it issued three further bounties, including one on [Abdukadir Mohamed Abdukadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdukadir_Mohamed_Abdukadir), who it said coordinated Al-Shabaab's recruitment activities in Kenya.[^cite_note-Guotbftseltaq-316] On 27 September 2014, after Godane's death, the Somali [National Intelligence and Security Agency](81014.md) announced its own bounty, offering \$2 million for information leading to the arrest of the new emir, Umar, and a separate \$1 million reward for information leading to his killing.[^cite_note-Sgpboasl-317] Several further Somali bounties were issued on 10 April 2015, with rewards ranging between \$100,000 and \$250,000.[^cite_note-Gsgpboasl-318] In a separate programme, on 3 April 2015, the Kenyan government offered [KSh.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyan_shilling) 20 million (\$215,000) for information leading to the arrest of Mohamed Mohamud, a commander of Al-Shabaab's operations in Kenya.[^cite_note-Asswkpolgw-319]&#x20;
