In the Philippines, **regions** ([Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *rehiyon*; [ISO 3166-2\:PH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3166-2\:PH)) are [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_Philippines) that primarily serve to coordinate planning and organize national government services across multiple [local government units](22088280.md) (LGUs). Most national government agencies provide services through their regional offices instead of maintaining direct provincial or city offices. Regional offices are usually, but not necessarily, located in the city designated as the regional center.

As of 2024, the Philippines is divided into 18 regions. Seventeen are administrative groupings, each with a [regional development council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_development_council) (RDC) designated by the [President](271126.md). In the case of the [National Capital Region](72340.md) (Metro Manila), a [metropolitan development authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Manila_Development_Authority) serves as the coordinating and policy-making body. Only one, the [Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](37260549.md), has an elected government and [parliament](42303279.md), to which the [Congress of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Philippines) has delegated certain powers and responsibilities.

## History

Regions first came to existence on September 24, 1972, when the provinces of the Philippines were organized into eleven regions under Presidential Decree No. 1 as part of the Integrated Reorganization Plan of President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos).[^cite_note-1] Since that time, other regions have been created and some provinces have been transferred from one region to another.

## List of regions

As of June 30, 2024, the Philippines is divided into 18 regions.[^cite_note-psa-35] The traditional island groups of [Luzon](69375.md), the [Visayas](72418.md), and [Mindanao](20546.md) are composed of eight (Regions I, II, III, IV-A, and V, and CAR, NCR, and Mimaropa), four (VI, VII, VIII, and NIR), and six (IX, X, XI, XII, XIII, and BARMM) regions, respectively. The names of [Calabarzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabarzon), [Mimaropa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimaropa), and [Soccsksargen](298888.md) are acronyms signifying their component provinces and cities; and are usually capitalized in official government documents.

### Types of regions

#### Administrative region

An administrative region is a grouping of geographically adjacent LGUs that may be established, disestablished, and modified by the [president of the Philippines](271126.md) based on the need to formulate coherent economic development policies, more efficiently provide national government services, and coordinate activities beneficial to the development of larger area beyond the province level. No plebiscites have been conducted so far to democratically confirm the creation, abolition or alteration of the boundaries of regular administrative regions, as the Constitution does not mandate it.[^cite_note-artx-36]

An administrative region is not a local government unit (LGU), but rather a group of LGUs to which the president[^cite_note-eo325-37] has provided an unelected policy-making and coordinating structure, called the Regional Development Council (RDC).[^cite_note-neda-38] [Metro Manila](72340.md) is recognized in law as a "special development and administrative region", and was thus given the [Metropolitan Manila Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Manila_Development_Authority) (MMDA);[^cite_note-ra7924-39] the Metro Manila Council within the MMDA serves as the National Capital Region's RDC.[^cite_note-neda-38]

#### Autonomous region

Main article: [Autonomous regions of the Philippines](45522517.md)

The 1987 Constitution allows for the creation of [autonomous regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division) in the [Cordillera Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Luzon\)) of [Luzon](69375.md) and the [Muslim-majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_people) areas of [Mindanao](20546.md).[^cite_note-artx-36] However, only the [Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](37260549.md) and its predecessor, the [Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_in_Muslim_Mindanao), have been approved by voters in plebiscites held in 1989, [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Autonomous_Region_in_Muslim_Mindanao_expansion_and_inclusion_plebiscite), and [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Bangsamoro_autonomy_plebiscite). Voters in the Cordilleras rejected autonomy in 1990 and [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Cordillera_Autonomous_Region_creation_plebiscite); hence the [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region) remains as a regular administrative region with no delegated powers or responsibilities.

The [Supreme Court](1335534.md) has ruled that an autonomous region established by statute must be composed of more than one province, thereby invalidating the proposed establishment of the *Autonomous Region of [Ifugao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifugao)* following the results of the original 1990 Cordillera autonomy plebiscite, which saw only Ifugao's voters casting a majority 'yes' vote towards autonomy.[^cite_note-40]

### Judicial regions

For a list of these judicial regions, see [Regional Trial Court § List](36842278.md#List).

As far as the [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) is concerned, specifically the first and second level courts, the country is divided into [judicial regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_Philippines#Judicial_regions) as provided by *[Batas Pambansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batas_Pambansa) Bilang 129*. The coverage of these judicial regions generally coincides with that of the administrative regions in 1980, with some exceptions.

### Legislative districts

Representation for the [Interim Batasang Pambansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Batasang_Pambansa) was mostly through parliamentary districts based on how regions were organized in 1978. Metro Manila was "Region IV", while Southern Tagalog was "Region IV-A". This was the only time the national legislature was represented via regions; [in a 1984 plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Philippine_constitutional_plebiscite), voters approved a constitutional amendment that reverted to representation per province and city.

## Defunct regions

The following are regions that no longer exist, listed along with their current status:

- [Southern Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Tagalog) (Region IV, now divided into [Calabarzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabarzon), [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) ([Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\))), [Metro Manila](72340.md) (several cities that were part of [Rizal](232458.md)), and [Mimaropa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mimaropa); the name remains as a cultural-geographic region only)

- Western Mindanao (renamed as [Zamboanga Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Peninsula), still designated as Region IX)

- Central Mindanao (now mostly [Soccsksargen](298888.md), still designated as Region XII)

- Southern Mindanao (now mostly [Davao Region](296162.md), still designated as Region XI)

- [Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_in_Muslim_Mindanao) (replaced by [Bangsamoro](37260549.md))
