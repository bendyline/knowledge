**India–Qatar relations** refers to the bilateral ties between [India](14533.md) and [Qatar](8238258.md). India maintains an [embassy in Doha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_India,_Doha),[^cite_note-3] while Qatar maintains an embassy in [New Delhi](51585.md) and a consulate in [Mumbai](19189.md).[^cite_note-4]

## History

Diplomatic relations between India and Qatar were established in 1973.[^cite_note-embassydoha-5]

During a visit made by Emir [Tamim bin Hamad al-Thani](6146151.md) in March 2015, five MoUs entailing co-operation in several fields were signed. Additionally, an agreement on prisoner repatriation was made. According to this agreement, citizens of India or Qatar who are convicted and sentenced for a crime can be extradited to their native country to spend the remaining years of their prison sentence.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

In June 2024, Indian External Affairs Minister [S. Jaishankar](40435868.md) visited Doha, underscoring the increasing importance of Middle Eastern relations in India's foreign policy. Jaishankar met with Qatar's Prime Minister and Foreign Minister, Sheikh [Mohammed bin Abdulrahman bin Jassim Al Thani](49695374.md), to review the bilateral ties. While the Ministry of External Affairs' statement was brief, it highlighted historic and friendly relations, covering a range of topics including trade, investment, energy, and security. This visit followed Jaishankar's trip to the [UAE](69328.md), where India-UAE relations have seen significant growth, evidenced by a trade volume of \$83.74 billion in 2023-2024. Qatar and India's relationship is deepening, particularly with the recent \$78 billion LNG deal between [QatarEnergy](11292570.md) and India's Petronet, which is set to save India \$6 billion. Despite some complexities, India's strategic engagement with Qatar is bolstered by the significant Indian expatriate population in Qatar, forming about 25% of its populace, primarily as migrant workers.[^cite_note-8]

In July 2026, India observed a day of national mourning as a mark of respect to former [Emir of Qatar](399230.md) [Hamad bin Khalifa Al Thani](295929.md).[^cite_note-9]

## Diplomatic visits

Emir of Qatar [Hamad bin Khalifa al Thani](295929.md) made diplomatic visits to India in April 1999, May 2005 and April 2012.[^cite_note-embassydoha-5]

On 4 June 2016, Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) arrived in [Doha](26214389.md) on a two-day visit which was focused on giving a new push to the economic ties, particularly in the hydrocarbon sector. During the visit he shared a meal with Indian workers living in [Qatar](8238258.md) and also addressed the NRIs at a gala event.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-toi2-11]



Prime Minister Narendra Modi of India with Emir of Qatar Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani in New Delhi on 17 February 2025

On 17 February 2025, Indian Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) welcomed Qatari Emir [Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani](6146151.md) to Delhi, India, to increase and boost trade and economic relations between the two Countries.[^cite_note-12]

## Military relations

During the first-ever diplomatic visit to Qatar made by Prime Minister [Manmohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmohan_Singh) of India in November 2008, a maritime defence agreement was approved between the two countries. The agreement was described by Indian government officials as a 'landmark' that would permit mutual maritime defence training and facilitate mutual visits. Indian officials have described the agreement as “just short of stationing troops”.[^cite_note-13] An agreement pertaining to law enforcement and national security was also signed during the meeting. This agreement was made with the intent of exchanging classified information in order to assist in suppressing threats raised by extremist elements.[^cite_note-14] As part of these agreements, the inaugural India-Qatar Joint Committee on Defence Cooperation meeting was hosted in the Qatari capital [Doha](26214389.md) in 2008. This was followed by a second meeting in [New Delhi](51585.md) in 2011 and a third meeting in Doha in 2013.[^cite_note-15]

### *Za’ir-Al-Bahr*

Za’ir-Al-Bahr (Roar of the Sea) is a joint exercise between the Qatari Emiri Navy and the Indian Navy. Its inaugural edition was conducted during 17–21 November 2019 at Doha. This inaugural edition of the Bilateral Maritime Exercise between the two navies would further strengthen the robust defence co-operation between the two countries, especially in the fight against terrorism, maritime piracy and maritime security.[^cite_note-\:0-16][^cite_note-17]

## Economic relations

In 2008, Qatar agreed to invest US\$5 billion in India's energy sector.[^cite_note-18] A deep-sea gas pipeline from Qatar to India through [Oman](22316.md) has also been proposed.[^cite_note-19]

In January 2016, Qatar agreed to decrease the selling price of gas to India from \$12–13 per unit to \$6–7 per unit. This deal came as a result of the global reduction in gas prices and the surplus in gas supply worldwide. In addition to reducing the price, Qatar also agreed to exempt India of the ₹12,000 crore (US\$1.2 billion) fee that was owed due to India's non-compliance in importing gas shipments which were previously agreed on in 2015.[^cite_note-20]

### Bilateral trade

India amounted to \$1.2 billion, or 3.8% of its overall exports in 2014. At a value of \$215.3 million, [cereals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal) were India's top exported commodity to Qatar. Machinery came second at \$108.6 million. The export value for electronic equipment was \$93.2 million, making it the third most significant Indian export to Qatar.[^cite_note-21]

Qatari exports to India totaled \$16.8 billion, or 12.7% of its overall exports in 2014. The highest valued export commodity is [oil](4207510.md), of which \$14.9 billion worth has been exported from Qatar to India. Vying for second place is [plastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic); a recorded \$700.8 million worth has been exported to India. The third highest valued Qatari export to India is [organic chemicals](22203.md), accounting for a total of \$554 million.[^cite_note-22]

During his June 2016 visit to Qatar, PM Narendra Modi urged Qatari business leaders to invest in India and take advantage of India's investment-friendly policies. He assuaged the concerns of Qatari businessmen and assured them that economic bottlenecks will be resolved in the months ahead.[^cite_note-23]
