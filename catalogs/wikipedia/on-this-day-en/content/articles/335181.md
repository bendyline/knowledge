**Rodriguez,** officially the **Municipality of Rodriguez** ([Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Bayan ng Rodriguez*), formerly named and still commonly known as **Montalban**, is a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_the_Philippines) in the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Province) of [Rizal](232458.md), [Philippines](23440.md). According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 451,383 people,[^cite_note-6] making it the most populous municipality in the country.

The town is one of the richest municipalities in the country, ranking third in 2016 with an income of [₱](227672.md)691 million.[^cite_note-COA_Report_2016-7]

## Etymology

The municipality's former official name of Montalban derives from the [Spanish](26825.md) word *monte*, which translates to "mountain," in reference to the numerous hills found within and surrounding the town.[^cite_note-history-8] A common name for the municipality up to the present, it was its official name from its founding in 1871 until 1982 when the [Batasang Pambansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batasang_Pambansa) officially renamed it to Rodriguez under Batas Pambansa Blg. 275 in honor of [Eulogio Rodriguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eulogio_Rodriguez), a native of the municipality who served as its first municipal president and [Philippine Senate president](8108148.md).[^cite_note-rename-1]

## History



Aerial view of Montalban, circa 1930s

Based on the 1954 journal *History and Cultural Life of Montalban and its Barrios*, what would then be the town of Montalban was formerly the [barrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrio) of *Balite* under the jurisdiction of [San Mateo](892217.md) during the Spanish period.[^cite_note-history-8] San Mateo, with its large size, was partitioned on April 27, 1871, when Captain General [Rafael Izquierdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Izquierdo_y_Guti%C3%A9rrez) issued a decree separating the barrios of Balite, Burgos, Marang and Calipahan from San Mateo and formed them into the new municipality of Montalban.[^cite_note-9] The town was then headed by its first parish priest Fr. Eustaquio Infante of [Pasig](359702.md), who led the dedication of the Most Holy Rosary parish church on June 2, 1871, in the term of Gregorio Melitón Martínez as [Archbishop of Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Manila).[^cite_note-history-8]

According to the Executive Summary included in the Annual Audit Reports of Rodriguez, Rizal, the municipality of Montalban was founded on June 30, 1871, under the *Acta de Erección* (Deed of Foundation).[^cite_note-audit_report_2012-10] The town, including its *[población](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poblacion)*, was initially part of the [Province of Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_\(province\)), while its present-day mountainous central and eastern portion, according to old maps, was under the jurisdiction of the [District of Morong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morong_\(district\)).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Additionally, an 1882 Spanish map indicates that the northeastern areas of the present-day municipality were once claimed as part of [Bulacan](252408.md).[^cite_note-13] The municipality was then incorporated to the newly created province of Rizal on June 11, 1901, by virtue of [Philippine Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commission) Act No. 137, after having been a part of the defunct province of [Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_\(province\)).[^cite_note-act_137-14]

Montalban was then returned to its mother municipality of San Mateo by virtue of Philippine Commission Act No. 942 on October 12, 1903. San Mateo served as the seat of government in line with the economic centralization.[^cite_note-act_942-15] Montalban then separated from San Mateo to become an independent municipality again by virtue of Executive Order No. 20 on February 29, 1908.



Historical marker installed at the municipal hall in 2009

In 1982, the town was officially renamed to Rodriguez under Batas Pambansa Blg. 275 in honor of the former [Senate president](8108148.md) and town native [Eulogio Rodriguez Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eulogio_A._Rodriguez_Sr.)[^cite_note-rename-1]

On October 30, 1997, rebels from the [New People's Army](1262096.md) (NPA), the militant arm of the [Communist Party of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Philippines), raided the police station of Rodriguez before noon, ransacking its armoury, killing a police officer, and kidnapping police chief René Francisco. He was later released on December 5, 1997, in [Tanay, Rizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanay,_Rizal).[^cite_note-16]

### Proposed cityhood

As early as 2018, the town's Sangguniang Bayan approved Resolution No. 60, Series of 2018 requesting the Senate of the Philippines through its [president](8108148.md), [Vicente Sotto III](3873709.md) and the [House of Representatives](293605.md) through its [speaker](7835723.md), [Gloria Macapagal Arroyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Macapagal_Arroyo) to co-sponsor a bill for Rodriguez's conversion into a city.

### Proposed reversion of town name to Montalban



Official document from 2020 bearing the town name "Montalban" instead of the official name "Rodriguez".

In mid-to-late 2019, before a formal ruling could be enacted, the municipal government under the term of Mayor [Dennis Hernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Hernandez) began referring to the town as "Montalban".

Notable examples were dropping "Rodriguez" in official documents, office names and correspondences, and the municipal government using a municipal seal with the name "Montalban". In February 2020, another municipal seal that bears the year 1871 as the date of establishment was used.

In April 2020, the municipality celebrated its 149th Foundation Day (*Araw ng Montalban*); in the previous year April 2019, at the time when 1909 was still the recognized date of establishment, the municipality celebrated its 110th founding anniversary.[^cite_note-montalban_usage_2019-17]

In response to the numerous requests from the town's native residents to return the town to its former name, on July 3, 2019, during the [18th Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_Congress_of_the_Philippines), [Rizal's 2nd District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal's_2nd_congressional_district) Representative Juan Fidel Nograles filed *House Bill No. 337*.[^cite_note-return_montalban-18][^cite_note-hb337_18th_congress-19] The bill was later substituted by *HB No. 8899* filed March 5, 2021;[^cite_note-hb8899_18th_congress-20] it was approved by the House of Representatives on March 17 and was received by the [Senate](299521.md) on March 27. It was introduced by Senator [Imee Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imee_Marcos) under *Senate Bill No. 2258* on June 1.[^cite_note-sb2258_18th_congress-21][^cite_note-22]

In the Senate hearing of the Committee on Local Government on January 21, 2022, the [National Historical Commission of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historical_Commission_of_the_Philippines) did not pose any objection on the proposed measure and clarified that the proposed change will only apply to the municipality's name; structures and other features such as buildings, schools in the municipality dedicated in memory of Rodriguez are not covered by the proposed bill and should be retained.[^cite_note-23]

With further minor amendments, the bill was approved on third and final reading on January 31. The [House](293605.md) adopted the Senate amendment and the bill was submitted to the [President](271126.md) for his signature.

On June 2, 2022, Republic Act No. 11812, the act reverting the town's name to Montalban and repealing Batas Pambansa Blg. 275 had lapsed into law. A [plebiscite](51582.md), having no set date, is to be supervised by the [Commission on Elections](1847922.md).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## Geography

Rodriguez is a town of mountains. It is located on the slopes of the [Sierra Madre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_\(Philippines\)) mountain range. From its north to south, a series of sloping ridges, hills and mountains ranges adorn the town. In fact, around 27% of the town is occupied by mountains and slopes. The town's highest peak is Mount Irid, 1,469 meters (4,820 ft) above sea level.

Rodriguez makes up 26.23% of the 1,191.94-square-kilometer (460.21 sq mi) total area of Rizal. Making it the largest town in Rizal province with an area of 312.70 square kilometers (120.73 square miles). The municipality borders [San Mateo](892217.md) and [Antipolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipolo), Rizal on the south, [Norzagaray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norzagaray) and [San Jose del Monte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_del_Monte), Bulacan on the north, [Quezon City](284223.md) and [Caloocan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caloocan) on the west, [General Nakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Nakar), [Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon) on the east, and [Tanay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanay,_Rizal) on the south-east. Rodriguez's economic activities include agriculture and commerce such as the Avilon Zoo.

Rodriguez is the northernmost town in the province and comes after [San Mateo, Rizal](892217.md), and [Quezon City](284223.md) coming from [Metro Manila](72340.md). It is also the location of the Montalban Gorge that is associated with the Legend of *[Bernardo Carpio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Carpio)*. The gorge forms part of a protected area known as the [Pamitinan Protected Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamitinan_Protected_Landscape).

### Elevation and slope



The mountainous interior of Rodriguez



Coralline limestone along the Mascap-Puray Road

Rodriguez is generally very rough in topography, with 83% of its total land area composed of upland areas, hills and mountain ranges. The remaining 17% low-lying terrain and rolling lands are found at the south-western portion of the municipality, along with the northern portions of the Municipality of [San Mateo](892217.md).[^cite_note-26]

The rolling slopes comprise the Marikina River Valley, where water from higher elevations drain towards the [Marikina River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marikina_River) and its tributaries at the south-western portion of the municipality. Elevations at these western lowlands range from 10 to 30 meters (33 to 98 ft) above sea level. The western portion of the flatlands gently rises towards the west, which is part of [Quezon City](284223.md).

The mountainous regions of Rodriguez are found at the central and eastern areas, with sleepy sloping ridges and mountain ranges, traversing north to south. The terrain in these mountain ranges has slopes ranging from 30 to 50%. Very steep hills and mountains have slopes greater than 50%. Rolling to hilly areas 18–30% in slope can also be found. These high elevations are drained by the Tanay, Puray, and Rodriguez Rivers.

## Transportation



A patok jeepney plying the route of Montalban - Cubao. They are known for booming music, bright lights and colorful and artistic designs.

The main transportation used in the municipality are jeepneys, tricycles, [UV Express](40666464.md), and buses. There are jeepneys that ply through the towns of San Mateo, Marikina and Cubao in Quezon City via [Marcos Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marikina%E2%80%93Infanta_Highway), while some ply the route that leads to [Philcoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Coconut_Authority) in Quezon City via [Batasan Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batasan_Road) in San Mateo, others connect Rodriguez to Litex Road in Quezon City. UV Express vehicles ply the routes to Cubao in Quezon City and Santa Lucia Grand Mall in [Cainta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cainta,_Rizal). During the 2010s, there were additional UV Express vehicles that serve the routes to [North EDSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_North_EDSA) in Quezon City and [Quiapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quiapo,_Manila) in Manila, and the route to Cubao operates via Marikina and via Payatas Road.

Formerly, there was railway service under the [Manila Railroad Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Railways) that traversed the [San Mateo](892217.md), [Marikina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marikina), and [Pasig](359702.md) areas and had its terminus at Barangay Balitè behind the Santo Rosario Parish Church. It had been long dismantled and the railway line converted into roadway that now consists of the road aptly named *Daang Bakal*.

It is known that 'patok' jeepneys originated from this town way back during the 1980s. These jeepneys are known for their notoriety on the road. Despite this, they are popular among the passengers hence the name *patók* which means 'popular'. They still ply this town up to this day, most plying the Montalban-Cubao route via Marcos Highway. These jeepneys have already spread throughout the Rizal province as well as in Marikina since then.

The Marikina Auto Line Transport Corporation (MALTC) buses is the only one that regularly travels to farther places like [Makati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makati) and [Parañaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para%C3%B1aque). However, during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the [Land Transportation Franchising and Regulatory Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Transportation_Franchising_and_Regulatory_Board) (LTFRB) implemented a route rationalization system for [city buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bus_routes_in_Metro_Manila). Currently, a city bus route connects Montalban with the capital city of [Manila](184334.md) and [Parañaque Integrated Terminal Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para%C3%B1aque_Integrated_Terminal_Exchange).

The proposed San Mateo Railway will connect the municipality of Rodriguez to the [Light Rail Transit Line 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LRT_Line_2), in which 6 stations are in development.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

## Education

There are two schools district offices which govern all educational institutions within the municipality. They oversee the management and operations of all private and public, from primary to secondary schools.[^cite_note-49] These are the Rodriguez I Schools District, and Rodriguez II Schools District.

There are numerous elementary and high schools, both public and private, offering primary, secondary and tertiary education in the town of Rodriguez. There are also colleges and institutions that offer undergraduate and graduate programs in town.
