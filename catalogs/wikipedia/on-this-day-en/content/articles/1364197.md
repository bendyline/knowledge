**Antalya Province** ([Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Antalya ili*) is a [province](428896.md) and [metropolitan municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_municipalities_in_Turkey) of [Turkey](11125639.md). It is located on the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Region,_Turkey) coast of south-west Turkey, between the [Taurus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_Mountains) and the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md). Its area is 20,177 km<sup>2</sup>,[^cite_note-2] and its population is 2,688,004 (2022).[^cite_note-tuik-1]

Antalya Province is the centre of Turkey's tourism industry, attracting 30% of foreign tourists visiting Turkey. Its capital city of the same name was the world's third most visited city by number of international arrivals in 2011, displacing [New York](645042.md). Antalya is Turkey's biggest international [sea resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort_town). The province of Antalya corresponds to the lands of ancient [Lycia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycia) to the west, [Pamphylia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamphylia) to the east, and part of [Pisidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisidia) to the north. It features a shoreline of 657 km (408 mi) with beaches, ports, and ancient cities scattered throughout, including the [World Heritage Site](44940.md) [Xanthos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xanthos). The provincial capital is [Antalya](361527.md) city with a population of 1,344,000.

Antalya is the fastest-growing province in Turkey; with a 4.17% yearly population growth rate between years 1990–2000, compared with the national rate of 1.83%. This growth is due to a fast rate of urbanization, particularly driven by tourism and other service sectors on the coast.

## Etymology

The city and thus the province are named after [Attalos II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attalos_II), king of [Pergamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon), who founded the city in the 2nd century BC.

## History



[Aspendos Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspendos)

### Antiquity

Antalya has been settled since pre-historic times. Evidence of human habitation dating back to the early Paleolithic age (150,000–º200,000 years ago) has been discovered in the [Karain cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karain_cave?action=edit\&redlink=1), 30 km (19 mi) of the north of Antalya city.[^cite_note-3] Other finds dating back to the [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic) ([Beldibi Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beldibi_Cave)), [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) (Bademağacı Höyüğü) and more recent periods show that the area has been populated by various civilizations throughout the ages.

According to [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus), the earliest known inhabitants were a [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) people called the [Milyae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milyae) (Milyans), who referred to the area as [Milyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milyas) and spoke an [Indo-European language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages) known as [Milyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milyan). A people called the [Termilae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termilae), from [Crete](6591.md), also settled and eventually dominated the coastal margins, which were known as *Trm̃mis* (while the Milyae became concentrated in the mountains). According to [Greek legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology), an exiled [Athenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Athens) called [Lykos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycus_\(mythology\)) (Latin: *Lycus*) became prominent in the region. Records from the [Hittite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hittites) period refer to the inhabitas and the area as [Lukka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukka_lands), and document lively interactions with neighboring regions in the 2nd millennium BC. It is commonly accepted that Lukka is cognate with the later, [Latinised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization) exonym [Lycia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycia). The Lukka were known for their seafaring skills (including piracy) and demonstrated a fiery, independent spirit; neither the Hittites, nor the [Arzawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Arzawa), to the west, could ever dominate them for long.

According to Greek legend, there was immigration by a Greek tribe called the [Akhaioi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaeans_\(tribe\)) (from the northern [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese)) to the area, after the [Trojan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trojan_War), and eventually many Greek settlements built up along the coast and inland. In the [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period), the western parts of the later Antalya were regarded as **Lycia**, whereas the east was **[Pamphylia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamphylia)**, and the extremes east **[Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilicia)**, while **[Pisidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisidia)** was to the north. These communities grew into independent cities, and eventually a federation was set up, under the name of Pamphylia.



[Lycian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycia) tombs in [Simena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simena)

Before the [Ancient Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) conquest Lycia was a polity with the first [democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy) [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) in the world, which later partly inspired the [U.S. Constitution](31644.md).[^cite_note-4]

Antalya was part of the [Lydian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydia) kingdom from the 7th century BC until Lydia was defeated by the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) during the [Battle of Sardis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sardis_\(547_BC\)) in 546 BC. The [Macedonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(ancient_kingdom\)) commander [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) ended Persian rule and in around 334 BC conquered the cities of the area one by one—except for [Termessos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termessos) and [Sillyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sillyon) which managed to repulse his armies in 333 BC. After the [death of Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Alexander_the_Great) in 323 BC, a [long battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_the_Diadochi) erupted between his generals that lasted until 188 BC.

The reign of the kingdom of [Pergamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergamon) began with the defeat of the [Seleucid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) army at Apamea. Shortly after this the city of [Antalya](361527.md) was founded. When [Attalos III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attalus_III), the last king of Pergamom, died in 133 BC he left his kingdom to the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic). At this time the area is dominated by pirates based in small cities along the coast.

### The Byzantines



[Hıdırlık Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C4%B1d%C4%B1rl%C4%B1k_Tower) dating back to the [Hellenistic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_era)

During the mid-[Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) era (the 5th and 6th centuries) the city of Antalya grew beyond the city walls.

From the [early Muslim conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Muslim_conquests) of the 7th century [Muslim Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Muslims) started to be dominant in the [Levant region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) and Antalya later played a part in the Christian [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) against [Islam](6037917.md). The army of King [Louis VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_VII_of_France) of France sailed from Antalya for [Syria](7515849.md) during the [Second Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Crusade) in 1148, and the fleet of [Richard I of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_I_of_England) rallied here before the conquest of [Cyprus](5593.md) during the [Third Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Crusade). In the late 11th and early 12th Century much of the area of the modern province fell to the [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) especially the [Danishmends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danishmends). From 1120 to March 1207, Antalya was again under the sovereignty of Byzantines.[^cite_note-5]



[St. Nicholas Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Nicholas_Church,_Demre), [Demre](4226294.md)

### The Seljuk Turks

The area was conquered by the [Seljuk Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuks) and recaptured by the Byzantines again and again from 1076 onwards as the Seljuks strove to establish a trading base on the [Mediterranean](19006.md). At one stage Turkish lord [Kilij Arslan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilij_Arslan_I) had a palace here. In 1220 Byzantine rule ended for the last time and the city was quickly divided into Christian and Muslim sections, the Christian trading communities including [Venetians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) and [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa). [Alanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanya) also grew and throve during the Seljuk period.

### The Ottoman Empire onwards



[Alanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanya) is located in Antalya province

The area passed through many hands before its final occupation by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) under [Murad II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murad_II) during the [Ottoman expansion into Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_wars_in_Europe) in 1432. Antalya was also occupied by [Kingdom of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Cyprus) between 1361 and 1373. Ottoman rule of the coast persisted until the [empire's collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) at the end of the [First World War](4764461.md). The [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) was guaranteed the province in the [Agreement of Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agreement_of_Saint-Jean-de-Maurienne), although the other members of the [Allied Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) disputed the legality of this treaty. Antalya was briefly occupied by the [Royal Italian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Italian_Army) before becoming part of the [Republic of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Turkey) during the [Turkish War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_War_of_Independence) led by [Atatürk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atat%C3%BCrk) in 1921.[^cite_note-6]

There are many archaeological sites in the province including three National Parks and three Specially Protected Areas.

Antalya province is situated in south-west [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia), between the longitudes 29°20'-32°35'East and latitudes 36°07'-37°29'North. The province covers an area of 20,591 square miles. The southern border of the province is the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) while the [Taurus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_Mountains) draw the land border. From west to east the province is bordered by [Muğla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu%C4%9Fla_Province), [Burdur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burdur_Province), [Isparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isparta_Province), [Konya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konya_Province), [Karaman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaman_Province) and [Mersin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mersin_province) provinces. The land of the province is 77.8% mountainous, 10.2% plain and 12% uneven. Many of the peaks of the Taurus mountains are above 500–3000 metres. The [Teke Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teke_Peninsula) (corresponds to ancient [Lycia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycia)) in the west includes wide plateaus and river basins. Climate, agriculture, demographics and habitation patterns differ greatly between the inland mountain areas and the coastal plain.

The Antalya basin consists of three sub-basins: the [Manavgat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manavgat_River) in the east, the [Köprü Çay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6pr%C3%BC_%C3%87ay) in the middle, and the [Aksu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aksu_River_\(Turkey\)) in the west.[^cite_note-Poisson_et_al_2011-9]<sup>: 135 </sup>

The western part of the Antalya plain is crossed by two rivers: the [Karaman Çay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karaman_%C3%87ay?action=edit\&redlink=1), west of the city, and the [Düden Çay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCden_River), east of the city.[^cite_note-Poisson_et_al_2011-9]<sup>: 142 </sup> A wide undersea canyon extends to the south of the area where these rivers flow into the Antalya Gulf.[^cite_note-Poisson_et_al_2011-9]<sup>: 142 </sup> Offshore from the Aksu's mouth, there is no canyon; there is instead a shallow marine shelf where the river deposits sediments in a broad area.[^cite_note-Poisson_et_al_2011-9]<sup>: 142 </sup>

West of the Aksu basin is a 30x40 km-wide area of [tufa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufa) and [travertine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travertine) deposits that mark the SW boundary of the basin; the city of Antalya is built on top of this area.[^cite_note-Kaya_2014-10]<sup>: 9–10 </sup> These deposits were formed from prehistoric cold springs in the early [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene).[^cite_note-Kaya_2014-10]<sup>: 17 </sup> They are deepest at the west end, where they are 250 m deep; they get shallower towards the east and are 30 m deep at the easternmost part.[^cite_note-Kaya_2014-10]<sup>: 9 </sup>

A narrow band of limestone deposits stretch from [Gebiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebiz,_Turkey?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the north to the [Küçük Asar Tepe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%BC%C3%A7%C3%BCk_Asar_Tepe?action=edit\&redlink=1) hills in the south.[^cite_note-Poisson_et_al_2011-9]<sup>: 139 </sup> This is known as the [Gebiz limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebiz_limestone?action=edit\&redlink=1) and was likely deposited from shallow coral reefs on the east side of the Aksu basin.[^cite_note-Poisson_et_al_2011-9]<sup>: 139–40 </sup> There are abundant [gastropod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastropod) and [bivalve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bivalve) fossils in these deposits.[^cite_note-Kaya_2014-10]<sup>: 7 </sup>

## Education

The province of Antalya has two public ([Akdeniz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akdeniz_University), [Alanya Alaaddin Keykubat University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanya_Alaaddin_Keykubat_University)), and three private universities (Alanya University, Antalya Belek University, Antalya Science University). Antalya's compulsory education system is run by the Turkish [Ministry of National Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Education_\(Turkey\))

## Places of interest



[Perga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perga), the capital of [Pamphylia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pamphylia), is the second best preserved ancient city of Turkey after Ephesus



[Temple of Apollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Apollo_\(Side\)) in [Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side,_Turkey)

The highest point of the [Taurus mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_Mountains) is [Akdağ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akda%C4%9F) at an altitude of 3025 metres. Located to the northwest of the city centre and at a distance of 50 km (31 mi) is the [Saklıkent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakl%C4%B1kent) ski and recreation centre on [Beydağları](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyda%C4%9Flar%C4%B1_Coastal_National_Park). Here one can ski and then go down to the shore and into the sea during the months of January–April. In Saklıkent there are two teleski and ski courses along with villas and accommodation facilities available in the area. Situated on Bakırlı Hill behind Saklıkent is the [Antalya National Space Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antalya_National_Space_Observatory?action=edit\&redlink=1). In rivers rafting and canoeing sports and on the Taurus mountains jeep safari, hunting and trekking tours are organised. Alternatively, there is rock-climbing, bird watching tourism, photo-safari, mountain tourism, line fishing and health tourism at the thalasso and dialysis centres in [Geyikbayırı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geyikbay%C4%B1r%C4%B1). Holy tourism activities are organized in [Myra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myra) and [Patara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patara_\(Lycia\)) where [St Nicholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Nicholas) was born and lived. The eternal flames of [Chimaera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimera_\(geography\)) that was the inspiration for the [Chimera myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimera_\(mythology\)) is also located in Antalya. In the area of [Belek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belek), 30 km (19 mi). east of Antalya, there is a big potential for golf tourism with the bonus of the cultural, historical and natural sightseeing of the region. There are five golf facilities already operating in Belek. There are underwater diving centres on the coast, primarily in [Kemer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemer), [Çamyuva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87amyuva), [Olympos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympos_\(Lycia\)), [Adrasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrasan), [Kaş](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ka%C5%9F), [Kalkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalkan), [Üçağız](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9C%C3%A7a%C4%9F%C4%B1z?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Kekova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kekova), [Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side,_Turkey) and [Alanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alanya). In addition, yacht tours are organized from the port of Antalya to [Düden Waterfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCden_Waterfall)-[Karpuzkaldıran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karpuzkald%C4%B1ran?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Kemer, from Side to Alanya, from [Demre](4226294.md)-[Çayağzı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87aya%C4%9Fz%C4%B1,_Antalya?action=edit\&redlink=1) to Kekova, from Kaş to Kekova, from Kemer to [Çıralı](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87%C4%B1ral%C4%B1)-Olympos-Adrasan and [Cape Gelidonya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Gelidonya). There are also boat tours on [Manavgat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manavgat) creek and canoe tours on [Xanthos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xanthos) creek. In addition, [Blue Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Cruise), which covers the southwestern Anatolian coast, ends in Antalya. There are ferryboat trips from Alanya to [Cyprus](5593.md) and from Antalya to [Italy](14532.md). Antalya today is a tourism centre with touristic accommodation facilities, nature and historic sites.
