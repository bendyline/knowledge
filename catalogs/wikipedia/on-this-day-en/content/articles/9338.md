The **politics of Ecuador** are [multi-party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system).[^cite_note-1] The central government polity is a quadrennially [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system), unicameral [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy). The [President of Ecuador](840787.md) is [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of the army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_army) on a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system), and leads a [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Ecuador) with further [executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power). [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is not limited to the [National Assembly](29017747.md), as it may to a lesser degree be exercised by the executive which consists of the President convening an appointed executive cabinet. Subsequent acts of the National Assembly are supreme over Executive Orders where sufficient votes have been cast by the legislators. The [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) is independent of the executive and the legislature. Ecuador is also considered a constitutional republic.[^cite_note-2]&#x20;

The [Constitution of Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Ecuador) provides for a four-year term of office for the President, Vice-President, and members of the National Assembly with concurrent elections. Presidents and legislators may be re-elected immediately. Citizens must be at least 16 years of age to vote: [suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffrage) is universal and compulsory for literate persons aged 18 to 65 and optional for 16 and 17 years of age and other eligible voters.&#x20;

Ecuador has been characterized as a fragile democracy, with [democratic backsliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_backsliding) under the presidencies of [Rafael Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Correa) (2007–2017) and [Daniel Noboa](73980059.md) (2023–).[^cite_note-3] The [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md) rated Ecuador a "[hybrid regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Index#Definitions)" in 2022.[^cite_note-democracy-index-4]&#x20;

## Political conditions

Ecuador's [political parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) have historically been small, loose organizations that depended more on populist, often charismatic, leaders to retain support than on programs or ideology.[^cite_note-5] Frequent internal splits have produced extreme factionalism. However, a pattern has emerged in which administrations from the center-left alternate with those from the center-right. Although Ecuador's political [elite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite) is highly factionalized along regional, ideological, and personal lines, a strong desire for consensus on major issues often leads to compromise. Opposition forces in Congress are loosely organized, but historically they often unite to block the administration's initiatives and to remove cabinet ministers.&#x20;

Constitutional changes enacted by a specially elected National Constitutional Assembly in 1998 took effect on 10 August 1998. The new constitution strengthens the executive branch by eliminating mid-term congressional elections and by circumscribing Congress' power to challenge cabinet ministers. Party discipline is traditionally weak, and routinely many deputies switch allegiance during each Congress. However, after the new Constitution took effect, the Congress passed a Code of Ethics which imposes penalties on members who defy their party leadership on key votes.[^cite_note-6]&#x20;

Beginning with the 1996 election, the more indigenous, less Spanish-rooted, ethnic groups abandoned their traditional policy of shunning the official political system and participated actively. The indigenous population has established itself as a significant force in Ecuadorian politics, as shown by the selection of indigenous representative [Nina Pacari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nina_Pacari), who led the indigenous political party, Pachakutik, as Second Vice-President of the 1998 Congress.[^cite_note-7]&#x20;

### The former Supreme Court of Ecuador

New justices of the Supreme Court of Ecuador were elected by the sitting members of the court. A bare majority of Congress, acting in a special session called by former President [Lucio Gutiérrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucio_Guti%C3%A9rrez) in December 2004, ousted 27 of the 31 justices and replaced them with new members chosen by Congress; notwithstanding, the lack of any provisions permitting impeachment of Supreme Court justices by Congress and the specific provisions giving the Court the power to select new members.[^cite_note-8] Earlier, in November 2004, Congress replaced the majority of judges on the country's Electoral Court and [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Ecuador) by a similar process.&#x20;

### Reorganization of Court (2008)

After the adoption of a new Constitution in 2008, the judicial branch of the country was completely renewed to provide a cooperative leadership by having a judicial and an administrative head. As such, the bodies of Ecuador's judicial branch now consisted of the National Court of Justice, provincial courts (created by the National Court), tribunals and judges, National Council of the Judicature, Public Defendants' Office, and State Attorneys' Office. The 2008 Constitution also led to the creation of the Constitutional Court of Ecuador.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]&#x20;

#### National Court of Justice

The National Court of Justice seats 21 judges elected for a period of 9 years. They are elected by the Judiciary Council based on a merits contest held by that office. They are the final stage of any judicial process serving as a Court of Cassation and create binding precedent based on Triple Reiterative Rulings from the Chambers of the Court. The President of the Court is elected among the members of the Court for a Period of three years, representing the Judicial Branch before the State.[^cite_note-11]&#x20;

#### Judiciary Council

The administrative branch of the judicial power consists of the Judiciary Council. The Council is formed by 9 Vocals who are elected by the Branch of Transparency and Social Control, which is formed by the Control Authorities of the State. The Vocals are elected also by a merits contest and it shall be formed by six experts in law and 3 experts in management, economics and other related areas. However, after the National Referendum that took place on 5 May 2011 led to the passing of a proposition impulsed by the government, the Judiciary Council changed its formation by making a constitutional amendment. Currently, a Tri-Party Commission is serving as a Transitional Council with delegates from the Legislative, Executive and Transparency Branch, in order to reform the broken judicial system of the country.&#x20;

### Constitutional Court of Ecuador

The [Constitutional Court of Ecuador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Ecuador) does not exercise legal revision, but rather constitutional control of situations where constitutional rights are violated. Also they are the sole body in the State to interpret what the Constitution says.&#x20;

## Executive branch



[Carondelet Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carondelet_Palace), seats the [executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power).

### Structure

The executive branch includes 28 ministries. Provincial governors and councilors, like mayors, aldermen and parish boards, are directly elected. Congress meets throughout the year except for recess in July and December. There are 20 seven-member congressional committees.[^cite_note-\:0-13]&#x20;

### Presidency

The President and Vice-President are elected on the same ticket by popular vote for a four-year term.[^cite_note-\:0-13]&#x20;

#### Controversy surrounding Lucio Gutiérrez

On 20 April 2005, by an act of Congress, [Lucio Gutiérrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucio_Gutierrez) was dramatically removed from office. The last election was held on 20 October 2002 and a runoff election on 24 November 2002 (with the next one to be held in 2006 per the four-year term limits).[^cite_note-nyulawglobal.org-14]&#x20;

Former Vice-President [Alfredo Palacio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Palacio) assumed the presidency on 20 April 2005 after Congress removed [Lucio Gutiérrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucio_Guti%C3%A9rrez) amid escalating street protests precipitated by growing criticism of Gutiérrez's Supreme Court appointments.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]&#x20;

#### Presidency of Rafael Correa

A presidential [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_general_election,_2006) was held on 15 October and 26 November 2006. [Rafael Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Correa) defeated [Álvaro Noboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Noboa) in a [run-off election](25932.md), or second and final round. Correa won with 56.8% of the vote.[^cite_note-17] There was an [attempted coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Ecuador_crisis) against President Rafael Correa in 2010.[^cite_note-18] *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* described Correa as "a left-wing populist",[^cite_note-19] while *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* has characterized Correa's ideological approach as being "economically populist, socially conservative, \[and] quasi-authoritarian".[^cite_note-20]&#x20;

Rafael Correa's three consecutive terms (from 2007 to 2017) were followed by [Lenín Moreno](8187373.md)'s four years as president (2017–21).&#x20;

#### Current officeholders

The 11 April 2021 [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Ecuadorian_general_election) run-off vote ended in a win for conservative former banker, [Guillermo Lasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Lasso), taking 52.4% of the vote compared to 47.6% of left-wing economist [Andrés Arauz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Arauz), supported by exiled former president, Rafael Correa. Previously, President-elect Lasso finished second in the 2013 and 2017 presidential elections.[^cite_note-21] On 24 May 2021, Guillermo Lasso was sworn in as the new President of Ecuador, becoming the country's first right-wing leader in 14 years.[^cite_note-22]&#x20;

On 15 October 2023, center-right candidate [Daniel Noboa](73980059.md) won the run-off of the premature p[residential election](70348458.md) with 52.3% of the vote against leftist candidate [Luisa González](74006819.md).[^cite_note-23] On 23 November 2023, Daniel Noboa was sworn in as Ecuador’s new president.[^cite_note-24] In April 2025, President Daniel Noboa won the run-off round of Ecuador's [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Ecuadorian_general_election), with [Maria Jose Pinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Jose_Pinto)[^cite_note-25] as vice president, meaning he will now serve a full four-year term.[^cite_note-26]&#x20;

## Legislative branch



[National Assembly](29017747.md)

Ecuador has a [unicameral](204304.md) [National Assembly](29017747.md) (*Asamblea Nacional* in Spanish), and it has 137 primary (seat-holding) members (all of whom are elected by popular vote to serve four-year terms). It is based on provincial [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency), but it also has members coming from a national list and it has members representing the emigrant community.[^cite_note-27]&#x20;

### History

On 29 November 2007, the [Ecuadorian Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecuadorian_Constituent_Assembly) dismissed [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_\(Ecuador\)) on charges of corruption and then assumed legislative powers for itself. The Constituent Assembly then proposed a new National Assembly, which is the current institution.[^cite_note-28]&#x20;

## Legal system

Ecuador's legal system is based on the civil law system. Ecuador recently accepted compulsory [International Court of Justice](14918.md) jurisdiction.[^cite_note-nyulawglobal.org-14]&#x20;

## Female representation in the Assembly

In 1979, there was no female representation. By 1984, there was only 4.2% of female representation with three deputies. In 1986, the number was reduced to one female parliamentarian.&#x20;

Between 1988 and 1996, the average percentage of female representation was around of 5%. The following trends occurred:&#x20;

- 1998: 13%

- 2003: 18%

- 2006: 26%

- 2007: 35%

- 2009: 29%

- 2013: 42%

In 2017, the leadership was led by three women: [Gabriela Rivadeneira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Rivadeneira) (President), [Rosana Alvarado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosana_Alvarado) (First Vice-President) and [Marcela Aguiñaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcela_Agui%C3%B1aga) (Second Vice-President).[^cite_note-29]&#x20;
