A **press secretary** or **press officer** is a senior advisor who provides advice on how to deal with the [news media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media) and, using [news management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_management) techniques, helps their employer to maintain a positive public image and avoid negative media coverage.

## Duties and functions

They often, but not always, act as the organization's senior [spokesperson](338047.md). Many [governments](12229.md) also have deputy press secretaries. A deputy press secretary is typically a mid-level political staffer who assists the press secretary and communications director with aspects of public outreach. They often write the [press releases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_release) and media advisories for review by the press secretary and [communications director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communications_director). There are usually assistant press secretaries and press officers that support the press secretary. Press secretaries also give declarations to the media when a particular event happens or an issue arises inside an organization. They are expected, therefore, to have in-depth knowledge about the institution or organization they represent, and to be able to explain and answer questions about the organization's policies, views upon a particular issue and its official standpoint on problematic questions.

## History

The role of the press officer originated in Europe in the 19th century, created by governments.[^cite_note-1]

In the United States, [Stephen T. Early](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Early), who worked for President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt), is credited as being America's first modern presidential press secretary.[^cite_note-2]
