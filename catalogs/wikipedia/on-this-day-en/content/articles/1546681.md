The **Embraer EMB 110 Bandeirante** (English: *pioneer*[^cite_note-centhist_embraer-2]) is a Brazilian twin-[turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop) light transport [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft) designed by [Embraer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer) for [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) and [civil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_aviation) use.

The EMB 110 was designed by the French engineer [Max Holste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Holste); it had been designed in line with specifications issued by the [Brazilian Ministry of Aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_General_Command_for_Aerospace_Technology_\(CTA\)) in 1965.[^cite_note-Pioneer_p163-4-3] The goal was to create a general purpose aircraft, suitable for both civilian and military roles with a low operational cost and high reliability. On 26 October 1968, the *YV-95* prototype performed its [maiden flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_flight); an additional two *EMB 110* development aircraft would follow along with an initial order for 80 transport aircraft for the [Brazilian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Air_Force) in the following year. [Type certification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certification) was received from the Brazilian aviation authorities in late 1972, permitting its entry to service in April 1973 with the Brazilian airline company [Transbrasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransBrasil_\(airline\)).

Various customers in both the military and civilian sectors opted to procure the EMB 110 during its 22-year production run. Over one hundred examples would serve with the Brazilian Air Force, who would modernise numerous examples during the twenty-first century to permit their continued operation. The EMB 110, being customisable to suit various roles and operator requirements, was adapted for various specialist roles, including [aerial observation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_observation), [maritime patrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_patrol_aircraft), and [search and rescue](172599.md) missions. During the 1970s, Embraer opted to design an enlarged derivative of the EMB 110, designated as the [EMB 120 Brasilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_EMB_120_Brasilia); being faster, outfitted with a [pressurized cabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_pressurization), and able to accommodate up to 30 passengers, Embraer opted to concentrate its resources on the new aircraft. As a result, production of the EMB 110 was terminated in 1990.

## Design and development

### Background



YC-95 first prototype (EMB-100) in [Aerospace Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museu_Aeroespacial), [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md)

The origins of the EMB 110 Bandeirante can be traced back to the issuing of a directive by the [Brazilian Ministry of Aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_General_Command_for_Aerospace_Technology_\(CTA\)) in 1965; this called for the production of a transport aircraft for both civilian and military operations that would be reliable and possess low operating costs. It was to be equipped with [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop) engines, a low-mounted wing, and have sufficient capacity to accommodate eight personnel; these stipulations had been drawn from a study of Brazilian commercial air traffic, and aimed to produce an aircraft that would be well suited to the existing airport infrastructure of the country at that time. The resulting specification that had been generated under the *IPD-6504* programme would greatly shape the future aircraft.[^cite_note-Pioneer_p163-4-3][^cite_note-centhist_embraer-2]

Early work on what would become the EMB 110 actually predates the establishment of its manufacturer, [Embraer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer), which was founded in August 1969. The lead designer was the French engineer Max Holste.[^cite_note-Pioneer_p163-4-3] Construction of the prototype was supervised by the Brazilian [aeronautical engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeronautical_engineer) Ozires Silva, who would also play a key role in founding and running of Embraer. The company was created to undertake the aircraft's commercialisation and serial production.[^cite_note-busairnews_2018-4]



EMB 110A cockpit



EMB 110 cabin, operated by [Air Rarotonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Rarotonga)

On 26 October 1968, the first prototype, carrying the military designation *YC-95*, performed its [maiden flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_flight) from [São José dos Campos Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Jos%C3%A9_dos_Campos_Airport).[^cite_note-Pioneer_p164-5] Piloted by José Mariotto Ferreira and accompanied by flight engineer Michel Cury, it landed after roughly 50 minutes. Prior to this point, a total of 110,000 project hours had been worked, producing 12,000 manufacturing drawings, supported by 22,000 hours of structural and aerodynamic calculations; an estimated 282,000 hours of aircraft manufacturing and tooling has also been expended.[^cite_note-busairnews_2018-4] The prototype was officially presented before various civil and military officials along with members of the press at an event held four days later, during which its maiden flight was repeated.[^cite_note-busairnews_2018-4] The positive performance of the prototype led to production of the aircraft, and thus the establishment of Embraer, being approved in mid-1969; the newly created company would assume responsibility for its production on 2 January 1970.[^cite_note-centhist_embraer-2]

### Redesign and quantity production



*EMB 110 Bandeirante* prototype at [Memorial Aeroespacial Brasileiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Aeroespacial_Brasileiro?action=edit\&redlink=1), [São Jose dos Campos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Jose_dos_Campos)

An additional pair of prototypes were constructed, which were designated *EMB 100*.[^cite_note-6] On 19 October 1969, the second prototype performed its first flight, while the third prototype followed on 29 June 1970. While these prototypes yielded positive test results, it was recognised that market conditions had shifted to the point where an eight-seat aircraft appeared to be less viable than it had previously appeared; thus, it was decided to quickly redesign the EMB 100 into the *EMB 110 Bandeirante*, which featured several technological advances along with greater capacity.[^cite_note-centhist_embraer-2]

In May 1970, the programme was bolstered by the [Brazilian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Air_Force) (FAB) deciding to issue Embraer with an initial order for 80 production aircraft.[^cite_note-centhist_embraer-2] Near the end of 1972, the Bandeirante received its Brazilian [airworthiness certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate).[^cite_note-Pioneer_p165-7] On 9 February 1973, the first delivery was made to FAB.[^cite_note-Pioneer_p165-7][^cite_note-8]

In a typical configuration, the EMB 110 seated between 15 and 21 passengers, and was flown by a pair of pilots. Various configurations and customisations were possible in order to suit customers' diverse requirements and operating conditions. The *EMB 110P1A/41* model, which was furnished with seats for 18 passengers, had a length of 15.1 metres (50 ft), a height of 4.92 metres (16.1 ft), and a wingspan of 15.33 metres (50.3 ft).[^cite_note-JAWA88-9] It has a maximum cruising speed of 411 km/h (222 kn), while its more economical cruising speed was 341 km/h (184 kn), at which speed an effective range of 1,964 km (1,060 nmi) can be achieved even while retaining reserve fuels for another 45 minutes of flight. The EMB 110 has a service ceiling of 21,500 feet (6,600 m).[^cite_note-JAWA88-9]

During the 1970s, Embraer opted to build on the success of the EMB 110 by designing an enlarged derivative of the aircraft, designated as the [EMB 120 Brasilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_EMB_120_Brasilia); beyond being large enough to accommodate up to 30 passengers, it was also faster and furnished with a [pressurized cabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_pressurization). All further development of the EMB 110 was halted by Embraer during the 1980s in order to concentrate its resources on the further development and production of the newer EMB 120 instead.

## Operational history



EMB 110 registration G-TABS, operated by Skydrift, loading through the large cargo door

Between 1968 and 1990, Embraer constructed a total of 494 aircraft in numerous configurations for a variety of roles. The [passenger model](51215.md) first flew on 9 August 1972 and entered commercial service on 16 April 1973 with the now defunct Brazilian airline company [Transbrasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransBrasil_\(airline\)). On 8 July 1985, the first aircraft to be operated by the [Irish](147575.md) [budget airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budget_airline) [Ryanair](262878.md) in 1985 was 15-seat EMB 110; the airline continued to operate the type up until 1989. By October 2018, 50 years after its first flight and 498 deliveries, about 150 EMB 110s were still operating at airlines, [air taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_taxi), government entities, and [air forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force) around the world.[^cite_note-10] Production of the type came to an end in 1990, the EMB 110 having been superseded by the [Embraer EMB 120 Brasilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embraer_EMB_120_Brasilia), a derived successor.



Embraer EMB-111 patrol aircraft



EMB-110P1 air ambulance, operated by the Mexican Social Security Institute (IMSS) on regular routes and schedules from 1979 to 1982

During February 1973, deliveries of the type commenced to the Brazilian Air Force.[^cite_note-Pioneer_p165-7] A pair of *EMB 111A Patrulha* [maritime patrol aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_patrol_aircraft) were leased to the [Argentine Navy](490409.md) during the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War), acting as a stop-gap measure between the retirement of the service's last [Lockheed SP-2H Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_P-2_Neptune) and the introduction of modified [Lockheed L-188 Electras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_L-188_Electra).[^cite_note-trackerenmalvinas.com.ar-11] On 15 December 2010, the Brazilian Air Force flew its first upgraded EMB 110, which had been equipped with modern avionics equipment. Designated as *C/P-95*, the aircraft has had several new systems installed by Israeli firm [Elbit Systems](1341669.md)' Brazilian subsidiary, *Aeroeletronica*. At the time, the Brazilian Air Force had an active fleet of 96 EMB 110s.[^cite_note-Flightglobal-12] In 2017, the Brazilian Air Force was reportedly operating 48 EMB 110s.[^cite_note-13]

The two crew members died.

No fatalities.

The aircraft crashed on populated area. All 13 occupants of the aircraft and 2 persons on the ground died.

  [^cite_note-50]

All 12 passengers and crew aboard died.

  [^cite_note-51]

  [^cite_note-52]

- 13 January 1993: A [Titan Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_Airways) cargo flight crashed into a hill near [Sellafield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sellafield), en route from [London Southend Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Southend_Airport) to [Glasgow International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow_International_Airport). The flight used *G-ZAPE*, a 110P, and both pilots were killed in the crash.

  [^cite_note-53]

All crew of 3 died.

  [^cite_note-54]

- 19 July 1994: [Alas Chiricanas Flight 00901](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alas_Chiricanas_Flight_00901) Panamanian domestic airline ALAS, registration HP-1202AC using an EMB 110P1, crashed after a bomb exploded in the cabin killing 21, twelve Jewish businessmen were among the passengers.

- 24 May 1995: G-OEAA, an EMB-110-P1 operated by [UK](31717.md) domestic airline [Knight Air Flight 816](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knight_Air_Flight_816) between [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_Bradford_International_Airport) and [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen_Airport) entered a steeply descending spiral dive, broke up in flight and crashed into farmland at [Dunkeswick Moor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunkeswick_Moor) near Leeds. All 12 occupants were killed. The probable cause of the accident was the failure of one or both [artificial horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attitude_indicator) instruments. There was no standby artificial horizon installed (as there was no airworthiness requirement for one on this aircraft) and the accident report concluded that this left the crew without a single instrument available for assured attitude reference or simple means of determining which flight instruments had failed. The aircraft entered a spiral dive from which the pilot, who was likely to have become spatially disoriented, was unable to recover.

The crew of two died.

  [^cite_note-58]

- 17 November 1996: [Brazilian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Air_Force), a P-95 Bandeirante registration FAB-7102 flying from [Salvador da Bahia Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputado_Lu%C3%ADs_Eduardo_Magalh%C3%A3es_International_Airport) to [Natal Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natal_Air_Force_Base) had an accident in the vicinity of [Caruaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caruaru). Four Brazilian Air Force Bandeirantes were flying on formation from Salvador to Natal when the tail of FAB-7102 was struck by the propeller of another aircraft. It crashed after control of the aircraft was lost. All nine occupants died.

  [^cite_note-59]

- 24 July 1999: an [Air Fiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Fiji) EMB 110 Bandeirante registration DQ-AFN on a domestic flight from Nausori to Nadi in the Fiji Islands operating as [Air Fiji Flight 121](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Fiji_Flight_121) crashed on a slope of a ridge. The aircraft had apparently descended below the 5,400 feet (1,600 m) safety altitude until the right wing struck a tree on a ridgeline at 1,300 feet (400 m) altitude. The Bandeirante then broke up and impacted the slope of a ridge 1.3 km (0.81 mi) further on. The tail section and right wing were found 150 m (490 ft) from the main wreckage. Weather at 05\:00 was good: nil wind, 40 km (25 mi) visibility, scattered clouds at 2,200 feet (670 m) and an insignificant small shower band. Investigation revealed that the captain had insufficient rest prior to the flight and that he had consumed an above-therapeutic level of antihistamine prior to the flight, which would have degraded his ability to safely pilot the aircraft. Also Air Fiji's published standard operating procedures were inadequate for the Bandeirante aircraft.

- 26 December 2002: [Brazilian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Air_Force), an EMB 110 Bandeirante registration FAB-2292 en route from [São Paulo-Campo de Marte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_de_Marte_Airport) to [Florianópolis Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florian%C3%B3polis_Air_Force_Base), crashed while trying to carry out an emergency landing at [Curitiba-Afonso Pena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_Pena_International_Airport). Reportedly, both engines had quit. The airplane had taken off with insufficient fuel on board to complete the flight to Florianópolis. Three passengers and crew of the 16 aboard died.

  [^cite_note-60]

- 8 November 2005: an EMB 110, operated by [Wiggins Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiggins_Airways), registration N7801Q, flying a cargo route from [Manchester-Boston Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester-Boston_Regional_Airport) to [Bangor International Airport](1682920.md) suffered an engine failure shortly after takeoff, causing the aircraft to bank and fall to the ground, landing inside of a [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart) garden center 1.4 km (0.87 mi) away from Runway 17/35. The pilot was the only occupant and survived the crash, and the aircraft was written off.

- 7 February 2009: an EMB 110, operated by [Manaus Aerotáxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manaus_Aerot%C3%A1xi), registration PT-SEA, flying a domestic route in Brazil from Coari to Manaus (Amazonas) struggled in bad weather conditions and [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Manaus_Aerot%C3%A1xi_crash) 80 km (50 mi) from Manaus killing 24 passengers. 4 survivors were reported.

  [^cite_note-61]

  [^cite_note-62]

- 3 July 2013: An EMB 110, operated by Batair Cargo, registration ZS-NVB, en route from [Lanseria Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanseria_Airport) in [Johannesburg](58893.md) for Lubumbashi in the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md), crashed while attempting to land in Francistown, Botswana. The pilots had planned to land and refuel but thick mist on the ground caused them to miss the landing strip on their first pass. They called in to the control tower to notify that they would make a second pass because they could see the landing strip, but never did. The wreckage was found two hours later about 10 km (6.2 mi) from the airport. The plane crashed with no survivors.

  [^cite_note-63]

- 17 November 2022: An EMB 110, registration C6-CAB, operated by LeAir Charter Services, flying from [Cap-Haïtien International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap-Ha%C3%AFtien_International_Airport), Haiti, to [Lynden Pindling International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynden_Pindling_International_Airport), Nassau, Bahamas, was substantially damaged when the nosewheel collapsed and it skidded off of the runway when landing at Nassau. During the landing approach, the crew observed problems with the landing gear. After performing a low pass so that the landing gear could be observed by the control tower, the flight circled for several minutes to burn off fuel before another approach to landing, during which the nosewheel collapsed. No injuries were reported.

  [^cite_note-64]

- 16 September 2023: An Embraer EMB 110 Bandeirante carrying 12 passengers and 2 crew from [Eduardo Gomes International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Gomes_International_Airport) [veered off the runway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Manaus_Aerot%C3%A1xi_Embraer_Bandeirante_crash) at [Barcelos Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelos_Airport). All 14 occupants were killed.
