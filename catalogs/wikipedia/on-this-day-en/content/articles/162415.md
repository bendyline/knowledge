The **secretary-general of the United Nations** (**UNSG** or **UNSECGEN**) is the [de facto](43594.md) head of the United Nations who oversees the [United Nations Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretariat), one of the [six principal organs of the United Nations](216034.md#Six_principal_organs), which also represents all 193 nations on the global stage. While the president of the Security Council chairs its meeting, the secretary-general acts as the council's executive officer who oversees the implementation of its decisions and mandates.&#x20;

The role of the secretary-general and of the secretariat is laid out by [Chapter XV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapter_XV_of_the_United_Nations_Charter) (Articles 97 to 101) of the [United Nations Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Charter). However, the office's qualifications, selection process and tenure are open to interpretation; they have been established by custom.[^cite_note-1]

## Selection and term of office

See also: [United Nations Secretary-General selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection)



The [Secretariat Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretariat_Building) is a 154-metre-tall (505 ft) skyscraper and the centerpiece of the [Headquarters of the United Nations](188955.md).

The secretary-general is appointed by the [General Assembly](31957.md) upon the recommendation of the [Security Council](31956.md). As the recommendation must come from the Security Council, any of the five [permanent members of the council can veto](1900272.md) a nomination. Most secretaries-general are compromise candidates from [middle powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_power) and have little prior fame.

Unofficial qualifications for the job have been set by precedent in previous selections. The appointee may not be a citizen of any of the Security Council's five permanent members.[^cite_note-2] The General Assembly resolution 51/241 in 1997 stated that, in the appointment of "the best candidate", due regard should be given to regional (continental) rotation of the appointee's national origin and to gender equality,[^cite_note-SRR2015-3]<sup>: 5 </sup> although no woman has yet served as secretary-general. All appointees to date have been [career diplomats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Career_diplomat).[^cite_note-\:1-4]

The length of the term is discretionary, but all secretaries-general since 1971 have been appointed to five-year terms. Every secretary-general since 1961 has been re-selected for a second term, with the exception of [Boutros Boutros-Ghali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boutros_Boutros-Ghali), who was [vetoed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_vetoed_United_Nations_Security_Council_resolutions) by the United States in the [1996 selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection). While the position does not have a formal [term limit](505825.md),[^cite_note-5] incumbent secretaries-general have avoided seeking a third term since the [1981 selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection), when China cast a record 16 vetoes against a third term for [Kurt Waldheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Waldheim).

The selection process is opaque and is often compared to a [papal conclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_conclave).[^cite_note-nytimes20160722-6][^cite_note-7] Since 1981, the Security Council has voted in secret in a series of [straw polls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straw_polls); it then submits the winning candidate to the General Assembly for ratification. No candidate has ever been rejected by the General Assembly, and only once, [in 1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection), has a candidate been voted upon despite a UNSC veto.[^cite_note-8]

In 2016, the General Assembly and the Security Council sought nominations and conducted public debates for the first time. However, the Security Council voted in private and followed the same process as previous selections, leading the [president of the General Assembly](780080.md) to complain that it "does not live up to the expectations of the membership and the new standard of openness and transparency".[^cite_note-9]

## Powers and duties

The UN Charter designates the secretary-general as the "[chief administrative officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_administrative_officer)" of the UN, and gives the secretary-general the power to make employment decisions about Secretariat staff. The Charter also empowers the secretary-general to inform the Security Council of "any matter which in his opinion may threaten the maintenance of international peace and security", and allows the secretary-general to perform "such other functions as are entrusted" by other United Nations organs. These provisions have been interpreted as providing broad leeway for officeholders to serve a variety of roles as suited to their preferences, skill set, or circumstances.[^cite_note-\:1-4] The UN describes the role of the secretary-general as combining the functions and responsibilities of an advocate, diplomat, civil servant, and chief executive officer.[^cite_note-\:0-10]

The secretary-general's routine duties include overseeing the activities and duties of the secretariat; attending sessions with United Nations bodies; consulting with world leaders, government officials, and other stakeholders; and travelling the world to engage with global constituents and bring attention to certain international issues.[^cite_note-\:0-10] The secretary-general publishes an annual report on the work of the UN, which includes an assessment of its activities and an outline future priorities. The secretary-general is also the chairman of the United Nations System Chief Executives Board for Coordination (CEB), a body composed of the heads of all UN funds, programmes, and specialized agencies, which meets twice a year to discuss substantive and management issues facing the [United Nations System](216034.md).[^cite_note-\:0-10]

Many of the secretary-general's powers are informal and left open to individual interpretation; some appointees have opted for more activist roles, while others have been more technocratic or administrative.[^cite_note-\:1-4] The secretary-general is often reliant upon the use of their "good offices", described as "steps taken publicly and in private, drawing upon his independence, impartiality and integrity, to prevent international disputes from arising, escalating or spreading".[^cite_note-\:0-10] Consequently, observers have variably described the office as the "world's most visible [bully pulpit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bully_pulpit)" or as the "world's moderator".[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-\:1-4] Examples include [Dag Hammarskjöld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dag_Hammarskj%C3%B6ld)'s promotion of an armistice between the warring parties of [Arab-Israel conflict](7960202.md), [Javier Perez de Cuellar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_P%C3%A9rez_de_Cu%C3%A9llar)'s negotiation of a ceasefire in the [Iran-Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), and [U Thant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_Thant)'s role in deescalating the [Cuban Missile Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Missile_Crisis).[^cite_note-\:1-4]

## Residence

The official residence of the secretary-general is a townhouse at 3 [Sutton Place, Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton_Place,_Manhattan), in New York City, United States. The townhouse was built for [Anne Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Morgan_\(philanthropist\)) in 1921 and donated to the United Nations in 1972.[^cite_note-12]

## Initiatives

### Youth 2030

Youth 2030 is a UN system-wide Youth Strategy,[^cite_note-13] launched on 24 September 2018 by the Secretary-General. It "seeks to strengthen and increase commitments at the global, regional and national level to meet young people's needs, help them realize their rights, and recognize their positive contributions as agents of change. Denmark was the first member country to make a financial commitment to the strategy, and on 19 September the [Misk Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misk_Foundation) signed a partnership agreement with the Office of the United Nations Secretary-General's Envoy on Youth, pledging support.[^cite_note-14]

### Our Common Agenda

Main article: [Our Common Agenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Common_Agenda)

[Our Common Agenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Common_Agenda) is "the Secretary-General's vision for the future of global cooperation". It calls for cooperation and multilateralism to promote action that achieves the Sustainable Development Goals.[^cite_note-15]

### Youth Advisory Group on Climate Change

The Secretary-General's Youth Advisory Group on Climate Change was launched on 27 July 2020, in the midst of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-16] The group was convened under the Youth2030 strategy and Our Common Agenda, as a way for the Secretary-General to engage directly with young people on issues related to [climate change](5042951.md).[^cite_note-yag2025-17] Initially established with seven members,[^cite_note-18] it was expanded to 14 with the third cohort beginning its term on 12 August 2025.[^cite_note-yag2025-17] Each group has a two-year term.[^cite_note-19]

| Pérez de Cuéllar was selected after a five-week deadlock between the re-election of Waldheim and China's candidate, [Salim Ahmed Salim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salim_Ahmed_Salim) of [Tanzania](30118.md). Pérez de Cuéllar, a Peruvian diplomat who a decade earlier had served as President of the UN Security Council during his time as Peruvian ambassador to the UN, was a compromise candidate. He became the first and thus far only secretary-general from the Americas. He was re-elected unanimously in 1986.[^cite_note-una-usa-fact-23] |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| **6** |  | [Boutros Boutros-Ghali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boutros_Boutros-Ghali) (1922–2016)[^cite_note-31] | [1 January 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection) | 31 December 1996 | 5 years | [Egypt](8087628.md) | [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Group) |

With a majority in the General Assembly and the support of China, the "Non-Aligned Movement had the votes necessary to block any unfavorable candidate". The Security Council conducted five anonymous straw polls and Boutros-Ghali emerged with 11 votes on the fifth round. In 1996, the United States vetoed the re-appointment of Boutros-Ghali due to political fallout over the [Battle of Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_\(1993\)).[^cite_note-una-usa-fact-23] |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |
| **7** |  | [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan) (1938–2018)[^cite_note-32] | [1 January 1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection) | 31 December 2006 | 10 years | [Ghana](12067.md) | [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Group) |

| **8** |  | [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) (b. 1944)[^cite_note-36] | [1 January 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection) | 31 December 2016 | 10 years | [South Korea](27019.md) | [Asia-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Group) |
| Ban was Foreign Minister of [South Korea](27019.md)[^cite_note-auto-37] and became the first East Asian to be selected as the secretary-general.[^cite_note-auto-37] He was unanimously elected to a second term on 21 June 2011.[^cite_note-38] |  |  |  |  |  |  |  |

| Guterres is the first former head of government to become secretary-general, and the first secretary-general born after the establishment of the United Nations. He was the [prime minister of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Portugal) from 1995 to 2002. He has also been president of the [Socialist International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_International) (1999–2005) and [United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees](31964.md) (2005–2015).
