# Defection

For the song, see [Defection (song)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defection_\(song\)).

"Defector" redirects here. For other uses, see [Defector (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defector_\(disambiguation\)).

Not to be confused with [Defecation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defecation).



A Soviet [Lavochkin La-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavochkin_La-7) fighter aircraft that crash-landed in [Sweden](5058739.md) after being flown there by a defecting pilot, May 1949

In [politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics), a **defector** is a person who gives up [allegiance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegiance) to one state in exchange for allegiance to another, changing sides in a way that is considered illegitimate by the first state.[^cite_note-1] More broadly, **defection** involves abandoning a person, cause, or doctrine to which one is bound by some tie, as of allegiance or duty.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

This term is also applied, often pejoratively, to anyone who switches loyalty to another [religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_disaffiliation), [sports team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_team), [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party), or other rival faction. In that sense, the defector is often considered a [traitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traitor) by their original side.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

A memorial to those who died trying to cross the [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall) stood for ten months in 2004 and 2005 near [Checkpoint Charlie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkpoint_Charlie).

The physical act of defection usually occurs in a manner that violates the laws of the nation or political entity from which the person is seeking to depart. By contrast, mere changes in [citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship), or working with allied militia, usually do not violate any law.

For example, in the 1950s, [East Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_German) were increasingly prohibited from traveling to the western [Federal Republic of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany), where they were automatically regarded as citizens according to [exclusive mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_mandate). To enforce the policy, the Communist [German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Democratic_Republic) erected fortifications along the [Inner German border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_German_border), beginning in 1952, and the [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall) in 1961. When people tried to defect from the GDR, they were to be shot on sight. Several hundred people [were killed along that border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_deaths_at_the_Berlin_Wall) in their [Republikflucht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republikflucht) attempt. Official crossings did exist, but permissions to leave temporarily or permanently were seldom granted. On the other hand, the GDR citizenship of some "inconvenient" East Germans was revoked, and they had to leave their home on short notice against their will. Others, like singer [Wolf Biermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Biermann), were prohibited from returning to the GDR.



&#x20;East German border guard [Konrad Schumann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konrad_Schumann) jumping the border in 1961

During the [Cold War](325329.md), the many people illegally emigrating from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) or [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) to [the West](21208200.md) were called defectors. Westerners defected to the Eastern Bloc as well, often to avoid prosecution as spies. Some of the more famous cases were British spy [Kim Philby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kim_Philby), who defected to the USSR to avoid exposure as a [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB) mole, and [22 Allied POWs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_American_and_British_defectors_in_the_Korean_War) (one Briton and twenty-one Americans) who declined repatriation after the [Korean War](16772.md), electing to remain in China.

When an individual leaves their country and provides information to a foreign [intelligence service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_service), they are considered a [HUMINT source defector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clandestine_HUMINT#Defector). In some cases, defectors remain in the country or with the political entity they were against, functioning as a [defector in place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clandestine_HUMINT#Defector_in_place). Intelligence services are always concerned when debriefing defectors with the possibility of a [fake defection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_defection).

Entire militaries can defect and [choose not to follow orders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insubordination) from a state's leaders. During the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) protests, militaries in Egypt and Tunisia refused orders to fire upon protesters or use other methods to disperse them.[^cite_note-\:0-6][^cite_note-7] The decision to defect can be driven by the desire to prevent insubordination: if a military leader judges that lower officers will disobey orders to fire upon protesters, they could be more likely to defect.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

## Notable defectors

### Artists

- [Paquito D'Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paquito_D'Rivera), Cuban saxophonist and clarinetist, who defected to the United States in 1980.

- [Mikhail Baryshnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Baryshnikov), [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_people) (Russian) dancer, who defected to [Canada](5042916.md) in 1974, while in [Toronto](64646.md), touring with the [Kirov Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirov_Ballet).

  [^cite_note-8]

  &#x20;He later moved to the [United States](3434750.md).

- [Natalia Makarova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Makarova), Soviet (Russian) dancer, who defected while in London in 1970.

- [Georgi Markov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgi_Markov), Bulgarian author, who defected in 1968, eventually settling in London, England, later assassinated.

- [Rudolf Nureyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Nureyev), Soviet (Russian) dancer, who defected while in [Paris](22989.md) touring with the [Kirov Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirov_Ballet) in 1961.

  [^cite_note-9]

- [George Balanchine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Balanchine), Georgian [choreographer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choreography), who defected to the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic) in 1924.

- [Arturo Sandoval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Sandoval), Cuban trumpeter, pianist, and composer, who defected to the United States in 1990.

- [Jan Sobota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Sobota), Czech fine bookbinder, who defected to Switzerland in 1982, and settled in the United States in 1984.

### Military

- [Larry Allen Abshier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Allen_Abshier), the first of six American soldiers to defect to North Korea between the years 1962–1982. He died in 1983 from a heart attack while residing in [Pyongyang](47948.md).

- [Benedict Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedict_Arnold)‚ a colonial general who during the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) defected to the [British Army](4887.md).

- [Riad al-Asaad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riad_al-Asaad), founder of the [Free Syrian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Syrian_Army) and the entire Tlass Family during the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md).

- [Viktor Belenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Belenko), a [Soviet Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Force) lieutenant who flew a [MiG-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-25) fighter to Japan in 1976 and gained [political asylum](1621432.md) in the United States.

  [^cite_note-12]

- [James Joseph Dresnok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Joseph_Dresnok), a [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army) private who defected to [North Korea](21255.md) by sneaking across the Demilitarized Zone in 1962. He would live the remainder of his life in the DPRK until his death in 2016.

- [Igor Gouzenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Gouzenko), a Soviet [cipher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cipher) clerk who defected to Canada and released information regarding Soviet espionage activities in western society. Credited as one of the triggering factors for the beginning of the Cold War.

- [No Kum-Sok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_Kum-Sok) (later Kenneth Rowe) is known for having been a [lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant) in the [North Korean Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korean_Air_Force) during the [Korean War](16772.md) who defected to [South Korea](27019.md). On September 21, 1953, he flew his [MiG-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-15) to the [Kimpo Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimpo_Air_Base) in South Korea, claiming that he wanted to get away from the "red deceit" and is often associated with [Operation Moolah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Moolah).

  [^cite_note-nmusaf-13]

- [Maxim Kuzminov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxim_Kuzminov), former Russian military pilot-navigator of the Mi-8AMTSh military transport helicopter. During the Russian-Ukrainian War, on August 9, 2023, he flew across the front line to the Ukrainian side as part of the special [Operation Synytsia](74756277.md), prepared by the Main Intelligence Directorate of the Ukrainian Defense Ministry.

  [^cite_note-14]

- [Genrikh Lyushkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genrikh_Lyushkov), the [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD) chief in the [Russian Far East](173135.md), defected to [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo) in 1938 under the [Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Purge) and then cooperated with the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army).

- [Ivan Mazepa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Mazepa), [Ukrainian](145144.md) [Hetman of Zaporizhian Host](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetman_of_Zaporizhian_Host) from 1687–1708 who defected from the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) to the [Swedish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire) during the [Battle of Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poltava) of the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War).

- [Lee Harvey Oswald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Harvey_Oswald), the later [assassin of President John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_John_F._Kennedy) had claimed defection to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in October 1959 but was ultimately refused citizenship and returned to the United States in 1962.

- [Ion Mihai Pacepa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Mihai_Pacepa), a [Romanian](25445.md) [Securitate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securitate) general who defected to the United States from the [Socialist Republic of Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania) in 1978.

- [Matiur Rahman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matiur_Rahman_\(military_pilot\)), a Pakistani/Bangladeshi pilot who in 1971 attempted to defect with a T-33 along with confidential Pakistani war plans to India to join the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War). However his plan was foiled by Flt.Lt [Rashid Minhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Minhas) who crashed the plane after a brief struggle for control over the aircraft. The plane crashed some 50 Kilometres from the border.

- [Heng Samrin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heng_Samrin), a top-brass military figure in [Democratic Kampuchea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Kampuchea) defected to [Vietnam](202354.md) during the [Khmer Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge) purges of the Eastern Zone after considering the fate of [So Phim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/So_Phim), his superior in command.

  [^cite_note-16]

- [Samson Makintsev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samson_Makintsev) (1740–1849), a sergeant of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) who deserted to [Qajar Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran) and became a general in the Persian military, becoming the commander of the [Bogatyr battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogatyr_battalion), a Persian military battalion made up of Russian deserters, during the reign of [Abbas Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_Mirza).

- [Travis King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_King), a US Army private who defected to North Korea, possibly to avoid facing a dishonorable discharge and legal charges, in 2023. North Korea would later return him to American custody.

### Others

- [Viktor Korchnoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Korchnoi), Russian chess Grandmaster, defected in Amsterdam in 1976.

- [Walter Polovchak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Polovchak), minor, defected to the United States in 1980 at 12. He and his parents moved to the United States from [Soviet Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_SSR) in 1980 but later that year his parents decided to move back to Ukraine. He did not wish to return with them and was the subject of a five-year struggle to stay permanently. He won the right to permanent sanctuary in 1985 upon turning 18.

- The Soviet crew of the [oil tanker Tuapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_the_Tuapse), held hostage in 1954 by the government of [Taiwan](25734.md) during the [White Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Terror_\(Taiwan\)). An unusual case of forced defection, where the crew were forced to defect to the United States to secure their release. Those who refused were subjected to various forms of torture, while those who subsequently retracted their defection and returned to the Soviet Union were sentenced for treason but later pardoned. All surviving crew were released in 1988.

- [Vaas Feniks Nokard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaas_Feniks_Nokard?action=edit\&redlink=1), in order to defect from Russia in 2021, swam from [Kunashir Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunashir_Island) to [Hokkaido](58092.md), a distance of about 20 kilometers, in 23 hours.

- [Oh Chong-song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oh_Chong-song) is a North Korean defector who was shot 5 times by North Korean soldiers for crossing the [Military Demarcation Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Demarcation_Line), he was rescued by South Korean soldiers.
