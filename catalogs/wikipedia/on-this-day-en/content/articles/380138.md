The **Dnieper** or **Dnepr** ([/(də)ˈniːpər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*(də-)NEE-pər*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)),[^cite_note-2] also known as the **Dnipro** ([/(də)ˈniːproʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*(də-)NEE-proh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)),[^cite_note-3] is one of the major [transboundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transboundary_river) [rivers of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Europe), rising in the [Valdai Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdai_Hills) near [Smolensk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smolensk), Russia, before flowing through Belarus and Ukraine to the [Black Sea](3386.md). Approximately 2,200 km (1,400 mi) long,[^cite_note-statistics-4] with a [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) of 504,000 square kilometres (195,000 mi<sup>2</sup>), it is the longest river of Ukraine and Belarus and the fourth-[longest river in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Europe#Rivers_of_Europe_by_length), after the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga), [Danube](21209639.md), and [Ural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_\(river\)) rivers.[^cite_note-5]

In antiquity, the river was part of the [Amber Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber_Road) trade routes. During [the Ruin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ruin_\(Ukrainian_history\)) in the later 17th century, the area was contested between the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) and the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Russia), dividing what is now Ukraine into areas described by its [right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-bank_Ukraine) and [left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-bank_Ukraine) banks. During the [Soviet period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), the river became noted for its major [hydroelectric dams](381399.md) and large reservoirs. The 1986 [Chernobyl disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster) occurred on the [Pripyat River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pripyat_\(river\)), a tributary of the Dnieper, just upstream from its confluence with the Dnieper. The Dnieper is an important navigable [waterway](174851.md) for the [economy of Ukraine](31829.md) and is connected by the [Dnieper–Bug Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper%E2%80%93Bug_Canal) to other waterways in Europe. During the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), certain segments of the river were made part of the defensive lines between territory controlled by the Russians and the Ukrainians.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Names

### Dnieper



Human representation of the Dnieper river (known as [Borysthenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borysthenes)) on an Ancient Greek coin of [Pontic Olbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic_Olbia), 4th–3rd century BC



Pre-1918 photo with the old spelling of Dnieper (Днѣпръ)

The river is also sometimes called by the [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) name *Dnepr*[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] (Днепр,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFelecanBughesiu2021406:_"To_Skythians_\[...\]_are_traditionally_ascribed_Russia's_oldest_surviving_toponyms—river_names_''Дон''_(''Don'')_and_''Днепр''_(''Dnepr,_Dnieper'')"-11][^cite_note-\:2-12] [pre-revolutionary spelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reforms_of_Russian_orthography#The_post-revolution_reform) Днѣпръ, *Dněpr*).[^cite_note-13] The initial D in Dnieper is generally silent when pronounced in English, although it may be sounded: [/ˈniːpər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)[^cite_note-14] or [/dəˈniːpər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English).[^cite_note-\:0-15]

*Dnipro* derives from [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Дніпро, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Dnipro*.[^cite_note-\:2-12] The English pronunciation is [/dəˈniːproʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English).[^cite_note-16] The Ukrainian name has a rare form Дніпр, *Dnipr* and rare dialectal Дніпер, *Dniper*.[^cite_note-\:1-17] The [Middle Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenian_language) form attested in the 16th to 18th centuries was Днѣпръ, *Dnǐpr*.[^cite_note-\:1-17] The city of [Dnipro](989795.md) is named for the river.

In [Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language), the river is called Дняпро, *Dniapro*, or Днепр, *Dniepr*.[^cite_note-18]

These names are all cognate, deriving from [Old East Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_East_Slavic_language) Дънѣпръ (*Dŭněprŭ*). The origin of this name is disputed but generally derived from either [Sarmatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarmatian_language) \**Dānu Apara* ("Farther River") in parallel with the [Dniester](376633.md) ("Nearer River") or from [Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythian_language) \**Dānu Apr* ("Deep River") in reference to its lack of [fords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_ford),[^cite_note-Mallory,_J.P_2000._p._106-19][^cite_note-20] from which was also derived the [Late Antique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Antiquity) name of the river, Δάναπρις *Danapris*, as found in the [Ravenna Cosmography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna_Cosmography).[^cite_note-smithdictborys-21][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFelecanBughesiu2021406-22]

### Borysthenes

The earlier Graeco-Roman name of the river, as attested by [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus), was "[Borysthenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borysthenes)" ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Βορυσθένης, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ancient_Greek): *Borusthénēs*;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFelecanBughesiu2021406-22] [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Borysthenes*, [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Бористен, Борисфен, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Borysten, Borysfen*[^cite_note-\:1-17]) and later Δάναπρις *Danapris*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFelecanBughesiu2021406-22] The name *Borysthenes* was derived from a Scythian name whose form was:

- either *Baurastāna*, meaning "yellow place",

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBukharin201323-23]

- or *Baurustāna* meant "place of beavers".

  [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKullanda201339–41-24]

  - This name was linked to the mantle of beaver skins worn by the Iranic water goddess [Arəduuī Sūrā Anāhitā](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anahita), whose epithet of *āp* ([Avestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avestan_language): 𐬁𐬞, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'water') was connected to the name of the daughter of the river-god Borysthenēs in Scythian mythology, the Earth-and-Water goddess [Api](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythian_religion#Api), whose own name meant "water".

    [^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKullanda201339–41-24]

[Ovid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovid) used *Borysthenius*, an adjective derived from *Borysthenes*, as the river's poetic Latin name.[^cite_note-l\&sborysthenes2-25]

### Var

The [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns)' name for the river, *Var*, was derived from Scythian *\*Varu*, meaning "Broad". This name was connected to the Graeco-Roman name of the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga) river, *Oarus* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Ὄαρος, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ancient_Greek): *Óaros*; [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Oarus*), which was also derived from Scythian *\*Varu*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarmatta1999129-26]

### Other names

In Ukrainian it is also known poetically as Славутич, *Slavutych* or Славута, *Slavuta*,[^cite_note-\:1-17] from an old name used in [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus'). This is due to the influence of the [Old East Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_East_Slavic) [epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epic_poetry) *[The Tale of Igor's Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tale_of_Igor's_Campaign)* and its modern adaptations on Ukrainian literature. This usage also lent its name to the city of [Slavutych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavutych), founded in the wake of the [Chernobyl disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_disaster) in 1986 to house displaced workers,[^cite_note-Yatzik2-27] and to the [Slavutych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavutych_\(Kyiv_Metro\)) station of the [Kyiv Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Metro).

In [Crimean Tatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatar_language), the river is known as *Özü*. In [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language) it is *Özü* or *Özi* ([Ottoman Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turkish_language): اوزی),[^cite_note-28] which was derived from [Ochakiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochakiv).[^cite_note-29]

## Geography

See also: [List of crossings of the Dnieper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_crossings_of_the_Dnieper_River)

The total length of the river is variously given as 2,145 kilometres (1,333 mi)[^cite_note-statistics-4] or 2,201 km (1,368 mi),[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] of which 485 km (301 mi) are within Russia, 700 km (430 mi) are within [Belarus](3457.md),[^cite_note-statistics-4] and 1,095 km (680 mi) are within [Ukraine](31750.md). Its basin covers 504,000 square kilometres (195,000 mi<sup>2</sup>), of which 289,000 km<sup>2</sup> (112,000 mi<sup>2</sup>) are within Ukraine,[^cite_note-EoU-34] 118,360 km<sup>2</sup> (45,700 mi<sup>2</sup>) are within Belarus.[^cite_note-statistics-4]

The source of the Dnieper is the sedge bogs (Akseninsky Mokh) of the [Valdai Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdai_Hills) in central Russia, at an elevation of 220 m (720 ft).[^cite_note-EoU-34] For 115 km (71 mi) of its length, it serves as the border between Belarus and Ukraine. Its estuary, or [liman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liman_\(landform\)), used to be defended by the strong fortress of Ochakiv.[^cite_note-35]

The southernmost point in Belarus is on the Dnieper to the south of [Kamaryn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamaryn) in [Brahin Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahin_Raion).[^cite_note-36]

### Rapids



Rapids at Dnieper in 1915



Tractus Borysthenis or Dnieper (from Bovzin city to Chortyca island) in 1662

The [Dnieper Rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Rapids) were part of the [trade route from the Varangians to the Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route_from_the_Varangians_to_the_Greeks), first mentioned in the [Kyiv Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Chronicle). The route was probably established in the late 8th and early 9th centuries and gained importance from the 10th until the early part of the 11th century. On the Dnieper the [Varangians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varangian) had to [portage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portage) their ships round seven rapids, where they had to be on guard for [Pecheneg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecheneg) nomads.

Along this middle flow of the Dnieper, there were 9 major rapids (although some sources cite a smaller number), obstructing almost the whole width of the river, about 30 to 40 smaller rapids, obstructing only part of the river, and about 60 islands and islets.

After the Dnieper hydroelectric station was built in 1932, they were inundated by [Dnieper Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Reservoir).

### Canals

There are a number of canals connected to the Dnieper:

- The [Dnipro – Donbas Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_%E2%80%93_Donbas_Canal);

- The [Dnipro – Kryvyi Rih Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_%E2%80%93_Kryvyi_Rih_Canal);

- The [Kakhovka Irrigation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakhovka_Irrigation_System) (including [Kakhovka Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakhovka_Canal), SE [Kherson Oblast](1273370.md));

- The Krasnoznamianka Irrigation System (SW [Kherson Oblast](1273370.md));

- The [North Crimean Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Crimean_Canal)—will largely solve the water problem of the peninsula, especially in the arid northern and eastern [Crimea](163045.md);

- The Inhulets Irrigation System.

### Fauna

The river is part of the [quagga mussel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quagga_mussel)'s native range.[^cite_note-Benson-38] The mussel has been accidentally introduced around the world, where it has become an [invasive species](67554.md).[^cite_note-Benson-38]

### Delta

The city of [Kherson](1025861.md) lies on the northern bank, upstream of the Dnieper [delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta), before the Dnieper meets the [Southern Bug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Bug) river in the [Dnieper–Bug estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper%E2%80%93Bug_estuary).

## Ecology

Nowadays the Dnieper River suffers from [anthropogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropogenic_hazard) influence resulting in numerous emissions of pollutants.[^cite_note-39] The Dnieper is close to the [Prydniprovsky Chemical Plant radioactive dumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prydniprovsky_Chemical_Plant_radioactive_dumps) (near [Kamianske](1261408.md)) and susceptible to leakage of its radioactive waste. The river is also close to the [Chernobyl Nuclear Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_Nuclear_Power_Station) ([Chernobyl Exclusion Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_Exclusion_Zone)) which is located next to the mouth of the [Pripyat River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pripyat_River).

## Navigation

Almost 2,000 km (1,200 mi) of the river is navigable (to the city of [Dorogobuzh](3048981.md)).[^cite_note-splendid-37] 90% of [river transport in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Ukraine) occurs on the Dnieper,[^cite_note-40] however, its overall contribution to total transport is less than 0.5%.[^cite_note-41] Its reservoirs have large ship locks, allowing vessels of up to 270 by 18 metres (886 ft × 59 ft) access as far as the port of [Kyiv](585629.md), and thus are an important transportation corridor. The river is used by passenger vessels as well. Inland cruises on the rivers [Danube](21209639.md) and Dnieper have had a growing market in recent decades.

Upstream from Kyiv, the Dnieper receives the water of the [Pripyat River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pripyat_River). This navigable river connects to the [Dnieper-Bug canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper-Bug_canal), the link with the [Bug River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bug_River). Historically, a connection with the Western European waterways was possible, but a [weir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weir) without any ship lock near the town of [Brest, Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brest,_Belarus), has interrupted this international waterway. Poor political relations between Western Europe and Belarus mean there is little likelihood of reopening this waterway in the near future.[^cite_note-42] River navigation is interrupted each year by freezing and severe winter storms.

From the mouth of the Pripyat to the [Kakhovka Hydroelectric Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakhovka_Hydroelectric_Station), there are six sets of dams and [hydroelectric stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric_station), which produce 10% of Ukraine's electricity.[^cite_note-splendid-37] The Kakhovka dam was [destroyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destruction_of_the_Kakhovka_Dam) on 6 June 2023 during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine),[^cite_note-ISW_6June2023-43] with the subsequent drying up of the [Kakhovka Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakhovka_Reservoir) revealing the original course of the river in the area and disconnecting four canal networks known as the [Great Meadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Meadow,_Ukraine).[^cite_note-44]

The first constructed was the [Dnieper Hydroelectric Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Hydroelectric_Station) (or DniproHES) near [Zaporizhzhia](1873579.md), built between 1927 and 1932 with an output of 558 MW.[^cite_note-45] It was destroyed during [World War II](32927.md), but was rebuilt from 1944–1950 with an output of 750 MW, supplying 3.5 billion kWh of electricity annually.[^cite_note-46]

## In the arts

### Literature

The River Dnieper has been a subject of chapter X of a story by [Nikolai Gogol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Gogol) *[A Terrible Vengeance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Terrible_Vengeance)* (1831, published in 1832 as a part of the *[Evenings on a Farm Near Dikanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenings_on_a_Farm_Near_Dikanka)* short stories collection). It is considered as a classical example of the description of nature in Russian literature. The river was also described in the works of [Taras Shevchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko).

In the adventure novel *[The Long Ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Long_Ships)* (also translated *Red Orm*), set during the [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age), a [Scanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scania) chieftain travels to the Dnieper Rapids to retrieve a treasure hidden there by his brother, encountering many difficulties. The novel was very popular in Sweden and is one of few to depict a Viking voyage to eastern Europe.

### Films

The River Dnieper makes an appearance in the 1964 Hungarian drama film *The Sons of the Stone-Hearted Man* (based on the novel of the same name by [Mór Jókai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B3r_J%C3%B3kai)), where it appears when two characters are leaving [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md) but get attacked by wolves.

In 1983, the concert program "Song of the Dnieper" from the "Victory Salute" series was released, dedicated to the 40th anniversary of the liberation of the city of [Kiev](585629.md) from the German fascist invaders. The program includes songs by Soviet composers, [Ukrainian folk songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_folk_music), and dances performed by the Song and Dance Ensemble of the Kiev Military District led by A. ### Popular culture

The Dnieper is one of the national symbols of Ukraine.[^cite_note-50] It is mentioned in the [country's national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_anthem_of_Ukraine). Several historical geographical names relating to Ukraine include the name of the river, such as [Dnieper Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_Ukraine) (*Naddniprianshchyna*), [Right-bank Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-bank_Ukraine), and [Left-bank Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-bank_Ukraine). The Ukrainian cities of [Dnipro](989795.md), [Dniprorudne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dniprorudne), [Kamianka-Dniprovska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamianka-Dniprovska) are named after the river.

The [Zaporozhian Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporozhian_Cossacks) lived on the lower Dnieper and their name refers to their location, "beyond the [rapids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapids)".[^cite_note-51]

The [folk metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_metal) band [Turisas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turisas) have a song called "The Dnieper Rapids" on their 2007 album *[The Varangian Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Varangian_Way)*.[^cite_note-52]
