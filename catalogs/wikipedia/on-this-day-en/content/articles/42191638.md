**Smiljan Radić Clarke**[^cite_note-1] (born 21 June 1965) is a [Chilean](5489.md) architect. His work includes residential buildings, cultural institutions, and temporary installations. He won the [Pritzker Architecture Prize](53422.md) in 2026.[^cite_note-Pritzker2026-2]

## Early life and education

Radić was born on 21 June 1965 in [Santiago](51572.md) to [Smiljan Radic Piraíno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiljan_Radic_Pira%C3%ADno?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Cora Clarke Ramírez.[^cite_note-x543-3] His paternal grandfather [emigrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Chileans) to Chile from the island of [Brač](226332.md), in present-day [Croatia](5573.md), in 1919,[^cite_note-Bomb2009-4] and his maternal family has origins in the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-Pritzker2026-2][^cite_note-ArchDaily2026-5]

He studied architecture at the [Pontifical Catholic University of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Catholic_University_of_Chile), graduating in 1989.[^cite_note-ElCroquis2013-6][^cite_note-ElCroquis2025-7] After completing his degree, he studied history at the [Istituto Universitario di Architettura di Venezia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A0_Iuav_di_Venezia) in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), [Italy](14532.md), and traveled in [Europe](9239.md).[^cite_note-Pritzker2026-2]

## Career

Radić founded his architectural practice in Santiago in 1995.[^cite_note-ArchDaily2026-5] The studio has remained small but has produced numerous projects both in Chile and internationally. During his university studies he met the sculptor [Marcela Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcela_Correa), whom he later married.[^cite_note-Pritzker2026-2] The two have collaborated on several projects, including Casa Chica ([Vilches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilches,_Chile?action=edit\&redlink=1), Chile, 1997), a small house constructed in [the Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Andes) of [Central Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Chile).[^cite_note-Pritzker2026-2]

Radić's early projects included residential buildings in Chile, such as Copper House 2 (Talca, 2004–2005)[^cite_note-v593-8] and Pite House (Papudo, 2003–2005).[^cite_note-w561-9] His later work includes cultural and public buildings, installations, and temporary structures. For example, he designed the 2014 edition of the annual [Serpentine Gallery Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpentine_Galleries) in London. The structure consisted of a translucent [fiberglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiberglass) shell supported by large [quarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarry) stones.[^cite_note-10] In 2017 Radić established the Fundación de Arquitectura Frágil in Santiago, which organizes exhibitions and research activities related to experimental architecture.[^cite_note-Pritzker2026-2] Other completed projects include the Regional Theater of Bío-Bío in [Concepción, Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concepci%C3%B3n,_Chile) (2018) and NAVE, a performing arts center in Santiago.[^cite_note-ArchDaily2026-5]

Radić had exhibitions in [Tokyo](30057.md) (2010, 2013, 2016), [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima) (2012), [Zagreb](55906.md) (2015), New York ([MoMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MoMA) 2015 – 2016, 2018).[^cite_note-\:0-11]

In March 2026, he received the [Pritzker Architecture Prize](53422.md).[^cite_note-Pritzker2026-2][^cite_note-Pogrebin-2026-12] Since 2009, he has been an honorary member of the [American Institute of Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Architects), and since 2020 a [corresponding member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academician#Corresponding_member) of the [Croatian Academy of Sciences and Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Academy_of_Sciences_and_Arts).[^cite_note-\:0-11]

In 2026, he was awarded the [National Architecture Award of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Architecture_Award_of_Chile) alongside [Alejandro Aravena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Aravena) "in tribute to a body of work of excellence, widely recognized for its quality, influence, and contribution to the development of the discipline, constituting a legacy of exceptional value for national architecture."[^cite_note-13]

## Architectural approach

Radić's work frequently combines materials such as concrete, stone, timber, glass, and fiberglass. His projects often emphasize relationships between buildings and their surrounding landscapes and environmental conditions.[^cite_note-ArchDaily2026-5]

His designs have been associated with experimentation in construction methods, material expression, and small-scale architectural interventions.[^cite_note-ArchDaily2026-5]

## Personal life

Radic has a long-standing relationship with sculptor [Marcela Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcela_Correa). The two met during their university years and eventually married, establishing a partnership characterized by an ongoing exchange of ideas between sculpture and architecture.[^cite_note-ArchDaily2026-5]
