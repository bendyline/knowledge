The **2025 FIFA Futsal Women's World Cup** was the inaugural edition of the [FIFA Futsal Women's World Cup](72507794.md), an international [futsal](188197.md) competition organized by [FIFA](11049.md) for women's futsal national teams across the world. It was hosted by the [Philippines](23440.md) from November 21 to December 7.[^cite_note-abs-fifa-pff-prep-1][^cite_note-2]

This was the first FIFA tournament hosted by the Philippines,[^cite_note-dugout-ph-to-host-3] the fifth FIFA tournament held in Southeast Asia, after the [1997 FIFA World Youth Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) in Malaysia, the [2004 FIFA U-19 Women's World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_FIFA_U-19_Women's_World_Championship) and [2012 FIFA Futsal World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_FIFA_Futsal_World_Cup) in Thailand and the [2023 FIFA U-17 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIFA_U-17_World_Cup) in Indonesia, and the second futsal World Cup in the region.

A total of 16 teams took part. Continental championships decided the qualifiers in every region, with the exception of Europe, which organized its own qualification process. Qualification took place between August 2024 and May 2025. [Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzania_women's_national_futsal_team) qualified for their first-ever senior FIFA tournament, while this was also [Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_women's_national_futsal_team)'s first FIFA women's competition.

[Brazil](44894373.md) were champions of this maiden edition, defeating [Portugal](59976997.md) in the final and became the first Brazilian women's team to win a FIFA tournament.[^cite_note-4] Besides that pioneering result, they coupled their title with their [fellow countrymen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_national_futsal_team)'s success at the [2024 Futsal World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_FIFA_Futsal_World_Cup).[^cite_note-5]

## Host selection

The World Cup was first announced by [FIFA](11049.md) in December 2022. This was followed by countries expressing their interests to host, including [Finland](10577.md).[^cite_note-6]

On May 15, 2024, the [Philippines](23440.md) was awarded the hosting rights,[^cite_note-FIFA-2024-05-15a-7][^cite_note-FIFA-2024-05-15b-8][^cite_note-9] beating other bidding nations which include [Brazil](3383.md), [Italy](14532.md),[^cite_note-10] and [Spain](26667.md).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] The presence and quality of [indoor arenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_indoor_arenas_in_the_Philippines) in the Philippines and its successful organization of the [2023 FIBA Basketball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIBA_Basketball_World_Cup) were cited as reasons for the selection.[^cite_note-dugout-ph-to-host-3]

## Format

The 16 teams were divided into four groups of four. The top two teams from each group advanced to the knockout phase, comprising quarter-finals, semi-finals, a third-place play-off, and the final match.[^cite_note-FIFA-2024-05-15b-8]

## Venue

All matches of the tournament were held at the [PhilSports Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PhilSports_Arena) in [Pasig](359702.md), [Metro Manila](72340.md).[^cite_note-drops-20]

Originally, there were two confirmed venues; the other site was the [Victorias City Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorias_City_Coliseum) in [Victorias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorias), [Negros Occidental](266813.md). The two venues were approved in January 2025 and confirmed in June 2025.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Victorias was dropped as a host city in September 2025.[^cite_note-drops-20]

Other venues considered within [Metro Manila](72340.md) were the [Mall of Asia Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mall_of_Asia_Arena) in [Pasay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasay), the [Smart Araneta Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Araneta_Coliseum) in [Quezon City](284223.md) and the [Ninoy Aquino Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninoy_Aquino_Stadium) in [Manila](184334.md) were considered.[^cite_note-dugout-ph-to-host-3] Outside the metropolis, named venues included the [Hoops Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoops_Dome) in [Lapu-Lapu City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapu-Lapu_City), the [Seaside City Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaside_City_Arena) in [Cebu City](285868.md),[^cite_note-iloilo-victorias-23][^cite_note-24] and the [University of San Agustin Gym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_San_Agustin) in [Iloilo City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iloilo_City).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-iloilo-victorias-23]

The draw was held at 17\:00 [PHT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Standard_Time) on September 15, 2025, at the BGC Arts Center in [Bonifacio Global City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonifacio_Global_City) in [Taguig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taguig).[^cite_note-26] Sports presenter Mara Aquino hosted the draw. The guests were Brazilian futsal legend [Falcão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falc%C3%A3o_\(futsal_player\)), Croatian coach [Tihana Nemčić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tihana_Nem%C4%8Di%C4%87), Filipino international footballer [Hali Long](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hali_Long), and volleyball player [Vanie Gandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanie_Gandler), all of whom assisted with the draw. The 16 teams are drawn into four groups of four teams, with the host Philippines automatically seeded to Pot 1 and placed into the first position of Group A.[^cite_note-27] The draw then continued with, in order, pots 1, 2, 3, and 4 being drawn, with each team selected then allocated into the first available group alphabetically. The position for the team within the group would then be drawn (for the purpose of the schedule). The sixteen participating teams were drawn in groups of four.[^cite_note-draw-28] Teams from the same confederation could not be drawn into the same group.

## Squads

Main article: [2025 FIFA Futsal Women's World Cup squads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Futsal_Women's_World_Cup_squads)

Each team had to name a preliminary squad of a maximum of 30 players (three of whom must be goalkeepers). From the preliminary squad, the team had to name a final squad of 14 players (two of whom must be goalkeepers) by the FIFA deadline. Players in the final squad could be replaced by a player from the preliminary squad due to serious injury or illness up to 24 hours before the kickoff of the team's first match.

## Group stage

All times are local, [PHT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Standard_Time) ([UTC+8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UTC+08\:00)). The schedule was announced following the draw.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

The top two teams of each group advanced to the quarter-finals.&#x20;

### Tiebreakers

The rankings of teams in each group were determined as follows:[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

1. points obtained in all group matches;

2. points obtained in the matches played between the teams in question;

3. [goal difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_difference) in the matches played between the teams in question;

4. number of goals scored in the matches played between the teams in question;

If two or more teams were equal on the basis of the above four criteria, their rankings were determined as follows:

5. goal difference in all group matches;

6. number of goals scored in all group matches;

7. fair play points in all group matches (only one deduction could be applied to a person in a single match):&#x20;

   - Yellow card: −1 points;

   - Indirect red card (second yellow card): −3 points;

   - Direct red card: −4 points;

   - Yellow card and direct red card: −5 points;

8. drawing of lots by FIFA.

## Marketing

### Logo and slogan



Branding at the PhilSports Arena with a banner of the official slogan "Master The Speed"

The official emblem was launched on March 18, 2025, at the Manila House in [Bonifacio Global City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonifacio_Global_City), [Taguig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taguig).[^cite_note-dynamic-42][^cite_note-43]

The emblem is a combination of visual elements depicting futsal and references to street art and motifs in the Philippines. Some of the Filipino culture references featured in the logo include the [vinta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinta), the [gabbang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabbang) instrument, and traditional [Binakael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binakael) patterns.[^cite_note-dynamic-42]

The visual identity accompanying the logo, alongside the official slogan of the tournament, "Master The Speed", reflects a game that is constantly on the move and the sights and sounds of the host nation, which are combined with depictions of the futsal ball, the shape of the pitch, and the five members of each team. The design also features arrows that portray the fine line between defense and attack in a form of football renowned for its captivating speed and skill.[^cite_note-dynamic-42]

## Controversies

### Neglect of Philippine team

According to a report from [Spin.ph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spin.ph), several players from the Philippine team stated they were "billeted in substandard quarters" and "with no potable water and inadequate food" as they trained before the start of the tournament. An unnamed player reportedly "nearly fainted from hunger" after "having fasted for many hours as she waited for a blood test". There were also days when "no shuttle service was available for the team". When players complained regarding their accommodation, some of them allegedly had to pay for alternative housing. The team also had no strength and conditioning coach, nor a nutritionist. This led to some players resorting to buying from food delivery services themselves. A representative from the Philippine Football Federation (PFF) reached out to publishing website of the article and said that "no such issues were relayed to the association".[^cite_note-48]
