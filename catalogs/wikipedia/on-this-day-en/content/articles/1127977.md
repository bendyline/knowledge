The **Northern Territory Police Force** is the [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police) body that has legal jurisdiction over the [Northern Territory](21638.md) of [Australia](4689264.md). The police force has 1,552 police officers (2023-24 financial year) made up of 87 senior sergeants, 293 sergeants, 868 constables, 182 auxiliaries, and 55 Aboriginal Community Police Officers.[^cite_note-AR2024-1] The rest of the positions are officers of commissioned rank. It also has a civilian staff working across the NT Police, Fire and Emergency Services.

Police in the Northern Territory are part of a tri-service: the Northern Territory Police, Fire and Emergency Services with the Commissioner of Police as the CEO of the tri-service.

The Northern Territory Police traces its roots back to the South Australian Mounted Police from 1870 when Inspector [Paul Foelsche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Foelsche) and six other police officers arrived in the Territory. A small rural constabulary (part-time force) had existed earlier but was disbanded. The [Native Police Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Police_Corps) was formed in 1884. Their role was mostly as a security force to protect the early inhabitants of the Northern Territory than as a police force. The current NTP came into existence in 1911. In 1931, the two Territories Central and Northern became the Northern Territory of Australia and the authority of the Commissioner of Police was established in the Administrator of the Northern Territory, in Darwin.

In December 1869, the governor commissioned Paul Foelsche, a Corporal in the SA Mounted Police stationed at Strathalbyn, to be the first sub-inspector of police at Palmerston. He sailed for Darwin soon afterwards. The police uniform then worn in the Territory was the same as that worn in [South Australia](26716.md). It consisted of a short cut-away blue serge tunic with nine regulation buttons, silver twisted cord shoulder knots, black braid on the sleeves and silver chevrons for non-commissioned officers. The riding breeches were dark blue corkscrew serge with a white stripe.

The earliest firearms used were [Snider–Enfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snider%E2%80%93Enfield) rifles and carbines firing a [.577 calibre cartridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.577_Snider). Later, [Martini–Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martini%E2%80%93Henry) rifles were used, and [Webley revolvers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Webley_Revolver) were issued. Like their predecessors, the Rural Constabulary at [Escape Cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_Cliffs), the first detachment of police at Palmerston had as their first responsibility the maintenance of law and order in the community.

The police were frequently engaged following the discovery of gold near [Pine Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Creek,_Northern_Territory) in 1872. Stations were established at [Adelaide River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide_River,_Northern_Territory), Yam Creek, [Pine Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Creek,_Northern_Territory), [Roper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roper_River) and later at [Daly River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daly_River,_Northern_Territory). The first police fatality occurred in 1872 when Mounted Constable Davis, a noted swimmer, disobeyed a local Standing Order and swam in the sea. He was killed by a crocodile. Darwin's first police station was constructed of poles and plaster measuring 6.1 metres (20 ft) by 3.7 metres (12 ft). The inspector lived nearby in three rooms. A small stone building with two cells was the accommodation for those in custody. These are now incorporated in the Administrator's offices on the Esplanade.

In [Central Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Australia) the police were part of the South Australian Mounted Police. Mounted Constable Shirley was the first mounted trooper in charge at Alice Springs (first called Stuart). At one time there were two Commissioners of Police in the Northern Territory: one for the Territory of [North Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Australia_\(territory\)) and one for the Territory of [Central Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Australia_\(territory\)). In 1931, the two Territories became the Northern Territory of Australia and the authority of the Commissioner of Police was vested in the [Administrator of the Northern Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_the_Northern_Territory), in [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory).

On 1 July 1964, Clive William Graham, a police officer of long standing in the Territory, was appointed as Commissioner and the force as a whole was administered as part of the Public Service of the Northern Territory. In recent years, various cases have made national and international headlines: the end of the Petrov Affair occurred in Darwin; the 1968 month-long bush search for Larry-Boy who murdered his wife and seriously injured a stockman at [Elsey Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elsey_Station); and the 1971 attempted hijack of a plane at Alice Springs airport in which a Territory police officer, who was badly wounded, displayed great heroism. Events connected with search and rescue operations at sea, in swamps and the desert have also made the news.[^cite_note-2] Auxiliaries and Aboriginal Community Police Officers. The Joint Emergency Services Communications Centre in Darwin has instant contact with all stations, vehicles, aircraft and vessels and provides for the Police, Fire, Emergency Services and St John Ambulance Service.

### Female officers

Females were accepted as officers prior or from 1960.[^cite_note-3] In 1962, both male and female candidates had to be unmarried, male applicants aged 21 to 30 years of age, up to 35 years with previous police experience; yet female applicants had to be between 25 and 35 (unless previous police experience).[^cite_note-4] By 1970, only female candidates had to be unmarried.[^cite_note-5] Believed-to-be Australia's first female police motorcyclist, in April 1980, Constable Kate Vanderlaan rode a Honda 750 cc police special around Darwin.[^cite_note-6] She later rose to be a deputy commissioner of the force.[^cite_note-7]

### Recent history

In 1955, there were 80 police officers. As of June 2011, the number of sworn Police, Auxiliaries and Aboriginal Community Police Officers in the service was 1,381.[^cite_note-annualreport-8]

In 1989, the Northern Territory Police, Fire and Emergency Services were joined to become a Tri-Service. The Commissioner of Police also becoming the Chief Executive Officer for the Fire and Rescue Service and the Emergency Services.

In July 2019, Commissioner [Reece Kershaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reece_Kershaw) was appointed Commissioner of the [Australian Federal Police](341221.md), after being at the helm of NT Police for five years.[^cite_note-9]

In 2012, the colour of the police uniform changed from [khaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaki) to [blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue) following a ballot in 2011 in which nearly 60% of officers voted in favour of changing the colour to blue. The roll-out of the new blue uniform, with a new design including the word "Police" displayed on the back of the shirt, started in February 2012 and finished in July 2012.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

In May 2023, commissioner [Jamie Chalker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Chalker) abruptly resigned shortly before he was due to give evidence against the NT Government.[^cite_note-Chalker-12]

In 2024, NT Police enforced a series of [curfews in Alice Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Alice_Springs_curfews).[^cite_note-13]

On 9 March 2025, Michael Murphy's employment as commissioner was terminated following the [ICAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Commissioner_Against_Corruption_\(Northern_Territory\))'s finding that Murphy had been engaged in a scandal that involved Murphy employing his close friend.[^cite_note-14] Following this announcement Martin Dole became the acting commissioner.[^cite_note-15] Dole was sworn in as commissioner in October 2026.[^cite_note-16]

In June 2025, the Northern Territory government announced that transit safety and public housing safety officers would become part of the NTPF known as Police Public Safety Officers (PPSO), together with police auxiliaries, with training to commence in December 2025 and the officers to be operational in early 2026.[^cite_note-17] PPSOs will wear NTPF uniforms and will be armed with firearms.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] In June 2026, the first squad of PPSOs to graduate from the 18-week training program commenced patrols in Darwin.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-ABCNewsJune2026-22] The government said PPSOs will also patrol [Palmerston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmerston,_Northern_Territory), [Alice Springs](183935.md) and [Katherine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine,_Northern_Territory).[^cite_note-ABCNewsJune2026-22]

## Organisation



Casuarina Police Station



The old Palmerston Police Station

The headquarters of the Northern Territory Police is located at NAB House on [Smith Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_Street,_Darwin), [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory). The Department of Police, Fire and Emergency Services is administered from the Peter McAulay Centre in [Berrimah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berrimah,_Northern_Territory). The Northern Territory Police maintains 63 local police stations and 5 police shopfronts coordinated by their respective Local Area Commands.[^cite_note-annualreport-8]

A number of specialist units have been established, including the [Territory Response Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_Response_Group), Accident Investigation Unit, Computer Crime Unit, Drug Intelligence Unit, Substance Abuse Intelligence Desk (SAID), Indigenous Development Unit, Highway Patrol Unit, Missing Persons Unit, Remote Area Traffic Patrol Unit and Air Support Unit.

### Air Wing

The NT Police Air Wing was formed in 1979 with bases in Darwin and [Alice Springs](183935.md), operating two fixed wing aircraft. The area of operation covers 1,346,200 square kilometres (332,700,000 acres), being some 1,610 kilometres (1,000 mi) north to the south and 934 kilometres (580 mi) east to the west. This around one sixth of the Australian landmass, but is very remote, having less than 200,000 residents (1% of the national population). The commonwealth government funded an extra two planes to be based in Darwin. The planes were later handed back due to lack of money.[^cite_note-24]

### CitySafe

The CitySafe and Licensing Patrol Unit was forged during New Year's Eve celebrations in 2008/2009. CitySafe was officially launched by the NT Chief Minister [Paul Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Henderson) on 25 February 2009.[^cite_note-25] After this was deemed a success, NT police were looking at establishing a specialist licensing enforcement unit in 2010.[^cite_note-26]

### Bottle Shop Security

Police Auxiliaries now guard bottle shops in Katherine, Tennant Creek and Alice Springs. They are called liquor inspectors.[^cite_note-27]

## Firearms and equipment

Officers now carry the [Glock 22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glock_22) or the [Glock 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glock_27) [.40-calibre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.40_S\&W) pistol for plain clothes members. Other weapons used in the Northern Territory Police include the [AR-15 semi-automatic rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AR-15) which is used by specialist groups and specifically trained members in rural areas. Officers also carry [Remington model 870 pump action shotgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remington_870) and [Remington model 700 (.308) bolt-action rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remington_700), which is gradually replacing the older BRNO model 601 bolt-action rifles in the same calibre. The NT Police introduced the Model X-26 Advanced [TASER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taser) into operational service for General Duties members in February 2008, distributing 74 units. The X26 Taser has now been replaced with the Taser X-2.[^cite_note-28] as a less-lethal force option available to each frontline patrol.

Restraints used are [ADI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thales_Australia) Saf-Lok Mark-IV and V [handcuffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handcuffs) and Flexi-cuffs. Mk-6 and Mk-9 First Defense oleoresin capsicum (OC) [spray](157302.md) are also general issue.

## Vehicles



Toyota Hilux



Patrol boat

The Northern Territory Police mostly use LAC response vehicles include [Ford Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Falcon_\(Australia\)) sedans, [Holden VE Commodore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden_VE_Commodore)[^cite_note-29] and Toyota Hilux dual cab utes as caged vehicles (4x4 and 2WD) Turbo diesel.[^cite_note-30] Specialist vehicles include the [Toyota Land Cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Land_Cruiser) 4WD.

Highway Patrol vehicles usually consist of a combination of marked and unmarked [Holden VY SS Commodores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holden_VY_Commodore#Commodore_SS) and [Ford Falcon XR6II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Falcon_XR6_Turbo). Other specialist sections and units use a variety of police vehicles including [Isuzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isuzu) trucks, and fixed wing [Pilatus PC-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_PC-12) aeroplanes.

The Northern Territory police recently acquired Kia stingers to add to their Road Policing Command fleet.

## Controversies

On 17 August 1980, an infant [Azaria Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Azaria_Chamberlain) and her family members were camping near [Ayers Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uluru). It was alleged the girl was snatched away by a dingo, but for a number of reasons, the parents were extradited to the Northern Territory and their vehicle seized. The parents Michael and [Lindy Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindy_Chamberlain-Creighton) were criminally charged and convicted; later overturned in 1988. A review of the forensic science section,[^cite_note-60] a royal commission, and several inquests were held into the police investigation and cause of Azaria's death.[^cite_note-61]
