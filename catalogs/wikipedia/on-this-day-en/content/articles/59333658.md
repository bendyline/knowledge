**Gukesh Dommaraju** (born 29 May 2006) is an Indian [chess grandmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_grandmaster) and the reigning [World Chess Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Champion). A [chess prodigy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_prodigy), Gukesh is the youngest undisputed[^cite_note-Undisputed-2] World Champion; the youngest player to have surpassed a [FIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE) rating of 2750, doing so at the age of 17; and the third-youngest to have surpassed 2700 [Elo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elo_rating_system) at age 16. He earned the title of grandmaster at age 12 and is the [fourth-youngest grandmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_youngest_grandmasters) in chess history.&#x20;

Gukesh started playing chess at age 7. He won the under-12 title at the [World Youth Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Youth_Chess_Championship) in 2018, and multiple gold medals at the 2018 [Asian Youth Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Youth_Chess_Championship). He became an [International Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Master) in March 2017, becoming a GM on 15 January 2019, at the age of 12 years, 7 months, and 17 days, making him the second-youngest grandmaster in the history of the game, after [Sergey Karjakin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Karjakin). He was part of the [Indian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_at_the_2022_Asian_Games) that won the silver medal at the [2022 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Asian_Games) in the [men's team competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_at_the_2022_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Men's_team_standard).

Gukesh won the team bronze and the individual gold medal at the [44th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/44th_Chess_Olympiad) in 2022. In the September 2023 rating list, Gukesh became the top-rated Indian player, ending [Viswanathan Anand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viswanathan_Anand)'s 37-year record.[^cite_note-Menon-3][^cite_note-Watson-4] In the [45th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_Chess_Olympiad) in 2024, he won both team and individual gold medals. In 2024, he became the youngest winner of the [Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_Tournament_2024) and successfully challenged [Ding Liren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ding_Liren) in the [World Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_2024), becoming the 18th and youngest undisputed world champion in history, at 18 years and 195 days.[^cite_note-5]

## Early life

### Birth and background

Gukesh Dommaraju was born on 29 May 2006 in [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai) into a [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_people) family.[^cite_note-\:0-6][^cite_note-\:1-7][^cite_note-\:2-8] His mother, Padmakumari, is a [microbiologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbiologist), and his father, Rajinikanth, is an [ENT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otorhinolaryngology) surgeon who moved to Chennai to pursue his medical career.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Gukesh studied at the Velammal Vidyalaya School in [Mel Ayanambakkam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayanambakkam), Chennai.[^cite_note-11]

Gukesh's family hails from the village of Chenchuraju Kandriga, near [Satyavedu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satyavedu) in the [Tirupati district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirupati_district) of [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md).[^cite_note-\:0-6][^cite_note-\:1-7][^cite_note-\:2-8] His father later settled in Chennai to pursue a medical career, where he married Padmakumari.[^cite_note-\:1-7][^cite_note-\:0-6][^cite_note-12]

### Chess beginnings

Gukesh learned to play chess at the age of seven, and eventually began structured one-hour sessions three times a week.[^cite_note-13] He stopped attending school regularly after Class IV (in [elementary school](25058.md)) to focus on his chess career. In 2017, his father quit his job to travel with Gukesh to various tournaments; Gukesh was sponsored by his parents' friends at this time,[^cite_note-14] support about which he has often spoken since.[^cite_note-15] His extraordinary talent was recognized institutionally early on, and he became one of the many beneficiaries of the robust Indian chess ecosystem.[^cite_note-16]

Gukesh's childhood coach, GM [Vishnu Prasanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu_Prasanna_V.), restrained him from using [chess engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_engine) for analysis and training during the formative years of his career, in what has been described as a "radical experiment", with goal of developing independent analytical skills and intuition; he started using engines after crossing the 2500 rating threshold.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Career

### 2015–2021

Gukesh won the under-9 section of the Asian School Chess Championships in 2015.[^cite_note-19] He won the [World Youth Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Youth_Chess_Championship) in 2018 in the under-12 category.[^cite_note-20] In the 2018 [Asian Youth Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Youth_Chess_Championship), he won a record five gold medals in the under-12 events in individual rapid, blitz and classical formats, and the team rapid and blitz competitions.[^cite_note-21] He completed the requirements for the title of [International Master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Master) in March 2017 at the 34th [Cappelle-la-Grande Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappelle-la-Grande_Open).[^cite_note-22]

On 15 January 2019, Gukesh became the then [second-youngest grandmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_youngest_grandmasters) in the history of the game at the age of 12 years, 7 months, and 17 days, behind [Sergey Karjakin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Karjakin).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-26] In June 2021, he won the Julius Baer Challengers Chess Tour, Gelfand Challenge, scoring 14 out of 19 points.[^cite_note-27]

### 2022–2023

In August 2022, Gukesh won the individual gold medal on the first board in the [open event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_event_at_the_44th_Chess_Olympiad) at the [44th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/44th_Chess_Olympiad) in Chennai with a score of 9 out of 11. He was part of the India-2 team which won the bronze medal in the same tournament.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] In September 2022, he was part of the [Indian team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_at_the_2022_Asian_Games) that won the silver medal at the [2022 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Asian_Games) in the [men's team competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_at_the_2022_Asian_Games_%E2%80%93_Men's_team_standard).[^cite_note-30] In the same month, Gukesh reached a [FIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE) [rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elo_rating) of over 2700 for the first time with a rating of 2726, and became the third-youngest to do so after [Wei Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei_Yi) and [Alireza Firouzja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alireza_Firouzja).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] During the Aimchess Rapid tournament in October 2022, Gukesh became the youngest to beat [Magnus Carlsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Carlsen), the reigning [World Chess Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Champion) at that time.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

In August 2023, Gukesh became the youngest player ever to reach a rating of 2750, breaking Carlsen's record.[^cite_note-36] In the [Chess World Cup 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_World_Cup_2023) at [Baku](4566.md), he advanced to the quarterfinals, where he lost to Carlsen.[^cite_note-37] In the September 2023 rating list, Gukesh surpassed [Viswanathan Anand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viswanathan_Anand) as the top-ranked Indian player, marking the first time in 37 years that Anand was not the top-ranked Indian player.[^cite_note-Menon-3][^cite_note-Watson-4]

In December 2023, Gukesh won the inaugural [Chennai Grand Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai_Grand_Masters), scoring 4½/7 points and finishing level with [Arjun Erigaisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arjun_Erigaisi) before winning on tiebreak.[^cite_note-38] The win contributed to his qualification for the [Candidates Tournament 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_Tournament_2024).[^cite_note-39]

By the end of 2023, Gukesh qualified for the [2024 Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_Tournament_2024), to be conducted to identify the challenger to [Ding Liren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ding_Liren) for the [World Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship).[^cite_note-40] He finished 2nd in the [FIDE Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIDE_Circuit) behind [Fabiano Caruana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabiano_Caruana), and took the qualifying spot reserved for the winner, as Caruana had already qualified through the Chess World Cup.[^cite_note-41] He was the third-youngest player to qualify for a Candidates tournament, behind [Bobby Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Fischer) and Carlsen.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

In August 2023, Gukesh became the youngest player ever to reach a rating of 2750, breaking Carlsen's record.[^cite_note-44] In the [Chess World Cup 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_World_Cup_2023) at [Baku](4566.md), he advanced to the quarterfinals, where he lost to Carlsen.[^cite_note-45] In the September 2023 rating list, Gukesh surpassed [Viswanathan Anand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viswanathan_Anand) as the top-ranked Indian player, marking the first time in 37 years that Anand was not the top-ranked Indian player.[^cite_note-Menon-3][^cite_note-Watson-4]

In December 2023, Gukesh qualified for the [2024 Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_Tournament_2024), to be conducted to identify the challenger to [Ding Liren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ding_Liren) for the [World Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship).[^cite_note-46] He finished 2nd in the [FIDE Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIDE_Circuit) behind [Fabiano Caruana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabiano_Caruana), and took the qualifying spot reserved for the winner, as Caruana had already qualified through the Chess World Cup.[^cite_note-47] He was the third-youngest player to qualify for a Candidates tournament, behind [Bobby Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Fischer) and Carlsen.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

### 2024



Gukesh (left) playing [Alireza Firouzja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alireza_Firouzja) at the [2024 Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_Tournament_2024)

In January, Gukesh finished in a four-way tie for first place in the [Tata Steel Chess Tournament 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Steel_Chess_Tournament_2024) with a score of 8½/13 rounds. He defeated [Anish Giri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anish_Giri) in the semifinals before losing to [Wei Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei_Yi) in the finals of the tiebreaker, and finished second in the final standings.[^cite_note-50]

In April, Gukesh was part of the eight-player [Candidates Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candidates_Tournament_2024) held in [Toronto](64646.md). He won five games against [R Praggnanandhaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_Praggnanandhaa) and [Vidit Gujrathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidit_Gujrathi) playing as Black, Firouzja playing as White, and [Nijat Abasov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nijat_Abasov) playing as both Black and White. With a single loss coming against Firouzja, he finished with nine points from 14 rounds to win the tournament.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] He was the youngest-ever winner of the Candidates tournament.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

In September, Gukesh was part of the Indian team that won gold medal in the [open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_event_at_the_45th_Chess_Olympiad) section of the [45th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/45th_Chess_Olympiad). He successfully defended his gold medal on Board 1, this time going undefeated with the score 9/10 (+8=2-0) and a performance rating of 3056, His performance helped India win its first ever Gold medal at the [Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Olympiad).[^cite_note-56] As a result of the win, Gukesh entered the top 5 in the [FIDE rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_rankings) for the first time on 1 October 2024.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

The [2024 World Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_World_Chess_Championship) was held in November–December 2024 between Gukesh and [Ding Liren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ding_Liren). Gukesh scored three wins against two wins for Ding, and nine draws in the 14 classical rounds of the match. He won the 14th and final game on 12 December 2024, and as a result, the World Chess Championship by a scoreline of 7½–6½.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] The win made him the youngest undisputed[^cite_note-Undisputed-2] World Chess Champion, breaking the record previously held by [Garry Kasparov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Kasparov).[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] FIDE commented on Gukesh's gameplay as having "near-perfect accuracy", and Ding reacted that it was his best tournament of the year, and that he had no regrets in losing the title to Gukesh.[^cite_note-63] Gukesh’s team included grandmasters [Grzegorz Gajewski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grzegorz_Gajewski), [Radosław Wojtaszek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rados%C5%82aw_Wojtaszek), [Jan-Krzysztof Duda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Krzysztof_Duda), [Jan Klimkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Klimkowski), [Pentala Harikrishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentala_Harikrishna), and [Vincent Keymer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincent_Keymer), along with his mental coach [Paddy Upton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_Upton).[^cite_note-64]

### 2025

On 17 January, Gukesh received the prestigious Major Dhyan Chand [Khel Ratna Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khel_Ratna_Award) from President [Droupadi Murmu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Droupadi_Murmu) at the [Rashtrapati Bhavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrapati_Bhavan) for his World Championship accomplishment. He was 18 years, 7 months, and 20 days old when he received the award. This made him the youngest ever recipient of the award, breaking the record previously held by [Abhinav Bindra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhinav_Bindra).&#x20;

In January, Gukesh tied for first with [R Praggnanandhaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_Praggnanandhaa) in the [Tata Steel Chess Tournament 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Steel_Chess_Tournament_2025) with a score of 8½ in 13 rounds. He lost 2–1 in the blitz tiebreaker.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

Gukesh participated in the [Freestyle Chess Grand Slam Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freestyle_Chess_Grand_Slam_Tour), a series of [Chess960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess960) tournaments. In February, he finished in eighth place in the [first leg in Weissenhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weissenhaus_Freestyle_Chess_Grand_Slam).[^cite_note-68] In April, he finished eleventh in the [second leg in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Freestyle_Chess_Grand_Slam).[^cite_note-69]

In May, Gukesh participated in the second leg of the [Grand Chess Tour 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Chess_Tour_2025) in Romania, where he finished in a four way tie for sixth.[^cite_note-70]

In May-June, Gukesh participated in [Norway Chess 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_Chess_2025). Gukesh lost his round 1 game to [Magnus Carlsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Carlsen), and his round 2 game to [Arjun Erigaisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arjun_Erigaisi). In round 3, he scored a crucial win against [Hikaru Nakamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hikaru_Nakamura), his first, which was Nakamura's only loss in the tournament. In round 6, Gukesh played Carlsen with the white pieces, He was in a worse position but played a series of accurate moves to equalise, Carlsen blundered in an equal position to lose in a dramatic fashion. This was Gukesh's first win against Carlsen and Carlsen's only loss in the tournament. In round 7 he defeated Erigaisi, which was Gukesh's first win against the longtime rival. Going into the last round, he was still in first place contention but lost his game against [Fabiano Caruana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabiano_Caruana) by blundering a knight fork in an equal position. Gukesh finished the tournament in third place behind Carlsen and Caruana.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-\:6-72][^cite_note-\:7-73][^cite_note-74]

In July, Gukesh participated in the [Super United Rapid and Blitz Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Chess_Tour_2025), In the Rapid section, after losing his 1st Round game to [Duda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan-Krzysztof_Duda), he won his next five games against [Alireza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alireza_Firouzja), [Pragg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_Praggnanandhaa), [Abdusattorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nodirbek_Abdusattorov), [Caruana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabiano_Caruana) and [Carlsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Carlsen), drew the next 2 games, and won in the last round against [Wesley So](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesley_So) to finish on 14/18 points, 3 points ahead of second place, Duda, In the Blitz section he only scored 5.5/18 points. His total score of 19.5/38 got him 3rd place, behind Carlsen and So.[^cite_note-75]

In the [Grand Swiss tournament 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE_Grand_Swiss_Tournament_2025), he finished in 41st place, winning four games against [Etienne Bacrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etienne_Bacrot), [Daniil Yuffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Yuffa), [Gabriel Sargissian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Sargissian) and [Andrei Volokitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Volokitin), losing to [Abhimanyu Mishra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abhimanyu_Mishra), [Nikolas Theodorou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolas_Theodorou), and [Ediz Gurel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ediz_Gurel), and drawing with [Arjun Erigaisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arjun_Erigaisi), [Yagiz Kaan Erdogmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yagiz_Kaan_Erdogmus), [Robert Hovhannisyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Hovhannisyan) and [Divya Deshmukh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divya_Deshmukh).[^cite_note-76]

In October, he helped the Superchess team win gold at 41st [European Chess Club Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Chess_Club_Cup), winning individual board 1 gold himself.[^cite_note-77], This marked his first win since becoming world champion.&#x20;

Later the same month, he finished last in the inaugural Clutch Chess: Champions Showdown among a field of 4 players.[^cite_note-78]

As the reigning World Chess Champion, Gukesh played in the [Chess World Cup](78998807.md) in November 2025 as the first seed. There, he was eliminated by [Frederik Svane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik_Svane) in the third round.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

In late December, Gukesh participated in both the [2025 FIDE World Rapid Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Rapid_Chess_Championship_2025#) and [2025 FIDE World Blitz Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Blitz_Chess_Championship_2025). He finished in 20th place during the FIDE World Rapid Championship with a score of 8.5/13.[^cite_note-81] During the Open Swiss portion of the FIDE World Blitz Championship, Gukesh finished in 53rd with a score of 11/19,[^cite_note-82] and did not qualify for the finals.

### 2026

Beginning in January and ending in February, Gukesh participated in the [Tata Steel Chess Tournament 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Steel_Chess_Tournament_2026). He finished in a three-way tie for 8th place with a score of 6.5/13, but officially came in 10th after [Sonneborn-Berger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonneborn%E2%80%93Berger_score) tiebreaks.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

In March, Gukesh participated in the Masters portion of the [2026 Prague International Chess Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Chess_Festival). Gukesh finished in 9th place out of 10 with a score of 3.5/9(+1-3=5), avoiding last place on tiebreaks.[^cite_note-85]

Gukesh withdrew from [Grand Chess Tour 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Chess_Tour_2026), citing recent poor performances and his [upcoming World Championship](75936044.md) match, opting to only play in GCT's Rapid & Blitz Events. Gukesh finished 6th place at [Super Rapid & Blitz Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Chess_Tour_2026#Super_Rapid_&_Blitz_Poland); he defeated his World Championship Challenger [Javokhir Sindarov](58816590.md) in Rapid but lost both their Blitz games, [^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] In July he finished 6th at Super Rapid & Blitz Croatia.[^cite_note-88]

In May-June, Gukesh finished last of 6 players at [Norway Chess 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_Chess_2026#Open_Section), losing 5 classical games, including twice to World no.1 [Magnus Carlsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_Carlsen), and winning only one, against eventual winner [R Praggnanandhaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_Praggnanandhaa).[^cite_note-89] In July, Gukesh finished last of 8 players at [Chennai Grand Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai_Grand_Masters#2026), (+0-3=4), losing 14 [rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_rating_system) points to reach 2703.[^cite_note-90]

In September, Gukesh participated in [46th Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/46th_Chess_Olympiad). In contrast with the previous two [Olympiads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Olympiad) where he had played and won the Individual Gold medals on Board 1, Gukesh played on Board 4. He recovered part of his form with respectable 8½/11 (+7=3-1) points, He was the highest scoring Indian Player and won Individual Silver on Board 4, His critical win against [Alexander Donchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Donchenko) in round 9 helped India overtake evantual Bronze medalists [Germany](11867.md). His only loss came in the final round against Hungarian [Gleb Dudin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gleb_Dudin). India was unable to defend their Olympiad title and finished on 2nd Place, following Uzbekistan, who defeated them in a close Round 6 encounter.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

## Playing style

Gukesh plays a reactive game and is notable for his ability to calculate under time pressure, which often leads to complex tactical battles in his games.[^cite_note-95] His mentor Anand describes him as having "incredible calculating abilities".[^cite_note-96] [Raymond Keene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Keene) writes that Gukesh's forte is his ability to keep the game alive, avoiding inferior positions while simultaneously avoiding liquidation to draws.[^cite_note-97] His style has also borne comparison with former world champion [Anatoly Karpov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Karpov)'s subtle, incremental, anaconda-like gains.[^cite_note-98] Carlsen regards Gukesh's style of play as "pure counter" and opined that Gukesh makes very few mistakes, which makes him "an extremely dangerous opponent under any circumstances".[^cite_note-99] However, Gukesh is often dangerously optimistic about his position, causing him to take chances that he shouldn't.[^cite_note-100]
