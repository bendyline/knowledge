The **Women's Asia Cup** is a biennial [cricket](25675557.md) tournament organised by the [Asian Cricket Council](584073.md). It is contested by the senior national women's cricket teams of Asia to determine the continental champion.[^cite_note-1]

The first Women's Asia Cup was held in 2004 in [Colombo](56636.md) and [Kandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandy), Sri Lanka. The 2020 edition was postponed to 2021 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) but was later cancelled. The [ICC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council) has ruled that all matches played in the Asia Cup carry either ODI or T20I status. The 2012 edition was the first to be played in the T20 format.

As of 2026, ten Women's Asia Cup tournaments have been held, with [India](2281524.md) winning the most titles (four WODI and four WT20I).[^cite_note-2]

### One-Day Internationals

#### [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Women's_Asia_Cup)

The first Women's Asia Cup was played in [Sri Lanka](26750.md) in April 2004. Only two teams took part, [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_national_women's_cricket_team) and [Sri Lanka ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_national_women's_cricket_team) and they played a ten match [One-Day International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_One-day_International_cricket) series against each other. India won all five matches in the first Women's Asia Cup.[^cite_note-3]

#### [2005–06](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_Women's_Asia_Cup)

[Karachi](17123.md), [Pakistan](23235.md) hosted the second Women's Asia Cup in December 2005 and January 2006.[^cite_note-4] [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_national_women's_cricket_team) made their first appearance in the tournament.[^cite_note-5] [India ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_national_women's_cricket_team) again won the tournament, beating [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_national_women's_cricket_team) by 97 runs in the final.[^cite_note-6]

#### [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Women's_Asia_Cup)

The third Women's Asia Cup tournament was played in [Jaipur](166537.md), [India](14533.md) in December 2006.[^cite_note-7] The tournament went very much the way of the previous event. [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_national_women's_cricket_team) beat [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_national_women's_cricket_team) in the final, this time by eight wickets.[^cite_note-8]

#### [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Women's_Asia_Cup)

The fourth Women's Asia Cup tournament was played in [Sri Lanka](26750.md) in May 2008. [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_national_women's_cricket_team) again won the tournament, defeating [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_national_women's_cricket_team) by 177 runs in the final.[^cite_note-9]

### Twenty20 cricket

#### [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Women's_Twenty20_Asia_Cup)

The fifth Women's Asia Cup Tournament was played in [Guanggong Cricket Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanggong_Cricket_Stadium), [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou), [China](5405.md) from 24 to 31 October 2012. India defeated Pakistan by 19 runs in the final [^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

#### [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Women's_Twenty20_Asia_Cup)

The sixth Women's Asia Cup tournament was played in [Thailand](30128.md), from 27 November to 4 December 2016. India beat Pakistan by 17 runs in the final, becoming champion for the 6th time consecutively.

#### [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Women's_Twenty20_Asia_Cup)

The seventh Women's Asia Cup tournament was played in [Malaysia](3607937.md), from 3 June to 10 June 2018.[^cite_note-12] [Bangladesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_women's_national_cricket_team) beat six-time winner India by 3 wickets in the final to clinch their first Asia Cup title.[^cite_note-13]

#### [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Women's_Twenty20_Asia_Cup)

A tournament was due to take place in 2020 in Bangladesh,[^cite_note-14] but was postponed to 2021 (and eventually 2022) due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-WAC-15] The 2022 edition of the tournament took place at [Sylhet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylhet_International_Cricket_Stadium), [Bangladesh](3454.md) in October 2022.[^cite_note-16] India beat Sri Lanka in the final, this time by eight wickets by chasing a modest total of 65 and became 7th time winner. [Jemimah Rodrigues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jemimah_Rodrigues) was the highest run scorer of this tournament while [Inoka Ranaweera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inoka_Ranaweera) and [Deepti Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deepti_Sharma) were the joint-highest wicket takers of this tournament.

#### [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Women's_Twenty20_Asia_Cup)

The ninth edition was hosted by Sri Lanka.[^cite_note-17] A total of 15 games were played in the edition among the teams including semi finals and final. All the matches were held at [Rangiri Dambulla International Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangiri_Dambulla_International_Stadium) in [Dambulla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dambulla). Sri Lanka women defeated India women in the final to win their maiden Women's Asia Cup title. Sri Lanka's [Chamari Athapaththu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamari_Athapaththu) was the leading run scorer of the season with 243 runs from four matches[^cite_note-18]
