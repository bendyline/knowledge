The **Czech Republic national football team** ([Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language): *Česká fotbalová reprezentace*), recognised by [FIFA](11049.md) as **Czechia**,[^cite_note-2] represents the [Czech Republic](5321.md) in men's international [football](10568.md). The team is controlled by the [Football Association of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Association_of_the_Czech_Republic) (FAČR). Historically, the team participated in [FIFA](11049.md) and [UEFA](32332.md) competitions as [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia) and [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Following the [dissolution of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_Czechoslovakia), the first international competition of the Czech Republic was [UEFA Euro 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996), where they finished runners-up. They have taken part at every European Championship since. Following the separation, they have featured at two FIFA World Cups, in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2026](36581929.md).

## History

See also: [Czechoslovakia national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team)

### 1901–1911 (Bohemia)

While part of [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), the national team of [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia) (established on 19 October 1901) played its first international on 5 April 1903, a 2–1 loss for [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) in [Budapest](36787.md). On 7 October, Hungary came to [Prague](23844.md) for a 4–4 draw. The two countries played three more matches up to 1908, including Bohemia's only victory on 6 October 1907. Bohemia played its last match on 13 June 1908, losing 4–0 at home to [England](9904.md).[^cite_note-eufoot-8]

After being expelled from [FIFA](11049.md) due to objections from the [Austrian Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Football_Association), the [ČSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Association_of_the_Czech_Republic) founded the [UIAFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UIAFA) along with French [USFSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_des_Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9s_Fran%C3%A7aises_de_Sports_Athl%C3%A9tiques) and English [Amateur Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_Football_Association) in March 1909. Bohemia won the UIAFA [Great European football tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_European_football_tournament) in 1911, defeating the AFA [England team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_AFA_national_football_team) 2–1 in the final.[^cite_note-9]

### 1939

With the dissolution of Czechoslovakia and the creation of the [Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia) in March 1939 by Nazi Germany, the old Czechoslovak national team temporarily ceased to exist. Unlike the [Slovak Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_Republic_\(1939-1945\)) (which proclaimed independence and operated an active, regular national team for several years), the Protectorate could not develop autonomous and prolonged international activity.\
&#x20;However, during the course of 1939, the selection of Bohemia and Moravia (Reprezentace Čech a Moravy) was temporarily reactivated and took to the field to play three matches:\


- August 27, 1939 (Prague): Bohemia and Moravia - Yugoslavia 7-3 (unofficial friendly)

- October 22, 1939 (Prague): Bohemia and Moravia - Austria (Ostmark) 5-5 (unofficial friendly)

- November 12, 1939 (Breslau): Germany - Bohemia and Moravia 4-4 (official friendly)

These matches saw the participation of great stars of the era who had driven Czechoslovakia to become runners-up at the 1934 World Cup, such as [Josef Bican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Bican), [Oldřich Nejedlý](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old%C5%99ich_Nejedl%C3%BD), and [Antonín Puč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton%C3%ADn_Pu%C4%8D).\
&#x20;After November 1939, however, the pressure and control of the Nazi regime abruptly brought the experiment to an end: the national team was disbanded, and Czech players continued to play only at the club level in local championships (the Národní liga), no longer able to wear an international representative jersey until the end of World War II and the re-establishment of Czechoslovakia.

### 1990s

When Czechoslovakia split into the Czech Republic and [Slovakia](26830.md), the Czech Republic team was formed. They played their first friendly match away to [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) on 23 February 1994.[^cite_note-10] The newly formed team played their first home game in [Ostrava](464078.md), against [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania_national_football_team), in which they registered their first home win.[^cite_note-11]

Their first competitive match was part of the [UEFA Euro 1996 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996_qualifying) campaign, in which they defeated [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_national_football_team) 6–1 in Ostrava. During the campaign, the Czech Republic registered six wins, three draws, and a defeat against [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_national_football_team), finishing their qualifying Group 5 in first place, ahead of group favourites the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team). In the final tournament, hosted by England, the Czechs progressed from the group stage, despite a 2–0 opening game defeat to Germany. They progressed to the [UEFA Euro 1996 Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996_Final), losing 2–1 to Germany at [Wembley Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)).

The Czechs finished third in the 1998 FIFA World Cup qualifying group, behind [Spain](609825.md) and [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro_national_football_team), and subsequently missed the tournament.[^cite_note-12]

### 2000s

The Czech Republic qualified for [Euro 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), winning all of their group games and conceding five goals.[^cite_note-13] In the finals the team were drawn in Group D, alongside [France](250197.md), the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) and [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team).[^cite_note-14] The team lost to the Netherlands after a last-minute penalty[^cite_note-bbceuro2000-15] and were defeated in second match by France, which eliminated them from advancing to the knockout round. The Czech Republic managed a 2–0 win against Denmark in their final game courtesy of two goals from [Vladimír Šmicer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladim%C3%ADr_%C5%A0micer).[^cite_note-bbceuro2000-15]

Once again, the Czech Republic failed to qualify for the World Cup, this time finishing second in their 2002 qualification group, behind Denmark, and then being beaten 1–0 in both legs by [Belgium](679716.md) in the UEFA play-offs for a place in the finals.[^cite_note-16]

A team settled with [Pavel Nedvěd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Nedv%C4%9Bd), [Jan Koller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Koller), [Tomáš Rosický](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Rosick%C3%BD), [Milan Baroš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Baro%C5%A1), [Marek Jankulovski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marek_Jankulovski), [Tomáš Galásek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Gal%C3%A1sek) together with the emergence of goalkeeper [Petr Čech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_%C4%8Cech) were unbeaten in 2002 and 2003, scoring 53 goals in 19 games and qualifying for [Euro 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004) in the process. The Czech Republic went on a 20-game unbeaten streak, which finally ended in [Dublin](8504.md) on 31 March 2004 in a friendly match against the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team).[^cite_note-17] The Czechs entered the Euro finals in Group D, alongside the Netherlands, Germany and [Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-18] The team trailed 2–0 to the Netherlands before winning the game 3–2 and beat Germany in the final group match.[^cite_note-19] The Czech Republic beat Denmark in the quarter-final, went into the semi-final against Greece and Tomáš Rosický hit the bar after just two minutes, Jan Koller had shots saved by the Greek goalkeeper and Pavel Nedvěd left the pitch injured in the end of the first half. The first 90 minutes finished goalless and Greece won the game in the last minute of the first half of extra-time with a [silver goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_goal).[^cite_note-20]



Czech Republic (red) vs [Ghana](1029620.md) (white) at the [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup)

The Czech Republic achieved their record win during the [2006 World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)), thrashing [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra_national_football_team) 8–1 in a qualification match in [Liberec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberec). In the same match, Jan Koller became the all-time top scorer for the national team with his 35th international goal.[^cite_note-21] At the end of the campaign, after finishing in second place in Group 1 and defeating [Norway](203277.md) in a playoff, the Czechs qualified for their first [World Cup](11370.md).[^cite_note-22] The team was boosted prior to the play-off matches by the return of Pavel Nedvěd,[^cite_note-23] who had initially retired from international football after Euro 2004. The squad for the [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) in Germany included 18 players from the Euro 2004 team. With the team ranked second in the world,[^cite_note-24] they started the tournament with a 3–0 win over the [United States](582488.md). During the game, however, Jan Koller was forced to leave with a hamstring injury,[^cite_note-italy2006-25] exiting the tournament. In the next game, with Milan Baroš still recovering from injury, the team suffered a 2–0 loss to [Ghana](1029620.md).[^cite_note-ghana2006-26] Baroš returned for the final game against [Italy](362466.md), a must-win for a round of 16 appearance. The team were reduced to ten men as [Jan Polák](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Pol%C3%A1k) was dismissed before half-time for two bookable offences.[^cite_note-italy2006-25] Italy went on to win 2–0. Pavel Nedvěd, [Karel Poborský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Poborsk%C3%BD) and [Vratislav Lokvenc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vratislav_Lokvenc) retired from the national team after the tournament.[^cite_note-27]

In the [qualifying campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_qualifying) for [Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008), they finished top of their group, above Germany on head-to-head record. The Czech Republic beat co-hosts [Switzerland](681862.md) 1–0 in their opening game of the finals, before being beaten 3–1 by [Portugal](576328.md). This meant the Czechs and Turkey carried identical records going into the final group game. Although the Czechs took a 2–0 lead just past the hour mark and looked set to qualify, Turkey scored three goals in the final 15 minutes of the game to win the game 3–2.[^cite_note-28]

The Czechs entered [2010 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification), being drawn in [Group 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_-_UEFA_Group_3), under the guidance of coach [Petr Rada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Rada). They started with a 0–0 away draw against [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team), before losing to [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team). A late goal from [Libor Sionko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libor_Sionko) won the next game 1–0 against [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_national_football_team). This was followed by a win against [San Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marino_national_football_team), and a goalless draw in Slovenia. In their following match, against neighbours [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_national_football_team), a 2–1 defeat at home left Czech Republic in a precarious qualifying position. Manager Petr Rada was dismissed and six players were suspended.[^cite_note-29] [Ivan Hašek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Ha%C5%A1ek) took temporary charge as manager,[^cite_note-30] gaining four points from his first two matches, as the team drew away to group leaders Slovakia and thrashed San Marino 7–0 in [Uherské Hradiště](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhersk%C3%A9_Hradi%C5%A1t%C4%9B). They subsequently beat Poland in Prague but followed this result with a goalless draw against Northern Ireland, finishing third in the group and failing to qualify for the World Cup. Hašek announced his immediate resignation.[^cite_note-31]

### 2010s

A changed team under [Michal Bílek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michal_B%C3%ADlek) entered the [Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012) [qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012_qualifying) and began with a home loss to [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuania_national_football_team). But a win at home to [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team) was followed by wins against [Liechtenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liechtenstein_national_football_team). [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_men's_national_football_team) defeated Czech Republic in between the Liechtenstein games, but the play-off spot was still in their hands. In the next game, a last minute penalty from [Michal Kadlec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michal_Kadlec) away to Scotland secured a 2–2 draw.[^cite_note-32] Despite Scotland winning their next two games and the Czechs again being defeated by Spain, the team could finish second if they could beat Lithuania away from home in the final game, assuming Spain would beat Scotland at home. Spain won 3–1 and Czech Republic defeated Lithuania 4–1 to seal second spot and a place in the play-offs. Czech Republic were drawn to face [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegro_national_football_team) in the two-legged play-off. A goal from [Václav Pilař](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Pila%C5%99) and a last minute second from [Tomáš Sivok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Sivok) helped the Czechs to a 2–0 first leg lead. In the second leg in [Podgorica](172136.md), a late goal from [Petr Jiráček](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Jir%C3%A1%C4%8Dek) sealed a 1–0 win and the Czechs ran out 3–0 aggregate winners and qualified for Euro 2012.[^cite_note-33]

At the tournament, the Czechs lost their opening game 4–1 to [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team), with their only goal coming from Václav Pilař. In their second match, against Greece, the Czech Republic went 2–0 up within the first six minutes thanks to goals from Petr Jiráček and a second from Pilař. Following the half-time substitution of captain Tomáš Rosický, Greece scored a second-half goal following a mistake from Czech goalkeeper Petr Čech, although there were no more goals and the Czech Republic recorded their first win of the tournament.[^cite_note-34] Going into their third and final group match, the Czech Republic needed at least a draw against co-hosts Poland to advance to the knockout stage. A second-half strike by Jiráček proved the difference between the teams as the Czechs ran out as 1–0 winners. Due to Greece beating Russia in the other group game, the Czech Republic subsequently finished top of Group A,[^cite_note-35] becoming the first team ever to win a group at the European Championships with a negative [goal difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_difference).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

Bílek stayed on as coach, despite unrest amongst fans, and was tasked with [qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)) for the [2014 World Cup](656933.md).[^cite_note-bk1-38] The Czechs were drawn into [UEFA qualifying Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_B) along with Italy, Denmark, [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team), [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia_national_football_team) and [Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malta_national_football_team). The campaign started with goalless draws against Denmark and Bulgaria, paired with a narrow win against Malta. The team then lost 0–3 to Denmark at home. The Czechs were then able to beat Armenia and draw with group leaders Italy, but lost to both Armenia and Italy in the rematches.[^cite_note-bk1-38] Bílek resigned[^cite_note-bk1-38] after the loss and was replaced with assistant coach [Josef Pešice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Pe%C5%A1ice).[^cite_note-39]

[Pavel Vrba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Vrba) was appointed as the team's new coach in 2014, ahead of [Euro 2016 qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_qualifying).[^cite_note-40] The Czech team was drawn into[^cite_note-41] [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_qualifying_Group_A), along with Netherlands, Turkey, [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_national_football_team), Latvia and [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan_national_football_team). The Czech team began with a win, defeating Netherlands, and followed up with victories over Turkey, Kazakhstan and Iceland, leaving them as group leaders with maximum points after four matches. A draw at home against Latvia followed; nonetheless, Czech Republic remained group leaders, and on 6 September 2015, qualified for their sixth European Championship. They only got one point from a draw with [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team), losing to Spain and Turkey. During a friendly match against [Australia](4792249.md) on 1 June 2018, the Czechs recorded their biggest defeat losing 0–4 in [Sankt Pölten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankt_P%C3%B6lten), Austria.[^cite_note-42] That would eventually be eclipsed during their first qualifier for [Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_qualifying), as they were beaten 0–5 at [Wembley Stadium](8913012.md) by [England](9904.md).[^cite_note-43]

On the other hand, they qualified directly for [Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), finishing 2nd in [Group A during the qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_qualifying_Group_A), with a record of five wins and three defeats in eight games. The team won at home against [England](9904.md), the group leader (2–1) and lost all three away matches, against England (0–5), [Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_national_football_team) (1–2) and [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team) (0–1).

### 2020s

On 18 November 2020, the Czech Republic, then placed in [Group 2 of League B in the 2020–2021 edition of the Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Nations_League_B#Group_2), obtained promotion to League A for the next edition thanks to a win at home against [Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovakia_national_football_team) on the last day of the group stages (2–0), allowing the national team to overtake [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team), which was defeated by [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_national_football_team) (0–1) at the same time.[^cite_note-44] The Czechs were drawn into [Group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_Group_D) with qualification opponents England, along with [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team) and Scotland.

2021 started on a positive note for the Czech Republic, who managed to draw with [Belgium](679716.md) at home 1–1 in the [qualifiers for the 2022 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_E). Above all, [the Euros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), postponed to the summer of 2021 due to [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Europe), saw the Czech men advance from the group. The Czech Republic won the first game against Scotland, who were one of 12 host countries for the Euros, with a double from [Patrik Schick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrik_Schick) (2–0), including a long-range goal. The Czechs then drew 1–1 against Croatia before losing to England at [Wembley](8913012.md) 1–0. The Czechs finished among the four best third-place teams. In the round of 16 the Czech Republic faced the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), surprising them with a 2–0 win with goals from [Tomáš Holeš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Hole%C5%A1) and Patrik Schick while the Dutch team finished the game with 10 players.[^cite_note-45] Appearing in the quarter-finals for the first time since [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012), they were beaten by Denmark 2–1, having conceded the first Danish goal after 5 minutes of play due to a refereeing error caused by a non-existent corner.[^cite_note-46]

The Czech Republic failed to qualify for the [World Cup in Qatar](17742072.md), finishing [third in their group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_E), behind [Belgium](679716.md) and [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team), whom they failed to beat (home draw and away defeat in both cases), but they made it to the [play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round) thanks to their position in the [2020–21 UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Nations_League). They were eliminated in the [semi-finals of the B path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round#Semi-finals_2), following an away defeat against [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) in extra time (1–0).[^cite_note-47] They were then relegated to League B after finishing bottom of their group in the [2022–2023 Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Nations_League_A#Group_2).

The Czech Republic, without injured star striker Schick for the entire [Euro 2024 qualifying campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying_Group_E), had a difficult and chaotic campaign, but qualified for [the Euros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) in Germany by finishing second in their qualifying group thanks to a 3–0 home win over [Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldova_national_football_team) on the final day. After qualifying, coach [Jaroslav Šilhavý](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaroslav_%C5%A0ilhav%C3%BD) resigned in the face of criticism from fans and controversy surrounding some of the team's players.[^cite_note-48]

On 4 January 2024, [Ivan Hašek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Ha%C5%A1ek) returned to the Czech football team as the head coach until November 2025, with an option to continue if the team qualifies for the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md). He oversaw 2–1 victories against [Norway](203277.md) and [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia_national_football_team). At [Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024), the Czech Republic failed to qualify for the round of 16, losing 2–1 to [Portugal](576328.md), which was followed by a 1–1 draw to [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_national_football_team) with many opportunities missed, and a 2–1 loss to [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) in the final match of the group after being quickly reduced to 10 players following a second yellow card for [Antonín Barák](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton%C3%ADn_Bar%C3%A1k), the third time that Czech Republic lost a decisive game against Turkey after [Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008) and [Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016). They finished fourth in Group F.[^cite_note-49]

On 23 March 2026, [Ladislav Krejčí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladislav_Krej%C4%8D%C3%AD_\(footballer,_born_1999\)) was confirmed as the new captain of the Czech Republic, after [Tomáš Souček](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Sou%C4%8Dek)'s earlier dismissal.[^cite_note-50] Later that month, the Czech Republic won both ties in the [2026 World Cup qualification second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round) against the Republic of Ireland and Denmark on penalty shootouts, qualifying for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) after their last appearance in 2006.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] At the 2026 World Cup, losses against South Korea and Mexico, along with a draw against South Africa, led to the Czech Republic's early elimination at bottom of Group A with one point.[^cite_note-53]

## Stadiums

16 different cities have hosted Czech Republic football matches between 1994 and 2025.[^cite_note-56] The most commonly used stadium was [Stadion Letná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_Letn%C3%A1), the home stadium of [Sparta Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Sparta_Prague), which was renovated in 1994. Another frequently used stadium was [Na Stínadlech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Na_St%C3%ADnadlech) in [Teplice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teplice). However, since the [2018 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)), major matches are usually played at the newer [Stadion Eden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortuna_Arena), the home stadium of [Slavia Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Slavia_Prague), which was built in 2008. Since 2012, competitive games have also been held [Doosan Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doosan_Arena), [Plzeň](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plze%C5%88). Some matches are also played in [Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravia) at [Andrův stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C5%AFv_stadion) in [Olomouc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olomouc) and [Městský stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%9Bstsk%C3%BD_stadion_\(Ostrava\)) in [Ostrava](464078.md).
