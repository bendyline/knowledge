The **2026 Stanley Cup playoffs** was the [playoff tournament](10490910.md) of the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL) for the [2025–26 season](79321758.md). The playoffs began on April 18, 2026, and concluded on June 14, 2026, with the [Carolina Hurricanes](70523.md) winning their second [Stanley Cup](66968.md) in franchise history, defeating the [Vegas Golden Knights](47712000.md) four games to two in the [Stanley Cup Final](80157147.md).[^cite_note-1]

The [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md) qualified for the playoffs as the [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy) winners with the most points (i.e., best record) during the regular season. The [Buffalo Sabres](69918.md) ended their NHL-record 14-year playoff drought, clinching a playoff spot for the first time since [2011](26981832.md). The [Anaheim Ducks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Ducks) qualified for the playoffs for the first time since [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), ending their seven-year playoff drought. The [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) also ended their five-year playoff drought by making the playoffs for the first time since [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Stanley_Cup_playoffs). The [Toronto Maple Leafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs) failed to qualify for the Stanley Cup playoffs for the first time since the [2015–16 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_NHL_season), ending what was the longest playoff streak in the NHL. The longest active playoff streak is now held jointly by the [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md) and [Tampa Bay Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning), who have qualified for the playoffs every year since 2018. Additionally, the two-time defending champion [Florida Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Panthers) failed to qualify for the playoffs, becoming the first defending champions to fail to qualify since the [2014–15 Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Los_Angeles_Kings_season), and the first two-time defending champions to miss the playoffs since the [1969–70 Montreal Canadiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969%E2%80%9370_Montreal_Canadiens_season).[^cite_note-2] The [Winnipeg Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnipeg_Jets) also became the fifth defending Presidents' Trophy winner to fail to qualify for the playoffs. This is also the first time in league history that consecutive defending Presidents' Trophy winners failed to make the playoffs, as the New York Rangers failed to make the 2025 Stanley Cup Playoffs after winning the Presidents’ Trophy in 2024. The [Utah Mammoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Mammoth) tied the [Seattle Kraken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Kraken) as the second-fastest expansion team to clinch their first playoff berth, doing so in their second season, and for the first time since [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965%E2%80%9366_NHL_season#Playoffs), none of the [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area)-based teams (the [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils), [New York Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Islanders), and [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers)) qualified for the playoffs.[^cite_note-3]

For the first time since [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), following Tampa Bay's elimination in the first round, neither of the NHL's Florida-based teams made it to the Stanley Cup Final. The Carolina Hurricanes became the first team to have a 12–1 record going into the Final, and the fewest number of losses since the [1975–76 Montreal Canadiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_Montreal_Canadiens_season).[^cite_note-4]

The Hurricanes finished the playoffs with a 16–3 record, the fewest amount of losses for a Stanley Cup winner since the [1987–88 Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987%E2%80%9388_Edmonton_Oilers_season).[^cite_note-5]

## Playoff bracket

In each round, teams competed in a best-of-seven series following a 2–2–1–1–1 format (scores in the bracket indicate the number of games won in each best-of-seven series). The team with [home ice advantage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_advantage) plays at home for games one and two (and games five and seven, if necessary), and the other team plays at home for games three and four (and game six, if necessary). The top three teams in each division made the playoffs, along with two wild cards in each conference, for a total of eight teams from each conference.&#x20;

In the first round, the lower [seeded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_\(sports\)) wild card in the conference played against the division winner with the best record while the other wild card played against the other division winner, and both wild cards were [de facto](43594.md) #4 seeds. The other series matched the second and third-place teams from the divisions. In the first two rounds, home-ice advantage was awarded to the team with the better seed. Thereafter, it was awarded to the team that had the better regular season record.

## First round

### Eastern Conference first round

#### (A1) Buffalo Sabres vs. (WC1) Boston Bruins

The Buffalo Sabres finished first in the Atlantic Division with 109 points. The Boston Bruins earned 100 points to finish as the first wild card in the Eastern Conference. This was the ninth playoff meeting between these two teams with Boston winning six of the eight previous series. They last met in the [2010 Eastern Conference quarterfinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(3\)_Buffalo_Sabres_vs._\(6\)_Boston_Bruins), which Boston won in six games. The Sabres qualified for the playoffs for the first time since [2011](26981832.md). Boston won three of the four games in the regular season series.

The Sabres defeated the Bruins in six games. [Tage Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tage_Thompson) scored two of the four Sabres goals in the third period of game one, coming back from a two-goal deficit to defeat Boston 4–3.[^cite_note-7] In game two, [Viktor Arvidsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Arvidsson) scored twice for Boston as the Bruins emerged victorious with a 4–2 victory.[^cite_note-8] [Alex Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Lyon_\(ice_hockey\)) made 24 saves in game three, backstopping the Sabres to a 3–1 victory.[^cite_note-9] In game four, Lyon stopped 23 shots as six different players scored for the Sabres, defeating the Bruins 6–1 to take a 3–1 series lead.[^cite_note-10] [David Pastrňák](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Pastr%C5%88%C3%A1k) kept the Bruins' season alive in game five, scoring the overtime goal to force a sixth game in a 2–1 affair.[^cite_note-11] In game six, Lyon made 25 saves and Tage Thompson assisted twice for the Sabres, sending Buffalo to the second round for the first time since [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) with a 4–1 triumph.

#### (A2) Tampa Bay Lightning vs. (A3) Montreal Canadiens

The Tampa Bay Lightning finished second in the Atlantic Division with 106 points. The Montreal Canadiens also earned 106 points to finish third in the Atlantic Division as Tampa Bay won the first tie-breaker of regulation wins. This was the fifth playoff meeting between these two teams with Tampa Bay winning three of the four previous series. They last met in the [2021 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Stanley_Cup_Final), which Tampa Bay won in five games. These teams split their four-game regular season series.

The Canadiens defeated the Lightning in seven games, with each game-winning goal scored in the third period or overtime. In addition, each game was decided by one goal, the first time in a seven-game series since [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) and the third overall. In game one, [Juraj Slafkovský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juraj_Slafkovsk%C3%BD)'s hat trick goal in overtime gave the Canadiens a 4–3 victory.[^cite_note-12] Game two also required overtime in which [J. J. Moser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._J._Moser) scored for Tampa Bay to tie the series 1–1 with a 3–2 victory.[^cite_note-13] In game three, [Lane Hutson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lane_Hutson) scored the overtime winner for the Canadiens, giving Montreal a 3–2 victory.[^cite_note-14] [Brandon Hagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Hagel) scored twice in game four, tying the series 2–2 for the Lightning with a 3–2 victory.[^cite_note-15] In game five, [Jakub Dobeš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_Dobe%C5%A1) made 38 saves for the Canadiens in a 3–2 victory, taking a 3–2 series lead in the process.[^cite_note-16] The Lightning forced a seventh game as [Andrei Vasilevskiy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Vasilevskiy) stopped all 30 shots he faced and [Gage Goncalves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gage_Goncalves) scored in overtime in a 1–0 victory.[^cite_note-17] In game seven, [Kaiden Guhle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiden_Guhle) assisted twice and Dobeš made 28 saves to send Montreal to the second round with a 2–1 victory.[^cite_note-18] The Canadiens set an NHL record for the fewest shots on goal in a playoff win, registering only nine shots.[^cite_note-19]

### Western Conference first round

#### (C1) Colorado Avalanche vs. (WC2) Los Angeles Kings

The Colorado Avalanche earned the [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy) as the NHL's best regular season team with 121 points. The Los Angeles Kings earned 90 points to finish as the second wild card in the Western Conference. This was the third playoff meeting between these two teams with Colorado winning both previous series. They last met in the [2002 Western Conference quarterfinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(2\)_Colorado_Avalanche_vs._\(7\)_Los_Angeles_Kings), which Colorado won in seven games. Colorado won all three games in the regular season series.

The Avalanche defeated the Kings in a four-game sweep. [Scott Wedgewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Wedgewood) made 24 saves in game one as the Avalanche defeated the Kings 2–1.[^cite_note-31] [Nicolas Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_Roy) scored the overtime-winning goal for the Avalanche in game two, defeating the Kings 2–1.[^cite_note-32] In game three, [Artturi Lehkonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artturi_Lehkonen) provided a goal and [assist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assist_\(ice_hockey\)) for the Avalanche, taking a 3–0 series lead with a 4–2 victory.[^cite_note-33] In game four, [Nathan MacKinnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_MacKinnon) scored twice and provided an assist in a 5–1 victory, advancing the Avalanche to the second round.[^cite_note-34]

#### (P2) Edmonton Oilers vs. (P3) Anaheim Ducks

The Edmonton Oilers finished second in the Pacific Division with 93 points. The Anaheim Ducks earned 92 points to finish third in the Pacific. This was the third playoff meeting between these two teams with both teams splitting the two previous series. They last met in the [2017 Western Conference second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(P1\)_Anaheim_Ducks_vs._\(P2\)_Edmonton_Oilers), which Anaheim won in seven games. The Ducks qualified for the playoffs for the first time since [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Stanley_Cup_playoffs). Edmonton won two of the three games in the regular season series.

The Ducks defeated the Oilers in six games. [Kasperi Kapanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasperi_Kapanen) spoiled the Ducks' comeback in game one, scoring with 1\:54 left in the game to make it 4–3 for the Oilers.[^cite_note-47] In game two, [Jackson LaCombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_LaCombe) provided three assists for the Ducks, defeating the Oilers 6–4.[^cite_note-48] [Mikael Granlund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikael_Granlund) scored a goal and assisted thrice in game three, giving the Ducks a 7–4 victory.[^cite_note-49] In game four, the Ducks came back from a two-goal deficit to force overtime. In overtime, [Ryan Poehling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Poehling) initially scored the winning goal, but a controversial call on the goal required video review to confirm whether the puck had crossed the goal line. After the video review, Poehling's goal stood and the Ducks took a 3–1 series lead with a 4–3 victory.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] The Oilers avoided in elimination in game five as [Leon Draisaitl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Draisaitl) scored twice in a 4–1 affair.[^cite_note-52][Troy Terry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troy_Terry) and [Chris Kreider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Kreider) provided a goal and two assists in game six, giving Anaheim a 5–2 victory and its first playoff series win since [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Stanley_Cup_playoffs).[^cite_note-53]

#### (A1) Buffalo Sabres vs. (A3) Montreal Canadiens

This was the eighth playoff meeting between these two teams with Montreal winning four of the seven previous series. They last met in the [1998 Eastern Conference semifinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(6\)_Buffalo_Sabres_vs._\(7\)_Montreal_Canadiens), which Buffalo won in a four-game sweep. These teams split their four-game regular season series.

The Canadiens defeated the Sabres in seven games. In game one, [Josh Doan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Doan) and [Ryan McLeod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_McLeod) both provided a goal and an assist for the Sabres, defeating the Canadiens 4–2.[^cite_note-54] [Alex Newhook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Newhook) scored twice and [Jakub Dobeš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_Dobe%C5%A1) made 29 saves for the Canadiens, defeating the Sabres 5–1.[^cite_note-55] Newhook scored twice again in game three as the Canadiens routed the Sabres 6–2.[^cite_note-56] In game four, [Tage Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tage_Thompson) provided a goal and an assist to give the Sabres a 3–2 victory, tying the series 2–2.[^cite_note-57] [Nick Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Suzuki) scored a goal and two assists and [Juraj Slafkovský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juraj_Slafkovsk%C3%BD) assisted thrice in game five, helping Montreal secure a 6–3 victory and a 3–2 series lead.[^cite_note-58] Buffalo came back from a two-goal deficit in game six, scoring seven unanswered goals en route to an 8–3 victory and forcing a seventh game.[^cite_note-59] In game seven, the Sabres forced overtime after being down 2–0. In overtime, [Alex Newhook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Newhook) scored to give Montreal a 3–2 victory, and a conference final berth.

#### (M1) Carolina Hurricanes vs. (M3) Philadelphia Flyers

This was the first playoff meeting between these two teams. Carolina won three of the four games in the regular season series.

The Hurricanes defeated the Flyers in a four-game sweep. In game one, [Logan Stankoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Stankoven) continued his goal-scoring streak, scoring twice while [Frederik Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik_Andersen) stopped all 19 shots to defeat the Flyers 3–0.[^cite_note-60] The Hurricanes came back from a two-goal deficit in game two as [Taylor Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Hall) scored in overtime to give Carolina a 2–0 series lead.[^cite_note-61] In game three, [Andrei Svechnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Svechnikov) scored a goal and provided an assist, giving Carolina a 3–0 series lead with a 4–1 victory.[^cite_note-62] Game four was tied 2–2 going into overtime, wherein, [Jackson Blake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_Blake) scored his second goal of the game, sending Carolina to the conference final with a 3–2 victory.[^cite_note-63] Carolina became the first team to have back-to-back series sweeps since the [1992 Chicago Blackhawks and Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#Stanley_Cup_Finals). Carolina is also the first team since the [1985 Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(S1\)_Edmonton_Oilers_vs._\(N2\)_Chicago_Black_Hawks) to start the playoffs 8–0 and the first to do so since the four-round, best-of-seven format was introduced in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Stanley_Cup_playoffs).[^cite_note-64]

### Western Conference second round

#### (C1) Colorado Avalanche vs. (C3) Minnesota Wild

This was the fourth playoff meeting between these two teams, with Minnesota winning two of the three previous series. They last met in the [2014 Western Conference first round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(C1\)_Colorado_Avalanche_vs._\(WC1\)_Minnesota_Wild), which Minnesota won in seven games. These teams split their four-game regular-season series.

The Avalanche defeated the Wild in five games. Game one was a high-scoring affair as [Cale Makar](53736994.md), [Devon Toews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devon_Toews), and [Martin Nečas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Ne%C4%8Das) each had three or more points for the Avalanche while [Quinn Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinn_Hughes) had three for the Wild. During the game, the Wild came back from a three-goal deficit to briefly take the lead 5–4, but the Avalanche scored five more goals to emerge victorious 9–6.[^cite_note-65] In game two, [Nathan MacKinnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_MacKinnon) scored a goal and assisted twice for the Avalanche as they defeated the Wild 5–2.[^cite_note-66] [Kirill Kaprizov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirill_Kaprizov) had three points and [Jesper Wallstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesper_Wallstedt) made 35 saves in game three for the Wild, defeating the Avalanche 5–1.[^cite_note-67] In game four, the Avalanche recovered from an early 1–0 deficit defeating Minnesota 5–2 to take a 3–1 series lead.[^cite_note-68] The Avalanche came back from a 3–0 deficit in game five, scoring in overtime on [Brett Kulak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Kulak), sending Colorado to the conference final with a 4–3 victory.[^cite_note-69]

## Conference finals

Main article: [NHL conference finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_conference_finals)

### Eastern Conference final

#### (M1) Carolina Hurricanes vs. (A3) Montreal Canadiens

This was the eighth playoff meeting between these two teams, with Montreal winning five of the seven previous series. They last met in the [2006 Eastern Conference quarterfinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(2\)_Carolina_Hurricanes_vs._\(7\)_Montreal_Canadiens), which Carolina won in six games. Carolina made their second consecutive and seventh overall conference finals appearance. Carolina lost the [previous year's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#Eastern_Conference_final) Eastern Conference final against the [Florida Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Panthers), in five games. This was Montreal's ninth appearance in the semifinal/conference finals since the league began using a 16-team or greater playoff format in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979%E2%80%9380_NHL_season). Their most recent appearance was in the [2021 semifinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(1\)_Vegas_Golden_Knights_vs._\(4\)_Montreal_Canadiens), which Montreal won against the [Vegas Golden Knights](47712000.md) in six games. Montreal won all three games in the regular season series.

Carolina defeated the Canadiens in five games. [Juraj Slafkovský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juraj_Slafkovsk%C3%BD) scored twice in Montreal's 6–2 rout of Carolina in game one, with the Canadiens scoring four goals in the first period.[^cite_note-75] The Hurricanes then tied the series in game two with [Nikolaj Ehlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolaj_Ehlers)'s overtime goal, his second goal of the game, winning 3–2.[^cite_note-76] In game three, [Andrei Svechnikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Svechnikov) scored for Carolina in overtime, giving the Hurricanes a 3–2 victory.[^cite_note-77] The Hurricanes shut out the Canadiens 4–0 in game four with [Frederik Andersen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederik_Andersen) stopping all 18 shots he faced.[^cite_note-78] In game five, [Logan Stankoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Stankoven) provided a goal and two assists for the Hurricanes, defeating Montreal 6–1 to advance to the Stanley Cup Final.[^cite_note-79]

### Western Conference final

#### (C1) Colorado Avalanche vs. (P1) Vegas Golden Knights

This was the second playoff series between these two teams. Their only previous series was in the [2021 West Division second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(W1\)_Colorado_Avalanche_vs._\(W2\)_Vegas_Golden_Knights), which Vegas won in six games. This was Vegas's fifth semifinals/conference finals appearance. Their last appearance was in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#\(P1\)_Vegas_Golden_Knights_vs._\(C2\)_Dallas_Stars) against the [Dallas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Stars), which Vegas won in six games. This was Colorado's tenth appearance in the conference final. They last made the conference final in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Stanley_Cup_playoffs#Western_Conference_final), which Colorado won against the [Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers) in a four-game sweep. Colorado won two of the three games in this year's regular season series.

The Golden Knights defeated the Avalanche in a four-game sweep. In game one, [Carter Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carter_Hart) made 36 saves for the Golden Knights, holding off the Avalanche for a 4–2 victory.[^cite_note-80] [Ivan Barbashev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Barbashev) scored twice and provided an assist for the Golden Knights as Hart made 30 saves in game two, backstopping Vegas to a 3–1 victory.[^cite_note-81] The Golden Knights came back from a three-goal deficit in game three, taking a 3–0 series lead with a 5–3 triumph.[^cite_note-82] In game four, Vegas held off the Avalanche in the third period, giving them a 2–1 victory, and a Stanley Cup Final berth. With the series victory, the Golden Knights became the first team to record a sweep over a [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy)-winning team since the [Columbus Blue Jackets](73128.md) in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Tampa_Bay_Lightning%E2%80%93Columbus_Blue_Jackets_playoff_series). [^cite_note-83] This was the only series of the playoffs not to have a game go into overtime.

## Media

### Canada

This was the 12th and final postseason of the league's Canadian national broadcast rights deal with [Rogers Sports & Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Sports_&_Media) before a new 12-season contract begins next season (2026–27).[^cite_note-SN_2014_deal-86][^cite_note-SN_2026_deal-87] Shortly after the 2026 Stanley Cup Final concluded, the [CBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Broadcasting_Corporation) announced that it would no longer sublicense NHL games from Rogers, making game six the last NHL game to air on [CBC Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_Television).[^cite_note-88]

Games aired across [Sportsnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_on_Sportsnet), [SN1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsnet_One), [SN360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsnet_360), and CBC under the *[Hockey Night in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Night_in_Canada)* brand. For first and second-round U.S.–U.S. games not on CBC, Sportsnet used some the U.S. feed instead of producing their own telecast. The 2026 Stanley Cup Final was simulcast on both CBC and Sportsnet.[^cite_note-SN_round1-89]

[Sportsnet+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsnet+) streamed all games.[^cite_note-SN_round1-89]

### United States

This was the fifth season of the league's seven-year U.S. national broadcast rights deals with the [ESPN family of networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_on_ESPN) and [TNT Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_on_TNT).[^cite_note-cnbc-90]

First- and second-round games were split between ESPN-produced telecasts (either on ESPN, [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_on_ABC), or [ESPN2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN2)) and TNT Sports-produced telecasts (either on TNT or [TBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBS_\(American_TV_channel\)), with selected simulcasts on [TruTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TruTV)). Each U.S. team's regional broadcaster also televised local coverage of first-round games.[^cite_note-ESPN_round1-1-91][^cite_note-TNT_round-1-1-92]

ESPN/ABC had the first choice of which conference final series to air,[^cite_note-cnbc-90] choosing the Western Conference finals this season. As a result, TNT Sports broadcast the Eastern Conference finals.[^cite_note-3rd-round-93] As per the alternating rotation, ABC has coverage of the 2026 Stanley Cup Final.[^cite_note-cnbc-90]

All ESPN games streamed on the [ESPN app](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_app)'s unlimited tier, while TNT games streamed on [HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max).[^cite_note-ESPN_round1-1-91][^cite_note-TNT_round-1-1-92]
