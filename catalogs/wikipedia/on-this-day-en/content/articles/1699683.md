**Šalčininkai** (Lithuanian pronunciation: [\[ˈʃaːlʲt͡ɕɪnʲɪŋkɐɪ̯\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Lithuanian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b8/Salcininkai.ogg/Salcininkai.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b8/Salcininkai.ogg/Salcininkai.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Soleczniki*; *Solechnik*; [Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language): Салечнікі) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_\(Lithuania\)) in [Vilnius County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilnius_County), in south-eastern [Lithuania](17675.md), situated south-east of [Vilnius](32597.md), near the border with [Belarus](3457.md).

## Etymology

The name of the city derives from [Šalčia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0al%C4%8Dia) river, *šalta* meaning cold in [ Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_language).[^cite_note-2]

## History



Church in 1937

In the [medieval period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_period) the region around Šalčininkai was dominated by [Lithuanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanians) and it was the birthplace of many authors of the earliest Lithuanian-language texts (including [Stanislovas Rapalionis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislovas_Rapalionis), [Jurgis Zablockis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurgis_Zablockis) and [Aleksandras Rodūnonis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandras_Rod%C5%ABnonis?action=edit\&redlink=1)). In 1420, Lithuanian-speaking [Nicholas of Šalčininkai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_of_%C5%A0al%C4%8Dininkai) was born in Šalčininkai and in 1453–1467 he served as the [Bishop of Vilnius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Vilnius), the capital of the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania).[^cite_note-3]

In the late 19th century many of the local inhabitants mixed with the neighbouring [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians) and called themselves *[tutejszy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tutejszy)* ("the locals"), while staying [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholics), they didn't assign themselves to a single ethnic group.

The region is known for its uncodified [Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language)[^cite_note-4] vernacular (also known as '[po prostu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_prostu)', meaning 'simply' or 'plainly')[^cite_note-5] and the city itself is considered the provincial centre of [Polish culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_in_Lithuania) in Lithuania (the urban centre being [Vilnius](32597.md)).

Šalčininkai attained the town status in 1956 and is now a [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) of the [Šalčininkai district municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0al%C4%8Dininkai_district_municipality).

## Demographics

[According to the latest census of 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_census_of_2021), Šalčininkai had 6857 inhabitants and features a multi-ethnic population of 4930 [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poles_in_Lithuania) (71.9%), 920 [Lithuanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_people) (15.7%), 438 [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Lithuania) (6.4%), 286 [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians_in_Lithuania) (4.2%), 61 [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_Lithuania) (0,9%) and 222 people of other background (3.2%). 12.2% of all inhabitants in [Šalčininkai district municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0al%C4%8Dininkai_district_municipality), according to the 2021 census were born abroad, while 87.8% were born in [Lithuania](17675.md). This was a decrease from 14.3%, recorded by [the previous – 2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_census_of_2011). Out of 34.5 thousand inhabitants in 2011, 3711 or 10.7% of all the inhabitants were born in [Belarus](3457.md), 728 or 2.1% in [Russia](25391.md).[^cite_note-6]

In 2000 coat of arms of Šalčininkai was adopted by a decree of the [President of the Republic of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Republic_of_Lithuania), designed by [Arvydas Každailis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvydas_Ka%C5%BEdailis), coat of arms consists of three [hazelnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corylus_avellana) symbolizing solidarity.[^cite_note-7]
