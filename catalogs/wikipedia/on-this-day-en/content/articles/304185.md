**Lanzarote** ([UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/ˌlænzəˈrɒti/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English),[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/-ˈroʊteɪ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), Spanish: [\[lanθaˈɾote\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b8/Pronunciation_of_Lanzarote_in_Spanish.ogg/Pronunciation_of_Lanzarote_in_Spanish.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b8/Pronunciation_of_Lanzarote_in_Spanish.ogg/Pronunciation_of_Lanzarote_in_Spanish.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, locally [\[lansaˈɾote\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is a Spanish island, the easternmost of the [Canary Islands](5717.md), 125 kilometres (80 miles) off the north coast of [Africa](5334607.md) and 1,000 kilometres (600 miles) from the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula).&#x20;

Covering 845.94 square kilometres (326.62 square miles), Lanzarote is the fourth-largest of the islands in the archipelago. With 163,230 inhabitants at the beginning of 2024,[^cite_note-pob_2024-2] it is the third most populous Canary Island, after [Tenerife](269223.md) and [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria). Located in the centre-west of the island is [Timanfaya National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timanfaya_National_Park), one of its main attractions. The island was declared a [biosphere reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere_reserve) by [UNESCO](21786641.md) in 1993.[^cite_note-biospherereserve-5] The island's capital is [Arrecife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrecife), which lies on the eastern coastline.[^cite_note-6] It is the smaller main island of the [Province of Las Palmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Las_Palmas).

The first recorded name for the island, given by Italian-Majorcan cartographer [Angelino Dulcert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angelino_Dulcert), was *Insula de Lanzarotus Marocelus*, after the [Genoese](47332321.md) navigator [Lancelotto Malocello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancelotto_Malocello), from which the modern name is derived. The island's name in the native [Guanche language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanche_language) was *Tyterogaka* or *Tytheroygaka*, which may mean "one that is all [ochre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochre)" (referring to the island's predominant colour previous to the [Timanfaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timanfaya_National_Park) [eruption](8921015.md)).[^cite_note-Diccionario_Ínsuloamaziq-Tyterogaka-7]

## History



Flag of Lanzarote

Lanzarote is believed to have been the first Canary Island to be settled. The [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicians) may have visited or settled there, though no material evidence survives. The first known record came from [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) author [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) in the encyclopaedia *[Naturalis Historia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalis_Historia)* on an expedition to the Canary Islands.[^cite_note-8] The names of the islands (then called [*Insulae Fortunatae* or the "Fortunate Isles"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortunate_Isles)) were recorded as *Junonia* ([Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura)), *Canaria* ([Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria)), *Ninguaria* ([Tenerife](269223.md)), *Junonia Major* ([La Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Palma)), *Pluvialia* ([El Hierro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Hierro)), and *Capraria* ([La Gomera](220016.md)). Lanzarote and Fuerteventura, the two easternmost Canary Islands, were only mentioned as the archipelago of the "purple islands". The Roman poet [Lucan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucan) and the Greek astronomer and geographer [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy) gave their precise locations.[^cite_note-archaeology9705-9] It was settled by the Majos tribe of the [Guanches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanches).[^cite_note-10] After the [fall of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire), interaction with the Canary Islands is unrecorded before 999, when the [Arabs](2185.md) arrived at the island which they dubbed *al-Djezir al-Khalida* (among other names).

In 1336, a ship arrived from [Lisbon](18091.md) under the guidance of Genoese navigator [Lancelotto Malocello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancelotto_Malocello), who used the alias "Lanzarote da Framqua". A fort was later built in the area of Montaña de Guanapay near today's Teguise. Castilian slaving expeditions in 1385 and 1393 seized hundreds of Guanches and sold them in Spain, initiating the slave trade in the islands.[^cite_note-Crosby2004-11][^cite_note-Mercer1980-12] French explorer [Jean de Béthencourt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_de_B%C3%A9thencourt) arrived in 1402, heading a private expedition under Castilian auspices. Bethencourt first visited the south of Lanzarote at Playas de Papagayo, and the French overran the island within a matter of months. The island lacked mountains and gorges to serve as hideouts for the remaining Guanche population, and so many Guanches were taken away as slaves that only 300 Guanche men were said to have remained.

At the southern end of the [Yaiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaiza) municipality, the first European settlement in the Canary Islands appeared in 1402 in the area known as *El Rubicón*, where the conquest of the Archipelago began.[^cite_note-juliosanchezrodriguez.com-13] In this place, the Cathedral of [Saint Martial of Limoges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Martial) was built. The cathedral was destroyed by [English](18803164.md) pirates in the 16th century. A diocese was moved in 1483 to [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria) ([Roman Catholic Diocese of Canarias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Canarias)).[^cite_note-juliosanchezrodriguez.com-13] In 1404, the Castilians (with the support of the King of Castile) came and fought the local Guanches, who were further decimated. The islands of Fuerteventura and El Hierro were later similarly conquered. In 1477, a decision by the royal council of Castile confirmed a grant of Lanzarote and Fuerteventura, with the smaller islands of Ferro and Gomera to the Castilian nobles Herrera, who held their fief until the end of the 18th century.[^cite_note-Kamen2004-14] In 1586, the [Ottoman](22278.md) admiral [Murat Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murat_Reis_the_Elder) [temporarily seized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Lanzarote_\(1586\)) Lanzarote. In the 17th century, pirates raided the island and took 1,000 inhabitants into slavery in [Cueva de los Verdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cueva_de_los_Verdes).

Lanzarote and [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura) would be the main exporters of wheat and cereals to the central islands of the archipelago during the 16th, 17th and 18th centuries; [Tenerife](269223.md) and [Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Canaria).[^cite_note-A-15] Although this trade was almost never reversed for the inhabitants of Lanzarote and Fuerteventura (due to the fact that the landowners of these islands profited from this activity), producing periods of famine, so the population of these islands had to travel to Tenerife and Gran Canaria. The island of Tenerife is a major focus of attraction for the inhabitants of Lanzarote and Fuerteventura, hence the feeling of union that has always existed in the popular sphere with Tenerife.[^cite_note-A-15]



Lava and pyroclast-covered fields in the central part of Lanzarote, with volcanoes from the 1730-1736 eruption in the background.

From 1730 to 1736, the island was hit by a series of [volcanic eruptions](8921015.md), producing 32 new volcanoes in a stretch of 18 kilometres (11 miles).[^cite_note-onlinelibrary.wiley.com-16] The priest of Yaiza, Don Andrés Lorenzo Curbelo, documented the eruption in detail until 1731. Lava covered a quarter of the island's surface, including the most fertile soil and 11 villages. 100 smaller volcanoes were located in the area called *Montañas del Fuego*, the "Mountains of Fire".[^cite_note-17] In 1768, drought affected the deforested island, and winter rains did not fall. Much of the population was forced to emigrate to [Cuba](5042481.md) and the [Americas](29833.md), including a group which formed a significant addition to the Spanish settlers in [Texas](29810.md) at [San Antonio de Bexar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio_de_Bexar) in 1731.

In 1927, Lanzarote and Fuerteventura became part of the province of [Las Palmas](163701.md). Several archaeological expeditions have uncovered the prehistoric settlement at the archaeologic site of *El Bebedero* in the village of [Teguise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teguise_\(village\)).[^cite_note-18] In one of those expeditions, by a team from the [University of Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Las_Palmas_de_Gran_Canaria) and a team from the [University of Zaragoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zaragoza), yielded about 100 Roman potsherds, nine pieces of metal, and one piece of glass. The artefacts were found in strata dated between the 1st and 4th centuries. They show that Romans did trade with the Canarians, though there is no evidence of settlements.[^cite_note-archaeology9705-9]



Characteristic white houses of the towns and villages on the volcanic landscape of Lanzarote.

The island has a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosphere_Reserve) protected site status. According to a report in the *[Financial Times](136566.md)*, this status was endangered by a local corruption scandal. Since May 2009, police have arrested the former president of Lanzarote, the former mayor of Arrecife and more than 20 politicians and businessmen in connection with illegal building permits along Lanzarote's coastline. UNESCO has threatened to revoke Lanzarote's Biosphere Reserve status, "if the developments are not respecting local needs and are impacting on the environment".[^cite_note-ft-eco-19][^cite_note-indepenent-ft-20] The President of the [Cabildo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabildo_\(council\)) of Lanzarote denied "any threat to Lanzarote's UNESCO status".[^cite_note-greenslade-controversy-21]

## Geography



Atlantic Ocean at Los Picollos, Lanzarote

Lanzarote is located 125 kilometres (80 miles) north-west of [Africa](5334607.md), 11 km (7 mi) north-east of [Fuerteventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerteventura) and just over 1 km (5⁄8 mi) from [La Graciosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graciosa,_Canary_Islands). The dimensions of the island are 60 km (37 mi) from north to south and 25 km (16 mi) from west to east. Lanzarote has 213 kilometres (132 miles) of coastline, of which 10 km (6 mi) are sand, 16.5 km (10+1⁄4 mi) are beach, and the remainder is rocky. Its landscape includes the mountain ranges of [Famara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famara) (671 metres or 2,201 feet)[^cite_note-22] in the north and [Ajaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajaches?action=edit\&redlink=1) (608 metres or 1,995 feet) to the south. South of the Famara massif is the El Jable desert, which separates Famara and Montañas del Fuego. The highest peak is Peñas del Chache, rising to 670 metres (2,200 feet) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level). The "[Tunnel of Atlantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel_of_Atlantis)", the largest underwater volcanic tunnel in the world, is part of the [Cueva de los Verdes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cueva_de_los_Verdes) [lava tube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_tube).[^cite_note-LS-23]

### Climate

Often called the "Island of Eternal Spring",[^cite_note-\:0-24] Lanzarote has a subtropical [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) (*BWh*) according to the [Köppen climatic classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climatic_classification).[^cite_note-25] The small amount of precipitation is mainly concentrated in the winter. Rainfall during summer is a rare phenomenon and very often summers are completely dry without any precipitation. On average the island receives approximately 16 days of precipitation between December and February.[^cite_note-\:0-24] Sometimes, the hot [sirocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirocco) wind prevails, causing dry and dusty conditions across the island.[^cite_note-\:0-24] Average precipitation in June and August is less than 0.5 millimetres (0.020 inches).

## Geology

See also: [Geology of the Canary Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology_of_the_Canary_Islands)



Caldera de Los Cuervos in Los Volcanes Natural Park



Montaña Colorada in Los Volcanes Natural Park

Lanzarote is the northernmost and easternmost island of the main Canary Islands and has a volcanic origin.[^cite_note-29] The island emerged about 15 million years ago as product of the [Canary hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_hotspot). The island, along with others, emerged after the breakup of the African and the American continental plates. The greatest recorded eruptions occurred between 1730 and 1736. The eruption period started outside the [Timanfaya National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timanfaya_National_Park) area, at [Caldera de Los Cuervos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera_de_Los_Cuervos?action=edit\&redlink=1) volcano on 1 September 1730 and ended with the eruption of [Montaña Colorada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monta%C3%B1a_Colorada?action=edit\&redlink=1) volcano, 1.7 km (1.1 mi) north-east of El Cuervo, which has ceased the activity on 16 April 1736.[^cite_note-30]

## Biodiversity



[Malvasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malvasia) grape vines growing in topsoil covered in [lapilli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapilli), in [La Geria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Geria). The low, curved walls protect the vines from the constant, drying wind.

### Flora

There are five hundred different kinds of plants on the island, of which 17 species are [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism). These plants have adapted to the relative scarcity of water in the same way as [succulents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succulent). They include the [Canary Island date palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Island_date_palm) (*Phoenix canariensis*), which is found in damper areas of the north, the [Canary Island pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Island_pine) (*Pinus canariensis*), [ferns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fern), and [wild olive trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olea_europaea) (*Olea europaea*). *[Laurisilva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurisilva)* trees, which once covered the highest parts of Risco de Famara, are rarely found today. After winter rainfall, the vegetation comes to a colourful bloom between February and March.

The [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyard) of La Gería, [Lanzarote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzarote_\(DO\)) *[DO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denominaci%C3%B3n_de_origen)* wine region, are a protected area. Single vines are planted in pits 4–5 metres (13–16 feet) wide and 2–3 metres (6 feet 7 inches – 9 feet 10 inches) deep, with small stone walls around each pit. This agricultural technique is designed to harvest rainfall and overnight dew and to protect the plants from the winds.[^cite_note-onlinelibrary.wiley.com-16]

### Fungi

There are 180 different species of [lichen-forming fungi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen). These survive in the suitable areas like rock surfaces, and promote weathering.

Apart from the native bats and the mammals which accompanied humans to the island (including the [dromedary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dromedary), which was used for agriculture and is now a tourist attraction), there are few vertebrate species on Lanzarote. These include birds (such as [falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon)) and reptiles. Some interesting endemic animals are the *[Gallotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallotia)* lizards and the blind *[Munidopsis polymorpha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munidopsis_polymorpha)* crabs found in the [Jameos del Agua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jameos_del_Agua) salt lake, which was formed by a volcanic eruption. The island is also home to one of two surviving populations of the threatened [Canarian Egyptian vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_Egyptian_vulture).

The island was formerly inhabited by the now extinct [lava mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_mouse) (*Malpaisomys insularis*), though the [Canarian shrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canarian_shrew) (*Crocidura canariensis*) still inhabits the island.[^cite_note-31]

### Air

The island's main point of entry is [César Manrique-Lanzarote Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanzarote_Airport) which, in 2018, handled 7327019 passengers.[^cite_note-aena_intro-38] It was renamed in 2019 to include the name of local artist [César Manrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Manrique), in honour of the legacy he left behind on the island and coinciding with the centenary of his birth.[^cite_note-airport_name-39] The airport has two passenger terminal buildings, T1 and T2, with T2 being used exclusively for inter-island flights to and from the other Canary Islands. These inter-island flights are operated by regional airlines [Binter Canarias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binter_Canarias) and [Canaryfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaryfly). Lanzarote Airport is located about 5 kilometres (3.1 mi) southwest of the island's capital, Arrecife, to which it is connected by the [LZ-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LZ2_\(Lanzarote\)) road.

### Sea

Most of the goods arrive by sea through the Port of Arrecife, [Puerto de los Mármoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_de_los_M%C3%A1rmoles?action=edit\&redlink=1). This port is also used by cruise ships. There are regular ferry lines that connect the Port of Arrecife with [Las Palmas de Gran Canaria](163701.md), [Santa Cruz de Tenerife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_de_Tenerife) and [Cadiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1diz).

Three ferry companies (Fred Olsen Express, Naviera Armas and Líneas Marítimas Romero) operate several times a day between Playa Blanca, the southernmost town on Lanzarote, and Corralejo on the northern coast of Fuerteventura.[^cite_note-40] Ferries to the island of [La Graciosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Graciosa) depart from [Órzola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93rzola), Lanzarote's northernmost town. The two main companies operating these services are [Fred Olsen Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Olsen_Express) and [Naviera Armas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naviera_Armas).

### Road

The [LZ-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LZ1_\(Lanzarote\)) road connects the capital, Arrecife, to the northernmost town of Órzola. The [LZ-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LZ2_\(Lanzarote\)) road connects Arrecife to the southernmost town of Playa Blanca. [LZ-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LZ3_\(Lanzarote\)) is a [highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa) that acts as a ring road around Arrecife, connecting Puerto de los Mármoles on the northern side of the city to LZ-2 on the southern side. These three roads form the island's central road axis from which other roads connect to the rest of the island's towns, settlements and points of interest.

Public transport on the island is provided by [Arrecife Bus](https://arrecifebus.com/), operating under the name of Intercity Bus Lanzarote. The company operates 30 bus lines connecting the island's major and minor settlements, as well as serving the airport, and includes internal bus services in the towns of [Playa Blanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_Blanca), [San Bartolomé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bartolom%C3%A9,_Las_Palmas) and [Tías](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%ADas,_Las_Palmas). Most lines begin or end in the capital, Arrecife.[^cite_note-intercitybuslines-41] The public bus service within the city of Arrecife is provided by the local council and consists of five lines, including one to the neighbouring town of Playa Honda.[^cite_note-guaguasarrecife-42]

## Festivals

The most established festival on the island is held each year on 15 September in the village of [Mancha Blanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mancha_Blanca), in honour of [Our Lady of Dolours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Sorrows) (*Virgen de los Dolores*), also called the "Virgin of the Volcanoes" (the Patron Saint of Lanzarote). People from all over the island participate in this pilgrimage, mostly dressed in traditional costumes.

## Sport

The island's only professional [football](10568.md) team is [UD Lanzarote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UD_Lanzarote), founded in 1970 who play at the 7,000 capacity [Ciudad Deportiva de Lanzarote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Deportiva_de_Lanzarote). The island is also home to smaller clubs [CD Teguise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD_Teguise) and [CD Orientación Marítima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CD_Orientaci%C3%B3n_Mar%C3%ADtima). In tennis, the [Open Isla de Lanzarote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Isla_de_Lanzarote) was hosted between 2006 and 2008. The island is well known for its epic Ironman Triathlon race held there for 34 years
