**Suriname**,[^cite_note-17] officially the **Republic of Suriname**,[^cite_note-18] is a country on the northern coast of [South America](26769.md), also considered part of the [Caribbean region](18956035.md). Situated [slightly north of the equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics), over 90% of its territory is covered by [rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest), [the second-highest proportion of forest cover in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_forest_area_\(percentage\)). Suriname is bordered by the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) to the north, [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) to the east, [Brazil](3383.md) to the south, and [Guyana](17238691.md) to the west. It is [the smallest sovereign country in South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_American_countries_by_area) by both population and territory,[^cite_note-19] with around 612,985 inhabitants in 2021 in an area of approximately 165,940 square kilometres (64,070 square miles).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Suriname_country_profile-12][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-UN_WPP-22][^cite_note-UN_WPP_2022-23] The capital and largest city is [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo), which is home to roughly half the population.

Suriname was inhabited as early as the fourth millennium BC by various [Indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas), including the [Lokono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokono), [Kalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalina_people), and [Wayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayana). Europeans arrived and contested the area in the 16th century, with the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) controlling much of the country's current territory by the late 17th century. Under [Dutch rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Surinam), Suriname was a lucrative [plantation colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation_economy) focused mostly on [sugar](27712.md); its economy was driven by [African slave labour](49005.md) until the abolition of slavery in 1863. Approximately 300,000 enslaved Africans were taken to Suriname during the transatlantic slave trade, from the mid-1600s to the early 1800s. After 1863, [indentured servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indentured_servitude) were recruited mostly from [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) and the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md). In 1954, Suriname became a [constituent country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_country_of_the_Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands) of the [Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands). On 25 November 1975, it became [independent](62450.md) following negotiations with the Dutch government. Suriname continues to maintain close diplomatic, economic, and cultural ties with the Netherlands. Surinamese culture and society strongly reflect the legacy of Dutch colonial rule.&#x20;

Suriname is the only independent state outside Europe where [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) is the official and prevailing language of government, business, media, and education;[^cite_note-cia-24] an estimated 60% of the population speaks Dutch as a native language.[^cite_note-25] [Sranan Tongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sranan_Tongo), an English-based [creole language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_creole), is a widely used *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca)* and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) itself is also widely spoken, understood and used. Most Surinamese are descendants of slaves brought from [Africa](5334607.md) by Europeans, and indentured labourers brought from [Asia](689.md) by the Dutch. Suriname is highly diverse, with no ethnic group forming a majority; proportionally, its Muslim and Hindu populations are some of the largest in the Americas. Most people live along the northern coast, centred on Paramaribo, making Suriname [one of the least densely populated countries on Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population_density).

It is a [developing country](78449.md) with a [high level of human development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index); its [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Suriname) is heavily dependent on its abundant [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resource), namely [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite), [gold](12240.md), [petroleum](23195.md), and [agricultural products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture). Suriname is a member of the [Caribbean Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Community) (CARICOM), the [United Nations](31769.md), the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md) and the [Organization of American States](22788.md).

## Etymology

The name *Suriname* may derive from an Indigenous people called *Surinen*, who inhabited the area at the time of European contact.[^cite_note-Britannica-26] The suffix -ame, common in Surinamese river and place names (see also the [Coppename River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coppename_River)), may come from *aima* or *eima*, meaning river or creek mouth, in [Lokono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokono_language), an [Arawakan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawakan_languages) spoken in the country.[^cite_note-27]

The earliest European sources give variants of "Suriname" as the name of the [river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriname_River) on which colonies were eventually founded. [Lawrence Kemys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence_Kemys) wrote in his *Relation of the Second Voyage to Guiana* of passing a river called "*Shurinama*" as he travelled along the coast. In 1598, a fleet of three Dutch ships visiting the Wild Coast mention passing the river "*Surinamo*". In 1617, a Dutch notary spelled the name of the river on which a Dutch trading post had existed three years earlier as "*Surrenant*".[^cite_note-De_Naam_Suriname-28]

British settlers, who in 1630 founded the first European colony at Marshall's Creek along the Suriname River, spelled the name "*Surinam*"; this would long remain the standard spelling in English.[^cite_note-Marshall-29] The Dutch navigator [David Pietersz. de Vries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Pietersz._de_Vries) wrote of travelling up the "*Sername*" river in 1634 until he encountered the English colony there; the terminal vowel remained in future Dutch spellings and pronunciations. The river was called *Soronama* in a 1640 Spanish manuscript entitled "General Description of All His Majesty's Dominions in America". In 1653, instructions given to a British fleet sailing to meet [Lord Willoughby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Willoughby,_5th_Baron_Willoughby_of_Parham) in [Barbados](3455.md), which at the time was the seat of English colonial government in the region, again spelled the name of the colony *Surinam*. A 1663 royal charter said the region around the river was "called Serrinam also Surrinam".[^cite_note-De_Naam_Suriname-28]

As a result of the *Surrinam* spelling, 19th-century British sources offered the folk etymology *Surryham*, saying it was the name given to the Suriname River by Lord Willoughby in the 1660s in honour of the [Duke of Norfolk and Earl of Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Howard,_5th_Duke_of_Norfolk) when an English [colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinam_\(English_colony\)) was established under a grant from King [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England).[^cite_note-De_Naam_Suriname-28] This folk etymology can be found repeated in later English-language sources.[^cite_note-International_Boundaries:_A_Case_Study-30][^cite_note-United_Services_Magazine-31]

When the territory was taken over by the Dutch, it became part of a group of colonies known as [Dutch Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonisation_of_the_Guianas). The official spelling of the country's English name was changed from "Surinam" to "Suriname" in January 1978, but "Surinam" can still be found in English, such as [Surinam Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinam_Airways) and the [Surinam toad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinam_toad). The older English name is reflected in the English pronunciation, [/ˈsjʊərɪnæm, -nɑːm/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English). In [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language), the official language of Suriname, the pronunciation is [\[ˌsyːriˈnaːmə\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch), with a [schwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwa) terminal vowel and the main stress on the third syllable.

## History

Main article: [History of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Suriname)

### Pre-colonial

Indigenous settlement of Suriname dates back to 3,000 BC. The largest tribes were the [Lokono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokono), a nomadic coastal tribe that subsisted on hunting, foraging and fishing. The Lokono represent the first described inhabitants in the area. The [Carib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalina_people) also settled in the area and conquered the Lokono by using their superior sailing ships. They settled in Galibi (*Kupali Yumï*, meaning "tree of the forefathers") at the mouth of the [Marowijne River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marowijne_River). While the larger Lokono and Carib tribes lived along the coast and savanna, smaller groups of indigenous people lived in the inland rainforest, such as the [Akurio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akurio_people), [Trió](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiriy%C3%B3_people), [Warao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warao_people), and [Wayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayana).

### Colonial period

Main articles: [Surinam (English colony)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinam_\(English_colony\)) and [Surinam (Dutch colony)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinam_\(Dutch_colony\))



The earliest known map of Suriname.

Beginning in the 16th century, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_France), [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Spain) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England) explorers visited the area. A century later, [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_England) settlers established [plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation) colonies along the many rivers in the fertile Guiana plains. The earliest documented colony in [Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guianas) was an English settlement named Marshall's Creek along the Suriname River.[^cite_note-Marshall-29] After that, there was another short-lived English colony called [Surinam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinam_\(English_colony\)) that lasted from 1650 to 1667.

From the early 17th century, Jews began to settle there after the expulsion from Spain and Portugal. They were granted freedom of religion in 1665 and the right to have their own courts and civil guard. They continued to enjoy autonomy after the colony was handed over to the Netherlands. Over half the sugarcane plantations in Suriname were owned by Jews, who received a permit to work areas that were pure jungle into agriculture.[^cite_note-32] In 1825, these special rights were rescinded.[^cite_note-33]

Disputes arose between the Dutch and the English for control of this territory. In 1667, during negotiations leading to the [Treaty of Breda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Breda_\(1667\)) after the [Second Anglo-Dutch War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Dutch_War), the Dutch decided to keep the nascent plantation colony of Surinam they had gained from the English. In return the English kept [New Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam), the main city of the former colony of [New Netherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland) in North America on the mid-Atlantic coast. The British renamed it [New York](645042.md), after the [Duke of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_of_York) who would later become King [James II of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_II_of_England).[^cite_note-34]

In 1683, the [Society of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Suriname) was founded by the city of [Amsterdam](844.md), the [Van Aerssen van Sommelsdijck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelis_van_Aerssen_van_Sommelsdijck) family, and the [Dutch West India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_West_India_Company). The society was chartered to manage and defend the colony. The planters of the colony relied heavily on [African slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Africa) to cultivate, harvest and process the commodity crops of coffee, cocoa, sugar cane and cotton plantations along the rivers. Approximately 300,000 enslaved Africans were taken to Suriname during the transatlantic slave trade, from the mid-1600s to the early 1800s. The treatment of slaves was notoriously brutal even by the standards of the time[^cite_note-35]—historian [C. R. Boxer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._R._Boxer) wrote that "man's inhumanity to man just about reached its limits in Surinam"[^cite_note-36]—and many slaves escaped plantations. In November 1795, the Society was nationalized by the [Batavian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavian_Republic) and from then on the Batavian Republic and its legal successors (the Kingdom of Holland and the Kingdom of the Netherlands) governed the territory as a national colony – barring two periods of British occupation, between 1799 and 1802, and between 1804 and 1816.

&#x20;\




[Slave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Suriname) auction in Paramaribo, in 1831

With the help of the [native South Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) living in the adjoining rain forests, runaway African slaves established a new and unique culture in the interior that was highly successful in its own right. They were known collectively in English as [Maroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maroon_\(people\)), in French as *Nèg'Marrons* (literally meaning "brown negroes", that is "pale-skinned negroes"), and in Dutch as *Marrons*. The Maroons gradually developed several independent tribes through a process of [ethnogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnogenesis), as they were from different African ethnicities. These tribes include the [Saramaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saramaka), [Paramaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaccan_people), [Ndyuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndyuka_people) or Aukan, [Kwinti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwinti), [Aluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluku) or Boni, and [Matawai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matawai_people).

The Maroons often raided plantations to recruit new members from the enslaved and capture women, as well as to acquire weapons, food, and supplies. They sometimes killed the Dutch planters and their families in the raids. European colonists built defences, which were significant enough that they were shown on 18th-century maps.[^cite_note-37]

The Dutch colonists also mounted armed campaigns against the Maroons, who generally escaped through the rainforest, which they knew much better than the colonists did. To end hostilities, in the 18th century, the European colonial authorities signed several peace treaties with different tribes. They granted the Maroons sovereign status and trade rights in their inland territories.

### Abolition of slavery

Further information: [Human rights in Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Suriname)

From 1861 to 1863, with the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) underway, and enslaved people escaping to Northern territory controlled by the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)), United States President [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) and [his administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Abraham_Lincoln) looked abroad for places to relocate people who were freed from [enslavement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enslavement) and who wanted to leave the United States. It opened negotiations with the Dutch government regarding African American emigration to and colonization of the [Dutch colony of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinam_\(Dutch_colony\)). Nothing came of the idea, which was dropped after 1864.[^cite_note-38]

The Netherlands abolished slavery in Suriname in 1863, under a gradual process that required slaves to work on plantations for 10 transition years for minimal pay, which was considered as partial compensation for their masters.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] After that transition period expired in 1873, most [freedmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedmen) largely abandoned the plantations where they had worked for several generations in favour of the capital city, [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo). Some of them were able to purchase the plantations they worked on, especially in the district of Para and Coronie. Their descendants still live on those grounds today. Several plantation owners did not pay their former enslaved workers the pay they owed them for the ten years following 1863. They paid the workers with the property rights of the ground of the plantation in order to escape their debt to the workers.[^cite_note-41] 1 July 1863 is called [Ketikoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketikoti).



[Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people) [indentured servants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indentured_servants) in Marienbourg plantation, c. 1930

As a plantation colony, Suriname had an economy dependent on labour-intensive commodity crops. To make up for a shortage of labour, the Dutch recruited and transported contract or [indentured labourers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indentured_labourer) from the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) (modern Indonesia) and India (the latter through an arrangement with the British, who then ruled the area). In addition, during the late 19th and early 20th centuries, small numbers of labourers, mostly men, were recruited from China and the Middle East.

Although Suriname's population remains relatively small, because of this complex colonisation and exploitation, it is one of the most ethnically and culturally diverse countries in the world.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

&#x20;During [World War II](32927.md), on 23 November 1941, under an agreement with the [Netherlands government-in-exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_government-in-exile), the United States sent 2,000 soldiers to Suriname to protect the [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite) mines to support the Allies' war effort.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] In 1942, the Dutch government-in-exile began to review the relations between the Netherlands and its colonies in terms of the post-war period.[^cite_note-46]



Maroon village, along [Suriname River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriname_River), 1955



Waterfront houses in [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo), 1955

In 1954, Suriname became one of the constituent countries of the [Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands), along with the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles) and the Netherlands. In this construction, the Netherlands retained control of its defence and foreign affairs. In 1974, the local government, led by the [National Party of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_of_Suriname) (NPS) (whose membership was largely [Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_peoples), meaning ethnically African or mixed African-European), started negotiations with the Dutch government leading towards full independence; in contrast to [Indonesia](14579.md)'s earlier [war for independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution) from the Netherlands, the path toward Suriname's independence had been an initiative of the [then left-wing Dutch government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_Uyl_cabinet). Independence was granted on 25 November 1975. A large part of Suriname's economy for the first decade following independence was fuelled by foreign aid provided by the Dutch government.

### Independence



Prime Minister [Henck Arron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henck_Arron), future queen [Beatrix of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrix_of_the_Netherlands) and President [Johan Ferrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Ferrier) on 25 November 1975

The first president of the country was [Johan Ferrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Ferrier), the former governor, with [Henck Arron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henck_Arron) (the then leader of the NPS) as prime minister. In the years leading up to independence, nearly one-third of the population of Suriname emigrated to the Netherlands, amidst concern that the new country would fare worse under independence than it had as a constituent country of the Kingdom of the Netherlands. Surinamese politics did degenerate into ethnic polarisation and corruption soon after independence, with the NPS using Dutch aid money for partisan purposes. Its leaders were accused of fraud in the [1977 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Surinamese_general_election), in which Arron won a further term, and the discontent was such that a large portion of the population fled to the Netherlands, joining the already significant Surinamese community there.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

### 1980 military coup

Main article: [1980 Surinamese coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Surinamese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat)

On 25 February 1980, a [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Surinamese_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) overthrew Arron's government. It was initiated by a group of 16 sergeants, led by [Dési Bouterse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9si_Bouterse).[^cite_note-cia-24] Opponents of the military regime attempted counter-coups in April 1980, August 1980, 15 March 1981, and again on 12 March 1982. The first counter attempt was led by [Fred Ormskerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Ormskerk),[^cite_note-Janssen2011-50] the second by [Marxist-Leninists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism),[^cite_note-51] the third by [Wilfred Hawker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilfred_Hawker), and the fourth by [Surendre Rambocus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surendre_Rambocus).

Hawker escaped from prison during the fourth counter-coup attempt, but he was captured and summarily executed. Between 2 am and 5 am on 7 December 1982, the military, under Bouterse's leadership, rounded up 13 prominent citizens who had criticized the military dictatorship and held them at [Fort Zeelandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Zeelandia_\(Paramaribo\)) in Paramaribo.[^cite_note-52] The dictatorship had all these men [executed over the next three days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_murders), along with Rambocus and [Jiwansingh Sheombar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiwansingh_Sheombar) (who was also involved in the fourth counter-coup attempt).

### Civil war, elections, and constitution

The brutal [civil war between the Suriname army and Maroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriname_Guerrilla_War) loyal to rebel leader [Ronnie Brunswijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronnie_Brunswijk), begun in 1986, continued and its effects further weakened Bouterse's position during the 1990s. Due to the civil war, more than 10,000 Surinamese, mostly Maroons, fled to [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) in the late 1980s.[^cite_note-53]

National elections were held in 1987. The National Assembly adopted a new constitution that allowed Bouterse to remain in charge of the army. Dissatisfied with the government, Bouterse summarily dismissed the ministers in 1990, by telephone. This event became popularly known as the "Telephone Coup". His power began to wane after the 1991 elections.

At the [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics) in [Seoul](19159283.md), Suriname became the smallest independent South American state to win its first ever Olympic medal as [Anthony Nesty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Nesty) won gold in the [100-metre butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_1988_Summer_Olympics).

The first half of 1999 was marked by non-violent [national protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Surinamese_protests) against poor general economic and social conditions. By mid-year, the Netherlands tried Bouterse *[in absentia](582826.md)* on drug-smuggling charges. He was convicted and sentenced to prison but remained in Suriname.[^cite_note-54]

### 21st century

In 2002, the inner historic city in Suriname's capital, [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo), was inscribed on the list as a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md), for its outstanding collection of Dutch colonial architecture.

On 19 July 2010, Bouterse returned to power when he was elected as the president of Suriname.[^cite_note-55] Before his election in 2010, he, along with 24 others, had been charged with the murders of 15 prominent dissidents in the [December murders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_murders). However, in 2012, two months before the verdict in the trial, the National Assembly extended its [amnesty](18940624.md) law and provided Bouterse and the others with amnesty of these charges. He was reelected on 14 July 2015.[^cite_note-56] However, Bouterse was convicted by a Surinamese court on 29 November 2019 and given a 20-year sentence for his role in the 1982 killings.[^cite_note-57]



Protesters stormed the [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_\(Suriname\)) building in February 2023

After winning the [2020 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Surinamese_general_election),[^cite_note-58] [Chan Santokhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Santokhi) was the sole nomination for [president of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Suriname).[^cite_note-59] On 13 July, Santokhi was elected president by acclamation in an [uncontested election](5051448.md).[^cite_note-60] He was inaugurated on 16 July in a ceremony without public attendance due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Suriname).[^cite_note-61]

In February 2023, there were heavy protests against rising living costs in the capital Paramaribo. Protesters accused the government of President Chan Santokhi of corruption. They stormed the National Assembly, demanding the government to resign. However, the government condemned the protests.[^cite_note-62] In December 2024, Desi Bouterse, Suriname's fugitive former president, died.[^cite_note-63]

On 6 July 2025, [Jennifer Geerlings-Simons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Geerlings-Simons) of the [National Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Party_\(Suriname\)) (NDP) was elected as Suriname's first woman president by the parliament.[^cite_note-64]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Suriname)



[Brokopondo Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokopondo_Reservoir) surrounded by tropical rainforest



The [Coppename river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coppename_River), one of many rivers in the interior



Geology of Suriname

Suriname is the smallest sovereign country in South America. Situated on the [Guiana Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiana_Shield), it lies mostly between latitudes [1°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_parallel_north) and [6°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_parallel_north), and longitudes [54°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54th_meridian_west) and [58°W](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/58th_meridian_west). The country can be divided into two main geographic regions. The northern, lowland coastal area (roughly above the line Albina-Paranam-Wageningen) has been cultivated, and most of the population lives here. The southern part consists of tropical [rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest) and sparsely inhabited [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna) along the border with Brazil, covering about 80% of Suriname's land surface.

The two main mountain ranges are the [Bakhuys Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakhuys_Mountains) and the [Van Asch Van Wijck Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Asch_Van_Wijck_Mountains). [Julianatop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julianatop) is the highest mountain in the country at 1,286 metres (4,219 ft) above sea level. Other mountains include [Tafelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafelberg,_Suriname) at 1,026 metres (3,366 ft), Mount [Kasikasima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasikasima) at 718 metres (2,356 ft), Goliathberg at 358 metres (1,175 ft) and [Voltzberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltzberg) at 240 metres (790 ft).

Suriname contains six terrestrial ecoregions: [Guayanan Highlands moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guayanan_Highlands_moist_forests), [Guianan moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guianan_moist_forests), [Paramaribo swamp forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo_swamp_forests), [Tepuis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepuis), [Guianan savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guianan_savanna), and [Guianan mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guianan_mangroves).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-65] Its [forest cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover) is 90.2%, the highest of any nation in the world. The country had a 2019 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 9.39/10, ranking it fifth globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-FLII-Supplementary-66]

### Borders

Main article: [Borders of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_of_Suriname)



Map of Suriname with disputed territories in light green.

Suriname is situated between [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) to the east and [Guyana](17238691.md) to the west. The southern border is shared with [Brazil](3383.md) and the northern border is the [Atlantic](698.md) coast. The southernmost borders with French Guiana and Guyana are disputed by Suriname along the [Marowijne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maroni_River) and [Corantijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courantyne_River) rivers, respectively, while a part of the disputed [maritime boundary](29613061.md) with Guyana was arbitrated by the [Permanent Court of Arbitration](38058.md) convened under the rules set out in [Annex VII](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/United%20Nations%20Convention%20on%20the%20Law%20of%20the%20Sea/Annex%20VII) of the [United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea](31940.md) on 20 September 2007.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

### Climate

Lying [two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_parallel_north) to [five degrees north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_parallel_north) of the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator), Suriname has a very hot and wet [tropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_climate), and temperatures do not vary much throughout the year. Average relative humidity is between 80% and 90%. Its average temperature ranges from 29 to 34 °C (84 to 93 °F). Due to the high humidity, actual temperatures are distorted and may therefore feel up to 6 °C (11 °F) hotter than the recorded temperature.

The year has two [wet seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season), from April to August and from November to February. It also has two [dry seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season), from August to November and February to April.



Seasons in Suriname

[Climate change in Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Suriname) is leading to warmer temperatures and more [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather) events. As a relatively poor country, its contributions to global [climate change](5042951.md) have been limited. Because of the large forest cover, the country has been running a [carbon negative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide_removal) economy since 2014.[^cite_note-\:3-69]

### Biodiversity and conservation

Main article: [Biodiversity in Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_in_Suriname)



Share of forest area in total land area, top countries (2021). Suriname has the highest percentage of forest cover in the world.



[Leatherback sea turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leatherback_sea_turtle) on the beach near the village of Galibi



The [blue poison dart frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_poison_dart_frog) is endemic to Suriname.

Due to the variety of habitats and temperatures, biodiversity in Suriname is considered high.[^cite_note-70] In October 2013, 16 international scientists researching the ecosystems during a three-week expedition in Suriname's [Upper Palumeu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Palumeu_River?action=edit\&redlink=1) Watershed catalogued 1,378 species and found 60—including six frogs, one snake, and 11 fish—that may be previously unknown species.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] According to the environmental non-profit [Conservation International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International), which funded the expedition, Suriname's ample supply of fresh water is vital to the biodiversity and healthy ecosystems of the region.[^cite_note-75]

Snakewood (*[Brosimum guianense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brosimum_guianense)*), a tree, is native to this tropical region of the Americas. Customs in Suriname report that snakewood is often illegally exported to French Guiana, thought to be for the crafts industry.[^cite_note-76]

On 21 March 2013, Suriname's REDD+ Readiness Preparation Proposal (R-PP 2013) was approved by the member countries of the Participants Committee of the [Forest Carbon Partnership Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Carbon_Partnership_Facility) (FCPF).[^cite_note-77]

As in other parts of Central and South America, indigenous communities have increased their activism to protect their lands and preserve habitat. In March 2015, the "[Trio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trio_people) and [Wayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayana_people) communities presented a declaration of cooperation to the [National Assembly of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Suriname) that announces an indigenous conservation corridor spanning 72,000 square kilometers (27,799 square miles) of southern Suriname. The declaration, led by these indigenous communities and with the support of [Conservation International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International) (CI) and [World Wildlife Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wildlife_Fund) [(WWF) Guianas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWF_Guianas), comprises almost half of the total area of Suriname.[^cite_note-guardians-78] This area includes large forests and is considered "essential for the country's [climate resilience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_resilience), freshwater security, and green development strategy."

The [Central Suriname Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Suriname_Nature_Reserve) has been designated a UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md) for its unspoiled forests and [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity). There are many national parks in the country, including Galibi National Reserve along the coast; [Brownsberg Nature Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownsberg_Nature_Park) and [Eilerts de Haan Nature Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eilerts_de_Haan_Nature_Park) in central Suriname; and the [Sipaliwani Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sipaliwani_Nature_Reserve) on the Brazilian border. In all, 16% of the country's land area is national parks and lakes, according to the UNEP World Conservation Monitoring Centre.[^cite_note-79]

Suriname's extensive tree cover is vital to the country's efforts to [mitigate climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Suriname) and maintain [carbon negativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_negativity).[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

However, extensive and apparently unchecked illegal gold mining with mercury continues to cause large-scale environmental degradation, especially along the Lawa and Maroni rivers, which form the border with French Guiana.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

[Conservation International Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International_Suriname) (CI-Suriname), the Suriname country programme of [Conservation International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_International),is based in [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo).[^cite_note-CISurinameAbout-84] Work linked to CI-Suriname includes support for protected-area planning and management in and around the [Central Suriname Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Suriname_Nature_Reserve) and nature-based coastal protection and mangrove rehabilitation at [Weg naar Zee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weg_naar_Zee) near Paramaribo.[^cite_note-CSNR-COA-2020-85][^cite_note-Wetlands2016-BWN-86]

## Government and politics

Main article: [Politics of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Suriname)



[Jennifer Geerlings-Simons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Geerlings-Simons)\

[Gregory Rusland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Rusland)\
[Vice President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_President_of_Suriname)

Court of Justice

The Republic of Suriname is a [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) republic with an installed assembly-independent system in place,[^cite_note-cnet-8] based on the [Constitution of 1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Suriname). The legislative branch of government consists of a 51-member [unicameral](204304.md) [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Suriname), simultaneously and popularly elected for a five-year term.

In the elections held on Tuesday 25 May 2010, the *Megacombinatie* won 23 of the National Assembly seats, followed by *Nationale Front* with 20 seats. A much smaller number, important for coalition-building, went to the "A-combinatie" and to the *Volksalliantie.* The parties held negotiations to form coalitions. Elections were held on 25 May 2015, and the National Assembly again elected Dési Bouterse as president.[^cite_note-87]

The [president of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Suriname) is elected for a five-year term by a two-thirds majority of the National Assembly. If at least two-thirds of the National Assembly cannot agree to vote for one presidential candidate, a People's Assembly is formed from all National Assembly delegates and regional and municipal representatives who were elected by popular vote in the most recent national election. The president may be elected by a majority of the People's Assembly called for the special election.



[Presidential Palace of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace_of_Suriname)

As head of government, the president appoints a sixteen-minister cabinet. A vice president is normally elected for a five-year term at the same time as the president, by a simple majority in the National Assembly or People's Assembly. There is no constitutional provision for removal or replacement of the president, except in the case of resignation.

The judiciary is headed by the [High Court of Justice of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justice_of_Suriname) (Supreme Court). This court supervises the magistrate courts. Members are appointed for life by the president in consultation with the National Assembly, the State Advisory Council, and the National Order of Private Attorneys.

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Suriname)



President [Chan Santokhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Santokhi) (right) with Dutch politician [Wopke Hoekstra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wopke_Hoekstra), visiting to celebrate [Ketikoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketikoti) 2023 in [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo)



Diplomatic relations of Suriname

Due to Suriname's Dutch colonial history, Suriname had a long-standing special relationship with the Netherlands.

In 1999, Dési Bouterse was convicted and sentenced *in absentia* in the Netherlands to 11 years of imprisonment for drug trafficking. He was the main suspect in the court case concerning the December murders, the 1982 assassination of opponents of military rule in [Fort Zeelandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Zeelandia_\(Paramaribo\)), Paramaribo. He served as president between 2010 and 2020. These two cases still strain relations between the Netherlands and Suriname.[^cite_note-minbuza-88]

The Dutch government stated during that time that it would maintain limited contact with the president.[^cite_note-minbuza-88]

Bouterse was elected as president of Suriname in 2010. The Netherlands in July 2014 dropped Suriname as a member of its development program.[^cite_note-89]

Since 1991, the United States has maintained positive relations with Suriname. The two countries work together through the [Caribbean Basin Security Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Basin_Security_Initiative?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CBSI) and the U.S. President's Emergency Plan for AIDS Relief (PEPFAR). Suriname also receives military funding from the U.S. Department of Defense.[^cite_note-90]

Suriname has been a member of [The Forum of Small States (FOSS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forum_of_Small_States_\(FOSS\)) since the group's founding in 1992.[^cite_note-singaporebook-91]

[European Union](9317.md) relations and cooperation with Suriname are carried out both on a bilateral and a regional basis. There are ongoing EU-[Community of Latin American and Caribbean States](26313243.md) (CELAC) and EU-[CARIFORUM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARIFORUM) dialogues. Suriname is party to the [Cotonou Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotonou_Agreement), the partnership agreement among the members of the African, Caribbean and Pacific Group of States and the European Union.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-Suriname_country_profile-12]

On 17 February 2005, the leaders of [Barbados](3455.md) and Suriname signed the "Agreement for the deepening of bilateral cooperation between the Government of Barbados and the Government of the Republic of Suriname."[^cite_note-93] On 23–24 April 2009, both nations formed a Joint Commission in [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo), Suriname, to improve relations and to expand into various areas of cooperation.[^cite_note-94] They held a second meeting toward this goal on 3–4 March 2011, in Dover, Barbados. Their representatives reviewed issues of agriculture, trade, investment, as well as international transport.[^cite_note-95]

In the late 2000s, Suriname intensified development cooperation with other developing countries. China's South-South cooperation with Suriname has included a number of large-scale infrastructure projects, including port rehabilitation and road construction. Brazil signed agreements to cooperate with Suriname in education, health, agriculture, and energy production.[^cite_note-96]

### Military

Main article: [Military of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Suriname)

The Armed Forces of Suriname have three branches: the Army, the Air Force, and the Navy. The president of the Republic is the Supreme Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces (*Opperbevelhebber van de Strijdkrachten*). The president is assisted by the minister of defence. Beneath the president and minister of defence is the commander of the armed forces (*Bevelhebber van de Strijdkrachten*). The military branches and regional military commands report to the commander.

After the creation of the [Statute of the Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_the_Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands), the [Royal Netherlands Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Army) was entrusted with the defence of Suriname, while the defence of the [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles) was the responsibility of the [Royal Netherlands Navy](198478.md). The army set up a separate *Troepenmacht in Suriname* (Forces in Suriname, TRIS). Upon independence in 1975, this force was turned into the *Surinaamse Krijgsmacht* (SKM):, Surinamese Armed Forces. After the 1980 overthrow of the government, the SKM was rebranded as the *Nationaal Leger* (NL), National Army.

In 1965, the Dutch and Americans used Suriname's Coronie site for multiple Nike Apache [sounding rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sounding_rocket) launches.[^cite_note-97]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Suriname)



[Rosebel gold mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosebel_gold_mine)



A rice field in [Nickerie District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickerie_District)



[Suralco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suralco?action=edit\&redlink=1) bauxite factory in [Paranam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranam)

Suriname's democracy gained some strength after the turbulent 1990s, and its economy became more diversified and less dependent on Dutch financial assistance. [Bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite) (aluminium ore) mining used to be a strong revenue source, since before the independence of the country up to 2015. Because [Alcoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoa) stopped all bauxite operations, the bauxite era in Suriname also ended.

The discovery, exploration and exploitation of oil and gold in modern times contributes substantially to Suriname's economic independence. Agriculture, especially rice and bananas, remains a strong component of the economy, and [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) is providing new economic opportunities. More than 93% of Suriname's landmass consists of unspoiled rainforest.[^cite_note-99] However Suriname reported a net increase in forest area of 5 370 ha per year in 1990–2000 but a net loss of forest area of 16 400 ha per year in 2015–2025.[^cite_note-100]

With the establishment of the [Central Suriname Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Suriname_Nature_Reserve) in 1998, Suriname signaled its commitment to the conservation of this precious resource. The Central Suriname Nature Reserve became a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 2000.



Ministry of Finance

The economy of Suriname was dominated by the bauxite industry, which accounted for more than 15% of GDP and 70% of export earnings up to 2015. Currently gold exports make up 60–80% of all exports earnings. In 2021 the gold industry accounted for 8.5% of the GDP. The share of large-scale mining in total gold production is 58% compared to 42% of small-scale mining.[^cite_note-101] With an export value of US\$1.83 billion in 2023, the gold sector makes an important contribution to the economy.[^cite_note-102] The gold production of Suriname in 2015 is 30 tonnes.[^cite_note-103]

The exploration and exploitation of oil adds substantially to the economy of Suriname at about 10% of the GDP.[^cite_note-104] The national oil company, [STAATSOLIE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatsolie_Maatschappij_Suriname), is the motor behind Suriname's oil industry. Their core business is oil extraction and refining. In 2022 they made a revenue of US\$840 million. In that year their contribution to the state treasury was US\$320 million.[^cite_note-105] In 2023 they made a revenue of US\$722 million. The drop in revenue was because of the lower price for oil per barrel that year. Their contribution to the Surinamese state treasury was US\$335 million.[^cite_note-106]

Other main export products include rice, bananas, and [shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp). Suriname has recently started exploiting some of its sizeable [oil](23195.md)[^cite_note-107] and gold[^cite_note-108] reserves. About a quarter of the people work in the agricultural sector. The Surinamese economy is very dependent on commerce, its main trade partners being Switzerland, China, the Netherlands, the United States, Canada, and [Caribbean](18956035.md) countries, mainly [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) and the islands of the former [Netherlands Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Antilles).[^cite_note-109]



[Central Bank of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Suriname) building in Paramaribo

After assuming power in the fall of 1996, the [Wijdenbosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Wijdenbosch) government ended the [structural adjustment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_adjustment) program of the previous government, claiming it was unfair to the poorer elements of society. Tax revenues fell as old taxes lapsed and the government failed to implement new tax alternatives. By the end of 1997, the allocation of new Dutch development funds was frozen as Surinamese Government relations with the Netherlands deteriorated. Economic growth slowed in 1998, with decline in the mining, construction, and [utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility) sectors. Rampant government expenditures, poor tax collection, a bloated civil service, and reduced foreign aid in 1999 contributed to the fiscal deficit, estimated at 11% of GDP. The government sought to cover this deficit through monetary expansion, which led to a dramatic increase in inflation. It takes longer on average to register a new business in Suriname than virtually any other country in the world (694 days or about 99 weeks).[^cite_note-110]

### Tourism



[Central Suriname Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Suriname_Nature_Reserve) seen from the Voltzberg

Most tourists visit Suriname for the [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity) of the [Amazonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Basin) rain forests in the south of the country, which are noted for their flora and fauna. The [Central Suriname Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Suriname_Nature_Reserve) is the biggest and one of the most popular reserves, along with the [Brownsberg Nature Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brownsberg_Nature_Park) which overlooks the [Brokopondo Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokopondo_Reservoir), one of the largest human-made lakes in the world. In 2008, the [Berg en Dal Eco & Cultural Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berg_en_Dal_\(Suriname\)) opened in Brokopondo.[^cite_note-111] [Tonka Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonka_Island?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the reservoir is home to a rustic [eco-tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eco-tourism) project run by the Saramaccaner Maroons.[^cite_note-112] [Pangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangi_\(Maroon\)) wraps and bowls made of calabashes are the two main products manufactured for tourists. The Maroons have learned that colorful and ornate pangis are popular with tourists.[^cite_note-113] [\[1\]](https://whatnaturalslove.com/en-int/blogs/life-in-suriname/different-ways-to-wear-a-pangi-a-handmade-wrap-from-the-maroons-from-suriname?srsltid=AfmBOoplixGP8po2BPqyEgrR0Rt_f7p7vdLx1ICM1pKAjrpo0HqYj31b\&scrlybrkr=0606a35b) Other popular decorative souvenirs are hand-carved purple-hardwood made into bowls, plates, canes, wooden boxes, and wall decors.



The remains of the synagogue on the [Jodensavanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodensavanne)

There are also many waterfalls throughout the country. Raleighvallen, or [Raleigh Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raleighfalls?action=edit\&redlink=1), is a 56,000-hectare (140,000-acre) nature reserve on the [Coppename River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coppename_River), rich in bird life. Also are the [Blanche Marie Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blanche_Marie_Falls) on the [Nickerie River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickerie_River) and the [Wonotobo Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonotobo_Falls). [Tafelberg Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafelberg,_Suriname) in the centre of the country is surrounded by its own reserve – the [Tafelberg Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafelberg_Nature_Reserve) – around the source of the [Saramacca River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saramacca_River), as is the Voltzberg Nature Reserve further north on the Coppename River at Raleighvallen. In the interior are many [Maroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maroon_\(people\)) and [Amerindian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerindian) villages, many of which have their own reserves that are generally open to visitors.

Suriname is one of the few countries in the world where at least one of each [biome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome) that the state possesses has been declared a wildlife reserve. Around 30% of the total land area of Suriname is protected by law as reserves.



[Fort Zeelandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Zeelandia_\(Paramaribo\)) in Paramaribo

Other attractions include plantations such as [Laarwijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laarwijk), which is situated along the Suriname River. This plantation can be reached only by boat via Domburg, in the north central [Wanica District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanica_District) of Suriname.

Crime rates continue to rise in Paramaribo and armed robberies are not uncommon. According to the current U.S. Department of State Travel Advisory at the date of the 2025 report's publication, Suriname has been assessed as Level 1: exercise normal precautions.[^cite_note-114]

#### Landmarks



The [Cathedral of St. Peter and Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter_and_Paul_Cathedral,_Paramaribo) in Paramaribo

The [Jules Wijdenbosch Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Wijdenbosch_Bridge) is a bridge over the river Suriname between Paramaribo and Meerzorg in the [Commewijne district](682745.md). The bridge was built during the tenure of President [Jules Albert Wijdenbosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Albert_Wijdenbosch) (1996–2000) and was completed in 2000. The bridge is 52 metres (171 ft) high, and 1,504 metres (4,934 ft) long. It connects Paramaribo with Commewijne, a connection which previously could only be made by ferry. The purpose of the bridge was to facilitate and promote the development of the eastern part of Suriname. The bridge consists of two lanes (one lane each way).

The construction of the [Sts. Peter and Paul Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter_and_Paul_Cathedral,_Paramaribo) started on 13 January 1883. Before it became a cathedral it was a theatre. The theatre was built in 1809 and burned down in 1820.

Suriname is one of the few countries in the world where [a synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neveh_Shalom_Synagogue) is located next to [a mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosque_Keizerstraat).[^cite_note-115] The two buildings are located next to each other in the centre of Paramaribo and have been known to share a parking facility during their respective religious rites, should they happen to coincide with one another.

A relatively new landmark is the Hindu [Arya Diwaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arya_Diwaker) temple in the Johan Adolf Pengelstraat in [Wanica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanica), Paramaribo, which was inaugurated in 2001. A special characteristic of the temple is that it does not have images of the Hindu divinities, as they are forbidden in the [Arya Samaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arya_Samaj), the Hindu movement to which the people who built the temple belong. Instead, the building is covered by many texts derived from the Vedas and other Hindu scriptures. The architecture makes the temple a tourist attraction.

[Jules Wijdenbosch Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Wijdenbosch_Bridge) over the [Suriname River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriname_River)

Suriname, along with neighbouring [Guyana](17238691.md), is one of only two countries on the mainland South American continent that drive on the left, although many vehicles are left-hand-drive as well as right-hand-drive.[^cite_note-116] One explanation for this practice is that at the time of its colonisation of Suriname, the Netherlands itself used left-hand traffic, also introducing the practice in the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md), now [Indonesia](14579.md).[^cite_note-New_Scientist-117] Another is that Suriname was first colonised by the British, and for practical reasons, this was not changed when it came under Dutch administration.[^cite_note-Kincaid_Rule-118] Although the Netherlands converted to driving to the right at the end of the 18th century,[^cite_note-New_Scientist-117] Suriname did not. As of 2003, Suriname had 4303 km (2674 miles) of roads, of which 1119 km (695 miles) are paved.[^cite_note-119]

## Demographics

Main articles: [Demographics of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Suriname) and [Surinamese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinamese_people)



The population of Suriname from 1961 to 2003, in units of 1000. The slowdown and decline in population growth \~1969–1985 reflects a mass migration to the Netherlands and French Guiana.

In 2022, Suriname had a population of roughly 624,900 according to estimates by the [United Nations](31769.md).[^cite_note-122] This compares to 541,638 inhabitants from the 2012 census.[^cite_note-statistics-suriname1-5] The Surinamese populace is characterized by high levels of diversity, wherein no particular demographic group constitutes a majority. This is a legacy of centuries of Dutch rule, which entailed successive periods of forced, contracted, or voluntary migration by various nationalities and ethnic groups from around the world.

### Largest cities

Most municipalities are located within the capital's metropolitan area, or along the densely populated coastline; about 90% of the population lives in [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo) or on the coast.[^cite_note-123]

The largest ethnic group are [Asian Surinamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Surinamese) (about 43%), with the largest subgroup being [Indo-Surinamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Surinamese), locally referred to as *Hindustanis*, who form over a quarter of the population (27.4%).[^cite_note-rutgers-1][^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127][^cite_note-statistics-suriname12-128][^cite_note-129] The vast majority are descendants of 19th-century [indentured workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_indenture_system) from [Northern India](1552939.md) and Southern [Nepal](171166.md), hailing mostly from the areas of modern [Bihar](442482.md), [Jharkhand](197225.md) and [Eastern Uttar Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Uttar_Pradesh), and also the [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal) region that consists of modern [West Bengal](34040.md) and [Bangladesh](3454.md). If counted as one ethnic group, the [Afro-Surinamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Surinamese) are the second largest community, at around 37.4%; however, they are usually divided into two cultural/ethnic groups: the [Creoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_peoples) and the Maroons. Surinamese Maroons, whose ancestors are mostly runaway slaves that fled to the interior, comprise 21.7% of the population. They are divided into six tribes: [Ndyuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndyuka_people) (Aucans), [Saramaccans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saramaka), [Paramaccans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaccan), [Kwinti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwinti), [Aluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluku) (Boni) and [Matawai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matawai_people). Surinamese [Creoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_peoples), mixed people descending from African slaves and Europeans (mostly [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) and/or [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Suriname#Identity)), form 15.7% of the population. [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_Surinamese) make up 14% of the population, and like the East Indians, descend largely from workers contracted from the island of [Java](69336.md) in the former [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) (modern [Indonesia](14579.md)).[^cite_note-javanese_in_suriname-130] 13.4% of the population identifies as being of mixed ethnic heritage. [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Surinamese), originating from 19th-century indentured workers and some recent migration, make up 7.3% of the population.

Other groups include [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people), primarily [Maronites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronites), and [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Suriname) of [Sephardic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi_Jews) and [Ashkenazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews) origin, whose centre of population was [Jodensavanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodensavanne). Various [indigenous peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) make up 3.7% of the population, with the main groups being the [Akurio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akurio_people), [Lokono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokono), [Kalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalina_people) (Caribs), [Tiriyó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiriy%C3%B3_people) and [Wayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayana_people). They live mainly in the districts of [Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaribo), [Wanica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanica_District), [Para](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para_District), [Marowijne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marowijne_District) and [Sipaliwini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sipaliwini_District). A small but influential number of [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Surinamese) remain in the country, comprising about 1% of the population. They are descended mostly from [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) 19th-century immigrant farmers, known as "[Boeroes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Surinamese)" (derived from *boer*, the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) word for "farmer"), and to a lesser degree other European groups, such as [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people). Many Boeroes [left after independence in 1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_flight).

More recently Suriname has seen a new wave of immigrants, namely [Brazilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilians_in_Suriname), [Haitians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haitians) and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Surinamese) (many of them laborers mining for gold). Most do not have legal status.[^cite_note-131]

### Emigration



Immigrants from India

The option to choose between Surinamese or Dutch citizenship in the years leading up to Suriname's independence in 1975 led to a mass migration to the Netherlands. This migration continued in the period immediately after independence and during military rule in the 1980s and for largely economic reasons extended throughout the 1990s. The [Surinamese community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinamese_people_in_the_Netherlands) in the Netherlands numbered 350,300 as of 2013 (including children and grandchildren of Suriname migrants born in the Netherlands), compared to approximately 566,000 Surinamese in Suriname itself.[^cite_note-cia-24]

According to the [International Organization for Migration](36893.md), around 272,600 people from Suriname lived in other countries in the late 2010s, in particular in the Netherlands (c. 192,000), France (c. 25,000, most of them in [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana)),[^cite_note-132] the United States (c. 15,000), [Guyana](17238691.md) (c. 5,000), [Aruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aruba) (c. 1,500), and Canada (c. 1,000).[^cite_note-133]

### Languages

"Surinamese English" redirects here. For the [English-based creole language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-based_creole_language), see [Sranan Tongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sranan_Tongo).



Butcher in the [Central Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Market_\(Paramaribo\)) in Paramaribo with signs written in [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language)

Suriname has roughly 14 local languages, but [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) (*Nederlands*) is the sole official language and is the language used in education, government, business, and the media.[^cite_note-cia-24] Over 60% of the population are [native speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_language) of Dutch[^cite_note-taalgebied-134] and around 20%–30% speak it as a second language. In 2004, Suriname became an associate member of the [Dutch Language Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Language_Union).[^cite_note-135]

Suriname is one of three Dutch-speaking sovereign countries in the world (the others being the [Netherlands](21148.md) and [Belgium](3343.md)). It is also the only area in the Americas where Dutch is spoken by a majority of the population (as territories in the [Dutch Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Caribbean) all have other majority languages). Finally, Suriname and English-speaking [Guyana](17238691.md) are the only countries in [South America](26769.md) along with the English-speaking British dependent territory of the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md) where a [Romance language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_language) does not predominate.



A [Sranan Tongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sranan_Tongo)-language poetry sign located in [Rotterdam](26049.md), Netherlands

In Paramaribo, Dutch is the main home language in two-thirds of the households.[^cite_note-Census-4] The recognition of *"Surinaams-Nederlands"* ("[Surinamese Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinamese_Dutch)") as a national dialect equal to *"Nederlands-Nederlands"* ("Dutch Dutch") and *"Vlaams-Nederlands"* ("[Flemish Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flemish_Dutch)") was expressed in 2009 by the publication of the *Woordenboek Surinaams Nederlands* (*Surinamese–Dutch Dictionary*).[^cite_note-136] It is the most commonly spoken language in urban areas. The local languages are only more predominant than Dutch in the interior of Suriname (namely parts of [Sipaliwini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sipaliwini_District) and [Brokopondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brokopondo_District)).

[Sranan Tongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sranan_Tongo), a local English-based [creole language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_language), is the most widely used [vernacular language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernacular_language) in daily life and business among the Surinamese. Together with Dutch, it is considered to be one of the two principal languages of Surinamese [diglossia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diglossia). Both are further influenced by other spoken languages which are spoken primarily within ethnic communities. Sranan Tongo is often used interchangeably with Dutch depending on the formality of the setting; Dutch is seen as a [prestige dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prestige_dialect) and Sranan Tongo the common [vernacular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernacular).[^cite_note-NYT2008-137]

[Sarnami Hindustani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarn%C3%A1mi_Hindust%C3%A1ni), an [Indo-Aryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_language) [koiné language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koin%C3%A9_language) and the Surinamese [variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(linguistics\)) of [Caribbean Hindustani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Hindustani), is the third-most used language. It is primarily spoken by the [descendants of Indian indentured labourers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Caribbeans) from the former [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj).

The six [Maroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maroon_\(people\)) languages of Suriname are also considered English-based creole languages, and include [Saramaccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saramaccan_language), [Aukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ndyuka_\(language\)), [Aluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluku), [Paramaccan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paramaccan_people), [Matawai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matawai_language) and [Kwinti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwinti_language). Aluku, Paramaccan, and Kwinti are so mutually intelligible with Aukan that they can be considered dialects of the Aukan language. The same can be said about Matawai, which is mutually intelligible with Saramaccan.

[Surinamese-Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language#Surinamese-Javanese) is used by the residents of Suriname who are descendants of the [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people) indentured laborers once sent from the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) (now [Indonesia](14579.md)).

[Amerindian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) languages include [Akurio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akurio_language), [Lokono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokono_language), [Carib-Kari'nja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carib_language), [Sikiana-Kashuyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikiana_language), [Tiro-Tiriyó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiriy%C3%B3_language), [Waiwai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waiwai_language), [Warao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warao_language), and [Wayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayana_language).

[Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_language) is spoken by the descendants of the Chinese indentured labourers. [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese) and [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Mandarin) are spoken by the recent wave of Chinese immigrants.

[English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), [Guyanese English Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guyanese_Creole), [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language), [Spanish](26825.md), [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language), and [French Guianese Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guianese_Creole) are spoken at areas near the country's borders where there are many migrants from neighbouring countries speaking their respective languages. English is spoken by the majority of the population.

Suriname's religious makeup is heterogeneous and reflective of the country's multicultural character.[^cite_note-statistics-suriname12-128]

According to the 2012 census, 48.4% of Surinamese were Christians; 26.7% were [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants) (11.18% [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostal), 11.16% [Moravian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Church), 0.7% [Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism) (including [Remonstrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remonstrants)), and 3.7% other Protestant denominations), while 21.6% were [Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholics).[^cite_note-CENSUS2012-7] Hindus are the second largest religious group in Suriname, comprising one-fifth of the population, the third largest proportion of any country in the Western Hemisphere, after [Guyana](17238691.md) and [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md), both of which also have large proportions of [Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Caribbeans). Likewise, almost all practitioners of Hinduism are found among the Indo-Surinamese population. Suriname has the highest proportion of Muslims (13.9%) in the Americas. They are largely of Javanese or Indian descent.[^cite_note-CENSUS2012-7] Other religions are [Winti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winti), an [Afro-American religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-American_religion) practiced mostly by those of Maroon ancestry, [Javanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kejaw%C3%A8n) (0.8%),[^cite_note-CENSUS2012-7] a syncretic faith found among some Javanese Surinamese, and various indigenous folk traditions that are often incorporated into one of the larger religions (usually Christianity).

### Health

Main article: [Health in Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Suriname)



[Academic Hospital Paramaribo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academic_Hospital_Paramaribo) is the largest hospital in Suriname with 465 beds

The [Global Burden of Disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Burden_of_Disease) Study provides an on-line data source for analyzing updated estimates of health for 359 diseases and injuries and 84 risk factors from 1990 to 2017 in most of the world's countries.[^cite_note-138] Comparing Suriname with other Caribbean nations show that in 2017 the age-standardized [death rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_rate) for all causes was 793 (males 969, females 641) per 100,000, far below the 1219 of [Haiti](13373.md), somewhat below the 944 of [Guyana](17238691.md) but considerably above the 424 of [Bermuda](3460.md). In 1990, the death rate was 960 per 100,000. [Life expectancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) in 2017 was 72 years (males 69, females 75). The death rate for children < 5 years was 581 per 100,000 compared to 1308 in Haiti and 102 in Bermuda. In 1990 and 2017, the leading causes of age-standardized death rates were cardiovascular disease, cancer, and diabetes/chronic kidney disease.

### Education

Main article: [Education in Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Suriname)



[Anton de Kom University of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_de_Kom_University_of_Suriname)

Education in Suriname is compulsory until the age of 12,[^cite_note-139] and the nation had a net primary enrollment rate of 94% in 2004.[^cite_note-hdrstats.undp.org-140] [Literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) is very common, particularly among men.[^cite_note-hdrstats.undp.org-140] The main university in the country is the [Anton de Kom University of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_de_Kom_University_of_Suriname).

From elementary school to high school there are 13 grades. The elementary school has six grades, middle school four grades, and high school three grades. Students take a test at the end of elementary school to determine whether they will go to the MULO (secondary modern school) or a middle school of lower standards like LBO.

### Media

Traditionally, *[De Ware Tijd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Ware_Tijd)* was the major newspaper of the country, but since the '90s *[Times of Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_of_Suriname), [De West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_West)* and *[Dagblad Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagblad_Suriname)* have also been well-read newspapers. All publish primarily in Dutch.[^cite_note-142]

Suriname has twenty-four radio stations, most of them also broadcast through the Internet. There are twelve television sources: [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampie's_Broadcasting_Corporation) (Ch. 4–1, 2), RBN (Ch. 5–1, 2), Rasonic TV (Ch. 7), [STVS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STVS) (Ch. 8–1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6), [Apintie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apintie) (Ch. 10–1), [ATV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATV_\(Suriname\)) (Ch. 12–1, 2, 3, 4), Radika (Ch. 14), SCCN (Ch. 17–1, 2, 3), Pipel TV (Ch. 18–1, 2), Trishul (Ch. 20–1, 2, 3, 4), Garuda (Ch. 23–1, 2, 3), Sangeetmala (Ch. 26), Ch. 30, Ch. 31, Ch.32, Ch.38, SCTV (Ch. 45). Also listened to is [mArt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mart_\(broadcaster\)), a broadcaster from Amsterdam founded by people from Suriname. [Kondreman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kondreman?action=edit\&redlink=1) is one of the popular cartoons in Suriname.

There are also three major news sites: Starnieuws, Suriname Herald, and GFC Nieuws.

In 2022, Suriname ranked 52nd in the worldwide [Press Freedom Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Freedom_Index) by the organization [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md),[^cite_note-143] a strong drop in the ranking compared to the 2018–2021 period (about location 20).

### Sports



André Kamperveen Stadium



Franklin Essed Stadium

The most popular sport in Suriname is [football](10568.md), followed by [basketball](3921.md) and [volleyball](32558.md). The [Suriname Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriname_Olympic_Committee) is the national governing body for sports in Suriname. The major [mind sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mind_sport) are [chess](5134.md), [draughts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draughts), [bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_\(card_game\)) and [troefcall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troefcall).

Many Suriname-born football players and Dutch-born football players of Surinamese descent have turned out to play for the [Dutch national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), including [Gerald Vanenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Vanenburg), [Ruud Gullit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruud_Gullit), [Frank Rijkaard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Rijkaard), [Edgar Davids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Davids), [Clarence Seedorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarence_Seedorf), [Patrick Kluivert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Kluivert), [Aron Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aron_Winter), [Ryan Babel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Babel), [Georginio Wijnaldum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georginio_Wijnaldum), [Virgil van Dijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil_van_Dijk), and [Jimmy Floyd Hasselbaink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Floyd_Hasselbaink), [Donyell Malen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donyell_Malen), [Xavi Simons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavi_Simons), [Ryan Gravenberch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Gravenberch), and [Denzel Dumfries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denzel_Dumfries). In 1999, [Humphrey Mijnals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey_Mijnals), who played for both [Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriname_national_football_team) and the Netherlands, was elected Surinamese footballer of the century.[^cite_note-144] Another famous player is [André Kamperveen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Kamperveen), who captained Suriname in the 1940s and was the first Surinamese to play professionally in the Netherlands.

In 2021 [Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriname_national_football_team) participated in their first [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) where they played against Costa Rica, Jamaica and Guadeloupe in Group C. Suriname lost its first two matches against Jamaica and Costa Rica, but ended third in the group following a 2–1 win against Guadeloupe.

Swimmer [Anthony Nesty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Nesty) is the only [Olympic](22576.md) medalist for Suriname. He won gold in the 100-meter butterfly at the [1988 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Summer_Olympics) in [Seoul](19159283.md) and he won bronze in the same discipline at the 1992 Summer Olympics in [Barcelona](4443.md). Originally from [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md), he now lives in [Gainesville, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gainesville,_Florida), and is the head coach of the University of Florida swim team.



Purcy R. Olivieira Sports Centre

The most famous international track & field athlete from Suriname is [Letitia Vriesde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letitia_Vriesde), who won a silver medal at the [1995 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_World_Championships_in_Athletics) behind Ana Quirot in the 800 metres, the first medal won by a South American female athlete in World Championship competition. In addition, she also won a bronze medal at the [2001 World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_World_Championships_in_Athletics) and won several medals in the 800 and 1500 metres at the [Pan-American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-American_Games) and [Central American and Caribbean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_and_Caribbean_Games). [Tommy Asinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Asinga) also received acclaim for winning a bronze medal in the 800 metres at the [1991 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Pan_American_Games).

[Cricket](25675557.md) is popular in Suriname to some extent, influenced by its popularity [in the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_in_the_Netherlands) and in neighbouring Guyana. The [Surinaamse Cricket Bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinaamse_Cricket_Bond) is an [associate member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_International_Cricket_Council_members) of the [International Cricket Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Cricket_Council) (ICC). Suriname and Argentina were the only ICC associate members in South America when ICC had a three tiered membership, although Guyana is represented on the [West Indies Cricket Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies_Cricket_Board), a full member. [The national cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriname_national_cricket_team) was ranked 47th in the world and sixth in the [ICC Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Americas) region as of June 2014, and competes in the [World Cricket League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cricket_League) (WCL) and [ICC Americas Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICC_Americas_Championship). [Iris Jharap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iris_Jharap), born in Paramaribo, played [women's One Day International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_One_Day_International) matches for [the Dutch national side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_women's_cricket_team), the only Surinamese to do so.[^cite_note-145]

In the sport of [badminton](3956.md), another popular sport in Suriname especially with the youth, the local heroes are [Virgil Soeroredjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil_Soeroredjo), [Mitchel Wongsodikromo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchel_Wongsodikromo), [Sören Opti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B6ren_Opti) and also [Crystal Leefmans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Leefmans). All winning medals for Suriname at the [Carebaco Caribbean Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carebaco_International), the [Central American and Caribbean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_and_Caribbean_Games) (CACSO Games)[^cite_note-146] and also at the [South American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_Games), better known as the [ODESUR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ODESUR) Games. Virgil Soeroredjo also participated for Suriname at the [2012 London Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badminton_at_the_2012_Summer_Olympics), only the second badminton player, after [Oscar Brandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Brandon), for Suriname to achieve this.[^cite_note-147] [National Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surinamese_National_Badminton_Championships) [Sören Opti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%B6ren_Opti) became the third Surinamese badminton player to participate at the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics) in 2016.

Multiple time [K-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-1) [kickboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickboxing) world champions [Ernesto Hoost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Hoost) and [Remy Bonjasky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remy_Bonjasky) were born in Suriname or are of Surinamese descent. Other kickboxing world champions include [Gilbert Ballantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Ballantine), [Rayen Simson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayen_Simson), [Melvin Manhoef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melvin_Manhoef), [Tyrone Spong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrone_Spong), [Andy Ristie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Ristie), [Jairzinho Rozenstruik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jairzinho_Rozenstruik), [Regian Eersel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regian_Eersel), and [Donovan Wisse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donovan_Wisse).

Suriname also has a [national korfball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriname_national_korfball_team), with [korfball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korfball) being a Dutch sport. [Vinkensport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinkensport) is also practised.

In 2016, the [Sports Hall of Fame Suriname](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Hall_of_Fame_Suriname) was established in the building of the Suriname Olympic Committee and is dedicated to the achievements of the Surinamese sporters.[^cite_note-SN-148]
