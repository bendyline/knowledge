An unusually strong and rare [nor'easter](402179.md) caused widespread impacts across the [Northeastern United States](431669.md) in late September 2026. Although nor'easters typically strike in the late fall and winter, forecasters described this system as a rare, high-intensity early-season storm, drawing comparisons to [Hurricane Sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Sandy), [Hurricane Irene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Irene), [Hurricane Bob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Bob) and [Hurricane Gloria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Gloria) because of the scale of expected coastal flooding.[^cite_note-CNN_Sept_24-2][^cite_note-Streamlinefeed-3] Originating from an upper-level low as a [frontal low-pressure system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_front) on September 23, the system meandered erratically off the [U.S. East Coast](89126.md) gradually undergoing [cyclogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclogenesis) as it approached the U.S. Northeast.

As of September 28, more than 200,000 people total have lost power across the Northeast, one person died in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn) from a fallen tree, and nearly 550 flights have been cancelled.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

Meteorologists emphasized how unusual the storm's timing was, noting that nor'easters are far more common in the late fall and winter than in September.[^cite_note-BostonGlobe921-11] According to the Boston Globe, only about 1 percent of nor'easters on record have developed in September, a pattern typically associated with a surface front stalling off the mid-Atlantic coast beneath a dipping jet stream.[^cite_note-BostonGlobe921-11] NBC10 Boston similarly noted that while nor'easters can form at any time of year, they are most frequent in winter, making a storm of this strength forming in September especially rare, according to the National Weather Service.[^cite_note-NBC10Boston-12]

In late-September 2026, the [Weather Prediction Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather_Prediction_Center) (WPC) began highlighting the possibility for development of a meandering coastal storm off the [Eastern Seaboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Seaboard).[^cite_note-13] On September 23, a surface [low pressure system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-pressure_area) developed under a slow-moving [upper-level low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold-core_low) off the Northern [Mid-Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_\(United_States\)) coastline.[^cite_note-14] [Model forecasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forecasting#Categories_of_forecasting_methods) were in good agreement that the low would meander south or southeast of [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island), though slight differences in position could cause significantly different impacts in wind speed and direction.[^cite_note-15] Throughout the next day, the low continued to meander offshore the Northern Mid-Atlantic and Northeastern U.S. bringing broad onshore flow onto those regions promoting marine hazards such as rip currents and high surf.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]



Satellite loop of the nor'easter swirling off the East Coast on September 26

On the morning of September 26, the nor'easter reached its peak intensity with a minimum central pressure of 986 mbar (29.12 inHg) as conditions began to deteriorate across the Northeast region.[^cite_note-18] At 15\:00 UTC, the Weather Prediction Center began issuing storm summaries, an [occluded front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occluded_front) extended eastward from the low south of [Cape Cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Cod), with radar indicating a large area of rain wrapping around the northern side of the low over the [Delmarva Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delmarva_Peninsula), eastern [Pennsylvania](23332.md), New Jersey, Long Island, and portions of [southern New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_New_England).[^cite_note-19] At 02\:00 UTC on September 27, the nor'easter was positioned about 100 miles south of Long Island, where [Montauk Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montauk,_New_York) recorded a max wind gust of 81 mph (130 km/h).[^cite_note-20] The nor'easter began to gradually weaken whilst centered along the New Jersey coast near [Point Pleasant Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Pleasant_Beach,_New_Jersey) as moderate coastal flooding continued to occur at that morning’s high tide.[^cite_note-21] The low weakened further that night, weakening to a central pressure of 998 mbar (29.47 inHg) along the coast of [Cape May, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_May,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-storm_summary_number-22] On September 29, the nor'easter turned to the northeast near far eastern Massachusetts.[^cite_note-23]

## Preparations

### Mid-Atlantic



New York MTA officials providing a weather update ahead of the nor'easter

Governor [Kathy Hochul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Hochul) declared a state of emergency for [New York City](645042.md) (NYC), [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island), and [Westchester County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westchester_County), while Governor [Mikie Sherrill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikie_Sherrill) declared one for eight counties in [New Jersey](21648.md), both citing the threat of high winds, heavy rainfall, and coastal flooding.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Mayor [Zohran Mamdani](64618347.md) separately issued a travel advisory for NYC.[^cite_note-26] Ahead of the storm, the [MTA](384273.md) issued a weather update to New Yorkers outlining precautionary measures for the transit system.

Businesses in [Downtown Annapolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Annapolis,_Maryland) used sandbags provided by the city and other protective measures to prevent potential flooding from the nor'easter at [high tide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tide).[^cite_note-27] A festival in [Ocean City, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_City,_Maryland) was cancelled as a result of strong winds and beach erosion. Winds were gusting to 30 mph (48 km/h) and were expected to possibly reach 50 mph (80 km/h) on September 26.[^cite_note-28] Coastal flood warnings were issued across beach communities including Ocean City, and [Rehoboth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehoboth_Beach,_Delaware) and [Bethany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethany_Beach,_Delaware) beaches in [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware), as well as communities in [Virginia](32432.md). Southeast Virginia, from [Virginia Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Beach,_Virginia) to [Norfolk](57898.md), faced the highest risk for coastal flood inundation, at 1–3 ft (0.3–0.9 m).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

[Philadelphia](50585.md) was placed under a coastal flood and wind advisory by the [National Weather Service](286989.md) office in [Harrisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg,_Pennsylvania) due to its proximity to the shoreline; portions of central and eastern Pennsylvania were forecast to see gusts in excess of 28 mph (45 km/h).[^cite_note-31]

### New England

All [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) service between [New York City](645042.md) and [Boston](24437894.md) was suspended during the nor'easter.[^cite_note-32]

Governor [Ned Lamont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ned_Lamont) activated Connecticut's [Emergency Operations Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emergency_operations_center) (EOC) ahead of strong winds and heavy rain across the state, and ferry services between [New London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_London,_Connecticut) and [Orient, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient,_New_York) were also cancelled.[^cite_note-33] Boaters in [Old Saybrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Saybrook,_Connecticut) were encouraged to tighten and double their lines to prevent storm damage to their vessels and the dock.[^cite_note-34]

The southern part of [Rhode Island](25410.md) was put under a high wind warning, while wind advisories were issued for the northern part of the state. Four emergency shelters extended their hours in [Providence](19356538.md), and access to the [Mount Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hope_Bridge), [Newport Pell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claiborne_Pell_Newport_Bridge), and [Jamestown Verrazzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown_Verrazzano_Bridge) bridges was restricted by the [Rhode Island Turnpike and Bridge Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Turnpike_and_Bridge_Authority). Ferry cancellations occurred as well.[^cite_note-35]

State officials in [Massachusetts](1645518.md) urged the public to reduce the risk of flooding by clearing debris from nearby storm drains, and encouraged drivers to minimize travel but to proceed with caution if necessary; all ferry services were cancelled from September 26–27.[^cite_note-36] The [City of Boston](24437894.md) was placed under a coastal storm warning from September 25–27 by Mayor [Michelle Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_Wu), with forecasters predicting 2–4 in (51–102 mm) of rainfall for the city.[^cite_note-37]

Though near the northern periphery of the storm, high surf and coastal flood advisories were issued along coastal [Maine](19977.md) due to the risk of swells that may build to nearly 18 ft (5.5 m) offshore [Cape Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Elizabeth,_Maine), and minor coastal flooding of about 1–2 ft (0.3–0.6 m) along the rest of the Maine coast.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

The National Weather Service placed New Hampshire's coast under a [gale warning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gale_warning), a [high surf advisory](2241482.md), a [small craft advisory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_craft_advisory), and a [coastal flood advisory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_flood_advisory), effective the morning of September 25, 2026.[^cite_note-40]

## Impact



The September 2026 nor'easter made landfall in New Jersey on September 27, 2026

### Mid-Atlantic

#### New York and New Jersey

As the strong nor'easter pounded Long Island, New York City experienced flooding particularly in streets and highways in low-lying areas by the coast, two people abandoned their vehicles due to rising floodwaters in [Elmhurst, Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmhurst,_Queens), occasional lightning strikes occurred within embedded [convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convectional_storm) from the outer bands of the storm as it moved in from the east.[^cite_note-41] Storm surge reached 3.54 ft (1.08 m) in [Kings Park, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Park,_New_York).[^cite_note-42] [Jersey Central Power and Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_Central_Power_and_Light) reported more than 17,300 power outages in New Jersey statewide as strong winds approaching 50 mph (80 km/h) brought down trees, large branches and power lines. [New Jersey Route 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Route_35) remained closed at the Sayreville-Old Bridge border due to flooding.[^cite_note-43] A high tide on September 26 pushed water levels in [Atlantic City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_City,_New_Jersey) to their highest point since [Hurricane Sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Sandy) in 2012, and officials in the city warned that conditions were among the worst since that storm.[^cite_note-CNN_Sept_26-44][^cite_note-CBS_News-45] Rainfall totals in New Jersey reached 6.78 in (172 mm) in [Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptune_Township,_New_Jersey).[^cite_note-46] The [Long Beach Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach_Branch) of the [Long Island Rail Road](37139.md) was suspended from [Valley Stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Stream,_New_York) to [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach,_New_York).[^cite_note-47] Fallen trees also resulted in the [North Jersey Coast Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Jersey_Coast_Line) of [NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit) being suspended.[^cite_note-48] A swimmer off the coast of [Robert Moses State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Moses_State_Park) went missing, though was later found alive.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] The [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees) were forced to cancel their regular season finale due to the nor'easter impacts.[^cite_note-51] Up to 10.40 in (264 mm) of rain was recorded at [Orient Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient_Point,_New_York).[^cite_note-52] In [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn), one tree fell and killed a man, while another fell and injured at least four others.[^cite_note-53]

#### Delaware, Maryland, and Pennsylvania

High tides and waves caused beach erosion in Delaware.[^cite_note-54] Flooding resulted in the closure of [Delaware Route 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Route_1) between [Bethany Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethany_Beach,_Delaware) to [Dewey Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewey_Beach,_Delaware).[^cite_note-\:2-55] In Maryland, [Ocean City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_City,_Maryland) was impacted by coastal flooding and beaches saw significant erosion.[^cite_note-56] To the north in Pennsylvania, over 14,000 customers were without power on September 26.[^cite_note-57] Downed trees and utility poles caused some property damage.[^cite_note-58] Several delays were reported at the [30th Street Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30th_Street_Station) in [Philadelphia](50585.md).[^cite_note-\:2-55]

### New England

#### Southern New England

Fallen trees and flooding resulted in road closures across Connecticut.[^cite_note-59] A state maximum of 4.2 in (107 mm) of rain fell over [Durham, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durham,_Connecticut).[^cite_note-storm_summary_number-22] Across the state, 35,000 customers lost power.[^cite_note-60]

In Massachusetts, winds began to intensify on the afternoon of September 25. Gusts of over 60 mph (97 km/h) were recorded in parts of the state, with [Wellfleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellfleet,_Massachusetts) reporting a gust of 70 mph (110 km/h). About 6,600 customers lost power across the state by 4 p.m. on September 26,[^cite_note-61] and approximately 1,500 customers remained without power around 3 a.m. Monday, September 28, particularly in [Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrence,_Massachusetts), [Woburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woburn,_Massachusetts), and [Bedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford,_Massachusetts).[^cite_note-62]

Winds reaching close to 55 mph (89 km/h) struck Rhode Island. [Rhode Island Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhode_Island_Energy) reported over 31,000 customers were impacted due to falling trees damaging power lines.[^cite_note-63]

#### Northern New England

High surf impacted the coast of Maine, though was not as high as originally forecasted. The [Pemaquid Point Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pemaquid_Point_Light) was closed due to the storm. A car was damaged by a wave in [Kennebunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennebunk,_Maine).[^cite_note-64]

As of Sunday, September 27, 2026, New Hampshire officials were reporting 4,500 power outages.[^cite_note-65]

### Other states

[North Carolina Highway 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Carolina_Highway_12) was closed after it was damaged by heavy overwash and strong winds, cutting off the islands of [Hatteras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatteras_Island) and [Ocracoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocracoke,_North_Carolina) from the mainland.[^cite_note-66]

A wind gust of 51 mph (82 km/h) was recorded at [Cape Henry, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Henry,_Virginia).[^cite_note-67]
