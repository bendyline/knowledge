The **Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic** (**SADR**),[^cite_note-20] also known as the **Sahrawi Republic** and **Western Sahara**, is a [partially recognised state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_with_limited_recognition) in the western [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb), which claims the [non-self-governing territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_list_of_non-self-governing_territories) of [Western Sahara](33220.md), but controls only [the easternmost one-fifth of that territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Zone_\(region\)). It is recognised by [34 UN member states](32672915.md) and [South Ossetia](318381.md). Between 1884 and 1975, Western Sahara was known as [Spanish Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Sahara), a [Spanish colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) (later an overseas province). The SADR is one of the two African states in which [Spanish](26825.md) is a significant language, the other being [Equatorial Guinea](9366.md).

The SADR was proclaimed by the [Polisario Front](201574.md) on 27 February 1976, in [Bir Lehlou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Lehlou), Western Sahara. The SADR government calls the territories under its control the *Liberated Territories* or the *[Free Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Zone_\(region\))*. [Morocco](19291.md) occupies the rest of the disputed territory, and calls these lands its [Southern Provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan-occupied_Western_Sahara). The claimed [capital city](181337.md) of the SADR is [Laayoune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laayoune) (the largest city of the Western Sahara territory). Since the SADR does not control Laayoune, it has established a [temporary capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_capital) in [Tifariti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tifariti), although most of the day-to-day administration happens in [Rabuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabouni_refugee_camp), one of the [Sahrawi refugee camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_refugee_camps) located in [Tindouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tindouf), Algeria.

The Polisario Front's government-in-exile has been accused by Morocco of being a [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) used by [Algeria](358.md) to fight a [proxy war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proxy_war) against Morocco.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

The SADR maintains diplomatic relations with 45 [United Nations](31769.md) states, and is a full member of the [African Union](10634933.md). With a population of about half a million, it is the most [sparsely populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population_density) state in Africa, and the second-most sparsely populated state in the world,[^cite_note-23] second only to [Mongolia](19271.md).

## Etymology

The name *Sahrawi* is the [romanisation of the Arabic word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic) *Ṣaḥrāwī* صحراوي, meaning 'Inhabitant of [the Desert](325363.md)',[^cite_note-24] derived from the [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) word *Ṣaḥrāʼ* (صحراء), meaning 'desert'.[^cite_note-25]

## History

Main article: [History of Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Western_Sahara)

Following the evacuation of the Spaniards, due to the Moroccan [Green March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_March), [Spain](26667.md), [Morocco](19291.md), and [Mauritania](18778516.md) signed the [Madrid Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_Accords) on 14 November 1975, six days before [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco) died. Morocco and Mauritania responded by [annexing the territory of Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Western_Sahara). On 26 February 1976, Spain informed the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN) that as of that date it had terminated its presence in Western Sahara and relinquished its responsibilities, which left the region devoid of any Administering Power.[^cite_note-26] Neither Morocco nor Mauritania gained international recognition, and war ensued with the independence-seeking [Polisario Front](201574.md). The UN considers the Polisario Front to be the legitimate representative of the [Sahrawi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawis), and maintains that the people of Western Sahara have a right to "[self-determination](29269.md) and independence".[^cite_note-27]

The creation of the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic was proclaimed on 27 February 1976, as the [Polisario](201574.md) declared the need for a new entity to fill what they considered a political void left by the departing Spanish colonial administration. While the claimed capital is the former Western Sahara capital [Laayoune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laayoune) (which is in Moroccan-occupied territory), the proclamation was made in the [government-in-exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government-in-exile)'s provisional capital, [Bir Lehlou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Lehlou), which remained in Polisario-held territory under the 1991 [ceasefire](592591.md) (see [Settlement Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlement_Plan)). On 27 February 2008, the provisional capital was formally moved to [Tifariti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tifariti).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] Day-to-day business, however, is conducted in the [Sahrawi refugee camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_refugee_camps) in [Tindouf Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tindouf_Province), Algeria, which house most of the Sahrawi exile community.

## Government and politics

Main articles: [Politics of the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic), [Government of the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic), and [Elections in the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_the_Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic)

The Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic is officially a [one-party](102459.md) [semi-presidential republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_republic).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Since August 1982, the highest office of the republic has been the [President of the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic), a post held by the secretary-general of the Polisario Front, presently [Brahim Ghali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahim_Ghali),[^cite_note-Zunes_2010-32] who appoints the [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_the_Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic), currently [Bouchraya Hammoudi Bayoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouchraya_Hammoudi_Bayoun). The SADR's government structure consists of a Council of Ministers (a cabinet led by the Prime Minister), a judicial branch (with judges appointed by the President) and the parliamentary [Sahrawi National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_National_Council) (SNC; the present [speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_\(politics\)) is [Bashir Mustafa Sayed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashir_Mustafa_Sayed?action=edit\&redlink=1)). Since its inception in 1976, the various [constitutional revisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic) have transformed the republic from an *ad hoc* managerial structure into something approaching a governing apparatus. From the late 1980s the parliament began to take steps to institute a [division of powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_powers) and to disentangle the republic's structures from those of the Polisario Front, although without clear effect to date.

Its various ministries are responsible for a variety of services and functions. The [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary), complete with trial courts, appeals courts and a [supreme court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic), operates in the same areas. As a [government-in-exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_in_exile), many branches of government do not fully function, and has affected the constitutional roles of the institutions. Institutions parallel to government structures also have arisen within the Polisario Front, which is fused with the SADR's governing apparatus, and with operational competences overlapping between these party and governmental institutions and offices. A 2012 report mentioned the existence of the Sahrawi Bar Association.[^cite_note-33] In 2016, the bar association (going by the name Union of Sahrawi Lawyers) issued a report calling for the implementation of political and civil rights.[^cite_note-34] Unfortunately, there is no clear indication as to how certain demographic groups, such as women, have fared in the legal field.

The SNC is weak in its legislative role, having been instituted as a mainly consultative and [consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_decision-making)-building institution, but it has strengthened its theoretical legislative and controlling powers during later constitutional revisions. Among other things, it has added a ban on the [death penalty](5902.md) to the constitution, and brought down the government in 1999 through a [vote of no-confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_of_no-confidence).

The Sahrawi National Council is composed of 53 members, all from the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Saguia el-Hamra and Río de Oro](201574.md).

### Constitution

Main article: [Constitution of the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic)

A 1999 Constitution of the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic took a form similar to the [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentarism) constitutions of many European states, but with some paragraphs suspended until the achievement of "full independence". Among key points, the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) is constitutionally the Secretary General of the Polisario Front during what is referred to as the "pre-independence phase", with provision in the constitution that on independence, Polisario is supposed to be dismantled or separated completely from the government structure. Provisions are detailed for a transitory phase beginning with independence, in which the present SADR is supposed to act as Western Sahara's government, ending with a constitutional reform and eventual establishment of a state along the lines specified in the constitution.

The broad guidelines laid down in the constitution for an eventual Western Saharan state include eventual [multi-party democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system) with a [market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy). The constitution also defines Sahrawis as a [Muslim](19541.md), [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_diaspora) and [Arab](2185.md) people.[^cite_note-35] The Constitution also declares a commitment to the principles of [human rights](13831.md) and to the concept of a [Greater Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Maghreb_Union), as a regional variant of [Pan-Arabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Arabism).

### Military

Main article: [Sahrawi People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_People's_Liberation_Army)

The [Sahrawi People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_People's_Liberation_Army) is the official [military force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_force) of the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic and previously served as the armed wing of the Polisario Front prior to the foundation of the state.

### International recognition and membership

Main articles: [Political status of Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Western_Sahara), [International recognition of the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic](32672915.md), and [Foreign relations of the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_the_Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic)

As of September 2022, the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic has been recognised by 85 states, at one time or another. Of these, 39 have "frozen" or "withdrawn" recognition for a number of reasons. A total of 29 UN states maintain an embassy from the SADR, with Vietnam being the only nation not hosting an embassy but only sending their own mission.[^cite_note-36] [Sahrawi embassies exist in 18 states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_the_Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic). Six UN states have other diplomatic relations, while a further nine UN nations and South Ossetia[^cite_note-37] also recognise the state either by previous regimes or through international agreements in the past, but do not have any active relations at the moment (see [foreign relations of the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_the_Sahrawi_Arab_Democratic_Republic) for more details).

Paraguay,[^cite_note-38] Australia,[^cite_note-39] Brazil,[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] and Sweden[^cite_note-44] have all internally voted to recognise the SADR, but none have yet ratified it.

Although it is not recognised by the UN, the SADR has held full membership of the [African Union](10634933.md) (AU, formerly the [Organisation of African Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_African_Unity), OAU) since 1982. Morocco withdrew from the OAU in protest during 1984, and from the time of [South Africa](17416221.md)'s admittance to the OAU in 1994 was the only African UN member not also a member of the AU, until it was readmitted on 30 January 2017.[^cite_note-worldbulletin-45] The SADR participates as a guest in meetings of the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_Summit_of_the_Non-Aligned_Movement)[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] and the [New Asian–African Strategic Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Asian%E2%80%93African_Strategic_Partnership),[^cite_note-NAASP-48][^cite_note-Asian-African_Strategic_Partnership-49] over Moroccan objections to SADR participation.[^cite_note-Asian-African_Strategic_Partnership_2-50]

The SADR also participated in a conference of the Permanent Conference of Political Parties of the Latin American and the Caribbean ([COPPPAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COPPPAL)) in 2006;[^cite_note-Permanent_Conference_of_Political_Parties_of_the_Latin_American_and_the_Caribbean-51] the SADR ambassador to Nicaragua participated in the opening conference of the [Central American Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Parliament) in 2010,[^cite_note-52] and a SADR delegation participated in the meeting of COPPPAL and [International Conference of Asian Political Parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Conference_of_Asian_Political_Parties) in [Mexico City](18987.md) in 2012.[^cite_note-53]

On 27 February 2011, the 35th anniversary of the proclamation of SADR was marked in [Tifariti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tifariti), Western Sahara. Delegations, including parliamentarians, ambassadors, NGOs and activists from many countries participated in this event.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

The SADR is not a member of the [Arab League](52625.md), nor of the [Arab Maghreb Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Maghreb_Union), both of which include Morocco as a full member.

### Area of authority

The SADR acted as a government administration in the [Sahrawi refugee camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_refugee_camps) located in the [Tindouf Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tindouf_Province) of western Algeria. It is headquartered in Camp Rabouni, south of [Tindouf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tindouf), although some official events have taken place in [towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Western_Sahara) in the [Free Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Zone_\(region\)), including the provisional capitals, first [Bir Lehlou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bir_Lehlou) until 2008, then Tifariti. The government of the SADR claims sovereignty over all of the Western Sahara territory, but has control only within the Free Zone. Several foreign [aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aid) agencies, including the [United Nations High Commissioner for Refugees](31964.md) and [non-governmental organisations](46539.md), are continually active in the camps.

### Proposed Western Sahara Authority

Under the [Baker Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Plan) created by [James Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Baker), former UN Secretary-General [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan)'s personal envoy to Western Sahara, the SADR would have been replaced with a five-year transitional [Western Sahara Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Sahara_Authority) (WSA), a non-sovereign [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_administrative_division) supervised by Morocco, to be followed by a referendum on independence. It was endorsed by the UN in 2003. As Morocco has declined to participate, however, the plan appears dead.

In April 2007, the government of Morocco suggested that a self-governing entity, through the [Royal Advisory Council for Saharan Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Advisory_Council_for_Saharan_Affairs) (CORCAS), should govern the territory with some degree of autonomy for Western Sahara. The project was presented to the [UN Security Council](31956.md) in mid-April 2007. A stalemate over the Moroccan proposal led the UN, in an April 2007 "Report of the UN Secretary-General", to ask the parties to enter into direct and unconditional negotiations to reach a mutually accepted political solution.[^cite_note-UN-56]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Western_Sahara)

The official currency of the SADR is the [Sahrawi peseta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_peseta), though, in practice, the [Algerian dinar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_dinar) and [Mauritanian ouguiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritanian_ouguiya) are the main currencies used within the controlled territories. The [Moroccan dirham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_dirham) is also accepted, though it is mainly only used in the Moroccan-occupied territories.

## Demographics

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Demographics of Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Western_Sahara).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Demographics_of_Western_Sahara\&action=edit)]

All demographic data of [Western Sahara](33220.md) is widely regarded as being extremely prone to error. It is among the [world's most sparsely populated territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population_density) and the last official census there was conducted by [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Sahara) in 1974. Currently, [Western Sahara's political status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Western_Sahara) is disputed between [Morocco](19291.md) and the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic, which is governed by the [Polisario Front](201574.md). About 80% of Western Sahara is under [Moroccan occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan-occupied_Western_Sahara), while the Sahrawi Arab Democratic Republic controls the rest of the territory that lies to the east of the Moroccan-built [Berm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_Western_Sahara_Wall). The entirety of Western Sahara is classified by the [United Nations](31769.md) as a [non-self-governing territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_list_of_non-self-governing_territories) that is still [awaiting decolonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Committee_on_Decolonization).

Hundreds of thousands of [Moroccan settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_settlers) have migrated into occupied Western Sahara since the [Green March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_March) in 1975. As of 2015, it is estimated that Moroccan settlers account for at least two thirds of Western Sahara's entire population of 500,000 people.[^cite_note-57] Under international law, Morocco's settlement schemes in the occupied territory constitute a direct violation of [Article 49 of the Fourth Geneva Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Geneva_Convention#Article_49:_Deportations,_transfers,_evacuations).[^cite_note-58]

The dominant religion in Western Sahara is [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam). Major ethnic groups include [Arabs](2185.md) and [Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers). The most common languages in the territory are [Hassaniya Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassaniya_Arabic) and [Moroccan Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moroccan_Arabic).[^cite_note-59]

### Language

Main article: [Languages of Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Western_Sahara)

[Modern Standard Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Standard_Arabic) is the sole constitutionally recognised official and national language of the Sahrawi Republic.[^cite_note-60] [Hassaniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassaniya), a [variety of Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Arabic) also spoken in neighbouring countries such as [Mauritania](18778516.md), is the common vernacular language of the [Sahrawi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_people).

[Spanish](26825.md) was introduced during the [Spanish colonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Sahara) in the late 19th century, and remains as the preferred [second language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_language) of the Sahrawi, also enjoying a *de facto* [working language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_language) status.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartos20141199–1202-61] In 2018, President [Brahim Gali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahim_Gali) stated that the SADR is the only Arab country in the world where Spanish is an official language.[^cite_note-sps-3] [Instituto Cervantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instituto_Cervantes) estimates that around 20,000 Sahrawis have limited competencies in [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharan_Spanish).[^cite_note-62]

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Western_Sahara)

See also: [Catholic Church in Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Western_Sahara)

Religion in Western Sahara (ARDA 2020 est.)

[^cite_note-63]

1. [Islam](6037917.md) (99.5%)

2. [Christianity](5211.md) (0.15%)

3. [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) (0.04%)

4. [None](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion) (0.25%)

The predominant religion practiced by Sahrawis is the [Maliki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maliki) school of [Sunni Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam), which is constitutionally recognised as the official religion of the SADR and a source of law. Virtually all Sahrawis identify as Muslim according to the [CIA World Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_World_Factbook), which makes the country one of the most religiously homogeneous nations in the world.

The [Catholic Church](606848.md) had an important presence during [Spanish rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Sahara), with 20,000 Spanish Catholics present before Spain abandoned the territory (30% of the population). Today around 300 people in the Moroccan-occupied areas are Catholic (mostly of Spanish origin), being able to attend the [St. Francis of Assisi Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Francis_of_Assisi_Cathedral,_Laayoune) in [El Aaiún](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Aai%C3%BAn) and the [Our Lady of Mount Carmel Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady_of_Mount_Carmel_Church,_Dakhla) in [Dakhla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakhla,_Western_Sahara).
