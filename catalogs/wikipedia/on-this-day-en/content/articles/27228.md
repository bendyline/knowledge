**Saint Vincent and the Grenadines**,[^cite_note-9] sometimes known simply as **Saint Vincent**, is an [archipelagic country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archipelagic_country) in the eastern [Caribbean](18956035.md). It is located in the southeast [Windward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_Islands) of the [Lesser Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Antilles), which lie in the [West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies), at the southern end of the eastern border between the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md) and the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md). To the north lies [Saint Lucia](27208.md), to the east is [Barbados](3455.md) and [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada) lies to the south.

Spanning a land area of 389 km<sup>2</sup> (150 sq mi), most of its territory consists of the northernmost island of [Saint Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_\(Antilles\)), which includes the capital and largest city, [Kingstown](742159.md). To the south lie the northern two-thirds of the [Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenadines), a chain of 32 smaller islands; the remaining southern third make up Grenada. Seven of the islands are inhabited,[^cite_note-10] of which the largest and most populous are [Bequia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bequia), [Mustique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustique), [Canouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canouan) and [Union Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Island).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

With a population of around 109,296, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines has a population density of 281 inhabitants/km<sup>2</sup> (730 per sq mi).[^cite_note-auto-6] The majority of its people are descendants of enslaved Africans brought by France and then later Great Britain, which contested the islands during the 18th century. SVG remained a colony of the British Empire from 1783 until 1979, when it peacefully achieved independence; the country's culture, language, government and legal system reflect the long legacy of British rule. It is part of the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) and is a [Commonwealth realm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm), with [King Charles III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Charles_III) as its official head of state.

Saint Vincent is a member of the [Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_Eastern_Caribbean_States), [CARICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Community), the [Bolivarian Alliance for the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivarian_Alliance_for_the_Americas), and the [Community of Latin American and Caribbean States](26313243.md) (CELAC).

In April 2021, the [La Soufrière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Soufri%C3%A8re_\(Saint_Vincent\)) volcano [erupted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_eruption_of_La_Soufri%C3%A8re) several times with "explosive events" continuing for two weeks, resulting in the evacuation of 16,000 residents.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Assistance and emergency financial support was provided by several nearby islands, the [United Kingdom](31717.md), and agencies such as the [United Nations](31769.md). The first significant offer of long-term funding of US\$20 million was announced on 13 April 2021 by the [World Bank](36752.md).[^cite_note-15]

## Etymology

[Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus), the first European to reach the island, named it after [St. Vincent of Saragossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Vincent_of_Saragossa) (*San Vicente de Zaragoza*) whose [feast day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calendar_of_saints) was on the day Columbus first saw it (22 January 1496). The name of the Grenadines refers to the Spanish city of [Granada](38264.md), but to differentiate it from [the island of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada), the diminutive was used. Before the arrival of the Spaniards, the [Kalinago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinago) natives who inhabited the island of St. Vincent called it Youloumain, in honour of Youlouca, the spirit of the rainbows, whom they believed inhabited the island.[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16][^cite_note-thecommonwealth.org-17]

## History

Main articles: [History of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines), [French West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_West_Indies), and [British West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_West_Indies)

### Pre-colonial period

[Saint Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_\(Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines\)) was first inhabited by [Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_South_America) people from [South America](26769.md) around 5000 B.C. They were succeeded by the [Arawaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawak) around the 3rd century A.D., who were later displaced by the [Kalinago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinago) in the 14th century.[^cite_note-thecommonwealth.org-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-britannica.com-20] The Kalinago named the island Youloumain.[^cite_note-21]

### European arrival and early colonial period

It is thought that [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) sighted the island in 1498, giving it the name St Vincent.[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16][^cite_note-22] The [Kalinago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinago) people aggressively opposed European settlement on Saint Vincent.[^cite_note-britannica.com-20][^cite_note-thecommonwealth.org-17]

### French and British colonisation and First Carib War

Various attempts by the English and Dutch to claim the island proved unsuccessful, and it was the French who were first able to colonise the island, settling in the town of [Barrouallie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrouallie) on the [leeward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_and_leeward) side of St Vincent in 1719.[^cite_note-britannica.com-20] The French imported Black slaves to work on plantations producing sugar, coffee, indigo, tobacco, cotton and cocoa.[^cite_note-23]



Depiction of the 1773 treaty negotiations between the British and the [Garifuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garifuna)

The British captured the island and drove out the French from Barrouallie during the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War), a claim confirmed by the [Treaty of Paris (1763)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1763\)).[^cite_note-britannica.com-20] On taking control of the island in 1763, the British laid the foundations of [Fort Charlotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Charlotte,_Saint_Vincent) and also continued the importation of slaves to work on the island's plantations. The [Garifuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garifuna), an Afro-Indigenous creole people on the island, were opposed to the British presence and entered into open conflict against the British, starting the [First Carib War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Carib_War), which lasted from 1772 to 1773.[^cite_note-britannica.com-20][^cite_note-24]

During the [Anglo-French War (1778–1783)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-French_War_\(1778%E2%80%931783\)), the French [recaptured St Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_capture_of_Saint_Vincent) in 1779. However, the British regained control under the [Treaty of Versailles (1783)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles_\(1783\)).[^cite_note-britannica.com-20][^cite_note-thecommonwealth.org-17]

### British colonial period and Second Carib War

Main article: [British Windward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Windward_Islands)

The uneasy peace between the British and the Garifuna led to the [Second Carib War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Carib_War), which lasted from 1795 to 1797.[^cite_note-britannica.com-20] The Garifuna were led by paramount chief [Joseph Chatoyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Chatoyer) and supported by the French, notably [Victor Hugues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Hugues) who was based on the island of [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique). They were eventually defeated in 1797 by British forces under the command Sir [Ralph Abercromby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Abercromby); a peace treaty agreement was made which resulted in almost 5,000 Garifuna being deported to [Roatán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roat%C3%A1n), an island off the coast of [Honduras](13394.md), and to [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize) and [Baliceaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baliceaux) in the Grenadines.[^cite_note-thecommonwealth.org-17]

In 1806, the construction of Fort Charlotte was completed.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

The [La Soufrière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Soufri%C3%A8re_\(volcano\)) volcano erupted in 1812, resulting in considerable destruction.[^cite_note-Pyle,_David-27][^cite_note-thecommonwealth.org-17]



Colonial flag (to 1979)

The British [abolished](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_Kingdom) slavery in Saint Vincent (as well as in all other [British West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_West_Indies) colonies) in 1834, and an apprenticeship period followed which ended in 1838.[^cite_note-thecommonwealth.org-17][^cite_note-britannica.com-20] After its end, labour shortages on the plantations resulted, and were initially addressed by the immigration of indentured servants; starting from 1845, many [Portuguese Catholic](563502.md) settlers arrived from [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira), with around 2,100 arrivals from [Portugal](23033.md) recorded from 1845 to 1850.[^cite_note-28] Between 1861 and 1888, a new wave of immigration occurred, with shiploads of [Indian labourers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Caribbeans) arriving.[^cite_note-britannica.com-20][^cite_note-29]

### 20th century

Main article: [West Indies Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies_Federation)



Residents of Saint Vincent making *[casabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casabe)* (cassava bread) in the 1910s

In 1902, the [La Soufrière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Soufri%C3%A8re_\(volcano\)) volcano erupted again, killing 1,500–2,000 people; much farmland was damaged, and the economy deteriorated.[^cite_note-thecommonwealth.org-17][^cite_note-britannica.com-20][^cite_note-Pyle,_David-27]

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines passed through various stages of colonial status under the British. A representative assembly was authorised in 1876, Crown Colony government was installed in 1877, a [legislative council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_council) was created in 1925 with a limited franchise,[^cite_note-britannica.com-20] and [universal adult suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage) was granted in 1951.[^cite_note-britannica.com-20] During the period of its control of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines, Britain made several attempts to unify the island with the other Windward Islands as a single entity, to simplify British control in the sub-region through a single unified administration.[^cite_note-britannica.com-20] In the 1960s, the British again tried to unify all of its regional islands, including Saint Vincent, into a single politically unified entity under British control. The unification was to be called the [West Indies Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indies_Federation) and was driven by a desire to gain independence from the British government. However, the attempt collapsed in 1962.[^cite_note-britannica.com-20]

Saint Vincent was granted "associate statehood" status by Britain on 27 October 1969.[^cite_note-britannica.com-20] This gave Saint Vincent complete control over its internal affairs but fell short of full independence in law.[^cite_note-30]

In April 1979, [La Soufrière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Soufri%C3%A8re_\(volcano\)) erupted once more. Although no one was killed, thousands were evacuated and extensive agricultural damage occurred.[^cite_note-Pyle,_David-27]

On 27 October 1979, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines gained full independence;[^cite_note-britannica.com-20][^cite_note-thecommonwealth.org-17] the date is now the country's [Independence Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_independence_days), a [public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines).[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16] The country opted to remain within the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations), retaining the then-Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II) as [Monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines), represented locally by a [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines).[^cite_note-31]

### Post-independence era

[Milton Cato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Cato) of the centre-left [Saint Vincent Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_Labour_Party) (SVLP) was the country's first Prime Minister (he had been Premier since 1974), ruling until his defeat in the [1984 Vincentian general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Vincentian_general_election) by [James Fitz-Allen Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Fitz-Allen_Mitchell) of the centre-right [New Democratic Party](1836145.md) (NDP).[^cite_note-britannica.com-20] During Cato's time in office, there was a [breif rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Union_Island_Uprising) on [Union Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Island) in December 1979 led by Lennox 'Bumba' Charles. Inspired by the recent revolution on Grenada, Charles alleged neglect of the Union by the central government. However, the revolt was swiftly put down and Charles was arrested.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] There were also a series of strikes in the early 1980s.[^cite_note-thecommonwealth.org-17] James Mitchell remained Prime Minister for 16 years until 2000, winning three consecutive [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines).[^cite_note-britannica.com-20] Mitchell was at the forefront of attempts to improve regional integration.[^cite_note-thecommonwealth.org-17] In [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Allen) and 1987, hurricanes damaged many banana and coconut plantations. Hurricane seasons were also very active in 1998 and 1999, with [Hurricane Lenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Lenny) in 1999 causing extensive damage to the west coast of the island.

In 2000, [Arnhim Eustace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhim_Eustace) became Prime Minister after taking over the leadership of the NDP following Mitchell's retirement; he was [defeated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Vincentian_general_election) a year later by [Ralph Gonsalves](393744.md) of the [Unity Labour Party](1836153.md) (successor party to the SVLP).[^cite_note-caribbeanelections2001-34][^cite_note-britannica.com-20] Gonsalves, a left-winger known in the country as "Comrade Ralph",[^cite_note-BBCLeaders-35][^cite_note-36] argued that European nations owe Caribbean nations [reparations](38009444.md) for their role in the Atlantic slave trade.[^cite_note-37] Gonsalves won a second term in 2005,[^cite_note-BBCLeaders-35] a third in 2010,[^cite_note-BBCLeaders-35] and a fourth in 2015.[^cite_note-38]

In 2009, [a referendum was held on a proposal to adopt a new constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincentian_constitutional_referendum,_2009) that would make the country a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic), replacing Queen [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II) as head of state with a non-executive President, a proposal supported by Prime Minister Gonsalves. A two-thirds majority was required, but the referendum was defeated 29,019 votes (55.64 per cent) to 22,493 (43.13 per cent).[^cite_note-antillean-39][^cite_note-britannica.com-20]

In November 2020, Ralph Gonsalves, Prime Minister of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines since 2001, made history by securing the fifth consecutive victory of his [Unity Labour Party](1836153.md) (ULP) in general election.[^cite_note-40]

In 2021, on 9 April, the [La Soufrière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_eruption_of_La_Soufri%C3%A8re) volcano erupted, sending ash several miles into the atmosphere. Approximately 16,000 people were evacuated in the days leading up to the eruption.[^cite_note-41]

In the [2025 general election](76606789.md), [Godwin Friday](52936025.md)'s NDP defeated Gonsalves' ULP, forming government by winning 14 out of 15 seats.[^cite_note-42]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines)



A map of [Saint Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_\(Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines\)) and the [Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenadines)

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines lies to the west of [Barbados](3455.md), south of [Saint Lucia](27208.md) and north of [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada) in the [Windward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_Islands) of the [Lesser Antilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Antilles), an [island arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_arc) of the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md). The islands of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines include the main island of [Saint Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_\(Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines\)) (344 km<sup>2</sup> (133 sq mi)) and the northern two-thirds of the [Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenadines) (45 km<sup>2</sup> (17 sq mi) in total), which are a chain of smaller islands stretching south from Saint Vincent to Grenada.

There are 32 islands and [cays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cay) that make up St Vincent and the Grenadines (SVG). Nine are inhabited, including the mainland St Vincent and the Grenadines islands: Young Island, [Bequia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bequia), [Mustique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustique), [Canouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canouan), [Union Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Island), [Mayreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayreau), Petit St Vincent and Palm Island. Prominent uninhabited islands of the Grenadines include [Petit Nevis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petit_Nevis), used by [whalers](33557.md), and [Petit Mustique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petite_Mustique), which was the centre of a prominent real-estate scam in the early 2000s.[^cite_note-43]

The capital of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is [Kingstown](742159.md), Saint Vincent.[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16] The main island of Saint Vincent measures 26 km (16 mi) long, 15 km (9.3 mi) in width and 344 km<sup>2</sup> (133 sq mi) in area. From the most northern to the most southern points, the Grenadine islands belonging to Saint Vincent span 60.4 km (37.5 mi), with a combined area of 45 km<sup>2</sup> (17 sq mi).[^cite_note-44]

The island of Saint Vincent is volcanic and heavily forested and includes little level ground.[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16] The windward side of the island is very rocky and steep, while the leeward side has more sandy beaches and bays. Saint Vincent's highest peak is [La Soufrière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Soufri%C3%A8re_\(volcano\)) volcano at 1,234 m (4,049 ft).[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16] Other major mountains on St Vincent are (from north to south) Richmond Peak, [Mount Brisbane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Brisbane_\(Saint_Vincent\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), Colonarie Mountain, [Grand Bonhomme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Bonhomme), Petit Bonhomme and Mount St Andrew.[^cite_note-45]

The country is home to two terrestrial [ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion): [Windward Islands moist forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_Islands_moist_forests) and the [Lesser Antillean dry forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Antillean_dry_forests).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-46] It had a 2019 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 6.95/10, ranking it 61st globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-FLII-Supplementary-47]

Most of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines lies within the [Main Development Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Development_Region) for Atlantic [tropical cyclones](8282374.md). Recently, in 2023, the islands were directly impacted by [Tropical Storm Bret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Atlantic_hurricane_season#Tropical_Storm_Bret).[^cite_note-48]

## Government and politics

Main article: [Politics of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines)

Main office-holders

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is a [parliamentary democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_democracy) and [constitutional monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_realm), with [Charles III](125248.md) as [King of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines).[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16] He does not reside in the islands and is represented as [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) in the country by the [Governor-General of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines), currently [Stanley John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_John) (since 5 January 2026).[^cite_note-49]

The office of Governor-General has mostly ceremonial functions including the opening of the islands' [House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Assembly_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines) and the appointment of various government officials. Control of the government rests with the elected [Prime Minister](466897.md) and their cabinet. The current Prime Minister is [Godwin Friday](52936025.md), elected in 2025.[^cite_note-50]

The legislative branch of government is the unicameral [House of Assembly of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Assembly_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines), seating 15 elected members representing single-member [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district) and six appointed members known as Senators. The parliamentary term of office is five years, although the Prime Minister may call elections at any time.[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16]

The judicial branch of government is divided into district courts, the [Eastern Caribbean Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Caribbean_Supreme_Court) and the [Privy Council of the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Council_of_the_United_Kingdom) in London being the court of last resort.[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16]

### Political culture

The two political parties with parliamentary representation are the [New Democratic Party](1836145.md) (NDP) and the [Unity Labour Party](1836153.md) (ULP). The parliamentary opposition is made up of the largest minority stakeholder in the general elections, headed by the [Leader of the Opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines\)). The current opposition leader is [Ralph Gonsalves](393744.md).[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16]

### Foreign relations

Further information: [Foreign relations of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines)

#### International and regional relationships

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines maintains close ties to [Canada](5042916.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and the [US](3434750.md), and cooperates with regional political and economic organisations such as the [Organisation of Eastern Caribbean States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_Eastern_Caribbean_States) (OECS) and [CARICOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CARICOM).[^cite_note-ird.gov.tt-52] The island nation's sixth embassy overseas was opened on 8 August 2019 in Taipei, Taiwan, after Prime Minister Ralph Gonsalves's official visit to the [Republic of China (Taiwan)](25734.md);[^cite_note-53] the other five are located in London (a High Commission as Commonwealth countries have high commissions rather than embassies in each other's countries), Washington D.C., Havana, Caracas and Brussels.[^cite_note-54]

##### The Double Taxation Relief (CARICOM) Treaty

On 6 July 1994 at Sherbourne Conference Centre in [Barbados](3455.md), a representative of the Government of St. Vincent and the Grenadines, James Mitchell signed the [Double Taxation Relief (CARICOM) Treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_treaty).[^cite_note-ird.gov.tt-52] There were seven other signatories to the agreement on that day. The countries which were represented were [Antigua and Barbuda](951.md), [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize), [Grenada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenada), [Jamaica](15660.md), [Saint Kitts and Nevis](27198.md), [Saint Lucia](27208.md), and [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md).[^cite_note-JIS-55] An eighth country, [Guyana](17238691.md) signed the agreement on 19 August 2016. This treaty covered taxes, residence, tax jurisdictions, capital gains, business profits, interest, dividends, royalties and other areas.[^cite_note-ird.gov.tt-52]

##### FATCA

On 30 June 2014, St. Vincent and the Grenadines signed a Model 1 agreement with the United States of America with respect to Foreign Account Tax Compliance (Act) or FATCA.[^cite_note-56] According to the updated site as of 16 January 2017, on 13 May 2016 the agreement went to "In Force" status.

##### International and regional bodies to which Saint Vincent and the Grenadines belong

Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is a member of the [United Nations](31769.md), the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations), the [Organization of American States](22788.md), and the [Association of Caribbean States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Caribbean_States) (ACS).

In September 2017, at the [72nd Session of the UN General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventy-second_session_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly), the Prime Ministers of the Solomon Islands, Tuvalu, Vanuatu and Saint Vincent and the Grenadines called for UN action on alleged human rights abuses committed by [Indonesia](14579.md) on [Western New Guinea](1432822.md)'s [indigenous Papuans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_people_of_New_Guinea).[^cite_note-57] In [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_United_Nations_Security_Council_election), Saint Vincent and the Grenadines became the smallest country to ever be elected to the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council).

##### Organisation of American States

St Vincent and the Grenadines joined the [Organisation of American States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_of_American_States) on 27 October 1981.[^cite_note-58] It participates in the [Summits of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summits_of_the_Americas) and the Indigenous Leaders’ Summits of Americas.[^cite_note-59] In June 2022, St Vincent and the Grenadines boycotted the [9th Summit of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Summit_of_the_Americas).[^cite_note-60]

#### European nations

In 2013, Saint Vincent called for European nations to pay reparations for the [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery).[^cite_note-61] Upon a visit in April 2022, the British [Duke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Edward,_Duke_of_Edinburgh) and [Duchess of Edinburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophie,_Duchess_of_Edinburgh) were confronted with protesters calling for reparations for Britain's participation in the slave trade. Among the protesters was Jomo Thomas, former chair of the St. Vincent and the Grenadines National Reparations Committee, who called for reparations from the former colonial power.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

#### Venezuela

Saint Vincent protests against [Venezuela](32374.md)'s claim to give full effect to [Aves (Bird) Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aves_\(Bird\)_Island), which creates a Venezuelan [EEZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EEZ)/[continental shelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_shelf) extending over a large portion of the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md).[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16]

### Military

Main article: [Military of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines)

Saint Vincent has no formal armed forces, although the [Royal Saint Vincent and the Grenadines Police Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines_Police_Force) includes a Special Service Unit as well as a militia that has a supporting role on the island.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

In 2017, Saint Vincent signed the UN [treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons).[^cite_note-66]

### LGBT rights

Main article: [LGBT rights in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines)

"Acts of gross indecency", which may be defined to include [homosexual activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homosexual_activity), are illegal in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines.[^cite_note-67] Section 148 of the Criminal Code states:

> &#x20;Any person, who in public or private, commits an act of gross indecency with another person of the same sex, or procures or attempts to procure another person of the same sex to commit an act of gross indecency with him or her, is guilty of an offence and liable to imprisonment for five years.[^cite_note-68]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines)

Agriculture, dominated by [banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana) production, is the most important sector of this lower-middle-income economy. The services sector, based mostly on a growing tourist industry, is also important. The government has been relatively unsuccessful at introducing new industries, and the [unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_rate) remains high at 19.8% in the 1991 census[^cite_note-popin-69] to 15% in 2001.[^cite_note-cia-70] The continuing dependence on a single crop represents the biggest obstacle to the islands' development as tropical storms wiped out substantial portions of bananas in many years.

There is a small manufacturing sector and a small offshore financial sector serving international businesses; its secrecy laws have caused some international concern. There are increasing demands for international financial services like stock exchange and financial intermediaries financial activities in the country. In addition, the natives of [Bequia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bequia) are permitted to hunt up to four [humpback whales](231728.md) per year under [IWC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Whaling_Commission) subsistence quotas.[^cite_note-71]

### Communications

Main article: [Telecommunications in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telecommunications_in_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines)

In 2010, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines had 21,700 telephone land lines. Its land telephone system is fully automatic, covering the entire island and all of the inhabited Grenadine islands.[^cite_note-cia-70] In 2002, there were 10,000 mobile phones.[^cite_note-about-76] By 2010, this number had increased to 131,800.[^cite_note-cia-70] Mobile phone service is available in most areas of Saint Vincent as well as the Grenadines.[^cite_note-77]

Saint Vincent has two ISPs ([Digicel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digicel), [Flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_\(brand\))) that provide cellular telephone and internet service.[^cite_note-tourism-78]

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines)

The population as estimated in 2021 was 104,332.[^cite_note-UN_WPP-79][^cite_note-UN_WPP_2022-80] The ethnic composition was 66% [African descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Vincentians), 19% of mixed descent, 6% East Indian, 4% [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Caribbeans) (mainly Portuguese), 2% [Kalinago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinago) and 3% others.[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook_–_St_Vincent-16] Most Vincentians are the descendants of West-Central African people brought to the island to work on [plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation). There are other ethnic groups, such as [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) (from [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira)) and [East Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Vincentian), both brought in to work on the plantations after the abolishing of slavery by the British living on the island. There is also a growing Chinese population.[^cite_note-81]

### Languages

Main article: [Vincentian Creole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vincentian_Creole)

English is the official language. Most Vincentians speak Vincentian Creole.[^cite_note-82] English is used in education, government, religion, and other formal domains, while Creole (or 'dialect' as it is referred to locally) is used in informal situations, such as in the home and among friends.[^cite_note-83]

[Assumption Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assumption_Cathedral,_Kingstown), Kingstown

According to the 2023 census, 78.1% of the population of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines identified themselves as [Christian](5211.md), a modest decrease from 82.3% in 2012 census. The percentage of people with No religious affiliation nearly doubled from 7.5% in 2012 to 14.0% in 2023.[^cite_note-2001_census-84][^cite_note-\:0-85]

Though Christianity remains the dominant faith, the denominational composition of the country has changed drastically between 2001 and 2023. Traditional mainline churches like the [Anglican church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_church), [Methodist church](20119.md), and [Roman Catholic](606848.md) experienced steady declines, with Anglicans falling from 17.8% to 10.5%, Methodists from 10.9% to 6.3%, and Roman Catholics from 7.5% to 4.4%. Meanwhile, Pentecostalism grew substantially from 17.6% to 26.4%, becoming the largest Christian denomination in the country, [Evangelicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) almost doubled their share from 2.8% to 4.3%. [Seventh-Day Adventists](28632.md) grow marginally from 10.2% to 11.6% of the population.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-\:0-85][^cite_note-87]

[Rastafarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastafari) is the largest non-Christian faith in the country followed by 0.8% of the population, a decline from 1.5% from 2001. Other minority faiths include Muslim whose share increased to 0.2% from 0.1 % and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), which remained largely unchanged at around 0.1% of the population in both censuses.[^cite_note-\:0-85][^cite_note-88]

## Culture



The island of [Mustique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustique) in the [Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grenadines)

### Music

Main article: [Music of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines)

Music popular in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines includes big drum, [calypso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calypso_music), [soca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soca_music), [steelpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steelpan) and [reggae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggae). String band music, quadrille and traditional storytelling are also popular. One of the most successful St Vincent natives is [Kevin Lyttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Lyttle). He was named Cultural Ambassador for the Island 19 September 2013.[^cite_note-89] The national anthem of Saint Vincent and the Grenadines is "[Saint Vincent, Land so beautiful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent,_Land_so_beautiful)", adopted upon independence in 1979.[^cite_note-90]

### Media

See also: [List of newspapers in Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_newspapers_in_Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines)

Saint Vincent has twelve FM radio stations: 88.9 Adoration Fm,[^cite_note-91] 89.1 Jem Radio, 89.7 NBC Radio, 95.7 and 105.7 [Praise FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praise_FM_\(Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines\)), 96.7 Nice Radio, 97.1 Hot 97, 98.3 Star FM, 99.9 We FM, 103.7 Hitz, 102.7 EZee radio, 104.3 Xtreme FM and 106.9 Boom FM. There are several Internet radio stations including Chronicles Christian Radio.[^cite_note-92] It has one television broadcast station ZBG-TV (SVGTV)[^cite_note-svgtv-93] and one cable television provider.

St Vincent and the Grenadines Broadcasting Corporation is the parent company for SVGTV, Magic 103.7.[^cite_note-94]

### Sport

See also: [Cricket in the West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_in_the_West_Indies)

[Cricket](25675557.md), [association football](10568.md), and athletics are most popular among men whereas [netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netball) is most popular among women. Basketball, volleyball, rugby and tennis are also very popular.[^cite_note-95]

The country's prime [football](10568.md) league is the [NLA Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NLA_Premier_League), which provides its [national (association) football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines_national_football_team) with most players. A notable Vincentian footballer is [Ezra Hendrickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezra_Hendrickson), former national team captain who played at several Major League Soccer clubs in the United States and was head coach with the [Chicago Fire FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Fire_FC) from 2021–23.[^cite_note-fire-96]

The country regularly participates at the [Caribbean Basketball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Basketball_Championship) where a [men's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines_national_basketball_team) and a [women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines_women's_national_basketball_team) compete. Saint Vincent and the Grenadines also has its own [national rugby union team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_and_the_Grenadines_national_rugby_union_team) which is ranked 84th in the world. Other notable sports played at the regional level include track and field. Natasha Mayers won a gold medal in the [100m](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100m) at the [2010 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Commonwealth_Games).[^cite_note-97] [Kineke Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kineke_Alexander) won a bronze medal in the women's \[400m] at the [2015 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Pan_American_Games).[^cite_note-98] [Eswort Coombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eswort_Coombs) got a bronze medal in the 400m at the [1995 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Pan_American_Games).

At the Olympics Games France 2024, Saint Vincent and the Grenadines had two athletes, Shafiqua Maloney and Handal Roban.[^cite_note-99] Maloney became the first Vincentian athlete to reach the final stage of an Olympic event[^cite_note-100] when she placed 4th in the women's 800m.
