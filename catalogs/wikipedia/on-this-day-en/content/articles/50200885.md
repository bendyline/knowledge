**Sushila Karki**[^cite_note-1] (born 7 June 1952) is a Nepalese lawyer and stateswoman who served as [chief justice of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_justice_of_Nepal) from 2016 to 2017 and as interim [prime minister of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Nepal) from 2025 to 2026. She became prime minister following the [youth protests of September 2025](81013780.md) that led to her predecessor's resignation.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] Karki is the first female prime minister and female chief justice in Nepalese history.[^cite_note-thehimalayantimes.com-4][^cite_note-indianexpress-5]

## Early life and education

Karki was born on 7 June 1952 to a [Chhetri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhetri) family from Sankarpur (later part of [Biratnagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biratnagar)[^cite_note-\:11-6]), Nepal.[^cite_note-supremecourt-7][^cite_note-\:2-8] She is the eldest of her family's seven children.[^cite_note-ndtv-9]

Karki attended [Tribhuvan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribhuvan_University), earning a Bachelor of Arts from [Mahendra Morang College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahendra_Morang_Adarsh_Multiple_Campus) in 1972. She then studied political science in [India](14533.md), and in 1975 received a master's in political science from [Banaras Hindu University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banaras_Hindu_University). She returned to Tribhuvan University to study law, graduating in 1978.[^cite_note-\:0-10][^cite_note-supremecourt-7][^cite_note-thehindu.com-11]

## Early career

From 1986 to 1989, Karki worked as assistant teacher at [Mahendra Multiple Campus, Dharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahendra_Multiple_Campus,_Dharan); from 1988, she concurrently was [the bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(law\)) president of the Koshi Zonal Court until 1990.[^cite_note-supremecourt-7][^cite_note-\:11-6] That year, she participated in the [1990 People's Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_People's_Movement) to overthrow the [Panchayat regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayat_regime) and was imprisoned in Biratnagar Jail. She later wrote the novel *[Kara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_\(novel\))* inspired by her experiences.[^cite_note-\:1-12] In 2002, she was made president of the Biratnagar Appellate Court, a role she held until 2004, when she became a senior Advocate at the [Nepal Bar Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepal_Bar_Association).[^cite_note-supremecourt-7][^cite_note-\:11-6]

## Supreme Court of Nepal (2009–2017)

Karki was appointed an *ad hoc* justice of the [Supreme Court of Nepal](2707227.md) in 2009. Her position was made permanent the following year.[^cite_note-\:0-10][^cite_note-supremecourt-7] In March 2016, the Supreme Court heard a [writ petition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writ_petition) over the appointment of [Khil Raj Regmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khil_Raj_Regmi) as interim Prime Minister. The court held that the petition, originally filed in 2013, was no longer relevant and dismissed it; Karki and Chief Justice [Kalyan Shrestha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalyan_Shrestha) dissented and found that the appointment of Regmi was unconstitutional. Karki later argued that Regmi's appointment had caused lasting damage to the [Nepalese judiciary system](2707227.md).[^cite_note-\:9-13]

After the retirement of Chief Justice Shrestha in April 2016, Karki was recommended to take over the role by the Constitutional Council. She served on an *ad hoc* basis until a formal parliamentary hearing the following July confirmed her appointment.[^cite_note-ndtv-9][^cite_note-supremecourt-7] She was the first female Chief Justice and known at the time of her appointment for being strict and anti-corruption.[^cite_note-14] She faced opposition for those attributes during her tenure, and was accused by the government of working against them after the Supreme Court overturned the appointment of Jaya Bahadur Chand as [Chief of Nepal Police](14833748.md).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-\:3-16][^cite_note-\:0-10] Impeachment proceedings, which became easier to initiate after the 2015 adoption of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Nepal),[^cite_note-\:8-17][^cite_note-\:9-13] were started against her in Parliament in April 2017 by [Nepali Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_Congress) and [CPN (Maoist Centre)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPN_\(Maoist_Centre\)); she was automatically suspended.[^cite_note-\:0-10][^cite_note-\:3-16][^cite_note-\:52-18]

According to *[The Himalayan Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Himalayan_Times)*, the impeachment proceedings were viewed by many to be "politically motivated, intended to thwart the verdicts on some high-profile cases"[^cite_note-\:3-16] and [United Nations](31769.md) High Commissioner for Human Rights [Zeid Ra'ad al-Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeid_Ra'ad_al-Hussein) said that "the attempt to remove her gives rise to serious concerns about the Government's commitment to transitional justice and the rule of law".[^cite_note-19] Dissatisfied with decision to impeach her, the then [deputy prime minister](58719229.md) and [home minister](69917312.md), [Bimalendra Nidhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bimalendra_Nidhi) resigned[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] and the [Rastriya Prajatantra Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastriya_Prajatantra_Party) left the coalition it had formed with Nepali Congress and CPN (MC).[^cite_note-\:52-18] In May, [Cholendra Shumsher JB Rana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholendra_Shumsher_JB_Rana) of the Supreme Court issued a stay against the proceedings.[^cite_note-\:3-16] Due to public pressure a deal was made during a cabinet reshuffle, and the ruling parties withdrew.[^cite_note-\:8-17][^cite_note-\:2-8] Karki resigned 6 June 2017 on reaching the [mandatory retirement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_retirement) age of 65.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

After her retirement from the court, Karki wrote an [autobiography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autobiography) about her early life and career as a judge. Published as *[Nyaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyaya_\(book\))*, Karki argued that democracy relied on the independence of the [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary).[^cite_note-25] The next year, she published her novel *Kara*.[^cite_note-\:1-12]

## Interim prime minister of Nepal (2025–2026)

Main article: [Karki interim cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karki_interim_cabinet)

After the 2025 [Generation Z](4015968.md)-led [anti-corruption protests](81013780.md) forced Prime Minister [K. P. Sharma Oli](35540625.md) to resign, an [interim government](598010.md) was needed and Karki's name was suggested by activists due to her political neutrality.[^cite_note-30] Following a poll on the online communication platform [Discord](49271581.md), protester server members selected Karki out of five options, making her the first government official to be selected online.[^cite_note-\:10-31] The appointment was agreed upon during talks with the Nepalese army.[^cite_note-\:22-32][^cite_note-\:10-31][^cite_note-\:5-33]

Upon her recommendation, president [Ram Chandra Poudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram_Chandra_Poudel) dissolved the [Federal Parliament of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Parliament_of_Nepal) on 12 September,[^cite_note-\:5-33][^cite_note-34] and Karki was sworn in as interim Prime Minister based on article 61 of the [Constitution of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Nepal).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] She is the first woman in Nepal's history to hold the position of Prime Minister.[^cite_note-\:5-33]

### Tenure

Karki unveiled her [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karki_interim_cabinet) beginning 15 September 2025.[^cite_note-37] After taking her oath on 12 September, Karki vowed to bring peace, good governance, end corruption and conduct the fair [general election](69672716.md) in Nepal in six months starting in March 2026.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] In the same statement, she added those who died in the protests will be considered "martyrs".[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] After her swearing-in, [Prime Minister of India](24452.md) [Narendra Modi](444222.md), [China’s foreign ministry](7453497.md) spokesperson, and [US Ambassador to Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_the_United_States_to_Nepal) [Dean R. Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_R._Thompson) congratulated Karki on assuming the office of Prime Minister and affirmed their support to the interim government for peace and stability.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] On 13 September, Karki visited the hospitals of Kathmandu and met the injured Gen Z protesters, expressing solidarity and assuring them of government support.[^cite_note-49] On 14 September, Karki promised to investigate vandalism during the protests and announced compensation to the injured protesters of [NPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepalese_rupee) 1 million (\$7,000). Families, however, feel that further steps are required to appropriately honor the sacrifice made by those deceased.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] On 15 September, Khenpo Sonam Tenphel, Speaker of the Tibetan Parliament-in-Exile and Sikyong Penpa Tsering of the Central Tibetan Administration extended his heartfelt congratulations to Karki. Both highlighted the close relationship between the Nepalese and Tibetans and expressed gratitude to country's government and citizens for providing facilities for Tibetan exiles' rehabilitation since 1959.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

On 25 September, Karki declared that government projects which were poorly planned and carried out on a modest budget or scale by the previous government will be abandoned. In order to raise money for repairing the infrastructure that was harmed during the Gen Z protests, she also announced the creation of a reconstruction fund.[^cite_note-54] She further announced that the minimum voting age has been lowered from 18 to 16 years to increase youth voter participation for the upcoming election.[^cite_note-55] On 29 September, Karki Government suspended and froze the passports of ex-prime minister [K. P. Sharma Oli](35540625.md), ex-Home Minister [Ramesh Lekhak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesh_Lekhak), along with three other government officials who worked under him including then-home secretary Gokarna Mani Duwadi, then-National Investigation Department chief Hutaraj Thapa, and then-chief district officer of Kathmandu, Chhabi Rijal.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] By 15 October, 14 writ petitions had been filed in the [Supreme Court](2707227.md) against Karki and her government.[^cite_note-62]

On October 18, Karki further briefed international diplomats about the [March 2026 parliamentary elections](69672716.md) and stressed her government’s focus on transparency, anti-corruption measures, and restoring stability.[^cite_note-63] On 21 October, Karki met leaders from seven political parties to discuss the 2026 elections.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

## Second retirement (2026-present)

On 27 March 2026, Sushila Karki stepped down as [Nepal's interim Prime Minister](2888818.md) after successfully overseeing the country's transition through a political crisis and conducting general elections. Having always intended to serve only temporarily, she handed power to the [newly elected government](82600702.md) and returned to the retirement she had left in September 2025. Since leaving office, she has not held any further [government](21326.md) or [judicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) position and has resumed retirement.[^cite_note-68]

## Personal life

Karki married [Durga Prasad Subedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga_Prasad_Subedi), who as a youth wing leader of [Nepali Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_Congress) was one of the perpetrators behind the [1973 Royal Nepal Airlines DHC-6 hijacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Royal_Nepal_Airlines_DHC-6_hijacking).[^cite_note-\:6-29][^cite_note-\:22-32][^cite_note-\:4-69][^cite_note-70] They met while studying in Banaras Hindu University,[^cite_note-71] and he was her tutor.[^cite_note-\:6-29] They have at least one child.[^cite_note-\:4-69]

In addition to her native [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language), Karki speaks some [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language).[^cite_note-\:0-10][^cite_note-supremecourt-7]

## Recognitions

In 2026, Sushila Karki was honored with the *Nagarik Nayak-2083* award by [Nepal Republic Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media_in_Nepal) for her contributions to the justice and anti-corruption efforts.[^cite_note-72]
