**Las Vegas**,[^cite_note-11] [colloquially](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/colloquial) shortened to **Vegas**, is the most populous city in the U.S. state of [Nevada](21216.md) and the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Clark County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County,_Nevada). It is the [24th-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) in the United States, with 641,903 residents at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-QuickFacts-12] The [Las Vegas metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley) has an estimated 2.4 million residents and is the [29th-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) in the country. Las Vegas is an internationally renowned major [resort city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resort_city), known primarily for its [gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling), shopping, fine dining, entertainment, and [nightlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightlife). Most of these venues are located in [downtown Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Las_Vegas) or on the [Las Vegas Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip), which is outside city limits in the [unincorporated towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_towns_in_Nevada) of [Paradise](124384.md) and [Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester,_Nevada). The Las Vegas Valley serves as the leading financial, commercial, and cultural center in Nevada.

Las Vegas was settled in 1905 and officially incorporated in 1911.[^cite_note-13] At the close of the 20th century, it was the most populated North American city founded within that century. Population growth has accelerated since the 1960s and into the 21st century, and between 1990 and 2000 the population increased by 85.2%.

The city bills itself as [the Entertainment Capital of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Entertainment_Capital_of_the_World), and is famous for its luxurious and large casino-hotels. As of 2023, Las Vegas attracts over 40.8 million visitors annually,[^cite_note-14] making it one of the [most visited cities in the United States](577095.md) and consistently ranking among the world's top tourist destinations.[^cite_note-TravelandTourism-15][^cite_note-16] It is the third most popular U.S. destination for business conventions[^cite_note-17] and a global leader in the [hospitality industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospitality_industry).[^cite_note-18] The city's tolerance for numerous forms of [adult entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_entertainment) has earned it the nickname "[Sin City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Sin_Cities)",[^cite_note-19] and has made it a popular [setting for films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_set_in_Las_Vegas), literature, [television programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_shows_set_in_Las_Vegas), commercials and music videos.

## Toponymy

In 1829, Mexican trader and explorer [Antonio Armijo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Armijo) led a group consisting of 60 men and 100 mules along the [Old Spanish Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Spanish_Trail_\(trade_route\)) from modern day New Mexico to California. Along the way, the group stopped in what would become Las Vegas and noted its natural water sources, now referred to as the [Las Vegas Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Springs), which supported extensive vegetation such as grasses and [mesquite trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesquite). The springs were a significant natural feature in the valley, with streams that supported a meadow ecosystem. This region served as the winter residence for the [Southern Paiute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Paiute_people) people, who utilized the area's resources before moving to higher elevations during the summer months. The Spanish "las vegas" or "the meadows" (more precisely, lower land near a river) in English, was applied to describe the fertile lowlands near the springs. Over time, the name began to refer to the populated settlement.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

## History

See also: [Las Vegas in the 1940s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_in_the_1940s) and [Las Vegas in the 1950s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_in_the_1950s)



Southern [Paiutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moapa_Band_of_Paiute_Indians) at [Moapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moapa_River_Indian_Reservation) wearing traditional Paiute basket hats with Paiute cradleboard and rabbit robe

Nomadic [Paleo-Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians) traveled to the Las Vegas area 10,000 years ago, leaving behind [petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph). [Ancient Puebloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Pueblo_peoples) and [Paiute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Paiute) tribes followed at least 2,000 years ago.[^cite_note-23]



[Equestrian statue of Rafael Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrian_statue_of_Rafael_Rivera), the first non-native to arrive in Las Vegas

A young [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_people) scout named [Rafael Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Rivera) is credited as the first non-[Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) to encounter the valley, in 1829.[^cite_note-24] Trader [Antonio Armijo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Armijo) led a 60-man party along the [Spanish Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Spanish_Trail_\(trade_route\)) to [Los Angeles](18110.md), California, in 1829.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] In 1844, [John C. Frémont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Fr%C3%A9mont) arrived, and his writings helped lure pioneers to the area. Downtown Las Vegas's Fremont Street is named after him.

Eleven years later, members of [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) chose Las Vegas as the site to build a fort along the [Mormon Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon_Road), which ran between [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City) and southern California; the fort was abandoned in 1857. The remainder of this [Old Mormon Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Las_Vegas_Mormon_Fort_State_Historic_Park) can still be seen at the intersection of Las Vegas Boulevard and Washington Avenue.[^cite_note-27]

Las Vegas was founded as a city in 1905, when 110 acres (45 ha) of land adjacent to the [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad) tracks were auctioned in what would become the downtown area. In 1911, Las Vegas was incorporated as a city.[^cite_note-28]



[Golden Nugget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Nugget_Las_Vegas) and [Pioneer Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Club_Las_Vegas) along [Fremont Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_Street) in 1952



This view of downtown Las Vegas shows a [mushroom cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushroom_cloud) in the background. Scenes such as this were typical during the 1950s. From 1951 to 1962, the government conducted 100 atmospheric tests at the nearby [Nevada Test Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_Test_Site).

[^cite_note-simon-29]

The year 1931 was pivotal for Las Vegas. At that time, Nevada legalized casino gambling[^cite_note-30] and reduced residency requirements for divorce to six weeks.[^cite_note-31] This year also witnessed the beginning of construction of the tunnels of nearby [Hoover Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoover_Dam). The influx of construction workers and their families helped Las Vegas avoid economic calamity during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression). The construction work was completed in 1935.

In late 1941, [Las Vegas Army Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nellis_Air_Force_Base#History) was established. Renamed [Nellis Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nellis_Air_Force_Base) in 1950, it is now home to the [United States Air Force Thunderbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Thunderbirds) aerobatic team.[^cite_note-32]

Following World War II, lavishly decorated hotels, gambling casinos, and big-name entertainment became synonymous with Las Vegas.[^cite_note-33]

In 1951, [nuclear weapons testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_testing) began at the [Nevada Test Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_Test_Site), 65 miles (105 km) northwest of Las Vegas. During this time, the city was nicknamed the ["Atomic City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Las_Vegas#Atomic_testing)". Residents and visitors were able to witness the mushroom clouds (and were exposed to the fallout) until 1963 when the [Partial Nuclear Test Ban Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_Nuclear_Test_Ban_Treaty) required that nuclear tests be moved underground.[^cite_note-simon-29]

In 1955, the [Moulin Rouge Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moulin_Rouge_Hotel) opened and became the first racially integrated casino-hotel in Las Vegas.[^cite_note-34]

In 1995, the [Fremont Street Experience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_Street_Experience) opened in Las Vegas's downtown area. This canopied five-block area features 12.5 million LED lights and 550,000 watts of sound from dusk until midnight during shows held at the top of each hour.[^cite_note-35]

Due to the realization of many revitalization efforts, 2012 was dubbed "The Year of Downtown". Projects worth hundreds of millions of dollars made their debut at this time, including the [Smith Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts), the [Discovery Children's Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_Children's_Museum), the [Mob Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mob_Museum), the [Neon Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_Museum), a [new City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_City_Hall) complex, and renovations for a new [Zappos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zappos).com corporate headquarters in the [old City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_City_Hall_\(1973\)) building.[^cite_note-History-36][^cite_note-37]

## Geography



Astronaut photograph of Las Vegas at night



[Downtown Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Las_Vegas) with [Red Rock Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Rock_Canyon_National_Conservation_Area) in the background

Las Vegas is situated in a [basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basin_and_range_topography) on the floor of the [Mojave Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojave_Desert),[^cite_note-38] and is surrounded by mountain ranges. Much of the landscape is rocky and arid, with desert vegetation and wildlife. It can be subjected to torrential flash floods, although much has been done to mitigate the effects of flash floods through improved drainage systems.[^cite_note-39]

The city's elevation is approximately 2,030 ft (620 m) above sea level, though the surrounding peaks reach elevations of over 10,000 feet (3,000 m) and act as barriers to the strong flow of moisture from the surrounding area. According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has an area of 135.86 sq mi (351.9 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 135.81 sq mi (351.7 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 0.05 sq mi (0.13 km<sup>2</sup>) (0.03%) is water.[^cite_note-40]

After Alaska and California, Nevada is the third most seismically active state in the U.S. It has been estimated by the United States Geological Survey (USGS) that over the next 50 years, there is a 10–20% chance of an M6.0 or greater earthquake occurring within 50 km (31 mi) of Las Vegas.[^cite_note-41]

Within the city are many lawns, trees, and other greenery. Due to water resource issues, there has been a movement to encourage [xeriscapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xeriscaping). Another part of conservation efforts is scheduled watering days for residential landscaping. A [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Environmental_Protection_Agency) grant in 2008 funded a program that analyzed and forecast growth and environmental effects through 2019.[^cite_note-42]

### Climate



Desert scene at the [Red Rock Canyon National Conservation Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Rock_Canyon_National_Conservation_Area) in the Las Vegas area



Spring flowers at the [Red Rock Canyon National Conservation Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Rock_Canyon_National_Conservation_Area) in the Las Vegas area

Las Vegas has a [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_climate) [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BWh*, [Trewartha climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification) *BWhk*), typical of the [Mojave Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojave_Desert) in which it lies. This climate is typified by long, extremely hot summers; warm transitional seasons; and short winters with mild days and cool nights. There is abundant sunshine throughout the year, with an average of 310 sunny days and bright sunshine during 86% of all daylight hours.[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Las_Vegas,_NV_(VEF)_-_LASthr-43][^cite_note-noaasun-44] Rainfall is scarce, with an average of 4.2 in (110 mm) dispersed between roughly 26 total rainy days per year.[^cite_note-NCEI_Summary_of_Monthly_Normals_-_1991-2020-45] Las Vegas is among the sunniest, driest, and least humid locations in North America, with exceptionally low dew points and humidity that sometimes remains below 10%.[^cite_note-46]

The summer months of June through September are extremely hot, though moderated by the low humidity levels. July is the hottest month, with an average daytime high of 104.5 °F (40.3 °C). On average, 137 days per year reach or exceed 90 °F (32 °C), of which 78 days reach 100 °F (38 °C) and 10 days reach 110 °F (43 °C). During the peak intensity of summer, overnight lows frequently remain above 80 °F (27 °C), and occasionally above 85 °F (29 °C).[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Las_Vegas,_NV_(VEF)_-_LASthr-43]

While most summer days are consistently hot, dry, and cloudless, the [North American Monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Monsoon) sporadically interrupts this pattern and brings more cloud cover, thunderstorms, lightning, increased humidity, and brief spells of heavy rain. Potential monsoons affect Las Vegas between July and August. Summer in Las Vegas is marked by significant [diurnal temperature variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_temperature_variation). While less extreme than other parts of the state, nighttime lows in Las Vegas are often 30 °F (16.7 °C) or more lower than daytime highs.[^cite_note-47] The average hottest night of the year is 90 °F (32 °C). The all-time record is at 95 °F (35 °C).[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Las_Vegas,_NV_(VEF)_-_LASthr-43]

Las Vegas winters are relatively short, with typically mild daytime temperatures and chilly nights. Sunshine is abundant in all seasons. December is both the year's coolest and cloudiest month, with an average daytime high of 56.9 °F (13.8 °C) and sunshine occurring during 78% of its daylight hours. Winter evenings are defined by clear skies and swift drops in temperature after sunset, with overnight minima averaging around 40 °F (4.4 °C) in December and January. Owing to its elevation that ranges from 2,000 to 3,000 feet (610 to 910 m), Las Vegas experiences markedly cooler winters than other areas of the [Mojave Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojave_Desert) and the adjacent [Sonoran Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoran_Desert) that are closer to sea level. The city records freezing temperatures an average of 10 nights per winter. It is exceptionally rare for temperatures to reach or fall below 25 °F (−4 °C).[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Las_Vegas,_NV_(VEF)_-_LASthr-43]



Climate chart for Las Vegas

Most of the annual precipitation falls during the winter. February, the wettest month, averages only four days of measurable rain. The mountains immediately surrounding the Las Vegas Valley accumulate snow every winter, but significant accumulation within the city is rare, although moderate accumulations occur every few years. The most recent accumulations occurred on February 18, 2019, when parts of the city received about 1 to 2 inches (2.5 to 5.1 cm) of snow[^cite_note-48] and on February 20 when the city received almost 0.5 inches (1.3 cm).[^cite_note-49] Other recent significant snow accumulations occurred on December 25, 2015, and December 17, 2008.[^cite_note-50] Unofficially, Las Vegas's largest snowfall on record was the 12 inches (30 cm) that fell in 1909.[^cite_note-51] In recent times, ice days have not occurred, although 29 °F (−2 °C) was measured in 1963.[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Las_Vegas,_NV_(VEF)_-_LASthr-43] On average the coldest day is 44 °F (7 °C).[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Las_Vegas,_NV_(VEF)_-_LASthr-43]

The highest temperature officially observed for Las Vegas is 120 °F (48.9 °C), as measured at [Harry Reid International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Reid_International_Airport) on July 7, 2024.[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Las_Vegas,_NV_(VEF)_-_LASthr-43][^cite_note-52] The lowest temperature was 8 °F (−13 °C), recorded on two days: January 25, 1937, and January 13, 1963.[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Las_Vegas,_NV_(VEF)_-_LASthr-43] The official record hot daily minimum is 95 °F (35 °C) on July 19, 2005, and July 1, 2013. The official record cold daily maximum is 28 °F (−2 °C) on January 8 and 21, 1937.[^cite_note-NOWData_NWS_Las_Vegas,_NV_(VEF)_-_LASthr-43] July 2024 was the hottest month ever recorded in Las Vegas, with its highest recorded mean daily average temperature over the month of 99.9 °F (38 °C), its highest recorded mean daily maximum temperature of 111.5 °F (44 °C), and its highest recorded mean nightly minimum temperature of 88.3 °F (31 °C).[^cite_note-LasVegasHottestMonthEver-53]

Due to concerns about [climate change](18246585.md) in the wake of a 2002 drought, daily water consumption has been reduced from 314 US gallons (1,190 L; 261 imp gal) per resident in 2003 to around 205 US gallons (780 L; 171 imp gal) in 2015.[^cite_note-\:0-54]

### 2020 census

According to the [2020 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the city of Las Vegas had 644,883 people living in 244,429 [households](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household). The racial composition of the City of Las Vegas was 49.2% [white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Americans), 11.9% [black](2154.md), 1.1% [American Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) or [Alaska Native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Natives), 6.9% Asian, [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) residents of any race were 34.1% and 16.2% from [two or more races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_Americans). 40.8% were [non-Hispanic white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_whites).[^cite_note-\:4-62]

Approximately 5.8% of residents are under the age of five, 22.8% under the age of eighteen and 15.6% over 65 years old. Females are 50.0% of the total population.[^cite_note-\:4-62]



Map of racial distribution in the Las Vegas area, 2020 U.S. Census. Each dot is 25 people: ⬤ White ⬤ Black ⬤ Asian ⬤ Hispanic ⬤ Native American ⬤ Mixed or other

From 2019 to 2023, Las Vegas had approximately 244,429 [households](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Household), with an average of 2.63 persons per household. About 55.7% of housing units were owner-occupied, and the median value of owner-occupied housing was \$395,300. Median gross rent during this period was \$1,456 per month (in 2023 dollars).[^cite_note-\:4-62]

The median [household income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disposable_household_and_per_capita_income) in Las Vegas from 2019 to 2023 was \$70,723, while the [per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita) income was \$38,421 (in 2023 dollars). Approximately 14.2% of the population lived below the poverty line during the same period.[^cite_note-\:4-62]

Residents over 25 years old with a [high school diploma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_diploma) were 85.8% of the population with 27.3% having attained a [bachelor's degree or higher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education).[^cite_note-\:4-62]

About 33.0% of residents aged 5 and older speak a language other than English at home. 20.9% of residents are foreign-born.[^cite_note-\:4-62]

The mean travel time to work for residents aged 16 and older was approximately 25.8 minutes between 2019 and 2023. The vast majority of households in Las Vegas are [digitally connected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_access), with 95.6% having a computer and 89.1% subscribing to [broadband internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadband) services.[^cite_note-63]

### Ethnicity

See also: [Clark County, Nevada § Demographics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County,_Nevada#Demographics)

The largest ancestries in [Las Vegas Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley)/[Clark County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County,_Nevada) were [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Americans) (23.2%), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) (7.4%), [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Americans) (6.8%), [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Americans) (6.8%), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans) (6.2%), and [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Americans) (4.5%).[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] As of 2023, [Native Hawaiians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiians) numbered around 22,000 in the metropolitan area,[^cite_note-67] the largest Hawaiian population outside Hawaii. As a result, Las Vegas has been nicknamed the "ninth island of [Hawaii](13270.md)."

### Religion

According to the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), the majority of residents throughout [Las Vegas Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley)/[Clark County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County,_Nevada) are [Christian](5211.md) (61%) with [Catholics](606848.md) as the largest [denomination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_denomination) (22%), followed by [evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) (19%), [mainline Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainline_Protestant) (9%), and members of [historically Black Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_church) churches (6%). 6% of residents are members of the [Church of Jesus Christ of Latter Day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) ([Mormons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormons)).[^cite_note-\:5-68]

Agnostic (10%) and atheist (3%) are the largest groups of residents who are unaffiliated with a religion, which overall represent 34% of the population. Other religious groups, such as [Judaism](15624.md) (1%), [Islam](6037917.md) (2%), and [Buddhism](3267529.md) (1%) together compose about 5% of the population.[^cite_note-\:5-68]

### Marriage and divorce

See also: [Las Vegas weddings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_weddings)

According to a 2004 study, Las Vegas has one of the highest divorce rates[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] This is partly due to residents of states with more restrictive divorce laws traveling to Nevada, and in particular to Las Vegas, to get divorced.[^cite_note-\:1-71] As of 2022, the [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md) identified Nevada as a whole as having the highest divorce rate in the United States, with 4.4. divorces per 1,000 total population. This is down from 11.4 divorces per 1,000 population in 1990 and is part of a national trend of fewer divorces.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

Nevada marriage requirements are equally lax resulting in one of the highest marriage rates of U.S. cities, with many licenses issued to people from outside the area.[^cite_note-\:1-71]

## Economy

The primary drivers of the Las Vegas economy are tourism, [gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_\(Nevada_gaming_area\)), and conventions, which in turn feed the retail and restaurant industries.[^cite_note-79]

### Tourism



The [Golden Nugget Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Nugget_Las_Vegas)



The [Las Vegas Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip), primarily located in [Paradise](124384.md)



A view of the [Las Vegas Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley) looking north from the [Stratosphere Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratosphere_Tower)

The major attractions in Las Vegas are the casinos and the hotels, although in recent years other new attractions have begun to emerge.[^cite_note-80]

Most casinos in the downtown area are on [Fremont Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_Street), with [The STRAT Hotel, Casino & Skypod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratosphere_Las_Vegas) as one of the few exceptions. [Fremont East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_East), adjacent to the Fremont Street Experience, was granted variances to allow bars to be closer together, similar to the [Gaslamp Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaslamp_Quarter) of San Diego, the goal being to attract a different demographic than the Strip attracts.[^cite_note-81]

#### Downtown casinos

Main article: [Downtown (Nevada gaming area)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_\(Nevada_gaming_area\))

The [Golden Gate Hotel and Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Gate_Hotel_and_Casino), downtown along the Fremont Street Experience, is the oldest continuously operating hotel and casino in Las Vegas; it opened in 1906 as the Hotel Nevada.

In 1931, the [Northern Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Club_\(casino\)) (now the [La Bayou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Bayou)) opened.[^cite_note-lvrj20000727-82][^cite_note-83] The most notable of the early casinos may have been [Binion's Horseshoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binion's_Horseshoe) (now [Binion's Gambling Hall and Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binion's_Gambling_Hall_and_Hotel)) while it was run by [Benny Binion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Binion).[^cite_note-84]

[Boyd Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyd_Gaming) has a major presence downtown operating the [California Hotel & Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Hotel_&_Casino), the [Fremont Hotel & Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_Hotel_&_Casino), and the [Main Street Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Street_Casino). The [Four Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Queens) also operates downtown along the Fremont Street Experience.[^cite_note-85]

Downtown casinos that have undergone major renovations and revitalization in recent years include the [Golden Nugget Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Nugget_Las_Vegas), [The D Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_D_Las_Vegas) (formerly Fitzgerald's), the [Downtown Grand Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Grand) (formerly Lady Luck), the [El Cortez Hotel & Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cortez_\(Las_Vegas\)), and the [Plaza Hotel & Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Hotel_&_Casino).[^cite_note-86]

In 2020, [Circa Resort & Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circa_Resort_&_Casino) opened, becoming the first all-new hotel-casino to be built on Fremont Street since 1980.[^cite_note-Kat-87]

#### Las Vegas Strip

Main article: [Las Vegas Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip)

The center of the gambling and entertainment industry is the [Las Vegas Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip), outside the city limits in the surrounding unincorporated communities of [Paradise](124384.md) and [Winchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winchester,_Nevada) in Clark County. Some of the largest casinos and buildings are there.[^cite_note-LVS_2008-05-15-88]

#### Welcome signs



The original Welcome to Fabulous Las Vegas sign



Gateway Arches

In 1929, the city installed a welcome arch over [Fremont Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_Street), at the corner of Main Street.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] It remained in place until 1931.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

In 1959, the 25-foot-tall (7.6 m) [Welcome to Fabulous Las Vegas sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_to_Fabulous_Las_Vegas_sign) was installed at the south end of the [Las Vegas Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip). A replica [welcome sign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_sign), standing nearly 16 feet (4.9 m) tall, was installed within city limits in 2002, at [Las Vegas Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Boulevard) and Fourth Street.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] The replica was destroyed in 2016, when a pickup truck crashed into it.[^cite_note-97]

In 2018, the city approved plans for a new gateway landmark in the form of neon arches. It was built within city limits, in front of the [Strat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Strat) resort and north of [Sahara Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_Avenue).[^cite_note-98] The project, built by [YESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YESCO), cost \$6.5 million and stands 80 feet (24 m) high.[^cite_note-99] Officially known as the Gateway Arches, the project was completed in 2020. The steel arches are blue during the day, and light up in a variety of colors at night.[^cite_note-100]

Also located just north of the Strat are a pair of giant neon showgirls, initially added in 2018 as part of a \$400,000 welcome display. The original showgirls were 25 feet (7.6 m) tall, but were replaced by new ones in 2022, rising 50 feet (15 m).[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-Garcia-102] The originals were refurbished following weather damage and installed at the [Las Vegas Arts District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Arts_District).[^cite_note-Garcia-102][^cite_note-103]

### Development

See also: [List of tallest buildings in Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Las_Vegas)

When [The Mirage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mirage) opened in 1989, it started a trend of major resort development on the Las Vegas Strip outside the city. This resulted in a drop in tourism in the downtown area, but many recent projects have increased the number of visitors to downtown.[^cite_note-104]

An effort has been made by city officials to diversify the economy by attracting health-related, high-tech and other commercial interests. No state tax for individuals or corporations, as well as a lack of other forms of business-related taxes, have aided the success of these efforts.[^cite_note-105]

The Fremont Street Experience was built in an effort to draw tourists back to the area and has been popular since its startup in 1995.[^cite_note-106]

The city conducted a land-swap deal in 2000 with [Lehman Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehman_Brothers), acquiring 61 acres (25 ha) of property near downtown Las Vegas in exchange for 91 acres (37 ha) of the Las Vegas Technology Center.[^cite_note-107] In 2004, Las Vegas Mayor [Oscar Goodman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Goodman) announced that the area would become home to [Symphony Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_Park) (originally called "Union Park"[^cite_note-108]), a [mixed-use development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-use_development). The development is home to the [Cleveland Clinic Lou Ruvo Center for Brain Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Clinic_Lou_Ruvo_Center_for_Brain_Health), [The Smith Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Smith_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts), the [Discovery Children's Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_Children's_Museum), the Las Vegas [Chamber of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_commerce), and four residential projects totaling 600 residential units as of 2024.[^cite_note-109]



[World Market Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Market_Center_Las_Vegas) Building A

In 2005, the [World Market Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Market_Center) opened, consisting of three large buildings taking up 5,400,000 square feet (500,000 m<sup>2</sup>). Trade shows for the furniture and furnishing industries are held there semiannually.[^cite_note-110]

Also nearby is the Las Vegas North Premium Outlets. With a second expansion, completed in May 2015, the mall currently offers 175 stores.[^cite_note-111]

City offices moved to a new [Las Vegas City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_City_Hall) in February 2013 on downtown's Main Street. The former city hall building is now occupied by the corporate headquarters for the online retailer [Zappos.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zappos.com), which opened downtown in 2013. Zappos CEO [Tony Hsieh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Hsieh) took an interest in the urban area and contributed \$350 million toward a revitalization effort called the Downtown Project.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] Projects funded include Las Vegas's first independent bookstore, [The Writer's Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Writer's_Block).[^cite_note-114]

### Other industries

A number of new industries have moved to Las Vegas in recent decades. [Zappos.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zappos.com) (now an [Amazon](90451.md) subsidiary) was founded in San Francisco but by 2013 had moved its headquarters to downtown Las Vegas. [Allegiant Air](1372018.md), a low-cost air carrier, launched in 1997[^cite_note-115] with its first hub at [Harry Reid International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Reid_International_Airport) and headquarters in nearby Summerlin.[^cite_note-116]

[Planet 13 Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planet_13_Holdings), a cannabis company, opened the world's largest [cannabis dispensary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_shop) in Las Vegas at 112,000 sq ft (10,400 m<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

### Effects of growth on water supply

A growing population means the Las Vegas Valley used 1.2 billion US gal (4.5 billion L) more water in 2014 than in 2011. Although water conservation efforts implemented in the wake of a 2002 drought have had some success, local [water consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_footprint) remains 30 percent greater than in Los Angeles, and over three times that of San Francisco metropolitan area residents. The [Southern Nevada Water Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Nevada_Water_Authority) is building a \$1.4 billion tunnel and pumping station to bring water from [Lake Mead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mead), has purchased water rights throughout Nevada, and has planned a controversial \$3.2 billion [pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_transportation) across half the state. By law, the Las Vegas Water Service District "may deny any request for a water commitment or request for a water connection if the District has an inadequate supply of water." But limiting growth on the basis of an inadequate water supply has been unpopular with the casino and building industries.[^cite_note-\:0-54]

## Culture

Main article: [Las Vegas Valley § Culture and the arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley#Culture_and_the_arts)

See also: [List of Las Vegas landmarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Las_Vegas_landmarks)

"Las Vegas culture" redirects here. For the ancient Ecuadorian civilization, see [Las Vegas culture (archaeology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_culture_\(archaeology\)).



The [Smith Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts) & Discovery Museum



[Symphony Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_Park) in [Downtown Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Las_Vegas)

The city is home to several museums, including the [Neon Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neon_Museum) (the location for many of the historical signs from Las Vegas's mid-20th century heyday), The [Mob Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mob_Museum), the [Las Vegas Natural History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Natural_History_Museum), the Discovery Children's Museum, the Nevada State Museum and the Old Las Vegas Mormon Fort State Historic Park.[^cite_note-119]

The city's extensive [Downtown Arts District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Arts_District) hosts numerous galleries and events, including the annual Las Vegas Film Festival. "First Friday" is a monthly celebration that includes arts, music, special presentations and food in a section of the city's downtown region called 18b, The Las Vegas Arts District.[^cite_note-120] The festival extends into the Fremont East Entertainment District.[^cite_note-121] The Thursday evening before First Friday is known in the arts district as "Preview Thursday," which highlights new gallery exhibitions throughout the district.[^cite_note-122]

The [Las Vegas Academy of International Studies, Performing and Visual Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Academy) is a [Grammy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy) award-winning [magnet school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnet_school) located in [downtown Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Las_Vegas). The [Smith Center for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smith_Center_for_the_Performing_Arts) is downtown in [Symphony Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_Park) and hosts various Broadway shows and other artistic performances.[^cite_note-123]

Las Vegas has earned the moniker "Gambling Capital of the World," as it has the world's most land-based casinos.[^cite_note-124] The city is also host to more [AAA Five Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AAA_Five_Diamond_Award) hotels than any other city in the world.[^cite_note-125]

The Las Vegas metropolitan area has been the site of many prominent [combat sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_sport) events, such as [boxing](4243.md) and [MMA](228344.md), with Las Vegas being considered by many as the "fight capital of the world".[^cite_note-133] The mixed martial arts promotion, [Ultimate Fighting Championship](169660.md) (UFC), is headquartered in Las Vegas and also frequently holds fights in the city at T-Mobile Arena and at the UFC Apex training facility near the headquarters.[^cite_note-134]

Since 2023 there has been a [Formula One](10854.md) race on the streets of Las Vegas. The [Las Vegas Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Grand_Prix) takes place on the [Las Vegas Strip Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip_Circuit), a temporary 3.853 mile race circuit designed by [Carsten Tilke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carsten_Tilke?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-136]

## Government



[Las Vegas City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_City_Hall) in downtown Las Vegas

The city of Las Vegas has a [council–manager government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council%E2%80%93manager_government).[^cite_note-\:2-140] The mayor sits as a council member-at-large and presides over all [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_council) meetings.[^cite_note-\:2-140] If the mayor cannot preside over a city council meeting, then the Mayor [pro tempore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro_tempore) is the [presiding officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman) of the meeting until the Mayor returns to his/her seat.[^cite_note-141] The city manager is responsible for the administration and the day-to-day operations of all [municipal services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_services) and city departments.[^cite_note-\:3-142] The city manager maintains intergovernmental relationships with federal, state, county and other local governments.[^cite_note-\:3-142]

Out of the 2,265,461 people in Clark County as of the 2020 Census, approximately 1,030,000 people live in [unincorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unincorporated_area) [Clark County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County,_Nevada), and around 650,000 live in incorporated cities such as [North Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Las_Vegas,_Nevada), [Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson,_Nevada) and [Boulder City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boulder_City,_Nevada).[^cite_note-143] Las Vegas and Clark County share a police department, the [Las Vegas Metropolitan Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Metropolitan_Police_Department), which was formed after a 1973 merger of the [Las Vegas Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Police_Department_\(Nevada\)) and the [Clark County Sheriff's Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County_Sheriff's_Department_\(Nevada\)).[^cite_note-144] North Las Vegas, Henderson, Boulder City, Mesquite, UNLV and CCSD have their own police departments.[^cite_note-145]

The [federally-recognized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_federally_recognized_tribes_in_the_contiguous_United_States) [Las Vegas Tribe of Paiute Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Tribe_of_Paiute_Indians_of_the_Las_Vegas_Indian_Colony) ([Southern Paiute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_River_Numic_language): Nuvagantucim&#x69;**)** occupies a 31-acre (130,000 m<sup>2</sup>) [reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_reservation) just north downtown between [Interstate-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_15) and Main Street.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148]

Downtown is the location of [Lloyd D. George Federal District Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._District_Court_for_the_District_of_Nevada#Las_Vegas)[^cite_note-149] and the Regional Justice Center,[^cite_note-150] draws numerous companies providing bail, marriage, divorce, tax, [incorporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incorporation_\(business\)) and other legal services.

## Education

Main article: [Las Vegas Valley § Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Valley#Education)

### Primary and secondary schools

Main article: [Clark County School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County_School_District)

Primary and secondary [public education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_education) is provided by the [Clark County School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_County_School_District).[^cite_note-159]

### Public higher education

Public higher education is provided by the [Nevada System of Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_System_of_Higher_Education) (NSHE). Public institutions serving Las Vegas include the [University of Nevada, Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nevada,_Las_Vegas) (UNLV), the [College of Southern Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Southern_Nevada) (CSN), [Nevada State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_University) (NSU), and the [Desert Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Research_Institute) (DRI).[^cite_note-160]

UNLV is a public, land-grant, [R1 research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Classification_of_Institutions_of_Higher_Education) university and is home to the [Kirk Kerkorian School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNLV_School_of_Medicine)[^cite_note-161] and the [William S. Boyd School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Boyd_School_of_Law), the only law school in Nevada.[^cite_note-162] The university's campus is urban and located about two miles east of the Las Vegas strip. The Desert Research Institute's southern campus sits next to UNLV, while its northern campus is in Reno.[^cite_note-163]

CSN, with campuses throughout Clark County,[^cite_note-164] is a [community college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_college) with one of the largest enrollments in the United States.[^cite_note-165] In unincorporated Clark County, CSN's Charleston campus is home to the headquarters of [Nevada Public Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_Public_Radio) ([KNPR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KNPR)), an [NPR](29697232.md) member station.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167]

### Private higher education

[Touro University Nevada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touro_University_Nevada) located in [Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson,_Nevada) is a non-profit, private institution primarily focusing on medical education.[^cite_note-168] Other institutions include a number of [for-profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_college) private schools (e.g., [Le Cordon Bleu College of Culinary Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Cordon_Bleu_College_of_Culinary_Arts_Las_Vegas)[^cite_note-169] and [Carrington College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrington_College_\(US\)),[^cite_note-170] among others).[^cite_note-171]

## Media

Main article: [Media in Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_Las_Vegas)

### Newspapers



Las Vegas *Review-Journal* sign

- *[Las Vegas Review-Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Review-Journal)*, the area's largest daily newspaper, is published every morning. It was formed in 1909 but has roots back to 1905. It is the largest newspaper in Nevada and is ranked as one of the top 25 newspapers in the United States by circulation. In 2000, the *Review-Journal* installed the largest newspaper printing press in the world. It cost \$40 million, weighs 910 tons and consists of 16 towers.

  [^cite_note-172]

  &#x20;Until his death in January 2021, the newspaper was owned by casino magnate [Sheldon Adelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheldon_Adelson), who purchased it for \$140 million in December 2015. In 2018, the *Review-Journal* received the [Sigma Delta Chi Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_Delta_Chi_Award) from the [Society of Professional Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Professional_Journalists) for reporting the [Oct 1 mass shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Las_Vegas_shooting) on the Las Vegas Strip. In 2018 and 2022, *Editor and Publisher* magazine named the *Review-Journal* as one of 10 newspapers in the United States "doing it right."

  [^cite_note-173]

  [^cite_note-174]

- *[Las Vegas Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Sun)*, based in neighboring [Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson,_Nevada), is a daily newspaper. Although independently published, the print edition is distributed as a section inside the *Review-Journal*. The *Sun* is owned by the Greenspun family and is part of the [Greenspun Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenspun_Media_Group). It was founded independently in 1950 and in 1989 entered into a [Joint Operating Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Operating_Agreement) with the *Review-Journal*, which runs through 2040. The *Sun* has been described as "politically liberal".

## Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Las Vegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Las_Vegas)

### Public transit



Regional Transportation Commission (RTC) provides public bus transportation.



[Harry Reid International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Reid_International_Airport) provides private and public aviation services to the city.



Inside Terminal 3 at Harry Reid International Airport in Paradise

[RTC Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTC_Transit) is a public transportation system providing bus service throughout Las Vegas, Henderson, North Las Vegas and other areas of the valley. Inter-city bus service to and from Las Vegas is provided by [Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines), [BoltBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BoltBus), [Orange Belt Stages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Belt_Stages), [Tufesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tufesa), and several smaller carriers.[^cite_note-178]

[Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) trains have not served Las Vegas since the service via the *[Desert Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Wind)* at [Las Vegas station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Union_Plaza_station) ceased in 1997, but [Amtrak California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_California) operates [Amtrak Thruway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Thruway) dedicated service between the city and its passenger rail stations in [Bakersfield, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakersfield_station_\(Amtrak\)), as well as [Los Angeles Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Los_Angeles\)) via [Barstow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barstow_Harvey_House).[^cite_note-179]

[High-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) project [Brightline West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brightline_West) began construction in 2024 to connect Brightline's [Las Vegas station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_station_\(Brightline_West\)) and the [Rancho Cucamonga station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Cucamonga_station) in [Greater Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles).[^cite_note-180]

The [Las Vegas Monorail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Monorail) on the Strip was privately built, and upon bankruptcy taken over by the [Las Vegas Convention and Visitors Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Convention_and_Visitors_Authority).[^cite_note-181]

[Silver Rider Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Rider_Transit) operates three routes within Las Vegas, offering connections to Laughlin,[^cite_note-182] Mesquite,[^cite_note-183] and Sandy Valley.[^cite_note-184]

The [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad) is the only [Class I railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Class_I_railroad) providing rail freight service to the city. Until 1997, the Amtrak *Desert Wind* train service ran through Las Vegas using the Union Pacific Railroad tracks.

In March 2010, the RTC launched [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) link in Las Vegas called the *Strip & Downtown Express* with limited stops and frequent service that connects downtown Las Vegas, the Strip and the Las Vegas Convention Center. Shortly after the launch, the RTC dropped the *ACE* name.[^cite_note-185]

In 2016, 77.1 percent of working Las Vegas residents (those living in the city, but not necessarily working in the city) commuted by driving alone. About 11 percent commuted via carpool, 3.9 percent used public transportation, and 1.4 percent walked. About 2.3 percent of Las Vegas commuters used all other forms of transportation, including taxi, bicycle, and motorcycle. About 4.3% of working Las Vegas residents worked at home.[^cite_note-186] In 2015, 10.2 percent of city of Las Vegas households were without a car, which increased slightly to 10.5 percent in 2016. The national average was 8.7 percent in 2016. Las Vegas averaged 1.63 cars per household in 2016, compared to a national average of 1.8 per household.

### Roads



Last Vegas Strip night view

With some exceptions, including [Las Vegas Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Boulevard), [Boulder Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_Route_582) (SR 582), and [Rancho Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_Route_599) (SR 599), the majority of surface streets in Las Vegas are laid out in a grid along [Public Land Survey System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Land_Survey_System) [section lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Section_line). Many are maintained by the [Nevada Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_Department_of_Transportation) as [state highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_highway). The street numbering system is divided by the following streets:

- Far Hills Avenue, Greenmoor Lane, Westcliff Drive, [I-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_11)/[US 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_95_in_Nevada), Ogden Avenue, [Fremont Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fremont_Street), and [Charleston Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_Route_159) divide the north–south block numbers from west to east.

- [Las Vegas Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Boulevard) divides the east–west streets from the [Las Vegas Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Strip) to near the Stratosphere, then [Main Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_Route_601) becomes the dividing line from the Stratosphere to the North Las Vegas border, after which the Goldfield Street alignment divides east and west.

- On the east side of Las Vegas, block numbers between [Charleston Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_Route_159) and [Washington Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_Route_578) are different along Nellis Boulevard ([SR 612](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_Route_612)), which is the eastern border of the city limits.

Interstates 11 and 15 and US 95 lead out of the city in four directions. Two major freeways – [I-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_15_in_Nevada) and [I-11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_11)/[US 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_95_in_Nevada) – cross in downtown Las Vegas. I-15 connects Las Vegas to Los Angeles, and heads northeast to and beyond Salt Lake City. I-11 goes northwest to the [Las Vegas Paiute Indian Reservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Tribe_of_Paiute_Indians_of_the_Las_Vegas_Indian_Colony) and southeast to [Henderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henderson,_Nevada) and to the [Mike O'Callaghan–Pat Tillman Memorial Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_O'Callaghan%E2%80%93Pat_Tillman_Memorial_Bridge), where it will eventually continue along [US 93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_93_in_Arizona) toward [Phoenix](49121.md). US 95 (and eventually I-11) connects the city to northwestern Nevada, including [Carson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carson_City,_Nevada) and [Reno](26388.md). US 93 splits from I-15 northeast of Las Vegas and goes north through the eastern part of the state, serving [Ely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ely,_Nevada) and [Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells,_Nevada). US 95 heads south from US 93 near Henderson through far eastern California. [A partial beltway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Beltway) has been built, consisting of I-215 on the south and Clark County 215 (CC 215) on the west and north. Other radial routes include [Blue Diamond Road (SR 160)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_Route_160) to [Pahrump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahrump,_Nevada), [Lake Mead Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mead_Boulevard) ([unsigned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unsigned_highway) SR 147) and [Lake Mead Parkway (SR 564)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_Route_564) to [Lake Mead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mead), [SR 159](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevada_State_Route_159) to [Red Rock Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Rock_Canyon_National_Conservation_Area) and [Blue Diamond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Diamond,_Nevada), and [Las Vegas Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Boulevard) to [Sloan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloan,_Nevada), [Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean,_Nevada), the [Seven Magic Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Magic_Mountains), and [Primm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primm,_Nevada) in the southbound direction and [Apex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apex,_Nevada) and [Garnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garnet,_Nevada) in the northbound direction.
