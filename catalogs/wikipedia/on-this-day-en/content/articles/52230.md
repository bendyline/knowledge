**Osaka** ([Japanese](15606.md): 大阪市, [Hepburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepburn_romanization): *Ōsaka-shi*; pronounced [\[òó.sá.ká.ɕì, óó.-\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese);[^cite_note-NHKSJT-6][^cite_note-SMK2-7] commonly just 大阪, *Ōsaka* [\[òó.sá.ká, óó.-\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Japanese) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b4/Ja-Osaka.ogg/Ja-Osaka.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b4/Ja-Osaka.ogg/Ja-Osaka.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>[^cite_note-NHKSJT-6][^cite_note-SMK2-7]) is a [designated city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_designated_by_government_ordinance_of_Japan) in the [Kansai region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_region) of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu) in Japan. It is the capital of and most populous city in [Osaka Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Prefecture), and the second biggest prefectural city, [third-most populous city proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Japan) in Japan, following the [special wards of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_wards_of_Tokyo) and [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama). With an estimated population of 2.8 million as of October 1, 2025[^cite_note-大阪市_推計人口（毎月1日現在）・人口異動-2] and a population density of about 12,505 people per square kilometer, it is the largest component of the [Keihanshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keihanshin) metropolitan area, the [second-largest metropolitan area in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_Japan)[^cite_note-stats-boj-8] and the 10th-[largest urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_by_population) in the world with more than 19 million inhabitants.[^cite_note-japan2-4]

Osaka was traditionally considered Japan's economic hub. By the [Kofun period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofun_period) (300–538) it had developed into an important regional port, and in the seventh and eighth centuries it served briefly as the imperial capital. Osaka continued to flourish during the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period) (1603–1867) and became known as a center of Japanese culture. Following the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration), it greatly expanded in size and underwent rapid industrialization. This accelerated population growth throughout the following decades, and Osaka was the industrial hub in the [Meiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era) and [Taishō eras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taish%C5%8D_era). It made contributions to redevelopment, urban planning and zoning standards in the postwar period, and developed rapidly as one of the major financial centers in the Keihanshin Metropolitan Area.

Osaka is a major financial center of Japan and is recognized as one of its most multicultural and [cosmopolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmopolitanism) cities. It is home to the [Osaka Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Exchange) and the headquarters of multinational [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics) corporations such as [Panasonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panasonic) and [Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_Corporation). Osaka is an international center of [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development) and is represented by several major universities, including [Osaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_University), [Osaka Metropolitan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Metropolitan_University), and [Kansai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_University). Landmarks include [Osaka Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Castle), [Osaka Aquarium Kaiyukan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Aquarium_Kaiyukan), [Dōtonbori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dtonbori), [Tsūtenkaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ts%C5%ABtenkaku) in [Shinsekai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinsekai), [Tennōji Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenn%C5%8Dji_Park), [Abeno Harukas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abenobashi_Terminal_Building), [Sumiyoshi Taisha Grand Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumiyoshi_Taisha), and [Shitennō-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shitenn%C5%8D-ji), one of the oldest Buddhist temples in Japan.

## Etymology

*Ōsaka* means 'large hill' or 'large slope'. It is unclear when this name gained prominence over Naniwa, but the oldest written evidence for the name dates back to 1496.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

By the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period), 大坂 (*Ōsaka*) and 大阪 (*Ōsaka*) were mixed use, and the writer [Hamamatsu Utakuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamamatsu_Utakuni?action=edit\&redlink=1), in his book *Setsuyo Ochiboshu* published in 1808, states that the [kanji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanji) 坂 was abhorred because it means 'returns to the earth,' and thus 阪 was used. The kanji 土 ('earth') is also similar to the word 士 ('knight'), and 反 means 'against,' so 坂 can be understood as 'samurai rebellion'. 阪 became the official name in 1868 after the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration). The older kanji (坂) is still in very limited use, usually only in historical contexts. When used as an abbreviation, the modern kanji 阪 (*han*) refers to Osaka City or [Osaka Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Prefecture).

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Osaka).

### Origins: Jōmon and Yayoi period

During the [Jōmon period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dmon_period) (7,000 BCE), present-day Osaka was mostly submerged, and the [Uemachi Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uemachi_Plateau) (上町台地, *Uemachi Daichi*) formed a 12 km (7.5 mi) long and 2.5 km (1.6 mi) wide peninsula separating [Kawachi Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawachi_Bay) from the [Seto Inland Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seto_Inland_Sea).[^cite_note-Uemachidaichi-11][^cite_note-plain-12] It is considered one of the first places where inhabitants of Japan settled, both for the favorable geological conditions, rich in fresh water and lush vegetation, and because its position was defensible against military attack.[^cite_note-Uemachidaichi-11]



Ancient shells found in the Morinomiya *kaizuka* ([Jomon period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomon_period))

The earliest evidence of settlements in the Osaka area are the Morinomiya ruins (森ノ宮遺跡, *Morinomiya iseki*) which is located in the central [Chuo-ku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%AB%C5%8D-ku,_Osaka) district.[^cite_note-Uemachidaichi-11] Buried human skeletons and a *kaizuka* (a mound containing remains) were found, as well as shell mounds, oysters, and other interesting archeological discoveries from the Jomon period.[^cite_note-Uemachidaichi-11] In addition to the remains of consumed food, there were arrow heads, stone tools, fishing hooks and crockery with remains from rice processing. It is estimated that the ruins contain 2,000-year-old debris between the [Jomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jomon_period) and [Yayoi period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yayoi_period). The findings of the archeological sites are exhibited in an adjacent building.[^cite_note-osakahist-13][^cite_note-Uemachidaichi-11]

In the years between the end of the Jōmon period and the beginning of the Yayoi period, the sediments that were deposited north of the Uemachi peninsula / plateau transformed Kawachi Bay into a lagoon.[^cite_note-plain-12] During the [Yayoi period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yayoi_period) (300 BCE – 250 CE), permanent habitation on the plains grew as rice farming became popular.[^cite_note-osakahist-13]

At the beginning of the third century CE the grand shrine of [Sumiyoshi-taisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumiyoshi-taisha) was inaugurated near the harbor, commissioned by consort [Empress Jingū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empress_Jing%C5%AB). This [Shinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto) shrine structure survived historical events,[^cite_note-14] which inaugurated a new style in the construction of Shinto shrines, called [Sumiyoshi-zukuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumiyoshi-zukuri).[^cite_note-15] The maritime panorama enjoyed from the shrine gardens inspired several artists, and nowadays the representations of that type of landscape are called *Sumiyoshi drawings*.

Towards the end of the Yayoi period the Uemachi plateau-peninsula expanded further, transforming the Kawachi Lagoon into a lake (河内湖) connected to the mouth of the [Yodo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yodo_River), which had widened to the south.[^cite_note-plain-12]

### Kofun period

By the [Kofun period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofun_period), Osaka developed into a hub port connecting the region to the western part of Japan. The port of Naniwa-tsu was established and became the most important in Japan.[^cite_note-sumiyoshi-16] Trade with other areas of the country and the Asian continent intensified.[^cite_note-sumiyoshi-16] The large numbers of increasingly larger keyhole-shaped [Kofun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofun) mounds found in the plains of Osaka are evidence of political-power concentration, leading to the formation of a state.[^cite_note-osakahist-13][^cite_note-17] The findings in the neighboring plains, including the mausoleum of [Emperor Nintoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Nintoku) was discovered nearby in [Sakai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakai) testify to the status of imperial city that Osaka had reached. Four of these mounds can be seen in Osaka, in which important members of the nobility are buried. They are located in the southern districts of the city and date back to the 5th century.[^cite_note-sumiyoshi-16] A group of megalithic tombs called [Mozu Tombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozu_Tombs) are located in Sakai, Osaka Prefecture.[^cite_note-18]

Important works of the Kofun period is the excavation that diverted the course of the [Yamato River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_River), whose floods caused extensive damage, and the construction of important roads in the direction of [Sakai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakai) and [Nara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_Prefecture).[^cite_note-osakahist-13][^cite_note-19] Maritime traffic connected to the port of Naniwa-tsu increased in such a way that huge warehouses were built to stow material arriving and departing.[^cite_note-osakahist-13]

### Asuka and Nara period

The [Kojiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kojiki) records that during 390–430 CE, there was an imperial palace located at Osumi, in what is present day [Higashiyodogawa ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higashiyodogawa-ku,_Osaka), but it may have been a secondary imperial residence rather than a capital.[^cite_note-20]

In 645, [Emperor Kōtoku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_K%C5%8Dtoku) built his [Naniwa Nagara-Toyosaki Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naniwa_Nagara-Toyosaki_Palace) in what is now Osaka,[^cite_note-21] making it the capital of Japan. The city now known as Osaka was at this time referred to as Naniwa (written as 浪華 or 浪花) and this name and derivations of it are still in use for districts in central Osaka such as [Naniwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naniwa-ku,_Osaka) (浪速) and [Namba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namba) (難波). Although the capital was moved to [Asuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asuka,_Yamato) (in [Nara Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nara_Prefecture) today) in 655, Naniwa remained a vital connection, by land and sea, between [Yamato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamato_Province) (modern day Nara Prefecture), [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_history_of_Korea), and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_history_of_China).[^cite_note-osakahist-13][^cite_note-22]

Naniwa was declared the capital again in 744 by order of [Emperor Shōmu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Sh%C5%8Dmu), and remained so until 745, when the Imperial Court moved back to [Heijō-kyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heij%C5%8D-ky%C5%8D) (now [Nara](6792846.md)). By the end of the Nara period, Naniwa's seaport roles had been gradually taken over by neighboring areas, but it remained a lively center of river, channel, and land transportation between [Heian-kyō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto) (Kyoto today) and other destinations. [Sumiyoshi Taisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumiyoshi_Taisha) Grand Shrine was founded by Tamomi no Sukune in 211 CE.[^cite_note-23] [Shitennō-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shitenn%C5%8D-ji) was first built in 593 CE and is the oldest [Buddhist](3267529.md) temple in Japan.[^cite_note-24]

### Heian to Edo period

In 1496, [Jōdo Shinshū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Ddo_Shinsh%C5%AB) [Buddhists](3267529.md) established their headquarters in the heavily fortified [Ishiyama Hongan-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ishiyama_Hongan-ji), located directly on the site of the old Naniwa Imperial Palace. [Oda Nobunaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oda_Nobunaga) began a decade-long siege campaign on the temple in 1570 which ultimately resulted in the surrender of the monks and subsequent razing of the temple. [Toyotomi Hideyoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyotomi_Hideyoshi) constructed [Osaka Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Castle) in its place in 1583.[^cite_note-25] Osaka Castle played a pivotal role in the [Siege of Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Osaka) (1614–1615).

Osaka was long considered Japan's primary economic center,[^cite_note-26] with a large percentage of the population belonging to the merchant class (see [Four divisions of society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_divisions_of_society)). Over the course of the [Edo period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_period) (1603–1867), Osaka grew into one of Japan's major cities and returned to its ancient role as a lively and important port. *[Daimyōs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daimy%C5%8D)* (feudal lords) received most of their income in the form of rice. [Merchants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chonin) in Osaka thus began to organize [storehouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warehouse) where they would store a *daimyō*'s rice in exchange for a fee, trading it for either coin or a form of receipt; essentially a precursor to [paper money](208286.md). Many if not all of these [rice brokers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_brokers) also made loans, and would actually become quite wealthy and powerful. Osaka merchants coalesced their shops around [Dōjima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Djima), where the [Rice Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Djima_Rice_Exchange) was established in 1697 and where the world's first [futures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futures_exchange) market would come to exist to sell rice that was not yet harvested.[^cite_note-27]

The popular culture of Osaka[^cite_note-28] was closely related to *[ukiyo-e](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukiyo-e)* depictions of life in [Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_\(Tokyo\)). By 1780, Osaka had cultivated a vibrant arts culture, as typified by its famous [Kabuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabuki) and [Bunraku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunraku) theaters.[^cite_note-29] In 1837, [Ōshio Heihachirō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cshio_Heihachir%C5%8D), a low-ranking [samurai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samurai), led a peasant insurrection in response to the city's unwillingness to support the many poor and suffering families in the area. Approximately one-quarter of the city was razed before shogunal officials put down the rebellion, after which Ōshio killed himself.[^cite_note-30] Osaka was opened to foreign trade by the government of the [Bakufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakufu) at the same time as [Hyogo Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hy%C5%8Dgo_Prefecture) (modern [Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe)) on January 1, 1868, just before the advent of the [Boshin War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boshin_War) and the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration).[^cite_note-31] The [Kawaguchi foreign settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawaguchi_foreign_settlement), now the Kawaguchi subdistrict, is a legacy of the foreign presence in Osaka.

Osaka residents were stereotyped in Edo literature from at least the 18th century. [Jippensha Ikku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jippensha_Ikku) in 1802 depicted Osakans as stingy almost beyond belief. In 1809, the derogatory term "Kamigata zeeroku" was used by Edo residents to characterize inhabitants of the Osaka region in terms of calculation, shrewdness, lack of civic spirit, and the vulgarity of Osaka dialect. Edo writers aspired to samurai culture, and saw themselves as poor but generous, chaste, and public spirited. Edo writers by contrast saw "zeeroku" as obsequious apprentices, stingy, greedy, gluttonous, and lewd. To some degree, Osaka residents are still stigmatized by Tokyo observers in the same way today, especially in terms of gluttony, evidenced in the phrase, "Residents of Osaka devour their food until they collapse" (大阪は食倒れ, *"Ōsaka wa kuidaore"*).[^cite_note-32]

### Meiji to Heisei period

With the enormous changes that characterized the country after the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration) (1868), and the relocation of the capital from [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto) to Tokyo, Osaka entered a period of decline. From being the capital of the economy and finance, it became a predominantly industrial center.[^cite_note-osaka-info.jp-33] The modern municipality was established[^cite_note-osaka-info.jp-33] in 1889 by [government ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_designated_by_government_ordinance), with an initial area of 15 square kilometers (6 sq mi), overlapping today's [Chuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C5%AB%C5%8D-ku,_Osaka) and [Nishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishi-ku,_Osaka) wards. Later, the city went through three major expansions to reach its current size of 223 square kilometers (86 sq mi). Osaka was the industrial center most clearly defined in the development of capitalism in Japan. It became known as the "[Manchester](20206.md) and [Melbourne](17306237.md) of the Orient".[^cite_note-osaka-info.jp-33] In 1925, it was the largest and most populous city in Japan and the sixth largest in the world.[^cite_note-osaka-info.jp-33]

The rapid industrialization attracted many Asian immigrants (Indians, Chinese, and Koreans), who set up a life apart for themselves.[^cite_note-34] The political system was pluralistic, with a strong emphasis on promoting industrialization and modernization.[^cite_note-35] Literacy was high and the educational system expanded rapidly, producing a middle class with a taste for literature and a willingness to support the arts.[^cite_note-36] In 1927, [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) operated a factory called [Osaka Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_General_Motors_factories) until 1941, manufacturing [Chevrolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet), [Cadillac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadillac), [Pontiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac_\(automobile\)), [Oldsmobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldsmobile), and [Buick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buick) vehicles, operated and staffed by Japanese workers and managers.[^cite_note-37] In the nearby city of [Ikeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikeda,_Osaka) in Osaka Prefecture is the headquarters of [Daihatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daihatsu), one of Japan's oldest automobile manufacturers.

Like its European and American counterparts, Osaka displayed slums, unemployment, and poverty. In Japan it was here that municipal government first introduced a comprehensive system of poverty relief, copied in part from British models. Osaka policymakers stressed the importance of family formation and mutual assistance as the best way to combat poverty. This minimized the cost of welfare programs.[^cite_note-38]

During [World War II](32927.md), [Osaka came under air raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Osaka) in 1945 by the [United States Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) as part of the [air raids on Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_raids_on_Japan). On March 13, 1945, a total of 329 [Boeing B-29 Superfortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-29_Superfortress) [heavy bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_bombers) took part in the raid against Osaka. According to an American [prisoner of war](25008.md) who was held in the city, the air raid took almost the entire night and destroyed 25 square miles (65 km<sup>2</sup>) of the city. The U.S. bombed the city again twice in June 1945 and again on August 14, a day before [Japan's surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan's_surrender).[^cite_note-39]

In the decades following World War II, Osaka's reconstruction efforts and the industriousness of its residents brought the city even greater prosperity than before the war. Its population surpassed three million in the 1960s, initiating large-scale suburbanization within the prefecture, and eventually doubled to six million by the 1990s. With factories rebuilt and trade revived, Osaka rapidly developed into a major multicultural and financial center from [the 1950s through the 1980s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_economic_miracle). Osaka Prefecture was chosen as the venue for the prestigious [Expo '70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_'70), the first [world's fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_fair) ever held in an Asian country. Since then, numerous international events have been held in Osaka, including the 1995 [APEC Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Economic_Cooperation).

The modern municipality, which when it was established in 1889 occupied an area of just 15 km<sup>2</sup> (5.8 sq mi) including the districts of Chūō and Nishi, following three successive expansions has reached an area of 222 km<sup>2</sup> (86 sq mi). It was one of the first cities in Japan to obtain [designated city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_designated_by_government_ordinance_of_Japan) status in 1956.[^cite_note-40]

### 21st century to present

The plan to reorganize Osaka and its province into a [metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Metropolis_Plan) like Tokyo was met with stiff opposition in some municipalities, particularly the highly populated [Sakai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakai). [Tōru Hashimoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dru_Hashimoto) then fell back on a project that included the suppression of the 24 wards of Osaka, thus dividing the city into 5 new special districts with a status similar to that of the 23 [Special wards of Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_wards_of_Tokyo). It was introduced by former mayor Tōru Hashimoto, leader of the reform party [Osaka Restoration Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Restoration_Association) which he founded. The May 2015 referendum for the approval of this project saw the narrow victory of no, and consequently Hashimoto announced his withdrawal from politics.[^cite_note-41] A second referendum for a merger into 4 semi-autonomous wards was narrowly voted down by 692,996 (50.6%).[^cite_note-metropolis-2020-42]

According to the [Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes) list of *The World's Most Expensive Places To Live 2009*, Osaka was the second most expensive in the world after Tokyo.[^cite_note-43] By 2020 it slipped to the 5th rank of most expensive cities.[^cite_note-forbes-2020-44]

In March 2014, the 300-meter (980 ft) tall [Abeno Harukas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeno_Harukas) opened, which became the tallest building in Japan (surpassing the [Yokohama Landmark Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama_Landmark_Tower) in [Yokohama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama)), until it was in turn surpassed by the 330-meter (1,080 ft) tall [Azabudai Hills Main Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azabudai_Hills) in Tokyo following its completion in 2022.[^cite_note-Tallest_high-rise_nears_completion-45]

[Expo 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2025) was held at [Yumeshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yumeshima) Island, [Konohana-ku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konohana-ku,_Osaka) from April to October 2025. Osaka is the third city to host the [World Expo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Expo) twice, previously hosting [Expo '70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_'70). It is also the fourth [World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Fair) held in Japan following [Expo '90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_'90) and [Expo 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2005) in [Aichi Prefecture](524618.md). With Expo 2025, the event returned to its traditional 5-year scheduling cycle after the [Expo 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2020) in [Dubai](211583.md) was delayed to 2021 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-46] The projected visitor count is approximately 28 million.[^cite_note-about-47]

On same site where Expo 2025 was held, [MGM Resorts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM_Resorts) began construction on [MGM Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM_Osaka) in April 2025. MGM Osaka will be the first [integrated resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Integrated_resort) in Japan. It will include 2,300 hotel rooms, a casino, shopping and dining options, convention space and a 3,500-seat theatre.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

## Geography



A satellite image of Osaka

Osaka's west side is open to [Osaka Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Bay), and is otherwise completely surrounded by more than ten satellite cities, all of them in [Osaka Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Prefecture), with one exception: the city of [Amagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amagasaki), belonging to [Hyōgo Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hy%C5%8Dgo_Prefecture), in the northwest. The city occupies a larger area (about 13%) than any other city or village within Osaka Prefecture.[^cite_note-50]

When Osaka was established in 1889, it occupied roughly the area known today as the Chuo and Nishi wards, 15.27 square kilometers (6 mi<sup>2</sup>) that grew into today's 222.30 square kilometers (86 mi<sup>2</sup>) via incremental expansions. The largest was a single 126.01-square-kilometer (49 mi<sup>2</sup>) expansion in 1925. Osaka's highest point, located in [Tsurumi-ku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsurumi-ku,_Osaka), is 37.5 meters (123.0 ft) above [Tokyo Peil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geospatial_Information_Authority_of_Japan#Japanese_water_height_reference_point). The lowest point, in [Nishiyodogawa-ku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishiyodogawa-ku,_Osaka), is −2.2 meters (−7.2 ft) below Tokyo Peil. Osaka is situated at a [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude) of 34.67°, near the [35th parallel north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/35th_parallel_north), a latitude farther south than [Rome](25458.md) (41.90°), [Madrid](41188263.md) (40.41°), [San Francisco](49728.md) (37.77°) and [Seoul](19159283.md) (37.53°).[^cite_note-51]

### Climate

Osaka is located in the [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) zone ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*), with four distinct seasons. Its winters are generally mild. January is the coldest month, with an average high of 9.7 °C (49 °F). Osaka rarely sees snowfall during the winter. Spring in Osaka starts off mild, but ends up being hot and humid. Spring tends to be Osaka's wettest season, with the *[tsuyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_rainy_season)* (梅雨, *tsuyu*; "plum rain")—the rainy season—occurring between early June and late July. The average starting and ending dates of the rainy season are June 7 and July 21 respectively.[^cite_note-52]

Summers are very hot and humid. In August, the hottest month, the average daily high temperature reaches 33.7 °C (93 °F). Average nighttime low temperatures typically hover around 25.8 °C (78 °F). Fall in Osaka sees a cooling trend, with the early part of the season resembling summer, while the latter part of fall resembles winter.

Precipitation is abundant. Winter is the driest season. Monthly rainfall peaks in June with the "tsuyu" rainy season, which typically ends in mid to late July. From late July to late August, summer's heat and humidity peak, and rainfall decreases somewhat. Osaka has a second rainy period in September and early October, when tropical weather systems, including typhoons, coming from the south or southwest are possible.

### Neighborhoods

Central Osaka is roughly divided into downtown and uptown areas known as **Kita** ([キタ](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%82%AD%E3%82%BF); "north") and **Minami** ([ミナミ](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E3%83%9F%E3%83%8A%E3%83%9F); "south").[^cite_note-japan-guide.com-57][^cite_note-japan-guide.com1-58]

Kita is home to the [Umeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umeda) district and its immediate surrounding neighborhoods, a major business and retail hub that plays host to [Osaka Station City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csaka_Station) and a large subterranean network of shopping arcades.[^cite_note-japan-guide.com-57] Kita and nearby [Nakanoshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakanoshima) contain a prominent portion of the city's skyscrapers and are often featured in photographs of Osaka's skyline.

Minami, though meaning "south", is essentially in Chūō Ward (中央区, *Chūō-ku*) and geographically central within the city.[^cite_note-japan-guide.com1-58] Well known districts here include [Namba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namba) and [Shinsaibashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinsaibashi) shopping areas, the [Dōtonbori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C5%8Dtonbori) canal entertainment area, [Nipponbashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipponbashi) Den Den Town, as well as arts and fashion culture-oriented areas such as [Amerikamura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amerikamura) and Horie. The 300-meter tall [Abeno Harukas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abenobashi_Terminal_Building) was the tallest skyscraper in the country from 2014 until 2023.[^cite_note-Tallest_high-rise_nears_completion-45]

The business districts between Kita and Minami such as [Honmachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honmachi_\(Osaka\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Yodoyabashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yodoyabashi?action=edit\&redlink=1), called **Semba** ([船場](https://ja.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%88%B9%E5%A0%B4%20\(%E5%A4%A7%E9%98%AA%E5%B8%82\))), house the regional headquarters of many large-scale banks and corporations. The [Midōsuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid%C5%8Dsuji) boulevard runs through Semba and connects Kita and Minami.

Further south of Minami are neighborhoods such as [Shinsekai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinsekai) (with its [Tsūtenkaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsutenkaku) tower), [Tennoji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenn%C5%8Dji-ku,_Osaka) and [Abeno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abeno-ku,_Osaka) (with [Tennoji Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenn%C5%8Dji_Zoo), [Shitennō-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shitenn%C5%8D-ji) and [Abeno Harukas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abenobashi_Terminal_Building)), and the [Kamagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamagasaki) slums, the largest slum in Japan.[^cite_note-59]

The city's west side is a prominent [bay area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Bay)[^cite_note-60] which serves as its main port as well as a tourist destination with attractions such as [Kyocera Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyocera_Dome), [Universal Studios Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Studios_Japan) and the [Tempozan Harbor Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tenp%C5%8D). [Higashiosaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higashi%C5%8Dsaka,_Osaka) is zoned as a separate city, although the east side of Osaka city proper contains numerous residential neighborhoods including [Tsuruhashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsuruhashi_Station) KoreaTown, as well as the [Osaka Castle Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Castle_Park), [Osaka Business Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Business_Park) and the hub [Kyōbashi Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ky%C5%8Dbashi_Station_\(Osaka\)).

Osaka contains numerous urban canals and bridges, many of which serve as the namesake for their surrounding neighborhoods.[^cite_note-Eiichi_Watanabe-2004-61] The phrase "808 bridges of Naniwa" was an expression in old Japan used to indicate impressiveness and the "uncountable". Osaka numbered roughly 200 bridges by the Edo period[^cite_note-62] and 1,629 bridges by 1925. As many of the city's canals were gradually filled in, the number dropped to 872, of which 760 are currently managed by Osaka City.[^cite_note-Eiichi_Watanabe-2004-61]

Population numbers have been recorded in Osaka since as early as 1873, in the early [Meiji era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_era).[^cite_note-64] According to the census in 2005, there were 2,628,811 residents in Osaka, an increase of 30,037 or 1.2% from 2000.[^cite_note-65] There were 1,280,325 households with approximately 2.1 persons per household. The population density was 11,836 persons per km<sup>2</sup>. The [Great Kantō earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1923_Great_Kant%C5%8D_earthquake) caused a mass migration to Osaka between 1920 and 1930, and the city became Japan's largest city in 1930 with 2,453,573 people, outnumbering even Tokyo, which had a population of 2,070,913. The population peaked at 3,252,340 in 1940, and had a post-war peak of 3,156,222 in 1965, but has declined since, as the residents moved out to the suburbs.[^cite_note-66]

There were 144,123 registered foreigners, the two largest groups being Korean (60,110) and Chinese (39,551).[^cite_note-67] [Ikuno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikuno-ku,_Osaka), with its Tsuruhashi district, is the home to one of the largest population of Korean residents in Japan, with 20,397 registered [Zainichi Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zainichi_Koreans).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

### Dialect

See also: [Kansai dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_dialect)

The commonly spoken dialect of this area is *Osaka-ben*, a typical sub-dialect of *Kansai-ben*. Of the many other particularities that characterize Osaka-ben, examples include using the copula *ya* instead of *da*, and the suffix *-hen* instead of *-nai* in negative verb forms.

### Developments

In July 2012, a joint multi-party bill was submitted to the Diet that would allow for implementation of the [Osaka Metropolis plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Metropolis_plan) as pursued by the mayor of Osaka city, the governor of Osaka and [their party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Restoration_Association_\(1st\)). If implemented, Osaka City, neighboring [Sakai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakai,_Osaka) City and possibly other surrounding municipalities would dissolve and be reorganized as four [special wards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_wards_of_Tokyo) of Osaka prefecture – similar to former [Tokyo City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_City)'s successor wards within Tokyo prefecture. Special wards are municipal-level administrative units that leave some otherwise municipal administrative responsibilities and [revenues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenues) to the prefectural administration.[^cite_note-72]

In October 2018, the city of Osaka officially ended[^cite_note-73] its sister city relationship with San Francisco in the United States after the latter permitted a monument memorializing "comfort women" to remain on a city-owned property, circulating in the process a 10-page, 3,800-word letter in English addressed to San Francisco mayor [London Breed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Breed).[^cite_note-74]

In November 2020, a second referendum to merge Osaka's 24 wards into 4 semi-autonomous wards was narrowly voted down. There were 692,996 (50.6%) votes against and 675,829 (49.4%) votes supported it.[^cite_note-metropolis-2020-42] Osaka mayor and Osaka Ishin co-leader [Ichiro Matsui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ichiro_Matsui) said he would resign when his term ended in 2023.[^cite_note-metropolis-2020-42]

### Energy policies

#### Nuclear power

In February 2012, three Kansai cities, [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto), Osaka, and [Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe), jointly asked [Kansai Electric Power Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_Electric_Power_Company) to break its dependence on nuclear power. In a letter to KEPCO they also requested to disclose information on the demand and supply of electricity, and for lower and stable prices. The three cities were stockholders of the plant: Osaka owned 9% of the shares, Kobe had 3% and Kyoto 0.45%. In June 2012, Toru Hashimoto, the mayor of Osaka, announced a proposal to minimize the dependence on nuclear power for the shareholders meeting.[^cite_note-75]

In March 2012, the city of Osaka decided that as the largest shareholder of Kansai Electric Power Co, that at the next shareholders-meeting in June 2012 it would demand a series of changes:

- that Kansai Electric would be split into two companies, separating power generation from power transmission.

- a reduction of the number of the utility's executives and employees.

- the implementation of absolutely secure measurements to ensuring the safety of the nuclear facilities.

- the disposing of spent fuel.

- the installation of new kind of thermal power generation to secure non-nuclear supply of energy.

- selling all unnecessary assets including the stock holdings of KEPCO.

In this action, Osaka secured the support of two other cities and shareholders: [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto) and [Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe). With their combined voting-rights of 12.5 percent, they were not certain of the ultimate outcome. Two-thirds of the shareholders would be needed to agree to revise the corporate charter.[^cite_note-76]

At a meeting held on April 10, 2012, by the "energy strategy council", formed by the city of Osaka and the governments of the prefectures, it became clear that at the end of the fiscal year 2011 some 69 employees of Kansai Electric Power Company were former public servants. "[Amakudari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amakudari)" is the Japanese name for this practice of rewarding, by hiring officials that formerly controlled and supervised the firm. Such people included the following:

- 13 ex-officials of the: Ministry of Land, Infrastructure, Transport and Tourism

- 3 ex-officials of the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry,

- 2 ex-officials of the Ministry of the Environment,

- 16 former policemen,

- 10 former fire-fighters,

- 13 former civil engineers.

Besides this, it became known that Kansai Electric had made about 600 external financial donations, to a total sum of about 1.695 billion yen:

- 70 donations were paid to local governments: to a total of 699 million yen

- 100 donations to public-service organizations: 443 million yen,

- 430 donations to various organizations and foundations: a total of 553 million yen

During this meeting, some 8 conditions were compiled, that needed to be fulfilled before a restart of the No.3 and No.4 reactors Oi Nuclear Power Plant:

- the consent of the local people and government within 100 kilometer from the plant

- the installation of a new independent regulatory agency

- a nuclear safety agreement

- the establishment of new nuclear safety standards

- stress tests and evaluations based on these new safety rules

## Economy

Main articles: [Hanshin Industrial Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanshin_Industrial_Region) and [Keihanshin industrial region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keihanshin_industrial_region)



A street in Umeda, Osaka

The gross city product of Osaka in fiscal year 2004 was ¥21.3 trillion, an increase of 1.2% over the previous year. The figure accounts for about 55% of the total output in the Osaka Prefecture and 26.5% in the Kinki region. In 2004, commerce, services, and manufacturing have been the three major industries, accounting for 30%, 26%, and 11% of the total, respectively. The per capita income in the city was about ¥3.3 million, 10% higher than that of the Osaka Prefecture.[^cite_note-78] [MasterCard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MasterCard) Worldwide reported that Osaka ranks 19th among the world's leading cities and plays an important role in the global economy.[^cite_note-79] Osaka's GDP per capita (Nominal) was \$59,958.(\$1=\120.13)[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] However, by 2020, Osaka ranked as the 5th most expensive city due to flatlining consumer prices and government subsidies of public transportation.[^cite_note-forbes-2020-44]



[Osaka Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Exchange) in the Kitahama district of Osaka

Historically, Osaka was the center of commerce in Japan, especially in the middle and pre-modern ages. [Nomura Securities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomura_Securities), the first brokerage firm in Japan, was founded in the city in 1925, and Osaka still houses a leading futures exchange. Many major companies have since moved their main offices to Tokyo. However, several major companies, such as [Panasonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panasonic), [Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharp_Corporation), and [Sanyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanyo), are still headquartered in Osaka. In the 2017 [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index), Osaka was ranked as having the 15th most competitive financial center in the world and fifth most competitive in Asia (after [Singapore](57011.md#Banking), [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Hong_Kong#Banking_and_finance), [Tokyo](30057.md#Economy), and [Shanghai](27643.md#Economy)).[^cite_note-82]

The [Osaka Securities Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Securities_Exchange), specializing in derivatives such as [Nikkei 225](40293.md) futures, is based in Osaka. The merger with [JASDAQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JASDAQ) will help the Osaka Securities Exchange become the largest exchange in Japan for start-up companies.[^cite_note-83]

According to global consulting firm Mercer, Osaka was the second [most expensive city for expatriate employees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_cities_for_expatriate_employees) in the world in 2009. It jumped up nine places from 11th place in 2008 and was the eighth most expensive city in 2007. However, it was not ranked in the top ten places of the list in 2013.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] The [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md) (EIU) ranked Osaka as the second most expensive city in the world in its 2013 Cost of Living study.[^cite_note-86]

### Keihanshin region

&#x20;Osaka is part of the [metropolitan region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_region) called [Keihanshin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keihanshin) (also known as Greater Osaka) in the [Kansai region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_region). The [Osaka Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Metro) system alone ranks 8th in the world by annual passenger ridership, serving over 912 million people annually (a quarter of Greater Osaka Rail System's 4 billion annual riders), despite being only 8 of more than 70 lines in the metro area.

All [Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkansen) trains including *[Nozomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nozomi_\(train\))* stop at [Shin-Osaka Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shin-%C5%8Csaka_Station) and provide access to other major cities in Japan, such as Kobe, Kyoto, Nagoya, Yokohama, and Tokyo.

Regular bus services are provided by [Osaka City Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Municipal_Transportation_Bureau), as well Hankyu, Hanshin and Kintetsu, providing a dense network covering most parts of the city.

Osaka is served by two airports situated just outside the city, [Kansai International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_International_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA): **KIX**) which handles primarily international passenger flights and international cargo flights and the nearby [Osaka Itami Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_International_Airport) (IATA: **ITM**) which handles mostly domestic services.

Due to its geographical position, Osaka's international ferry connections are far greater than that of Tokyo, with international service to Shanghai, [Tianjin](50821.md), and [Busan](68115.md) along with domestic routes to [Kitakyushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitakyushu), [Kagoshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kagoshima), [Miyazaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miyazaki,_Miyazaki) and [Okinawa](22477.md).

### Shopping and food

Osaka has a large number of wholesalers and retail shops: 25,228 and 34,707 respectively in 2004.[^cite_note-89] Many of them are concentrated in the wards of Chuō (10,468 shops) and Kita (6,335 shops). Types of shops vary from malls to conventional [shōtengai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dtengai) shopping arcades, built both above- and underground.[^cite_note-90] Shōtengai are seen across Japan, and Osaka has the longest one in the country.[^cite_note-91] The Tenjinbashi-suji arcade stretches from the road approaching the [Tenmangū shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csaka_Tenman-g%C5%AB) and continues for 2.6 km (1.6 miles) going north to south. The stores along the arcade include commodities, clothing, and catering outlets.

Other shopping areas include [Den Den Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipponbashi), the electronic and manga/anime district, which is comparable to Akihabara in Tokyo, the [Umeda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umeda) district, which has the Hankyu Sanbangai shopping mall and [Yodobashi Camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yodobashi_Camera), a huge electrical appliance store that offers a vast range of fashion stores, restaurants, and a Shonen Jump store. Osaka is known for its food, in Japan and abroad. Author [Michael Booth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Booth_\(writer\)) and food critic [François Simon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Simon_\(food_critic\)) of *[Le Figaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Figaro)* have suggested that Osaka is the food capital of the world.[^cite_note-92]

Osakans' love for the culinary is made apparent in the old saying "Kyotoites are financially ruined by overspending on clothing, Osakans are ruined by spending on food."[^cite_note-93] Regional cuisine includes *_[okonomiyaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okonomiyaki)_* (お好み焼き; pan-fried batter cake), *_[takoyaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takoyaki)_* (たこ焼き; [octopus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octopus_as_food) in fried batter), *_[udon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udon)_* (うどん; a noodle dish), as well as the traditional *_[oshizushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshizushi)_* (押し寿司; pressed sushi), particularly *battera* (バッテラ; pressed mackerel sushi). Osaka is known for its fine sake, which is made with fresh water from the prefecture's mountains.[^cite_note-94]

Osaka's culinary prevalence is the result of regional access to high-quality ingredients, a high population of merchants, and proximity to the ocean and waterway trade.[^cite_note-95] In recent years, Osaka has started to garner more attention from foreigners with the increased popularity of cooking and dining in popular culture.[^cite_note-96]

- [Namba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namba) – main shopping, sightseeing, and restaurant area

- Osaka is home to the [National Bunraku Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bunraku_Theatre),

  [^cite_note-97]

  &#x20;where traditional puppet plays, [bunraku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunraku), are performed.

- At Osaka Shochiku-za, close to Namba station, [kabuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabuki) can be enjoyed as well as [manzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manzai).

- At Shin Kabuki-za, formerly near Namba and now near [Uehommachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csaka_Uehommachi_Station) area, [enka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enka) concerts and Japanese dramas are performed.

- [Yoshimoto Kogyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoshimoto_Kogyo), a Japanese entertainment conglomerate operates a hall in the city for manzai and other comedy shows: the Namba Grand Kagetsu hall.

- The Hanjō-tei opened in 2006, dedicated to [rakugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakugo). The theater is in the [Ōsaka Tenman-gū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Csaka_Tenman-g%C5%AB) area.

- [Umeda Arts Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umeda_Arts_Theater) opened in 2005 after relocating from its former 46-year-old Umeda Koma Theater. The theater has a main hall with 1,905 seats and a smaller theater-drama hall with 898 seats. Umeda Arts Theater stages various type of performances including musicals, music concerts, dramas, rakugo, and others.

- The Symphony Hall, built in 1982, is the first hall in Japan designed specially for classical music concerts. The Hall was opened with a concert by the [Osaka Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Philharmonic_Orchestra), which is based in the city. Orchestras such as the [Berlin Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Philharmonic) and [Vienna Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Philharmonic) have played here during their world tours as well.

- [Osaka-jō Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka-j%C5%8D_Hall) is a multi-purpose arena in [Osaka-jō park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Castle_Park) with a capacity for up to 16,000 people. The hall has hosted numerous events and concerts including both Japanese and international artists.

- Nearby City Hall in [Nakanoshima Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakanoshima_Park), is Osaka Central Public Hall, a [Neo-Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Renaissance)-style building first opened in 1918. Re-opened in 2002 after major renovation, it serves as a multi-purpose rental facility for citizen events.

- The [Osaka Shiki Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiki_Theatre_Company)

  [^cite_note-98]

  &#x20;is one of the nine private halls operated nationwide by the [Shiki Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiki_Theatre_Company), staging straight plays and musicals.

- [Festival Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_Hall,_Osaka) was a hall hosting various performances including [noh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noh), [kyōgen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ky%C5%8Dgen), kabuki, ballets as well as classic concerts. The Bolshoi Ballet and the [Philharmonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philharmonia_Orchestra) are among the many that were welcomed on stage in the past. The hall has closed at the end of 2008, planned to re-open in 2013 in a new facility.

### Annual festivals



Tenjin Matsuri

One of the most famous festivals held in Osaka, the [Tenjin Matsuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenjin_Matsuri), is held on July 24 and 25 ([Osaka Tenmangū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Tenmang%C5%AB)). Other festivals in Osaka include the Aizen Matsuri (June 30 – July 2, Shōman-in Temple), the Sumiyoshi Matsuri (July 30 – August 1, [Sumiyoshi Taisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumiyoshi_Taisha)), Shōryō-e (April 22, [Shitennō-ji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shitenn%C5%8D-ji)) and Tōka-Ebisu (January 9–10, Imamiya Ebisu Jinja). The annual [Osaka Asian Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Asian_Film_Festival) takes place in Osaka every March while the Midosuji Parade takes place in October.[^cite_note-99]

### Museums and galleries

See also: [Museums in Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Museums_in_Osaka)

The [National Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_Art,_Osaka) (NMAO) is a subterranean Japanese and international art museum, housing mainly collections from the post-war era and regularly welcoming temporary exhibitions. [Osaka Science Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Science_Museum) is in a five storied building next to the National Museum of Art, with a planetarium and an [OMNIMAX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OMNIMAX) theater. The [Museum of Oriental Ceramics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Oriental_Ceramics,_Osaka) holds more than 2,000 pieces of ceramics, from China, Korea, Japan and Vietnam, featuring displays of some of their Korean [celadon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celadon) under natural light. [Osaka Municipal Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Municipal_Museum_of_Art) is inside [Tennōji Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenn%C5%8Dji_Park), housing over 8,000 pieces of Japanese and Chinese paintings and sculptures. The [Osaka Museum of History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Museum_of_History), opened in 2001, is located in a 13-story modern building providing a view of [Osaka Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Castle). Its exhibits cover the history of Osaka from pre-history to the present day. Osaka Museum of Natural History houses a collection related to natural history and life.

### Sports

Osaka hosts four professional sport teams: one of them is the [Orix Buffaloes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orix_Buffaloes), a [Nippon Professional Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Professional_Baseball) team, playing its home games at [Kyocera Dome Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Dome). Another baseball team, the [Hanshin Tigers](490998.md), although based in [Nishinomiya, Hyōgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishinomiya,_Hy%C5%8Dgo), plays a part of its home games in Kyocera Dome Osaka as well, when their homeground [Koshien Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koshien_Stadium) is occupied with the annual [National High School Baseball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_High_School_Baseball_Championship) games during summer season.

There are two [J.League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J.League) clubs, [Gamba Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamba_Osaka), plays its home games at [Suita City Football Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suita_City_Football_Stadium). Another club [Cerezo Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerezo_Osaka), plays its home games at [Yanmar Stadium Nagai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanmar_Stadium_Nagai). The city is home to [Osaka Evessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Evessa), a basketball team that plays in the [B.League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B.League). Evessa has won the first three championships of the league since its establishment. [Kintetsu Liners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintetsu_Liners), a [rugby union](25405.md) team, play in the [Top League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Rugby_League_One). After winning promotion in 2008–09, they will again remain in the competition for the 2009–10 season. Their base is the [Hanazono Rugby Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kintetsu_Hanazono_Rugby_Stadium).

The *_Haru Basho_* (春場所; "Spring Tournament"), one of the six regular tournaments of professional [sumo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumo), is held annually in Osaka at [Osaka Prefectural Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Prefectural_Gymnasium).

Another major annual sporting event that takes place in Osaka is [Osaka International Ladies Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_International_Ladies_Marathon). Held usually at the end of January every year, the 42.195 km (26.219-mile) race starts from Nagai Stadium, runs through [Nakanoshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakanoshima), [Midōsuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid%C5%8Dsuji) and [Osaka Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Castle) park, and returns to the stadium. Another yearly event held at Nagai Stadium is the Osaka Gran Prix Athletics games operated by the [International Association of Athletics Federations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Athletics_Federations) (IAAF) in May. The Osaka GP is the only IAAF games annually held in Japan.

Osaka made the bid for the [2008 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics) and the [2008 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Paralympics) but was eliminated in the first round of voting on July 13, 2001, which awarded both games to Beijing.

Osaka was one of the host cities of the official [Women's Volleyball World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Volleyball_World_Championship) for its [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIVB_Volleyball_Women's_World_Championship), [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIVB_Volleyball_Women's_World_Championship) and [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIVB_Volleyball_Women's_World_Championship) editions.

Osaka is the home of the 2011 created [Japan Bandy Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Bandy_Federation) and the introduction of [bandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandy), in the form of [rink bandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rink_bandy), was made in the city.[^cite_note-100] In July 2012 the first Japan Bandy Festival was organized.[^cite_note-101]

### Media

Osaka serves as one of the media hubs for Japan, housing headquarters of many media-related companies. Abundant television production takes place in the city and every nationwide TV network (with the exception of TXN network) registers its secondary-key station in Osaka. All five nationwide newspaper majors also house their regional headquarters, and most local newspapers nationwide have branches in Osaka. However major film productions are uncommon in the city. Most major films are produced in nearby [Kyoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto) or in Tokyo. The [Ad Council Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_Council_Japan) was founded in 1971 is based in Osaka, now it is the Osaka branch.

#### Newspapers

All five major national newspapers of Japan, *[The Asahi Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Asahi_Shimbun)*, *[Mainichi Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainichi_Shimbun)*, *[Nihon Keizai Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihon_Keizai_Shimbun)*, *[Sankei Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sankei_Shimbun)* and *[Yomiuri Shimbun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomiuri_Shimbun)*,[^cite_note-102] have their regional headquarters in Osaka and issue their regional editions. Furthermore, Osaka houses Osaka Nichi-nichi Shimbun, its newspaper press. Other newspaper-related companies located in Osaka include the regional headquarters of FujiSankei Business i.;Houchi Shimbunsha; [Nikkan Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikkan_Sports); [Sports Nippon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Nippon), and offices of [Kyodo News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyodo_News) [Jiji Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiji_Press); [Reuters](18998750.md); [Bloomberg L.P.](432006.md)

The five TV networks are represented by [Asahi Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahi_Broadcasting_Corporation) ([ANN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Nippon_News_Network)), [Kansai Telecasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_Telecasting_Corporation) ([FNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji_News_Network)), [Mainichi Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBS_\(TV_channel\)), Inc. ([JNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_News_Network)), [Television Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_Osaka), Inc. ([TXN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TX_Network)) and [Yomiuri Telecasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomiuri_Telecasting_Corporation) ([NNN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_News_Network)), headquartered in Osaka. [NHK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHK) has also its regional station based in the city. AM Radio services are provided by NHK as well as the ABC Radio (Asahi Broadcasting Corporation), MBS Radio (Mainichi Broadcasting System, Inc.) and Radio Osaka ([Osaka Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Broadcasting_Corporation)) and headquartered in the city. FM services are available from NHK, [FM OSAKA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM_OSAKA), [FM802](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM802) and [FM Cocolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM_Cocolo), the last providing programs in multiple languages including English.

#### Publishing companies

Osaka is home to many publishing companies, including Examina, Izumi Shoin, Kaihou Shuppansha, Keihanshin Elmagazine, Seibundo Shuppan, Sougensha, and Toho Shuppan.

## Education



[Kansai University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansai_University)



[Osaka Metropolitan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Metropolitan_University)

Public elementary and junior high schools in Osaka are operated by the city of Osaka. Its supervisory organization on educational matters is Osaka City Board of Education.[^cite_note-103] Likewise, public high schools are operated by the [Osaka Prefectural Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Prefectural_Board_of_Education).

Osaka once had a large number of universities and high schools, but because of growing campuses and the need for larger area, many chose to move to the suburbs, including [Osaka University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_University).[^cite_note-104]

Historically foreign expatriates in the Kansai region preferred to live in [Kobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobe) rather than Osaka. As a result, until 1991 the Osaka area had no schools catering to expatriate children.[^cite_note-Stewartp43-105] [Osaka International School of Kwansei Gakuin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_International_School_of_Kwansei_Gakuin), founded in 1991, is located in nearby [Minoh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoh,_Osaka),[^cite_note-106] and it was the first international school in the Osaka area.[^cite_note-Stewartp43-105] The [Great Hanshin-Awaji earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Hanshin_earthquake) of 1995 caused a decline in demand for international schools, as there were about 2,500 U.S. nationals resident in Osaka after the earthquake while the pre-earthquake number was about 5,000. [American Chamber of Commerce in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chamber_of_Commerce_in_Japan) (ACCJ) Kansai chapter president Norman Solberg stated that since 2002 the numbers of expatriates in Kansai were recovering "but the fact is there is still a persistent exodus to Tokyo."[^cite_note-107] In 2001 the city of Osaka and [YMCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YMCA) established the [Osaka YMCA International School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_YMCA_International_School).[^cite_note-Stewartp43-105]
