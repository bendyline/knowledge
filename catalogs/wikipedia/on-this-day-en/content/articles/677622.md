**Kolejowy Klub Sportowy Lech Poznań** (lit. 'Railway Sports Club Lech Poznań'), commonly referred to as **KKS Lech Poznań** or simply **Lech Poznań** (Polish pronunciation: [\[lɛx ˈpɔznaj̃\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish)), is a Polish professional [football](10568.md) club based in [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84) and currently competing in the [Ekstraklasa](30871677.md), the nation's highest division.

The club was established on 19 March 1922 as *KS Lutnia Dębiec*, later changing its name several times. Until 1994, the club was closely linked to [Polish State Railways (PKP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_State_Railways). As a result, its popular nickname is ***Kolejorz*** [\[kɔˈlɛjɔʂ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish), which means **The Railwayman** in local [slang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slang). The club's debut in the Polish top division took place in the year 1948. The brightest era of Lech was in the early 1980s and early 1990s when they won 5 Polish league titles, 3 Polish Cups and a Polish Super Cup. Lech has won the Polish league a total of ten times, most recently in 2026, and is the most popular football club in the [Greater Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland) region.[^cite_note-5]

## History

### Formation and early years (1920–1945)

In August 1920, a group of young teenagers from the Catholic Youth Association decided to split off and form their own football team. The founders of the club were: Jan Nowak, Antoni Dyzman, Jan Dyzman, Leon Nowicki, Józef Magdziak, Kazimierz Zmuda, Stanisław Nowicki, Stefan Fiedler, Józef Gośliński, Leon Stachowski, Józef Blumreder and Jan Wojtek. The origin of Lech can be traced back to 19 March 1922, when it was officially registered as a football club.[^cite_note-6] The club's first official name was Towarzystwo Sportowe Liga Dębiec. In September 1922 the club gained a football pitch on Grzybowa street. The first match for the club was played in May 1922 against Urania Starołęka, which ended in a 1–1 draw. The club started its foundation in a low tier league, which at the time was the Class C.\




Historic Ty51 steam locomotive with the Lech Poznań crest, representing the club's traditions, by the stadium in Poznań

The club achieved promotion in 1928 to the Class B after six years of being in Class C. In 1932 the club was promoted to Class A where the biggest teams of the region played. From there they could get promoted to the First National Division, but the club would not achieve that goal before the outbreak of World War II. In autumn of 1933 the Klub Sportowy Kolejowego Przysposobienia Wojskowego Poznań ("Poznań Military Training Railway Sports Club") was founded or KPW. In 1945, shortly after the war ended, sporting officials made Lech the first club from the city.

### Downfall and the Miracle of Błażejewko (1947–1979)

In 1947, the Polish Football Association (PZPN) decided to create the first national division (Ekstraklasa). At first, the club was not admitted to the top flight, but the Kolejorz ("the railwayman", the popular nickname of the club) filed an appeal and the PZPN decided, in a special meeting, to extend the First Division to 14 teams, including the KKS (at that time called Kolejowy Klub Sportowy Poznań) and [Widzew Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widzew_%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA). The first match was against Widzew Łódź, lost 3–4.

The club changed its name again in January 1957, this time to Klub Sportowy Lech Poznań and in December to Kolejowy Klub Sportowy Lech Poznań, which lasted throughout the history of the team. That same year turned out to be one of the worst for the club, since it finished last and was relegated to the second division. Lech only gained twelve points in 22 games, despite having striker Teodor Anioła, the club's top scorer, with 141 goals and top scorer of the Polish championship in three consecutive editions (1949–1951).[^cite_note-7] Along with Edmund Białas and Henryk Czapczyk, Anioła formed the famous trio known as ABC. During that period, the club managed to finish third in the top division twice, as the best result, before its relegation to second division.

Lech managed to return to the top division in 1961, but after two seasons with poor results, the blue team was relegated again in 1963. The club was even demoted to the third division, then known as the Interprovincial Division (Liga międzywojewódzka), in one of the biggest sports crisis of the organization. In 1972 the club returned to the first division, in which they had to fight again to avoid relegation every season. Coach Jerzy Kopa, who arrived from [Szombierki Bytom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szombierki_Bytom), was responsible for reviving Lech spectacularly. He took over the team in 1976, when they were bottom of the table. Kopa gathered players at a training camp in [Błażejewko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C5%82a%C5%BCejewko), saved the team from relegation and twelve months later qualified for the first time to play in Europe after finishing third in the league, just two points behind the champion, Wisła Kraków. Therefore, this transformation became known as The Miracle of Błażejewko.[^cite_note-8] The club's first participation in the [UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup) in [1978–79](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978%E2%80%9379_UEFA_Cup) was brief, as they were eliminated in the first round by MSV Duisburg.

### Golden age of Lech (1980–1993)

The arrival of coach [Wojciech Łazarek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojciech_%C5%81azarek) in 1980 at the club was key to overcome third place and European participation. That year the team reached the final of the Polish Cup for the first time, losing 0–5 to [Legia Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legia_Warsaw) in [Częstochowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cz%C4%99stochowa). Two years later, the club managed to win the first title in its history, the Polish Cup, by defeating [Pogoń Szczecin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogo%C5%84_Szczecin) 1–0 in [Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wroc%C5%82aw).



The striker [Andrzej Juskowiak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Juskowiak), top goalscorer and champion in the [Ekstraklasa](30871677.md) in 1990 with 18 goals.

The league championships of 1983 and 1984 went down in history as they were the first two league titles of the Kolejorz and for winning on such tight margins against Widzew Łódź. The first league championship for Lech was a point of advantage (39) over Widzew (38). The 15 goals scored by the top scorer of the tournament, [Mirosław Okoński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miros%C5%82aw_Oko%C5%84ski) and the participation of other players like [Krzysztof Pawlak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzysztof_Pawlak) and [Józef Adamiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%B3zef_Adamiec) were very important to win their first league championship. Meanwhile, the championship of the following season both teams staged an exciting tournament and tied at 42 points. Lech defended championship by having a better difference of goals than Widzew to break the tie. That season was historic for the blue team, as they got their first double by becoming champions of the Polish Cup, after winning in the final at Wisła Kraków (3–0).

As Polish champions, Lech participated for the first time in the European Cup, although they could not pass the first round in the two seasons. In its first season it was eliminated by [Athletic Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao). In the first leg in Poland, Mariusz Niewiadomski and Mirosław Okoński scored the first two Lech goals in the tournament and the team won 2–0. However, the return match in San Mamés was a nightmare for the Poles and the Spanish team qualified by winning 4–0. The following season the team faced the current champion, [Liverpool F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.), who won by a 5–0 aggregate.

In 1988, Lech won another Cup by beating Legia in Łódź in the penalty shootout. In the second round of the European Cup, Lech faced [Barcelona](68187.md), coached by [Johan Cruyff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Cruyff). After finishing the two games in a 1–1 draw, Barcelona, in the end the tournament, could only eliminate Lech in the penalty shootout.

Jerzy Kopa returned to Lech in 1990 along with [Andrzej Strugarek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Strugarek) and Kolejorz returned to be proclaimed league champions for the third time. [Andrzej Juskowiak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrzej_Juskowiak) was the top scorer of the tournament with 18 goals and his team finished with 42 points, two more than the runner-up, [Zagłębie Lubin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zag%C5%82%C4%99bie_Lubin). [Henryk Apostel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henryk_Apostel), however, was the coach who led Lech to two new championships in 1992 and 1993. The first one was achieved with a win over GKS Katowice, while the second one tied in points with the second team, Legia, and only won because Legia was penalized for disputed match fixing.\
&#x20;In the autumn of 1990, Lech played one of the most spectacular qualifiers of the last decade in the European Cup. At Bułgarska street stadium the Polish club defeated [Olympique Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille) 3–2 in the first leg of the second round. The return match at the Stade Vélodrome, the French team, thrashed Lech 6–1, in a match in which most of the Polish players complained of food poisoning. Since 1993 the club entered into a major financial crisis and had to sell its most important players to continue in professional football.

### New disappointments and successes (1994–present)

Lech managed to stay in the middle of the table and their best result was fourth place in 1999, which allowed them to play in the [1999–00 UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999%E2%80%9300_UEFA_Cup), where they eliminated [Liepājas Metalurgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Liep%C4%81jas_Metalurgs) in the qualifying round and were defeated by [IFK Göteborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFK_G%C3%B6teborg) in the first round. However, just a few months later, in 2000, Lech was relegated to the second division after 28 years of presence in the top flight. Lech's first season in the second division was a disaster, as they were very close to falling to the third division. It was only with a great effort that the club was saved from relegation and even won the promotion the next season to the first division.



[Robert Lewandowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lewandowski) scored 32 goals in 58 matches with Lech Poznań (2008–2010).

In their first year of the return to the I league (2002–03) Lech focused on ensuring permanence. The following season began with a very negative dynamic for the Kolejorz. After five days, the club hired a new coach, [Czesław Michniewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czes%C5%82aw_Michniewicz).[^cite_note-9] The unexpected appointment of the young coach turned out to be a shock, since Lech finished the season in sixth position. Most important, however, was the conquest of a Polish Cup by defeating their great rival, Legia Warsaw, in the final two games in 2004. Several days later, the fans in Poznań celebrated the victory of Lech in the Super Cup against Wisła Kraków. Although the next two seasons did not bring any success of that proportion, Lech managed to finish at the top of the table of each season with coach [Franciszek Smuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciszek_Smuda).\


Smuda formed a strong team with the arrival at the club of players like [Robert Lewandowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Lewandowski), [Hernan Rengifo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernan_Rengifo), [Semir Štilić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semir_%C5%A0tili%C4%87), [Marcin Zając](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcin_Zaj%C4%85c) and [Rafał Murawski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafa%C5%82_Murawski). In the Ekstraklasa 2008–09 season, Lech had a great season and finished in third place and qualified for the UEFA Europa League thanks, in part, to the 14 goals scored by Robert Lewandowski. On 19 May 2009, Lech won the Cup for the fifth time by beating Ruch Chorzów with a solo goal by [Sławomir Peszko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C5%82awomir_Peszko) at [Stadion Śląski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_%C5%9Al%C4%85ski).

The following season, [Jacek Zieliński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacek_Zieli%C5%84ski_\(footballer,_born_1961\)) replaced Franciszek Smuda, who was hired as the manager of the [Poland national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team). With many of the players who achieved third place and the cup last season, Zieliński managed to lead Lech to the sixth championship in their history in the [2009–10 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Ekstraklasa). The striker Robert Lewandowski returned to be a reference in attack and was top scorer of the championship with 18 goals. In their participation in the 2010–11 Champions League, they were eliminated by [Sparta Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Sparta_Prague) in the third round and without Lewandowski, who was transferred to [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund). One of their most successful European appearance was in the [2010–11 UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_UEFA_Europa_League), in which they eliminated [Dnipro Dnipropetrovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_Dnipropetrovsk) to enter the group stage of the tournament for the first time. Lech finished second in their group with [Manchester City](165813.md), leaving [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC) and [FC Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Red_Bull_Salzburg) out of the tournament. However, they were eliminated by [S.C. Braga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.C._Braga), runner-up of the tournament months later, in the round of 32 after winning in Poland (1–0) and losing in Portugal (2–0).

After crashing out of UEFA Europa League qualifying rounds in August 2014, [Maciej Skorża](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maciej_Skor%C5%BCa) replaced [Mariusz Rumak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariusz_Rumak) as manager.[^cite_note-10] At the end of the [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Ekstraklasa), Lech claimed their seventh league title.[^cite_note-11]

In the [2021–22 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Lech_Pozna%C5%84_season), during which Lech celebrated its 100th anniversary, the team again led by Maciej Skorża finished runners-up in the [Polish Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Polish_Cup), losing 3–1 against [Raków Częstochowa](8538149.md),[^cite_note-12] and won the club's eighth [championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Ekstraklasa), their first in seven years.[^cite_note-13]

Skorża left the team shortly after, citing personal reasons, and was replaced by Dutchman [John van den Brom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_van_den_Brom) for the [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Lech_Pozna%C5%84_season). Lech crashed out of the UEFA Champions League [first qualifying round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Champions_League#First_qualifying_round) after losing to Azerbaijani side [Qarabağ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaraba%C4%9F_FK) 1–5 on aggregate.[^cite_note-14] The team went on to have a successful [UEFA Europa Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Europa_Conference_League) campaign, reaching second place in the group stage which included [Villarreal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villarreal_CF), [Austria Wien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Austria_Wien) and [Hapoel Be'er Sheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapoel_Be'er_Sheva_F.C.),[^cite_note-15] then eliminating [Nordic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_countries) teams [Bodø/Glimt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Bod%C3%B8/Glimt) and [Djurgårdens IF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djurg%C3%A5rdens_IF_Fotboll) to reach the quarter-finals,[^cite_note-16] where their successful run ended after a 4–6 defeat on aggregate to [Fiorentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACF_Fiorentina).[^cite_note-17] They were the first Polish team to win a knockout phase round of a European competition that took place after the winter break since Legia Warsaw in 1991, and the first to move through two post-winter rounds since [Górnik Zabrze](677580.md) in 1970.[^cite_note-18]

The following [2023–24 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Lech_Pozna%C5%84_season) was marked by disappointment. Lech failed to progress past the third qualifying round of the Europa Conference League, losing to Slovak side [Spartak Trnava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Spartak_Trnava) 3–4 on aggregate, despite winning the first game at home 2–1.[^cite_note-19] In December 2023, trailing in the league by eight points by the winter break, van den Brom was dismissed and replaced with [Mariusz Rumak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariusz_Rumak),[^cite_note-Komunikat_klubu-20] returning to the role after nine-and-a-half years. Lech's form did not improve under Rumak; they were unable to take the lead in the table, lost to lower-placed teams [Puszcza Niepołomice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puszcza_Niepo%C5%82omice) and [Ruch Chorzów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruch_Chorz%C3%B3w),[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] and were eliminated from the [2023–24 Polish Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Polish_Cup) after conceding in the 119th minute of a quarter-final match against [Pogoń Szczecin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogo%C5%84_Szczecin).[^cite_note-23] Still in title contention with three games to go,[^cite_note-24] Lech finished the [season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Ekstraklasa) in 5th after losing at home to [Korona Kielce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korona_Kielce) on 25 May, hours after their [reserve team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_Pozna%C5%84_II) was demoted to the [fourth division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/III_liga),[^cite_note-25] allowing Korona to stay in the top-flight in lieu of Lech's neighbors [Warta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warta_Pozna%C5%84).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

For the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Lech_Pozna%C5%84_season), Lech appointed [Niels Frederiksen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niels_Frederiksen) as manager, with the goal of reclaiming the Polish title. After finishing 2024 on top of the table, Lech fell behind Raków Częstochowa early into 2025. Following a late surge in form, and a 1–0 home win against [Piast Gliwice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piast_Gliwice) in the last matchday of the season, Lech won their ninth league title, their second in the last three years.[^cite_note-28]

Ahead of the [2025–26 season](80152861.md), Lech reinforced their roster with several signings, including winger [Luis Palma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Palma), striker [Yannick Agnero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yannick_Agnero), and last season's Ekstraklasa Defender of the Season [Mateusz Skrzypczak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mateusz_Skrzypczak), while also facing long-term injuries of key players [Patrik Wålemark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrik_W%C3%A5lemark), [Ali Gholizadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Gholizadeh) and [Radosław Murawski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rados%C5%82aw_Murawski).[^cite_note-29] Their season began with a [2025 Polish Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Polish_Super_Cup) loss to [Legia Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legia_Warsaw) on 13 July.[^cite_note-30] After crashing out of UEFA Champions League and UEFA Europa League qualifying, Lech entered the UEFA Conference League [league phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_UEFA_Conference_League_league_phase), where they finished 11th to advance to the knockout stages. Lech ended their European campaign when they were eliminated in the round of 16 by Ukrainian side [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk).[^cite_note-31] Soon after, they exited the [2025–26 Polish Cup](79976641.md) in the quarter-finals after losing to eventual winners [Górnik Zabrze](677580.md).[^cite_note-32] On 16 May 2026, Lech defeated [Radomiak Radom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radomiak_Radom) 3–1 away to win their second consecutive title with one game to go; it was their first successful title defense since 1993.[^cite_note-33]

## European participation

Lech Poznań has played over 160 matches in European competition since 1978. Among the most memorable games in the club's history were the clashes against [Barcelona](68187.md) in the 1988–89 season of the [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) second round. After both matches ended with 1–1 draw, Lech Poznań lost the penalty shoot-out 4–5. Barcelona eventually went on to win the tournament.

During the [1983–84 European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_European_Cup) season, Lech earned a 2–0 win at home against Spanish champions [Athletic Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao). During the 1990–91 season, Lech eliminated the Greek champions [Panathinaikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panathinaikos_F.C.) in the first round, with a 5–1 score on aggregate. In the next tie, Lech was knocked out by [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille) but won the first leg 3–2 at home.

During the [2008–09 UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_UEFA_Cup) season, Lech made it to the group stage of the competition after knocking out higher seeded teams of [Grasshopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasshopper_Club_Z%C3%BCrich) (notching its then-greatest margin of victory in Europe, with a 6–0 win at home) and [Austria Wien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Austria_Wien) (scoring the decisive goal in the last minute of extra-time). In the group stage, Lech finished third-placed ahead of [Nancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Nancy) and [Feyenoord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feyenoord) to secure a place in the third round, where it was knocked out by the Italian side [Udinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udinese_Calcio).

[^cite_note-34]

The 2010–11 European campaign saw Lech not only qualify for the group stage of the [UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_UEFA_Europa_League), but also progress from their group ahead of Italian giants [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC), before losing at the round of 32 to eventual finalists [Braga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.C._Braga). In later years, they reached the UEFA Europa League group stages twice more, in the [2015–16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_UEFA_Europa_League_group_stage) and [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_UEFA_Europa_League_group_stage) seasons.

In the 2022–23 season, while competing in the [UEFA Europa Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Europa_Conference_League), Lech reached their first ever European quarter-final where they were eliminated by Italian club [Fiorentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACF_Fiorentina). In 2025, after failing to qualify to UEFA Champions League and UEFA Europa League, they entered the [2025–26 UEFA Conference League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_UEFA_Conference_League), eventually progressing to the round of 16 where they were knocked out by Ukrainian side [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk).[^cite_note-35]

### Notable former players

- [Poland](22936.md) [Teodor Anioła](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teodor_Anio%C5%82a) – striker, the best all-time league goalscorer in the club's history, represented Lech from 1948 until 1957.

- [Poland](22936.md) [Jarosław Araszkiewicz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaros%C5%82aw_Araszkiewicz) – attacking midfielder, won five championship titles with Lech Poznań. Finished his career in the age of 38.

- [Poland](22936.md) [Jacek Bąk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacek_B%C4%85k) – defender, played in the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) and [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), former player of French clubs [Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais) and [Lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_Lens).

## Stadiums

### Dębiec Stadium

Initially the club's first stadium was located in the [Dębiec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C4%99biec,_Pozna%C5%84) district between two train tracks.[^cite_note-46] It belonged to [PKP (the Polish state railways)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_State_Railways) and was demolished in 2013 after a long period of inactivity.[^cite_note-47]

### Edmund Szyc Stadium

Main article: [Stadion Edmunda Szyca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_Edmunda_Szyca)

Edmund Szyc Stadium is a currently ruined [multi-purpose stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-purpose_stadium) in the [Wilda district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilda,_Pozna%C5%84), named after [Edmund Szyc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Szyc?action=edit\&redlink=1), one of founders of [Warta Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warta_Pozna%C5%84).[^cite_note-48] It is the historical home of the other football team [Warta Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warta_Pozna%C5%84),[^cite_note-49] but Lech played there sporadically between the 1950s and 1970s.

### Stadion Poznań

Main article: [Stadion Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_Pozna%C5%84)

The Stadion Poznań is the home ground of Lech Poznań, and was one of the venues for the group phase of [Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012). It has a league capacity of 42,837 (all seated). The stadium was originally built between 1968 and 1980. From its inauguration in August 1980 Lech Poznań has used the ground as its main venue; since 2010 it has also been used by [Warta Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warta_Pozna%C5%84), which currently plays in Fortuna 1. Liga.[^cite_note-50] The ground is situated on the street *ul. Bułgarska 17* in the southwestern part of the city ([Grunwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grunwald,_Pozna%C5%84) district).

In the years 2003–10 the stadium underwent a complete reconstruction, including the building of four new fully covered stands.[^cite_note-51] Currently it is the fifth largest stadium in Poland (after [Kazimierz Górski National Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazimierz_G%C3%B3rski_National_Stadium), [Silesia Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesian_Stadium), [The Municipal Stadium in Wroclaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_Miejski_\(Wroc%C5%82aw\)) and [PGE Arena Gdańsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_Energa_Gda%C5%84sk)) and third largest in [Ekstraklasa](30871677.md) (after the latter two).[^cite_note-52] The grand opening after final renovation took place on 20 September 2010, with [Sting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sting_\(musician\))'s [*Symphonicity Tour*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphonicities#Symphonicity_Tour) concert.

## Supporters

Academic studies identify Lech Poznań’s organised fan scene as closely associated with right-wing nationalism in Poland. Research distinguishes Poznań’s ultras from apolitical hooligans, noting that political meaning is deliberately expressed, especially in European fixtures or matches with symbolic opponents.[^cite_note-A_Squad_with_No_Left_Wingers-53] News media also describes Lech's [ultras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultras) as politically either right-wing or far-right.[^cite_note-Marca_6_Nov_2025-54][^cite_note-TVP_World_6_Nov_2025-55] Lech Poznań has been repeatedly sanctioned by [UEFA](32332.md) for racist chanting and racist stadium displays of its supporters, including monetary fines and stadium bans.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

In August 2013, during a Europa League qualifier at home to [Žalgiris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_%C5%BDalgiris), a group of Lech Poznań ultras hung a banner reading "Litewski chamie, klęknij przed polskim panem" ("Lithuanian scum, kneel before the Polish master").[^cite_note-58] Four individuals were later convicted in 2016 by a Poznań district court for publicly insulting people of Lithuanian nationality; they received community service penalties. The court overturned a broad ban on attending mass events that had also been imposed.[^cite_note-TVN24_2016-59] The incident drew official condemnation from the Polish Ministry of Foreign Affairs and the Lithuanian diplomatic service, and the club's authorities publicly condemned the banner.[^cite_note-TVN24_2016-59] In September 2013, UEFA sanctioned Lech Poznań for the incident and the club was ordered to close *Kocioł*, the most vocal supporters' stand, for its next European home match, as well as receiving a €5,000 fine.[^cite_note-60]

In January 2014, several Jewish organisations criticised a decision by a Poznań prosecutor not to pursue charges against Lech's supporters accused of antisemitic chanting during a match against [Widzew Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widzew_%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA), a club from a city with a large pre-war Jewish population.[^cite_note-CNN_16_Jan_2014-61] Ultras shouted slogans including "You belong in [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz)", "Ride on, Jews", "Into the ovens", and "Go to the gas, RTS", with RTS referring to the Łódź club. The prosecutor’s office stated that no charges would be brought because the chants were directed at rival fans rather than Jews as a group and therefore were not deemed an incitement to racial hatred. Prosecutor's spokeswoman Magdalena Mazur-Prus described the chants as "reprehensible and unacceptable" but argued that they did not meet the legal threshold for a criminal offence.[^cite_note-CNN_16_Jan_2014-61][^cite_note-62]

In July 2015, ahead of a UEFA Champions League qualifying match between Lech Poznań and [Sarajevo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Sarajevo), Lech hooligans clashed with Sarajevo supporters in the streets of Sarajevo, with police reporting 27 injuries involving fans from both clubs and officers, significant property damage, and at least one detention.[^cite_note-63] During the same qualifying tie, Lech Poznań supporters displayed a banner reading "The Piła Legion – the blood of our race", which UEFA classified as racist.[^cite_note-Inside_World_Football_10_August_2015-64] In response, UEFA's Control, Ethics and Disciplinary Body ordered Lech Poznań to play one home match behind closed doors and imposed a €50,000 fine. The Polish Football Association (PZPN) formally protested the sanction, stating that it accepted UEFA’s zero tolerance policy on racism and Nazism but argued that the banner did not constitute racist content and had previously appeared at European matches.[^cite_note-Inside_World_Football_10_August_2015-64]

Lech Poznań supporters threatened and carried out a boycott of a 17 September 2015 Europa League match against [Belenenses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.F._Os_Belenenses) over UEFA's decision to donate €1 from each ticket to refugee assistance. Approximately 8,0000 spectators attended this match, well below the club's average of 20,000, and a "Stop Islamization" banner was displayed at the stadium entrance; ultras had earlier displayed anti-refugee flags.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

On 1 October 2017, ultras displayed a banner reading "The Lech Poznań stadium and fans...the last bastion of freedom in the city frazzled by leftist paranoia" during the match against Legia Warsaw. The phrase "leftist paranoia" referred to the mayor of Poznań, [Jacek Jaśkowiak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacek_Ja%C5%9Bkowiak), participating in a local march for Equality and publicly supporting LGBTQ rights.[^cite_note-A_Squad_with_No_Left_Wingers-53]

On 20 May 2018, during a decisive Ekstraklasa match against Legia Warsaw, Lech's ultras threw flares onto the pitch, resulting in the referee stopping play in the 77th minute. Dark smoke and supporters pushing against barriers led riot police to intervene. Lech were ordered to play eight subsequent matches without spectators by the regional governor, covering the first five league matches of the next season and their first three UEFA Europa League fixtures. Legia were awarded a 3–0 victory.[^cite_note-67]

In August 2025, the [Football Federation of Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_Kosovo) condemned the display of a "Kosovo is Serbia" banner by Lech Poznań supporters during a Champions League third qualifying round match against [Red Star Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Star_Belgrade).[^cite_note-telegrafi_13_August_2025-68] The federation described the banner as a political provocation and a breach of UEFA rules prohibiting political and discriminatory messages at football matches. Red Star Belgrade subsequently thanked Lech Poznań and its supporters publicly, which the FFK said further legitimised the message. The FFK submitted a formal complaint to UEFA seeking disciplinary action against both clubs.[^cite_note-69] UEFA ruled in favour of the FFK, imposing a €10,000 fine on Lech Poznań for the conduct of its supporters.[^cite_note-telegrafi_13_August_2025-68][^cite_note-70]

In November 2025, Lech's ultras and [Rayo Vallecano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayo_Vallecano)'s ultra group Bukaneros clashed in the Vallecas district of Madrid ahead of their Conference League match, with several hundred participants involved in street fighting that required police intervention, including warning shots, and resulted in at least one injury and one arrest. News media reported that the confrontation followed an earlier provocation involving Lech Poznań graffiti and stickers at the Rayo Vallecano stadium, and described the clash as rooted in opposing political ideologies, with Bukaneros identified as left wing and Lech Poznań's ultras as right wing.[^cite_note-Marca_6_Nov_2025-54][^cite_note-TVP_World_6_Nov_2025-55]

### Friendships and rivalries

For over three decades, Lech supporters have had a fellowship with fans from [Arka Gdynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arka_Gdynia) and [Cracovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KS_Cracovia), sometimes called the *Wielka Triada* or *The Great Triad*. Close friendship also links Lech fans with [KSZO Ostrowiec Świętokrzyski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSZO_Ostrowiec_%C5%9Awi%C4%99tokrzyski) and [ŁKS Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81KS_%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA) supporters. Among the more ardent element of supporters, there are some private contacts with [Fratria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fratria), fans of [Spartak Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Spartak_Moscow), and [Crveni Đavoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crveni_%C4%90avoli), fans of [Radnički Kragujevac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Radni%C4%8Dki_1923) from [Serbia](29265.md).



Lech supporters during 2014–15 Ekstraklasa season

The biggest rival is [Legia Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legia_Warsaw) with whom they contest the "Derby of Poland". [Wisła Kraków](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wis%C5%82a_Krak%C3%B3w), [Lechia Gdańsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lechia_Gda%C5%84sk) and [Śląsk Wrocław](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Al%C4%85sk_Wroc%C5%82aw) are also big rivals due to the fans friendship with [Arka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arka_Gdynia) and [Cracovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KS_Cracovia_\(football\)), similarly [Korona Kielce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korona_Kielce) are disliked due to the friendship with [KSZO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KSZO_Ostrowiec_%C5%9Awi%C4%99tokrzyski) and [Widzew Łódź](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Widzew_%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA) due to [ŁKS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81KS_%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA). Other teams that can be considered rivals are [Ruch Chorzów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruch_Chorz%C3%B3w) and [Pogoń Szczecin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogo%C5%84_Szczecin). In past the "Greater Poland derby" was played against regional rivals [Dyskobolia Grodzisk Wielkopolski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyskobolia_Grodzisk_Wielkopolski) before their decline.

Relations with local rival [Warta Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warta_Pozna%C5%84) are neutral as the clubs have almost always played in different leagues and many fans attend matches of both teams.

### The Poznań

Main article: [The Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pozna%C5%84)

The fans' goal celebration involving the turning of their backs to the pitch, joining arms and jumping up and down in unison—originated in 1961. It is known in the English speaking world as "The Poznan" after [Manchester City](165813.md) began using the celebration following their clash with Lech Poznań in the group stages of the 2010–11 UEFA Europa League. Also popular with fans of Scottish club Celtic who call their version "The Huddle", in homage to the team's pre-match ritual of a huddle before every game kicks off. In 2019, the expression "the Poznan" was included in the Cambridge English Dictionary.[^cite_note-71]

### Rap music

Many Polish rappers who hail from [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84) have been strongly linked to the Lech supporter scene and the club prominently features in their music. [Peja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peja_\(rapper\)) was an ardent supporter since he was 15 years old, and was active in the [hooligan scene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_hooliganism_in_Poland) in the 90s.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] Evtis,[^cite_note-74] Ascetoholix,[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] Bzyk[^cite_note-77] and DJ Decks are all prominent supporters. The fans have produced, recorded and released two rap CD's called *Definicja Kibol* and *Definicja Kibol 2* as compilation of various artists.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

## Other departments

### Lech Poznań II

Main article: [Lech Poznań II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_Pozna%C5%84_II)

The club operates a reserve team.

They gained promotion in the 2003–04 season to the [third tier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/II_liga) after winning the league and beating [Jarota Jarocin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarota_Jarocin) 2–0 twice, 4–0 on aggregate. In that same season, they reached the 1st round of the [Polish Cup](772047.md) but were knocked out by [Górnik Konin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%B3rnik_Konin) following a 3–1 loss. After the 2006–07 season, the reserve teams were scrapped in favour of a [central youth league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C5%82oda_Ekstraklasa), meaning that between 2007 and 2013 the team ceased to exist. They were reinstated to their previous league position for the 2013–14 season.

### Lech Poznań UAM

Main article: [Lech Poznań UAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech_Pozna%C5%84_UAM?action=edit\&redlink=1)

Lech's women section was opened on 26 August 2021.[^cite_note-80] It was formed through a partnership with [Adam Mickiewicz University in Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Mickiewicz_University_in_Pozna%C5%84). It currently competes in [the top division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekstraliga_\(women's_football\)) after three promotions in four years, and is coached by Alicja Zając.

### Lech Poznań Academy

The **Lech Poznań Academy** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Akademia Lecha Poznań*) is the club's youth system, with several teams across all children's ages up until its most senior U-19 youth team.[^cite_note-81] The teams play in the [Central Junior League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Junior_League_\(Poland\)), which was at first formed to replace the clubs' reserve teams which participated in the league pyramid. The club's youth system is the most extensive and advanced in the country and has produced many players which went on to play in the senior team.

## KKS Wiara Lecha

Main article: [KKS Wiara Lecha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KKS_Wiara_Lecha)

**KKS Wiara Lecha** is a football club founded by Lech Poznań supporters in 2011. Only active supporters can play in the team and they have to have made a contribution to the supporter scene in order to be admitted to the squad.
