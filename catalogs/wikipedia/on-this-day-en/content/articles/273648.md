The **prime minister of Greenland** ([Greenlandic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_language): *Naalakkersuisut siulittaasuat*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Leader of the government'; [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_language): *Landsstyreformand*), also known as the **premier of Greenland**,[^cite_note-2] is the [head of government](37570.md) of [Greenland](12118.md), a territory of the [Kingdom of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Denmark). The prime minister is usually the person who is leader of the majority party in the [Parliament of Greenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Greenland) (Inatsisartut). [Jonathan Motzfeldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Motzfeldt) became the first prime minister after [home rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_rule) was granted to Greenland in 1979. The incumbent prime minister is [Jens-Frederik Nielsen](79438070.md).

## Difference from the prime minister of Denmark

Greenland's prime minister leads the domestic Greenlandic government ([Naalakkersuisut](23389805.md)) in areas transferred to self-rule, while the [prime minister of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Denmark) leads the [national government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Denmark) handling reserved matters (foreign policy, defense, monetary issues, citizenship, etc.). Under the 2009 Self-Government Act, Greenland controls its internal legislative and executive powers, whereas the Danish prime minister (as head of [the realm](11746442.md)'s government) handles foreign affairs, defense, and other Kingdom-wide issues, with formal limits and required consultations.[^cite_note-AckrenJakobsen-2015-3][^cite_note-Ackren-2022-4]

Within Greenland's self-government framework, the Greenlandic prime minister implements laws in transferred policy areas. By law, Greenlandic authorities "include legislative, executive and judicial powers in those areas that are within Greenlandic competence",[^cite_note-Ackren-2022-4] so the Greenlandic prime minister leads the autonomous Greenland government accountable to Greenland's parliament ([Inatsisartut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inatsisartut)). In contrast, the Danish prime minister has no direct executive or legislative authority in Greenland's self-governing areas.[^cite_note-Constantin-2026-5] The Self-Government Act explicitly treats "foreign, defence and security policy" as the prerogative of the Danish Government (after consulting Greenland if Greenlandic interests are involved).[^cite_note-AckrenJakobsen-2015-3] Thus, the Danish prime minister (via the Government) manages Denmark's foreign and security policy (limited by required [Folketing](429743.md) consent for wars or territorial changes), whereas the Greenlandic prime minister has no role in these areas.[^cite_note-Antsygina-2025-6]

Specific powers the Danish prime minister/Government has regarding Greenland include: negotiating international [treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) and agreements for the Kingdom (with mandatory information or consultation for those important to Greenland); deciding Greenland's defense and security policy (subject to [constitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Denmark) constraints and Folketing approval);[^cite_note-Antsygina-2025-6] presenting bills to the Folketing that would apply to Greenland (after submitting them to the Greenland government for comment as required by the Self-Government Act); issuing administrative orders affecting Greenland (also subject to prior Greenlandic consultation); and overseeing common matters like the annual subsidy to Greenland's self-government. In the case of [Greenlandic moves toward independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenlandic_independence), the Danish prime minister must negotiate terms with Greenland's government, and any final agreement would need Inatsisartut's approval, a Greenland referendum, and Folketing consent.[^cite_note-7]
