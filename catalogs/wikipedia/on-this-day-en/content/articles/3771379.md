The **Sikorsky S-64 Skycrane** is an American twin-engine [heavy-lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_crane) [helicopter](8286923.md). It is the civilian version of the [United States Army](32087.md)'s [Sikorsky CH-54 Tarhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_CH-54_Tarhe) heavy lifter.

Since 1992, the [type certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate) has been owned by [Erickson Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erickson_Inc.) (then trading as *Erickson Air-Crane*; in addition to the provision of [OEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_equipment_manufacturer) and [MRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maintenance,_repair_and_operations) functions,[^cite_note-1] Erickson has undertaken the manufacture of new S-64s, as well as the remanufacturing of existing CH-54s, using the **S-64 Aircrane** designation.

## Development

### Under Sikorsky

The Sikorsky S-64 was designed as an enlarged version of the prototype *flying crane* helicopter, the [Sikorsky S-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_S-60). One issue encountered during development was the lack of a suitable [turboshaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboshaft) powerplant; to tackle this availability issue, Sikorsky opted to cooperate with the American engine manufacturer [Pratt and Whitney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_and_Whitney) to modify their [JT12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_JT12) [turbojet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbojet) to produce a suitable engine for it.[^cite_note-Chandler_1994-2] One of the more innovative features that Sikorsky opted for was the incorporation of an automatic [flight control system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_control_system), effectively a [fly-by-wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fly-by-wire) arrangement, which was in part responsible for the rotorcraft being relatively easy to fly under [instrument flight rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_flight_rules) (IFR).[^cite_note-Chandler_1994-2]

The S-64 was equipped with a six-blade main rotor and was powered by a pair of 4,050 shaft horsepower (3,020 kW) [Pratt & Whitney JFTD12A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_T73) turboshaft engines. The prototype S-64 made its [maiden flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_flight) on 9 May 1962;[^cite_note-Janes_76_p386-3] it was promptly followed by two further prototypes. Amongst other activities, one prototype was flight tested by the [United States Army](32087.md) at [Fort Benning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Benning) as a potential replacement for the [Sikorsky CH-37 Mojave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_CH-37_Mojave) while the other two underwent an evaluation in [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany).[^cite_note-Chandler_1994-2][^cite_note-Jackson-4] While the Germans ultimately did not place an order for the type, the US Army did issue an initial order for six S-64A helicopters (under the designation [YCH-54A Tarhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CH-54_Tarhe)).[^cite_note-Chandler_1994-2] Separately, an initial batch of seven S-64E helicopters were built by Sikorsky for the civil market.

From the onset of development, Sikorsky intended for the S-64 to be capable of carrying as diverse a range of cargoes as possible; this ethos led to the incorporation of fittings for the carriage of [barges](4177.md) and of a specially-designed multipurpose "people pod" that was suited for use for personnel transport or as mobile hospital.[^cite_note-Chandler_1994-2][^cite_note-sikorsky_95-5] During the late 1960s, both Sikorsky and the S-64 were participants in the development of a 'flying lounge' module that could be carried by the S-64. Specifically, this module (built by the [Budd Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budd_Company)) was trial-operated in conjunction with [AirportTransit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AirportTransit?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Los Angeles Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Airways) as a faster means of transferring passengers between the airport and the city itself during 1967.[^cite_note-6]

### Under Erickson

Originally a [Sikorsky Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikorsky_Aircraft) product, the [type certificate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_certificate) and manufacturing rights were purchased from them by [Erickson Air-Crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erickson_Air-Crane) in 1992. Since that time, Erickson Air-Crane has become the manufacturer and world's largest operator of S-64 Aircranes and has made over 1,350 changes to the airframe, instrumentation, and payload capabilities of the helicopter. The Aircrane can be fitted with a 2,650 US gal (10,000 L) fixed retardant tank to assist in the control of bush fires.[^cite_note-7] The helicopter is capable of refilling its entire tank of water in 45 seconds from a tube (typically referred to as a snorkel) 18 inches (46 cm) in diameter.[^cite_note-8]

Erickson has introduced numerous improvements to the S-64 over the years. During March 2020, a new [composite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_material) main rotor blade, designed by Erickson for the S-64E to increase its overall lift capacity by roughly 3,000lb as well as reduce manufacturing costs by 33 percent and decrease maintenance costs by 75 percent, received formal approval from the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA);[^cite_note-9] these rotor blades have been designed to replace the metal counterparts on existing rotorcraft of various models.[^cite_note-10] In the mid 2010s, the company introduced a new avionics package for the platform, providing [Automatic Dependent Surveillance–Broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_Dependent_Surveillance%E2%80%93Broadcast) functionality along with measures to improve the pilot's situational awareness, specifically the Helicopter Terrain and Awareness System (HTAWS).[^cite_note-11] Starting in 2018, Erickson been developing an improved [water cannon](431462.md), which is lighter and electrically-driven in comparison to the traditional [hydraulically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulics)-driven counterparts.[^cite_note-12]

During the late 2010s and 2020s, Erickson has worked on the development of its autonomous S-64F+,[^cite_note-13] which it has promoted to the US Army as a potential optionally-piloted cargo delivery helicopter.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] In 2020, the company announced its plans to restart production of new-build S-64s.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

## Operational history

The S-64 fleet owned by Erickson Inc. has been frequently leased to various operators across the world, including companies and government agencies alike, for either short-term or longer term use to conduct fire suppression, civil protection, heavy lift construction, and timber harvesting missions. Erickson gives each of its S-64s an individual name, the best-known being ["Elvis"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_\(helicopter\)), used in fighting fires in Australia alongside "The Incredible Hulk" and "Isabelle". Other operators, such as Siller Brothers, have followed with their Sikorsky S-64E, *Andy's Pride*. One S-64E, nicknamed "Olga", was used to lift the top section of the [CN Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CN_Tower) into place in [Toronto](64646.md), Ontario, Canada.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

During the 2010s, multiple S-64s were sold to both the Italian and Korean Forest Services for fire suppression and emergency response duties.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] In April 2024, it was announced that Erickson Inc.’s aerial firefighting division, along with its entire S-64 fleet, had been purchased by [Helicopter Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_Express?action=edit\&redlink=1), resulting in the firm possessing a total of 19 examples.[^cite_note-22]

  [^cite_note-38]

- N173AC "Christine" – S-64E; ditched into a small dam within Melbourne's water catchment with no casualties during a firefighting operation in Gippsland, Victoria, Australia on 28 January 2019. The crew, consisting of two pilots and the flight engineer, were able to bail from the aircraft in 7 to 10 feet (2 to 3 m) of water and swim to safety with no life-threatening injuries.

  [^cite_note-39]

  &#x20;The aircraft was rebuilt at Erickson's [Central Point, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Point,_Oregon) facility and flew again in early 2021.

- N4037S - An S-64E operated by Siller Helicopters sustained damage to the right main landing gear wheel and tire assembly after a midair collision with a Bell 407 near [Cabazon, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabazon,_California) while both aircraft were responding to a reported fire on 6 August 2023. The S-64 landed near the collision site with no injuries to the two pilots on board. The Bell 407 sustained substantial damage in the collision, leaving a debris field approximately 1,000 feet (300 m) in length before coming to rest on a rocky hillside where it was consumed by a post-crash fire. The contract pilot along with a Cal Fire Assistant Chief and Cal Fire Captain on board the Bell 407 were killed.
