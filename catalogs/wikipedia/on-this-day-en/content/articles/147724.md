The **French Open** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Internationaux de France de tennis*), also known as **Roland-Garros** (French: [\[ʁɔlɑ̃ ɡaʁos\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)), is a [tennis](29773.md) tournament organized by the [French Tennis Federation](5730197.md) annually at [Stade Roland Garros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Roland_Garros) in [Paris](22989.md), France. It is chronologically the second of the four [Grand Slam](197638.md) tennis events every year, held after the [Australian Open](97851.md) and before [Wimbledon](91195.md) and the [US Open](82729.md). It was established in 1891, but it did not become a Grand Slam level event until 1925.

The French Open begins in late May and continues for two weeks.[^cite_note-3] The tournament and venue are named after the French [aviation pioneer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_pioneer) and [fighter pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fighter_pilot) [Roland Garros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Garros_\(aviator\)).[^cite_note-4]

The French Open is the premier [clay court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_court) tournament in the world and the only Grand Slam tournament currently held on this [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_surface). Until 1975, the French Open was the only major tournament not played on [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass_court). Due to the clay surface characteristics favoring long rallies and high physical demand, and the men's seven rounds of best-of-five sets needed for a championship, the French Open is widely regarded as the most physically demanding tournament in tennis.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## History

Officially named in French ***Internationaux de France de Tennis*** ("French Internationals of Tennis"),[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] the tournament uses the name ***Roland-Garros***[^cite_note-name-14] in all languages,[^cite_note-15] and it is usually called the **French Open** in English.[^cite_note-16]

In 1891, the *Championnat de France*, which is commonly referred to in English as the "French Championships", began. This was only open to tennis players who were members of French clubs. The first winner was [H. Briggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._Briggs), a Briton who resided in Paris and was a member of the club Stade Français. In the final, he defeated [P. Baigneres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._Baigneres) in straight sets.[^cite_note-PastWinners-17] The first women's singles tournament, with four entries, was held in 1897. The mixed doubles event was added in 1902 and the women's doubles in 1907. In the period of 1915–1919, no tournament was organized due to [World War I](4764461.md). This tournament was played until 1924, using four venues:

- Societé de Sport de l'Île de Puteaux (an island in the river [Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine)), in [Puteaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puteaux); played on the club's ten sand grounds laid out on a bed of rubble. 1891, 1893, 1894 (men's singles), 1895 (men's singles), 1897 (women's singles), 1902 (women's singles and mixed doubles), 1905 (women's singles and mixed doubles), 1907 (men's singles, women's singles, mixed doubles) editions.

- The Croix-Catelan of the [Racing Club de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Club_de_France) (a club founded in 1882, which initially had two lawn-tennis courts with four more [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass_court) (*pelouse*) courts opened some years later, but due to the difficulty of maintenance, they were eventually transformed into [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay_court) courts) in the [Bois de Boulogne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bois_de_Boulogne), Paris. 1892, 1894 (men's doubles), 1895 (men's doubles), 1897 (women's singles), 1901 (men's doubles), 1903 (men's doubles and mixed doubles), 1904, 1907 (men's doubles), 1908, 1910–1914, 1920–1924 editions.

- [Tennis Club de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_Club_de_Paris) (a club founded in 1895, which initially had four indoor wood courts and five outdoor clay courts), at 71, Boulevard Exelmans in the [Auteuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auteuil,_Paris) neighborhood, Paris. 1896, 1897 (men's singles), 1898, 1899, 1900, 1901 (men's and women's singles), 1902 (men's singles), 1903 (men's singles and women's singles), 1905 (men's singles) and 1906 editions.

- Société Athlétique de la Villa Primrose in [Bordeaux](4097.md), on clay. Only played in 1909.

In 1925, the French Championships became open to all amateurs internationally and was designated a major championship by the [International Lawn Tennis Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lawn_Tennis_Federation). It was held on clay courts at the [Stade Français](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Fran%C3%A7ais) in [Saint-Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Cloud) (site of the previous World Hard Court Championships) in 1925 and 1927. In 1926 the Croix-Catelan of the Racing Club de France hosted the event in Paris, the site of the previous French club members-only tournament, also on clay.

Another clay court tournament, called the [World Hard Court Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Hard_Court_Championships), is sometimes considered the true precursor to the modern French Open as it admitted international competitors. This was held at Stade Français in Saint-Cloud, from 1912 to 1914, 1920, 1921 and 1923, with the 1922 event held in [Brussels](3708.md), Belgium. Winners of this tournament included world No. 1s such as [Anthony Wilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Wilding) from New Zealand (1913, 1914) and Bill Tilden from the US (1921). In 1924 there was no World Hard Court Championships due to tennis being played at the [Paris Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Summer_Olympics) in [Colombes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombes).

After the [Mousquetaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Four_Musketeers_\(tennis\)) or Philadelphia Four ([René Lacoste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Lacoste), [Jean Borotra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Borotra), [Henri Cochet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Cochet), and [Jacques Brugnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Brugnon)) won the [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Cup) on American soil in 1927, the French decided to defend the cup in 1928 at a new tennis stadium at Porte d'Auteuil. The Stade Français president [Émile Lesieur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89mile_Lesieur) had offered the tennis authorities three hectares of land with the condition that the new stadium must be named after the World War I [aviator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviator) hero [Roland Garros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_Garros_\(aviator\)), who was also a former [HEC Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HEC_Paris) business school classmate.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] The new [Stade de Roland Garros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_de_Roland_Garros) (whose central court was renamed [Court Philippe Chatrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_Philippe_Chatrier) in 2001) hosted that Davis Cup challenge. On 24 May 1928, the French International Championships moved there, and the event has been held there ever since.[^cite_note-Historypage-20]

During World War II, the [Tournoi de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournoi_de_France_\(tennis\)) was not held in 1940 and from 1941 through 1945 it took place on the same grounds, but those events are not recognized by the French governing body, the [Fédération Française de Tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_Fran%C3%A7aise_de_Tennis).[^cite_note-21] In 1946 and 1947, the French Championships were held after Wimbledon, making it the third [Grand Slam](197638.md) event of the year. In 1968, the year of the [French General Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_68), the French Championships became the first Grand Slam tournament to go [open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Tennis), allowing both amateurs and professionals to compete.[^cite_note-Historypage-20]

Since 1981, new prizes have been presented: the Prix Orange (for the player demonstrating the best sportsmanship and cooperative attitude with the press), the Prix Citron (for the player with the strongest character and personality) and the Prix Bourgeon (for the tennis player revelation of the year). In another novelty, since 2006 the tournament has begun on a Sunday, featuring 12 singles matches played on the three main courts. Additionally, on the eve of the tournament's opening, the traditional Benny Berthet exhibition day takes place, where the profits go to different charity associations. In March 2007, it was announced that the event would provide equal prize money for both men and women in all rounds for the first time.[^cite_note-22] In 2010, it was announced that the tournament was considering a move away from Roland Garros as part of a continuing rejuvenation.[^cite_note-23] Plans to renovate and expand Roland Garros have put aside any such consideration, and the tournament remains in its long time home.

The 2022 edition finally saw a new [tiebreaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiebreaker) format.[^cite_note-24] If the deciding set is tied at six-all, the match is decided in a 10-point format. Should the tiebreaker game be tied at 9-all (or any tie hereafter), whoever goes two points ahead wins.[^cite_note-25] The decision was made by the Grand Slam Board for all four Grand Slams "based on a strong desire to create greater consistency in the rules of the game at the grand slams, and thus enhance the experience for the players and fans alike", a statement from the Board read.[^cite_note-26] The 2024 edition marked the first time that a member of the [Big Three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Three_\(tennis\)) ([Roger Federer](262376.md), [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) and [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md)) was not featured in the final since 2004.[^cite_note-27]

### Expansion



A match between [Roger Federer](262376.md) and [Julien Benneteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julien_Benneteau) on Court Philippe Chatrier during the [2013 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_French_Open)

From 2004 to 2008, plans were developed to build a covered stadium with a roof, as complaints continued over delayed matches.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Various proposals were put forward to expand the facility or to move the tournament to a completely new, 55-court venue outside of Paris city limits. In 2011 the decision was taken to maintain the tournament within its existing venue.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The expansion project called for a new stadium to be built alongside the historical [Auteuil's greenhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jardin_des_Serres_d'Auteuil) and expansion of old stadiums and the tournament village.[^cite_note-33] A wide-ranging project to overhaul the venue was presented in 2011, including building a roof over [Court Philippe-Chatrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Roland_Garros#Court_Philippe_Chatrier), demolishing and replacing Court No. 1 with a grassy hill for outdoors viewing, and geographical extension of the venue eastward into the [Jardin des Serres d'Auteuil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jardin_des_Serres_d'Auteuil).[^cite_note-34]

Legal opposition from environmental defence associations and other stakeholders delayed the works for several years as litigation ensued.[^cite_note-35] In particular, the city council voted in May 2015 against the expansion project, but on 9 June 2015 Paris Mayor [Anne Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Hidalgo) announced the signing of the construction permits, with work scheduled to begin in September of that year and conclude in 2019.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] In December 2015, the Administrative Court of Paris once again halted renovation work, but the French Tennis Federation won the right to proceed with the renovation on appeal.[^cite_note-38]



Court Philippe Chatrier in 2023 during a match between [Alexander Zverev](39755766.md) and [Alex Molčan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Mol%C4%8Dan), after the renovation

Renovation work finally commenced at the close of the 2018 edition of the tournament. Redeveloped seating and a retractable roof was constructed for Court Philippe-Chatrier and the new 5,000-seat Court [Simonne-Mathieu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simonne_Mathieu) was opened, having been named after France's second-highest achieving female tennis player, and noted for its innovative use of greenhouse encasing architecture.[^cite_note-39] The renewal of the venue has been generally well received by the players and the public.[^cite_note-40] The 2020 edition of the tournament, which was the first to be assisted by the roof over Philippe-Chatrier, was postponed to late September and early October and was played in front of limited spectators, due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-41] Floodlights were also installed over each of the courts in the precinct, allowing the tournament to facilitate night matches for the first time.[^cite_note-42] In 2021, the tournament was back in the traditional slot of late May and early June.[^cite_note-43]

### Rafael Nadal tribute



Commemorative plaque to [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) unveiled during a tribute at the [2025 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open), on the soil of the Court Philippe Chatrier

On 25 May 2025, the opening day of the [2025 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_French_Open), Roland Garros organized a special farewell ceremony to honor 14-time champion [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal) following his retirement from professional tennis the previous year. Held on Court Philippe Chatrier, the ceremony featured tribute videos, a standing ovation from thousands of spectators, and on-court appearances by his longtime rivals [Roger Federer](262376.md), [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md), and [Andy Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Murray).[^cite_note-44] The event culminated in the unveiling of a permanent commemorative white marble plaque embedded on the court surface near the net post. Designed in strict confidentiality by the [French Tennis Federation](5730197.md) (FFT), the 40×30 cm plaque features Nadal's name, an engraving of the *Coupe des Mousquetaires* with the number "14", and a replica of his right footprint set directly into the Parisian clay.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

## Surface

The French Open has been the only major played on clay courts since 1978, when the US Open changed to [hard courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_\(tennis\)).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] Clay is the slowest tennis court surface, as the fine layer of red clay dust on the topmost reduces the ball speed and causes it to bounce much higher when compared with hard courts and [grass courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass_court).[^cite_note-WilsonClay-49] For this specificity, the clay surface takes away some of the advantages of big servers and serve-and-volleyers, making it harder for these types of players to dominate on the surface.[^cite_note-WilsonClay-49] [Pete Sampras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Sampras), 14 Grand Slam winner and known for his excellent serve, never won the French Open – his best result being a semi-final in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_French_Open). Other notable players who have won multiple Grand Slam events but have never won the French Open, includes [John McEnroe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McEnroe), [Jimmy Connors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Connors), [John Newcombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Newcombe), [Venus Williams](167378.md), [Stefan Edberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Edberg), [Virginia Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Wade), [Boris Becker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boris_Becker), [Andy Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Murray), [Louise Brough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louise_Brough), and [Martina Hingis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Hingis); McEnroe and Edberg lost their only French Open finals appearances in five sets.

On the other hand, notable players have won multiple Grand Slam events including the French Open; among them are [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal), [Björn Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rn_Borg), [Steffi Graf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steffi_Graf), [Ivan Lendl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Lendl), [Monica Seles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Seles), [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md), [Carlos Alcaraz](63121147.md), [Mats Wilander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mats_Wilander), [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams), [Stan Wawrinka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Wawrinka), [Justine Henin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justine_Henin) and [Chris Evert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Evert), showing proof of their great versatility. In the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_open_era), the male players who have won both the French Open and [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Championships,_Wimbledon), played on faster grass courts, are [Rod Laver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Laver), [Jan Kodeš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kode%C5%A1), [Björn Borg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bj%C3%B6rn_Borg), [Andre Agassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Agassi), [Rafael Nadal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Nadal), [Roger Federer](262376.md), [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) and [Carlos Alcaraz](63121147.md), for the female players those are [Evonne Goolagong Cawley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evonne_Goolagong_Cawley), [Margaret Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Court), [Billie Jean King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Jean_King), [Chris Evert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Evert), [Martina Navratilova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Navratilova), [Steffi Graf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steffi_Graf), [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams), [Iga Swiatek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iga_Swiatek), [Maria Sharapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sharapova), [Garbiñe Muguruza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza), [Simona Halep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simona_Halep), [Ashleigh Barty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashleigh_Barty) and [Barbora Krejčíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1). Borg's French Open–Wimbledon double was achieved three times consecutively.[^cite_note-Wimbledonrecords-50]

The clay surface also makes the French Open unique in that it is the only Grand Slam tournament that still uses human line judges after Wimbledon's move to [fully automated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_line_judge) line calls in 2025. The challenge system does not exist at Roland Garros either due to not having the Hawk-Eye system installed; it is claimed that it is less reliable on clay courts due to dust coming up during play meaning that it cannot provide an accurate ruling. Players may still appeal close calls by requesting the chair umpire to come down and physically examine a ball mark made in the clay to determine whether a particular shot was in or out.[^cite_note-51]

## Trophies

The trophies have been awarded to the winners since 1953 and are manufactured by [Mellerio dits Meller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellerio_dits_Meller), a famous Parisian jewelry house. They are all made of pure silver with finely etched decorations on their side. Each new singles winner gets their name written on the base of the trophy. Winners receive custom-made pure silver replicas of the trophies they have won.[^cite_note-53] They are usually presented by the president of the [French Tennis Federation](5730197.md) (FFT).



Rafael Nadal holding the [Coupe des Mousquetaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_des_Mousquetaires) in 2006

The trophy awarded to the winner of the men's singles is called the *[Coupe des Mousquetaires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_des_Mousquetaires)* (The Musketeers' Cup). It is named in honor of the "[Four Musketeers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Four_Musketeers_\(tennis\))". The trophy weighs 14 kg, is 40 cm high and 19 cm wide.[^cite_note-54] The current design was created in 1981 by the Mellerio dit Meller. Each winner gets a smaller-size replica and the original remains property of the FFT at all times.[^cite_note-trophy-55]

The trophy awarded to the winner of the women's singles is called the *Coupe Suzanne Lenglen* (Suzanne Lenglen Cup) since 1979. The current cup was awarded for the first time in 1986. It is, with a few details, a replica of a cup offered at the time by the city of [Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nice) to Suzanne Lenglen. This trophy, donated by Suzanne Lenglen's family to the [Musée National du Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mus%C3%A9e_National_du_Sport), was awarded between 1979 and 1985 to every winner until the FFT made a copy. Each winner receives a smaller-size replica and the original remains property of the FFT at all times.[^cite_note-trophy-55]

## Media coverage and broadcasting

### France

[France Télévisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_T%C3%A9l%C3%A9visions) and [Amazon Prime Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Prime_Video) hold the broadcast rights to the French Open until 2027.[^cite_note-60] All 11 "night sessions" will remain exclusive to Prime Video.[^cite_note-61] Studio presentation for the French Open on France Télévisions is historically hosted on a terrace in a corner of the Court Philippe Chatrier.[^cite_note-62]

### United States

[NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_on_NBC)'s coverage of the French Open began in [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_French_Open).[^cite_note-63] In 2007,[^cite_note-64] [Tennis Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_Channel) acquired the pay television rights to the tournament and sub-licensed coverage of morning window (U.S. time) matches to [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) for broadcast by [ESPN2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN2) from 2007 through 2015.[^cite_note-aa-espnfrenchopen-65] In August 2015, ESPN announced that it would discontinue its sub-licensing and drop coverage of the French Open beginning in 2016, with network staff citing that because of the structure of the arrangement, its coverage "did not fit our successful model at the other three Majors"—where ESPN is the exclusive rightsholder.[^cite_note-aa-espnfrenchopen-65] Rather than find another partner to sub-license coverage to, Tennis Channel chose to retain the rights under its new owner [Sinclair Broadcast Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinclair_Broadcast_Group), nearly doubling the amount of Tennis Channel's coverage.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

Starting with the 2023 edition and continuing the following year, NBC moved some of its coverage of the French Open exclusively to its streaming service, [Peacock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacock_\(streaming_service\)).[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

In June 2024, it was reported that the U.S. rights had been acquired by [TNT Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_Sports_\(United_States\)) beginning in 2025, as part of a deal worth \$650 million over 10 years with [Warner Bros. Discovery](67824355.md), and a renewal of its pan-European rights with [Eurosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosport).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Matches air primarily on [TNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_\(American_TV_network\)), with [TruTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TruTV) providing a daily multiplex. Full coverage is available via streaming on [HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max), and replays on [Discovery+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery+).[^cite_note-72]

### United Kingdom

[BBC](19344654.md) began broadcasting French Open finals annually in 1981[^cite_note-73] (often in their *[Grandstand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandstand_\(TV_programme\))* or *Sunday Grandstand* programmes). The BBC's coverage continued until 2011. From 2012 until 2021, [ITV4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV4) televised the French Open in the United Kingdom. Eurosport began broadcasting the tournament in 1989.[^cite_note-74] As of 2022, [Eurosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosport) (later as [TNT Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_Sports_\(United_Kingdom\)) starting from 2025) holds exclusive UK broadcast rights to the tournament.[^cite_note-75] Studio presentation for the French Open on Eurosport is hosted by [Barbara Schett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbara_Schett) with [Mats Wilander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mats_Wilander). Commentators include Simon Reed, Chris Bradnam, Nick Lester, Barry Millns alongside [Jo Durie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo_Durie), [Annabel Croft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annabel_Croft), [Frew McMillan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frew_McMillan), [Miles Maclagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miles_Maclagan), [Arvind Parmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvind_Parmar) and [Chris Wilkinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Wilkinson).[^cite_note-76]

### India

[Star Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Sports_\(Indian_TV_network\)) had the exclusive broadcast rights of the French Open, up until the 2021 edition.[^cite_note-77] Starting 2022, Sony Pictures Sports Network, owned by [Sony Pictures Networks India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures_Networks_India), acquired the broadcast rights through 2024.[^cite_note-78] The deal was extended in May 2025 for three more editions, until 2027.[^cite_note-79]

### Europe

Excluding France, the tournament is broadcast on [Eurosport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosport) in more than 50 territories, until 2030.[^cite_note-80]

## Ball boys and ball girls

Every year, 280 "ramasseurs de balles" (literally "gatherers of balls" in English) are scheduled to be selected for the tournament.[^cite_note-81]

Aged between 11 and 16 years old and dressed in matching [Lacoste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacoste) shirts and shorts, the ball boys and ball girls are chosen to take part in the French Open through an application process, only available to those licensed of the [French Tennis Federation](5730197.md), which in 2023 had approximately 4,000 applicants from across France.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] Upon selection they are trained in the weeks leading up to the event.[^cite_note-85]
