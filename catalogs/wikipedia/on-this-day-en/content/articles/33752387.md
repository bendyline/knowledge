**Rethymno** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Ρέθυμνο [\[ˈreθimno\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Greek), [Italian](14708.md): *Retimo*, formerly also *Rettimo* or *Retimno*, [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin): *Civitas Rethymnæ*) is a city in [Greece](12108.md) on the island of [Crete](6591.md). It is the capital of [Rethymno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rethymno_\(regional_unit\)) regional unit, and has a population of more than 35,000 inhabitants (nearly 40,000 for the municipal unit). It is believed to have been built on the site of the earlier city of [Rhithymna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhithymna).

## History



View of the old harbour



Inside the [Fortezza of Rethymno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortezza_of_Rethymno)

Rethymno is believed to have been built on the site of the earlier city of [Rhithymna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhithymna).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] Some sources mention a city called [Arsinoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsinoe_\(Crete\)) which some scholars have proposed stood at the same site.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

Rethymno began a period of growth when the [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) conquerors of the island decided to establish an intermediate commercial station between [Heraklion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraklion) and [Chania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chania), acquiring its own [bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_\(Eastern_Orthodox_Church\)) and nobility in the process. Today's old town (*palia poli*) was almost entirely built by the [Republic of Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice). It is one of the best-preserved old towns in Crete.

From circa 1250 the city was the seat of the Latin [Diocese of Retimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Retimo%E2%80%93Ario), which was renamed Retimo–Ario after the absorption in 1551 of the Diocese of Ario.

The town still maintains its old aristocratic appearance, with its buildings dating from the 16th century, arched doorways, stone staircases, [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) remains, the small Venetian harbour and narrow streets. The Venetian [Loggia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loggia) houses the information office of the [Ministry of Culture and Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_and_Sports_\(Greece\)). A Wine Festival is held there annually at the beginning of July. Another festival, in memory of the destruction of the [Arkadi Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkadi_Monastery), is held on 7–8 November.

The city's Venetian-era citadel, the [Fortezza of Rethymno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortezza_of_Rethymno), is one of the best-preserved castles in Crete. Other monuments include the [Neradje Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neradje_Mosque) (the Municipal Odeon arts centre), the Great Gate (Μεγάλη Πόρτα or "Porta Guora"), the Piazza Rimondi and the Loggia.

The town was conquered by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in 1646 during the [Cretan War (1645–69)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretan_War_\(1645%E2%80%9369\)). The town, called *Resmo* in Turkish, was the centre of a *[sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak)* (administrative part of a province) during Ottoman rule.

During the [Cretan Revolt of 1866–1869](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretan_Revolt_\(1866%E2%80%9369\)), the nearby [Arkadi Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkadi_Monastery) became a major centre of resistance against Ottoman rule. In November 1866, a large Ottoman force besieged the monastery, where 259 defenders and more than 700 women and children had taken refuge.

After several days of fighting, Ottoman troops entered the monastery. Rather than surrender, the defenders ignited barrels of gunpowder stored inside, causing an explosion that killed most of those sheltering there, as well as many of the attackers. Thirty-six insurgents who had taken refuge in the monastery’s refectory were subsequently massacred. The event became known as the *[Holocaust of Arkadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkadi_Monastery)* and attracted considerable sympathy for the Cretan cause across Europe.

During the [Battle of Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Crete) (20–30 May 1941), the [Battle of Rethymno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Rethymno) was fought between [German](21212.md) [paratroopers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallschirmj%C3%A4ger) and combined forces of the [Second Australian Imperial Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Australian_Imperial_Force) and the [Hellenic Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenic_Army). Although initially unsuccessful, eventually the Germans won the battle after receiving reinforcements airlifted to [Maleme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maleme_Airport) in the northwestern part of the island.

Today the city's main source of income is tourism, with many new facilities having been built in the past 20 years. Agriculture is also notable, especially [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil) and other Mediterranean products.

## Culture

Rethymno is home to the following museums:

- [Archaeological Museum of Rethymno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum_of_Rethymno)

- Historical and Folklore Museum of Rethymno

- Municipal Gallery "L. Kanakakis"

- The Frantzeskaki Collection

- [Museum of Sea Life at Rethymno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Sea_Life_at_Rethymno)

The [Treasure Hunt of Rethymno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasure_Hunt_of_Rethymno) is a game played by local people and takes place two weeks before [Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival).[^cite_note-9]

### Literature

[Pandelis Prevelakis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandelis_Prevelakis) wrote *Το χρονικό μιας πολιτείας* (1937), *The Chronicle of a Town*, a nostalgic depiction of Rethymno from the period of the [Cretan State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretan_State) (1898) to the [expulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_exchange_between_Greece_and_Turkey) of the [Cretan Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretan_Turks) (1924).

## Education

In the Rethymno Campus of the University of Crete are located the School of Philosophy, the School of Education, the School of [Social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_sciences), [Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) and [Political Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science), and the University Library of the [University of Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Crete). On a yearly basis, there are about 8.000 students studying at "Galos" where the Campus and the Academic Institute of Mediterranean Studies are located. Also in Rethymnon is located the School of Music and Optoacoustic Technologies of the [Hellenic Mediterranean University](https://www.hmu.gr/).[^cite_note-11] Finally, in Rethymno, Tria Monastiria area is located the international research [Institute of Plasma Physics and Laser](http://www.ippl.hmu.gr/)[^cite_note-12] of the [Hellenic Mediterranean University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Www.hmu.gr?action=edit\&redlink=1) which is the access point of the National Research Facility [HELLAS-CH](https://hellasch.iesl.forth.gr/?page_id=2\&lang=en) .
