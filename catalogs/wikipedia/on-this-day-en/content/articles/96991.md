**Gunnison County** is a [county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County) located in the U.S. state of [Colorado](5399.md). As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the population was 16,918.[^cite_note-2020Census-1] The [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) is [Gunnison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunnison,_Colorado).[^cite_note-GR6-2] The county was named for [John W. Gunnison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Gunnison), a United States Army officer and captain in the [Army Topographical Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps_of_Topographical_Engineers), who surveyed for the transcontinental railroad in 1853.

## History

Archeological studies have dated the [Ute people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ute_people)'s appearance in the [Uncompahgre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncompahgre_Peak) region of Colorado as early as 1150 A.D. Possibilities exist that they are descendants of an earlier people living in the area as far back as 1500 B.C.[^cite_note-3] They were a nomadic people moving about the Western Slope of Colorado in the various parts of the year.[^cite_note-4] In the early to mid-1600s the [Spaniards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) of [New Mexico](21649.md) introduced the horse which changed their patterns of hunting taking them across the divide to the eastern slopes and into conflict with the [Plains Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plains_Indians) which soon became their bitter enemies.[^cite_note-5]

The first recorded expedition of Western Colorado wilderness was led by Don [Juan Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Rivera_\(explorer\)) in 1765. In 1776, two Spanish priests, Fathers [Escalante and Domínguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom%C3%ADnguez%E2%80%93Escalante_expedition), led a party into the area around [Montrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montrose,_Colorado) and [Paonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paonia,_Colorado).[^cite_note-6]

The 1830s brought the mountainmen into the area to trap beaver. An old cabin located on [Cochetopa Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochetopa_Creek) discovered by Sidney Jocknick was most likely built between 1830 and 1840 and a crude fort was discovered on a tributary of [Tomichi Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomichi_Creek) bore signs of a conflict.[^cite_note-7]

In 1853, Capt. [John W. Gunnison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Gunnison) surveyed the area for the [transcontinental railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_transcontinental_railroad) route.[^cite_note-8]

In 1858, gold was discovered near Denver bringing the white man across the divide into the western slope in search of the precious metal. In 1859 a party settled on Texas Gulch in Union Park.[^cite_note-9]

Placer gold was found at Washington Gulch in 1861 as part of the [Colorado Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Gold_Rush).[^cite_note-10]

In 1861, the [Territory of Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_of_Colorado) was organized. The territorial governor was made *ex officio* Superintentant of Indian Affairs. A conference on October 1, 1863, established a boundary line for a reservation.[^cite_note-11] This treaty averted a possible dangerous situation by giving the Utes some cattle and sheep, a blacksmith and 20,000 dollars a year in goods and provisions. The government failed to fulfill any these obligations straining the relations further.[^cite_note-12] The treaty of 1868 recognized Chief Ouray as the sole spokesman for seven tribes of the Ute People. He held this power over his people through diplomacy and understanding.[^cite_note-13]

The Los Pinos Agency was developed through the Treaties of 1868 and 1873. The first agent was 2nd Lieutenant Calvin T. Speer. In 1871, a cow camp was started near the present site of Gunnison with James P. Kelley in charge. In this year, Jabez Nelson Trask, a Harvard grad, relieved Speer as agent upon orders from Governor [Edward M. McCook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_M._McCook).[^cite_note-14] In 1872 Trask was replaced by Charles Adams.

In 1875, orders from Washington to move the agency to the Uncompahgre Valley were completed in November.[^cite_note-15]

In 1876, Colorado entered the Union and **Gunnison County** was formed. 1879 was a year of expansion due to the miners, speculators and adventurers seeking wealth. The cattle industry was established by 1880. The short growing season was not conducive to farming and the ranchers had to level fields and construct irrigation ditches to water the fields for hay.

## Geography

According to the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census_Bureau), the county has a total area of 3,260 square miles (8,400 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 3,239 square miles (8,390 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 21 square miles (54 km<sup>2</sup>) (0.6%) is water.[^cite_note-GR1-16] It is the fifth-largest county by area in Colorado. The county seat is [Gunnison, Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunnison,_Colorado) which is located in a wide valley at the confluence of [Tomichi Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomichi_Creek) and [Gunnison River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunnison_River). The county rests in the Gunnison Basin formed by the [Continental Divide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Divide) to the east, [Collegiate Peaks Wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_Peaks_Wilderness) rises in the northeast, [Maroon Bells–Snowmass Wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maroon_Bells%E2%80%93Snowmass_Wilderness) and the [White River National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_River_National_Forest) to the north, the [West Elk Wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Elk_Wilderness) rises in the west of the county with Delta and Montrose Counties on its western slopes. The [Uncompahgre Wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncompahgre_Wilderness) rises in the southwest of the county and the [Powderhorn Wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powderhorn_Wilderness) east of there and Saquache County being south of Gunnison county eastward over to [Marshall Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Pass) southeast of the county.[^cite_note-17]

[Taylor Park Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Park_Reservoir) is a man-made lake created by the Taylor Dam constructed in 1934 with appropriations of 2,725,000 dollars.[^cite_note-18]

### 2000 census

As of the [2000 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_census), there were 13,956 people, 5,649 households, and 2,965 families residing in the county. The [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) was 4 people per square mile (1.5 people/km<sup>2</sup>). There were 9,135 housing units at an average density of 3 units per square mile (1.2 units/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of the county was 95.08% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), 0.49% [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)) or [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), 0.70% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), 0.54% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), 0.04% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), 1.44% from [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), and 1.72% from two or more races. 5.02% of the population were [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)) of any race.

There were 5,649 households, out of which 24.10% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 44.20% were married couples living together, 5.40% had a female householder with no husband present, and 47.50% were non-families. 27.20% of all households were made up of individuals, and 4.60% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.30 and the average family size was 2.84.

In the county, the age demographic is distributed, with 17.90% under the age of 18, 21.10% from 18 to 24, 32.90% from 25 to 44, 21.20% from 45 to 64, and 6.90% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 30 years. For every 100 females there were 118.30 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 120.90 males.

The median income for a household in the county was \$36,916, and the median income for a family was \$51,950. Males had a median income of \$30,885 versus \$25,000 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the county was \$21,407. About 6.00% of families and 15.00% of the population were below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line), including 9.40% of those under age 18 and 7.20% of those age 65 or over.

## Education

### K-12 Education

Gunnison County is served by Gunnison Watershed School District RE-1J, which operates schools in Gunnison, Crested Butte, and Marble.

### Higher Education

Notably, Gunnison County is home to [Western Colorado University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Colorado_University), located in the city of Gunnison. WCU is a public four-year university with an enrollment of roughly 3,500 students in the 2022-2023 academic year.[^cite_note-30]

## Politics

For most of the 20th century, except 1964, Gunnison County was a Republican stronghold. Despite the nationwide swing against him, this was one of the few counties that swung towards [Gerald Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Ford) in 1976. However, beginning in the late 1980s, it began trending more Democratic, as [Bill Clinton](3356.md) won the county both times in 1992 and 1996. The county gave a 43.2% plurality to [George W. Bush](3414021.md) in the 2000 election, and has voted for the Democratic candidate in every election since. In 2020, Joe Biden won a higher percentage of the vote than any Democrat since 1936.
