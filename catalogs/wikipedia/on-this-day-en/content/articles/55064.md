**Inner Mongolia**,[^cite_note-7] officially the **Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region**[^cite_note-8] (**IMAR**), is an [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_regions_of_China) of [China](5405.md). Its border includes two-thirds of the length of China's [border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Mongolia_border) with the country of [Mongolia](19271.md), as well as a [small section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Russia_border) with [Russia](25391.md) ([Zabaykalsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabaykalsky_Krai)). Its capital is [Hohhot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohhot); other major cities include [Baotou](1149172.md), [Chifeng](1674149.md), [Tongliao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongliao) and [Ordos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordos_City).

The autonomous region was established in 1947, incorporating the areas of the former [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) provinces of [Suiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suiyuan), [Chahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chahar_Province), [Rehe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehe_Province), [Liaobei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaobei), and [Xing'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xing'an_Province), along with the northern parts of [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) and [Ningxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningxia).

Its area makes it the [third largest Chinese administrative subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_area), constituting approximately 1,200,000 km<sup>2</sup> (463,000 sq mi) and 12% of China's total land area. Due to its long span from east to west, Inner Mongolia is geographically divided into eastern and western divisions. The eastern division is often included in [Northeastern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_China) (Dongbei), with major cities including [Tongliao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongliao), [Chifeng](1674149.md), [Hailar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailar_District), and [Ulanhot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulanhot). The western division is included in [North China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China), with major cities including Baotou and Hohhot. It recorded a population of 24,706,321 in the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_National_Population_Census_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), accounting for 1.84% of [Mainland China](55079.md)'s total population. Inner Mongolia is the country's [23rd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_population) most populous [province-level division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China).[^cite_note-9] [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) make up the majority of the population in the region; [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols_in_China) constitute a significant minority with over 4 million people,[^cite_note-Ethnicity-10] making it the largest Mongol population in the world (larger than that of the country [Mongolia](19271.md)).[^cite_note-11] Inner Mongolia is one of the more economically developed provinces in China with annual GDP per capita at US\$14,343 (2022), ranked 8th in the nation. As of 2021, [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese) is the region's sole official language.[^cite_note-12] In the region, [Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language) is written in the [traditional Mongolian script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_script), as opposed to the [Mongolian Cyrillic alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_Cyrillic_alphabet), which is used alongside the traditional script in the country of Mongolia (formerly described as [Outer Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Mongolia)).

In Chinese, the region is known as "Inner Mongolia", where the terms of "Inner" and "Outer" are derived from [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_language) *dorgi* and *tulergi* (cf. [Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language) *dotugadu* and *gadagadu*). Inner Mongolia is distinct from [Outer Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Mongolia), which was a term used by the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) and previous governments to refer to what is now the independent [state](23604120.md) of [Mongolia](19271.md) and the [Republic of Tuva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuva) in [Russia](25391.md).&#x20;

Due to the perceived [Sinocentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinocentrism) nature of the name "Inner Mongolia", some Mongols outside of China, particularly those in the state of Mongolia and separatist groups in the West, prefer the name "Southern Mongolia". However, this name has not been adopted officially by any government bodies.[^cite_note-Borjigin-13]

## History

See also: [History of Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Mongolia)

Much of what is known about the history of the [Mongolian Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_Plateau) is taken from Chinese chronicles and historians. Before the rise of the Mongols in the 13th century, what is now central and western Inner Mongolia, especially the [Hetao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetao) region, alternated in control between [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) farming communities in the south, and [Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiongnu), [Xianbei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianbei), [Khitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khitan_people), [Jurchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurchen_people), [Tujue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujue), and nomadic [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols_in_China) of the north. The historical narrative of what is now Eastern Inner Mongolia mostly consists of alternations between different [Tungusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungusic_peoples) and Mongol tribes, rather than the struggle between nomads and Chinese farmers.

### Early history

See also: [Han dynasty in Inner Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty_in_Inner_Asia) and [Tang dynasty in Inner Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty_in_Inner_Asia)

[Slab Grave cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slab_Grave_Culture) monuments are found in Northern, Central and Eastern [Mongolia](19271.md), Inner Mongolia, North-Western China, Southern, Central-Eastern and Southern [Baikal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikal_Lake) territory. Mongolian scholars prove that this culture related to the [Proto-Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Mongols).[^cite_note-14]

During the [Zhou dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty), Central and Western Inner Mongolia (the [Hetao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetao) region and surrounding areas) were inhabited by nomadic peoples such as the [Loufan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loufan_\(people\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), Linhu and [Dí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beidi), while Eastern Inner Mongolia was inhabited by the [Donghu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donghu_people). During the [Warring States period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period), [King Wuling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Wuling_of_Zhao) (340–295 BC) of the [state of Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Zhao) based in what is now [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei) and [Shanxi Provinces](81262.md) pursued an expansionist policy towards the region. After destroying the Dí [state of Zhongshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Zhongshan) in what is now Hebei province, he defeated the Linhu and Loufan and created the [Yunzhong Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunzhong_Commandery) near modern [Hohhot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohhot). King Wuling of Zhao also built a long wall stretching through the Hetao region. After [Qin Shi Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang) created the first unified Chinese empire in 221 BC, he sent the general [Meng Tian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meng_Tian) to [drive the Xiongnu from the region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin's_campaign_against_the_Xiongnu) and incorporated the old Zhao wall into the Qin dynasty Great Wall of China. He also maintained two commanderies in the region: [Jiuyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuyuan_Commandery?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Yunzhong and moved 30,000 households there to solidify the region. After the Qin dynasty collapsed in 206 BC, these efforts were abandoned.[^cite_note-15]

During the [Western Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Han_dynasty), [Emperor Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Wu_of_Han) sent the general [Wei Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wei_Qing) to [reconquer the Hetao region from the Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han%E2%80%93Xiongnu_War) in 127 BC. After the conquest, Emperor Wu continued the policy of building settlements in Hetao to defend against the Xiong-Nu. In that same year, he established the commanderies of [Shuofang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuofang_Commandery) and [Wuyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuyuan_Commandery) in Hetao. At the same time, what is now Eastern Inner Mongolia was controlled by the [Xianbei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xianbei), who would, later on, eclipse the Xiongnu in power and influence.

During the [Eastern Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Han_dynasty) (25–220 AD), Xiongnu who surrendered to the Han dynasty began to be settled in Hetao and intermingled with the Han immigrants in the area. Later on, during the [Western Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Jin_dynasty), it was a Xiongnu noble from Hetao, [Liu Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Yuan_\(Han_Zhao\)), who established the [Han Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Zhao) kingdom in the region, thereby beginning the [Sixteen Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixteen_Kingdoms) period that saw the disintegration of northern China under a variety of Han and non-Han (including Xiongnu and Xianbei) regimes.

The [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty) (581–618) and [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) (618–907) re-established a unified Chinese empire and like their predecessors, they conquered and settled people into Hetao, though once again these efforts were aborted when the Tang empire began to collapse. Hetao (along with the rest of what now consists Inner Mongolia) was then taken over by the [Liao dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liao_dynasty) founded by the [Khitans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khitan_people), a nomadic people originally from what is now the southern part of Manchuria and Eastern Inner Mongolia. They were followed by the [Western Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Xia) of the [Tanguts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangut_people), who took control of what is now the western part of Inner Mongolia (including Western Hetao). The Khitans were later replaced by the [Jurchens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurchens), precursors to the modern [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people), who established the [Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_\(1115%E2%80%931234\)) over Manchuria and Northern China.

### Mongol and Ming periods

Main articles: [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire), [Mongolia under Yuan rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia_under_Yuan_rule), [Northern Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Yuan), and [Ming dynasty in Inner Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty_in_Inner_Asia)



[Persian miniature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_miniature) depicting [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan) entering [Beijing](18603746.md)



The Northern Yuan at its greatest extent

After Genghis Khan unified the [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol) tribes in 1206 and founded the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire), the [Tangut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanguts) [Western Xia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Xia) empire was ultimately conquered in 1227, and the [Jurchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurchens) [Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_\(1115%E2%80%931234\)) fell in 1234. In 1271, [Kublai Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kublai_Khan), the grandson of Genghis Khan established the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty). Kublai Khan's summer capital [Shangdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangdu) (aka Xanadu) was located near present-day [Dolonnor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duolun_County). During that time [Ongud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ongud) and [Khunggirad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khunggirad) peoples dominated the area of what is now Inner Mongolia. After the Yuan dynasty was overthrown by the Han-led [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) in 1368, the Ming captured parts of Inner Mongolia including Shangdu and [Yingchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yingchang). The Ming rebuilt the [Great Wall of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wall_of_China) at its present location, which roughly follows the southern border of the modern Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region (though it deviates significantly at the Hebei-Inner Mongolia border). The Ming established the Three Guards composed of the Mongols there. Soon after the [Tumu incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumu_incident) in 1449, when the Oirat ruler [Esen taishi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esen_taishi) captured the Chinese emperor, Mongols flooded south from Outer Mongolia to Inner Mongolia. Thus from then on until 1635, Inner Mongolia was the political and cultural centre of the Mongols during the [Northern Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Yuan_dynasty).[^cite_note-16]

### Qing period

Main article: [Mongolia under Qing rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia_under_Qing_rule)

The eastern Mongol tribes near and in Manchuria, particularly the [Khorchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorchin) and Southern [Khalkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalkha) in today's Inner Mongolia intermarried, formed alliances with, and fought against the [Jurchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurchen_people) tribes until [Nurhaci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurhaci), the founder of the new Jin dynasty, consolidated his control over all groups in the area in 1593.[^cite_note-17] The [Manchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchus) gained far-reaching control of the Inner Mongolian tribes in 1635, when [Ligden Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligden_Khan)'s son surrendered the [Chakhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chahar_Mongols) Mongol tribes to the Manchus. The Manchus subsequently invaded Ming China in 1644, bringing it under the control of their newly established [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty). Under the Qing dynasty (1644–1912), the [Mongolian Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_Plateau) was [administered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia_under_Qing_rule) in a different way for each region:

- "Outer Mongolia": This region corresponds to the modern state of [Mongolia](19271.md), plus the Russian-administered region of [Tannu Uriankhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannu_Uriankhai), and modern-day aimag of [Bayan-Ölgii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayan-%C3%96lgii) which historically was a part of northern [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) under China's Qing Dynasty. It included the four leagues (*aimag*) of the [Khalkha Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalkha_Mongols) north of the [Gobi Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobi_Desert), as well as the [Tannu Uriankhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tannu_Uriankhai) which largely corresponds to modern-day [Tuva Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuva_Republic) of Russia, and [Khovd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khovd_Province) regions in northwestern Mongolia, which were overseen by the [General of Uliastai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uliastai_General) from the city of [Uliastai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uliastai).

- "Inner Mongolia": This region corresponded to most of modern Inner Mongolia and some neighbouring areas in [Liaoning](155516.md) and [Jilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jilin) provinces. The [banners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banners_of_Inner_Mongolia) and tribes in this region came under six [leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leagues_of_China) (*chuulghan*): [Jirim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongliao), [Juuuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juuuda), [Josutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josutu), [Xilingol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xilingol), [Ulanqab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulanqab), and [Yekejuu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekejuu).

- "Taoxi Mongolia": The [Alashan Öölüd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alashan_%C3%96%C3%B6l%C3%BCd?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Ejine Torghuud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejine_Torghuud?action=edit\&redlink=1) banners were separate from the aimags of Outer Mongolia and the chuulghans of Inner Mongolia. This territory is equivalent to modern-day [Alxa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alxa_League), the westernmost part of what is now Inner Mongolia.

- The [Chahar Banners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Banners_of_Chahar) were controlled by the military commander of Chahar (now [Zhangjiakou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangjiakou)). Their extent corresponded to southern Ulanqab and [Bayannur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayannur) in modern Inner Mongolia, plus the region around [Zhangjiakou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangjiakou) in [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei) province. At the same time, the jurisdiction of some border departments of [Zhili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhili_Province) and [Shanxi](81262.md) provinces also belonged to this region.

- The [Guihua Tümed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guihua_T%C3%BCmed?action=edit\&redlink=1) banner was controlled by the military commander of Suiyuan (now [Hohhot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohhot)). This corresponds to the vicinities of the modern city of [Hohhot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohhot). At the same time, the jurisdiction of some border departments of modern [Shanxi](81262.md) province also belonged to this region.

- The [Hulunbuir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulunbuir) region in what is now northeastern Inner Mongolia was part of the jurisdiction of the General of [Heilongjiang](173816.md), one of the three generals of [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria).

The Inner Mongolian [Chahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chahar_Mongols) leader [Ligdan Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligdan_Khan), a descendant of Genghis Khan, opposed and fought against the Qing until he died of smallpox in 1634. Thereafter, the Inner Mongols under his son [Ejei Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejei_Khan) surrendered to the Qing. Ejei Khan was given the title of Prince (親王; *qīn wáng*), and Inner Mongolian nobility became closely tied to the Qing royal family and intermarried with them extensively. Ejei Khan died in 1661 and was succeeded by his brother Abunai. After Abunai showed disaffection with Manchu Qing rule, he was placed under house arrest in 1669 in [Shenyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenyang) and the Kangxi Emperor gave his title to his son Borni. Abunai then bid his time and then he and his brother Lubuzung revolted against the Qing in 1675 during the [Revolt of the Three Feudatories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolt_of_the_Three_Feudatories), with 3,000 Chahar Mongol followers joining in on the revolt. The revolt was put down within two months, the Qing then crushed the rebels in a battle on 20 April 1675, killing Abunai and all his followers. Their title was abolished, all Chahar Mongol royal males were executed even if they were born to Manchu Qing princesses, and all Chahar Mongol royal females were sold into slavery except the Manchu Qing princesses. The Chahar Mongols were then reorganized into the [Eight Banners of Chahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Banners_of_Chahar) under the direct control of the Qing Emperor, unlike the other Inner Mongol leagues which maintained their autonomy.

Despite officially prohibiting Han Chinese settlement on the Manchu and Mongol lands, by the 18th century the Qing decided to settle Han refugees from northern China who were suffering from famine, floods, and drought into Manchuria and Inner Mongolia. As a result, the Han Chinese farmed 500,000 hectares in Manchuria and tens of thousands of hectares in Inner Mongolia by the 1780s.[^cite_note-20]

Ordinary Mongols were not allowed to travel outside their own leagues. Mongols were forbidden by the Qing from crossing the borders of their banners, even into other Mongol Banners and from crossing into neidi (the Han Chinese 18 provinces) and were given serious punishments if they did in order to keep the Mongols divided against each other to benefit the Qing.[^cite_note-21] Mongol pilgrims wanting to leave their banner's borders for religious reasons such as pilgrimage had to apply for passports to give them permission.[^cite_note-22]

During the eighteenth century, growing numbers of [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) settlers had illegally begun to move into the Inner Mongolian steppe. By 1791, there had been so many Han Chinese settlers in the [Front Gorlos Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qian_Gorlos_Mongol_Autonomous_County) that the jasak had petitioned the Qing government to legalise the status of the peasants who had already settled there.[^cite_note-23]

During the nineteenth century, the Manchus were becoming increasingly sinicised and faced with the Russian threat, they began to encourage Han Chinese farmers to settle in both Mongolia and Manchuria. This policy was followed by subsequent governments. The railroads that were being built in these regions were especially useful to the Han Chinese settlers. Land was either sold by Mongol Princes, or leased to Han Chinese farmers, or simply taken away from the nomads and given to Han Chinese farmers.

A group of Han Chinese during the Qing dynasty called "Mongol followers" immigrated to Inner Mongolia who worked as servants for Mongols and Mongol princes and married Mongol women. Their descendants continued to marry Mongol women and changed their ethnicity to Mongol as they assimilated into the Mongol people, an example of this were the ancestors of [Li Shouxin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Shouxin). They distinguished themselves apart from "true Mongols" 真蒙古.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

### Republic of China and the Second World War periods

Further information: [Mongolia (1911–24)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolia_\(1911%E2%80%9324\)), [Inner Mongolian People's Revolutionary Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Mongolian_People's_Revolutionary_Party), [Mengjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengjiang), and [Mongol United Autonomous Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_United_Autonomous_Government)



Mongols stand in front of a yurt, 1912

Outer Mongolia gained independence from the Qing dynasty in 1911, when the Jebtsundamba Khutugtu of the Khalkha was declared the [Bogd Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogd_Khan) of Mongolia. Although almost all banners of Inner Mongolia recognised the Bogd Khan as the supreme ruler of Mongols, the internal strife within the region prevented a full reunification. The Mongol rebellions in Inner Mongolia were counterbalanced by princes who hoped to see a restored Qing dynasty in Manchuria and Mongolia, as they considered the theocratic rule of the Bogd Khan would be against their modernising objectives for Mongolia.[^cite_note-27] Eventually, the newly formed [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)) promised a new nation of five races ([Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu), [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols_in_China), [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people) and [Uyghur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uyghur_people)).[^cite_note-28] and suppressed the Mongol rebellions in the area.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

The Republic of China reorganised Inner Mongolia into provinces:

- [Rehe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehe_\(province\)) province was created to include the Juuuda and Josutu leagues, plus the [Chengde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengde) area in what is now northern [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei).

- [Chahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chahar_\(province\)) province was created to include Xilingol league as well as much of the former territory of the Eight Banners.

- [Suiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suiyuan) province was created to include Ulanqab league, Yekejuu league, and the Hetao region (former Guihua Tümed territory).

- Hulunbuir stayed within [Heilongjiang](173816.md) in Manchuria, which had become a province.

- Most of Jirim league came under the new province of [Fengtian](155516.md) in southern Manchuria.

- Taoxi Mongolia, i.e., Alashan and Ejine leagues, was incorporated into neighbouring [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) province. Later on [Ningxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningxia) province was split out of northern Gansu, and Taoxi Mongolia became part of Ningxia.

Some [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China) maps still show this structure.

The history of Inner Mongolia during the Second World War is complicated, with Japanese invasion and different kinds of resistance movements. In 1931, Manchuria came under the control of the Japanese puppet state [Manchukuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo), taking some Mongol areas in the Manchurian provinces (i.e., Hulunbuir and Jirim leagues) along. Rehe was also incorporated into Manchukuo in 1933, taking Juu Uda and Josutu leagues along with it. These areas were occupied by Manchukuo until the end of [World War II](32927.md) in 1945.

In 1937, the [Empire of Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) openly and fully invaded the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)). On 8 December 1937, Mongolian Prince [Demchugdongrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demchugdongrub) (also known as "De Wang") declared independence for the remaining parts of Inner Mongolia (i.e., the Suiyuan and Chahar provinces) as [Mengjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengjiang), and signed agreements with Manchukuo and Japan. Its capital was established at [Zhangbei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangbei_Town) (now in [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei) province), with the Japanese puppet government's control extending as far west as the [Hohhot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohhot) region. The Japanese advance was defeated by Hui Muslim General [Ma Hongbin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Hongbin) at the [Battle of West Suiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_West_Suiyuan) and [Battle of Wuyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Wuyuan). Since 1945, Inner Mongolia has remained part of China.

The Mongol [Ulanhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulanhu) fought against the Japanese.



Delegates of Inner Mongolia People's Congress shouting slogans

Ethnic Mongolian guerrilla units were created by the Kuomintang Nationalists to fight against the Japanese during the war in the late 30s and early 40s. These Mongol militias were created by the Ejine and Alashaa based commissioner's offices created by the Kuomintang.[^cite_note-Lin-31][^cite_note-32] Prince Demchugdongrub's Mongols were targeted by Kuomintang Mongols to defect to the Republic of China. The Nationalists recruited 1,700 ethnic minority fighters in Inner Mongolia and created war zones in the Tumet Banner, Ulanchab League, and Ordos Yekejuu League.[^cite_note-Lin-31][^cite_note-Lin2-33]

The [Inner Mongolian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Mongolian_People's_Republic) was founded shortly after the Second World War. It existed from 9 September 1945 until 6 November 1945.

### People's Republic of China

The Communist movement gradually gained momentum as part of the Third Communist International in Inner Mongolia during the Japanese period. By the end of WWII, the Inner Mongolian faction of the Comintern had a functional militia and actively opposed the attempts at independence by De Wang's Chinggisid princes on the grounds of fighting feudalism. Following the end of [World War II](32927.md), the [Chinese Communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_China) gained control of Manchuria as well as the Inner Mongolian Communists with decisive Soviet support and established the Inner Mongolia Autonomous Region in 1947. The Comintern army was absorbed into the People's Liberation Army. Initially, the autonomous region included just the Hulunbuir region. Over the next decade, as the communists established the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) and consolidated control over mainland China, Inner Mongolia was expanded westwards to include five of the six original leagues (except Josutu League, which remains in [Liaoning](155516.md) province), the northern part of the Chahar region, by then a league as well (southern Chahar remains in [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei) province), the Hetao region, and the Alashan and Ejine banners. Eventually, nearly all areas with sizeable Mongol populations were incorporated into the region, giving present-day Inner Mongolia its elongated shape. The leader of Inner Mongolia during that time, as both regional CPC secretary and head of regional government, was [Ulanhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulanhu).

During the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution), the administration of Ulanhu was purged, and a wave of repressions was initiated against the Mongol population of the autonomous region.[^cite_note-34] Among the victims in Inner Mongolia, 75 per cent were Mongols, even though they only constituted 10 per cent of the population.[^cite_note-35] In 1969, much of Inner Mongolia was distributed among surrounding provinces, with Hulunbuir divided between [Heilongjiang](173816.md) and [Jilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jilin), Jirim going to Jilin, Juu Uda to [Liaoning](155516.md), and the Alashan and Ejine region divided among [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu) and [Ningxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningxia). This decision was reversed in 1979.

Inner Mongolia was underdeveloped until the early 2000s, when huge [mineral deposits](58230808.md) including [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_in_China) (such as Dongsheng Coalfield) and [rare earth metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earth_industry_in_China) were discovered.[^cite_note-Zhan-2022-36]<sup>: 47 </sup> GDP growth has continually been over 10%, even 15% and connections with the [Wolf Economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Economy) to the north has helped development. However, growth has come at a cost with huge amounts of pollution and degradation to the grasslands.[^cite_note-37] Attempts to attract [ethnic Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) to migrate from other regions, as well as urbanise those rural nomads and peasants has led to huge amounts of corruption and waste in public spending, such as the [Kangbashi district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangbashi_District) of [Ordos City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordos_City).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] However, the district's population has steadily increased in the years since, reaching nearly 153,000 people by 2017, a significant rise from 30,000 in 2009, demonstrating the growth and development brought about by these efforts.[^cite_note-40] Acute uneven [wealth distribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_distribution) has further exacerbated ethnic tensions, many indigenous Mongolians feeling they are increasingly marginalised in their own homeland, leading to [riots in 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Inner_Mongolia_unrest) and 2013.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

On 31 August 2020, [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Inner_Mongolia_protests) broke out in ethnic Mongol communities due to plans by the government to phase out Mongol-only medium teaching to be replaced with bilingual medium teaching.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-Qin_2020-44][^cite_note-45]

## Geography



Inner Mongolian steppes



Topography of Inner Mongolia in China

Further information: [Agulugou Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agulugou_Formation)

Inner Mongolia is a provincial-level subdivision of [North China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China), but its great stretch means that parts of it belong to [Northeast China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_China) and [Northwest China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_China) as well. It borders eight provincial-level divisions in all three of the aforementioned regions ([Heilongjiang](173816.md), [Jilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jilin), [Liaoning](155516.md), [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei), [Shanxi](81262.md), [Shaanxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi), [Ningxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningxia), and [Gansu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gansu)), tying with Shaanxi for the greatest number of bordering provincial-level divisions. Most of its international border is with Mongolia,[^cite_note-46] which, in Chinese, is sometimes called "Outer Mongolia", while a small portion is with Russia's [Zabaykalsky Krai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabaykalsky_Krai).

Inner Mongolia largely consists of the northern side of the [North China Craton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_craton), a tilted and sedimented [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) block. In the extreme southwest is the edge of the Tibetan Plateau where the autonomous region's highest peak, [Main Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Peak,_Helan_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the [Helan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helan_Mountains) reaches 3,556 metres (11,670 ft), and is still being pushed up today in short bursts.[^cite_note-quaternary-2] Most of Inner Mongolia is part of the [Mongolian Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_Plateau), averaging around 1,200 metres (3,940 ft) in altitude and covered by extensive [loess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loess) and [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand) deposits. The northern part consists of the [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic) era [Khingan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khingan_Mountains), and is owing to the cooler climate more forested, chiefly with [Manchurian elm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulmus_laciniata), [ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_\(Fraxinus\)), [birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch), [Mongolian oak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_mongolica) and a number of [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus) and [spruce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Picea) species. Where [discontinuous permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discontinuous_permafrost) is present north of [Hailar District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailar_District), forests are almost exclusively coniferous. In the south, the natural vegetation is grassland in the east and very sparse in the arid west, and grazing is the dominant economic activity.

Owing to the ancient, weathered rocks lying under its deep sedimentary cover, Inner Mongolia is a major mining district, possessing large reserves of [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal), [iron ore](145352.md) and [rare-earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare-earth) minerals, which have made it a major industrial region today.

### Climate

Köppen–Geiger climate classification map at 1-km resolution for Inner Mongolia (China) for 1991–2020

Köppen–Geiger climate classification map at 1-km resolution for Inner Mongolia



Winter in Ulanbutan Grassland, [Hexigten Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexigten_Banner)

Due to its elongated shape, Inner Mongolia has a four-season [monsoon](57630.md) climate with regional variations. The winters in Inner Mongolia are very long, cold, and dry with frequent blizzards, though snowfall is so light that Inner Mongolia has no modern glaciers[^cite_note-quaternary-2] even on the highest Helan peaks. The spring is short, mild and arid, with large, dangerous [sandstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstorms), whilst the summer is very warm to hot and relatively humid except in the west where it remains dry. Autumn is brief and sees a steady cooling, with temperatures below 0 °C (32 °F) reached in October in the north and November in the south. Officially, most of Inner Mongolia is classified as either a [cold arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_arid) or [steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe_climate) regime ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BWk, BSk*, respectively). The small portion besides these are classified as [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental) (Köppen *Dwa/Dwb*) in the northeast, or [subarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate) (Köppen *Dwc*) in the far north near [Hulunbuir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulunbuir).[^cite_note-47]

#### Climate crisis

The region has increasing [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_China) and frequent sandstorms, which damages agriculture.[^cite_note-48]

## Administrative divisions

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of administrative divisions of Inner Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_administrative_divisions_of_Inner_Mongolia) and [List of township-level divisions of Inner Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_township-level_divisions_of_Inner_Mongolia).

See also: [List of cities in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_China)

Inner Mongolia is divided into twelve [prefecture-level divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_China#Prefectural_level). Until the late 1990s, most of Inner Mongolia's prefectural regions were known as *[leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leagues_of_China)* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 盟), a usage retained from Mongol divisions of the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty). Similarly, county-level divisions are often known as *banners* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 旗). Since the 1990s, numerous leagues have been converted into [prefecture-level cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture-level_city), although banners remain. The restructuring led to the conversion of primate cities in most leagues to convert to districts administratively (i.e.: [Hailar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailar_District), [Jining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jining_District) and [Dongsheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongsheng)). Some newly founded prefecture-level cities have chosen to retain the original name of league (i.e.: Hulunbuir, Bayannur and Ulanqab), some have adopted the Chinese name of their [primate city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate_city) ([Chifeng](1674149.md), [Tongliao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongliao)), and one league (Yekejuu) simply renamed itself [Ordos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordos_City). Despite these recent administrative changes, there is no indication that the Alxa, Hinggan, and Xilingol Leagues will convert to prefecture-level cities in the near future.

## Economy

Farming of crops such as [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) takes precedence along the river valleys. In the more arid grasslands, herding of [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat), [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep) and so on is a traditional method of subsistence. [Forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry) and [hunting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunting) are somewhat important in the [Greater Khingan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khingan) ranges in the east. [Reindeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reindeer) herding is carried out by [Evenks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenks) in the Evenk Autonomous Banner. More recently, growing [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape) and [winemaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winemaking) have become an economic factor in the [Wuhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhai) area.



Theater in [Hohhot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohhot)

[Mining-related industries](58230808.md) are a major part of Inner Mongolia's economy.[^cite_note-Zhan-2022-36]<sup>: 23 </sup> Inner Mongolia has an abundance of resources especially coal, [cashmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cashmere_wool), natural gas, [rare-earth elements](145440.md), and has more deposits of naturally occurring [niobium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niobium), [zirconium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zirconium) and [beryllium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryllium) than any other [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province)-level region in China. The region is also a source of [crude oil](23195.md), with sites such as the [Xifeng oil field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xifeng_oil_field) producing tens of thousands of barrels per day. However, in the past, the exploitation and utilisation of resources were rather inefficient, which resulted in poor returns from rich resources. Inner Mongolia is also an important coal production base, with more than a quarter of the world's coal reserves located in the province.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-59] It plans to double annual coal output by 2010 (from the 2005 volume of 260 million tons) to 500 million tons of coal a year.[^cite_note-60]



Inner Mongolia Gymnasium

Industry in Inner Mongolia has grown up mainly around coal, [power generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_generation), forestry-related industries, and related industries. Inner Mongolia now encourages six competitive industries: energy, chemicals, metallurgy, equipment manufacturing, processing of farm (including [dairy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy)) produce, and high technology. Well-known Inner Mongolian enterprises include companies such as [Erdos Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erdos_Group), [Yili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yili_Group), and [Mengniu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengniu). As with much of China, economic growth has led to a boom in construction, including new commercial development and large apartment complexes.

The GDP of Inner Mongolia in 2022 was CN¥2.3 trillion (US\$344 billion in nominal),[^cite_note-data2022-61] with an average annual increase of 10% from the period 2010–2015. Its per capita GDP was CN¥96,474 (US\$14,343 in nominal), ranking 8th among all the 31 provincial divisions of China.[^cite_note-data2022-61] The primary, secondary and tertiary industries contributed ¥265 billion (\$39.45 billion), ¥1.12 trillion (\$167.1 billion) and ¥926 billion (\$137.7 billion) to the GDP respectively.[^cite_note-data2022-61]

In addition to its large reserves of natural resources, Inner Mongolia also has the largest usable wind power capacity in China[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-59] thanks to strong winds which develop in the province's grasslands. Some private companies have set up [wind parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power_in_China) in parts of Inner Mongolia such as [Bailingmiao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bailingmiao), Hutengliang and Zhouzi.

East of Jilantai, Inner Mongolia, there is a ballistic missile training area used by the [People's Liberation Army Rocket Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Army_Rocket_Force) (PLARF) to train missile crews for mobile missile launchers, their support vehicles, and silo-based ballistic missiles.[^cite_note-62]

## Government and politics

See also: [List of leaders of Inner Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_leaders_of_Inner_Mongolia)

Under the [Constitution of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_China), articles 112–122, [autonomous regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_region) have limited autonomy in both the political and economic arena. Autonomous regions have more discretion in administering economic policy in the region in accordance with national guidelines. Structurally, the Chairman—who legally must be a Mongol—is always kept in check by the [Communist Party Regional Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Mongolia_Autonomous_Regional_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) Secretary, who is usually from a different part of China (to reduce corruption) and Han Chinese. As of May 2023, the current party secretary is [Sun Shaocheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Shaocheng). The Inner Mongolian government and its subsidiaries follow roughly the same structure as that of a Chinese province. With regards to economic policy, as a part of increased [federalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_federalism) characteristics in China, Inner Mongolia has become more independent in implementing its own economic roadmap.

Because of Inner Mongolia's status as an autonomous region, Mongols, as the titular nationality, enjoy preferential policies in education and employment, including a guarantee that the chairman of the regional government will be of the Mongol ethnic group. The percentage of Mongols in the [Inner Mongolia People's Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_Mongolia_Autonomous_Regional_People's_Congress) has been fixed at 37–39 percent since 1954. Thus, the Mongols are vastly overrepresented compared to their percentage of the population while still remaining a minority in the government and legislature.[^cite_note-Atwood-65]

The local administration of Inner Mongolia contains a parallel system of [leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leagues_of_Inner_Mongolia), [banners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banners_of_Inner_Mongolia) and [sums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sums_of_Inner_Mongolia), in addition to the system of [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefecture-level_divisions_of_China), [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County-level_divisions_of_China) and [townships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townships_of_China) used in the rest of China. While their structures are identical, the former units must be headed by Mongols. The sums are almost always majority Mongol, and are dominated by Mongolian-speaking cadres in pastoral areas.[^cite_note-Atwood-65]

The position of Chairman of Inner Mongolia alternates between [Khorchin Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorchin_Mongols) in the east and [Tumed Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumed) in the west. Since the end of the Cultural Revolution, this convention has not been broken. The family of [Ulanhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulanhu) has retained influence in regional politics ever since the founding the People's Republic. His son [Buhe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buhe_\(politician\)) and granddaughter [Bu Xiaolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bu_Xiaolin) both served as Chairman of the region.

When the autonomous region was established in 1947, Han Chinese comprised 83.6% of the population, while the Mongols comprised 14.8% of the population.[^cite_note-73] By 2020, the percentage of Han Chinese had dropped to 78.7%. While the Hetao region along the Yellow River has always alternated between farmers from the south and nomads from the north, the most recent wave of Han Chinese migration began in the early 18th century with encouragement from the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty), and continued into the 20th century. Han Chinese live mostly in the Hetao region as well as various population centres in central and eastern Inner Mongolia. Over 70% of Mongols are concentrated in less than 18% of Inner Mongolia's territory ([Hinggan League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinggan_League), and the prefectures of [Tongliao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongliao) and [Chifeng](1674149.md)).

Mongols are the second largest ethnic group, comprising 17.7% of the population as of the 2020 census.[^cite_note-Ethnicity-10] They include many diverse Mongolian-speaking groups; groups such as the [Buryats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryats) and the [Oirats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oirats) are also officially considered to be Mongols in China. In addition to the Manchus, other [Tungusic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungusic_peoples) ethnic groups, the [Oroqen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oroqen_people), and the [Evenks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenks) also populate parts of northeastern Inner Mongolia. There is also a significant number of [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) and [Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans_in_China).

Many of the traditionally nomadic Mongols have settled in permanent homes as their pastoral economy was collectivised during the Mao era, and some have taken jobs in cities as migrant labourers; however, some Mongols continue in their nomadic tradition. In practice, highly educated Mongols tend to migrate to big urban centres after which they become essentially indistinct with ethnic Han Chinese populations.

Inter-marriage between Mongol and non-Mongol populations is very common, particularly in areas where Mongols are in regular contact with other groups. There was little cultural stigma within Mongol families for marrying outside the ethnic group, and in urban centres in particular, Mongol men and women married non-Mongols at relatively similar rates. The rates of intermarriage stands in very sharp contrast to ethnic Tibetans and Uyghurs in their respective autonomous regions. By the 1980s, for instance, in [the former Jirim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongliao), nearly 40% of marriages with at least one Mongol spouse was a mixed Mongol–Han Chinese marriage.[^cite_note-rut1999-74] However, anecdotal reports have also demonstrated an increase in Mongol-female, Han Chinese-male pairings in which the woman is of a rural background, ostensibly shutting rural Mongol males from the marriage market as the sex ratio in China becomes more skewed with a much higher proportion of men.[^cite_note-socio-75]

## Language and culture

See also: [Culture of Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Mongolia), [Music of Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Mongolia), [Music of Inner Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Inner_Mongolia), and [Mongolian language in Inner Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language_in_Inner_Mongolia)



A [KFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KFC) in Hohhot, the capital, with a bilingual street sign in [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) and [Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_Language)



Inner Mongolian carpet c. 1870



Jade dragon of the [Hongshan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongshan_culture) (4700 BC – 2900 BC) found in [Ongniud Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ongniud_Banner), [Chifeng](1674149.md)

The use of Mongolian by Inner Mongolia's 4.1 million ethnic Mongols has sharply declined since the 1980s.[^cite_note-82] Across the whole of China, the language is spoken by roughly half of the country's 5.8 million ethnic Mongols (2005 estimate).[^cite_note-Mongolian-83] with 2.1 million Mongol speakers in Inner Mongolia.[^cite_note-84] The use of Mongolian in China, specifically in Inner Mongolia, has witnessed periods of decline and revival over the last few hundred years. The language experienced a decline during the late Qing period, a revival between 1947 and 1965, a second decline between 1966 and 1976, a second revival between 1977 and 1992, and a third decline between 1995 and 2012.[^cite_note-85] However, in spite of the decline of the Mongolian language in some of Inner Mongolia's urban areas and educational spheres, the ethnic identity of the urbanised Chinese-speaking Mongols is most likely going to survive due to the presence of urban ethnic communities.[^cite_note-86] The multilingual situation in Inner Mongolia does not appear to obstruct efforts by ethnic Mongols to preserve their language.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] Although an unknown number of Mongols in China, such as the Tumets, may have completely or partially lost the ability to speak their language, they are still registered as ethnic Mongols and continue to identify themselves as ethnic Mongols.[^cite_note-Mongolian-83][^cite_note-89] The children of inter-ethnic Mongol-Chinese marriages also claim to be and are registered as ethnic Mongols.[^cite_note-90]

By law, all street signs, commercial outlets, and government documents must be bilingual, written in both Mongolian and Chinese. There are three Mongolian TV channels in the Inner Mongolia Satellite TV network. In public transportation, all announcements are to be bilingual.

[Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) in Inner Mongolia speak [Mongolian dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language) such as [Chakhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakhar_Mongolian), Xilingol, [Baarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baarin_Mongolian), [Khorchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorchin_Mongolian) and Kharchin Mongolian and, depending on definition and analysis, further dialects[^cite_note-91] or closely related independent Central [Mongolic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolic_languages)[^cite_note-92] such as [Ordos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordos_Mongolian), [Khamnigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khamnigan_Mongol), Barghu [Buryat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat_language) and the arguably [Oirat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oirat_language) dialect [Alasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alasha_dialect). The standard pronunciation of Mongolian in China is based on the Chakhar dialect of the [Plain Blue Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_Blue_Banner,_Inner_Mongolia), located in central Inner Mongolia, while the grammar is based on all [Inner Mongolian dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_language_in_Inner_Mongolia).[^cite_note-93] This is different from the Mongolian state, where the standard pronunciation is based on the closely related [Khalkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalkha_Mongolian) dialect. There are a number of independent languages spoken in [Hulunbuir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulunbuir) such as the somewhat more distant Mongolic language [Dagur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daur_language) and the [Tungusic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungusic_languages) [Evenki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evenki_language). Officially, even the Evenki dialect [Oroqin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oroqen_language) is considered a language.[^cite_note-94]

The [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) of Inner Mongolia speak a variety of dialects, depending on the region. Those in the eastern parts tend to speak [Northeastern Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_Mandarin), which belongs to the [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese) group of dialects; those in the central parts, such as the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River) valley, speak varieties of [Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_Chinese), another subdivision of Chinese, due to its proximity to other Jin-speaking areas in China such as the [Shanxi](81262.md) province. Cities such as Hohhot and Baotou both have their unique brand of Jin Chinese such as the [Zhangjiakou–Hohhot dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangjiakou%E2%80%93Hohhot_dialect) which are sometimes incomprehensible with dialects spoken in northeastern regions such as [Hailar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hailar_District).

The vast grasslands have long symbolised Inner Mongolia. Mongolian art often depicts the grassland in an uplifting fashion and emphasises Mongolian nomadic traditions. The [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols_in_China) of Inner Mongolia still practice their traditional arts. Inner Mongolian cuisine has Mongol roots and consists of [dairy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy)-related products and *hand-held lamb* (手扒肉). In recent years, franchises based on [hot pot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_pot) have appeared in Inner Mongolia, the best known of which is [Little Sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Sheep_Group). Notable Inner Mongolian commercial brand names include [Mengniu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengniu) and [Yili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yili_Group), both of which began as dairy product and [ice cream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_cream) producers.

Among the Han Chinese of Inner Mongolia, [Shanxi opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanxi_opera) is a popular traditional form of entertainment. See also: [Shanxi](81262.md). A popular career in Inner Mongolia is circus acrobatics. The internationally known Inner Mongolia Acrobatic Troupe travels and performs with the renowned [Ringling Bros. and Barnum & Bailey Circus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ringling_Bros._and_Barnum_&_Bailey_Circus).

The [cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_\(religious_practice\)) of [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan), present in the form of various Genghis Khan temples, is a tradition of [Mongolian shamanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_shamanism), in which he is considered a [cultural hero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_hero) and divine ancestor, an embodiment of the *[Tenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengri)* (Heaven, God of Heaven).[^cite_note-100] His worship in special temples, greatly developed in Inner Mongolia since the 1980s, is also shared by the [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), claiming his spirit as the founding principle of the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty).[^cite_note-101]

[Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism) ([Mongolian Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_Mongolia), locally also known as "Yellow Buddhism") is the dominant form of Buddhism in Inner Mongolia, also practised by many Han Chinese. Another form of Buddhism, practised by the Chinese, are the schools of [Chinese Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Buddhism).

## Tourism

In the capital city [Hohhot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohhot):

- [Da Zhao Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dazhao_Temple_\(Hohhot\)) is a [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism) temple built in 1580. Dazhao Temple is known for three sites: a [statue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue) of [Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha) made from [silver](27119.md), elaborate carvings of [dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon), and [murals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mural).

- [Five-pagoda Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Pagoda_Temple_\(Hohhot\)) is located in the capital of Inner Mongolia Hohhot. It is also called Jingangzuo Dagoba, used to be one building of the Cideng Temple (Temple of Merciful Light) built in 1727.

- [Residence of Gurun Princess Kejing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residence_of_Gurun_Princess_Kejing) is a mansion typical of [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) architectural style that was built in 1705 by the [Kangxi Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor) for his daughter.

- Wanbu-Huayanjing Pagoda (万部华严经塔) in Hohhot. It was built during the reign of [Emperor Shengzong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Shengzong_of_Liao) (983–1031) of the Khitan Liao dynasty (907–1125) and is still well preserved.

- Xiaozhao Temple, also known as Chongfu temple, is a [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism) temple built in 1697 and favoured by the [Kangxi Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangxi_Emperor) of the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty).

- [Xilitu Zhao / Siregtu juu Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xilitu_Zhao) is the largest Buddhist temple in the Höhhot area, and once the centre of power of [Tibetan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_Buddhism) in the region.

- [Zhaojun Tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhaojun_Tomb) is the tomb of [Wang Zhaojun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Zhaojun), a [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) palace lady-in-waiting who became the consort of the [Xiongnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiongnu) ruler Huhanye Shanyu in 33BC.



The ruins of [Shangdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangdu)

Elsewhere in Inner Mongolia:

- Baotou's urban grassland, Saihantala, with elevations ranging from 1,034 to 1,058 meters, features yurts, god hills, wrestling, and horse racing activities.

- The [Mausoleum of Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Genghis_Khan), the [cenotaph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenotaph) of [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan), is located in [Ordos City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordos_City).

- [Bashang Grasslands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashang_Grasslands), on the border close to [Beijing](18603746.md), is a popular retreat for urban residents wanting to get a taste of grasslands life.

- The Arshihaty Stone Forest in [Hexigten Global Geopark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexigten_Global_Geopark) has magnificent granite rock formations formed from natural erosion.

- [Xiangshawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangshawan), or "singing sands gorge", is located in the [Gobi Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobi_Desert) and contains numerous tourist attractions including sand sledding and camel rides.

- Remains of Zhongjing (Central Capital) built in 1003 by Emperor Shengzong of the [Khitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khitan_people) [Liao dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liao_dynasty) (907–1125) in Ningcheng County.

- Remains of [Shangjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shangjing_Linhuangfu) (Upper Capital) built in 918 by [Yelu Abaoji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelu_Abaoji) the 1st emperor of the Khitan Liao dynasty (907–1125). Also called Huangdu, it was one of the five capitals of the Liao dynasty.

- Zuling Mausoleum of Abaoji Khan. It was built in 926 for Abaoji the 1st Emperor of the Liao dynasty. Located north-west of Shifangzi village.

- Tablets of Juyan. Han dynasty (206 BC – 220 AD) inscriptions on wood and bamboo. In 1930 Folke Bergman of the Sino-Swedish expedition first discovered 10,000 tablets at [Ejin Khoshuu](24750432.md) in the Gobi Desert.

Sign of the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center

One of China's space vehicle launch facilities, [Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuquan_Satellite_Launch_Center), is located in the [Alxa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alxa_League)'s [Ejin Banner](24750432.md), in the west of Inner Mongolia. It was founded in 1958, making it the PRC's first launch facility. As of 2021, Jiuquan has documented more launches than any other launch facilities in China, and is still the only launch site for crewed space missions ([Shenzhou program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_program)). While geologically located inside Inner Mongolia, the launch centre is named after Jiuquan, which is the nearest urban centre in the nearby province of Gansu. As military facilities, the core areas at Jiuquan Center are highly restricted and can only be visited by tourist buses operated by the centre, while the visitor centre is open to the public and can be accessed from the south gate.

Inner Mongolia is also home to the two (and only two) space vehicles landing sites in China, the [Siziwang Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siziwang_Banner) Landing Site in Ulanqab and the Dongfeng Landing Site in Alxa.

### Colleges and universities

### Language policy and protest

Main article: [2020 Inner Mongolia protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Inner_Mongolia_protests)

Inner Mongolia under the People's Republic of China has historically allowed the Mongolian language as a [medium of instruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_of_instruction). A 1979 article from the *[People's Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Daily)* praises the re-establishment of Mongolian-language education after "interference and vandalism by the [Gang of Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gang_of_Four)" during the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution).[^cite_note-102]

It was reported by *[The New York Times](30680.md)* on 31 August 2020 that in the summer 2020, the Chinese government announced a new education policy, which called for Chinese to gradually replace Mongolian as the language of instruction in three subjects, including language and literature, politics, and history, in elementary and middle schools around the Inner Mongolia region. Thousands of ethnic Mongolians in northern China gathered to protest the policy.[^cite_note-Qin_2020-44]
