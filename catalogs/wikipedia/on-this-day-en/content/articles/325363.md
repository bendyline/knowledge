The **Sahara** ([/səˈhɑːrə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [/səˈhɛrə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is a [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert) spanning [North Africa](21714.md). With an area of 9,200,000 square kilometres (3,600,000 sq mi), it is the largest hot desert in the world and the [third-largest desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_deserts_by_area) overall, smaller only than the deserts of [Antarctica](18959138.md) and the northern [Arctic](36971.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-geology.com-3][^cite_note-Size_and_Population_of_Sahara-4]

The desert covers much of [North Africa](21714.md), excluding the fertile region on the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) coast, the [Atlas Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Mountains) of the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb), and the [Nile Valley](21244.md) in [Egypt](8087628.md) and the [Sudan](27421.md).[^cite_note-brit-1]

It stretches from the [Red Sea](26191.md) in the east and the Mediterranean in the north to the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) in the west, where the landscape gradually changes from desert to coastal plains. To the south it is bounded by the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel), a belt of [semi-arid tropical savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_grasslands,_savannas,_and_shrublands) around the [Niger River](21437.md) valley and the [Sudan region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_\(region\)) of [sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md). The Sahara can be divided into several regions, including the western Sahara, the central [Ahaggar Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahaggar_Mountains), the [Tibesti Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibesti_Mountains), the [Aïr Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%AFr_Mountains), the [Ténéré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9n%C3%A9r%C3%A9) desert, and the [Libyan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Desert).

For several hundred thousand years, the Sahara has alternated between desert and [savanna grassland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_humid_period) in a 20,000-year cycle[^cite_note-5] caused by the [precession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precession) of [Earth's axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt#Earth) (about 26,000 years) as it rotates around the Sun, which changes the location of the [North African monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_climate_cycles).

## Toponymy

The name "Sahara" is derived from [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): صَحَارَى, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *ṣaḥārā* [/sˤaħaːraː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic), a [broken plural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broken_plural) form of *ṣaḥrā'* (صَحْرَاء [/sˤaħraːʔ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic)), meaning "desert".[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

## Geography



The main biomes in Africa



A 2002 satellite image of the Sahara by [NASA WorldWind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA_WorldWind)

The Sahara covers large parts of [Algeria](358.md), [Chad](5488.md), [Egypt](8087628.md), [Libya](17633.md), [Mali](19127.md), [Mauritania](18778516.md), [Niger](21373.md), [Western Sahara](33220.md) and [Sudan](27421.md), and parts of southern [Morocco](19291.md) and [Tunisia](30188.md). It covers 9 million square kilometres (3,500,000 sq mi), 31% of the African continent. If all areas with a mean annual precipitation of less than 250 mm (9.8 in) were included, the Sahara would be 11 million square kilometres (4,200,000 sq mi). It is one of three distinct [physiographic provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiographic_provinces) of the [African massive physiographic division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiographic_regions_of_the_world#Compendium_table_of_the_worlds_physiographic_regions). The Sahara is so large and bright that, in theory, it could be detected from other stars as a surface feature of Earth, with near-current technology.[^cite_note-10]

The Sahara is a highly diverse desert region composed of [hamada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamada) (rocky plateaus), [ergs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erg_\(landform\)) (sand seas covered with [sand dunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_dune)), gravel plains (*reg*), [dry valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi) (*wadi*), [dry lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_lake), and [salt flats](390410.md) (*shatt* or *chott*).[^cite_note-PA1327-11] Many of its sand dunes rise over 180 metres (590 ft),[^cite_note-12] and its landscapes are shaped by [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolian_processes) and rare rainfall. The region also includes mountain massifs, volcanic uplands, oases, and unusual landforms such as the [Richat Structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richat_Structure) in Mauritania.&#x20;

&#x20;\
&#x20;Several deeply dissected mountains, many volcanic, rise from the desert, including the [Aïr Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%AFr_Mountains), [Ahaggar Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahaggar_Mountains), [Saharan Atlas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharan_Atlas), [Tibesti Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibesti_Mountains), [Adrar des Iforas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrar_des_Iforas), and the [Red Sea Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Sea_Hills). The highest peak in the Sahara is [Emi Koussi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emi_Koussi), a [shield volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shield_volcano) in the [Tibesti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibesti) range of northern Chad.&#x20;

&#x20;\
&#x20;The central Sahara is [hyperarid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperarid), with sparse vegetation. The northern and southern reaches of the desert, along with the highlands, have areas of sparse grassland and [desert shrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_shrub), with trees and taller shrubs in [wadis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi), where moisture collects. In the central, hyperarid region, there are many subdivisions: [Tanezrouft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanezrouft), the [Ténéré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9n%C3%A9r%C3%A9), the [Libyan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Desert), the [Eastern Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Desert), the [Nubian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_Desert) and others. These extremely arid areas often receive no rain for years.&#x20;

&#x20;\
&#x20;To the north, the Sahara skirts the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) in Egypt and portions of Libya, but in [Cyrenaica](75451.md) and the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb), the Sahara borders the [Mediterranean forest, woodland, and scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_forest,_woodland,_and_scrub) eco-regions of northern Africa, all of which have a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) characterized by hot summers and cool and rainy winters. According to the botanical criteria of [Frank White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_White_\(botanist\))[^cite_note-13] and geographer Robert Capot-Rey,[^cite_note-Grove1958-14][^cite_note-Bisson2003-15] the northern limit of the Sahara corresponds to the northern limit of [date palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm) cultivation and the southern limit of the range of [esparto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esparto), a [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaceae) typical of the Mediterranean climate portion of the Maghreb and [Iberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberia). The northern limit also corresponds to the 100 mm (3.9 in) [isohyet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotherm_\(contour_line\)) of annual precipitation.[^cite_note-Walton2007-16]

To the south, the Sahara is bounded by the Sahel, a belt of dry [tropical savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna) with a summer rainy season that extends across Africa from east to west. The southern limit of the Sahara is indicated botanically by the southern limit of *[Cornulaca monacantha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornulaca_monacantha)* (a drought-tolerant member of the [Chenopodiaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chenopodiaceae)), or northern limit of *[Cenchrus biflorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenchrus_biflorus)*, a [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaceae) typical of the Sahel.[^cite_note-Grove1958-14][^cite_note-Bisson2003-15] According to climatic criteria, the southern limit of the Sahara corresponds to the 150 mm (5.9 in) isohyet of annual precipitation (this is a long-term average, since [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) varies annually).[^cite_note-Walton2007-16]

Important cities located in the Sahara include [Nouakchott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nouakchott), the capital of Mauritania; [Tamanrasset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamanrasset), [Ouargla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouargla), [Béchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9char), [Hassi Messaoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassi_Messaoud), [Ghardaïa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharda%C3%AFa), and [El Oued](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Oued) in Algeria; [Timbuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu) in Mali; [Agadez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agadez) in Niger; [Ghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghat,_Libya) in Libya; and [Faya-Largeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faya-Largeau) in Chad.&#x20;

## Climate



An [oasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis) in the [Ahaggar Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahaggar_Mountains)

The Sahara is the world's largest [hot desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] It is located in the [horse latitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_latitudes) under the [subtropical ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical_ridge), a significant belt of semi-permanent subtropical warm-core high pressure where the air from the upper troposphere usually descends, warming and drying the lower troposphere and preventing cloud formation.&#x20;

The permanent absence of clouds allows unhindered light and thermal radiation. The [stability of the atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_instability#Stable_atmosphere) above the desert prevents any convective overturning, thus making rainfall virtually non-existent. As a consequence, the weather tends to be sunny, dry and stable with a minimal chance of rainfall. Subsiding, diverging, dry air masses associated with subtropical [high-pressure systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-pressure_system) are extremely unfavorable for the development of convectional showers. The subtropical ridge is the predominant factor that explains the [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BWh*) of this vast region. The descending airflow is the strongest and the most effective over the eastern part of the Great Desert, in the [Libyan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Desert): this is the sunniest, driest and the most nearly "rainless" place on the planet, rivaling the [Atacama Desert](18952975.md), lying in [Chile](5489.md) and [Peru](170691.md).&#x20;

The rainfall inhibition and the dissipation of cloud cover are most accentuated over the eastern section of the Sahara rather than the western. The prevailing [air mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_mass) lying above the Sahara is the continental tropical (cT) air mass, which is hot and dry. Hot, dry air masses primarily form over the North-African desert from the heating of the vast continental land area, and it affects the whole desert during most of the year. Because of this [extreme heating](293270.md) process, a [thermal low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_low) is usually noticed near the surface, and is the strongest and the most developed during the summertime. The [Sahara High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_High?action=edit\&redlink=1) represents the eastern continental extension of the [Azores High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azores_High), centered over the [North Atlantic Ocean](698.md). The subsidence of the Sahara High nearly reaches the ground during the coolest part of the year, while it is confined to the upper troposphere during the hottest periods.&#x20;

The effects of local surface low pressure are extremely limited because upper-level subsidence still continues to block any form of air ascent. Also, to be protected against rain-bearing weather systems by the atmospheric circulation itself, the desert is made even drier by its geographical configuration and location. Indeed, the extreme aridity of the Sahara is not only explained by the subtropical high pressure: the [Atlas Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Mountains) of Algeria, Morocco and Tunisia also help to enhance the aridity of the northern part of the desert. These major mountain ranges act as a barrier, causing a strong [rain shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_shadow) effect on the leeward side by dropping much of the humidity brought by atmospheric disturbances along the [polar front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_front) which affects the surrounding Mediterranean climates.&#x20;

The primary source of rain in the Sahara is the [Intertropical Convergence Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intertropical_Convergence_Zone), a continuous belt of low-pressure systems near the equator which bring the brief, short and irregular [rainy season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainy_season) to the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) and southern Sahara. Rainfall in this giant desert has to overcome the physical and atmospheric barriers that normally prevent the production of precipitation. The harsh climate of the Sahara is characterized by: extremely low, unreliable, highly erratic rainfall; extremely high sunshine duration values; high temperatures year-round; negligible rates of [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity); a significant [diurnal temperature variation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_temperature_variation); and extremely high levels of [potential evaporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_evaporation) which are the highest recorded worldwide.[^cite_note-19]

### Temperature

&#x20;The sky is usually clear above the desert, and the [sunshine duration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_duration) is extremely high everywhere in the Sahara. Most of the desert has more than 3,600 hours of bright sunshine per year (over 82% of daylight hours), and a wide area in the eastern part has over 4,000 hours of bright sunshine per year (over 91% of daylight hours). The highest values are very close to the theoretical maximum: a value of 4,300 hours, representing 98% of the total number of daylight hours per year, has been recorded both in [Upper Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Egypt) ([Aswan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aswan), [Luxor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxor)) and in the Nubian Desert ([Wadi Halfa](1126657.md)).[^cite_note-books.google.fr-20] The annual average direct [solar irradiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_irradiance) is around 2,800 kWh/(m<sup>2</sup> year) in the Great Desert. The Sahara has a huge potential for solar energy production.



[Sand dunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_dunes) in the [Algerian](358.md) Sahara

The high position of the Sun, the extremely low relative humidity, and the lack of [vegetation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetation) and rainfall make the Great Desert the hottest large region in the world, and the hottest place on Earth during summer in some spots. The average high temperature exceeds 38 to 40 °C (100.4 to 104.0 °F) during the hottest month nearly everywhere in the desert except at very high altitudes. The world's highest mean monthly maximum temperature was 47 °C (116.6 °F) in a remote desert town in the [Algerian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Desert) called [Bou Bernous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bou_Bernous), at an elevation of 378 metres (1,240 ft) above sea level;[^cite_note-books.google.fr-20] it is rivaled only by [Death Valley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Valley,_California).[^cite_note-21]

Other hot spots in Algeria such as [Adrar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrar,_Algeria), [Timimoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timimoun), [In Salah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Salah), [Ouallene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouallene), [Aoulef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aoulef), [Reggane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggane) with an elevation between 200 and 400 metres (660 and 1,310 ft) above sea level get slightly lower summer average highs, around 46 °C (114.8 °F) during the hottest months of the year. Salah, well known in Algeria for its extreme heat, has average high temperatures of 43.8 °C (110.8 °F), 46.4 °C (115.5 °F), 45.5 °C (113.9 °F) and 41.9 °C (107.4 °F) in June, July, August and September respectively. There are even hotter spots in the Sahara, but they are located in extremely remote areas, especially in the [Azalai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azalai), lying in northern Mali. The major part of the desert experiences around three to five months when the average high exceeds 40 °C (104 °F); while in the southern central part of the desert, there are up to six or seven months when the average high temperature exceeds 40 °C (104 °F). Some examples of this are [Bilma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilma), Niger and Faya-Largeau, Chad. The annual average daily temperature exceeds 20 °C (68 °F) everywhere and can approach 30 °C (86 °F) in the hottest regions year-round. However, most of the desert has a value in excess of 25 °C (77 °F).&#x20;



[Camel train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camel_train) at sunset in the Sahara

Sand and ground temperatures are even more extreme. During daytime, the sand temperature is extremely high: it can easily reach 80 °C (176 °F) or more.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-22] A sand temperature of 83.5 °C (182.3 °F) has been recorded in [Port Sudan](213572.md).[^cite_note-ReferenceB-22] Ground temperatures of 72 °C (161.6 °F) have been recorded in the [Adrar of Mauritania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrar_Plateau) and a value of 75 °C (167 °F) has been measured in [Borkou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borkou), northern Chad.[^cite_note-ReferenceB-22]

Due to lack of cloud cover and very low humidity, the desert usually has high [diurnal temperature variations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_temperature_variation) between days and nights. On average, nighttime temperatures tend to be 13–20 °C (23–36 °F) cooler than in the daytime. The smallest variations are found along the coastal regions due to high humidity and are often even lower than a 10 °C (18 °F) difference, while the largest variations are found in inland desert areas where the humidity is the lowest, mainly in the southern Sahara. Still, it is true that winter nights can be cold, as it can drop to the freezing point and even below, especially in high-elevation areas. The frequency of subfreezing winter nights in the Sahara is strongly influenced by the [North Atlantic Oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Oscillation) (NAO), with warmer winter temperatures during negative NAO events and cooler winters with more frosts when the NAO is positive.[^cite_note-23] This is because the weaker clockwise flow around the eastern side of the subtropical anticyclone during negative NAO winters, although too dry to produce more than negligible precipitation, does reduce the flow of dry, cold air from higher latitudes of Eurasia into the Sahara significantly.[^cite_note-24]

### Precipitation

The average annual rainfall ranges from very low in the northern and southern fringes of the desert to nearly non-existent over the central and the eastern part. The thin northern fringe of the desert receives more winter cloudiness and rainfall due to the arrival of [low pressure systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_pressure_system) over the Mediterranean Sea along the polar front, although very attenuated by the rain shadow effects of the mountains and the annual average rainfall ranges from 100 millimetres (4 in) to 250 millimetres (10 in). For example, [Biskra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biskra), Algeria, and [Ouarzazate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouarzazate), Morocco, are found in this zone. The southern fringe of the desert along the border with the Sahel receives summer cloudiness and rainfall due to the arrival of the Intertropical Convergence Zone from the south and the annual average rainfall ranges from 100 millimetres (4 in) to 250 millimetres (10 in). For example, Timbuktu, Mali and [Agadez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agadez), Niger are found in this zone.&#x20;



A natural rock arch in south western Libya

The vast central hyper-arid core of the desert is virtually never affected by northerly or southerly atmospheric disturbances and permanently remains under the influence of the strongest anticyclonic weather regime, and the annual average rainfall can drop to less than 1 millimetre (0.04 in). In fact, most of the Sahara receives less than 20 millimetres (0.8 in). Of the 9,000,000 square kilometres (3,500,000 sq mi) of desert land in the Sahara, an area of about 2,800,000 square kilometres (1,100,000 sq mi) (about 31% of the total area) receives an annual average rainfall amount of 10 millimetres (0.4 in) or less, while some 1,500,000 square kilometres (580,000 sq mi) (about 17% of the total area) receives an average of 5 millimetres (0.2 in) or less.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-25]

The annual average rainfall is virtually zero over a wide area of some 1,000,000 square kilometres (390,000 sq mi) in the eastern Sahara comprising deserts of: Libya, Egypt and Sudan ([Tazirbu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tazirbu), [Kufra](3337477.md), [Dakhla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakhla_Oasis), [Kharga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharga), [Farafra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farafra), [Siwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siwa_Oasis), [Asyut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asyut), [Sohag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohag), Luxor, Aswan, [Abu Simbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Simbel_temples), Wadi Halfa) where the long-term mean approximates 0.5 millimetres (0.02 in) per year.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-25] Rainfall is very unreliable and erratic in the Sahara as it may vary considerably year by year. In full contrast to the negligible annual rainfall amounts, the annual rates of potential evaporation are extraordinarily high, roughly ranging from 2,500 millimetres (100 in) per year to more than 6,000 millimetres (240 in) per year in the whole desert.[^cite_note-26] Nowhere else on Earth has air been found as dry and evaporative as in the Sahara region. However, at least two instances of snowfall have been recorded in Sahara, in February 1979 and December 2016, both in the town of [Ain Sefra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ain_Sefra).[^cite_note-27]

### Desertification and prehistoric climate

Main articles: [Sahara pump theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_pump_theory), [African humid period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_humid_period), [Prehistoric North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_North_Africa), and [North African climate cycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_climate_cycles)



Vegetation and water bodies in early [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) (top), between about 12,000 and 7,000 years ago, and [Eemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eemian) (bottom)

One theory for the formation of the Sahara is that the monsoon in Northern Africa was weakened because of glaciation during the [Quaternary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaternary) period, starting two or three million years ago. Another theory is that the monsoon was weakened when the ancient [Tethys Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tethys_Ocean) dried up during the [Tortonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortonian) period around 7 million years ago.[^cite_note-28]

The climate of the Sahara has undergone enormous variations between wet and dry over the last few hundred thousand years,[^cite_note-Lakes-29] believed to be caused by long-term changes in the [North African climate cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_climate_cycle) that alters the path of the [North African Monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_Monsoon) – usually southward. The cycle is caused by a 41,000-year cycle in which the tilt of the earth changes between 22° and 24.5°.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-25] At present, it is in a dry period, but it is expected that the Sahara will become green again in 15,000 years. When the North African monsoon is at its strongest, annual precipitation and subsequent vegetation in the Sahara region increase, resulting in conditions commonly referred to as the "[green Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Sahara)". For a relatively weak North African monsoon, the opposite is true, with decreased annual precipitation and less vegetation resulting in a phase of the Sahara climate cycle known as the "desert Sahara".[^cite_note-Foley1-30]

The idea that changes in [insolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insolation) (solar heating) caused by long-term changes in Earth's orbit are a controlling factor for the long-term variations in the strength of monsoon patterns across the globe was first suggested by Rudolf Spitaler in the late nineteenth century,[^cite_note-Ruddiman3-31] The hypothesis was later formally proposed and tested by the meteorologist John Kutzbach in 1981.[^cite_note-Kutzbach4-32] Kutzbach's ideas about the impacts of insolation on global monsoonal patterns have become widely accepted today as the underlying driver of long-term monsoonal cycles. Kutzbach never formally named his hypothesis and as such it is referred to here as the "[Orbital Monsoon Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_climate_cycles#Orbital_Monsoon_Hypothesis)" as suggested by Ruddiman in 2001.[^cite_note-Ruddiman3-31]



[Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel) region of Mali

During the last [glacial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_period), the Sahara was much larger than it is today, extending south beyond its current boundaries.[^cite_note-33] The end of the glacial period brought more rain to the Sahara, from about 8000 BCE to 6000 BCE, perhaps because of [low pressure areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_pressure_area) over the collapsing [ice sheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_sheet) to the north.[^cite_note-34] Once the ice sheets were gone, the northern Sahara dried out. In the southern Sahara, the drying trend was initially counteracted by the [monsoon](57630.md), which brought rain further north than it does today. By around 4200 BCE, however, the monsoon retreated south to approximately where it is today,[^cite_note-Science_Daily-35] leading to the gradual [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification) of the Sahara.[^cite_note-36] The Sahara is now as dry as it was about 13,000 years ago.[^cite_note-Lakes-29]

[Lake Chad](59314.md) is the remnant of a former inland sea, paleolake Mega-Chad, which existed during the African humid period. At its largest extent, sometime before 5000 BCE, Lake Mega-Chad was the largest of four Saharan paleolakes, and is estimated to have covered an area of 350,000 km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-37]

The [Sahara pump theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_pump_theory) describes this cycle. During periods of a wet or "[Green Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Sahara)", the Sahara becomes a [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna) grassland and various flora and fauna become more common. Following inter-pluvial arid periods, the Sahara area then reverts to desert conditions and the flora and fauna are forced to retreat northwards to the [Atlas Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_Mountains), southwards into [West Africa](67393.md), or eastwards into the [Nile Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Valley). This separates [populations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_\(biology\)) of some of the species in areas with different [climates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate), forcing them to [adapt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adaptation), possibly giving rise to [allopatric speciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allopatric_speciation).



The [Great Green Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Green_Wall_\(Africa\)), participating countries and Sahel. In August 2023, it was reported that the GGW had covered only 18% of the planned area.

[^cite_note-38]

It is also proposed that humans accelerated the drying-out period from 6000 to 2500 BCE by pastoralists overgrazing available grassland.[^cite_note-39]

#### Evidence for cycles

The growth of [speleothems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speleothems) (which requires rainwater) was detected in Hol-Zakh, [Ashalim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashalim), Even-Sid, Ma'ale-ha-Meyshar, Ktora Cracks, Nagev Tzavoa Cave, and elsewhere, and has allowed tracking of prehistoric rainfall. The Red Sea coastal route was extremely arid before 140 and after 115 kya (thousands of years ago). Slightly wetter conditions appear at 90–87 kya, but it still was just one tenth the rainfall around 125 kya. In the southern [Negev Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negev_Desert) speleothems did not grow between 185 and 140 kya ([MIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_isotope_stage) 6), 110–90 (MIS 5.4–5.2), nor after 85 kya nor during most of the interglacial period (MIS 5.1), the glacial period and [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene). This suggests that the southern Negev was arid-to-hyper-arid in these periods.[^cite_note-Vaks2007-40]

During the [Last Glacial Maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Maximum) (LGM) the Sahara was more extensive than it is now with the extent of the tropical forests being greatly reduced,[^cite_note-41] and the lower temperatures reduced the strength of the [Hadley Cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadley_Cell). This is a climate cell which causes rising tropical air of the [Inter-Tropical Convergence Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Tropical_Convergence_Zone) (ITCZ) to bring rain to the tropics, while dry descending air, at about [20 degrees north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_parallel_north), flows back to the equator and brings desert conditions to this region. It is associated with high rates of wind-blown mineral dust, and these dust levels are found as expected in marine cores from the north tropical Atlantic. But around 12,500 BCE the amount of dust in the cores in the [Bølling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%B8lling_Oscillation)/[Allerød](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aller%C3%B8d_Oscillation) phase suddenly plummets and shows a period of much wetter conditions in the Sahara, indicating a [Dansgaard-Oeschger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dansgaard-Oeschger_event) (DO) event (a sudden warming followed by a slower cooling of the climate). The moister Saharan conditions had begun about 12,500 BCE, with the extension of the ITCZ northward in the northern hemisphere summer, bringing moist wet conditions and a savanna climate to the Sahara, which (apart from a short dry spell associated with the [Younger Dryas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Younger_Dryas)) peaked during the [Holocene thermal maximum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene_thermal_maximum) climatic phase at 4000 BCE when mid-latitude temperatures seem to have been between 2 and 3 degrees warmer than in the recent past. Analysis of [Nile River](21244.md) deposited sediments in the delta also shows this period had a higher proportion of sediments coming from the [Blue Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Nile), suggesting higher rainfall also in the [Ethiopian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Highlands). This was caused principally by a stronger [monsoonal](57630.md) circulation throughout the sub-tropical regions, affecting India, Arabia and the Sahara. [Lake Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Victoria) only recently became the source of the [White Nile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Nile) and dried out almost completely around 15 kya.[^cite_note-42]

The sudden subsequent movement of the ITCZ southwards with a [Heinrich event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_event) (a sudden cooling followed by a slower warming), linked to changes with the [El Niño-Southern Oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Ni%C3%B1o-Southern_Oscillation) cycle, led to a rapid drying out of the Saharan and Arabian regions, which quickly became desert. This is linked to a marked decline in the scale of the Nile floods between 2700 and 2100 BCE.[^cite_note-43]

## Ecoregions



The major topographic features of the Saharan region

The Sahara comprises several distinct [ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion). With their variations in temperature, rainfall, elevation, and soil, these regions harbor distinct communities of plants and animals.

- The [Atlantic coastal desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_coastal_desert) is a narrow strip along the Atlantic coast where fog generated offshore by the cool [Canary Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Current) provides sufficient moisture to sustain a variety of [lichens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lichen), [succulents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Succulent_plant), and shrubs. It covers an area of 39,900 square kilometers (15,400 sq mi) in the south of Morocco and Mauritania.

  [^cite_note-44]

- The [North Saharan steppe and woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Saharan_steppe_and_woodlands) is along the northern desert, next to the [Mediterranean forests, woodlands, and scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_forests,_woodlands,_and_scrub) ecoregions of the northern Maghreb and Cyrenaica. Winter rains sustain shrublands and dry woodlands that form a transition between the Mediterranean climate regions to the north and the hyper-arid Sahara proper to the south. It covers 1,675,300 square kilometers (646,840 sq mi) in Algeria, Egypt, Libya, Mauritania, Morocco, and Tunisia.

  [^cite_note-45]

- The [Sahara desert ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_desert_\(ecoregion\)) covers the hyper-arid central portion of the Sahara where rainfall is minimal and sporadic. Vegetation is rare, and this ecoregion consists mostly of sand dunes (*erg, chech, raoui*), stone plateaus (*hamadas*), gravel plains (*reg*), dry valleys (*wadis*), and salt flats. It covers 4,639,900 square kilometres (1,791,500 sq mi) of: Algeria, Chad, Egypt, Libya, Mali, Mauritania, Niger, and Sudan.

  [^cite_note-PA1327-11]

- The [South Saharan steppe and woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Saharan_steppe_and_woodlands) ecoregion is a narrow band running east and west between the hyper-arid Sahara and the Sahel savannas to the south. Movements of the equatorial Intertropical Convergence Zone (ITCZ) bring summer rains during July and August which average 100 to 200 mm (4 to 8 in) but vary greatly from year to year. These rains sustain summer pastures of grasses and herbs, with dry woodlands and shrublands along seasonal watercourses. This ecoregion covers 1,101,700 square kilometres (425,400 sq mi) in Algeria, Chad, Mali, Mauritania, and Sudan.

  [^cite_note-46]

- In the [West Saharan montane xeric woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Saharan_montane_xeric_woodlands), several volcanic highlands provide a cooler, moister environment that supports Saharo-Mediterranean woodlands and shrublands. The ecoregion covers 258,100 square kilometres (99,650 sq mi), mostly in the [Tassili n'Ajjer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tassili_n'Ajjer) of Algeria, with smaller enclaves in the [Aïr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%AFr_Mountains) of Niger, the [Adrar Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrar_Plateau) of Mauritania, and the [Adrar des Iforas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrar_des_Ifoghas) of Mali and Algeria.

  [^cite_note-47]

- The [Tibesti-Jebel Uweinat montane xeric woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibesti-Jebel_Uweinat_montane_xeric_woodlands) ecoregion consists of the Tibesti and [Jebel Uweinat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jebel_Uweinat) highlands. Higher and more regular rainfall and cooler temperatures support woodlands and shrublands of date palm, [acacias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia), [myrtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrtus_nivellei), [oleander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleander), [tamarix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamarix), and several rare and endemic plants. The ecoregion covers 82,200 square kilometres (31,700 sq mi) in the Tibesti of Chad and Libya, and Jebel Uweinat on the border of Egypt, Libya, and Sudan.

  [^cite_note-48]

- The Saharan halophytics is an area of seasonally flooded saline depressions which is home to [halophytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halophytic) (salt-adapted) plant communities. The Saharan halophytics cover 54,000 square kilometres (21,000 sq mi) including: the [Qattara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qattara_Depression) and [Siwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siwa_Depression) depressions in northern Egypt, the [Tunisian salt lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisian_salt_lakes) of central Tunisia, [Chott Melghir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chott_Melghir) in Algeria, and smaller areas of Algeria, Mauritania, and the southern part of Morocco.

  [^cite_note-49]

- The [Tanezrouft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanezrouft) is one of the Sahara's most arid regions, with no vegetation and very little life. A barren, flat gravel plain, it extends south of [Reggane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggane) in Algeria towards the [Adrar des Ifoghas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrar_des_Ifoghas) highlands in northern Mali.

## Flora and fauna

The flora of the Sahara is highly diversified based on the bio-geographical characteristics of this vast desert. [Floristically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floristics), the Sahara has three zones based on the amount of rainfall received – the Northern (Mediterranean), Central and Southern Zones. There are two transitional zones – the Mediterranean-Sahara transition and the Sahel transition zone.[^cite_note-Mares1999-50]

The Saharan flora comprises around 2800 species of [vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_plant). Approximately a quarter of these are [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism). About half of these species are common to the flora of the Arabian deserts.[^cite_note-Houérou2008-51]



Camels in the [Guelta d'Archei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelta_d'Archei), in north-eastern [Chad](5488.md)

The central Sahara is estimated to include five hundred species of plants, which is extremely low considering the huge extent of the area. Plants such as acacia trees, palms, succulents, spiny shrubs, and grasses have adapted to the arid conditions, by growing lower to avoid water loss by strong winds, by storing water in their thick stems to use it in dry periods, by having long roots that travel horizontally to reach the maximum area of water and to find any surface moisture, and by having small thick leaves or needles to prevent water loss by [evapotranspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evapotranspiration). Plant leaves may dry out totally and then recover.

Several species of fox live in the Sahara including: the [fennec fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fennec_fox), [pale fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_fox) and [Rüppell's fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%BCppell's_fox). The [addax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addax), a large white [antelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antelope), can go nearly a year in the desert without drinking. The [dorcas gazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorcas_gazelle) is a north African gazelle that can also go for a long time without water. Other notable gazelles include the [rhim gazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhim_gazelle) and [dama gazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dama_gazelle).

The Saharan [cheetah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheetah) ([northwest African cheetah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_African_cheetah)) lives in Algeria, [Togo](30138.md), Niger, Mali, [Benin](3459.md), and [Burkina Faso](3470.md). There remain fewer than 250 mature cheetahs, which are very cautious, fleeing any human presence. The cheetah avoids the sun from April to October, seeking the shelter of shrubs such as [balanites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balanites) and acacias. They are unusually pale.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] The other cheetah subspecies ([northeast African cheetah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_African_cheetah)) lives in Chad, Sudan and the eastern region of Niger. However, it is currently extinct in the wild in Egypt and Libya. There are approximately 2000 mature individuals left in the wild.



An [Idehan Ubari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idehan_Ubari) oasis lake, with [native](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_plant) grasses and [date palms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenix_dactylifera)

Other animals include the [monitor lizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitor_lizard), [hyrax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyrax), [sand vipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerastes_\(genus\)), and small populations of [African wild dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_wild_dog),[^cite_note-iucn-54] in perhaps only 14 countries[^cite_note-Borrell-55] and [red-necked ostrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-necked_ostrich). Other animals exist in the Sahara (birds in particular) such as [African silverbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_silverbill) and [black-faced firefinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-faced_firefinch), among others. There are also small [desert crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_crocodile) in Mauritania and the [Ennedi Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ennedi_Plateau) of Chad.[^cite_note-56]

The [deathstalker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deathstalker) scorpion can be 10 cm (3.9 in) long. Its venom contains large amounts of [agitoxin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agitoxin) and [scyllatoxin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scyllatoxin), though its sting rarely kills a healthy adult. The [Saharan silver ant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharan_silver_ant) is unique in that due to the extreme high temperatures of their habitat, and the threat of predators, the ants are active outside their nest for only about ten minutes per day.[^cite_note-WehnerEtAl1992-57]

[Dromedary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dromedary) camels and [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat) are the domesticated animals most commonly found in the Sahara. Because of its qualities of endurance and speed, the dromedary is the favourite animal used by [nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad).

Human activities are more likely to affect the habitat in areas of permanent water (oases) or where water comes close to the surface. Here, the local pressure on natural resources can be intense. The remaining populations of large mammals have been greatly reduced by hunting for food and recreation. In recent years development projects have started in the deserts of Algeria and Tunisia using irrigated water pumped from underground aquifers. These schemes often lead to [soil degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_degradation) and [salinization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_salinity).

Researchers from [Hacettepe University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hacettepe_University) have reported that Saharan soil may have bio-available [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) and also some essential macro and micro nutrient elements suitable for use as fertilizer for growing wheat.[^cite_note-YTSB2011-58]

## History

See also: [Genetic history of North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_history_of_North_Africa)



[Saharan rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharan_rock_art) in the [Fezzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fezzan)

Scientists have identified more than 230 [humid periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_humid_period) in North Africa over the past eight million years, recurring at intervals of approximately 21,000 years.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

A modern laboratory examination of the [Uan Muhuggiag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uan_Muhuggiag) [child mummy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uan_Muhuggiag#Tashwinat_Mummy) and Tin Hanakaten child have suggested that the Central Saharan peoples from the [Epipaleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epipaleolithic), [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic), and [Pastoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoral_Period) periods possessed dark skin complexions.[^cite_note-62] The creators of the [Round Head rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Head_rock_art) possessed [dark skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_skin).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

A 2025 study sequenced individuals from [Takarkori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takarkori) (7,000 YBP) and discovered that most of their ancestry was from an unknown ancestral North African lineage, related to the African admixture component found in [Iberomaurusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberomaurusian).[^cite_note-65] According to the study, the Takarkori people were distinct from both contemporary sub-Saharan Africans and non-Africans/Eurasians. They had "only a minor component of non-African ancestry" but did "not carry sub-Saharan African ancestry, suggesting that, contrary to previous interpretations, the [Green Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Sahara) was not a corridor connecting Northern and sub-Saharan Africa."[^cite_note-66] As per [Johannes Krause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Krause) of the Max Planck Institute, one of the authors of the study "The Takarkori lineage likely represents a remnant of the genetic diversity present in northern Africa between 50,000 and 20,000 years ago."[^cite_note-67]

Later in the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic), from around 7,500 years ago, populations associated with [European Neolithic Farmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_European_Farmers) migrated into Northwest Africa from the Iberian Peninsula, while pastoralists from the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) also reached the region. Both groups contributed significantly to the ancestry of modern Northwest Africans.[^cite_note-68] The [proto-Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Berber_language) tribes evolved from these prehistoric communities during the late [Bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age)- and early [Iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) ages.[^cite_note-69] Ancient DNA provides evidence for genetic continuity in North Africa alongside contacts with populations from Western Asia. A whole genome from an individual buried at Nuwayrat in [Middle Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Egypt) around 2855–2570 BCE was modelled as predominantly deriving from North African Neolithic ancestry, with about 20% of his ancestry related to populations of the eastern [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent), including Mesopotamia.[^cite_note-70]



Haplogroup E1b1b is widely distributed across North Africa and parts of sub-Saharan Africa. Its major branches have distinct geographic distributions, and genetic studies have generally placed the origin of E-M35 within eastern Africa.

[^cite_note-71]

David Schoenbrun, Christopher Ehret, Steven A. Brandt and Shomarka Keita (2025) have highlighted the problematic categorisation of genetic haplogroups characterised as ‘[African](5334607.md)’ and ‘[Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian)' in North African genome studies. In reference to the van de Loosdrecht et al. 2018 study on the epipalaeolithic Taforalt remains from [Morocco](19291.md), which identified the [EM35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E-M35) (primarily [EM78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E-V68)) common in north-eastern Africa but characterised the [mtDNA (female lineage haplogroups)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitochondrial_DNA) of [U6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_U) and [M1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_M_\(mtDNA\)) as 'Eurasian', the authors questioned the classification of these maternal haplogroups despite their localised and long-established presence in ancient African populations.[^cite_note-72] In their view, identifying a range of African populations may still remain an issue "since the idea of ‘African’ still gets stereotyped or restricted. (Accepting the [southwestern Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Asian)/[Levantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) geographical continuity with Africa eliminates a conceptual barrier related to racio-typological thinking permitting an [Africasian construct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Asia) analogous to Eurasian.)"[^cite_note-73]

In the Central Sahara, engraved and painted rock art were created perhaps as early as 10,000 years ago, spanning the [Bubaline Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubaline_Period), [Kel Essuf Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kel_Essuf_Period), [Round Head Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_Head_Period), [Pastoral Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoral_Period), [Caballine Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caballine_Period?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Cameline Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameline_Period?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Soukopova-74] The Sahara was then a much wetter place than it is today. Over 30,000 [petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph) of river [animals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal) such as [crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodile)[^cite_note-Crocodiles-75] survive, with half found in the Tassili n'Ajjer in southeast Algeria. [Fossils](10958.md) of [dinosaurs](8311.md),[^cite_note-2018_Dinosaur-76] including *[Afrovenator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrovenator)*, *[Jobaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jobaria)* and *[Ouranosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouranosaurus)*, have also been found here. The modern Sahara, though, is not lush in vegetation, except in the [Nile](21244.md) Valley, at a few [oases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis), and in the northern highlands, where Mediterranean plants such as the [olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive) tree are found to grow. Shifts in [Earth's axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axial_tilt) increased temperatures and decreased precipitation, which caused an abrupt beginning of North Africa desertification about 5,400 years ago.[^cite_note-Science_Daily-35]

[Archaeological](18951655.md), [genetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics) and [anthropological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthropology) evidence indicates long-standing interactions between populations of the Sahara, North Africa and regions to the south of the Sahara.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] Y-chromosome [haplogroup E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E-M96) is widely distributed across Africa, including North Africa and the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel).[^cite_note-83] The [M35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E-M35) branch of haplogroup [E1b1b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_E-P2) is widely distributed in eastern and northeastern Africa and is also present in North Africa.[^cite_note-84] The [R haplogroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haplogroup_R_\(Y-DNA\)) has also been identified to have high frequencies in central Saharan Africa, among some [Afro-Asiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Asiatic) and [Nilo-Saharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan) language speaking groups.[^cite_note-85] In the view of [biological anthropologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_anthropology) Shomarka Keita, associate of National Human Genome Center, the E haplogroup connects groups across both sides of the Saharan territories which undermines traditional 'racial' taxonomy associated with a 'white' northern Africa and 'black' southern Africa.[^cite_note-86] He further added that modern scholarship should abandon terms such as "North Africa" and "Sub-Saharan Africa" which "ignore diversity within the regions and come from a form of stereotyping racialist thought, and categorical thinking that stems from a section of older discourse."[^cite_note-87]

### Kiffians

The [Kiffian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiffian_culture) is a prehistoric industry, or domain, that existed between 10,000 and 8,000 years ago in the Sahara, during the [Neolithic Subpluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Subpluvial). Human remains from this culture were found in 2000 at a site known as [Gobero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gobero), located in [Niger](21373.md) in the [Ténéré Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A9n%C3%A9r%C3%A9).[^cite_note-scidaily-88] The site is known as the largest and earliest grave of Stone Age people in the Sahara.[^cite_note-nytimes-89] The Kiffians were skilled [hunters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer). Bones of many large savannah animals that were discovered in the same area suggest that they lived on the shores of a lake that was present during the Holocene Wet Phase, a period when the Sahara was verdant and wet.[^cite_note-nytimes-89] The Kiffian people were tall, standing over six feet in height.[^cite_note-scidaily-88] Craniometric analysis indicates that this early [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) population was closely related to the Late Pleistocene [Iberomaurusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberomaurusian) and early Holocene [Capsians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsian_culture) of the Maghreb, as well as mid-Holocene [Mechta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechta-Afalou) groups.[^cite_note-90] Traces of the Kiffian culture do not exist after 8,000 years ago, as the Sahara went through a dry period for the next thousand years.[^cite_note-newscientist-91] After this time, the [Tenerian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerian_culture) colonized the area.

### Tenerians

Gobero was discovered in 2000 during an archaeological expedition led by [Paul Sereno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Sereno), which sought [dinosaur](8311.md) remains. Two distinct prehistoric cultures were discovered at the site: the early [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) Kiffian culture, and the middle Holocene [Tenerian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenerian_culture). The post-Kiffian desiccation lasted until around 4600 BCE, when the earliest artefacts associated with the Tenerians have been dated to. Some 200 skeletons have been discovered at Gobero. The Tenerians were considerably shorter in height and less robust than the earlier Kiffians. Craniometric analysis also indicates that they were osteologically distinct. The Kiffian skulls are akin to those of the [Late Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Pleistocene) [Iberomaurusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberomaurusian), early Holocene [Capsians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsian_culture), and mid-Holocene [Mechta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechta-Afalou) groups, whereas the Tenerian crania are more like those of Mediterranean groups.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] Graves show that the Tenerians observed spiritual traditions, as they were buried with artifacts such as jewelry made of hippo [tusks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusk) and clay pots. The most interesting find is a triple burial, dated to 5300 years ago, of an adult female and two children, estimated through their teeth as being five and eight years old, hugging each other. Pollen residue indicates they were buried on a bed of flowers. The three are assumed to have died within 24 hours of each other, but as their skeletons hold no apparent trauma (they did not die violently) and they have been buried so elaborately – unlikely if they had died of a plague – the cause of their deaths is a mystery.

### Tashwinat Mummy

[Uan Muhuggiag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uan_Muhuggiag) appears to have been inhabited from at least the 6th millennium BCE to about 2700 BCE, although not necessarily continuously.[^cite_note-Mori-1998-94] The most noteworthy find at Uan Muhuggiag is the well-preserved [mummy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mummy) of a young boy of approximately 2+1⁄2 years old. The child was in a fetal position, then embalmed, then placed in a sack made of antelope skin, which was insulated by a layer of leaves.[^cite_note-Hooke-2003-95] The boy's organs were removed, as evidenced by incisions in his stomach and thorax, and an organic preservative was inserted to stop his body from decomposing.[^cite_note-Cockburn-1980-96] An ostrich eggshell necklace was also found around his neck.[^cite_note-Mori-1998-94] [Radiocarbon dating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiocarbon_dating) determined the age of the mummy to be approximately 5600 years old, which makes it about 1000 years older than the earliest previously recorded mummy in ancient [Egypt](8087628.md).[^cite_note-Temehu-97] In 1958–59, an archaeological expedition led by [Antonio Ascenzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Ascenzi?action=edit\&redlink=1) conducted anthropological, radiological, histological and chemical analyses on the Uan Muhuggiag mummy. The team claimed that the mummy was a 30-month-old child of uncertain sex. They also found a long incision on the specimen's abdominal wall, which indicated that the body had been initially mummified by evisceration and later underwent natural desiccation. The team also stated that the mummy possessed "[Negroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negroid) features."[^cite_note-Giuffra2010-98] However, modern genetics has since proven that the final claim is unscientific and not supported by evidence.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] A more recent publication referenced a laboratory examination of the [cutaneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin) features of the [child mummy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uan_Muhuggiag#Tashwinat_Mummy) in which the results verified that the child possessed a dark skin complexion.[^cite_note-101] One other individual, an adult, was found at Uan Muhuggiag, buried in a crouched position.[^cite_note-Mori-1998-94] However, the body showed no evidence of evisceration or any other method of preservation. The body was estimated to date from about 7500 BP.[^cite_note-Soukopova-2012-102]

### Nubians

Further information: [Sahara pump theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_pump_theory) and [Neolithic Subpluvial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_Subpluvial)



[Beni Isguen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M'zab), a holy city surrounded by thick walls in the Algerian Sahara

During the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) Era, before the onset of [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification) around 9500 BCE, the central Sudan had been a rich environment supporting a large population ranging across what is now barren desert, like the Wadi el-Qa'ab. By the 5th millennium BCE, the people who inhabited what is now called [Nubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubia) were full participants in the "agricultural revolution", living a settled lifestyle with domesticated plants and animals. Saharan rock art of cattle and herdsmen suggests the presence of a [cattle cult](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_cult) like those found in Sudan and other pastoral societies in Africa today.[^cite_note-103]

[Megaliths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalith) found at [Nabta Playa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabta_Playa) are overt examples of probably the world's first known [archaeoastronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeoastronomy) devices, predating [Stonehenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehenge) by some 2,000 years.[^cite_note-104] This complexity, as observed at Nabta Playa, and as expressed by different levels of authority within the society there, likely formed the basis for the structure of both the Neolithic society at Nabta and the Old Kingdom of Egypt.[^cite_note-105]

Archaeological evidence has attested that population settlements occurred in Nubia as early as the Late [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) era and from the 5th millennium BCE onwards, whereas there is "no or scanty evidence" of human presence in the Egyptian Nile Valley during these periods, which may be due to problems in site preservation.[^cite_note-106]

### Egyptians

Further information: [Northeast Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Africa), [Predynastic Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predynastic_Egypt), and [North Africa](21714.md)



Neolithic rock art, over 7,000 years old, [Cave of Beasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_of_Beasts), Egypt

By 6000 BCE [predynastic Egyptians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Predynastic_Egypt) in the southwestern corner of Egypt were herding cattle and constructing large buildings. Subsistence in organized and permanent settlements in predynastic Egypt by the middle of the 6th millennium BCE centered predominantly on cereal and animal agriculture: cattle, goats, pigs and sheep. Metal objects replaced prior ones of stone. Tanning of animal skins, pottery and weaving were commonplace in this era also. There are indications of seasonal or only temporary occupation of the [Al Fayyum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Fayyum) in the 6th millennium BCE, with food activities centering on fishing, hunting and food-gathering. Stone [arrowheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrowhead), knives and scrapers from the era are commonly found.[^cite_note-Fayum-107] Burial items included pottery, jewelry, farming and hunting equipment, and assorted foods including dried meat and fruit. Burial in desert environments appears to enhance Egyptian preservation rites, and the dead were buried facing due west.[^cite_note-Predynastic-108]

Several scholars have argued that the African origins of the Egyptian civilisation derived from pastoral communities which emerged in both the Egyptian and Sudanese regions of the Nile Valley in the fifth millennium BCE.[^cite_note-109] According to [UNESCO](21786641.md) scholar, Alain Anselin, recent data "over the last thirty years" have confirmed the migration of peoples from the Sahara and [south of Egypt](27067.md) into the Nile Valley during the early, formative period.[^cite_note-General_history_of_Africa,_IX:_Gene-110]

Archaeologist, [Fekri Hassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fekri_Hassan), (2002) indicated that the [megalithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalithic) monuments in the Saharan region of [Niger](21373.md) and the Eastern Sahara which developed, as early as 4700 BCE, may have served as antecedents for the [mastabas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastabas) and pyramids of [ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt).[^cite_note-111]

By 3400 BCE, the Sahara was as dry as it is today, due to reduced precipitation and higher temperatures resulting from a shift in Earth's orbit.[^cite_note-Science_Daily-35] As a result of this aridification, it became a largely impenetrable barrier to humans, with the remaining settlements mainly being concentrated around the numerous oases that dot the landscape. Little trade or commerce is known to have passed through the interior in subsequent periods, the only major exception being the Nile Valley. The Nile, however, was impassable at several [cataracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cataracts_of_the_Nile), making trade and contact by boat difficult.

### Haratins

Main article: [Haratins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haratins)



Haratin women of Telouet, Southern [Morocco](19291.md), in a [houach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahwash) performance

The Haratin speak [Maghrebi Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghrebi_Arabic) dialects and [Berber languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages).[^cite_note-112] They are believed to largely descend from native ancient black populations that inhabited the Sahara.[^cite_note-\:1-113][^cite_note-\:2-114][^cite_note-115]

Historically, it was commonly believed that the Haratins were entirely descended from [Sub-Saharan](27067.md) slaves, but they also are descendants from groups native to southern Morocco[^cite_note-116] and the northern Sahara.[^cite_note-\:1-113][^cite_note-\:2-114] French academic [André Adam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9_Adam_\(academic\)) attributed their origin mostly to inhabitants of the Sahara.[^cite_note-117]

### Tichitt culture

Main article: [Tichitt culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tichitt_culture)

In 4000 BCE, the start of sophisticated social structure (e.g., trade of cattle as valued assets) developed among herders amid the [Pastoral Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoral_Period) of the Sahara.[^cite_note-Brass3-118] Saharan [pastoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoral) culture (e.g., fields of tumuli, lustrous stone rings, axes) was intricate.[^cite_note-Brass_II-119] By 1800 BCE, Saharan pastoral culture expanded throughout the Saharan and Sahelian regions.[^cite_note-Brass3-118] The initial stages of sophisticated social structure among Saharan herders served as the segue for the development of sophisticated [hierarchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchies) found in African settlements, such as [Dhar Tichitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhar_Tichitt).[^cite_note-Brass3-118] After migrating from the Central Sahara, proto-[Mande peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mande_peoples) established their civilization in the Tichitt region[^cite_note-Abd-El-Moniem-120] of the Western Sahara.[^cite_note-Kea-121] The Tichitt Tradition of eastern Mauritania dates from 2200 BCE[^cite_note-McDougall-122][^cite_note-Holl-123] to 200 BCE.[^cite_note-MacDonald_IV-124][^cite_note-Kay-125] Tichitt culture, at Dhar Néma, Dhar Tagant, Dhar Tichitt, and Dhar Walata, included a four-tiered hierarchal social structure, [farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farming) of [cereals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereals), [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy), numerous [funerary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funerary) tombs, and a [rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art) tradition.[^cite_note-Sterry-126] At Dhar Tichitt and Dhar Walata, [pearl millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_millet) may have also been independently tamed amid the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pastoral_Neolithic).[^cite_note-Champion-127] Dhar Tichitt, which includes Dakhlet el Atrouss, may have served as the primary regional center for the multi-tiered hierarchical social structure of the Tichitt Tradition,[^cite_note-Linares-Matás-128] and the [Malian Lakes Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malian_Lakes_Region), which includes [Tondidarou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tondidarou), may have served as a second regional center of the Tichitt Tradition.[^cite_note-Vernet-129] The [urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area)[^cite_note-Kea-121] Tichitt Tradition may have been the earliest large-scale, [complexly organized society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complex_society) in [West Africa](67393.md),[^cite_note-MacDonald_II-130] and an early [civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization) of the Sahara,[^cite_note-Abd-El-Moniem-120][^cite_note-McDougall-122] which may have served as the segue for [state formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_formation) in West Africa.[^cite_note-Brass_II-119]

As areas where the Tichitt cultural tradition were present, Dhar Tichitt and Dhar Walata were occupied more frequently than Dhar Néma.[^cite_note-MacDonald_II-130] Farming of crops (e.g., [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet)) may have been a feature of the Tichitt cultural tradition as early as 3rd millennium BCE in Dhar Tichitt.[^cite_note-MacDonald_II-130]

As part of a broader trend of iron metallurgy developed in the West African Sahel amid 1st millennium BCE, iron items (350 BCE – 100 CE) were found at Dhar Tagant, iron metalworking and/or items (800 BCE – 400 BCE) were found at [Dia Shoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dia,_Mali) and Walaldé, and the iron remnants (760 BCE – 400 BCE) found at Bou Khzama and Djiganyai.[^cite_note-MacDonald_II-130] The iron materials that were found are evidence of iron metalworking at Dhar Tagant.[^cite_note-Kay-125] In the late period of the Tichitt Tradition at Dhar Néma, tamed pearl millet was used to temper the tuyeres of an oval-shaped low shaft furnace; this furnace was one out of 16 iron furnaces located on elevated ground.[^cite_note-MacDonald_IV-124] [Iron metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_metallurgy_in_Africa) may have developed before the second half of 1st millennium BCE, as indicated by pottery dated between 800 BCE and 200 BCE.[^cite_note-MacDonald_IV-124] At Dhar Walata and Dhar Tichitt, [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) was also used.[^cite_note-Kea-121]

After its decline in Mauritania, the Tichitt Tradition spread to the [Middle Niger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Niger) region (e.g., [Méma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9ma), [Macina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macina,_Mali), [Dia Shoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dia,_Mali), [Jenne Jeno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenne_Jeno)) of Mali where it developed into and persisted as Faïta Facies ceramics between 1300 BCE and 400 BCE among [rammed earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rammed_earth) architecture and iron metallurgy (which had developed after 900 BCE).[^cite_note-MacDonald2-131] Thereafter, the [Ghana Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Empire) developed in the 1st millennium CE.[^cite_note-MacDonald2-131]

### Phoenicians

Further information: [History of Western Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Western_Sahara)



[Azalai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azalai) salt caravan. The French reported that the 1906 caravan numbered 20,000 camels.

The people of [Phoenicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia), who flourished from 1200 to 800 BCE, created a chain of settlements along the coast of North Africa and traded extensively with its inhabitants. This put them in contact with the people of [ancient Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Libya), who were the ancestors of people who speak [Berber languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages) in North Africa and the Sahara today.

The Libyco-Berber alphabet of the ancient Libyans of north Africa seems to have been based on Phoenician, and its descendant [Tifinagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tifinagh) is still used today by the (Berber) [Tuareg](103054.md) of the central Sahara.

The Periplus of the Phoenician navigator [Hanno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanno_the_Navigator), who lived sometime in the 5th century BCE, claims that he founded settlements along the Atlantic coast of Africa, possibly including the Western Sahara. The identification of the places discussed is controversial, and archeological confirmation is lacking.

### Greeks

By 500 BCE, [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) arrived in the desert. Greek traders spread along the eastern coast of the desert, establishing trading colonies along the [Red Sea](26191.md). The [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage) explored the Atlantic coast of the desert, but the turbulence of the waters and the lack of markets caused a lack of presence further south than modern Morocco. Centralized states thus surrounded the desert on the north and east; it remained outside the control of these states. Raids from the nomadic Berber people of the desert were of constant concern to those living on the edge of the desert.

### Garamantes



Market on the main square of [Ghardaïa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharda%C3%AFa) (1971)

An urban civilization, the [Garamantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garamantes), arose around 500 BCE in the Sahara, in a valley that is now called the [Wadi al-Ajal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadi_al-Ajal?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Fezzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fezzan), Libya.[^cite_note-Lakes-29] The Garamantes built a prosperous empire in the heart of the desert.[^cite_note-132] The Garamantes achieved this development by digging tunnels far into the mountains flanking the valley to tap [fossil water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_water) and bring it to their fields. The Garamantes grew populous and strong, conquering their neighbors, and capturing and enslaving many individuals who were forced to work by extending the tunnels. The ancient Greeks and the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome) knew of the Garamantes and regarded them as uncivilized nomads. However, they traded with them, and a [Roman bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_bath) has been found in the Garamantes' capital of Garama. [Archaeologists](18951655.md) have found eight major towns and many other important settlements in the Garamantes' territory. The Garamantes' civilization eventually collapsed after they had depleted available water in the [aquifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer) and could no longer sustain the effort to extend the tunnels further into the mountains.[^cite_note-Keys-133]

Between the first century BCE and the fourth century CE, several [Roman expeditions into the Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romans_in_Sub-Saharan_Africa) were conducted by groups of military and commercial units of [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire).

### Islamic and Arabic expansion

Main articles: [Trans-Saharan trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade) and [Islamization of Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization_of_Sudan)

The [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) ruled the northern shores of the Sahara from the 5th to the 7th centuries. After the Muslim conquest of Arabia, specifically the Arabian peninsula, the [Muslim conquest of North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_North_Africa) began in the mid-7th to early 8th centuries and Islamic influence expanded rapidly on the Sahara. By the end of 641 all of Egypt was in Muslim hands. Trade across the desert intensified, and a significant [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_slave_trade) crossed the desert. It has been estimated that from the 10th to 19th centuries some 6,000 to 7,000 slaves were transported north each year.[^cite_note-134]

The [Beni Ḥassān](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beni_%E1%B8%A4ass%C4%81n) and other nomadic Arab tribes dominated the [Sanhaja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanhaja) [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_people) tribes of the western Sahara after the [Char Bouba war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Char_Bouba_war) of the 17th century. As a result, Arabian culture and language came to dominate, and the Berber tribes underwent some [Arabization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabization).

### Ottoman Turkish era



The [Tuareg](103054.md) controlled the central Sahara and [its trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_trade), by [George Francis Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Francis_Lyon), 1821

In the 16th century the northern fringe of the Sahara, such as coastal regencies in present-day [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Algeria) and Tunisia, as well as some parts of present-day Libya, together with the semi-autonomous kingdom of Egypt, were occupied by the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). From 1517 Egypt was a valued part of the Ottoman Empire, ownership of which provided the Ottomans with control over the Nile Valley, the east Mediterranean and North Africa. The benefit of the Ottoman Empire was the freedom of movement for citizens and goods. Traders exploited the Ottoman land routes to handle the spices, gold and silk from the East, manufactured goods from Europe, and the slave and gold traffic from Africa. Arabic continued as the local language and Islamic culture was much reinforced. The Sahel and southern Sahara regions were home to several independent states or to roaming [Tuareg](103054.md) clans.

### European colonialism

[European colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonialism) in the Sahara began in the 19th century. [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_Monarchy) conquered the regency of Algiers from the Ottomans in 1830, and French rule spread south from [French Algeria](292341.md) and eastwards from [Senegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Senegal) into the upper [Niger](21437.md) to include present-day Algeria, Chad, Mali then [French Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Sudan) including [Timbuktu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timbuktu) (1893), Mauritania, Morocco (1912), Niger, and Tunisia (1881). By the beginning of the 20th century, the trans-Saharan trade had clearly declined because goods were moved through more modern and efficient means, such as airplanes, rather than across the desert.[^cite_note-135]

The French took advantage of long-standing animosity between the [Chaamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaamba) Arabs and the Tuareg. The newly raised *Méhariste* [camel corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9hariste) were originally recruited mainly from the Chaamba nomadic tribe. In 1902, the French penetrated the [Hoggar Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoggar_Mountains) and defeated [Ahaggar Tuareg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kel_Ahaggar) in the battle of [Tit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tit,_Tamanrasset).



The [French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire) (blue) was the dominant presence in the Sahara.

The French Colonial Empire was the dominant presence in the Sahara. It established regular air links from [Toulouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toulouse) (HQ of famed [Aéropostale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9ropostale_\(aviation\))), to [Oran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oran) and over the Hoggar to Timbuktu and West to [Bamako](56606.md) and [Dakar](44251.md), as well as trans-Sahara bus services run by La Compagnie Transsaharienne (est. 1927).[^cite_note-wauthier1-136] A remarkable film shot by famous aviator Captain [René Wauthier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Wauthier?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1933 documents the first crossing by a large truck convoy from Algiers to Tchad, across the Sahara.[^cite_note-Reconnaissance_saharienne-137]

Egypt, under [Muhammad Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ali_of_Egypt) and his successors, conquered Nubia in 1820–22, founded [Khartoum](17320.md) in 1823, and conquered [Darfur](605837.md) in 1874. Egypt, including Sudan, became a British protectorate in 1882. Egypt and [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) lost control of the Sudan from 1882 to 1898 as a result of the [Mahdist War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdist_War). After its capture by British troops in 1898, the Sudan became an Anglo-Egyptian [condominium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condominium_\(international_law\)).

[Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_\(Spain\)) captured present-day [Western Sahara](33220.md) after 1874, although Rio del Oro remained largely under [Sahrawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawi_people) influence. In 1912, [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) captured parts of what was to be named [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Libya) from the Ottomans. To promote the [Roman Catholic](606848.md) religion in the desert, [Pope Pius IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX) appointed a delegate Apostolic of the Sahara and the Sudan in 1868; later in the 19th century his jurisdiction was reorganized into the [Vicariate Apostolic of Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicariate_Apostolic_of_Sahara).

### Breakup of the empires and afterwards



British [SAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Air_Service) patrol in armed jeeps during the [North African campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_campaign) of World War II

Egypt became independent of Britain in 1936, although the [Anglo-Egyptian treaty of 1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Egyptian_treaty_of_1936) allowed Britain to keep troops in Egypt and to maintain the British-Egyptian condominium in the Sudan. British military forces were withdrawn in 1954.

Most of the Saharan states achieved independence after [World War II](32927.md): Libya in 1951; Morocco, Sudan, and Tunisia in 1956; Chad, Mali, Mauritania, and Niger in 1960; and Algeria in 1962. Spain withdrew from [Western Sahara](33220.md) in 1975, and it was partitioned between Mauritania and Morocco. Mauritania withdrew in 1979; Morocco continues to hold the territory (see [Western Sahara conflict](4255996.md)).[^cite_note-138]

[Tuareg people](103054.md) in Mali [rebelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_rebellions_\(disambiguation\)) several times during the 20th century before finally forcing the Malian armed forces to withdraw below the line demarcating [Azawad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azawad) from southern Mali during the [2012 rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_rebellion_\(2012\)).[^cite_note-139] Islamist rebels in the Sahara calling themselves [al-Qaeda in the Islamic Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qaeda_in_the_Islamic_Maghreb) have stepped up their violence in recent years.[^cite_note-140]

In the post–World War II era, several [mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) and communities have developed to use the desert's natural resources. These include large deposits of [oil](23195.md) and [natural gas](22131.md) in Algeria and Libya, and large deposits of [phosphates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphate) in Morocco and Western Sahara.[^cite_note-141] Libya's [Great Man-Made River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Man-Made_River) is the world's largest irrigation project.[^cite_note-142] The project uses a pipeline system that pumps [fossil water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_water) from the [Nubian Sandstone Aquifer System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_Sandstone_Aquifer_System) to cities in the populous Libyan northern Mediterranean coast including Tripoli and Benghazi.[^cite_note-143]

A number of [Trans-African highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-African_Highway_network) have been proposed across the Sahara, including the [Cairo–Dakar Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo%E2%80%93Dakar_Highway) along the Atlantic coast, the [Trans-Sahara Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Sahara_Highway) from [Algiers](1644.md) on the Mediterranean to [Kano](508782.md) in Nigeria, the [Tripoli – Cape Town Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoli_%E2%80%93_Cape_Town_Highway) from [Tripoli](31232.md) in Libya to [N'Djamena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Djamena) in Chad, and the [Cairo – Cape Town Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_%E2%80%93_Cape_Town_Highway) which follows the Nile. Each of these highways is partially complete, with significant gaps and unpaved sections.

## Peoples and languages

Further information: [Saharan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharan_languages), [Saharan rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharan_rock_art), and [Rock art § Sahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art#Sahara)



[Toubou people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toubou_people) from central Sahara speak [Saharan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharan_languages)

The people of the Sahara are of various origins. Among them the [Amazigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazigh) including the [Tuareg](103054.md), various [Arabized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabized) Amaziɣ groups such as the [Hassaniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassaniya)-speaking [Sahrawis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahrawis), whose populations include the [Znaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Znaga), a tribe whose name is a remnant of the pre-historic [Zenaga language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenaga_language). Other major groups of people include the: [Toubou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toubou), [Nubians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubians), [Zaghawa](1467480.md), [Kanuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanuri_people), [Hausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_people), [Songhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_people), [Beja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beja_people), and [Fula/Fulani](102383.md) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Peul*; [Fula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_language): *Fulɓe*). The archaeological evidence from the Holocene period has shown that [Nilo-Saharan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo-Saharan) speaking groups had populated the central and southern Sahara before the influx of [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages) and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) speakers, around 1500 years ago, who now largely populate the Sahara in the modern era.[^cite_note-144] The [Haratins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haratins), are believed to largely descend from native ancient black populations that inhabited the Sahara.[^cite_note-\:1-113][^cite_note-\:2-114]



Geographical distribution of [Afroasiatic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages) and [varieties of Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Arabic)

[Arabic dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_dialects) are the most widely spoken languages in the Sahara. Arabic, Berber and its variants now regrouped under the term [Amazigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazigh) (which includes the [Guanche language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guanche_language) spoken by the original Berber inhabitants of the Canary Islands) and Beja languages are part of the [Afro-Asiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Asiatic) or [Hamito-Semitic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamito-Semitic) family. Unlike neighboring [West Africa](67393.md) and the central governments of the states that comprise the Sahara, the French language bears little relevance to inter-personal discourse and commerce within the region, its people retaining staunch ethnic and political affiliations with [Tuareg](103054.md) and [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) leaders and culture.[^cite_note-145] The legacy of the French [colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_North_Africa) administration is primarily manifested in the territorial reorganization enacted by the [Third](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic) and [Fourth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Fourth_Republic) republics, which engendered artificial political divisions within a hitherto isolated and porous region.[^cite_note-146] Diplomacy with local clients was conducted primarily in Arabic, which was the traditional language of bureaucratic affairs. Mediation of disputes and inter-agency communication was served by interpreters contracted by the French government, who, according to Keenan, "documented a space of intercultural mediation," contributing much to preserving the indigenous cultural identities in the region.[^cite_note-147]
