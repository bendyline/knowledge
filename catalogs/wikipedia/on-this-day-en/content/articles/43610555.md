**Hamadan**[^cite_note-4] is a mountainous city in western Iran. It is located in the [Central District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_District_\(Hamadan_County\)) of [Hamadan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamadan_County) in [Hamadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamadan_province) province, serving as the capital of the province, county, and district.[^cite_note-Hamadan_Province_Structure-5] As of the 2016 Iranian census, it had a population of 554,406 people in 174,731 households.[^cite_note-2016_Hamadan_Province-2]

Hamadan is believed to be among the oldest Iranian cities. It was referred to in classical sources as [Ecbatana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecbatana) ([Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian) *Hamgmatāna*). It is possible that it was occupied by the [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria) in 1100 BCE; the Ancient Greek historian, [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus), states that it was the capital of the [Medes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes), around 700 BCE.

Hamadan is situated in a green mountainous area in the foothills of the 3,574-meter Alvand Mountain, in midwestern Iran. The city is 1,850 meters [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level). It is located approximately 360 kilometres (220 miles) southwest of [Tehran](57654.md).

The old city and its historic sites attract tourists during the summer. The major sights of this city are the [Ganj Nameh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganj_Nameh) inscription, the [Avicenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avicenna) monument and the [Baba Taher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baba_Taher) monument. The main language in the city is [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## History

Further information: [Ecbatana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecbatana) and [Timeline of Hamadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Hamadan)

### Pre-Islam



16th century map of Hamadan by [Matrakçı Nasuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matrak%C3%A7%C4%B1_Nasuh)

According to [Clifford Edmund Bosworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clifford_Edmund_Bosworth), "Hamadan is a very old city. It may conceivably, but improbably, be mentioned in cuneiform texts from ca. 1100 BC, the time of Assyrian king [Tiglath-Pileser I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiglath-Pileser_I), but is certainly mentioned by Herodotus who says that the king of Media Diokes built the city of [Agbatana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecbatana) or Ekbatana in the 7th century BC."[^cite_note-9]

Hamadan was established by the [Medes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes). It then became one of several capital cities of the [Achaemenid Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Dynasty).

The biblical [book of Ezra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Ezra) ([Ezra 6\:2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezra_6\:2)) mentions Hamadan as the place where a scroll was found giving the Jews permission from King Darius to rebuild the temple in Jerusalem. Its ancient name of Ecbatana is used in the Ezra text. Because it was a mile above sea level, it was a good place to preserve leather documents.

During the [Parthian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire) era, [Ctesiphon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ctesiphon) was the capital of the country, and Hamadan was the summer capital and residence of the Parthian rulers. After the Parthians, the [Sasanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) constructed their summer palaces in this city. In 642, the [Battle of Nahavand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nahavand) took place and Hamadan fell into the hands of the Muslim Arabs.

### Early Islamic Era

From the middle of the ninth century, the government of the city fell into the hands of Alavi Sadat. They ruled in Hamedan as Alawites. The valuable building of the Alavian dome is a relic of this period.

In 931 AD (319 AH), Hamedan was attacked by Mard Avij, the founder of the Al-Ziyar dynasty. Because the people of Hamedan had murdered his nephew, Abu al-Karadis, along with some of the army of Daylam, Mardavij turned the city into an arena of great plunder and massacre. Mardavij threw a stone lion through the gates of the city, and as a result, one of the two unique historical statues was completely shattered, and the other still remains.

### Buyid Rule

During the rule of the [Buyid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyid_dynasty), the city suffered much damage. However, the city regained its former glory under the rule of the [Buyid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyid) ruler [Fanna Khusraw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanna_Khusraw). The [Seljuks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuks) launched campaigns to take the city in the 1040s,[^cite_note-10] ultimately taking the final Kakuyid fortress in 1047.[^cite_note-11] The Seljuks later shifted their capital from [Baghdad](4492.md) to Hamadan. In 1220, Hamadan was destroyed by the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols)[^cite_note-12] during the [Mongol invasions of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasions_of_Georgia) before the [Battle of Khunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khunan). The city of Hamadan, its fortunes following the rise and fall of regional powers, was completely destroyed during the [Timurid invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_conquests_and_invasions), but later thrived during the [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid) era.

[Benjamin of Tudela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_of_Tudela) reported that Hamadan had 30,000 Jews in 1170.[^cite_note-13]

### Ottoman takeover and return to Iranian control



Silver drachma of Parthian king [Mithridates II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithridates_II_of_Parthia), made in [Ecbatana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecbatana) mint

Thereafter, in the 18th century, Hamadan was surrendered to the [Ottomans](22278.md), but due to the work of [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah), Hamadan was cleared of invaders and, as a result of a peace treaty between Iran and the Ottomans, it was returned to Iran; During the early 18th century chaos in Iran, various tribal groups, including [Kurdish tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_tribes), took advantage of the instability. In 1719, Kurdish tribes temporarily seized Hamadan and advanced nearly as far as [Isfahan](223149.md).[^cite_note-14] Hamadan stands on the [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road), and even in recent centuries the city enjoyed strong commerce and trade as a result of its location on the main road network in the western region of Iran. In the late 19th century, American missionaries, including [James W. Hawkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hawkes_\(missionary\)) and Belle Sherwood Hawke,[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-\:02-16] established schools in Hamadan.

### World War I



The [Ganjnameh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganjnameh), a [cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform) inscription in Hamadan

During [World War I](4764461.md), the city was the scene of heavy fighting between Russian and Turko-German forces. It was occupied by both armies, and finally by the British, before it was returned to the control of the Iranian government at the end of the war in 1918.

### Post World War

At the end of the [Qajar era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran), the city of Hamedan witnessed two major famines with a gap of 10 years. The first was the artificial famine of Hamedan during the reign of Ali Zahir al-Dawla due to the opposition of the Khans of Hamedan to the constitutionalist ideas of Zahir al-Dawla, the ruler of Hamadan, and the second famine was during World War I and in the following years. During this war, the city of Hamedan was alternately occupied by Russian, Ottoman, and British forces, and this city was the headquarters of their armies.

### Iran–Iraq War

During the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), the city of Hamedan was repeatedly targeted by Iraqi bombardments. One of the most intense bombing attacks was on Friday 16 July 1982, by Iraqi warplanes, which coincided with Quds Day. In this attack, dozens of residential houses in four districts of Hamedan city were destroyed, and 97 Hamedan citizens were killed and 595 others were injured.[^cite_note-17]

At the time of the 2006 National Census, the city's population was 473,149 in 127,812 households.[^cite_note-2006_Hamadan_Province-19] The following census in 2011 counted 525,794 people in 156,556 households.[^cite_note-2011_Hamadan_Province-20] The 2016 census measured the population of the city as 554,406 people in 174,731 households.[^cite_note-2016_Hamadan_Province-2]

## Climate

Hamadan has a [hot-summer, Mediterranean-influenced continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dsa*, [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dc*), in transition with a [cold semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BSk*). The city experiences hot, dry summers, and cold, snowy winters. The temperature may drop below −30 °C (−22 °F) on the coldest days. Heavy snowfall is common during winter and this can persist for periods of up to two months. During the short summer, the weather is hot, and mostly sunny.

## Sport

[PAS Hamedan F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAS_Hamedan_F.C.) were founded on 9 June 2007, after the dissolution of [PAS Tehran F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAS_Tehran_F.C.) The team, along with [Alvand Hamedan F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alvand_Hamedan_F.C.), is in the [Azadegan League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadegan_League).

Some sport complexes in this city include: [Qods Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qods_Stadium_\(Hamedan\)), [Shahid Mofatteh Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahid_Mofatteh_Stadium), Takhti Sport Complex and the National Stadium of Hamadan.

## Education



Hamadan University of Technology, in Hamadan

Before the [Persian Constitutional Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Constitutional_Revolution), education in Hamadan was limited to some [Maktab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maktab_\(disambiguation\)) Houses and theological schools. Fakhrie Mozafari School was the first modern school of Hamadan, which was built after that revolution. Alliance and Lazarist were also the first modern schools founded by foreign institutions in Hamadan.

## Notable people

Hamadan celebrities are divided into 3 categories: pre-Islamic, post-Islamic and contemporary people.

### Pre-Islamic

- [Mandane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandane_of_Media), the mother of [Cyrus the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_the_Great)

- [Astyages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astyages), the daughter of the last king of the [Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_kingdom)

- [Tomb of Esther and Mordechai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Esther_and_Mordechai), is a tomb located in Hamadan, Iran. Iranian Jews and Iranian Christians believe it houses the remains of the biblical Queen Esther and her cousin Mordechai, and it is the most important pilgrimage site for Jews and Christians in Iran.

## Incident

In February 1990, in the central branch of [Bank Keshavarzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_Keshavarzi_Iran) in Hamadan, a robbery resulted in the killing of the bank manager, [Abdulrahman Nafisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_Abdulrahman_Nafisi_and_his_family), his family, and a security guard. Nafisi prioritized the safety of the bank's funds over his own life and despite being under torture, pleaded with the robbers to take his personal belongings instead of the people's money.[^cite_note-31]
