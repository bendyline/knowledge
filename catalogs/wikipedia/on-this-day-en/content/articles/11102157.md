**Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS)** is a state corporation under the Ministry of Tourism and Wildlife established by an act of Parliament; Wildlife Conservation and Management Act CAP 376, of 1989, now repealed and replaced by the Wildlife Conservation and Management Act, 2013. At independence, the [Government of Kenya](20171676.md) committed itself to conserving wildlife for posterity with all the means at its disposal, including the places animals lived, forests and water catchment areas.

Kenya Wildlife Service conserves and manages national parks, wildlife conservation areas, and sanctuaries under its jurisdiction.

## History

In 1989 [Richard Leakey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Leakey) was appointed the head of the Wildlife Conservation and Management Department (WMCD) by [President Daniel Arap Moi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_arap_Moi) in response to the international outcry over the poaching of elephants and the impact it was having on the wildlife of Kenya.

Well-armed anti-poaching units were formed and were authorized to shoot poachers on sight.[^cite_note-2] The poaching menace was dramatically reduced. The transformation of the Kenya Wildlife Service got World Bank approved grants worth \$140 million. Richard Leakey, President Moi, and the WMCD made the international news headlines when a stockpile of 12 tons of ivory was burned in 1989 in [Nairobi National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairobi_National_Park).

These successes saw David Western appointed to serve as Director of the Kenya Wildlife Service (KWS) by retired President Moi in 1994. Others who served as KWS Directors are:

- Brig. (Rtd) John Waweru

- Kitili Mbathi

- William Kiprono

- Julius Kipng'etich

- Michael Wamithi

- Nehemiah Rotich

- Joseph Kioko

- Evans Mukolwe

On 20 July 2011 Kenya’s President [Mwai Kibaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwai_Kibaki) set on fire nearly 5 tonnes of elephant *ivory (*&#x33;35 tusks), with an estimated value of US\$16m.

On 30 April 2016, Kenyan President [Uhuru Kenyatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uhuru_Kenyatta) set alight the largest ever pile of ivory for destruction in the [Nairobi National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairobi_National_Park). The pile consisted of 105 tonnes of elephant ivory from about 8,000 elephants and 1.35 tonnes of horns from 343 [rhinoceroses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinoceros).[^cite_note-3] Estimates for the total [black market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_market) value of the destroyed contraband range from \$150 million to \$220 million. The [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory) was transported to the site in shipping containers then stacked into towers up to 10 ft (3.0 m) tall and 20 ft (6.1 m) in diameter. The ivory towers took personnel from the Kenya Wildlife Service ten days to build. The pyre also contained exotic animal skins. The amount of ivory destroyed equaled about 5% of the global stock. Gabonese President [Ali Bongo Ondimba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Bongo_Ondimba) was also in attendance.

## Conservation programmes

KWS runs specific programmes to assist Kenyan [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species) and their [habitats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_\(ecology\)) that are in particular danger. They have [forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest) and [Wetland conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland_conservation) programmes, as well as specific [elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant) and [rhino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinoceros) projects to help them recover from [poaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching). The [hirola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirola), which is in danger of [extinction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extinction), is also being monitored.

Within KWS there are several services, each responsible for a different area of work:

### Community Wildlife Service

This branch of the KWS works outside the national parks. They work instead in areas such as [wildlife corridors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_corridor), and teach the communities living there to encourage conservation and look after their resources.[^cite_note-5]

### Security Services

The job of this service is to eliminate poaching in the national parks and stop illegal trade. The Kenyan government has also erected fences which keeps the precious animals/wildlife inside the national park.

The Kenya wildlife service, through its rangers, offer extensive patrols and protection of the country’s wildlife in its wild spaces and as a result hundreds of rangers have lost their lives in the line of duty.

### Veterinary Services

This service ensures that healthy breeding populations of species are maintained throughout the country.

## Training

KWS has a training institute, also referred to as Kenya Wildlife Service Training Institute. The facility located in [Naivasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naivasha), is a middle-level college registered with the Ministry of Education, Science and Technology as a TVET institution. It offers specialized certificate and diploma courses in natural resource management, ecology and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) in an effort to enhance [conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservation_biology), management and sustainability of wildlife [bio-diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity) in Kenya and globally.

KWS also has a Law Enforcement Academy is situated in [Manyani Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manyani_Area?action=edit\&redlink=1), which caters for all law enforcers' paramilitary training.[^cite_note-6] Manyani area was established in 1990.[^cite_note-7]

## Education

KWS run several education centres:

- Nairobi Safari Walk

- Nairobi Education Centre

- Lake Nakuru Education Centre

- Tsavo East Education Centre

- Tsavo West Education Centre

These are located inside National Parks, and run programs to encourage people to care for their [environment.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_law) It is aimed at local people, particularly school groups, but is open to anyone.
