On 3 September 2026, the [Houthis](17240332.md) launched an offensive against the [Saudi](349303.md)-backed [Presidential Leadership Council](70488867.md) (PLC), the internationally recognized [government of Yemen](78626.md), amid the ongoing [Yemeni civil war](46215853.md).[^cite_note-sep3-56][^cite_note-Escalate-31] Clashes began in [Taiz Governorate](3604497.md), prompting a PLC counter-offensive against the Houthis in the [Al-Jawf](3604364.md) and [Al-Bayda governorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bayda_Governorate).[^cite_note-arn1-8][^cite_note-sep3-56]

The fighting coincides with the ongoing [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), which has marked the deadliest fighting in Yemen since the collapse of the 2022 ceasefire.[^cite_note-57] The Yemeni government lost areas around the [Bab al-Mandab Strait](62138.md) in Yemen's southwest after a swift [battle over Mokha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Mokha), resulting in the entire west coast of Yemen (except for the [Midi district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midi_district)) falling under control of the Houthi movement, who named the operation as **Operation God is Stronger in Might and More Severe in Punishment**.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

## Background

### 2022 ceasefire and the Red Sea crisis

Main article: [Red Sea crisis](75207766.md)

The Houthis and the internationally recognized government of Yemen reached a ceasefire agreement in 2022, which largely held until the 2026 campaign. Peace talks stalled in 2023 amidst the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-\:1-60] Starting on 19 October 2023, the Houthis began launching missiles and drones at international shipping crossing the [Bab al-Mandab Strait](62138.md) in solidarity with [Palestinians](23267.md) during the war in Gaza, triggering the [Red Sea crisis](75207766.md). In response, a [US](3434750.md)-led multinational coalition launched [Operation Prosperity Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Prosperity_Guardian) in late 2023 to secure shipping through the [Red Sea](26191.md). In early 2024, the United States and the [United Kingdom](31717.md) launched a series of [airstrikes targeting Houthi missile and drone launch sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US%E2%80%93UK_airstrikes_on_Yemen) across Yemen.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] In March 2025, the United States launched a [larger wave of strikes against the Houthis](79465918.md), after which the Houthis [agreed to stop attacking U.S. vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_States%E2%80%93Houthi_ceasefire) in the Red Sea.[^cite_note-63] Additionally, Israel previously launched [a series of airstrikes](79886126.md) on Houthi-controlled Yemen in response to missile attacks by the group, including [a July 2024 strike on Tel Aviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2024_Houthi%E2%80%93Israel_attacks) that killed one person.[^cite_note-\:32-64][^cite_note-65] Following the implementation of the [Gaza peace plan](81215079.md) in 2025, both parties briefly ceased attacks until March 2026, when the Houthis again launched missiles toward Israel in support of Iran and the [Axis of Resistance](36658251.md), all of which were intercepted.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

### 2026 Iran war and escalation

Main articles: [Houthis in the 2026 Iran war](82820319.md) and [Houthi blockade of Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_blockade_of_Saudi_Arabia)

On 28 February 2026, the United States and [Israel](9282173.md) launched a large-scale attack on Iran, [aimed at regime change](82756412.md). Iran and its allied militias, including [Hezbollah](13919.md) and the [Iraqi](7515928.md) [Popular Mobilization Forces](45671374.md), retaliated against U.S., Israeli, and other regional countries' military and energy infrastructure with drone and missile strikes. The Houthis initially refrained from joining the fighting, breaking with the rest of the [Axis of Resistance](36658251.md), only joining the war on 28 March.[^cite_note-\:2-68]

When the US navy reimposed [its naval blockade](82937256.md) against Iran on 14 July 2026, the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) of Iran threatened to cut "all other export corridors that benefit the US and its allies", with a senior Houthi official warning that the group was prepared to close the [Bab al-Mandab Strait](62138.md).[^cite_note-\:3-36] On 16 July 2026, Reuters reported that Iran had asked the Houthis to be ready to close the strait should the US strike Iranian power infrastructure. Soon after the Houthis began deploying drones and missiles near the Bab al-Mandeb Strait.[^cite_note-\:4-69]

On 13 July 2026, a [Mahan Air](375904.md) flight carrying IRGC commanders and weapons attempted to land at [Sanaa Airport](4202141.md), in the Houthi-controlled capital.[^cite_note-70] Yemeni government forces and [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) responded by [striking the airport's runway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Sanaa_airport_strike) to prevent the landing. In retaliation for the strike, the Houthis declared the ceasefire with the Yemeni government and Saudi Arabia over.[^cite_note-71] On 20 July, the Houthis announced a [naval blockade on Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houthi_blockade_of_Saudi_Arabia), amid the renewed U.S. naval blockade of Iran.[^cite_note-72] The Houthis subsequently began striking Saudi oil tankers in the Red Sea. On 24 July, Saudi Arabia retaliated against the earlier Houthi strikes on its oil tankers by bombing Houthi targets in the port city of [Hodeidah](769308.md). In response, the Houthis struck the [Jizan](1907222.md) oil refinery and the port city of [Yanbu](629638.md) in Saudi Arabia two days later.[^cite_note-\:5-73] Starting on 6 August, the Houthis also started striking Yemeni government forces across the country with missiles and drones, killing and injuring dozens of troops.[^cite_note-74]

## Timeline

### 3 September

The Houthis launched what was described as a "massive offensive" in the [Taiz Governorate](3604497.md), attempting to cut the road linking [Taiz](545270.md) to [Mokha](2082636.md) and thus sever Taiz city from the coast and escalate the [Siege of Taiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Taiz). At least 129 people were reportedly killed in the clashes. Despite initial Houthi gains, Yemeni government forces (PLC) were able to regain control of some of the lost territories.[^cite_note-sep3-56][^cite_note-75]

### 4 September

The PLC said it had recaptured the al-Khazan and al-Makahna hills in the [Jabal Habashi district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Habashi_district) west of Taiz city.[^cite_note-76]

### 6 September

The PLC claimed to have taken full control of the [Hays district](29273934.md) in [Al-Hudaydah Governorate](3604362.md), and advanced in western Taiz.[^cite_note-77]

### 7 September

The PLC claimed to have made advances on several fronts in the [Al-Jawf](3604364.md) and [Al-Bayda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bayda_Governorate) regions, taking control of the Al-Labanat military camp and moving toward the town of [Al Hazm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Hazm,_Yemen).[^cite_note-arn1-8]

The Houthis accused Saudi Arabia of launching airstrikes across Yemen,[^cite_note-saudicombatant-9] including on a prison in Al Hazm, killing 23 people.[^cite_note-78] They also claimed that a ballistic missile struck a military convoy en route to the Al-Wadiah military base in [Hadhramaut](325752.md).[^cite_note-regions-1]

The [Yemeni National Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_National_Resistance) claimed that several Somali [al-Shabaab](8726463.md) militants were killed fighting alongside the Houthis in western Yemen.[^cite_note-79]

### 8 September

The PLC said their offensive aimed to capture the Houthi-held capital, [Sanaa](18691665.md), and reported that government-aligned forces had advanced on the outskirts of [Dhi Na'im district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhi_Na'im_district) in Al-Bayda governorate and had captured the town of Yatma in Al-Jawf governorate.[^cite_note-regions-1]

The Houthis [struck](84173141.md) the cities of [Khamis Mushait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khamis_Mushait), [Abha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abha), [Jizan](1907222.md) and [Najran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najran) in Saudi Arabia with drones and missiles, wounding 73 people and damaging several oil sites.[^cite_note-80] Houthi shelling on an [internally displaced person](427408.md) camp in [Marib](3604484.md) killed two children and injured 15 others.[^cite_note-81] The Houthis also accused Saudi Arabia of conducting airstrikes on multiple sites in Yemen.[^cite_note-82]

The PLC said that it recaptured Al-Mukazama and Al-Mihal in Taiz and struck a Houthi gathering in [Jabal Habashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Habashi_district).[^cite_note-83]

During fighting in Taiz, the National Resistance said that it captured Ali al-Adhari, a senior leader in the Houthi rapid intervention unit in al-Barh.[^cite_note-84]

### 9 September

Houthi media reported 32 Saudi airstrikes in the regions of Marib, Al-Jawf, Taiz and Hodeidah.[^cite_note-85]

Yemeni army airstrikes also hit Houthi positions in [Maqbanah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maqbanah_district),[^cite_note-86] where an offensive was launched to capture Kadha. Also in Taiz, the PLC said that it retook Makazma, Mihal hill, Hamra, Sawda, and Shiek,[^cite_note-87] while a Houthi offensive targeting [al-Wazi'iyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Wazi'iyah_district) was repelled.[^cite_note-\:0-88]

The PLC launched a major offensive in southern Marib, capturing several areas, as well as villages in al-Kaddah in Taiz.[^cite_note-89] Government-run [Saba News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saba_News_Agency) reported that an attack by the Houthis in Marib killed three children.[^cite_note-sep3-56]

Clashes occurred in al-Bayda, al-Dhale, Hodeidah. Additionally, Houthi shelling killed two civilians in Hays. The [Southern Giants Brigades](59327857.md) said it struck a Houthi combat vehicle in al-Bayda, killing its occupants.[^cite_note-\:0-88]

An airstrike reportedly hit a gathering of Houthi leaders in al-Bahr that included Houthi defense minister [Mohamed al-Atifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_al-Atifi), although his fate was unclear.[^cite_note-90]

[Turki al-Maliki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turki_al-Maliki), spokesman for the Saudi-led coalition, said that the Houthis conducted drone and ballistic missile attacks on Khamis Mushait, Abha, and Jizan.[^cite_note-91] The Houthis seized the Khalid bin Walid camp after several clashes with the PLC in Taiz Governorate.[^cite_note-92]

A [Yemeni army (PLC)](70488867.md) convoy was ambushed while driving on a road in Hays, Hodeidah governorate when it was shot by suspected Houthi soldiers, killing one person and injuring a couple more.[^cite_note-93]

Remnants of the [Southern Transitional Council](54251566.md)—now identifying as the "[Southern Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Resistance)"—blocked local roads in [Dhale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhale) and ambushed a convoy transporting [Taher al-Aqili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taher_al-Aqili) with the use of light and heavy weaponry, alongside drones and the support of local rioters;[^cite_note-\:6-19][^cite_note-94] Alongside Tahir, a variety of important Yemeni political figures were also present at the convoy visiting the area, including the Governor of Al-Dhali’ Governorate *Ahmed al-Qubba*, the Commander of the [Eighth Military Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_regions_\(Yemen\)) *Hadi al-Awlaqi*, the Commander of the 33rd Armored Division Major General *Wajdi al-Zubaidi.*[^cite_note-\:6-19] On the same day the Southern Resistance and the PLC were reported jointly thwarthing a Houthi advance towards *Al-Masharih-Al-Thawkhab sector* in a confrontation which lasted one hour.[^cite_note-95]

### 10 September

Houthi forces captured the districts of Hays and [Al Khawkhah](29273806.md) in Hodeidah province, as well as the town of [Mokha](2082636.md) in western Taiz province, following advances.[^cite_note-96] The PLC said that its forces had retreated toward the [Dhubab district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhubab_district) near the [Bab al-Mandab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bab_al-Mandab) strait.[^cite_note-territory2-3][^cite_note-97]

A Houthi strike in Mafraq Harib in Marib killed three children and injured six others.[^cite_note-98] The Houthis said that they shot down a [Karayel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestel_Karayel) drone from Saudi Arabia over [Hajjah Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajjah_Governorate).[^cite_note-99]

Later in the day, reports emerged that the Houthis also captured the [Hanish Islands](3542308.md) in Hodeidah governorate, amid unverified claims of the capture of the strategically located Dhubab district and [Perim island](1907645.md) in the Bab al-Mandeb strait.[^cite_note-hanish-4][^cite_note-perim-5]

The Houthis said that Saudi Arabia had conducted 64 airstrikes in Taiz, Hodeidah, Marib, and Al-Jawf, adding that their air defenses repelled an aerial squadron over Taiz.[^cite_note-100]

Following their withdrawal from [Mokha district](2082636.md), the PLC declared a "kill box" around the area, intending to use "all weapons and firepower", and warning civilians to avoid the Taiz–Mokha road until further notice.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102]

Following the incident that occurred between the Southern Resistance and the PLC the day prior, Southern Resistance's Brigadier General Ali Shaif Al-Hariri, an official spokesperson of the group, issued a formal statement categorically rejecting and condemning the disruption of Minister Al-Aqili’s convoy. Al-Hariri labeled the ambush a "reprehensible and unacceptable act" that ran counter to the tribal customs and history of Dhale. The official leadership statement emphasized that internal political differences within the resistance must not escalate into violence or chaos, stating that his group should focus most of its firepower against the Houthis instead.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] On the same day, the General Union of Southern Arabian Tribes stated its support for the events that took place in Dhale and stated it was ready to support the Southern Resistance with men and equipment,[^cite_note-105] following up with an official call to arms the following day.[^cite_note-106]

### 11 September

The Houthis claimed control over Yemen's entire Red Sea coast, including the Perim Island and the al-Omari camp. The Yemeni government said that its forces had withdrawn from Perim, and that the Houthis had also captured the town of [Dhubab](33303157.md), on the Red Sea coast facing the island.[^cite_note-Reuters911-1-107][^cite_note-AJ911-108] Reports of the Houthis taking Perim islands were also confirmed by Saudi and PLC sources.[^cite_note-Reuters911-1-107]

*[Axios](52436164.md)* reported that Saudi Crown Prince [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md) called President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) twice on 10 September, urging him to launch strikes against the Houthis, but that Trump rejected the request. Two U.S. officials said the US did not intend to directly intervene at the time.[^cite_note-Reuters911-2-109] A PLC official also said the US withheld support from a full-scale Saudi air attack.[^cite_note-Mokha911-110] Saudi airstrikes targeted the Mokha airport.[^cite_note-Mokha911-110] The Yemeni Brigadier General Farouk al-Kholani of the 1st Infantry Division was initially reported killed while battling Houthi forces, along with his son on the west coast of Yemen.[^cite_note-alkholani-26][^cite_note-alkholani2-111] It was later revealed that al-Kholani was captured by Houthi fighters.[^cite_note-auto-22]

The *Support and Reinforcement Brigades*, who traditionally operated alongside the former Security Belt Forces in Aden and [Lahej](2480600.md) under the command of the STC prior to its fall, announced through their leader and spokesperson [Aidarus al-Zoubaidi](52145652.md) and through Brigadier General Nasr Atef al-Yafei, their full combat readiness to protect Southern Yemen and fight for the cause of South Yemen, calling all other formations of the Southern Resistance to unite in a front for national interest.[^cite_note-110926Marsad-23]

The PLC-aligned [Yemeni Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_Air_Force) began airstrikes on a designated "kill zone" in Taiz Governorate, with army spokesman Col. Majed Al-Nuzaily warning citizens to stay off the roads of Taiz-Mokha and Mokha-Dhubab.[^cite_note-112]

The Houthis also seized highland areas north of the coast in [Taiz Governorate](3604497.md), increasing pressure on the city.[^cite_note-113] The day witnessed intensive airstrikes and reciprocal shelling between the opposing factions, which included Houthi bombardments targeting positions within Saudi Arabia. According to Yemeni government sources, a significant number of Houthi fighters were killed during these engagements.[^cite_note-114]

Wajdi al-Nakhai, a prominent leader of the Southern Resistance, called upon all cells present within Aden, to integrate with the PLC forces temporarily in anticipation of a Houthi offensive. Residents and local fighters in the [Al Buraiqah district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Buraiqah_district) were directed to unify under Tariq al-Najdi's leadership. According to al-Nakha'i, domestic mobilization was coordinated with the PLC's military leadership, including the Yemeni Joint Forces and the [Southern Giants Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_Brigades), under the oversight of [PLC](70488867.md) Deputy Al-Rahman, [Abu Zara'a](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Zara'a), Al-Mahrami and Major General Hamdi Shukri.[^cite_note-115] Likewise, Southern Transitional Council (STC) of the village [Kash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karsh_\(Yemen\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) called on political, military and intelligence figures within the area to cooperate with PLC's military leadership, the Security Belt Forces, and the Southern Resistance.[^cite_note-116] The STC in [Radfan district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radfan_district) also reportedly called for general mobilization. They reportedly did not seek integration with the PLC but the creation of a cohesive Southern Resistance fighting force.[^cite_note-117]

The [International Organization for Migration](36893.md) warned that at least 76,000 people had been displaced in Yemen, with many facing repeated displacement.[^cite_note-118]

### 13 September

A mosque in [Jizan province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jizan_province) was hit by a projectile that injured two people.[^cite_note-119]

There were claims that the Southern Resistance and the Giants Brigade had liberated Dhubab, but they were contradicted by field reports, which stated that both the Southern Resistance and the Giants Brigade, under the leadership of Major General Hamdi Shukri and Brigadier General Ali Hassan Al-Radfani, had clashed with the Houthis.[^cite_note-120] Reports suggested that after securing the coast, the Houthis turned their attention towards offensives in the Lahaj and [Marib](3604484.md) governorates,[^cite_note-121] with *[Al Jazeera](30204052.md)* reporting clashes in the outskirts of Marib.[^cite_note-\:7-27] The PLC's reserve [Homeland Shield Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Shield_Forces) claimed that its 6th Brigade had downed five drones during clashes in the [al-Aghbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aghbara?action=edit\&redlink=1) area.[^cite_note-\:7-27]

Yemeni government forces claimed to have recaptured the Al-Ghail area in Al-Waziyah, seizing abandoned equipment and vehicles. They also stated their forces took the overlooks of Jabal Jardad in Al-Shamaytain, as well as advances on Ghayl Bani Omar, with airstrikes claimed to have destroyed vehicles, stored ammunition, and killing at least 10 Houthi fighters.[^cite_note-122]

### 14 September

[Al Jazeera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jazeera_English) reported that the Houthis advanced in PLC-held Marib and Taiz areas,[^cite_note-123] later reporting that the PLC repelled the offensive in western Taiz,[^cite_note-124] before once again reporting that Houthi militants had managed to breach the PLC's previously established front line between 14\:00 GMT and 15\:00 GMT.[^cite_note-125]

### 15 September

Houthis launched missile and drone attacks targeting Khamis Mushait, Abha and [Taif](201969.md), resulting in 13 civilians being injured and damaging property.[^cite_note-SA_respond-126][^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128]

The Houthis launched an offensive in Al-Jawf, showing images of destroyed vehicles and fighting, in addition to the retaliatory bombings by the Saudi-led coalition.[^cite_note-129]

### 16 September

According to the spokesman of the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen, Major General Turki al-Maliki, Saudi air defenses intercepted a drone south of the holy city of [Mecca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mecca). The Houthis denied targeting the city of Mecca.[^cite_note-130]

The Houthis claimed to have shot down a Saudi [F-15 fighter jet](11715.md) after the aircraft was hit by a locally made missile.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] Footage later released by the Houthis appeared to show the tail of a [Royal Saudi Air Force](570367.md) [F-15SR](495724.md#Saudi_Arabia) in the [Marib Governorate](3604391.md).[^cite_note-f-15lede-45][^cite_note-133]

The PLC stated that its forces bombarded outposts and equipment near Mokha and conducted drone attacks against Houthi forces in Marib.[^cite_note-134]

### 17 September

*Al Jazeera* reported significant clashes for the control of the Kahbub mountains between the PLC and the Houthis, with the PLC successfully repelling an offensive causing, according to its own sources, injuries and deaths on the other side whilst securing the arrest of two Houthi fighters.[^cite_note-135]

Yemeni forces including Giants Brigades, Homeland Shield and Subaiha tribes claimed to have fully recaptured the Kahbub mountains after clashes which dealt heavy losses to the Houthis.[^cite_note-Subaiha_tribes-28]

According to [Saudi Civil Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Civil_Defense) a Yemeni resident was killed and a Saudi woman and a Pakistani resident were injured in the Saudi city of [Taif](201969.md) by a Houthi drone attack. This marked the first civilian death in Saudi Arabia since the end of the 2022 cease fire.[^cite_note-\:9-136][^cite_note-137]

The Houthi-linked [Almasirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almasirah) reported that a civilian was killed in Taiz and another injured in strikes on the town of [Abs, Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abs,_Yemen), it also reported that three children were killed from Saudi airstrikes.[^cite_note-\:9-136]

Houthis also claimed to have shot a Saudi drone in [Harad district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harad_district).[^cite_note-138]

The Southern Resistance claimed that, with the alleged assistance of tribesmen from the [Subeihi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subeihi) region, they had successfully repelled the offensive of a Houthi offensive in al-Sudair-al-Dakhula, claiming to have captured 1 military vehicle, 2 Houthi militia members and having killed more than 12 Houthi fighters.[^cite_note-\:10-139]

### 18 September

[Italian](14532.md) defense minister [Guido Crosetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guido_Crosetto) announced that an [Italian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Air_Force) [Eurofighter Typhoon](167667.md) aircraft was damaged at the [King Fahd Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Fahd_Air_Base) during a Houthi strike.[^cite_note-ita-53]

The Southern Resistance claimed that, with the alleged assistance of tribesmen from the [Subeihi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subeihi) region, they had successfully repelled the offensive of a Houthi [brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigade) in al-Sudair-al-Dakhula, allegedly thwarting an offensive in the area for the second time.[^cite_note-\:10-139]

Saudi Arabia proposed a two-week ceasefire with the Houthis, mediated through Oman.[^cite_note-140]

### 19 September

Houthi major general Firaq Mohsen al-Asar, also known as "Abu Saqr", was reportedly killed alongside six of his staff in Kahboub.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142] The PLC said that Houthi offensives near Taiz were repelled, killing 30 Houthis and injuring 60 others.[^cite_note-143] It later said that it thwarted a Houthi attack in al-Fuliahan in Marib, killing 12 Houthis.[^cite_note-144] The army announced that 13 Yemeni soldiers were killed in the governorates of Taiz and [Lahej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahej_Governorate) in the past two days.[^cite_note-145]

Following an air raid alert in [Riyadh](51554.md), a fire and rising smoke was reported at the [King Khalid International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Khalid_International_Airport).[^cite_note-146] The Houthis claimed the attack, along with missile and drone strikes on [Saudi Aramco](290527.md) facilities in Yanbu.[^cite_note-147]

### 20 September

The Giants Brigades claimed to have launched "precise" attacks against Houthi-owned infrastructure and vehicles in [Al-Bayda Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bayda_Governorate).[^cite_note-148]

The PLC claimed to have captured Jabal Numan, west of Taiz; securing the Bani Bakari–Al-Ashrouh–Al-Birin road,[^cite_note-149] establishing a connection with the Al-Kadha front.[^cite_note-150]

The Yemeni army said that fighting outside Marib killed 80 Houthis.[^cite_note-151] Houthi media reported that a Saudi strike on telecommunications towers in [Khabb wa ash Sha'af district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabb_wa_ash_Sha'af_district) killed four civilians and injured three others.[^cite_note-152]

### 21 September

The Giants Brigades claimed responsibility for the destruction of five military vehicles belonging to the Houthis in Kahbub; one of the vehicles was transporting a Houthi commander, while the others carried reinforcements and weapons.[^cite_note-153]

The [Homeland Shield Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_Shield_Forces) claimed to have shot down 2 Houthi drones during the clashes.[^cite_note-154]

Houthi missile strikes hit a crowded market south of Kahboub, injuring several people, according to the PLC.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156]

### 22 September

Fighting and airstrikes in Taiz, al-Jawf, Marib, Saada, and Lahej killed 118 Houthis and 36 Yemeni soldiers since 20 September.[^cite_note-157] The Houthis also said that Saudi airstrikes killed six civilians in Mocha and nine people at a prison in al-Jawf.[^cite_note-158]

The PLC chief of staff for the Taiz military axis, Major General Abdulaziz al-Majidi said the army captured [Mount Qarfan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Qarfan?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Taiz, killing five Houthis and injuring 20.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161]

### 23 September

The Houthis said that Saudi airstrikes in Taiz and Al-Jawf killed ten civilians and injured 14 others.[^cite_note-162] The group also said that it shot down a Saudi-operated [CAIG Wing Loong II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAIG_Wing_Loong_II) drone over Mocha.[^cite_note-163]

### 24 September

Clashes in Lahej, Taiz, Hodeidah, and [Saada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada_Governorate) killed 98 Yemeni soldiers and 52 Houthis. The PLC said that it repelled an overnight Houthi infiltration attempt against al-Ashbout and Heijah al-Abd, a key route from Taiz to [Aden](38050.md),[^cite_note-164] in [Al Maqatirah district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Maqatirah_district), and al-Ahkum in [Hayfan district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayfan_district).[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

Saudi Arabia said that it shot down six ballistic missiles from Yemen targeting Taif and Yanbu.[^cite_note-167] Houthi spokesperson [Yahya Saree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Saree) later said that the group attacked Saudi military sites in Jazan, causing hundreds of casualties among Saudi and Yemeni forces, as well as Sudanese mercenaries.[^cite_note-168] Later, the Houthis claimed missile and drone strikes against Riyadh and Saudi Aramco sites in Yanbu.[^cite_note-169]

### 25 September

The Homeland Shield Forces stated that, with the support of the Giants Brigades and members of local tribes, they captured the summit of Mount Dar al-Kafir in Lahej Governorate following an infiltration and an assault.[^cite_note-170]

### 26 September

Clashes were reported in Marib and Taiz, with the PLC claiming that the Houthis bombarded their positions in northern Taiz with several projectiles.[^cite_note-171]

### 27 September

Saudi Arabia shifted schools and universities in Riyadh to remote learning for a week, seemingly due to escalating tensions.[^cite_note-172]

The Houthis said that a Saudi strike on a market in Taiz Governorate killed seven people and injured 40 others.[^cite_note-173] The Yemeni army said that it killed four Iranian missile and drone experts in the same region.[^cite_note-174]

### 28 September

Saudi Foreign Minister [Faisal bin Farhan Al Saud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_bin_Farhan_Al_Saud) met with US Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) to discuss the war, bilateral relations, and regional events. Prince Faisal urged foreign powers to protect shipping and advocated a comprehensive peace deal in Yemen.[^cite_note-175]

According to [Almasirah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almasirah), four civilians, including three women and an elderly man, were reportedly killed and three others wounded after a Saudi airstrike struck a civilian home in [Al-Maafer district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Maafer_district) of Taiz.[^cite_note-176]

The Giants Brigades claimed the destruction of a vehicle carrying a Houthi leader and his companions on the Khabub front.[^cite_note-177]

### 29 September

PLC spokesman Majid Al-Nuzaili claimed that PLC forces carried out 414 attacks against the Houthis in 24 hours.[^cite_note-178]

### 30 September

The PLC claimed to have launched attacks towards Al-Jund and the area surrounding Taiz International Airport.[^cite_note-179]

Clashes intensified in Taiz, Lahej and Marib, following the PLC's call for a general mobilization; a child died following a bombardment in the Sala area, east of Taiz.[^cite_note-180]

### 1 October

The PLC claimed to have carried out 27 attacks against the Houthis around the Taiz front, resulting in the destruction of vehicles and killing several Houthi fighters.[^cite_note-181]

On the same day, Saudi air defenses said they intercepted two ballistic missiles over Khamis Mushait and Jazan, in addition to four drones in Khamis Mushait.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184]

An unnamed individual of african descent who was under scrutiny for alleged ties with the Houthis and suspects of possible sabotage plans in Haifan-Ayrin area was captured by the Southern Resistance.[^cite_note-185]

### 2 October

The PLC bombarded Houthi positions in Bani Bakari, Al-Kadha, and Jabal Numan with airstrikes,[^cite_note-186] in addition to attacking Houthi supply lines in Jabal Rasen, Al-Waziyah.[^cite_note-187]

### 3 October

Houthi spokesman Yahya Saree claimed to have attacked the Aramco facility in Riyadh with ballistic missiles and drones.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-189]

The Houthis attacked positions belonging to the [Al-Islah party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Islah_\(Yemen\)) in [Same'a district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same'a_district), capturing the Al-Ja'sha area.[^cite_note-190]

## Foreign involvement

### Pro-Houthis

The Iranian [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) reportedly guided and armed the Houthi offensive in an effort to open a new front in the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md). Saudi-backed government sources claimed that the offensive across the [Tihamah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tihamah), the plains of Yemen's [Red Sea](26191.md) coast, was led by IRGC [Quds Force](4028355.md) officer [Abdolreza Shahlaei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdolreza_Shahlaei).[^cite_note-Shahlaei-191] There are reportedly hundreds of IRGC officers stationed in Yemen to support Houthi operations, aiming to close the [Strait of Bab al-Mandab](62138.md).[^cite_note-USIntel-192] The [Yemeni National Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemeni_National_Resistance) claimed that it seized documents from the laptop of a captured Houthi commander that detailed a four-stage plan by the IRGC to capture the Bab al-Mandab.[^cite_note-193] Nevertheless, the Iranian government denied any involvement in the conflict.[^cite_note-\:8-194] Iran Foreign Affairs spokesperson [Esmail Baghaei](81031766.md) said that the Houthis are independent actors with their own interests.[^cite_note-\:8-194][^cite_note-auto1-195]

According to the National Resistance, the bodies of several fighters from [al-Shabaab](8726463.md), a militant group based in Somalia, were recovered among Houthi casualties during the clashes and identified using personal documents and belongings. Although the claim was not independently verified, cooperation between the Houthis and al-Shabaab has been recorded since at least 2023, according to *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*.[^cite_note-WSJShabaab-196]

### Pro-PLC and Saudi Arabia

According to [CNN](62028.md), over 100 American military advisers who were stationed in Saudi Arabia weeks earlier due to signs that Iran was increasing aid to the Houthis were providing intelligence support to the Saudi military during the offensives. The support included a targeting tool that was compared to [Project Maven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Maven) as well as [geospatial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geospatial_intelligence) for strikes against the militia.[^cite_note-USIntel-192] US president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) rejected a request by Saudi leader [Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md) to strike the Houthis.[^cite_note-TrumpReject-197]

According to Lebanese newspaper [*Al Akhbar*](7614001.md), citing American officials, Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) offered to join a regional effort against the Houthis in support of countries in the Arabian peninsula. Additionally, Israeli news outlet [*Maariv*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maariv_\(newspaper\)) was closely monitoring Houthi movements following the capture of Mokha.[^cite_note-198] On 15 September, [*Walla!*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walla!_Communications) reported that Saudi Arabia sought Israeli intelligence support in talks mediated by the [United States Central Command](338482.md). The [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) advised Israel's leadership not to conduct strikes on the Houthis or other actions that would militarily involve Israel in the conflict.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200]

Turkish media reported that Pakistan was considering airstrikes against the Houthis under the [Mecca Joint Defence Agreement](83903032.md).[^cite_note-TürkToday-201] Pakistani defense minister [Khawaja Asif](16345137.md) warned on 9 September that the Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia could activate the pact.[^cite_note-MeccaPact-202] Pakistan said on 10 September that there were no talks regarding military actions, but that the country would act "when the time comes."[^cite_note-PakResponse-203] A condemnation of the Houthi attacks was made by Pakistani Prime Minister [Shehbaz Sharif](6502561.md) on 13 September, the [Saudi Press Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Press_Agency) reported.[^cite_note-SharifHouthis-204] Pakistan's DG ISPR [Ahmed Sharif Chaudhry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Sharif_Chaudhry) stated that Pakistan "stands with Saudi Arabia".[^cite_note-205]

Saudi Arabia requested military support from the [United Kingdom](31717.md) for both repelling the Bab al-Mandab offensive and defending Saudi oil infrastructure from Houthi attacks. British prime minister [Andy Burnham](414818.md) reportedly agreed to sending military advisers to Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-BurnhamHouthis-206] High-level officials from multiple regional states said [Turkey](11125639.md) planned to offer indirect military aid to Saudi Arabia, but not support for offensive operations.[^cite_note-TurkishMilAid-207]

## Consequences

### Camp Lemonnier

The Houthi capture of coastal territories bordering the [Strait of Bab al-Mandab](62138.md) brought Houthi positions on [Perim Island](1907645.md) within approximately 80 miles (130 km) of US-run [Camp Lemonnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Lemonnier) in [Djibouti](17207794.md), posing possible strategic risks for the base's future.[^cite_note-208][^cite_note-209]

### Wartime displacements

As of 15 September 2026, it was reported around an estimated 125,000 Yemeni nationals were displaced internally by conflict related reasons.[^cite_note-SA_respond-126]

The Djiboutian Economic Minister [Ilyas Moussa Dawaleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyas_Moussa_Dawaleh) reported that approximately 2,000+ Yemeni refugees arrived in Djibouti within 24 hours. Among the arrivals, 500 evacuees reached [Obock](203894.md), including women and children rescued at sea after their boats ran out of fuel.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] On 19 September, Al Jazeera reported that over 2,700 people fled to Djibouti.[^cite_note-212]

### Mecca Agreement

Some analysts noted that the Yemen conflict failed to activate the recently-concluded [Mecca Agreement](83903032.md), including defensive action by Turkey or Pakistan, suggesting that the agreement was being violated. However, others noted that the pact requires parliamentary sanction in those nations, but can be approved by decree in Saudi Arabia. Turkish officials also said the terms of the agreement, such as military interoperability, could take a few years to be fully implemented. As of 16 September, Pakistan said it did not know whether it had been approached by Saudi Arabia for aid.[^cite_note-MeccaNDTV-213][^cite_note-MeccaMEE-214]

## Analysis

While the Houthis have sensed an opportunity to advance by exploiting the regional chaos and global economic turmoil unleashed by Trump’s war on Iran, according to NYU professor and Near Eastern studies expert [Mohamad Bazzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamad_Bazzi), Trump has decided to avoid direct intervention in the Houthi-Saudi conflict, seemingly acknowledging that over a decade of bombing campaigns has failed to force them into submission.[^cite_note-215] [Gregory D. Johnsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_D._Johnsen) of the [Arab Gulf States Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Gulf_States_Institute) describes the anti-Houthi coalition as a disparate, incoherent group whose clashing internal goals lead them to fight each other more than the Houthis.[^cite_note-216]
