**Brandenburg**, officially the **State of Brandenburg** (see [Names](#Names)), is a landlocked [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) in northeastern Germany. Brandenburg borders [Poland](22936.md) and the states of [Berlin](3354.md), [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](38168.md), [Lower Saxony](18435.md), [Saxony-Anhalt](38138.md), and [Saxony](28395.md). It is the [fifth-largest German state by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_area) and the [tenth-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_German_states_by_population), with 2.5 million residents. [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam) is the state capital and largest city. Other major towns are [Cottbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottbus), [Brandenburg an der Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_an_der_Havel) and [Frankfurt (Oder)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_\(Oder\)).

Brandenburg surrounds the national capital and city-state of Berlin. Together they form the [Berlin/Brandenburg Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin/Brandenburg_Metropolitan_Region),[^cite_note-6] the third-largest [metropolitan area in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_regions_in_Germany). There was [an unsuccessful attempt to unify both states in 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_of_Berlin_and_Brandenburg#1996_fusion_attempt), and the states still cooperate on many matters.

Brandenburg originated in the [Northern March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_March) in the 900s AD, from areas conquered from the [Wends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wends). It later became the [Margraviate of Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Brandenburg), a major principality of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). In the 15th century, it came under the rule of the [House of Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenzollern), which later established [Brandenburg-Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg-Prussia), the core of the later [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia). From 1815 to 1947, Brandenburg was a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brandenburg) of [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia).

Following the [abolition of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolition_of_Prussia) after [World War II](32927.md), Brandenburg was established as [a state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Brandenburg_\(1947%E2%80%931952\)) by the [Soviet Military Administration in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Military_Administration_in_Germany). All lands that were previously part of Brandenburg but east of the [Oder–Neisse line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder%E2%80%93Neisse_line) had been ceded to [Poland](22936.md). In 1952, the [German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Democratic_Republic) dissolved the state and broke it up into multiple [regional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_East_Germany). After [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification), Brandenburg was re-established in 1990 as one of the five [new states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_states_of_Germany) of the Federal Republic.

Southeastern Brandenburg contains part of the historical [Lower Lusatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Lusatia), and most of these localities have two official languages, German and [Lower Sorbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Sorbian_language) (of the [Sorbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbs)/Wends).[^cite_note-7]

## Names

Brandenburg takes its name from [Brandenburg an der Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_an_der_Havel). The name's origin is unclear but it is thought to derive from the [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages) *branyi* ('marshy/boggy')[^cite_note-Duden-Geo-8][^cite_note-Frinta-9] or [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages) *branda* ('fire').[^cite_note-Duden-Geo-8] Other theories have included [West Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Slavic_languages) *branný bor* ('defensive forest')[^cite_note-Frinta-9] and *brani-bor* ('pine forest').[^cite_note-10] *[Burg](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Burg#German)* in German means 'castle'. In the extinct local language [Polabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polabian_language), Brandenburg was *Branibor*.

In the modern local languages, *Brandenburg* and the *State of Brandenburg* are named:

In late medieval and early modern times, Brandenburg was, with varying borders, one of seven [electoral states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-elector) of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), and, along with [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia), formed the original core of the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), the first unified German state. Governed by the [Hohenzollern dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenzollern) from 1415, it contained the future German capital Berlin. After 1618 the [Margraviate of Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Brandenburg) and the [Duchy of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Prussia) were combined to form [Brandenburg-Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg-Prussia), which was ruled by the same branch of the [House of Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenzollern). In 1701 the state was elevated as the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia). Franconian [Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuremberg) and [Ansbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansbach), Swabian [Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burg_Hohenzollern), the eastern European connections of Berlin, and the status of Brandenburg's ruler as [prince-elector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-elector) together were instrumental in the rise of that state.

### Early Middle Ages

Main article: [Northern March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_March)

Brandenburg is situated in territory known in antiquity as [Magna Germania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Germania), which reached to the Vistula river. By the 7th century, [Slavic people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_people) are believed to have settled in the Brandenburg area. The Slavs expanded from the east, possibly driven from their homelands in present-day Ukraine and perhaps Belarus by the invasions of the [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns) and [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Avars). They relied heavily on river transport. The two principal Slavic groups in the present-day area of Brandenburg were the [Hevelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hevelli) in the west and the [Sprevane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprevane) in the east.

Beginning in the early 10th century, [Henry the Fowler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_the_Fowler) and his successors conquered territory up to the [Oder River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder_River). Slavic settlements such as Brenna[^cite_note-11] ([Brandenburg an der Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_an_der_Havel)), Budusin[^cite_note-12] ([Bautzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bautzen)), and Chośebuz[^cite_note-13] ([Cottbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottbus)) came under [imperial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) control through the installation of margraves. Their main function was to defend and protect the eastern [marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marches). In 948, Emperor [Otto I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) established margraves to exert imperial control over the pagan Slavs west of the Oder River. Otto founded the Bishoprics of [Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Brandenburg) and [Havelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishopric_of_Havelberg). The [Northern March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_March) was founded as a northeastern border territory of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). However, a great uprising of [Wends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wends) drove imperial forces from the territory of present-day Brandenburg in 983. The region returned to the control of Slavic leaders. The eastern parts of current Brandenburg, i.e. the [Lubusz Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubusz_Land) and [Lower Lusatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Lusatia), became part of Poland in the 10th–11th centuries.

### Late Middle Ages

Main article: [Margraviate of Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Brandenburg)



Eisenhardt Castle in [Bad Belzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Belzig)

In the early 12th century, the Slavic [Duchy of Kopanica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Kopanica) was established in the central part of present-day Brandenburg, whereas the [Lubusz Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubusz_Land) in the east remained part of medieval Poland. During the 12th century, the German kings and emperors re-established control over the Slav-inhabited lands of present-day Brandenburg, excluding Polish-controlled Lubusz Land, although some Slavs like the [Sorbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbs) in [Lusatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusatia) adapted to [Germanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanization) while retaining their distinctiveness. The Roman Catholic Church brought [bishoprics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishoprics) which, with their walled towns, afforded protection from attacks for the townspeople. With the monks and bishops, the history of the town of [Brandenburg an der Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_\(town\)), which was the first center of the state of Brandenburg, began.

In 1134, in the wake of a German [crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Crusade) against the [Wends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wends), the German magnate, [Albert the Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_the_Bear), was granted the [Northern March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_March) by the Emperor [Lothar III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothair_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). He formally inherited the town of Brandenburg and the lands of the Hevelli from their last Wendish ruler, [Pribislav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pribislav_of_Brandenburg), in 1150. After crushing a force of Sprevane who occupied the town of Brandenburg in the 1150s, Albert proclaimed himself ruler of the new [Margraviate of Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margraviate_of_Brandenburg). The systematic settlement of the region by colonists from other parts of Germany and the [Low Countries](51402.md) began around 1157.[^cite_note-Koch-14] Albert, and his descendants the [Ascanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Ascania), made considerable progress in conquering, colonizing, Christianizing, and cultivating lands as far east as the Oder. Within this region, Slavic and German residents intermarried. During the 13th century, the Ascanians acquired the [Uckermark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uckermark) from [Pomerania-Stettin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomerania-Stettin) and the [Lebus Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebus_Land) from Poland (later known as the [Neumark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neumark), in contrast to the [Altmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altmark), the cradle of the March of Brandenburg).[^cite_note-Koch-14]

In 1320, the Brandenburg Ascanian line came to an end, and from 1323 up until 1415 Brandenburg was under the control of the [Wittelsbachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wittelsbach) of [Bavaria](3764.md), followed by the [Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Luxembourg) dynasty. Under the Wittelsbachs, the [Margrave of Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margrave_of_Brandenburg) gained the status of a [prince-elector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-elector) of the Holy Roman Empire. In 1415, the Electorate of Brandenburg was granted by Emperor [Sigismund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismund,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) to the [House of Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hohenzollern), which would rule until the end of World War I. The Hohenzollerns established their capital in Berlin, by then the economic center of Brandenburg.

### 16th and 17th centuries

Main article: [Brandenburg-Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg-Prussia)



Brandenburg's victory over Swedish forces at the [Battle of Fehrbellin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fehrbellin) in 1675

Brandenburg converted to Protestantism in 1539 in the wake of the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), and generally did quite well in the 16th century, with the expansion of trade along the Elbe, Havel, and Spree rivers. The Hohenzollerns expanded their territory by co-rulership since 1577 and acquiring the [Duchy of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Prussia) in 1618, the [Duchy of Cleves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Cleves) (1614) in the [Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhineland), and territories in [Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westphalia). The result was a sprawling, disconnected country known as [Brandenburg-Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg-Prussia) that was in poor shape to defend itself during the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War).

Beginning near the end of that devastating conflict, however, Brandenburg enjoyed a string of talented rulers who expanded their territory and power in Europe. The first of these was [Frederick William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_William,_the_Great_Elector), the so-called "Great Elector", who worked tirelessly to rebuild and consolidate the nation. He moved the royal residence to [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam). At the [Peace of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Westphalia), his envoy [Joachim Friedrich von Blumenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joachim_Friedrich_von_Blumenthal) negotiated the acquisition of several important territories such as [Halberstadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halberstadt). Under the [Treaty of Oliva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Oliva) Christoph Caspar [von Blumenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Von_Blumenthal) (son of the above) negotiated the incorporation of the [Duchy of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Prussia) into the Hohenzollern inheritance.

### Kingdom of Prussia and German Empire

Main articles: [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) and [Province of Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brandenburg)



The [Sanssouci Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanssouci) in [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam), the former summer palace of [Frederick the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_the_Great), is today a [World Heritage Site](44940.md).

When Frederick William died in 1688, he was followed by his son [Frederick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_I_of_Prussia), third of that name in Brandenburg. As the lands that had been acquired in Prussia were outside the boundaries of the Holy Roman Empire, Frederick assumed (as Frederick I) the title of "[King in Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_in_Prussia)" (1701). Although his self-promotion from margrave to king relied on his title to the Duchy of Prussia, Brandenburg was still the most important portion of the kingdom. However, this combined state is known as the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia).



The [Province of Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brandenburg), as superimposed on modern borders

Brandenburg remained the core of the Kingdom of Prussia, and it was the site of the kingdom's capitals, Berlin and Potsdam. When Prussia was subdivided into provinces in 1815, the territory of the Margraviate of Brandenburg became the [Province of Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Brandenburg), again subdivided into the [government region of Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regierungsbezirk_Frankfurt) and Potsdam. It also included [Lower Lusatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Lusatia), previously ruled at various times by Poland, [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Crown), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) and [Saxony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electorate_of_Saxony). In 1881, the City of Berlin was separated from the province of Brandenburg.[^cite_note-15] However, industrial towns ringing Berlin lay within Brandenburg, and the growth of the region's industrial economy brought an increase in the population of the province. The Province of Brandenburg had an area of 38,274 km<sup>2</sup> and a population of 3,023,443 (1939).[^cite_note-16]

Under the [Nazi government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_government) and during [World War II](32927.md), repressions of [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), especially autochthones in the eastern part of the province, intensified with [expulsions of Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Poles_by_Germany), censorship of Polish newspapers, invigilation, arrests and assassinations of Polish leaders, activists, teachers, entrepreneurs, editors, etc., deportations to [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps) and closure of Polish organizations, enterprises, schools and libraries.[^cite_note-17] The [Sachsenhausen concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sachsenhausen_concentration_camp) with a network of [subcamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_subcamps_of_Sachsenhausen) was located in Brandenburg, and several [prisoner-of-war camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II), including [Stalag III-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_III-A), Stalag III-B, [Stalag III-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_III-C), [Stalag III-D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_III-D), [Oflag II-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oflag_II-A), Oflag III-A, Oflag III-B, [Oflag III-C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oflag_III-C), Oflag 8 and Oflag 80 for Polish, Belgian, British, Dutch, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_prisoners_of_war_in_World_War_II), Serbian, [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Military_Internees), American, Czechoslovak, Soviet, Romanian, Greek, Bulgarian and other [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) POWs with numerous [forced labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_labour_under_German_rule_during_World_War_II) subcamps.[^cite_note-18] In early 1945, the [death marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_marches_during_the_Holocaust) of prisoners of various nationalities from various dissolved camps passed through the region.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] In the final stages of the war, it was the place of heavy fights, including the [Battle of the Seelow Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Seelow_Heights) and [Battle of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin), won by the Allied Soviet and Polish armies. After Germany's defeat, the territories of Brandenburg located east of the Oder–Neisse line were placed under Polish administration, pending a final peace settlement. The entire population of former East Brandenburg was [expelled en masse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_and_expulsion_of_Germans_from_Poland_during_and_after_World_War_II). The remainder of the province became a state in the [Soviet Zone of occupation in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_zone) when Prussia was dissolved in 1947.

### East Germany



[Glienicke Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glienicke_Bridge), which connected East Germany to the American sector of West Berlin, became known for the exchange of captured spies.

After the foundation of [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) in 1949, Brandenburg formed one of its component states. The State of Brandenburg was completely dissolved in 1952 by the [Socialist government of East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_the_GDR), doing away with all component states. The East German government then divided Brandenburg among several *Bezirke* or districts. (See [Administrative division of the German Democratic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division_of_the_German_Democratic_Republic)). Most of Brandenburg lay within the [Bezirke of Cottbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottbus_\(Bezirk\)), [Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_\(Bezirk\)), or [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_\(Bezirk\)), but parts of the former province passed to the [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin_\(Bezirk\)), [Neubrandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neubrandenburg_\(Bezirk\)) and [Magdeburg districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdeburg_\(Bezirk\)) (town [Havelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havelberg)). East Germany relied heavily on [lignite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite) (the lowest grade of coal) as an energy source, and lignite strip mines marred areas of south-eastern Brandenburg. The industrial towns surrounding Berlin were important to the East German economy, while rural Brandenburg remained mainly agricultural.

### Federal Republic of Germany

The present State of Brandenburg was re-established on 3 October 1990 upon [German reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_reunification).[^cite_note-21]

The Landtag of Brandenburg [elected on 4 October 1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Brandenburg_state_election) first met on 26 October 1990.[^cite_note-22] As in other former parts of East Germany, the lack of modern infrastructure and exposure to West Germany's competitive market economy brought widespread unemployment and economic difficulty in [the 1990s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s#Economics) and early [2000s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000s#Economics).In 2002, the First Schröder cabinet started the [Hartz concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartz_concept) to lower unemployment and to improve Germany's competitiveness. In 2023, the Hartz concept was replaced by the Citizens’ Basic Income ([Bürgergeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%BCrgergeld)).[^cite_note-23]

#### Berlin-Brandenburg fusion attempt

Main article: [Fusion of Berlin and Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_of_Berlin_and_Brandenburg)



The coat of arms proposed in the interstate treaty

The legal basis for a combined state of [Berlin](3354.md) and Brandenburg is different from other state fusion proposals. Normally, Article 29 of the [Basic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_for_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany) stipulates that states may only merge after a specific federal Act of Parliament is enacted.[^cite_note-24] However, a clause added to the Basic Law in 1994, Article 118a, allows Berlin and Brandenburg to unify without federal approval, requiring a referendum and a ratification by both state parliaments.[^cite_note-25]

In 1996, an attempt of unifying the states of Berlin and Brandenburg was rejected at referendum.[^cite_note-berlingeschichte-26] Both share a common history, dialect and culture and in 2020, over 225,000 residents of Brandenburg commuted to Berlin. The fusion had the near-unanimous support by a broad coalition of both state governments, political parties, media, business associations, trade unions and churches.[^cite_note-27] Though Berlin voted in favor by a small margin, largely based on support in former [West Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Berlin), Brandenburg voters disapproved of the fusion by a large margin.[^cite_note-28] It failed largely due to Brandenburg voters not wanting to take on Berlin's large and growing public debt and fearing losing identity and influence to the capital.[^cite_note-berlingeschichte-26]

## Geography

Brandenburg is bordered by [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](38168.md) in the north, [Poland](22936.md) in the east, the [Freistaat Sachsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_State_of_Saxony) in the south, [Saxony-Anhalt](38138.md) in the west, and [Lower Saxony](18435.md) in the northwest.

The [Oder river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder_river) forms a part of the eastern border, the [Elbe river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe) a portion of the western border. The main rivers in the state itself are the [Spree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spree_\(river\)) and the [Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havel). In the southeast, there is a wetlands region called the [Spreewald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spreewald); it is the northernmost part of [Lusatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lusatia).

## Politics

See also: [List of rulers of Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rulers_of_Brandenburg)

Politically, Brandenburg is a stronghold of the [Social Democratic Party](60585.md), which won the largest share of the vote and seats in every state election. All three [Minister-Presidents of Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Minister-Presidents_of_Brandenburg) have come from the Social Democratic Party (unlike any other state except [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_\(state\))) and they even won an absolute majority of seats and every single-member constituency in the [1994 state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Brandenburg_state_election).

On a federal level, the Social Democratic Party has also been the strongest party in most [federal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Germany#German_elections_since_1949), their strongholds being the [northwestern part of the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prignitz_%E2%80%93_Ostprignitz-Ruppin_%E2%80%93_Havelland_I) and [Potsdam and its surrounding areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_%E2%80%93_Potsdam-Mittelmark_II_%E2%80%93_Teltow-Fl%C3%A4ming_II). However, the [Christian Democratic Union](60584.md) won the most votes in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_German_federal_election), their [2013 landslide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_German_federal_election) and in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_German_federal_election). In [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_German_federal_election), [The Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Left_\(Germany\)) won the most votes in a year where, like in 2017, the social democrats collapsed. Prominent politicians from Brandenburg include Social Democrats [Frank-Walter Steinmeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank-Walter_Steinmeier), who served in the [Bundestag](3768.md) for Brandenburg before being elected [President of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Germany), and [Chancellor of Germany](20890626.md) [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz), who sits in the Bundestag for [Potsdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_%E2%80%93_Potsdam-Mittelmark_II_%E2%80%93_Teltow-Fl%C3%A4ming_II).

Like in all other [New states of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_states_of_Germany), the leftist party of [The Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Left_\(Germany\)) and, more recently, the far-right [Alternative for Germany](38708229.md) are especially strong in Brandenburg.

Brandenburg has 4 out of 69 votes in the [Bundesrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Bundesrat) and, as of 2021, 25 seats out of 736 in the [Bundestag](3768.md).

The [2024 Brandenburg state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Brandenburg_state_election) took place on 22 September. One important outcome of the election was that the number of parties with representation in the [state parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landtag_of_Brandenburg) went from six to four. The Greens, the Left, and the Brandenburg United Civic Movements/Free Voters all lost their representation while a new party, the [Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahra_Wagenknecht_Alliance) (BSW), gained 14 votes. The BSW was formed on 8 January 2024, mostly by members who had broken away from the Left. While it shares the Left's economic outlook, it is more closely aligned with stances traditionally held by the right on certain issues. For example, the BSW is broadly anti-immigration and anti-Nato, and has been accused of having [Russophile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russophile) tendencies, partially based on their desire for the war in [Ukraine](31750.md) to be ended by diplomacy.[^cite_note-h006-35]

Other German parties have generally been unwilling to work with the AfD and that trend continued with the formation of the new government based on this election. Assuming that the AfD would be in the opposition, the only two options that the SPD, with the plurality of seats, had to obtain a majority were to work with the BSW as well as the Christian Democratic Union (CDU) or to just work with the BSW. Ultimately, the latter option was chosen and a majority of 46 out of 88 seats was obtained.[^cite_note-w562-34]

While this should have been enough votes to reelect Woidke as the Minister-President, he lost the first vote, having only 43 supporters in the secret ballot. While there were only 40 votes against him (there were two abstentions and two votes that were invalid), an absolute majority, 45 votes, was required to win the first round. On 11 December, in the second round of voting, all that was needed was for Woidke to receive more votes in his favor than there were in opposition, and he won in this round with 50 votes.[^cite_note-s277-36] This meant that at least 4 members of the opposition, whether from the AfD, the CDU, or a combination of the two, had voted with the coalition government to reelect Woidke. The CDU accused the SPD of receiving help from the AfD, but it is impossible to know because the ballot was secret. The government in Brandenburg has been led by the SPD since 1990.[^cite_note-e857-37]

## Economy



[Gigafactory Berlin-Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigafactory_Berlin-Brandenburg)

The gross domestic product (GDP) of the state was 72.9 billion euros in 2018, accounting for 2.2% of German economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 26,700 euros or 88% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 91% of the EU average. The GDP per capita was the third lowest of all states in Germany.[^cite_note-39]

The unemployment rate stood at 6.1% in 2024 and was higher than the German average but lower than the average of Eastern Germany.[^cite_note-40]

The industrial sector in Brandenburg is mainly characterized by small and medium‑sized enterprises.[^cite_note-mwaek-41] Its economy is centered on a diverse range of sectors, including metal production and processing, mechanical engineering, the plastics and chemical industries, food production, optics, photonics, vehicle manufacturing and logistics.[^cite_note-mwaek-41]

Tesla [Gigafactory Berlin-Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigafactory_Berlin-Brandenburg) is located in Brandenburg.

## Transport



[Berlin Brandenburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Brandenburg_Airport)

[Berlin Schönefeld Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Sch%C3%B6nefeld_Airport) (IATA code: SXF) was the largest airport in Brandenburg. It was the second largest [international airport](1519705.md) of the Berlin-Brandenburg metropolitan region and was located 18 km (11 mi) southeast of central [Berlin](3354.md) in [Schönefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sch%C3%B6nefeld). The airport was a base for [Condor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condor_Flugdienst), [easyJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EasyJet) and [Ryanair](262878.md). In 2016, Schönefeld handled 11,652,922 passengers (an increase of 36.7%).

Schönefeld's existing infrastructure and terminals were incorporated into the new [Berlin Brandenburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Brandenburg_Airport) (BER),[^cite_note-42] which opened in 2020.[^cite_note-43] Berlin Brandenburg Airport had an initial capacity of 35–40 million passengers a year. Due to increasing air traffic in Berlin and Brandenburg, plans for airport expansions were in the making.

Berlin Brandenburg Airport receives over sixty combined passenger, charter and cargo airlines.

## Culture

### Music

The *[Brandenburg Concertos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_Concertos)* by [Johann Sebastian Bach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Sebastian_Bach) (original title: *Six Concerts à plusieurs instruments*)[^cite_note-bga-47] are a collection of six instrumental works presented by Bach to [Christian Ludwig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Ludwig_of_Brandenburg-Schwedt), [Margrave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margrave) of Brandenburg-[Schwedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwedt),[^cite_note-48] in 1721 (though probably composed earlier). They are widely regarded as among the finest musical compositions of the [Baroque era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_music) and are among the composer's best known works.

### Cuisine

Main article: [Brandenburg cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg_cuisine)



Spreewald gherkins

A famous speciality food from Brandenburg are the [Spreewald gherkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spreewald_gherkins). The wet soil of the [Spreewald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spreewald) makes the region ideal for growing cucumbers. Spreewald gherkins are protected by the EU as a [Protected Geographical Indication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_Geographical_Indication) (PGI). They are one of the biggest exports of Brandenburg.[^cite_note-49]
