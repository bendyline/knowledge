The **Sydney Olympic Park Tennis Centre** is a tennis and multi-purpose sports facility in the [Sydney Olympic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Olympic_Park) suburb of [Sydney](27862.md), [New South Wales](21654.md), [Australia](4689264.md). The centre was built in 1999 and hosted the [tennis events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2000_Summer_Olympics) for the [2000 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics). The venue hosted the [Sydney International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_International) tournament from 2000 to 2019, the [Sydney Tennis Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Sydney_Tennis_Classic) in 2022, the [ATP Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Cup) in 2020 and 2022, and the [United Cup](72152971.md) since 2023. The main stadium in the centre is the **Ken Rosewall Arena**, which has a seating capacity of 10,500, and is capable of hosting multiple sports, including [tennis](29773.md) and [netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netball).

## Facilities

In December 2008, the centre court was renamed **Ken Rosewall Arena**, named in honour of the Sydney-born tennis player and multiple Grand Slam winner [Ken Rosewall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Rosewall).[^cite_note-1] The stadium holds 10,500 people.[^cite_note-2] There are also two other show courts in the precinct seating 4,000 and 2,000 spectators respectively, as well as ten other match courts and six practice courts.[^cite_note-3]

## Refurbishment

In January 2019, the [New South Wales Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Wales_Government) announced a \$50.5 million upgrade of the Sydney Olympic Park Tennis Centre, to improve facilities for players and spectators ahead of the [ATP Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Cup) multi-nation tennis tournament, which began being held at the venue in 2020.[^cite_note-Upgrade2020-4][^cite_note-5] The biggest change to the venue was the construction of a large permanent roof over Ken Rosewall Arena, allowing play to continue on the arena irrespective of the weather conditions. In addition a hardwood playing surface was added to the floor of Ken Rosewall Arena, giving the venue the capability to host sports such as [netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netball) and [basketball](3921.md).[^cite_note-6]

The arena was chosen to host the finals matches of the first three editions of the ATP Cup, from 2020 until 2022.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The upgrade was completed in time for the [2020 ATP Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_ATP_Cup) in January.[^cite_note-9]

[Super Netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suncorp_Super_Netball) clubs the [New South Wales Swifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_South_Wales_Swifts) and [Giants Netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_Netball) were to have moved all home matches to Ken Rosewall Arena ahead of the 2020 season.[^cite_note-10] However, only three days before the 2020 season commenced the [New South Wales](21654.md)-[Queensland](59051.md) border was shut due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Australia), forcing both the Swifts and Giants out of the state and into a Queensland hub; subsequently, neither club played a single home match at the venue in 2020.[^cite_note-11]

Since 2022, the arena has hosted Pulse Alive, a showcase of the creative and performing arts featuring students from [NSW Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Education_\(New_South_Wales\)). Pulse Alive features more than 8800 students performing over three nights and includes dancers, singers, musicians and actors as well as students taking on behind the scenes roles on the VET student production team.[^cite_note-12]
