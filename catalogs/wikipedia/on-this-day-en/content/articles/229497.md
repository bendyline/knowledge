**Acre** ([/ˈɑːkrə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [*AH-krə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-5] Portuguese: [\[ˈakɾi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/ba/Pt-br_Acre.ogg/Pt-br_Acre.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/ba/Pt-br_Acre.ogg/Pt-br_Acre.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a state located in the west of the [North Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Region,_Brazil) of [Brazil](3383.md) and the [Amazonia Legal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonia_Legal). Located in the westernmost part of the country, at a two-hour [time difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Brazil) from [Brasília](4752.md), Acre is bordered clockwise by the Brazilian states of [Amazonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonas_\(Brazilian_state\)) and [Rondônia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rond%C3%B4nia) to the north and east, along with an [international border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil%E2%80%93Peru_border) with the [Bolivian](3462.md) [department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Bolivia) of [Pando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pando_Department) to the southeast, and the [Peruvian](170691.md) [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Peru) of [Madre de Dios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madre_de_Dios_Region), [Ucayali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ucayali_Region) and [Loreto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loreto_Region) to the south and west. Its capital and largest city is [Rio Branco](818818.md). Other important places include [Cruzeiro do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruzeiro_do_Sul,_Acre), [Sena Madureira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_Madureira), [Tarauacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarauac%C3%A1) and [Feijó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feij%C3%B3,_Acre). The state, which has 0.42% of the Brazilian population, generates 0.2% of the Brazilian [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP).[^cite_note-6]

Intense extractive activity in the rubber industry, which reached its height in the early 20th century, attracted Brazilians from many regions to the state. From the mixture of [sulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Region,_Brazil), [southeastern Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Region,_Brazil), [nordestino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Region,_Brazil), and [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) traditions arose a diverse cuisine.

[Fluvial transport](28769.md), concentrated on the [Juruá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juru%C3%A1_River) and [Moa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moa_River_\(Brazil\)) rivers, in the western part of the state, and the [Tarauacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarauac%C3%A1_River) and [Envira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envira_River) rivers in the northwest, is the principal form of transportation, especially between November and June. Heavy seasonal rains frequently make the [BR-364](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BR-364) impassable in those months; it usually connects Rio Branco to Cruzeiro do Sul.

## Etymology

The name, which was given to the territory in 1904, and to the state in 1962, is derived from one of the local rivers, perhaps originates from the [Tupi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupian_languages) word *a'kir ü* "green river", or from the form *a'kir*, of the Tupi word *ker*, "to sleep, to rest". It is believed more likely to be derived from *Aquiri*, a transliteration by European explorers of the term *Umákürü*, or *Uakiry*, from the Ipurinã dialect. Another hypothesis is that Acquiri derives from *Yasi'ri*, or *Ysi'ri*, meaning "flowing or swift water".

According to one account, agriculturist João Gabriel de Carvalho Melo wrote during an 1878 trip on the [Purús River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pur%C3%BAs_River) to merchant Viscount of Santo Elias (from Pará), asking him for goods to be sent to the "mouth of the Aquiri River". In [Belém](409573.md), the local merchant or his employees either misinterpreted Gabriel's handwriting, or he spelled the name wrong: the goods and invoice which Gabriel received were marked as having been sent to the [Acre River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_River).

Acre possesses some nicknames: the End of Brazil, The Rubber Tree State, the Latex State (from when it was a center of rubber production) and the Western End.

The native inhabitants of Acre are called *acr**i**ano**s***, in the singular *acr**i**an**o***. Until the entry according to the [Orthographic Agreement of 1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthographic_Agreement_of_1990), the correct spelling was *acr**e**an**o*** in the [singular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammatical_number) and in the plural *acr**e**ano**s***. In 2009, with the new orthographic agreement, the change generated controversy between the Academy of Letters of Acre (Academia Acreana de Letras) and the Brazilian Academy of Letters ([Academia Brasileira de Letras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academia_Brasileira_de_Letras)). The latter said that the change would mean the denial of the state's historical and cultural roots, by changing the last letter of the toponym from "[E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E)" to "[I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I)".

## Geography



Climate types of Acre

The state of Acre occupies an area of 152,581 km<sup>2</sup> (58,912 sq mi) (58,911 mi<sup>2</sup>) in the extreme west of Brazil. It is located at [70º west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70th_meridian_west) [longitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longitude) and at [9º south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_parallel_south) [latitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude). In Brazil, the state is part of the [North Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Region,_Brazil), forming borders with the states of [Amazonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonas_\(Brazilian_state\)) and [Rondônia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rond%C3%B4nia), and with two countries: [Peru](170691.md) and [Bolivia](3462.md).

Practically all of the [terrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrain) of the state of Acre is part of the low [sandstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) [plateau](538005.md), or [terra firme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terra_firme?action=edit\&redlink=1), [morphological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geomorphology) unit which dominates most of the Brazilian [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Basin). These [terranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terranes) rise, in Acre, from the [southeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_direction#Additional_points) to the [northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinal_direction#Additional_points), with very tabular [topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography) in general. In the extreme west is found the [Serra da Contamana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_da_Contamana?action=edit\&redlink=1) or Serra do Divisor, along the western [border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border), with the highest altitudes in the state (609 m; 1,998 ft). About 63% of the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Brazil)'s [surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface_area) lies between 200 and 300 m (660 and 980 ft) in height; 16% between 300 and 609 (984 and 1,998 ft); and 21% between 200 and 135 (656 and 443 ft).

The [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate) is [hot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperature) and very [humid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid), of the *Am* type in the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) system, and the monthly average [temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperatures) vary between 24 and 27 °C (75 and 81 °F), being the lowest average of the North Region. The rainfall reaches an annual total of 2,100 mm (83 in), with a clear [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season) in the months of June, July, and August.

Map of Acre showing tree-cover loss years, with forest remaining since 2000 in green and loss years shaded yellow through purple.

Tree-cover loss year in Acre, 2001-2024, from the [Global Forest Change dataset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Forest_Change_dataset).

The [Amazon rainforest](48139.md) covers all of the state territory. Very rich in [rubber trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber_trees) of the most valuable species (*Hevea brasiliensis*) and Brazil nut trees (*Bertholletia excelsa*), the forest guarantees that Acre is the greatest national producer of [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber) and [nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_\(fruit\)). Acre's principal rivers, mostly navigable during the wet season (the [Juruá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juru%C3%A1), [Tarauacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarauac%C3%A1_River), [Envira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envira_River), [Purús](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pur%C3%BAs_River), [Iaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iaco_River), and [Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_River)), cross the state with almost parallel courses which converge only outside of its territory.

The largest recorded [Black Caiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Caiman), measured at 7.7 metres (25 feet) and weighing 1,310 kg (2,890 lb), was shot in Acre in 1965.



The [Môa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B4a_River), at the [Serra do Divisor National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serra_do_Divisor_National_Park), [Juruá River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juru%C3%A1_River) Valley, Western Acre

The Amazon represents over half of the planet's remaining [rainforests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainforest) and comprises the largest and most species-rich tract of [tropical rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest) in the world. Wet tropical forests are the most species-rich [biome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome), and tropical forests in the Americas are consistently more species-rich than the wet forests in Africa and Asia.[^cite_note-Turner_2001-7] As the largest tract of tropical rainforest in the Americas, the Amazonian rainforests have unparalleled [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity). More than 1/3 of all species in the world live in the Amazon Rainforest.[^cite_note-8]

### Pre-Columbian era

The region of present-day Acre is thought to have been inhabited by [Pre-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian) civilizations since at least 2,100 years ago. Evidence includes complex [geoglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoglyph) of this age found in the area. The natives who crafted them are believed to have had a relatively advanced knowledge of this technology. Since at least the early 15th century, the region has been inhabited by peoples who spoke [Panoan languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panoan_languages); their territory was geographically close to that of the [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inca).

### Bolivian–Peruvian rule

In the mid-18th century, the region was [colonized by the Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas) and became part of the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru). Following the [Peruvian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_War_of_Independence) and [Bolivian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_War_of_Independence) wars of independence, which ended in 1826, the region and large portions around it became part of both Peru and Bolivia respectively, but independent of Spain and both states disputed the territory. It was a territory of the short-lived [Peru–Bolivian Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru%E2%80%93Bolivian_Confederation) (1836–1839), until the two countries separated and most of the region returned to Bolivian control.

The discovery of [rubber tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hevea_brasiliensis) groves in the region in the mid-19th century attracted numerous immigrants, especially from Brazil and Europe, seeking to build on the rubber boom. Despite the increased numbers of Brazilians, the [Treaty of Ayacucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ayacucho) (1867) determined that the region belonged to Bolivia. By 1877, Acre's population was composed almost entirely of Brazilians coming from the [Northeast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Region,_Brazil).

### Brazilian acquisition

In 1899, Brazilian settlers in Acre declared an independent state known as the [Republic of Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Acre). Bolivia attempted to reassert control, but Brazilian resistance led to armed clashes, culminating in the [Acre War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_War). On 17 November 1903, the [Treaty of Petrópolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Petr%C3%B3polis) was signed, transferring final possession of the region to Brazil. Acre was incorporated as a Brazilian territory divided into three departments. Brazil acquired the land for two million pounds sterling, with the territory taken from [Mato Grosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mato_Grosso) under terms that also facilitated the construction of the Madeira-Mamoré railway.

Once the Acre War was over, Peru did not recognize the annexation of Acre until 1909, with the Velarde-Río Branco Treaty, where the borders between Peru and Brazil were defined. It was signed in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), on September 8, 1909, by the Minister of Foreign Affairs of Brazil, [Baron of Rio Branco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Paranhos,_Baron_of_Rio_Branco), and the plenipotentiary minister of Peru in Brazil, Hernán Velarde, by President [Augusto B. Leguía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_B._Legu%C3%ADa) (first government) then ruled in Peru, and in Brazil by the president [Nilo Pecanha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilo_Pecanha), in order to solve the border problems between Peru and Brazil.

Note the differences from current western borders in the areas of [Pantanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pantanal) and the [Amazon basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_basin) — including the lack of Acre's territory, then still part of [Bolivia](3462.md)

Acre was united in 1920. On June 15, 1962, it was elevated to the category of state, and was the first to be governed by a woman, [Iolanda Fleming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iolanda_Fleming), a teacher. During the early twentieth century, rubber seedlings were taken to [Southeast Asia](28741.md), where competitive plantations were established, reducing the importance of the Amazon in production. During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), Japanese forces took over the rubber tree groves of [British Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Malaya).

Acre was called on to produce rubber for the Allied war effort. The [Rubber Soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber_Soldiers), natives mostly of the Ceará plantation, increased production and provided critical supplies to the Allies. Acre's decisive contribution to the Allied victory may have helped Brazil attract North American investment to form the National Steel Company (Companhia Siderúrgica Nacional) in the postwar era. This company aided in the industrialization of the Central-south, which did not yet possess basic heavy industries.

On April 4, 2008, Acre won a judicial debate with the state of [Amazonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonas_\(Brazilian_state\)) in relation to the dispute surrounding the Cunha Gomes Line. It annexed part of the municipalities of [Envira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Envira), [Guajará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guajar%C3%A1), [Boca do Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_do_Acre), [Pauini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauini), [Eirunepé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eirunep%C3%A9) and [Ipixuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipixuna). The territorial redefinition consolidated the incorporation of 1.2 million hectares of the Liberdade, Gregório, and Mogno forest complex to the territory of Acre, which corresponds to 11,583.87 km<sup>2</sup> (4,472.56 sq mi).

#### Initial settlement



Geoglyphs in the Amazon rainforest, Acre

Since the 1970s, numerous [geoglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_geoglyphs), major geometric earthworks, have been discovered on deforested land in Acre, and dated to between 1–1250 AD. These are cited as evidence of complex [Pre-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era) societies.[^cite_note-Simon_Romero-9][^cite_note-10] The [BBC](19344654.md)'s *[Unnatural Histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unnatural_Histories_\(TV_series\))* explored studies of this area, concluding that the [Amazon rainforest](48139.md), rather than being a pristine "[wilderness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilderness)", has been shaped by man for at least 11,000 years. Traditional ancient practices included [forest gardening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_gardening).[^cite_note-unnatural-histories-11] Ondemar Dias is credited as the first to discover the geoglyphs in 1977. Alceu Ranzi expanded their findings by flying over Acre.[^cite_note-unnatural-histories-11][^cite_note-12]

During the 17th century, Portuguese expeditions reached many of the far ends of present-day Brazil. The expansion of the exploration to the west followed, and they reached lands under control by the Spanish colonies. The two nations negotiated to establish their territories, under the Treaties of [Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Madrid_\(13_January_1750\)) (1750) and [San Ildefonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Treaty_of_San_Ildefonso) (1777).[^cite_note-13] Both of the treaties were based on the explorations of Portuguese *bandeirante* [Manoel Félix de Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manoel_F%C3%A9lix_de_Lima?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [Guaporé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guapor%C3%A9_River) and [Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira_River) river basins. The treaties established the riverbeds of the [Mamoré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamor%C3%A9) and Guaporé to their maximum western limits on the left bank of the [Javari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javari) as the border between the Spanish and Portuguese territories.

The Portuguese created the new royal [captaincy of Mato Grosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_of_Mato_Grosso?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1751), stimulating settlement toward the frontier. New centers developed: [Vila Bela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vila_Bela?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1752)[^cite_note-14] on the banks of the Guaporé, [Vila Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vila_Maria) (1778)[^cite_note-15] on the [Paraguay River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguay_River), and [Casalvasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casalvasco?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1783).[^cite_note-16] Until the mid-19th century, there was little effort to settle the area systematically. At that time, the great virgin source of rubber attracted commercial interest, and development followed.

The empire was directed towards agricultural exports, based on [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) as the most important commodity. The territories of the extreme west were unknown and usually overlooked. For example, although [Cândido Mendes de Almeida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A2ndido_Mendes_de_Almeida)'s *Atlas of the Empire of Brazil* (1868), was considered a model of its time, geographers knew nothing of the Acre River and its principal tributaries, which did not appear at all in the atlas.

Some few armed bands of Brazilian explorers exploited the rural and unpopulated region,[^cite_note-17] not knowing and little interested in whether they were "controlled" by Brazil, [Peru](170691.md), or [Bolivia](3462.md).

But the rubber boom of the mid-19th century, stimulated exploration by various expeditions to survey this resource and develop a plan for colonial settlement. At that time, João Rodrigues Cametá initiated the conquest of the [Purús River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pur%C3%BAs_River);[^cite_note-SILVA-18] Manuel Urbano da Encarnação, an Indian with extensive knowledge of the region, reached the [Acre River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_River), traveling up it as far as the vicinity of the [Xapuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xapuri);[^cite_note-SILVA-18] and João da Cunha Correia reached the [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) of the upper [Tarauacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarauac%C3%A1).[^cite_note-19] For the most part, these expeditions took place on [Bolivian](3462.md) land.

Exploitative activities, the industrial importance of the rubber reserves, and the penetration of Brazilian colonists in the region raised the attention of [Bolivia](3462.md), which solicited a better fixation of boundaries. After much failed negotiation, in 1867 the [Treaty of Ayacucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Ayacucho) was signed, which recognized the colonial *[uti possidetis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uti_possidetis)*, or use of that territory by Brazil.[^cite_note-20] A border was established parallel to the confluence of the [Beni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beni_River) and [Mamoré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamor%C3%A9) rivers, running eastward to the headwaters of the [Javari River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javari_River), even though the source of this river was not yet known.

#### Northeast occupation

As the price of rubber rose in the market,[^cite_note-21] the demand for it grew. The race to the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River) increased. [Plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantation) multiplied in the valleys of the Acre, [Purús](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pur%C3%BAs_River) and, farther west, the [Tarauacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarauac%C3%A1). In the year 1873–1874, in the [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) of the Purús, the population rose from around one thousand to four thousand inhabitants. The Brazilian imperial government, already sensitive to the resulting offerings of [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber), considered the entire valley of the Purús to be Brazilian.

In the second half of the 19th century, disturbances were registered in the demographic and geo-economic balance of the empire. The coffee boom in the south attracted financial resources and workers, to detriment of the northeast.[^cite_note-FURTADO-22] The growing impoverishment of that region stimulated migratory waves to the states of [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), [Minas Gerais](222651.md), and [São Paulo](390875.md).[^cite_note-FURTADO-22] The movement of population became particularly active during the prolonged drought of the [northeastern interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_interior?action=edit\&redlink=1), from 1877 to 1880. Hundreds of [Ceará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cear%C3%A1) indigenous people headed for the rubber plantations in search of work.[^cite_note-TOSCANO-23]

The Cearense migration reached the banks of the [Juruá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juru%C3%A1)[^cite_note-24] and accelerated the occupation of land which [Bolivia](3462.md) would later reclaim. The great fluvial rivers and their [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) systems were full of small ship fleets transporting colonists, goods, and supply material to the most isolated centers. The governments of [Amazonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonas_\(Brazilian_state\)) and [Pará](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Par%C3%A1) quickly established *supply houses*, which financed various types of operations, guaranteed credit, and promoted the commercial incentive of the [rubber tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber_tree) groves.[^cite_note-25]

The [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber) race had the frantic urgency of [gold rushes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_rush) of the 18th century.[^cite_note-GURGEL-26] The situation drew the attention of the government to the economic use and development of an almost completely unknown area. The activities of private businesses would enable the government to incorporate the new region.

#### Land dispute

In 1890, [José Manuel Pando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Pando), a Bolivian official, alerted his government to the fact that more than three hundred rubber plantations had been developed in the Jura basin, and most were occupied by [Brazilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilians) on what was nominally [Bolivian](3462.md) territory.[^cite_note-27] The Brazilian penetration had advanced west from the 64th meridian to beyond the 72nd, in an extension of one thousand kilometers, despite the borders having been established. The [Treaty of 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_1867?action=edit\&redlink=1) limited Brazil to land above the confluence of the [Beni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beni_River) and [Mamoré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamor%C3%A9) rivers.[^cite_note-28]

In 1895 a new commission to define the borders was created.[^cite_note-GURGEL-26] The Brazilian representative, Gregório Taumaturgo de Azevedo, resigned after verifying that the ratification of the [Treaty of 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_1867?action=edit\&redlink=1) would harm the Brazilian rubber gatherers already settled in Bolivian territory.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] In 1899, the [Bolivians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivians) established an administrative post in [Puerto Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Alonso?action=edit\&redlink=1), exacting taxes and customs duties upon Brazilian activities.[^cite_note-PORTOACRE-31] The following year, Brazil accepted the sovereignty of [Bolivia](3462.md) in the zone, when it officially recognized the old boundaries at the confluence of the [Beni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beni_River) and [Mamoré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamor%C3%A9) rivers.

Distant from the diplomatic process, the rubber workers judged their interests to have been cheated, and initiated insurrection movements. Some of this was in response to brutal treatment and abuse by forces managed by the major rubber companies. In the same year that Bolivia established administration in [Puerto Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Alonso?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1899), two serious uprisings occurred.[^cite_note-PORTOACRE-31]

In April, a [Cearense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cear%C3%A1) lawyer, José Carvalho, led an armed movement, which culminated in the expulsion of the Bolivian authorities.[^cite_note-GURGEL-26] Shortly thereafter, Bolivia began negotiations with an Anglo-American trust, the *Bolivian Syndicate*, in order to promote, with exceptional force (exacting of taxes, armed force), the political and economic incorporation of Acre into its territory.[^cite_note-GURGEL-26] The governor of [Amazonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonas_\(Brazilian_state\)), [Ramalho Júnior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramalho_J%C3%BAnior?action=edit\&redlink=1), informed of the agreement by a functionary of the Bolivian consulate in [Belém](409573.md), [Luis Gálvez Rodríguez de Arias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_G%C3%A1lvez_Rodr%C3%ADguez_de_Arias), sent military contingents forward to occupy [Puerto Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Alonso?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-GURGEL-26] Gálvez proclaimed the independence of Acre, in the form of a republic. He became its president with the acquiescence of the rubber gatherers. Under protests from Bolivia, President [Campos Sales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campos_Sales) abolished the ephemeral republic (March 1900).[^cite_note-GURGEL-26]

Bolivians, reinstated in the region, suffered in 1900 from the assault of the so-called Floriano Peixoto expedition, or "expedition of the poets".[^cite_note-SCHILLING-32] It was made up of intellectual bohemians from Manaus.[^cite_note-SCHILLING-32] Following brief fighting in the area surrounding Puerto Alonso, the expedition was completely scattered.[^cite_note-SCHILLING-32] Ultimately, the Bolivian government signed a contract with the *Bolivian Syndicate* (July 1901).[^cite_note-GURGEL-26] The Brazilian congress, shocked by the arbitrariness of the act, took measures, canceling commercial accords and navigation between the two countries, and suspending the right of travel to Bolivia.

At the same time, Brazilians organized a large armed assault on the disputed area.[^cite_note-GURGEL-26] The operations were led by a former student of the [Military School of Rio Grande do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_School_of_Rio_Grande_do_Sul?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([Escola Militar do Rio Grande do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escola_Militar_do_Rio_Grande_do_Sul?action=edit\&redlink=1)), [José Plácido de Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Pl%C3%A1cido_de_Castro). The rubber gatherers occupied the village of [Xapuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xapuri) in Alto Acre (August 1902), and took Bolivian officials into custody.[^cite_note-GURGEL-26] Finally, Plácido de Castro's forces besieged Puerto Alonso, proclaiming the Independent State of Acre, after the capitulation of Bolivian troops (February 1903).[^cite_note-GURGEL-26]

#### Diplomatic intervention



Revolução Square (Praça Plácido de Castro) in the capital, [Rio Branco](818818.md)

[José Plácido de Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Pl%C3%A1cido_de_Castro) was proclaimed governor of the new [Independent State of Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Acre),[^cite_note-33] and he had to discuss the question of borders in the diplomatic sphere.[^cite_note-34] The [Baron of Rio Branco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baron_of_Rio_Branco), who had just assumed the role of Brazil's [Minister of External Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_External_Relations_\(Brazil\)),[^cite_note-35] immediately opened channels which were meant to have put an end to the question.

The simplest problem, with the *Bolivian Syndicate*, was resolved by Brazil paying one hundred and ten thousand [pounds](270673.md) to renounce the contract (February 1903).[^cite_note-36] Next, commercial relations were reestablished with Bolivia,[^cite_note-GURGEL-26] while a part of the territory on the upper Purús and Juruá, militarily occupied in March 1903, was declared litigious.

Bolivia finally agreed to cede to Brazil an area of 142,800 km<sup>2</sup> (55,100 sq mi), in exchange for two million [pounds sterling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pounds_sterling), paid in two installments.[^cite_note-GURGEL-26] Brazil committed to the construction of a [Madeira-Mamoré Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeira-Mamor%C3%A9_Railway), connecting Porto Velho to Guajará-Mirim, at the confluence of the [Beni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beni_River) and Madeira rivers.[^cite_note-GURGEL-26] These actions were ratified in the [Treaty of Petrópolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Petr%C3%B3polis) (November 17, 1903), through which Brazil acquired the future territory, now state of Acre.[^cite_note-GURGEL-26]

[Peru](170691.md) had also claimed sovereignty over the entire territory of Acre and part of the state of [Amazonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonas_\(Brazilian_state\)), based on historic colonial titles.[^cite_note-37] After armed conflicts between [Brazilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilians) and [Peruvians](8796244.md) on the upper Purús and Juruá, a joint administration was established in those regions (1904).[^cite_note-TOSCANO-23] The studies to determine the borders proceeded until the end of 1909, when a treaty was signed that completed the political integration of Acre into Brazilian territory.

#### Development from territory to statehood



&#x20;The Sociedade Recreativa Tentâmen, in [Rio Branco](818818.md), built in 1924, with [Rural Gothic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpenter_Gothic), was, for many years, a symbol of Acre's [bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemianism), a reflection of the prosperous years from the [rubber cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_rubber_cycle)

Exercising a prominent role in national exports until 1913,[^cite_note-38] when rubber was introduced to European and North American markets, Acre enjoyed a [period of great prosperity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_rubber_cycle). At the start of the 20th century, in a period of less than ten years, it grew to have more than 50,000 inhabitants.

From 1946 on, the federal government undertook actions to revive the economy of the [Amazon Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Basin), and to include it in [regional development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_development) projects.

Attending to the judicial arrangements of the [Treaty of Petrópolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Petr%C3%B3polis), President [Rodrigues Alves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigues_Alves) sanctioned the law which created the Territory of Acre (1904),[^cite_note-39] further dividing it into three departments: **Alto Acre**, **Alto Purús**, and **Alto Juruá**, the latter being separated to form **Alto Tarauacá** (1912). The departmental administration was exercised until 1921 by mayors appointed by the [President of Brazil](273638.md).[^cite_note-40] At that time the arrangements were altered, passing the administration to a governor. The second [Constitution of Brazil](26847507.md) (1934) conceded to Acre the right to elect representatives to the [National Congress of Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Congress_of_Brazil).[^cite_note-41]



Members of an [uncontacted tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncontacted_peoples) encountered in 2008, at the [Brazil–Peru border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil%E2%80%93Peru_border)

During the [Estado Novo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_Novo_\(Brazil\)) (New State) political ideas involving the valorization of the interior took hold, with the intention of promoting the articulation of more isolated areas. Thereafter, the vote of 1946 commended the channeling of budgetary resources from the Union to the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Basin), determining that the [Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territory_\(administrative_division\)) of Acre would be elevated to the condition of [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Brazil) as soon as its revenue reached the equivalent of the lowest state tax exaction.[^cite_note-42]

In the 1960s, the second cycle of efforts to accelerate the progress of the Amazon area was begun with the Superintendency of the Development of the Amazon ([Superintendência do Desenvolvimento da Amazônia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superintend%C3%AAncia_do_Desenvolvimento_da_Amaz%C3%B4nia) or SUDAM, 1966).[^cite_note-43] Better networking of regional sub-sectors within the state was sought out, thus connecting the branch lines of the [Transamazônica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transamaz%C3%B4nica?action=edit\&redlink=1), which connected [Rio Branco](818818.md) and [Brasiléia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brasil%C3%A9ia), on the upper course of the [Acre River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_River), and [Cruzeiro do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruzeiro_do_Sul,_Acre), on the banks of the Juruá, crossing the valleys of the Purús and the Tarauacá. Planning politics developed, therefore, destined to correct the demographic, economic, and political distortions of national integration.

### Airports

Rio Branco International Bus Station

[Rio Branco International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Branco_International_Airport) is located in a rural zone of the municipality of Rio Branco, in the state of Acre. It was opened on November 2, 1999, with a unique characteristic: it moved 22 kilometers away from the previous airport site. Rio Branco Airport serves domestic and international flights (by scheduled carriers and air taxi firms) along with general and military aviation. The terminal can receive 270 thousand passengers a year and serves an average of 14 daily operations.

- [Cruzeiro do Sul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruzeiro_do_Sul,_Acre)

[Cruzeiro do Sul International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruzeiro_do_Sul_International_Airport) is located 18 kilometers away from the city center, which helps access to the Alto Juruá region. It was opened on October 28, 1970, and absorbed by [Infraero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infraero) on March 31, 1980. The airport infrastructure was built in 1976 by the municipal government. In 1994, the runway was completely renovated.

There are domestic airports at [Tarauacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarauac%C3%A1_Airport), [Sena Madureira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sena_Madureira) and [Brasiléia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brasil%C3%A9ia).

## Culture



[Yawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaminaw%C3%A1) festival in [Tarauacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarauac%C3%A1)

[^cite_note-53]

Despite gaining statehood through [war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_War), Acre's culture is close to that of other [Northern Brazilian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Region,_Brazil) states, alongside a high affinity for Northeastern Brazilian culture.&#x20;

In [Rio Branco](818818.md), there is a religious community of typical Acre indigenous origin, called Alto Santo (Universal Christian Enlightenment Center) that practices the [Santo Daime Ritual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Daime) with Daime, a natural tea made with leaves and vines, which indigenous tradition understands as a way of approaching God. The ritual involves drinking the tea, wearing uniforms, and singing hymnals.[^cite_note-54]

The best-known dish consumed by Acreans is called [Baixaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baixaria), which combines duck, [tucupi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucupi), cassava flour and [pirarucu fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arapaima_gigas) with a wide variety of Amazonian fruits traditionally consumed by Acre's [Indigenous peoples](55510.md).[^cite_note-55]

Some famous people from Acre include [João Donato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo%C3%A3o_Donato), Sansão Campos Pereira, [Chico Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chico_Mendes), Sergio Souto and Tião Natureza.[^cite_note-56]

## Flag

See also: [Flag of Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Acre)

The flag was adopted on March 15, 1921. It is a variation of the flags used by the secessionist state of Acre, with the yellow and green parts exchanged and mirrored. The yellow color symbolizes peace, green hope, and the star symbolizes the light which guided those who worked to make Acre a state of Brazil.
