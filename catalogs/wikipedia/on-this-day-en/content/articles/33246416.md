The **National Eligibility cum Entrance Test (Undergraduate)** or **NEET (UG)** (pronunciation: "neat"), formerly known as the [All India Pre-Medical Test (AIPMT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Pre_Medical_Test), is an Indian nationwide [entrance examination](1795037.md) conducted by the [National Testing Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Testing_Agency) (NTA) for admission in undergraduate medical programs.[^cite_note-5] As a mandatory exam for admission in medical programs,[^cite_note-6] it is the largest exam in India in terms of number of applicants.[^cite_note-7]

Until 2012, the AIPMT was conducted by the [Central Board of Secondary Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Secondary_Education) (CBSE). In 2013, NEET-UG was introduced, conducted by CBSE, replacing AIPMT. However, due to legal challenges, NEET was temporarily replaced by AIPMT in both 2014 and 2015. In 2016, NEET was reintroduced and conducted by CBSE. From 2019 onwards, the NTA has been responsible for conducting the NEET exam.

After the enactment of NMC Act 2019 in September 2019, NEET-UG became the sole entrance test for admissions to medical colleges in India including the [All India Institutes of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Institutes_of_Medical_Sciences) (AIIMS) and [Jawaharlal Institute of Postgraduate Medical Education & Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Institute_of_Postgraduate_Medical_Education_and_Research) (JIPMER) which until then conducted separate exams.[^cite_note-8]

## History



Official website of NTA NEET 2025

Before NEET (UG) was implemented nationwide, states conducted their own entrance tests and some medical colleges like such as [AIIMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Institutes_of_Medical_Sciences), [JIPMER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Institute_of_Postgraduate_Medical_Education_and_Research), [IMS-BHU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_Medical_Sciences,_Banaras_Hindu_University) and [CMC Vellore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Medical_College_Vellore) conducted their own entrance exams.

NEET was initially proposed to take place from 2012 onwards.[^cite_note-9] However, for several reasons, the [CBSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Secondary_Education) and [Medical Council of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_Council_of_India) deferred NEET by a year.[^cite_note-10] The test was announced by the [Government of India](553883.md) and was held for the first time on 5 May 2013 across India for students seeking admission for both undergraduate and postgraduate [medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine).[^cite_note-11] On 18 July 2013, the Supreme Court ruled in favor of 115 petitions and cancelled the NEET exam and announced that the MCI could not interfere with the admission process done by colleges.

Following the announcement from the Medical Council of India that it would introduce the NEET-UG exam in 2012, several states including Andhra Pradesh, Karnataka, Gujarat, West Bengal and Tamil Nadu strongly opposed the change, stating that there was a huge variation in the syllabus proposed by the MCI and their state syllabi.[^cite_note-12]

Even though NEET 2016 is conducted in English and Hindi, it was announced that students can write exams in [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language), [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language), [Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language), [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), [Assamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assamese_language) and [Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_language) languages from 2017 onwards.[^cite_note-13] [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada) and [Odia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language) languages are added to the list so that students can write the exams in nine Indian languages and English.[^cite_note-14] The Supreme Court of India quashed the NEET for admissions into all medical and dental colleges on 18 July 2013. The apex court ruled that the Medical Council of India cannot conduct a unified examination.[^cite_note-15]

According to a 2013 announcement by CBSE,[^cite_note-16] CBSE planned to conduct AIPMT on 4 May 2014.[^cite_note-17] The final decision on NEET UG was planned to be taken after the verdict of the Supreme Court of India.

The Central Board of Secondary Education announces the results and the All India Merit List for NEET-UG. The merit list and the waiting list are prepared as per the directives of the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md), DGHS, MCI and DCI. The results for 2013 were announced on 5 June.[^cite_note-NEET-18]

NEET was declared illegal and unconstitutional by the Supreme Court of India in 2013. However, it was restored on 11 April 2016, after a five-judge Constitution bench recalled the earlier verdict and allowed the Central Government and the Medical Council of India (MCI) to implement the common entrance test until the court decides afresh on its validity.

**Phase One Test (2016)**

The All India Pre Medical Test, also known as AIPMT, held on 1 May 2016, was considered as the first phase of the NEET. Students who registered for Phase One were given a chance to appear for the next phase of NEET held on 24 July 2016, but with a condition that candidates have to give up their NEET Phase 1 score.[^cite_note-19] The above dates are as per the order of the Supreme Court.[^cite_note-20]

## Exam pattern and structure

There are a total of 180 questions asked in the exam, 45 questions each from [Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics), [Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemistry), [Botany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botany) and [Zoology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology). Each correct response fetches 4 marks and each incorrect response gets −1 negative marking. The exam duration is 3 hours (180 min). The exam is of 720 marks (maximum marks).[^cite_note-u660-21]

## Syllabus

NEET (UG) syllabus consists of the core concepts of Physics, Chemistry and Biology taught in classes 11 and 12 as prescribed by the [NCERT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_of_Educational_Research_and_Training).[^cite_note-22]

## Organizing body

The [National Testing Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Testing_Agency) (NTA) has been made the nodal agency for conduct of all India competitive exams and conducts NEET (UG) from 2019. The [Central Board of Secondary Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Secondary_Education) conducted NEET between 2013 and 2018 before the setting of NTA.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

## Opposition and criticism

The introduction of NEET has received opposition from some entities, most notably the state of [Tamil Nadu](29918.md). The bulk of the major political parties represented in the state, including the [AIADMK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Anna_Dravida_Munnetra_Kazhagam) and the ruling [DMK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dravida_Munnetra_Kazhagam) have expressed resistance to the test on multiple grounds.[^cite_note-25] Notably many of these states had regional State Boards which taught slightly different materials in the 11th and 12th grade posing a significant disadvantage for aspirants.[^cite_note-26]

Albert P'Rayan writing for *[The Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hindu)* criticized NEET for introducing a Unified Standard Testing Examination that has encouraged the emergence of various [coaching institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cram_school) in India which end up favoring the rich, urban and of those part of the historical Indian upper caste. A 2023 report that interviewed 38 of the top 50 in NEET-2023, revealed that all but one had undergone some level of coaching. Among the 50, 29 belonged to the general category and 37 of the 50 also studied in urban area schools and reported investing significant amounts of money in coaching.[^cite_note-P'Rayan-27] To address these disparities, initiatives like the [National Level Common Entrance Examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Level_Common_Entrance_Examination) (NLCEE) stepped in, offering free online coaching during the COVID-19 pandemic.[^cite_note-28]

NEET-UG has also been criticized for not setting up a minimum qualifying percentage for each individual subject. A 2017 report that analyzed the results of 1,990 students who got admitted to MBBS with 0, single digit and negative marks in individual subjects through heavy donations.[^cite_note-29]

## Exam cutoff and reservation

Each year in the NEET exam, a substantial number of candidates *qualify* by meeting the minimum percentile-based criteria set by the NTA which only makes them eligible for counselling. However, the number of MBBS seats available is far lower: only about 60,000 seats for government medical college and 58,000 seats for private medical colleges in India. This causes a steep gap: many who *qualify* according to the eligibility criteria still do not get admission because the actual cutoff for admission into a college (based on marks, category, rank and seat availability) is much higher than the qualifying cutoff. This means only about 2.73% of students actually get a government sponsored medical seat.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

Many private medical seats go unfilled each year because of extremely high education fee, even though millions of students *qualify* NEET. While government medical colleges charge around ₹7,000 (US\$73) to ₹6 lakh (US\$6,200), private MBBS fee can go upwards of ₹1 crore (US\$100,000).&#x20;

Over 1,700 private medical seats in Uttar Pradesh alone were left vacant during the mop-up round solely due to unaffordable fee, lack of educational loans and government funding; also considering the socio-economic condition of Indian population, it is extremely difficult to cash a private medical seat.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

## Colleges and medical seats

As per the orders of the Supreme Court and the [NMC Act 2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medical_Commission), a single medical entrance exam is conducted all over India for admission into medical and dental colleges. Colleges and universities will not be able to conduct their own medical entrance exam and will accept students based on the All India Rank obtained in NEET-UG. After the declaration of the results, an All India Rank (AIR) is allotted to each candidate and a merit list is released.

The [National Medical Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Medical_Commission)[^cite_note-52] conducts counselling (allotment of seats according to merit and candidate choice) for 15% state seats, central institutes and deemed universities. The counselling for remaining 85% state quota seats and private colleges is done by the medical boards of respective states. States prepare their separate merit list on basis of NEET-UG results. The total number of seats offered combining Gov. and under NEET as of 2024 are 196,515 (109,170[^cite_note-NMCSeats-53] for [MBBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Medicine,_Bachelor_of_Surgery), 28,088 for [BDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dental_degree), 52,720 for [AYUSH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Ayush) and 6537 for [BVSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Veterinary_Science)).[^cite_note-Gohain2022-38]

## Controversies

The NEET examination has been criticised for harbouring institutionalised corruption and accusations reached a pinnacle in the 2024 examination year. Investigations where undetaken by the [CBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Investigation) which reported "no systemic or institutional corruption involving the exam body itself, but rather serious procedural negligence". The CBI however has itself been repeatedly accused of political bias, internal corruption, and eroded credibility. The NTA body was also criticised for its opacity.[^cite_note-57]

### 2024 controversy

Main article: [2024 NEET controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NEET_controversy)

#### Paper leak

The exam paper for the 2024 NEET-UG exam was allegedly leaked a day before the exam in Patna, Bihar. On 5 May 2024, the day of the exam, an [FIR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_information_report) was filed at the Shastri Nagar police station in Patna. According to police officials, the paper leak gang allegedly charged ₹30 lakh (US\$31,000) to ₹50 lakh (US\$52,000) from several candidates, providing them with the question papers, claimed to be those of NEET-UG, a day prior for memorization. The police have arrested several suspects in connection with the paper leak.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

On 22 June, the NTA chief, Subodh Kumar Singh, was removed from his position. The [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(India\)) handed over the case related to the leak to the [Central Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Investigation) (CBI).[^cite_note-60]

The CBI, on 18 July has carried out various arrests including four MBBS students from [AIIMS Patna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Institute_of_Medical_Sciences,_Patna) and a civil Engineering student from [NIT Jamshedpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Technology,_Jamshedpur) (Pankaj Kumar) in connection to the paper leak and solving of the leaked paper.[^cite_note-61] On 19 July, a first year MBBS student (Surabhi Kumari) from [RIMS Ranchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_Institute_of_Medical_Sciences) who was allegedly involved in solving the paper.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-zeenews.india.com-63]

The [CBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Investigation) so far has arrested 36 persons in relation to the [NEET Paper leak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NEET_controversy) case.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-zeenews.india.com-63]

#### Discrepancies in result

The results of the examination were declared on 4 June 2024, ten days earlier than the previously scheduled date. A total of 67 students achieved a perfect score, a significantly higher number than in previous examinations, which raised controversy. The [National Testing Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Testing_Agency) (NTA), the examination organizer, clarified that the higher number of top scorers was due to 44 of the 67 students answering a physics question incorrectly but still being awarded marks because of a mistake in the [NCERT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_of_Educational_Research_and_Training) book.[^cite_note-iecontro-65][^cite_note-66]

Several examinees received scores of 718 or 719, which students argued was mathematically impossible under the exam marking scheme. The NTA later clarified that 1,563 examinees, including six toppers from the same center,[^cite_note-hindustan1-67] were awarded compensatory marks for lost time.[^cite_note-68] Examinees at some centers in Haryana, Delhi, and Chhattisgarh complained that they were given less than the allotted time to complete their examination and filed writ petitions before the High Courts of [Punjab & Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_and_Haryana_High_Court), [Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_High_Court), and [Chhattisgarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhattisgarh_High_Court).[^cite_note-iecontro-65] However, after the intervention of the [Supreme Court](395852.md), the NTA decided to withdraw the grace marks and hold the examination again for those 1,563 candidates on 23 June 2024.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-hindustan1-67] Out of these, 813 students took the retest.[^cite_note-70]

#### Petitions filed in the Supreme Court

Several petitions were filed in the High Courts of different states and the Supreme Court of India, including one by [Alakh Pandey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alakh_Pandey), co-founder of the [ed-tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_technology) company [Physics Wallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physics_Wallah).[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] On 13 June 2024, the Supreme Court of India allowed the cancellation of the scorecards of the 1,563 candidates who were given grace marks and ordered a re-test for those candidates.

A Bench of Chief Justice of India [Dhananjaya Yeshwant Chandrachud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhananjaya_Y._Chandrachud) with Justices [Jamshed Burjor Pardiwala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._B._Pardiwala) and [Manoj Misra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manoj_Misra) dealt with the matter.[^cite_note-73]

On 8 July, the CJI led bench of the Supreme court ordered the [CBI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Investigation) Investigation officer to file an Investigation report and scheduled the next hearing for July 11 which was later postponed to 18 July.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

The petitions requesting re NEET were heard on 8 July, 11 July, 22 July as well as 23 July. The [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) led by [Chief Justice Dhananjaya Yeshwant Chandrachud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhananjaya_Y._Chandrachud) upheld the examinations and said that there was no indication of a widespread leak all over the country.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

### 2026 controversy

Main article: [2026 NEET controversy](83200188.md)

The 2026 examination was held on 3 May 2026. On May 11, it was reported that the [Rajasthan Special Operations Group (SOG)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajasthan_Police) had started an investigation after a "guess paper" circulated in Sikar was found to be very similar to the actual exam. According to reports, questions worth nearly 600 out of 720 marks matched the circulated material. While investigations are ongoing, the National Testing Agency (NTA) stated they are looking into these claims of paper leak and malpractice.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

On 12 May 2026, the examination that was held on 3 May was cancelled[^cite_note-\:0-80]. The exam was reconducted on 21 June 2026.[^cite_note-81] The leaked paper was circulated to a large number of students on social media platforms like Whatsapp, that made it difficult to trace who benefited from the leaked paper.[^cite_note-82] The issue was formally turned over to the [Central Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Investigation) for a thorough investigation. Initial investigations revealed that the leak may have occurred during the printing of the examination papers.[^cite_note-\:0-80]

## Computer-based exam

On 15 May 2026, the Union education minister [Dharmendra Pradhan](7119332.md) announced that the examination would be conducted in a fully computer-based test (CBT) mode from the following year (2027), marking a major reform to the national medical entrance examination system.[^cite_note-83]
