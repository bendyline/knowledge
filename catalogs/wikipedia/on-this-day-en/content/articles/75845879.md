The final match of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md)—the 23rd edition of the [FIFA](11049.md) competition for men's national [soccer](10568.md) teams—was played at [MetLife Stadium](5476983.md) in [East Rutherford, New Jersey](136878.md), part of the [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area), on July 19, 2026. It was contested by [Spain](609825.md) and defending champion [Argentina](454699.md).&#x20;

The tournament was contested by hosts [Canada](723688.md), [Mexico](808402.md), and the [United States](582488.md), along with 45 other teams who emerged from [the qualification phase](49459509.md), which was organized by the six [FIFA confederations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_federations#Continental). The 48 teams competed in a group stage, from which 32 teams qualified for the [knockout stage](81667005.md). En route to the final, Spain finished first in [Group H](81660199.md) with two wins and a draw, before defeating [Austria](695226.md) in the round of 32, [Portugal](576328.md) in the round of 16, [Belgium](679716.md) in the quarterfinal, and [France](250197.md) in the semifinal. Argentina finished first in [Group J](81660202.md) with three wins, after which it defeated [Cape Verde](1205575.md) in the round of 32, [Egypt](1022953.md) in the round of 16, [Switzerland](681862.md) in the quarterfinal, and [England](9904.md) in the semifinal. The final took place in front of 80,663 spectators, and was [refereed](501260.md) by [Slavko Vinčić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavko_Vin%C4%8Di%C4%87) of Slovenia.&#x20;

Spain dominated possession and created numerous goal-scoring opportunities throughout the match, with Argentina goalkeeper [Emiliano Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emiliano_Mart%C3%ADnez) repeatedly denying its offensive efforts. Argentina, reduced to ten players after [Enzo Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Fern%C3%A1ndez) was [sent off](3090014.md#Red_card_\(dismissal\)) in the second half, failed to register a single shot in [regulation time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#Regulation_time), a first for a World Cup final. The goalless deadlock carried into [extra time](633242.md), during which [Ferran Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferran_Torres) scored from a [Nico Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_Williams) header to secure a 1–0 victory for Spain. With a few minutes left, Argentina missed multiple opportunities to equalize the score. After the match, a brawl broke out between the two teams.&#x20;

Spain's win earned the country its [second World Cup title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_finals) and its first since [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_final). Torres was named the [man of the match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_of_the_match), and Emiliano Martínez's 11 saves set a record for the most saves by a goalkeeper in a World Cup final. The match was the first ever World Cup final to include a [halftime show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_final_halftime_show), which caused the full halftime break to last over 27 minutes, against [International Football Association Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Football_Association_Board) (IFAB) protocol. Spain became the first country to simultaneously hold the men's and women's World Cup titles, having won the [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) in [2023](45271353.md).

## Background



The *Trionda Final* ball used in the final.

The [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) was the 23rd edition of the [World Cup](11370.md), [FIFA](11049.md)'s [soccer](10568.md) competition for men's national teams, held in Canada, Mexico and the United States between June 11 and July 19, 2026.[^cite_note-Britanica-1][^cite_note-Reuters-2] The tournament featured 48 teams for the first time, up from 32 in the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md).[^cite_note-Anderson-2025-3] The national teams of [Canada](723688.md), [Mexico](808402.md) and the [United States](582488.md) qualified automatically for the tournament as hosts, while 206 teams competed for the remaining 45 slots through [qualifying rounds](49459509.md) organized by the six [FIFA confederations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_federations#Continental) and held between September 2023 and March 2026.[^cite_note-Anderson-2025-3][^cite_note-4] In the tournament, the teams were divided into twelve groups of four, with each team playing every other team once in a [round-robin](939742.md) format. The top two teams from each group, along with the top eight third-place teams, advanced to the [knockout stage](81667005.md).[^cite_note-5]

[Argentina](454699.md) had won the previous World Cup in Qatar in [2022](17742072.md), defeating [France](250197.md) in a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md) in the [final](70432805.md) after the match finished 3–3 after extra time. Argentina had previously won the World Cup in [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1986](157231.md), and finished as runners-up in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup), [1990](157232.md), and [2014](656933.md).[^cite_note-6] Argentina manager [Lionel Scaloni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Scaloni) was attempting to become the second manager to win multiple World Cup titles, after [Vittorio Pozzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vittorio_Pozzo) of Italy in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-7] Spain had won the World Cup once before in [2010](168079.md), defeating [the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_final) in [Johannesburg](58893.md), South Africa.[^cite_note-Britanica-1] Heading into the 2026 final, Spain was tied with [Italy](362466.md) for the [longest undefeated streak](66634086.md#Longest_streaks) in the history of men's international soccer, with 37 matches.[^cite_note-8]

Spain and Argentina had played each other fourteen times since 1952, with each team winning six matches. They had played one World Cup match before, in the group stage of the [1966 tournament](61629.md), with Argentina winning 2–1. Their most recent meeting before the 2026 World Cup was a [friendly](977090.md) match in [Madrid](41188263.md) on March 27, 2018, which Spain won 6–1.[^cite_note-9] Spain and Argentina, respective winners of the [UEFA European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_European_Championship) and [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) in 2024,[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] were scheduled to contest the 2026 Finalissima in Qatar in March 2026.[^cite_note-12] However, the match was cancelled due to the [Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The 2026 final was the first World Cup final to feature two reigning continental champions, who were the two top-ranked teams in the [FIFA World Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Ranking).[^cite_note-Athletic-Everything-15] It was also the second time that two [Spanish](26825.md)-speaking countries competed against each other, after [Uruguay](679782.md) and Argentina contested the inaugural [1930 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup_final). With Spain and Argentina co-hosting the next World Cup in [2030](39367087.md), it meant the winner of this final would be the first-ever team to host a World Cup as the defending champion.[^cite_note-19] The official match ball used in the semifinals, match for third place, and the final, was a variation of the [Adidas Trionda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas_Trionda) called the *Trionda Final*. It has the same technical specifications as the Adidas Trionda, but has a gold, white, and black design that resembles the [FIFA World Cup Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Trophy).[^cite_note-20]

## Venue

The final was played at MetLife Stadium in [East Rutherford, New Jersey](136878.md), 5 miles (8.0 km) west of [New York City](645042.md), which also hosted seven other matches in the tournament.[^cite_note-Dominski-2024-22][^cite_note-23] MetLife Stadium was temporarily renamed New York New Jersey Stadium for the World Cup.[^cite_note-NYT-April17-Name-21] The stadium first opened as New Meadowlands Stadium in April 2010 as a replacement for [Giants Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_Stadium).[^cite_note-24] It is the home of the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants) and [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Jets) of the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL) and is the largest stadium in the league, with a maximum capacity of 82,500.[^cite_note-25] The stadium had previously hosted various sporting events, including multiple matches of the [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) and [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup), [Super Bowl XLVIII](4057023.md) in 2014, the [Copa América Centenario final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica_Centenario_final) in 2016, and the [2025 FIFA Club World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_final).[^cite_note-29]

In August 2017, the United Bid Committee of the [United 2026 bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_2026_FIFA_World_Cup_bid) listed MetLife Stadium as a potential host venue.[^cite_note-30] It was later proposed as the ideal venue for the final.[^cite_note-bid_cities-31] After the United 2026 bid was [selected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/68th_FIFA_Congress#2026_FIFA_World_Cup_host) in June 2018,[^cite_note-32] MetLife Stadium was considered an early frontrunner to host the final due to its proximity to New York City and experience hosting major sporting events.[^cite_note-36] In 2021, a joint bid by New York and New Jersey was submitted for MetLife Stadium to host multiple matches during the 2026 World Cup.[^cite_note-37] As part of FIFA's Candidate Host City tour, MetLife Stadium and its facilities were visited by FIFA representatives that September.[^cite_note-Sherman-2021-35] In June 2022, the stadium was confirmed as one of sixteen U.S. host venues.[^cite_note-38] Alongside MetLife Stadium, [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) in California and [AT\&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Stadium) in Texas were frontrunners to host the final.[^cite_note-\:2-39] In January 2024, [*The Sun*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sun_\(United_Kingdom\)) circulated a rumor that AT\&T Stadium had been chosen.[^cite_note-\:2-39] According to *[The Athletic](54936152.md)*, the selection of MetLife Stadium the following month came as a surprise to local officials.*[^cite_note-40]*[^cite_note-Dominski-2024-22]

The [New Jersey Sports and Exposition Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Sports_and_Exposition_Authority) (NJSEA), which oversees MetLife Stadium, approved a \$15.9 million contract to expand the stadium in June 2023.[^cite_note-41] In preparation for the World Cup, the stadium's field was widened to meet FIFA's size requirements,[^cite_note-42] and [Bermuda grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_grass) was temporarily laid over the field's [artificial turf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_turf) for the World Cup.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] The field was later criticized by players, including [Vinícius Júnior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin%C3%ADcius_J%C3%BAnior) and [Adrien Rabiot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrien_Rabiot), both of whom said it was too dry. Worn patches on the pitch were noted by the media.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] The [Meadowlands Rail Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowlands_Rail_Line) and a \$35 million bus line, referred to as the [Secaucus-Meadowlands Transitway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit_in_New_Jersey#Secaucus_to_Meadowlands_Transitway), were used to transport attendees from the nearest transit hub at [Secaucus Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secaucus_Junction).[^cite_note-50] In April 2026, [NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit), which operates the Meadowlands Rail Line, announced that \$150 tickets, higher than the usual \$12.90 fares, would be issued for train services with dedicated queues at [New York Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Penn_Station), and \$80 fares for express buses.[^cite_note-\:3-51] These costs were intended to offset the \$48 million cost of providing event services and security that were not subsidized by FIFA and other grants.[^cite_note-\:3-51] Later in May, train fares were reduced to \$105 and ultimately to \$98, due to increased sponsorships and other funds.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

&#x20;Spain qualified for the 2026 World Cup after finishing first in its [qualification group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_E), and entered the tournament as the [bookmakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookmaker)' favorite to win.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Spain was drawn into [Group H](81660199.md), alongside [Cape Verde](1205575.md), [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team), and [Uruguay](679782.md).[^cite_note-56] Its first match was against Cape Verde in [Atlanta](3138.md) on June 15. The game ended in a goalless draw, due to a strong defensive performance from Cape Verde and its goalkeeper, [Vozinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vozinha).[^cite_note-CNN-CPV-57] Spain's second match was against Saudi Arabia on June 21. [Lamine Yamal](71691936.md) gave Spain the lead ten minutes into the game, scoring his first World Cup goal from a cross by [Mikel Oyarzabal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_Oyarzabal). Oyarzabal added a second goal from a [corner kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corner_kick) taken by Yamal, and within three minutes he scored again from [Dani Olmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dani_Olmo)'s [header](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Header_\(association_football\)) to give Spain a 3–0 halftime lead. In the second half, a [volley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volley_\(association_football\)) by Spain's [Marc Cucurella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Cucurella) was saved by Saudi Arabian goalkeeper [Hassan Al Tambakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Tambakti), but it deflected off [Hassan Al Tambakti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Al-Tambakti) for an [own goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal) to seal Spain's 4–0 victory.[^cite_note-61] Spain's last group game was against Uruguay in [Guadalajara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadalajara) on June 26. Spain won 1–0 after [Álex Baena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lex_Baena)'s shot slipped through the hands of Uruguay goalkeeper [Fernando Muslera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Muslera).[^cite_note-62] Spain advanced to the knockout stage as group winners with seven points.[^cite_note-Azzoni-2026-63]



Spain's starting lineup for the semifinal against France

In the [round of 32](81667041.md), Spain faced [Austria](695226.md) at [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) in [Inglewood, California](107640.md) on July 2. Oyarzabal gave Spain a 1–0 lead in the 38th minute, scoring from a cross by Cucurella. In the second half, [Pedro Porro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Porro) scored Spain's second goal when he headed the ball from a cross by Baena. One minute before the end of regulation time, Oyarzabal added a third goal from a cross by Cucurella to seal a 3–0 win for Spain.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-Smith-2026-65] Spain's victory was its first in a World Cup knockout stage match since the [2010 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_final).[^cite_note-Smith-2026-65]

Spain faced [Portugal](576328.md) in the [round of 16](81667005.md#Round_of_16) at [AT\&T Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Stadium) in [Arlington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington,_Texas), Texas on July 6. After a goalless first half, Spain's [Mikel Merino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_Merino), who had come on as a [substitute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitute_\(association_football\)), scored the only goal of the game in second-half [stoppage time](10568.md#Duration_and_tie-breaking_methods) after receiving a pass from Torres.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] Spain faced [Belgium](679716.md) in the quarterfinal in Inglewood on July 10. After Belgium goalkeeper [Thibaut Courtois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thibaut_Courtois) saved a shot from [Dani Olmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dani_Olmo), [Fabián Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabi%C3%A1n_Ruiz) scored to give Spain a 1–0 lead. [Charles De Ketelaere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_De_Ketelaere) of Belgium equalized the score at 1–1 with a header from a cross by [Timothy Castagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_Castagne) shortly before halftime. This was the only goal conceded by Spain in the tournament.[^cite_note-68] In the second half, Courtois left the pitch injured and was replaced by [Senne Lammens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senne_Lammens). After Lammens saved a 25 yards (23 m) shot from [Pau Cubarsi](74669990.md), Merino hit the rebound, scoring the winning goal two minutes before the end of regulation time to give Spain a 2–1 victory.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] On July 14, Spain played [France](250197.md) in the semifinal in Dallas, and was awarded a penalty in the first half after Yamal was fouled by [Lucas Digne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Digne). Oyarzabal took the penalty kick and scored to give Spain a 1–0 lead. In the 58th minute, Porro scored a second goal to give Spain a 2–0 win.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] Spain progressed to the final for the first time since 2010.[^cite_note-73]

Argentina earned its World Cup berth by placing first in [South American qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CONMEBOL\)), and entered the tournament also as one of the favorites to win.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] Argentina was drawn into [Group J](81660202.md), alongside [Algeria](1029528.md), [Austria](695226.md), and [Jordan](1090065.md).[^cite_note-76] In its first match against Algeria at [Arrowhead Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrowhead_Stadium) in [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City) on June 16, Argentina won 3–0 through a [hat trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_hat-tricks) by [Lionel Messi](2150841.md). Their second match was against Austria at AT\&T Stadium on June 22. Argentina took the lead in the first half with a goal by Messi, who surpassed [Miroslav Klose](201840.md) to become [the all-time top goalscorer](43255516.md) at World Cup tournaments, with 17 goals. Messi added a second goal in second-half [injury time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injury_time) to secure Argentina a 2–0 victory.[^cite_note-Ames-2026-77] Argentina’s last group game was against Jordan on June 27. Argentina won a free kick in the 19th minute, when [Mohannad Abu Taha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohannad_Abu_Taha) fouled [Giovani Lo Celso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovani_Lo_Celso). Celso took the kick and scored to give Argentina a 1–0 lead. After [Nizar Al-Rashdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizar_Al-Rashdan) of Jordan fouled Argentina's [Marcos Senesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_Senesi), [Lautaro Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lautaro_Mart%C3%ADnez) scored from the [penalty](511302.md) kick in the 30th minute, giving Argentina a 2–0 lead. In the second half, [Musa Al-Taamari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_Al-Taamari), who had come on as a substitute, scored once for Jordan to make the score 2–1. Messi took a free kick for Argentina after being fouled, and scored with a curling shot to give Argentina a 3–1 win. Argentina qualified as group winners with nine points.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]



Argentina's starting lineup during the final.

Argentina faced Cape Verde in the round of 32 at [Hard Rock Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Rock_Stadium) on July 3. Argentina took the lead in the 29th minute when Messi scored from close range after a pass from [Lisandro Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisandro_Mart%C3%ADnez). In the second half, Cape Verde's [Deroy Duarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deroy_Duarte) equalized the score at 1–1 after receiving a cross from [Ryan Mendes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Mendes). The game went into [extra time](633242.md), where Argentina retook the lead after two minutes from a goal by Martínez, before [Sidny Lopes Cabral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidny_Lopes_Cabral) of Cape Verde scored with a curling shot to even the score at 2–2 eleven minutes later. In the 111th minute, [Cristian Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Romero)'s header deflected off [Diney Borges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diney_\(footballer,_born_1995\))'s arm for an own goal, giving Argentina a 3–2 victory.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] In the round of 16, Argentina played Egypt in Atlanta on July 7. Egypt took the lead after fifteen minutes when [Yasser Ibrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Ibrahim) scored with a header from a corner kick taken by [Mohamed Hany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Hany). After [Mostafa Ziko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mostafa_Ziko)'s goal was disallowed by the [video assistant referee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_assistant_referee) due to a foul, Ziko proceeded to score again in the second half to give Egypt a 2–0 lead.[^cite_note-82] However, in what was described as "one of the great World Cup comebacks" by [BBC Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Sport), Argentina scored three goals in the final fifteen minutes—with a header by Romero, a goal by Messi, and a second header by [Enzo Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Fern%C3%A1ndez) to seal Argentina's 3–2 win.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-BBC-EGY-84]

Argentina's quarterfinal was against [Switzerland](681862.md) in Kansas City on July 11. Argentina took the lead after ten minutes when [Alexis Mac Allister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Mac_Allister) scored with a header. The match was tied at 1–1 in the 67th minute by [Dan Ndoye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Ndoye) of Switzerland.[^cite_note-ABC-SUI-85] Five minutes later, [Breel Embolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breel_Embolo) of Switzerland was [sent off](3090014.md#Red_card_\(dismissal\)) after being given a second yellow card for [diving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diving_\(association_football\)). Argentina eventually won 3–1 in extra time through a long-distance strike by [Julián Alvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juli%C3%A1n_Alvarez) and a rebound from Lautaro Martínez.[^cite_note-ABC-SUI-85][^cite_note-86] Argentina advanced to the semifinal on July 15 in Atlanta, where it faced [England](9904.md), a [longtime rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93England_football_rivalry) in the World Cup.[^cite_note-87] After [Anthony Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Gordon_\(footballer\))'s goal in the 55th minute gave England the lead, England head coach [Thomas Tuchel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Tuchel) switched to a five-defender formation with several substitutions.[^cite_note-Guardian-ENG-88][^cite_note-89] Messi provided assists for Fernández's equalizing goal in the 86th minute and for Martínez's game-winning header goal in the second minute of stoppage time. Argentina won 2–1 to reach their second consecutive World Cup final.[^cite_note-Guardian-ENG-88]

## Pre-match

### Officiating

On July 16, 2026, [Slavko Vinčić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavko_Vin%C4%8Di%C4%87) of Slovenia was named as the [referee](501260.md) for the final, with fellow Slovenians Tomaž Klančnik and Andraž Kovačič as [assistant referees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_referee_\(association_football\)#Assistant_referee). [Adham Makhadmeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adham_Makhadmeh) and Mohammad Al-Kalaf of Jordan were named as the fourth [official](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_referee_\(association_football\)#Fourth_official) and [reserve assistant referee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assistant_referee_\(association_football\)#Fifth_official_and_reserve_assistant_referee), respectively. The VAR team consisted of [Bastian Dankert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastian_Dankert), [Nicolas Gallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Gallo_\(referee\)), and [Khamis Al-Marri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khamis_Al-Marri). [Tatiana Guzman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatiana_Guzm%C3%A1n) and Guillermo Pacheco served as standby VARs.[^cite_note-90] Earlier in the 2026 World Cup, Vinčić officiated the Brazil–Morocco and Jordan–Algeria matches in the group stage and the Mexico–Ecuador match in the round of 32.[^cite_note-ESPN-Referee2-91][^cite_note-FIFA-2026-92] He had previously officiated the [2022 UEFA Europa League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_UEFA_Europa_League_final) and [2024 UEFA Champions League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_UEFA_Champions_League_final). He was also one of the referees at the [UEFA Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), [2022 World Cup](17742072.md), [UEFA Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024), and [2025 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup). He became the first Slovenian to referee a World Cup final.[^cite_note-FIFA-2026-92]

### Dignitaries in attendance

US president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) and First Lady [Melania Trump](1209075.md) attended the final with [secretary of state](32293.md) [Marco Rubio](5502549.md), along with Mexican president [Claudia Sheinbaum](57813311.md) and Canadian prime minister [Mark Carney](13571049.md).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] Sheinbaum had declined to attend previous matches to show solidarity with ordinary Mexicans unable to afford tickets, but decided to attend the final after receiving a direct invitation from Trump.[^cite_note-95] Representing Spain were King [Felipe VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_VI), [Queen Letizia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Letizia_of_Spain) and their daughters [Leonor, Princess of Asturias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonor,_Princess_of_Asturias) and [Infanta Sofía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infanta_Sof%C3%ADa_of_Spain), as well as prime minister [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md) and [Rafael Louzán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Louz%C3%A1n?action=edit\&redlink=1), president of the [Royal Spanish Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation). Argentina's president, [Javier Milei](56599840.md), declined to attend the match due to [superstition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstition).[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] In his place were [Alejandro Domínguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Dom%C3%ADnguez_\(football_executive\)), president of [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL), and [Claudio Tapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Tapia), president of the [Argentine Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Football_Association) (AFA).[^cite_note-99] Also among the attendees were [Zohran Mamdani](64618347.md), mayor of New York City, and [Kirsty Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsty_Coventry), president of the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) (IOC).[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101]

Although [UEFA](32332.md) President [Aleksander Čeferin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksander_%C4%8Ceferin) attended the tournament's [June 11 opening match in Mexico City](83423432.md), he boycotted the final after a [dispute with FIFA over a suspended disciplinary ban](83626869.md) issued to [United States](582488.md) player [Folarin Balogun](60360233.md).[^cite_note-102] Čeferin, representing the European confederation of which Spain is a member, criticized FIFA's decision to defer Balogun's automatic suspension after he received a red card during a round of 32 match against [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md), which the United States won 2–0.[^cite_note-103]

### Air quality concerns

There were concerns that smoke from the [ongoing Canadian wildfires](83302792.md) would affect the air quality of the final and impact player performance. Spain decided to practice under the smoke, while Argentina practiced in Atlanta to avoid the smoke. FIFA President [Gianni Infantino](9286117.md) met with Trump and [World Cup Task Force](80195854.md) leader [Andrew Giuliani](10194394.md) to discuss the wildfires.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] At the time, New York and New Jersey were under air quality alerts, and air quality readings in New York City were deemed "very unhealthy" and well above levels deemed dangerous to the general public.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] The day prior to the final, much of the smoke dissipated due to heavy thunderstorms, and the air quality improved to the point where there was no risk to the general public.[^cite_note-108]

## Match

### First half



Action during the first half

Argentina kicked off the match at 3\:06 p.m. [EDT](496028.md) (7\:06 p.m. [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time)) in front of a crowd of 80,663. The weather at [Teterboro Airport](475231.md), 4.4 mi (7.1 km) from the stadium,[^cite_note-111] was recorded as fair before kick-off, with a temperature of 80 °F (27 °C) and 40% humidity.[^cite_note-112] Five minutes into the game, [Lamine Yamal](71691936.md) had a chance to score for Spain when he received a pass from [Dani Olmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dani_Olmo). He took a shot that deflected off [Lisandro Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisandro_Mart%C3%ADnez)'s foot and was saved by Argentina's goalkeeper, [Emiliano Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emiliano_Mart%C3%ADnez). [Julián Álvarez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juli%C3%A1n_Alvarez) of Argentina attempted to pass the ball to [Lionel Messi](2150841.md), but Spain's goalkeeper [Unai Simón](58209770.md) ran 40 yards (36.5 m) from the goal to outpace him and kick it away. In the 11th minute, Yamal attempted to receive the ball from a cross by [Rodri](48849865.md), but it went out of the [touchline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_bounds). A minute later, Spain won a [corner kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corner_kick) after [Fabian Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabi%C3%A1n_Ruiz)'s shot was deflected by Emiliano Martínez in the [penalty area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_area).[^cite_note-\:02-113]

Two minutes later, [Alexis Mac Allister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Mac_Allister) of Argentina fouled Dani Olmo.[^cite_note-\:02-113] In the 15th minute, Argentina won a free kick 45 yards (41.1 m) from Spain's goal after Messi was [tackled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#Tackle) by [Alex Baena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lex_Baena). [Rodrigo De Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_De_Paul)'s kick went over the [wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#W) and was saved by Simón. In the 39th minute, Spain's [Mikel Oyarzabal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_Oyarzabal) received a pass from [Fabian Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabi%C3%A1n_Ruiz) outside of the penalty area and made a 20-yard (18 m) low shot at the goal, but it was saved by Emiliano Martínez. In the 41st minute, Lisandro Martínez was given a [yellow card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_card) for a foul on Oyarzabal, and a minute later a left-footed shot from [Marc Cucurella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Cucurella) went wide of the goal. Argentina was forced to make a [substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitute_\(association_football\)) after Lisandro Martínez became injured, with [Nicolás Otamendi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Otamendi) taking his place.[^cite_note-\:12-114][^cite_note-\:02-113]

### Second half

&#x20;The second half began with a substitution from Argentina, with [Leandro Paredes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandro_Paredes) replacing [Nicolás Gonzalez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Gonz%C3%A1lez_\(footballer,_born_1998\)). One minute later, Baena made a 22-yard (20 m) shot towards Argentina's goal which was saved by Emiliano Martínez. Paredes received a yellow card for pushing Rodri. In the 57th minute, Argentina brought [Nahuel Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuel_Molina) onto the pitch in place of [Gonzalo Montiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzalo_Montiel). In the 60th minute, Spain won a free kick when Paredes fouled [Pedro Porro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Porro) 25 yards (23 m) from Argentina's goal. Yamal took the kick and hit the [wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#Wall). Spain then made two substitutions, with [Pedri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedri) and [Ferran Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferran_Torres) coming on in place of Ruiz and Oyarzabal, respectively. In the 64th minute, Olmo hit a 22-yard (20 m) shot that deflected off Martinez and went wide of the goalpost. Three minutes later, Spain had what [Chris Sutton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Sutton) described as a "golden opportunity" when Yamal delivered a cross to Torres, who hit a goal-bound [header](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Header_\(association_football\)) that was saved by Martínez. In the 70th minute, Argentina made two substitutions when [Giuliano Simeone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuliano_Simeone) and [Facundo Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facundo_Medina) replaced [De Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_De_Paul) and [Cristian Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristian_Romero), respectively. Five minutes later, Spain sent [Nico Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_Williams) and [Mikel Merino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikel_Merino) onto the pitch in place of Baena and Olmo. In the 77th minute, Pedri made a shot from the edge of the penalty area, but it was saved by Martínez. [Cubarsí](74669990.md) then struck a 25-yard (23 m) shot at Argentina's goal, which Martinez saved with a dive.[^cite_note-\:12-114][^cite_note-\:02-113]



Enzo Fernández of Argentina received a second yellow card after tackling Pau Cubarsí

In the 80th minute, Nico Williams made a cross that [Aymeric Laporte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymeric_Laporte) tried to meet with a header, but Martínez caught the ball.[^cite_note-MTresumen-115] In the 82nd minute, Argentina's [Enzo Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Fern%C3%A1ndez) fell to the ground following contact from [Rodri](48849865.md), and the referee did not award a foul, leading Fernández to complain and then receive a yellow card for dissent. In the 3rd minute of [stoppage time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoppage_time), Argentina's [Giuliano Simeone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuliano_Simeone) made a low cross from the right, but [Unai Simón](58209770.md) intercepted the ball. On the resulting counterattack, Williams dribbled the ball for 30 meters before taking a shot from the edge of the [box](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#Penalty_area), which was also saved by Martínez.[^cite_note-MTresumen-115] A few minutes later, Fernández was [sent off](3090014.md) after receiving a second yellow card for tackling Pau Cubarsí while attempting to win a loose ball; a VAR review upheld the on-field decision.[^cite_note-116].[^cite_note-117] In the final seconds of regulation time, Yamal struck a free kick that Martínez managed to deflect away.[^cite_note-MTresumen-115] As regulation time concluded, Argentina became the first team to fail to register a shot during the first 90 minutes of a World Cup final.[^cite_note-118]

### Extra time



Ferran Torres of Spain, after scoring the game's only goal

In the 93rd minute of the match during [extra time](633242.md), Williams was not able to head a [Pedri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedri) cross with enough power, and Martínez dove to his right to make the save.[^cite_note-Bencid-2026-119] Three minutes later, Williams appeared to score, but the goal was disallowed because Merino had stepped on Otamendi's foot. Commentators noted that the contact was minor and was the type that referees sometimes overlook in the penalty area, and also observed that Otamendi had his arm around Merino prior to the incident.[^cite_note-123] Spain supporters sarcastically chanted "Infantino" following the disallowed goal, referencing allegations of FIFA favoring Argentina.[^cite_note-1.a.3-121]

Merino then headed the ball wide of Argentina's goal in the 102nd minute. At the start of the second period of extra time, Torres received a ball headed by Williams. He struck it with his left foot from close range and past Martínez, scoring the first goal of the match and putting Spain in the lead 1–0.[^cite_note-MTresumen-115][^cite_note-124]

After Spain's goal, Argentina began to counterattack in search of an [equalizer goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_association_football_terms#Equaliser). In the 113th minute, Torres appeared to score his second goal, but it was ruled out for being offside. Two minutes later, Messi attempted to catch Simón off guard with a cross that drifted toward the Spanish goal, passing over the crossbar. In the 116th minute, Williams positioned himself in Argentina's box after outrunning [Nahuel Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuel_Molina), but was unable to connect with the ball to shoot. A minute later, Messi made Argentina's first shot from the edge of the box, which struck Merino in the face as he blocked it, leaving him down on the pitch.[^cite_note-Bencid-2026-119][^cite_note-125]

In the 120th minute, Argentina's final attack came from a [Giuliano Simeone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuliano_Simeone) pass toward [Marcos Senesi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_Senesi) in the box, but Cubarsí cleared the ball.[^cite_note-126] The resulting corner kick led to a scramble inside the goal area, with the Spanish defense kicking the ball out of bounds. The next corner kick was deflected by Argentina's [Nicolás Tagliafico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Tagliafico) on the near post and fell to Simeone's feet, but his shot from close to the penalty spot went well over the crossbar.[^cite_note-127] In the final minutes, Spain kept possession away from its goal. A long free kick from Emiliano Martinez into Spain's goal area was cleared by its defense before the final whistle confirmed Spain's victory.[^cite_note-Bencid-2026-119]

## Post-match



Spain's players with their awards, pictured from left to right: Pau Cubarsí (Young Player Award), Rodri (Golden Ball), and Unai Simón (Golden Glove)

Spain's [Ferran Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferran_Torres), who scored the only goal of the final, was named as the [man of the match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_of_the_match).[^cite_note-MOTM-130] In the awards ceremony after the match, Spain's Rodri was awarded the [Golden Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Golden_Ball) as the best player of the tournament, while his teammate [Unai Simón](58209770.md) was awarded the [Golden Glove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Golden_Glove) as the best goalkeeper. Spain's [Pau Cubarsí](74669990.md) won the [FIFA Young Player Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Young_Player_Award) as the best young player of the tournament. [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) won the Silver Ball as the second-best player of the tournament, and the Silver Boot as the second-top goalscorer, with eight goals.[^cite_note-132] FIFA president [Gianni Infantino](9286117.md) and US president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) were booed by some spectators as they entered the field with the World Cup trophy, which they presented to Spain's captain [Rodri](48849865.md).[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

[Lamine Yamal](71691936.md) and Messi embraced after the match, a moment described by FIFA and several media outlets as symbolic of a generational transition in football, with Messi approaching the end of an illustrious career and Yamal, regarded as one of the sport's most promising talents, at the start of his.[^cite_note-2007photo-135][^cite_note-136] The encounter drew additional resonance from [a 2007 photograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Messi%E2%80%93Yamal_photo_shoot) in which Messi, then 20 years old, bathed the infant Yamal during a charity photo session while at [FC Barcelona](68187.md). The image had attracted renewed media attention as the two players prepared to face each other in the 2026 World Cup final.[^cite_note-2007photo-135]

Spain became the first country to simultaneously hold both the men's and women's World Cup titles, having won the Women's World Cup in [2023](45271353.md).[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] Spain also became the second European nation to win a World Cup held in the [Americas](29833.md), following [Germany](250204.md) in [2014](656933.md).[^cite_note-139] Messi became the second player in history to play in three World Cup finals, after [Cafu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cafu) of [Brazil](149286.md).[^cite_note-140] The 2026 final featured 11 saves by Argentina goalkeeper Emiliano Martínez, the most ever recorded in a World Cup final.[^cite_note-141]

### Altercations between players

After the match concluded, multiple altercations broke out between Argentina's and Spain's players.[^cite_note-142] Argentina's defender [Nahuel Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuel_Molina) was seen striking Spain's captain [Rodri](48849865.md) in the stomach, who was running to celebrate his team's victory. Shortly after, Argentina's [Leandro Paredes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandro_Paredes) tackled [Eric Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Garcia_\(footballer,_born_2001\)), who fell to the ground, resulting in a physical altercation. Just as he was getting up, Paredes shoved him by the throat, and shortly after, Argentina's [coaching staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaching_staff) protected Garcia from him. Paredes then wrestled [Gavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavi_\(footballer\)) to the ground, with substitute [Thiago Almada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiago_Almada) getting involved as he ripped his training bib.[^cite_note-guardian_2026-07-20-143][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145] Argentina's assistant coach [Roberto Ayala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Ayala) grabbed Spain's [Dani Olmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dani_Olmo) by the bib before punching him in the face.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] Multiple media outlets claimed that Paredes was shown a red card after the brawl,[^cite_note-148] but FIFA's official match report states that the referee did not penalize Paredes.[^cite_note-Final-128]

Argentina's actions during and after the match were described as disgraceful by several international media outlets.[^cite_note-149] Paredes was criticized for his violent behavior during and after the match,[^cite_note-150] and Messi was criticized for attempting to have [Marc Cucurella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Cucurella) sent off for briefly [covering his mouth](36581929.md#New_rules) after Fernández's red card.[^cite_note-151] FIFA announced an investigation into the behavior of Argentine players and staff, including Paredes, Molina and Ayala.[^cite_note-guardian_2026-07-20-143][^cite_note-152] In the days that followed, Ayala apologized for his behavior and Gavi spoke against possible suspensions for his rivals.[^cite_note-153] Several media outlets called Spain's win a victory for the sport of football.[^cite_note-154]

### Aftermath and reactions

The Spain national team returned to Madrid the next day and was welcomed at the [Madrid–Barajas Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid%E2%80%93Barajas_Airport).[^cite_note-155] A World Cup victory parade took place in central Madrid, with roughly two million people in attendance.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] Celebrations also broke out across various other parts of Spain.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] In [Ciudad Rodrigo, Salamanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciudad_Rodrigo), several revelers stood in a fountain which collapsed on them, killing a teenager and injuring three others.[^cite_note-160] A 20-year-old died in [Sonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonseca) during what was reported as a convulsion. Across Spain that evening, more than 80 people were reported injured and approximately 12 arrested.[^cite_note-161] In New York City, celebrations broke out in several parts of the city, including [Times Square](31389.md), [Central Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Park), and [Little Spain, Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Spain,_Manhattan).[^cite_note-162] In [Mexico City](18987.md), several Spain supporters and Mexico national team fans celebrated at the [Fountain of Cybele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuente_de_Cibeles_\(Mexico_City\)).[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164]

In [Buenos Aires](255919.md), fans and government officials gathered at [Ezeiza International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezeiza_International_Airport) to receive the Argentina national team, where the [Argentine Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Argentine_Republic) performed an orchestra rendition of the football chant *[Muchachos, ahora nos volvimos a ilusionar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muchachos,_ahora_nos_volvimos_a_ilusionar?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (transl. Guys, we have our hopes up again) and a [drone show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_art) marked their arrival.[^cite_note-165] Messi and several other players did not travel to Buenos Aires, instead flying directly to their club destinations or to begin their vacations. The arriving players were transported through the city on an open-top bus, greeted by large crowds and fireworks along the route.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167] Thousands of additional supporters gathered at the [Obelisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obelisco_de_Buenos_Aires).[^cite_note-168] Riots broke out amidst the celebrations, with people throwing stones, glass bottles, and other objects at police.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] Riot police responded with water cannons, tear gas, and rubber bullets to disperse the crowds. Local police reported at least fifteen arrests and several injuries during the clashes.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173]

On July 29, [FIFA](11049.md) opened formal disciplinary proceedings against the [Argentine Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Football_Association) (AFA), citing multiple infractions by the Argentina national team during the final and their semi-final match against [England](9904.md).[^cite_note-174] On August 21, FIFA's disciplinary committee announced a 10-match ban for [Leandro Paredes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandro_Paredes), a 7-match ban for [Nahuel Molina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahuel_Molina), a 3-match ban for [Roberto Ayala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Ayala), and a 1-match ban for both [Thiago Almada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiago_Almada) and Spain's [Gavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavi_\(footballer\)), in addition to financial sanctions for all of them, with fines totaling \$270,000. The committee fined the AFA \$321,000 (\$100,000 of which is suspended) for displaying a banner with the words "Las Malvinas son Argentinas" ("[The Falklands](7550182.md) are Argentine") during the semi-final, in reference to the territory [dispute between Argentina and the United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_sovereignty_dispute). FIFA also sanctioned the AFA with a maximum 50% capacity for its next two home matches.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176]

Smoke in the national colors of Spain and Argentina drifts across MetLife Stadium's pitch minutes before kickoff.

### Halftime show

Main article: [2026 FIFA World Cup final halftime show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_final_halftime_show)



[Shakira](6479315.md) and the [Ghetto Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghetto_Kids) during the show

The 2026 final was the first final in World Cup history to include a [halftime show](872675.md), similar to the halftime shows featured at the NFL [Super Bowl](27718.md). It was co-headlined by singer-songwriters [Madonna](142056.md) and [Shakira](6479315.md), South Korean boy band [BTS](39862325.md), and [Justin Bieber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Bieber), and featured guest performances from [Burna Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burna_Boy) and [Emmanuel Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Kelly). [Jason Sudeikis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Sudeikis) and [Brendan Hunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_Hunt) appeared as the characters [Ted Lasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Lasso_\(character\)) and [Coach Beard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_Beard), respectively, from the television series *[Ted Lasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Lasso).* Other performers included the rock group [Electric Mayhem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Teeth_and_the_Electric_Mayhem) from [The Muppets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Muppets), the [Ghetto Kids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghetto_Kids), the [PS22 Chorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PS22_Chorus), and a combined [New York Philharmonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Philharmonic) and [Simón Bolívar Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sim%C3%B3n_Bol%C3%ADvar_Symphony_Orchestra), with [Bijan Mortazavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijan_Mortazavi) and conducted by [Gustavo Dudamel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavo_Dudamel).[^cite_note-181] The show was part of an effort to reach \$100 million for the FIFA Global Citizen Education Fund, which seeks to increase access to education and sports for children.[^cite_note-182]

The halftime show was criticized by some commentators and fans as being too commercialized and an unwanted Americanization of the match.[^cite_note-183][^cite_note-Guardian-Halftime-184] The halftime break lasted 27 minutes and 22 seconds,[^cite_note-185] which broke the [IFAB Laws of the Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laws_of_the_Game_\(association_football\)) which state that the halftime break cannot exceed 15 minutes.[^cite_note-188]
