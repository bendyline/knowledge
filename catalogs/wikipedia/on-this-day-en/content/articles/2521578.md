# Spy ship

"Spy Ship" redirects here. For the 1942 film, see [Spy Ship (film)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy_Ship_\(film\)).



[USS *Liberty*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Liberty), a "[technical research ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_research_ship)", in 1967



*Oker*, an [*Oste*-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oste-class_fleet_service_ship) [ELINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELINT) and [reconnaissance](146911.md) ship, of the [German Navy](12960.md)



[SSV-33 *Ural*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_communications_ship_SSV-33), the largest spy ship ever constructed, used by the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) to observe and track US ballistic and orbital launches.

A **spy ship** or **reconnaissance vessel** is a dedicated [ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship) intended to gather [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_\(information_gathering\)), usually by means of sophisticated [electronic eavesdropping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIGINT). In a wider sense, any ship intended to gather information could be considered a spy ship.

Spy ships are usually controlled by a nation's [government](12229.md), due to the high costs and advanced equipment required. They tend to be parts of the nation's [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy), though they may also be operated by [secret services](81014.md).

[Naval trawlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_trawler) masquerade as civilian ships such as [fishing trawlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_trawler), which could be reasonably expected to remain in a certain area for a long time.

Ships which are used to infiltrate [spies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy) or [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces) are sometimes also called "spy ships".[^cite_note-1]

## History

An early version of what would become known as a spy ship is the [United States](3434750.md) civilian [cargo ship](786056.md) [USS *Gold Star* (AK-12)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Gold_Star), which made frequent voyages to [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan), [China](5405.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md) with cargo and passengers during the 1920s and 1930s.[^cite_note-Lademan-2] Starting in 1933 as a station ship she was assigned to monitor internal Japanese Fleet frequencies and direction finder azimuths. She had three intercept operators and one chief radioman supervised by an officer. *Gold Star* and ground stations provided significant intelligence before the Japanese attack on Pearl Harbor on 7 December 1941.[^cite_note-NSA-3]

Spy ships in the modern sense of being specially built and entirely dedicated to intelligence tasks came into being during the early [Cold War](325329.md), and they are in use by all major powers. Their uses, in addition to listening in on communications and spy on enemy fleet movements, were to monitor [nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_test) and [missile](19594.md) launches (especially of potential [ICBMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICBM)). One of the most important functions for both Cold War spy ship fleets, especially in the 1960s, was the gathering of [submarine](28825.md) "signatures" – the patterns of noise that could often identify the specific type of submarine and were thus valuable in [anti-submarine warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-submarine_warfare). During that era, the United States fielded about 80 vessels, usually classified as "environmental research" craft, while the Soviet Union had around 60 ships, often converted trawlers or [hydrographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Hydrographic_Service) research ships.[^cite_note-TIME-4]

In the late 1980s, the Soviet [fisheries fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_fleet) was known for having equipped many of their thousands of ships with sophisticated [SIGINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIGINT) and [ELINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELINT) equipment, thus functioning as auxiliary spy ships tracking western naval vessels and electronic communications (though their main function remained commercial fishing).[^cite_note-5]

## Operation



While [USNS *Vanguard*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USNS_Mission_San_Fernando_\(T-AO-122\)) was not strictly a spy ship, being used for space tracking, there is some overlap between her capabilities and those of a spy ship.

A spy ship usually stays in [international waters](394357.md)[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] (or at least outside territorial waters), so as to not violate territorial [borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border). From there, it will use its electronic equipment to [monitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance) sea and air traffic, [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio) and [radar](25676.md)[^cite_note-TIME-4] frequencies and also try to intercept and [decrypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decrypt) coded radio or [phone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phone) communications. This is mostly done via passive means such as radio receivers or passive [sonar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonar). Sometimes however, active measures such as radar or sonar may also be used to detect the movement of [aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft), [missiles](19594.md), [ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship) or other vehicles or troops.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] However, this risks revealing the ship's purpose.

As it is located much closer to the [surveilled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance) area than a fixed installation (given a close by [shoreline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoreline)), the monitoring is usually much more efficient and in some respects better than even that of spy satellites.

[Tracking vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracking_ship) also have some of the capabilities of spy ships, and as they are controlled by their national governments, they are also intermittently used for similar purposes, such as tracking enemy missile tests.

## Soviet AGI trawlers

As the United States Navy began deploying [ballistic missile submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missile_submarine) in 1960, the Soviet Union attempted to obtain more information about the capabilities of the [UGM-27 Polaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UGM-27_Polaris) missile and the locations of the submarines capable of launching them. While the Soviet Navy requested more sophisticated ships, they were allocated trawlers (called *tra-ou-lery*) from the fishing fleet equipped with more sophisticated sensors and communication equipment. Very capable crews were assigned to these trawlers of unremarkable appearance. They were assigned to patrol stations off United States naval bases to photograph and report arrival and departure of United States warships and auxiliaries. Other trawlers of similar appearance would patrol weapons firing ranges used by the United States Navy to observe practice firings of modern weapons and record the acoustic and/or electromagnetic signature of the sonar, search radar, fire-control radar, guidance, and/or command electronics of each weapons system.[^cite_note-enj-10] The United States Navy officially designated these trawlers as Auxiliary, General Intelligence (AGI), and they were informally known as "tattletales".[^cite_note-nip-11]

An AGI might be assigned to a single patrol station for as long as six months. These ships were not fast enough to keep up with most warships, but they sometimes congregated around [aircraft carriers](2219.md) conducting [air operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_United_States_Navy_carrier_air_operations) of the [United States Sixth Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Sixth_Fleet) in the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) or [United States Seventh Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Seventh_Fleet) in the western [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), or in suspected patrol areas of ballistic missile submarines. After the [Cuban Missile Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuban_Missile_Crisis), the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) authorized a *counter AGI program* for United States [destroyers](8779.md) to come alongside the AGIs to push against them, foul their [screws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propeller) with steel nets, and focus high power electromagnetic transmitters to burn out the amplifying circuitry of their electronic sensors. The AGI crews then revealed their ship-handling skills using superior maneuverability to evade the destroyers' intentions. This jousting in international waters continued until signing of the [U.S.–Soviet Incidents at Sea agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93Soviet_Incidents_at_Sea_agreement) in 1972.[^cite_note-enj-10]

In 1972, as the U.S. and U.K. partners started operating radar station [Cobra Mist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobra_Mist), it garnered attention from many Soviet spy trawlers.[^cite_note-bbc-12] A year later, the radar station was shut down due to interference. The source of the interference was never confirmed and some theories still hold Soviet countermeasures responsible.[^cite_note-bbc-12]
