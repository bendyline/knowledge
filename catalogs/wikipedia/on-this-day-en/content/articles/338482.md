The **United States Central Command** (**USCENTCOM** or **CENTCOM**) is one of the eleven [unified combatant commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_combatant_command) of the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md). It was established in 1983, taking over the previous responsibilities of the [Rapid Deployment Joint Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_Deployment_Joint_Task_Force) (RDJTF).&#x20;

Its [area of responsibility](9452512.md) (AOR) includes the [Middle East](19323.md) (including [Egypt](8087628.md) in [Africa](5334607.md)), [Central Asia](6742.md) and parts of [South Asia](21566765.md). The command has been the main American presence in many military operations, including the [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_War)'s Operation Desert Storm in 1991, the [war in Afghanistan](19666611.md), as well as the [Iraq War](5043324.md) from 2003 to 2011. As of 2015, CENTCOM forces were deployed primarily in Afghanistan under the auspices of [Operation Freedom's Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Freedom%27s_Sentinel), which was itself part of NATO's [Resolute Support Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_Support_Mission) (from 2015 to 2021), and in Iraq and Syria as part of [Operation Inherent Resolve](44120613.md) since 2014 in supporting and advise-and-assist roles.&#x20;

As of 8 August 2025, CENTCOM's commander is [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_\(United_States\)) [Brad Cooper](67555995.md), [U.S. Navy](20518076.md).[^cite_note-6] Two of the last three [United States secretaries of defense](43998.md)—[Lloyd Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Austin) and [James Mattis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Mattis), both of whom required congressional waivers to be confirmed—were recent CENTCOM commanders.[^cite_note-7]&#x20;

Of all seven American regional unified combatant commands, CENTCOM is among four that are headquartered outside their area of operations (the other three being [USAFRICOM](9347800.md), [USSOUTHCOM](1590932.md), and [USSPACECOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Command)). CENTCOM's main headquarters is located at [MacDill Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacDill_Air_Force_Base), in [Tampa, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida). A forward headquarters was established in 2002 at [Camp As Sayliyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As_Sayliyah_Army_Base) in [Doha](26214389.md), Qatar, which in 2009 transitioned to a forward headquarters at [Al Udeid Air Base](3830783.md) in [Qatar](8238258.md).&#x20;

In January 2021, [Israel](9282173.md) became the 21st country of the CENTCOM AOR, added to another 20 nations including [Afghanistan](737.md), [Bahrain](18933277.md), [Egypt](8087628.md), [Iran](14653.md), [Iraq](7515928.md), [Jordan](7515964.md), [Kazakhstan](16642.md), [Kuwait](7515890.md), [Kyrgyzstan](170131.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), [Oman](22316.md), [Pakistan](23235.md), [Qatar](8238258.md), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), [Syria](7515849.md), [Tajikistan](30108.md), [Turkmenistan](198149.md), the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), [Uzbekistan](31853.md) and [Yemen](350939.md).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]&#x20;

## History

The command was established on 1 January 1983.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECordesman1998-10] As its name implies, CENTCOM covers the "central" area of the globe located between then European and Pacific Commands (PACOM). (Changes since 1983 have seen the creation of Africa Command and change of title for PACOM to Indo-Pacific Command.) When the [hostage crisis in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_hostage_crisis) and the [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War) underlined the need to strengthen the U.S. presence in the region, President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) established the [Rapid Deployment Joint Task Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_Deployment_Joint_Task_Force) (RDJTF) in March 1980. Steps were taken to transform the RDJTF into a permanent unified command over a two-year period. The first step was to make the RDJTF independent of [U.S. Readiness Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Readiness_Command), followed by the activation of CENTCOM in January 1983.&#x20;

The command was configured to fight both the Soviets if necessary, and assure other U.S. interests in the area. In 1980, [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) of [Iraq](7515928.md) invaded Iran, beginning the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War). The war risked interruptions to the oil supply route out of the [Persian Gulf](24761.md)/Arabian Gulf through the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md). Developments like Iranian mining operations in the Gulf led to CENTCOM's first combat operations. On 17 May 1987, [USS *Stark*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Stark), conducting operations in the Persian Gulf, [was struck by Exocet missiles fired by an Iraqi aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Stark_incident), resulting in 37 casualties. Soon afterward, as part of what became known as the "[Tanker War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War#Attacks_on_shipping)", the [Federal government of the United States](195149.md) reflagged and renamed 11 [Kuwaiti](7515890.md) oil tankers. In [Operation Earnest Will](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Earnest_Will), these tankers were escorted by USCENTCOM's [Middle East Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Force) through the Persian Gulf to Kuwait and back through the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECordesman1998-10]&#x20;

By late 1988, the regional strategy still largely focused on the potential threat of a massive [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) invasion of Iran. [Exercise Internal Look](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Internal_Look) has been one of CENTCOM's primary planning events. It had frequently been used to train CENTCOM to be ready to defend the [Zagros Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros_Mountains) from a Soviet attack and was held annually.[^cite_note-SchwarzIDTH-11]&#x20;

In autumn 1989, the main CENTCOM contingency plan, OPLAN 1002-88, assumed a Soviet attack through Iran to the Persian Gulf. The plan called for five-and-two-thirds US divisions to deploy, mostly light and heavy forces at something less than full strength (apportioned to it by the Joint Strategic Capability Plan \[JSCAP]). The original plan called for these five-and-two-thirds divisions to march from the Persian Gulf to the [Zagros Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros_Mountains) and prevent the [Soviet Ground Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ground_Forces) (army) from seizing the [Iranian oil fields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_reserves_in_Iran).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESwain19976-12][^cite_note-13]&#x20;

After 1990, General [Norman Schwarzkopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Schwarzkopf) reoriented CENTCOM's planning to fend off a threat from Iraq, and Internal Look moved to a biennial schedule. There was a notable similarity between the 1990 Internal Look exercise scripts and the real-world movement of Iraqi forces which culminated in Iraq's [invasion of Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Kuwait) during the final days of the exercise.[^cite_note-SchwarzIDTH-11] U.S. President [George Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) responded quickly. A timely deployment of forces and the formation of a coalition deterred Iraq from invading [Saudi Arabia](349303.md), and the command began to focus on the liberation of Kuwait. The buildup of forces continued, reinforced by [United Nations Security Council Resolution 678](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_678), which called for Iraqi forces to leave Kuwait. On 17 January 1991, U.S. and coalition forces launched [Operation Desert Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) with a massive air interdiction campaign, which prepared the theater for a coalition ground assault. The primary coalition objective, the liberation of Kuwait, was achieved on 27 February, and the next morning a ceasefire was declared, just one hundred hours after the commencement of the ground campaign.&#x20;

The end of formal hostilities did not bring the end of difficulties with Iraq. [Operation Provide Comfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Provide_Comfort), implemented to provide humanitarian assistance to the Kurds and enforce a ["no-fly" zone in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_no-fly_zones_conflict), north of the 36th parallel, began in April 1991. In August 1992, [Operation Southern Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Southern_Watch) began in response to Saddam's noncompliance with [United Nations Security Council Resolution 688](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_688) condemning his brutal repression of Iraqi civilians in southeastern Iraq. Under the command and control of Joint Task Force Southwest Asia, coalition forces in this operation enforced a no-fly zone south of the 32nd parallel. In January 1997, [Operation Northern Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Northern_Watch) replaced Provide Comfort, with a focus on enforcing the northern no-fly zone. Throughout the decade, CENTCOM carried out a string of operations – [Vigilant Warrior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Vigilant_Warrior), [Vigilant Sentinel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Vigilant_Sentinel), [Desert Strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Strike), Desert Thunder (I and II), and [Desert Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Desert_Fox) – to try to coerce Saddam into greater compliance with U.S. wishes.&#x20;

The 1990s also brought significant challenges in [Somalia](27358.md) as well as from the growing threat of regional terrorism. To prevent widespread starvation as the [Somali Civil War](3070332.md) continued, CENTCOM began [Operation Provide Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Provide_Relief) in 1992 to supply humanitarian assistance to Somalia and northeastern [Kenya](188171.md). CENTCOM's [Operation Restore Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Restore_Hope) supported [United Nations Security Council Resolution 794](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_794) and a multinational Unified Task Force, which provided security until the U.N. created [UNOSOM II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNOSOM_II) in May 1993. In spite of some UNOSOM II success in the countryside, the situation in [Mogadishu](60154.md) worsened, and the significant casualties of the [Battle of Mogadishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mogadishu_\(1993\)) ultimately led President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) to order the withdrawal of all U.S. troops from Somalia.&#x20;

Throughout the 1990s, following the Gulf War, terrorist attacks had a major impact on CENTCOM forces. Faced with attacks such as the [1996 bombing of the Khobar Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khobar_Towers_bombing), which killed 19 American airmen, the command launched Operation Desert Focus, designed to relocate U.S. installations to more defensible locations (such as [Prince Sultan Air Base](5553272.md)), reduce the U.S. forward "footprint" by eliminating nonessential billets, and return dependents to the United States. In 1998 terrorists [attacked the U.S. embassies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_United_States_embassy_bombings) in Kenya and [Tanzania](30118.md), killing 250 persons, including 12 Americans. The October 2000 [attack on the USS *Cole*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Cole_bombing), resulting in the deaths of 17 U.S. sailors, was linked to [Osama bin Laden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_bin_Laden)'s [Al Qaida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Qaida) organization.&#x20;

From April to July 1999, CENTCOM conducted Exercise [Desert Crossing 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Crossing_1999), centered on the scenario of [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) being ousted as Iraq's dictator. It was held in the offices of [Booz Allen Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booz_Allen_Hamilton) in [McLean, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLean,_Virginia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGordonTrainor20126–7-14] The exercise concluded that unless measures were taken, "fragmentation and chaos" would ensue after Saddam Hussein's overthrow.&#x20;

The [September 11 terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11_terrorist_attacks) on New York and Washington DC led President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) to declare a war against international terrorism. CENTCOM soon launched [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom) to expel the Taliban government in Afghanistan, which was harboring Al Qaida terrorists and hosting terrorist training camps.&#x20;

[Exercise Internal Look](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Internal_Look) has been employed for explicit war planning on at least two occasions: Internal Look '90, which dealt with a threat from Iraq, and Internal Look '03, which was used to plan what became Operation Iraqi Freedom – the 2003 [United States invasion of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Iraq), which began on 19 March 2003. In the process, the existing operations plan, OPLAN 1003–98, aka 1003V, which had called for 500,000 troops for the invasion and control of Iraq, was radically slimmed down.[^cite_note-15]&#x20;

Following the defeat of both the Taliban regime in Afghanistan (9 November 2001) and Saddam Hussein's government in Iraq (8 April 2003), CENTCOM remained controlling the U.S. forces deployed there. U.S. forces were withdrawn from Iraq in 2010 (but returned years later); and were withdrawn from Afghanistan in 2021.&#x20;

Beginning in October 2002, CENTCOM conducted operations in the [Horn of Africa](62142.md). [Combined Joint Task Force – Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Joint_Task_Force_%E2%80%93_Horn_of_Africa) was established to carry these activities out. These operations involved a series of [Special Operations Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Operations_Forces) raids, humanitarian assistance, consequence management, and a variety of civic action programs.&#x20;

The command has also remained poised to provide disaster relief throughout the region; its most recent significant relief operations have been a response to the October 2005 earthquake in [Pakistan](23235.md), and the large-scale evacuation of American citizens from [Lebanon](17771.md) in 2006.&#x20;



U.S. armored vehicle in [Al-Hasakah](7573335.md), [Syria](7515849.md), May 2017

On 1 October 2008, the Department of War transferred responsibility for [Sudan](27421.md), [Eritrea](17238590.md), [Ethiopia](187749.md), [Djibouti](17207794.md), Kenya, and Somalia to the newly established Africa Command. [Egypt](8087628.md), home to [Exercise Bright Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exercise_Bright_Star), the Department of War's largest reoccurring military exercise, remained in the CENTCOM [Area of Responsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_of_Responsibility). On 15 January 2021, responsibility for [Israel](9282173.md) was transferred from Europe Command to CENTCOM.[^cite_note-ss20200115-16]&#x20;

In January 2015, CENTCOM's Twitter feed was reported to have been hacked on 11 January by [ISIS](9087364.md) sympathizers.[^cite_note-reu115-17] This situation lasted for less than one hour; no classified information was posted and "none of the information posted came from CENTCOM's server or social media sites";[^cite_note-abc-18] however, some of the slides came from the federally funded [Lincoln Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Laboratory) at the [Massachusetts Institute of Technology](18879.md).[^cite_note-reu115-17] In August 2015, [intelligence analysts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_analysts) working for CENTCOM complained to the media, alleging that CENTCOM's senior leadership was altering or distorting intelligence reports on the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant. In February 2017, the Inspector General of the [United States Department of War](7279897.md) completed its investigation and cleared the senior leadership of CENTCOM, concluding that "allegations of intelligence being intentionally altered, delayed or suppressed by top CENTCOM officials from mid-2014 to mid-2015 were largely unsubstantiated."[^cite_note-CNN20170201-19]&#x20;

In January 2018, [Turkey](11125639.md) urged the United States to remove its troops from [Syrian](7515849.md) city of [Manbij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manbij), saying that otherwise they might come under attack from Turkish troops; however, former CENTCOM commander [Joseph Votel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Votel) confirmed an American commitment to keeping troops in Manbij.[^cite_note-20] In 2019, the Iranian government designated Central Command a terrorist organization after the [Trump administration](52231341.md) branded Iran's [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) with the same label.[^cite_note-Eqbali-21]&#x20;

On 28 February 2026, CENTCOM led [Operation Epic Fury](82546969.md), a large-scale joint US-Israeli military operation against Iran. In a statement, CENTCOM said that US and Israeli forces had begun striking military infrastructure including [IRGC](1285472.md) command-and-control facilities, air defense capabilities, missile and drone launch sites, and military airfields. The command stated the operation involved "the largest regional concentration of American military firepower in a generation" and that it involved troops from all six branches of the US armed forces. CENTCOM commander Admiral Brad Cooper confirmed that US forces had "successfully defended against hundreds of Iranian missile and drone attacks" and that there were no US casualties. Initial operations included air, land and sea-launched precision munitions, along with the first employment of US-made one-way attack drones.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]&#x20;

## Structure

CENTCOM's main headquarters is located at [MacDill Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacDill_Air_Force_Base), in [Tampa, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida). CENTCOM headquarters staff directorates include personnel, intelligence, operations, logistics, plans & policy, information systems, training & exercises, and resources, and other functions. The intelligence section is known as Joint Intelligence Center, Central Command, or JICCENT, which serves as a [Joint Intelligence Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Intelligence_Center) for the co-ordination of [intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_\(information_gathering\)). Under the intelligence directorate, there are several divisions including the [Afghanistan-Pakistan Center of Excellence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan-Pakistan_Center_of_Excellence).&#x20;

Two major subordinate multi-service commands reporting to Central Command were responsible for Afghanistan: [Combined Joint Task Force 180](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Joint_Task_Force_180) and Combined Forces Command Afghanistan (CFC-A). CFC-A was disestablished in February 2007.[^cite_note-30] From that point onward, the [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) (ISAF) directed most U.S. forces in Afghanistan. A U.S. general ([Dan K. McNeill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_K._McNeill)) assumed command of ISAF that same month.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAuerswaldSaideman201496_onwards-31]&#x20;

Temporary task forces include the Central Command Forward – Jordan (CF-J), which was announced in April 2013.[^cite_note-DoD_CFJ_visit-32] CF-J's stated purpose was to work with the Jordanian armed forces to improve the latter's capabilities.[^cite_note-DoD_CFJ_visit-32] There was speculation, however, that another reason for its establishment was to serve as a base from which raids into Syria could be launched to seize Syrian WMD if necessary, and as a launch pad for looming American military action in Syria.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-NYT_CFJ_open-34][^cite_note-BF_Obama_bomb-35]&#x20;

On 1 October 2008 [Combined Joint Task Force – Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Joint_Task_Force_%E2%80%93_Horn_of_Africa) at [Camp Lemonnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Lemonnier) in Djibouti was transferred to [United States Africa Command](9347800.md) (USAFRICOM).[^cite_note-africom-stands-up-36] The [United States Forces – Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Forces_%E2%80%93_Iraq) (USF-I) was a major subordinate multi-service command during the [Iraq War order of battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War_order_of_battle) until it was disestablished in 2011.&#x20;



An Army officer briefs Secretary of State [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo) (left, foreground) on counter-ISIL social media activities at MacDill AFB, June 2018.



[U.S. Air Force](32090.md) [KC-135 Stratotankers](17441.md) at [Al Udeid Air Base](3830783.md) in Qatar

Elements of other [Unified Combatant Commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Combatant_Command), especially [United States Special Operations Command](45204749.md) (USSOCOM), operate in the CENTCOM area. It appears that SOCCENT does *not* direct the secretive [Task Force 88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_Force_88_\(anti-terrorist_unit\)), the ad-hoc grouping of [Joint Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Special_Operations_Command) 'black' units such as [Delta Force](328809.md) and [Army Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/75th_Ranger_Regiment), which is tasked to pursue the most sensitive high-value targets such as Al Qaeda and the Taliban leadership since 11 September 2001. Rather, TF 77, which started out as [Task Force 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_Force_11) and has gone through a number of name/number changes, reports directly to [Joint Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Special_Operations_Command), part of USSOCOM.&#x20;

As of 2015, CENTCOM forces are deployed primarily in Iraq and Afghanistan in combat roles and have support roles at bases in [Kuwait](7515890.md), [Bahrain](18933277.md), [Qatar](8238258.md), the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), [Oman](22316.md), and [central Asia](6742.md). CENTCOM forces have also been deployed in Jordan and Saudi Arabia.&#x20;

## Geographic scope



Current as of 1 June 2018

With the 1983 establishment of CENTCOM Egypt, Sudan, Kenya, Ethiopia, Somalia, and Djibouti came within the [area of responsibility](9452512.md) (AOR). Thus CENTCOM directed the 'Natural Bond' exercises with Sudan, the 'Eastern Wind' exercises with Somalia, and the 'Jade Tiger' exercises with Oman, Somalia, and Sudan. Exercise Jade Tiger involved the [31st Marine Expeditionary Unit](5885654.md) with Oman from 29 November 1982 to 8 December 1982.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEArkin2005404-43]&#x20;

On 7 February 2007, plans were announced for the creation of a [United States Africa Command](9347800.md) which transferred responsibility for U.S. military operations across Africa to the new USAFRICOM, except for Egypt. On 1 October 2008, the Africa Command became operational and [Combined Joint Task Force-Horn of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Joint_Task_Force-Horn_of_Africa), the primary CENTCOM force on the continent, started reporting to AFRICOM at [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) instead of CENTCOM in Tampa.&#x20;

The Department of Defense uses a variable number of base locations depending on its level of operations. With ongoing warfare in Iraq and Afghanistan in 2003, the [United States Air Force](32090.md) used 35 bases, while in 2006 it used 14, including four in Iraq. The [United States Navy](20518076.md) maintains one major base and one smaller installation, with extensive deployments afloat and ashore by U.S. Navy, [U.S. Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Marine_Corps) and [U.S. Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Coast_Guard) ships, aviation units and ground units.&#x20;

### Secretary of Defense

Two of the former U.S. Central Command Commanders would later serve as [United States Secretary of Defense](43998.md): General [James Mattis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mattis) and General [Lloyd Austin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_Austin). Mattis served as the 26th U.S. Secretary of Defense. Austin became the 28th U.S. Secretary of Defense on 22 January 2021, serving until 20 January 2025.[^cite_note-44]&#x20;
