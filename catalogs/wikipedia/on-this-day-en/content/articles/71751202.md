The **Personal Information Protection Commission** is the [national data protection authority](42742946.md) of [South Korea](27019.md). It is formed as an [independent agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_agency) in 2011 by the 'Personal Information Protection Act (PIPA, [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 개인정보 보호법)',[^cite_note-PIPA-2] and is now located in [Government Complex Seoul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Government_Complex_\(Seoul\)). The Commission is constituted with 9 commissioners and one of them is the [Chairperson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairperson_of_the_Personal_Information_Protection_Commission_\(South_Korea\)), who is appointed by the [President of South Korea](98630.md).

## History

The PIPA of South Korea was first enacted in 2011 to establish a general and comprehensive basis for regulation on [data protection](237536.md), overcoming formerly diffused and conflicting regulations around each type of data. The Act also had a goal to form PIPC as integrated apex authority governing over all data protection issues in South Korea. However, each of laws governing specified types of data continued to exist due to several legal issues, so PIPC was launched in 2011 with rather limited power to govern data protection issues. This limited position is reflected in organizational status of PIPC in 2011, as it was not an independent agency, yet rather an advisory panel for [Ministry of the Interior and Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_and_Safety_\(South_Korea\)) which was in charge of enforcing PIPA at that time.[^cite_note-3]

Later in 2020, the PIPA was amended to give complete power to PIPC as [independent regulatory agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_regulatory_agency), with power to investigate personal data privacy failure cases and jurisdiction over adjudicating complaints and disputes on personal information.[^cite_note-4] Now under article 7-8 and 7-9 of the PIPA, the PIPC can impose administrative fines. Also by article 7(1), the PIPC is positioned as independent agency under [Prime Minister](606354.md), and article 7(2) guarantees that investigation and adjudication of the PIPC cannot be supervised by the Prime Minister.[^cite_note-PIPA-2] This new power of the PIPC draws significant attempts to regulate big techs in South Korea. For example, the PIPC fined [Facebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facebook_\(company\)) \$6.1 million in November 2020 for sharing user's personal data without consent.[^cite_note-5] Also in September 2022, the PIPC fined [Google](1092923.md) \$50 million and [Meta Platforms](62420226.md) \$22 million for violating South Korean privacy regulations.[^cite_note-6]

## Organization

Further information: [Chairperson of the Personal Information Protection Commission (South Korea)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairperson_of_the_Personal_Information_Protection_Commission_\(South_Korea\))

After amendment of the PIPA in 2020, the PIPC is now a 'central administrative agency ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 중앙행정기관)', which is identical in status to executive departments of the [South Korean government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korean_government). Among nine commissioners of the PIPC, its [Chairperson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairperson_of_the_Personal_Information_Protection_Commission_\(South_Korea\)) is treated as one of minister in the government.[^cite_note-7] According to amended article 7-2(2) of the PIPA, both the Chairperson and the vice are only permanent commissioners among nine commissioners, and they are appointed by the [President](98630.md) with proposition from the [Prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_South_Korea). Under article 7-2(2) of the PIPA, other seven non-permanent commissioners are also appointed by the [President](98630.md) via following recommendations; two by recommendation of the Chairperson, other two from recommendation of [ruling party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruling_party), and other three from recommendation of [opposition parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_parties).[^cite_note-PIPA-2]
