The **Japan Series** (日本シリーズ *Nippon Shiriizu*, officially the **Japan Championship Series**, プロ野球日本選手権シリーズ *Puro Yakyū Nippon Senshuken Shiriizu*),[^cite_note-sponichi141101-1] also the **Nippon Series**,[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] is the annual [championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Championship) series in [Nippon Professional Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Professional_Baseball), the top [baseball](3850.md) league in Japan. It is a [best-of-seven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best-of-seven) series between the winning clubs of the league's two circuits, the [Central League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_League) and the [Pacific League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_League), and is played in October or November. The first team to win four games is the overall winner and is declared the Japan Series Champion (日本一, *Nippon Ichi*; number one in Japan) each year. The Japan Series uses a 2–3–2 format, with potential extra games if needed following ties.

The [home](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_advantage) team for games 1, 2 and eventually 6 and 7, alternates between the two leagues with the Pacific League having the advantage on the years ending with an odd number and the Central League on the years ending with an even number. [Designated hitters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designated_hitter) are used if the team from the Pacific League hosts the game. There is a 40-man postseason roster limit, and the rule on drawn games is changed to 12 innings, since 2018. If the series is tied after the seventh game, a Game 8 will be held with the same team hosting Games 6 and 7 hosting this game. Only once has a Game 8 been played in Japan Series history, where the [Seibu Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seibu_Lions) defeated the [Hiroshima Toyo Carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima_Toyo_Carp) in 1986. In the event that Game 8 does not decide the series, the next game would be played at the stadium that hosted Games 3 through 5 after a day of rest, and games will continue until one team wins four games.

Every current team in the NPB has won the Japan Series at least once. The team with the most championships is the [Yomiuri Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomiuri_Giants), who have won the Japan Series twenty-two times. In 2004, the Pacific League instituted a three-team stepladder [playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playoff) format to determine the league [champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champion), while the Central League champion had a long wait before the Japan Series. During this time, the Pacific League won four consecutive Series from 2003 to 2006. Starting with the 2007 postseason, both leagues adopted the [Climax Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climax_Series) to determine their champions. The Climax Series involves the top three finishers in each league, though the format gives a significant advantage to the team with the best record in each league.

On November 6, 2010, the [Chunichi Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chunichi_Dragons) and [Chiba Lotte Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiba_Lotte_Marines) played the longest game in Japan Series history. It lasted fifteen innings and resulted in a 2–2 draw, with the game lasting 5 hours and 43 minutes.[^cite_note-4] Only Game 2 of the 2022 Japan Series on October 23, 2022, came close, with the [Orix Buffaloes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orix_Buffaloes) and [Tokyo Yakult Swallows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Yakult_Swallows) playing to a 3–3 draw after 12 innings, lasting 5 hours and 3 minutes.

Even though the Central League is historically more victorious, in recent years, the Pacific League has been catching up in titles. Currently, each league has won 38 times. The Pacific League won eight consecutive Japan Series championships from 2013 to 2020, with six by the [Fukuoka SoftBank Hawks](318122.md), and one each by the [Hokkaido Nippon-Ham Fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaido_Nippon-Ham_Fighters) and [Tohoku Rakuten Golden Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tohoku_Rakuten_Golden_Eagles). The streak was broken in 2021, with the [Tokyo Yakult Swallows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Yakult_Swallows) beating the [Orix Buffaloes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orix_Buffaloes).

### Extra inning rules

Since 2018, there is a limit of 12 innings in the seven regulation games, with games being called tie games if tied after that time. From 1987 to 2020, the innings limit is abolished starting in extra games (Game 8, et al) if necessary. Since 2021, extra games are played to 12 innings as normal. Starting in the 13th inning of extra games, the [World Baseball Softball Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Baseball_Softball_Confederation) two-runner tiebreaker with runners at first and second base, similar to the [Japanese High School Baseball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_High_School_Baseball_Championship) (Koshien), will be implemented.

Historically:

- Until 1966 (except 1964): Game is called at sunset (all games were played as day games)&#x20;

  - 1964 (all games at night): No new inning may start after 10\:30 p.m.

- 1967–1981: No new inning may start after 5\:30 p.m.

- 1982–1986: No new inning may start after the game time reaches four-and-a-half hours

- 1987–1993: 18-inning limit until Game 7, unlimited innings Game 8 and onward (change introduced due to Game 8 being necessary in the 1986 series due to Game 1 being called after 14 innings)

- 1994: 18- (day game) / 15-inning (night game) limit until Game 7, unlimited innings Game 8 and onward

- 1995–2017: / 15-inning limit until Game 7, unlimited innings Game 8 and onward&#x20;

  - The three-and-a-half hour cut-off rule used in the [2011 regular season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Nippon_Professional_Baseball_season) was not used for the Japan Series.

- 2018–2020: 12-inning limit until Game 7, unlimited innings Game 8 and onward

- 2021–present: 12-inning limit. Starting in Game 8, further innings played use [WBSC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Baseball_Softball_Confederation) two-runner tiebreaker.

## Managers with multiple championships

Since 1950, fifteen managers have won the Japan Series multiple times. [Osamu Mihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osamu_Mihara) (Whales, Lions), [Shigeru Mizuhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeru_Mizuhara) (Giants, Flyers), and [Tatsuro Hirooka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatsuro_Hirooka) (Swallows, Lions) are the only managers to have multiple teams to wins in the Japan Series. In terms of pennants, eight managers have won a league pennant with multiple teams: [Katsuya Nomura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katsuya_Nomura), [Sadaharu Oh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadaharu_Oh), Shigeru Mizuhara, Osamu Mihara, Tatusro Hirooka, [Masataka Nashida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masataka_Nashida), [Yukio Nishimoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukio_Nishimoto), and [Senichi Hoshino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senichi_Hoshino). Nishimoto and Hoshino are the only ones to lead three different teams to the Japan Series.

## Streaks and droughts

- The Yomiuri Giants won nine consecutive Japan Series championships from 1965 to 1973. The second-longest streak is four consecutive championships, accomplished by the Fukuoka SoftBank Hawks from 2017 to 2020. Three consecutive championships have been accomplished by the Yomiuri Giants (1951–1953), the Nishitetsu Lions (1956–1958), the Hankyu Braves (1975–1977), and twice by the Seibu Lions (1986–1988 and 1990–1992).

- The Yomiuri Giants also won five consecutive Central League pennants from 1955 to 1959, and 19 of the 23 Central League pennants from 1951 to 1973. The Giants also won two or three consecutive Central League pennants on several other occasions. The other teams to win consecutive Central League pennants are the Hiroshima Toyo Carp (1979–1980 and 2016–2018), the Yakult Swallows (1992–1993 and 2021–2022), and the Chunichi Dragons (2006–2007 and 2010–2011).

- The Nankai Hawks won three consecutive Pacific League pennants twice (1951–1953 and 1964–1966). The Hankyu Braves won nine of the 12 Pacific League pennants from 1967 to 1978, including three consecutive (1967–1969) and four consecutive (1975–1978). The Seibu Lions won 11 of the 14 Pacific League pennants from 1985 to 1998, including four consecutive (1985–1988) and five consecutive (1990–1994). The Orix Buffaloes are the most recent team to win three consecutive Pacific League pennants (2021–2023).

## Individual awards

See also: [Japan Series Most Valuable Player Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Series_Most_Valuable_Player_Award)

Two individual awards are given out at the conclusion of the Japan Series: the [Most Valuable Player Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Series_Most_Valuable_Player_Award), given to the most impactful player on the winning team; and the Fighting Spirit Award, given to the most impactful player on the losing team.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] As stated above, the FSA was only given to a player on the winning team once; [Kazuhisa Inao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuhisa_Inao) won it in 1956, despite being on the winning side, the Nishitetsu Lions.

Six players have won the Japan Series MVP Award and gone on to become a manager in the Japan Series: [Hiroki Kokubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroki_Kokubo), [Kimiyasu Kudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimiyasu_Kudo), [Masaaki Mori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaaki_Mori), [Osamu Higashio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osamu_Higashio), [Shigeo Nagashima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shigeo_Nagashima), and [Tetsuharu Kawakami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetsuharu_Kawakami); of those six, five (Kudo, Mori, Nagashima, Kawakami, Kokubo) have won a MVP Award as a player and won a Japan Series as a manager.

Eight players have won both the MVP Award and the Fighting Spirit Award: [Hiromichi Ishige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiromichi_Ishige), [Hiroshi Oshita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshi_Oshita), [Hisashi Yamada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisashi_Yamada), [Isao Shibata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isao_Shibata), [Joe Stanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Stanka), [Kazuhisa Inao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuhisa_Inao), [Atsunori Inaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atsunori_Inaba), and [Takashi Nishimoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takashi_Nishimoto).

Players to have won the Fighting Spirit Award multiple times include: [Mitsuhiro Adachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitsuhiro_Adachi) (3), [Kazuhiro Kiyohara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuhiro_Kiyohara), [Kazuhiro Wada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuhiro_Wada), [Kazuhisa Inao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuhisa_Inao), [Kenshin Kawakami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenshin_Kawakami), and [Tokuji Nagaike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokuji_Nagaike) (2 each).
