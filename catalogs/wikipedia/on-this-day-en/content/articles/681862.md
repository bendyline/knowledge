The **Switzerland national football team** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Schweizer Fussballnationalmannschaft*, [Italian](14708.md): *Nazionale di calcio della Svizzera*, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Équipe nationale suisse de football*, [Romansh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language): *Squadra naziunala da ballape da la Svizra*, [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Turma Pediludica Nationalis Helvetica*) represents [Switzerland](26748.md) in men's international [football](10568.md). The national team is controlled by the [Swiss Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Football_Association).

Switzerland's best performances at the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) have been four quarter-final appearances, in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup), [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup), [1954](60523.md) and [2026](36581929.md). They hosted the competition in 1954, where they played against [Austria](695226.md) in their quarter-final match, losing [7–5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_v_Switzerland_\(1954_FIFA_World_Cup\)), which still stands as the highest scoring World Cup match ever.[^cite_note-RSSSF54-2] At the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), Switzerland set a FIFA World Cup record by being eliminated from the tournament despite not conceding a single goal, being eliminated by [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team) after penalties in the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup#Round_of_16). They did not concede a goal until a match against [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) at the [2010 World Cup](168079.md), conceding in the [75th minute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_H#Chile_vs_Switzerland), setting a World Cup tournament record for consecutive minutes without conceding a goal.[^cite_note-3]

Switzerland and [Austria](26964606.md) were the co-hosts of [UEFA Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008), where the Swiss made their third appearance in the competition, but failed for a third time to progress from the group stage.[^cite_note-auto-4][^cite_note-Switzerland_1-2_Turkey-5] However, since then, the Swiss made it to the round of 16 during [Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016), and achieved a record-best quarter-final showing at [Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) after eliminating world champions [France](250197.md). They then repeated this feat at [Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024), this time beating reigning champions [Italy](362466.md).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

Overall, Switzerland's best ever result at an official football competition was the [silver medal](526647.md) they earned in [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1924_Summer_Olympics), after losing to [Uruguay](679782.md) 3–0 in the finals of the [1924 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-fifa1924-8]

## History

### 1924–1966: early years, World Cup host nation



The Uruguay v. Switzerland line-up in the [Gold medal match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1924_Summer_Olympics#Gold_medal_match) at the [1924 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Summer_Olympics), held in [Paris](22989.md)

At the [1924 Paris Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1924_Summer_Olympics), Switzerland finished with a silver medal after losing to [Uruguay](679782.md) in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1924_Summer_Olympics), losing 3–0.[^cite_note-fifa1924-8] The team's debut appearance at the [World Cup](11370.md) was in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup), where they reached the [quarterfinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup#Quarterfinals) after beating the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) [3–2 in the round of sixteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup#Round_of_16) before getting knocked out by [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Switzerland once again reached the [quarterfinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup#Quarterfinals) in [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup), after beating [Germany](250204.md) in the [round of sixteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup#Round_of_16), winning 4–2 after a [replay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup#Replays) but were knocked out by [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team), losing 2–0.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] At the [1950 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup), Switzerland were drawn in a group with [Brazil](149286.md), [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_football_team) and [Mexico](808402.md), where they lost 4–0 to Yugoslavia in the opening match, drew 2–2 with Brazil in their second match and beating Mexico 2–1 in their final group mach, and finished third in their group.[^cite_note-14] On 22 July 1946, Switzerland was awarded the right to host the [1954 World Cup](60523.md) unopposed, in [Luxembourg City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxembourg_City).[^cite_note-15] At the World Cup, Switzerland finished second in their [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_4) behind [England](9904.md); beating [Italy](362466.md) and losing to England,[^cite_note-16] but qualified for the [quarterfinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Quarterfinals) after beating Italy in a group [play-off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playoffs).[^cite_note-17] They were knocked out of the tournament after losing [7–5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_v_Switzerland_\(1954_FIFA_World_Cup\)) to [Austria](695226.md).[^cite_note-RSSSF54-2] At the [1962 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup), Switzerland finished bottom of the group, losing all three games, losing 3–1 to [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team), 2–1 to [West Germany](250204.md) and 3–0 to Italy.[^cite_note-18] A similar result occurred at the [1966 World Cup](61629.md), where Switzerland again finished at the bottom of their group losing all three of their matches, 5–0 to West Germany, 2–1 to [Spain](609825.md) and 2–0 to [Argentina](454699.md).[^cite_note-19]

### 1992–1996: the Roy Hodgson era

In 1992, Switzerland appointed English manager [Roy Hodgson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Hodgson) as head coach of the national team; at the time of his appointment, the Swiss had not qualified for any major tournament since 1966.[^cite_note-20] Under his guidance, Switzerland rose to 3rd in the [FIFA World Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings) in August 1993, which still remains their highest FIFA ranking to this day.[^cite_note-21] Hodgson led Switzerland to the [1994 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup), losing just one game during qualifying, in a [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA_%E2%80%93_Group_1\)) that included [Italy](362466.md), [Portugal](576328.md), and [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team).[^cite_note-22] The Swiss won their home tie with Italy, and in the away game, took a 2–0 lead before being pegged back to a 2–2 draw, and also took four points from Scotland, winning 3–1 at home and drawing 1–1 away.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Against the Portuguese, Switzerland drew 1–1 at home and lost 1–0 in the away fixture in Porto, their only defeat of the qualifying campaign.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Their opening match against hosts [United States](582488.md), on 18 June 1994, was played indoors at the [Pontiac Silverdome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac_Silverdome) in [Detroit](8687.md), and the two teams drew 1–1 in the opening match of the World Cup.[^cite_note-28] In the next match, they won 4–1 over Romania, and in their final game against Colombia, the Swiss lost 2–0.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Nevertheless, Switzerland still qualified from [the group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_A), but were knocked out by [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_men's_national_football_team), losing 3–0.[^cite_note-31]

### 2000–2008: the Köbi Kuhn era

At UEFA Euro 1996, Switzerland once again easily qualified for the tournament hosted in [England](9316.md), as they topped their qualifying group, losing just once, which was a 1–2 defeat to Turkey.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] They were drawn in [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_1996_Group_A), but their tournament was disappointing overall; as they finished bottom of the group.[^cite_note-34] Their opening match was against hosts [England](9904.md), and the two sides drew 1–1.[^cite_note-35] In their second match, they lost 2–0 to the Netherlands, and in their final group game, lost 1–0 to Scotland.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Switzerland failed to qualify for the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), hosted in [France](5843419.md), as they finished [fourth in their qualifying group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_3), winning three games; 3–2 against [Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finland_national_football_team), 1–0 against [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) and 5–0 against [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_national_football_team), drawing one game against Hungary (1–1), and losing three games; 1–0 against Azerbaijan and losing both games against [Norway](203277.md), losing 1–0 at home and 5–0 away.[^cite_note-38]

In [qualifying for UEFA Euro 2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004_qualifying_Group_10), Switzerland finished top of a group that featured [Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia_national_football_team), the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team), [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania_national_football_team) and [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgia_national_football_team).[^cite_note-39] The Swiss finished with 21 points and qualified for the [finals in Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004), where they were drawn in [Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004_Group_B) with [defending champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000) [France](250197.md), [England](9904.md) and [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team). They began the tournament with a [0–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004_Group_B#Switzerland_vs_Croatia) draw with Croatia before succumbing to a [3–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004_Group_B#England_vs_Switzerland) defeat to England in the next match.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] They lost their final match against France; losing [3–1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2004_Group_B#Switzerland_vs_France) and finishing bottom of the group.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Their only goal of the entire tournament was scored by [Johan Vonlanthen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Vonlanthen), who became the youngest ever goalscorer at the *Euros* when he scored the equalizing goal against France; surpassing the previous record set only four days earlier by [Wayne Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Rooney) by three months.[^cite_note-44]

The Swiss managed to qualify for the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), overcoming [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) by the [away goals rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_goals_rule) in [Istanbul](3391396.md), the country's first World Cup since 1994.[^cite_note-45] In the tournament, Switzerland was drawn in [Group G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_G) with former world champions [France](250197.md), 2002 World Cup's fourth-place finisher [South Korea](1018627.md) and debutant [Togo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togo_national_football_team). In their first encounter, Switzerland bravely held the mighty France and [Zinedine Zidane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinedine_Zidane) 0–0,[^cite_note-46] before overcoming the Togolese 2–0 in the second match, tied with the South Koreans four points, however the Swiss were inferior to the Koreans by number of goals scored, meaning that the last game a must-win.[^cite_note-47] The Swiss then managed to beat South Korea 2–0 in the final match, occupying first place in their group while knocking the Asians out of the tournament.[^cite_note-48] In the round of sixteen, Switzerland faced [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_national_football_team), but lost on penalty shootout in a match that has been criticized as the worst game in World Cup history.[^cite_note-49] Yet, Switzerland was the only team to be eliminated without conceding a single goal.

Switzerland, along with Austria, were chosen as co-hosts of [UEFA Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008).[^cite_note-50] The Swiss were drawn in [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008_Group_A) with [Portugal](576328.md), [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_team) and the [Czech Republic](729739.md).[^cite_note-auto-4] Their opening match was a 1–0 loss to the Czech Republic, followed by a 1–2 defeat to Turkey.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-Switzerland_1-2_Turkey-5] Their third match was against Portugal, with Switzerland winning 2–0 to ensure that Portugal would top their group with a defeat.[^cite_note-52]

### 2008–2014: the Ottmar Hitzfeld era

In their first match at the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md), the team defeated eventual champions [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_men's_national_football_team) 1–0 with a goal by [Gelson Fernandes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelson_Fernandes), but they were still eliminated in the [group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_H).[^cite_note-53] In the second match, a goal scored by [Mark González](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Gonz%C3%A1lez) in the 75th minute of the game against [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) ended a 559-minute streak without conceding a goal in World Cup matches, beating the record previously held by [Italy](362466.md) by nine minutes.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-uefa2012-56] Switzerland did not advance further than the group after a 0–0 draw with [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team) in the third and final group match.[^cite_note-57]

Switzerland did not qualify for [UEFA Euro 2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2012); missing out on the tournament for the first time in a decade, as they finished third in the qualifying group, a group featuring [England](9904.md), [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegro_national_football_team), [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team) and [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team).[^cite_note-uefa2012-56] Switzerland's initial start in qualifying was overall poor; losing 1–3 to England in the first game played, in which [Xherdan Shaqiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xherdan_Shaqiri) scored his first goal for the national team, followed by a 1–0 defeat to Montenegro.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] Switzerland then recorded a 4–1 win over Wales before consecutive draws against Bulgaria (0–0) and England (2–2).[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] Switzerland's hopes of qualifying were restored with a 3–1 win over Bulgaria, with a [hat-trick](411541.md) from Xherdan Shaqiri.[^cite_note-64] However, following a 2–0 loss to Wales (in which [Reto Ziegler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reto_Ziegler) earned a [red card](3090014.md#Red_card_\(dismissal\))) and Montenegro's surprising last-minute equalizer against England in a 2–2 draw, Switzerland's hopes of qualifying were mathematically made impossible.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] In the final game, Switzerland earned redemption against Montenegro as they came out with a 2–0 win.[^cite_note-67] Switzerland's top goalscorer during the qualifying period was Xherdan Shaqiri, with 4 goals.[^cite_note-68]

At the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) in Brazil, Switzerland were drawn to play [France](250197.md), [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team) and [Ecuador](1016797.md) in the [group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E).[^cite_note-69] They advanced to the [round of sixteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Argentina_vs_Switzerland) with a 3–0 win over [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team), with a hat-trick from [Xherdan Shaqiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xherdan_Shaqiri). In the knockout match against Argentina, they lost 1–0, conceding to [Ángel Di María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Di_Mar%C3%ADa) in the 118th minute.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

### 2016–2021: the Vladimir Petković era

At [Euro 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016), Switzerland were selected to play in [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_Group_A) of the tournament; alongside hosts [France](250197.md), [Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albania_national_football_team) and [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_national_football_team).[^cite_note-72] In the first game, Switzerland won [1–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_Group_A#Albania_vs_Switzerland) over Albania, with the only goal being scored by [Fabian Schär](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabian_Sch%C3%A4r) in the fifth minute of the game.[^cite_note-73] The next match was a [1–1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_Group_A#Romania_vs_Switzerland) draw with Romania, with Switzerland initially conceding from a [penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_kick_\(association_football\)) but equalizing in the second half following a goal from [Admir Mehmedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admir_Mehmedi).[^cite_note-74] The final group game was against France, drawing [0–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_Group_A#Switzerland_vs_France). However, the game spread notoriety for several Swiss players' jerseys being ripped during challenges with the French players, and also for the ball bursting during a challenge between [Antoine Griezmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Griezmann) and [Valon Behrami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valon_Behrami) when they both converged on the ball, with the game also attracting attention for its poor surface, which was criticised by both coaches and players of the two teams; after the game, Switzerland's kit manufacturer had blamed "faulty material" for the incidents regarding the jerseys being ripped.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] Switzerland, due to the draw, finished second in [the group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_Group_A#Standings) to set up a tie against [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) in the [round of sixteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_knockout_phase#Switzerland_vs_Poland); initially the Swiss conceded but managed to find a late equalizer from [Xherdan Shaqiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xherdan_Shaqiri), who scored a [bicycle-kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_kick) to send the game into extra-time, but the Swiss were knocked out as [Granit Xhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granit_Xhaka) had missed the second penalty during the [penalty shootout](511302.md), as all other players managed to convert their penalties, with [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) winning 5–4 on penalties to go through and knock out the Swiss.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] In [qualifying for the 2018 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)), Switzerland were drawn with [Portugal](576328.md), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team), [Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroe_Islands_national_football_team), [Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia_national_football_team) and [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra_national_football_team).[^cite_note-QualEur-81] The Swiss began their [qualifying group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_B) with a shock 2–0 win over [European champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2016_Final) Portugal, who had won the tournament less than two months prior to playing with them on 6 September.[^cite_note-82] Afterwards, they beat Hungary 2–3, Andorra 2–1, Faroe Islands 2–0, Latvia 1–0 in the first five games, leading the group on maximum points.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] In the reverse fixtures, they beat Faroe Islands 2–0, Andorra 3–0, Latvia 3–0 and Hungary 5–2,[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] before facing Portugal in the final group game, where they lost 2–0,[^cite_note-91] meaning they would have to play in the [play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round); where they were ranked as the drawn to play [Northern Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_national_football_team). In the [first leg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round#Northern_Ireland_v_Switzerland), played on 9 November, they won 1–0 through a controversial penalty scored by [Ricardo Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Rodr%C3%ADguez_\(footballer\)), and three days later played in the [second leg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_second_round#Northern_Ireland_v_Switzerland), drawing 0–0 and advancing to the [World Cup finals in Russia](13327177.md) with a 1–0 aggregate win.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] Before the [World Cup](11370.md), Switzerland were ranked 6th in the [world ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings), even ranking higher than eventual [World Cup winners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) [France](250197.md).[^cite_note-96]

At the World Cup, Switzerland were drawn to play [Brazil](149286.md), [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_national_football_team) and [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team) in [Group E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E).[^cite_note-98] They began their campaign with a [1–1 draw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E#Brazil_vs_Switzerland) with Brazil,[^cite_note-99] before beating Serbia [2–1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E#Serbia_vs_Switzerland) through a late winning goal from [Xherdan Shaqiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xherdan_Shaqiri).[^cite_note-100] The game with Serbia sparked controversy for the celebrations performed by goalscorers Xherdan Shaqiri and [Granit Xhaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granit_Xhaka) (both ethnic [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians)), along with [Stephan Lichtsteiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephan_Lichtsteiner) as the trio performed a celebration where [they crossed their hands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossed_hands) to depict a [double-headed eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-headed_eagle#Albania), the [official emblem of Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Albania), considered by many as an [Albanian nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_nationalism) symbol, however, they were not banned by FIFA for this.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] Their final group game was with Costa Rica; which they drew [2–2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_E#Switzerland_vs_Costa_Rica), with [Blerim Džemaili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blerim_D%C5%BEemaili) and [Josip Drmić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Drmi%C4%87) scoring; thus finishing second in the group.[^cite_note-105] They were drawn to play [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) in the [round of sixteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Round_of_16), a fixture they lost [1–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Sweden_vs_Switzerland), getting knocked out of the tournament.[^cite_note-106]

On 23 January 2018, Switzerland were selected to play in [the inaugural edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Nations_League) of the [UEFA Nations League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Nations_League), a tournament contested by all [UEFA member's national teams](32332.md#Members), being drawn to play in [League A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Nations_League#League_A), in [Group 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_UEFA_Nations_League#Group_2), against [Belgium](679716.md) and [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_national_football_team).[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108]

At [Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020), postponed to 2021 due to [COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Europe), Switzerland finished third in [Group A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_Group_A) which contained Italy, Wales and Turkey; however, they managed to qualify to the knockout stage as one of the best third-placed teams. In the [round of 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020_knockout_phase), they defeated World Cup champions France on penalties, after finishing 3–3 and overcoming a 1–3 second half deficit, to have their first knockout phase win in a major tournament since the 1938 World Cup.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] In the subsequent quarterfinal game against [Spain](609825.md), they once again took the game to penalties, after trailing 1–0. However, after converting only one of their four penalties, they exited the tournament at this stage.[^cite_note-111]

### 2021–present: the Murat Yakin era

On 9 August 2021, [Murat Yakin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murat_Yakin) became the manager of the Swiss national team.[^cite_note-112] During the [2022 World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(UEFA\)), Switzerland finished in the first place ahead of Italy in [Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_UEFA_Group_C), which granted them a spot in the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) in Qatar.[^cite_note-113] During the World Cup, Switzerland finished second in [Group G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_G) to qualify the round of 16, where they lost 6–1 to Portugal.[^cite_note-114] In 2023, Switzerland played against Romania, [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_national_football_team), [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarus_national_football_team), [Kosovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_national_football_team), and Andorra to [qualify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024_qualifying_Group_I) for [Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024). The Swiss finished second behind Romania.[^cite_note-115]

The [Euro 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024) tournament was a major success for Switzerland, as they finished second in their group with 5 points, moving onto the Round of 16. During the Round of 16, Switzerland caused a major upset by defeating defending champions [Italy](362466.md) and reaching the quarterfinals for the second time in their history.[^cite_note-116]

### Kit

The Switzerland national team's traditional home kit is red shirts, white shorts and red socks, with the away kit being reverse with white shirts, red shorts and white socks, although all-red and all-white kits are not uncommon. Switzerland, since being established in 1895, have always had the same colour code, as tradition and homage to the national colours which are derived from the [Swiss flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Switzerland). The current kit manufacturer is [Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puma_\(brand\)), who have made their kits since 1998.

## Competitive record

The best result Switzerland have achieved thus far is the quarter-finals of the [World Cup](11370.md) on four occasions, in [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_FIFA_World_Cup), [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_FIFA_World_Cup), [1954](60523.md) and [2026](36581929.md), while they also reached the same stage at [Euro 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2020) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2024). They earned a silver medal at the [1924 Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Summer_Olympics), held in [Paris](22989.md), where they lost 3–0 to [Uruguay](679782.md) in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1924_Summer_Olympics#Gold_medal_match). This tournament, along with the 1928 edition, are considered by FIFA as World Cup finals.[^cite_note-fifa1924-8]
