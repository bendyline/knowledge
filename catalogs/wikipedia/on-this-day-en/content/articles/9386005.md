The **Bolivian Air Force** (**BAF**;[^cite_note-WL-1] [Spanish](26825.md): *Fuerza Aérea Boliviana*, *FAB*) is the [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force) of [Bolivia](3462.md) and branch of the [Bolivian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivian_Armed_Forces).

## History

By 1938 the Bolivian air force consisted of about 60 aircraft ([Curtiss Hawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtiss_Hawk) fighters, [Curtiss T-32 Condor II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtiss_T-32_Condor_II) and [Junkers W 34](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_W_34) bombers, [Junkers Ju 86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Ju_86) used as transport craft, and [Fokker C.V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_C.V), [Breguet 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breguet_19) and [Vickers Vespa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_Vespa) reconnaissance planes), and about 300 staff; the officers were trained in Italy.[^cite_note-SchnitzlerFeuchterSchulz-2]

In 2017 Bolivia finally retired the [Lockheed T-33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_T-33) marking the end of 44 years of service. Bolivia was the last operator of the T-33.[^cite_note-lastt33-3]

## Organization

FAB is organized into air brigades, which is formed by one to three air groups. The air groups are based at [La Paz](42821.md), Cochabamba, [Santa Cruz de la Sierra](673987.md), Puerto Suárez, Tarija, Villamontes, Cobija, Trinidad, Riberalta, Roboré, Uyuni, Oruro, Sucre and Chimoré.

## Civil aviation

The [General Directorate of Civil Aeronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direcci%C3%B3n_General_de_Aeron%C3%A1utica_Civil_\(Bolivia\)) (*Dirección General de Aeronáutica Civil—DGAC*) formerly part of the FAB, administers a civil aeronautics school called the National Institute of Civil Aeronautics (*Instituto Nacional de Aeronáutica Civil—INAC*), and two commercial air transport services; TAM and TAB.

### Bolivian Military Airline (TAM)

[Bolivian Military Airline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAM_-_Transporte_A%C3%A9reo_Militar) (*Transporte Aéreo Militar / TAM*) is an [airline](1942.md) based in [La Paz](42821.md), [Bolivia](3462.md). It is the civilian wing of the 'Fuerza Aérea Boliviana' (the Bolivian Air Force), operating passenger services to remote towns and communities in the North and Northeast of Bolivia. TAM (aka TAM Group 71) has been a part of the FAB since 1945.

A similar airline serving the [Beni Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beni_Department) with small planes is [Línea Aérea Amaszonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%ADnea_A%C3%A9rea_Amaszonas),[^cite_note-5] using smaller planes than TAM.

The Bolivian Ministry of Defence and Ministry of Public Works, Services and Housing announced on 8 December 2016 that TAM would cease transporting civilian passengers and cargo on 16 December 2016. The decision was to allow TAM to reorganize with a status akin to the state-sponsored [Boliviana de Aviacion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boliviana_de_Aviacion) prior to resuming service under civilian regulations.[^cite_note-6]

### Bolivian Air Transport (TAB)

Although a civil transport airline, [Bolivian Air Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAB_-_Transportes_A%C3%A9reos_Bolivianos) (*Transportes Aéreos Bolivianos / TAB*, was created as a subsidiary company of the FAB in 1977. It is subordinate to the Air Transport Management (*Gerencia de Transportes Aéreos*) and is headed by an FAB general. TAB, a charter heavy cargo airline, links Bolivia with most countries of the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md); its inventory included a fleet of [Lockheed C-130 Hercules](7697.md) aircraft. TAB's [Base of operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_of_operations) was headquartered at El Alto, adjacent to [La Paz](42821.md)'s [El Alto International Airport](2614073.md). TAB also flew to [Miami](53846.md) and [Houston](13774.md), with stops in [Panama](22997.md).

## Future acquisitions

During a 31 July 2017 ceremony, which was attended by the armed forces' high command, four models of lead-in fighter trainers (LIFT; [L-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongdu_JL-10), [M-346](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alenia_Aermacchi_M-346_Master), [T-50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KAI_T-50_Golden_Eagle), and [Yak-130](1513240.md)) were presented with a potential to replace the T-33 and reequip the GAC-31. The FAB's Commander expressed the FAB's preference for the Yak-130.[^cite_note-13]
