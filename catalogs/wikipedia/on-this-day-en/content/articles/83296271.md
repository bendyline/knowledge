Beginning in May 2026, several [U.S. states](18618239.md) have considered redrawing their [congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_district) ahead of the 2028 United States House of Representatives election, as a reaction to the [2025–2026 United States redistricting](81439539.md).

The redistricting began when the 2026 *[Louisiana v. Callais](80600781.md)* decision was released, which limited challenges to gerrymandered maps using the [Voting Rights Act of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_Rights_Act_of_1965), triggering Georgia Governor [Brian Kemp](27301061.md) to call a special session to redraw its congressional maps. In response, Democratic leaders urged state leaders in New York to redraw their own congressional map, which requires constitutional amendments and a referendum held in 2027.

## State redistricting efforts

### Georgia

On May 13, 2026, Governor [Brian Kemp](27301061.md) called for a special session of the Georgia Legislature to draw the state's congressional map based on the U.S. Supreme Courts decision in *Louisiana v. Callais*.[^cite_note-1] The new map is expected to dismantle at least one majority-minority district in the state that would allow Republicans to net gain at least 1 seat in the 2028 elections.[^cite_note-2]

### Missouri

[Proposition A](80917394.md) will appear on the November 3, 2026, ballot in the state of Missouri as a [veto referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_referendum). Voters will decide whether [congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri's_congressional_districts) that the [Missouri General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_General_Assembly) adopted in 2025 will or will not take effect.

In September 2025, Missouri lawmakers passed—and Missouri governor [Mike Kehoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Kehoe) signed into law—an act changing the state's congressional districts. The new districts dismantled the strongly democratic leaning [5th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri's_5th_congressional_district).&#x20;

However, before the map took effect, it faced a lawsuit and a veto referendum. The lawsuit argued the new map was unconstitutional, and violated the part of Missouri's Constitution stating that redistricting had to occur after a [United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_census). But the Missouri Supreme Court disagreed, ruling 4–3 that the state constitution requires redistricting to happen after a census, but does not otherwise explicitly prohibit it.

The veto referendum, in which voters decide whether to pass or reject a law, was initiated by a group named People Not Politicians Missouri after they gathered over 300,000 signatures. Although the number of signatures submitted was sufficient, Missouri Secretary of State [Denny Hoskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denny_Hoskins) refused to place the measure on the ballot, stating that it was an [unconstitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionality) measure.

On September 3, 2026, the [Supreme Court of Missouri](1771393.md) ruled that the veto referendum was constitutional, and that it could be held on redistricting, ordering the Secretary of State to use the enacted map from 2022 in the [November 2026 elections](79317975.md).[^cite_note-2026_Missouri_Proposition_A_\:6-3] Following the ruling, Missouri Attorney General [Catherine Hanaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Hanaway) filed an emergency appeal to the US Supreme Court and filed a motion requesting the state Supreme Court to pause its ruling from taking effect while the appeal plays out.[^cite_note-4] However, U.S. Supreme Court Justice [Brett Kavanaugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Kavanaugh) denied this request on September 8, keeping the 2022 map in effect for the 2026 election.[^cite_note-5] However, the same day, Chief U.S. District Judge [Stephen Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_R._Clark) of the [Eastern District of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Eastern_District_of_Missouri) issued a [temporary restraining order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_restraining_order) in a separate lawsuit prohibiting the state from using anything but the new 2025 map.[^cite_note-6] On September 10, the U.S. Supreme Court granted an emergency stay of Judge Clark's order, putting the 2022 map back into place for the 2026 elections.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] On September 21, the [United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Eighth_Circuit), ruling on the merits, affirmed the temporary restraining order and remanded the case to the lower court for entry of a [permanent injunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_injunction) enjoining Missouri to use the 2025 map, but stayed their ruling for a week pending appeal to the U.S. Supreme Court.[^cite_note-9] On September 25, the U.S. Supreme Court granted an emergency stay of the Eighth Circuit's order and Judge Clark's injunction, barring either court from issuing any new orders requiring the use of the 2025 map.[^cite_note-10]

### New York

Initially, in 2026, multiple Democratic leaders, including [Hakeem Jeffries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakeem_Jeffries), began to push New York Governor [Kathy Hochul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Hochul) to respond to redistricting in Texas and other Republican states by redrawing New York's congressional districts similar to California's process; however, the state's use of an Independent Redistricting Commission was the primary obstacle to achieving the goal in time for the 2026 elections.[^cite_note-11] To bypass the commission, the state house and state senate voted on one of the two constitutional amendments proposed this year, which passed on June 3, 2026.[^cite_note-12] The legislature will vote again after the 2026 elections, which would then allow the state to put the amendments on a referendum in 2027. If passed, it will take effect in time for the 2028 elections.[^cite_note-13]

### Maryland

Main article: [2026 Maryland Question 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Maryland_Question_3)

In 2026, [Maryland](18858.md) attempted to redraw its congressional maps with both Governor [Wes Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wes_Moore) and state Speaker of the House [Joseline Peña-Melnyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseline_Pe%C3%B1a-Melnyk)'s support. Despite a proposed map dismantling the sole Republican district passing the state house, state senate leader [Bill Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Ferguson_\(politician\)) opposed the passage of such map and refused to hold a vote.[^cite_note-14] However, after the *[Louisiana v. Callais](80600781.md)* ruling, Ferguson expressed interest in engaging in a discussion to redraw the congressional line before the 2028 elections.[^cite_note-15] Such a legislative session would likely involve a proposed constitutional amendment, which would then be proposed to voters as a ballot referendum.[^cite_note-16] The special legislative session to potential redistricting will take place in August 2026.[^cite_note-17] On August 26, a court blocked the referendum from appearing on the November ballot, a decision being appealed to the [Maryland Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryland_Supreme_Court).[^cite_note-18] On September 3, 2026, the Maryland Supreme Court ruled the ballot measure could appear on the November ballot, but ordered a wording change.[^cite_note-19]

## Potential expansion

### New Jersey

After the *Louisiana v. Callais* ruling was announced, [New Jersey](21648.md) Governor [Mikie Sherill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikie_Sherrill) announced her openness to redrawing the state's congressional districts, however, noted that the process would not be easy and would require a constitutional referendum.[^cite_note-20] Senate president [Nick Scutari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Scutari) also expressed interest in redrawing the map, however it is unknown whether Democrats have enough votes to go through with such proposal.[^cite_note-21]

### Washington

In May of 2026, [Washington](13015878.md) Governor [Bob Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Ferguson_\(politician\)) announced that he would support mid-decade redistricting if the Democrats won [supermajorities](638313.md) in both chambers of the [Washington Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Legislature) in the upcoming [2026 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Washington_House_of_Representatives_election).[^cite_note-22] Currently, Washington uses an independent redistricting commission to draw the state's legislative maps, which would need a constitutional amendment to get rid of. While not impossible, obtaining a supermajority for both chambers of the state legislature is difficult and will make redistricting attempts for the 2028 elections unlikely to happen.

### Wisconsin

In September 2026, the [Wisconsin Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Supreme_Court) heard oral arguments for a pair of Democratic lawsuits seeking to throw out the state's congressional map for the 2028 election.[^cite_note-23]
