**Gaza Freedom Flotilla** vessels and convoys have attempted to break the [blockade of Gaza](19224310.md) since 2010. The [first flotilla with six ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla) was launched by what would then become the [Freedom Flotilla Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flotilla_Coalition). It was subsequently [raided by Israeli forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid).

Since 2010, attempts from the [Freedom Flotilla II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flotilla_II) in 2011, [Freedom Flotilla III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flotilla_III) in 2015, [Women's Boat to Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Boat_to_Gaza) in 2016, Just Future for Palestine Flotilla in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla), and flotillas in [June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2025_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla) and [July 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2025_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla), have all been intercepted, raided or attacked by Israeli forces.

In August 2025, the Freedom Flotilla Coalition joined the [Global Sumud Flotilla](80704925.md), and began launching a series of flotilla convoys towards Gaza.

## Background

Further information: [Israeli blockade of the Gaza Strip (2023–present)](75013719.md), [Gaza humanitarian crisis (2023–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_humanitarian_crisis_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)), and [Gaza war](74998519.md)

In 2008, the first ships to breach the [Israeli blockade of the Gaza Strip](19224310.md) were launched by the [Free Gaza Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Gaza_Movement). Founded during the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War), activists launched 31 boats from 2008 to 2016, with five that succeeded in reaching Gaza.[^cite_note-AJ-1]

### 2010



Route of the [first Gaza Freedom Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla), June 2010

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [2010 Gaza Freedom Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2010_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla\&action=edit)]

The [2010 Gaza Freedom Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla) was a small [fleet](981096.md) of ships by the [Free Gaza Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Gaza_Movement) and the Turkish [Foundation for Human Rights and Freedoms and Humanitarian Relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHH_\(Turkish_NGO\)) (İHH). The Flotilla mission to Gaza had the explicit goal of carrying humanitarian aid and construction materials with the intention of breaking the Israeli [blockade of the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Gaza_Strip_\(2007-present\)).[^cite_note-2010_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla_FT_Attack-2][^cite_note-2010_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla_ibhsguardqa53-3][^cite_note-2010_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla_washingtonpost1-4] In typical circumstances, aid is first brought to Israel to be inspected and then transferred to Gaza.[^cite_note-5]

On 31 May 2010, Israeli forces [boarded the ships in a raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid) from speedboats and helicopters. Following resistance on one of the boats, nine activists were killed by Israeli forces. Widespread [international condemnation of and reaction to](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactions_to_the_Gaza_flotilla_raid) the raid followed, [Israel–Turkey relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Turkey_relations) were strained, and Israel subsequently eased its blockade on the Gaza Strip.

#### Raid



Routes of the Gaza-bound flotilla (green) and the Israeli Navy (orange)

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [2010 Gaza flotilla raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid\&action=edit)]

[Six civilian ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla) of the Gaza Freedom Flotilla were raided by Israel on 31 May 2010 in [international waters](394357.md) in the Mediterranean Sea. Nine of the [flotilla](981096.md) passengers were killed during the raid, with 30 wounded (including one who later died of his wounds).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid_Guardian_Shot-7] Ten Israeli soldiers were wounded, one seriously. The exact sequence of events is contested, in part due to the IDF's confiscation of the passengers' photographic evidence.[^cite_note-8] The flotilla, organized by the [Free Gaza Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Gaza_Movement) and the Turkish [Foundation for Human Rights and Freedoms and Humanitarian Relief (İHH)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHH_\(Turkish_NGO\)), was carrying humanitarian aid and construction materials, intending to break the Israeli naval [blockade of the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%93present_blockade_of_the_Gaza_Strip).

The Israeli Navy warned the flotilla via radio to stop approaching the naval blockade and to change course to the [port of Ashdod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Ashdod). This request was denied and on 31 May 2010, Israeli [Shayetet 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shayetet_13) naval commandos boarded the ships in international waters from speedboats[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUN_Palmer_Report201119-9] and helicopters. Aboard the Turkish ship [MV *Mavi Marmara*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MV_Mavi_Marmara), the Israeli Navy faced resistance from about 40 of the 590 passengers, including IHH activists[^cite_note-2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid_\:0-10] who were said to be armed with iron bars and knives.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUN_Palmer_Report201154–61-11] During the struggle, [nine](#Casualties) activists were killed, including eight [Turkish nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) and one [Turkish American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_American), and many were wounded.[^cite_note-12] On 23 May 2014, a tenth member of the flotilla died in hospital after being in a coma for four years.[^cite_note-2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid_imemc.org-13] Ten of the commandos were also wounded, one of them seriously.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUN_Palmer_Report201154–61-11][^cite_note-2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid_NYT-201005-14]

According to a [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md) report, all activist deaths were caused by gunshots, and "the circumstances of the killing of at least six of the passengers were in a manner consistent with an extra-legal, arbitrary and summary execution."[^cite_note-2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid_www2.ohchr.org-15] The five other ships in the flotilla employed [passive resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_resistance), which was suppressed without major incident. According to the UNHRC report, several of the passengers were injured and the leg of one was fractured.[^cite_note-2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid_www2.ohchr.org-15] The ships were towed to Israel. Some passengers were deported immediately, while about 600 were detained after they refused to sign deportation orders; a few of them were slated for prosecution. After international criticism, all of the detained activists were also deported.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] The raid drew [widespread condemnation internationally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reactions_to_the_2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid), resulted in a deterioration of [Israel–Turkey relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Turkey_relations), and Israel subsequently eased its blockade on the Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-18]

There were several probes into the incident. A [UNHRC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNHRC) report in September 2010 deemed the raid illegal and stated that Israel's actions were "disproportionate" and "betrayed an unacceptable level of brutality", with evidence of "wilful killing". [United Nations Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary-General) [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) announced a parallel probe in August 2010 by a four-member panel headed by [Geoffrey Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Palmer_\(New_Zealand_politician\)). The Palmer report was published in September 2011 and also concluded that the raid was illegal.[^cite_note-2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid_UNpanel5experts-19][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUN_Palmer_Report20114-20] The report stated that the degree of force used against the *Mavi Marmara* was "excessive and unreasonable",[^cite_note-2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid_Buchan-21] and that the way Israel treated detained crew members violated international human rights law.[^cite_note-2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid_Buchan-21] In response to the raid, Israel offered Turkey \$20 million in compensation,[^cite_note-22] which was discussed in March 2013,[^cite_note-2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid_bbc220313-23][^cite_note-2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid_CNN220313-24] with an agreement finalized in June 2016.[^cite_note-25]

### 2011

This section is an excerpt from [Freedom Flotilla II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flotilla_II).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Freedom_Flotilla_II\&action=edit)]

"[Freedom Flotilla II – Stay Human](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flotilla_II)"[^cite_note-26] was a [flotilla](981096.md) that planned to break the [maritime blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Gaza_Strip_\(2007-present\)) of the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) by Israel by sailing to Gaza on 5 July 2011. Ultimately, the sailing did not take place.[^cite_note-27]

The flotilla was organized by a coalition of 22 NGOs. Its name refers to the [May 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla) sailing of the Gaza Freedom Flotilla, which had been [raided by Israeli forces, killing a number of activists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid). Their stated goal was to end the blockade of Gaza "completely and permanently".[^cite_note-28] Initially, 10 ships, with 1,000 activists[^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_AJN_24Jun2011a-29] were set to sail for Gaza, but by 28 June 2011, the [number of expected participants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_participants_of_Freedom_Flotilla_II) had decreased to fewer than 300, plus a few dozen journalists, and by 7 July all but a few dozen activists had returned home.[^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_Katz-2010-30][^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_Magnezi-28-June-2011-31]

The flotilla was publicly opposed by the United States, France, the United Kingdom, [Turkey](11125639.md), Canada, the [Middle East Quartet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartet_on_the_Middle_East) (consisting of the EU, Russia, the United Nations, and the United States), and [United Nations Secretary General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary_General) [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon). It was publicly supported by [Hamas](13913.md).[^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_cbc.ca-32]

Activists on two ships, one in Greece and one in Turkey, found similar damage to their propeller shafts, and claimed that the ships had been sabotaged by Israel.[^cite_note-33] It was reported that the investigation by Turkish authorities on one of the ships determined that there may have been no sabotage at all, and that any damage happened before the boat arrived in Turkey, but these media reports appeared prior to the formal investigation beginning and the Turkish coastguard enquiry has yet to announce its findings. The media reports were disputed by Irish Ship to Gaza, the owner of the vessel.[^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_apgreece-34][^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_greece_blocks_boat-35][^cite_note-36] Greece forbade the vessels from leaving its ports, citing safety concerns,[^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_greek-37] but offered to instead send any humanitarian aid to Gaza in its own vessels, under United Nations supervision. Greece's offer was supported by Israel, the Palestinian Authority, and the United Nations, but rejected by the flotilla activists.[^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_voareject-38][^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_bill-39] Subsequently, two flotilla ships, the *Audacity of Hope* and *Tahrir*, disobeyed Greece's orders. They were intercepted by the Greek coast guard, brought back to shore, and a few activists were arrested. Activists responded by occupying the Spanish embassy in Athens.[^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_sunk-40]

Only the French ship *Dignité Al Karama* managed to approach Gaza. They advised the Greek authorities that their destination was the Egyptian port of [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria),[^cite_note-41] and then turned toward Gaza.[^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_AlJazeera-2011-07-18-42] On 19 July, the ship was intercepted and boarded without incident by Israeli commandos and escorted to the Israeli port of [Ashdod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashdod).[^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_II_BBC-2011-07-19-43]

### 2015

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Freedom Flotilla III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flotilla_III).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Freedom_Flotilla_III\&action=edit)]

[Freedom Flotilla III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flotilla_III) (#FF3) was a [flotilla](981096.md) that planned to break the [blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Gaza_Strip_\(2007-present\)) of the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) by [Israel](9282173.md) by sailing to Gaza. It started from [Sweden](5058739.md) on 10 May 2015 and stopped in several European cities along the way to Gaza. It started officially from [Athens](1216.md), [Greece](12108.md) on 25 June 2015.[^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_III_MEM-2015-44] Few details were released in advance.[^cite_note-Freedom_Flotilla_III_JP-2015-45]

On 29 June 2015 the Swedish-flagged vessel "Marianne" was intercepted by the Israeli navy[^cite_note-46] in international waters about 100 nautical miles from the Palestinian coast of [Gaza](12047.md). The Israeli military reported that no force was needed during the operation. However, eye-witness video published by Israeli [Channel 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Channel_2_\(Israel\)) TV shows people being tasered by [IDF](39237.md) commandos.[^cite_note-47] The *Marianne* was boarded by Israeli forces and taken to the [port of Ashdod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Ashdod), while the rest of the ships turned back.[^cite_note-48]

Organizers of the flotilla have repeatedly stated the boat was carrying humanitarian aid for Gaza. According to Israel's defense minister [Moshe Yaalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Yaalon) "there was no aid on board". The activists supplied a picture with two boxes containing a single [solar panel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panel) and a [nebulizer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebulizer) and added that the boat was to be donated to Gazan fishermen.[^cite_note-49]

On 30 June 2015, Israel began releasing some detainees. Former Tunisian President [Moncef Marzouki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moncef_Marzouki) was deported to [Paris](22989.md), France, while Member of European Parliament [Ana Miranda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Miranda_Paz) from [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Spain\)) was deported to [Spain](26667.md). [Arab](492331.md) Member of the Israeli [Knesset](74656.md) [Basel Ghattas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Ghattas), and Israeli journalist [Ohad Chamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohad_Chamo?action=edit\&redlink=1), were released.[^cite_note-50] Two Canadians and a Norwegian sailor were deported on July 2.[^cite_note-51] A Russian journalist and six Swedish participants remained in prison until July 6.[^cite_note-52]

### 2016

These paragraphs are an excerpt from [Women's Boat to Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Boat_to_Gaza).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Women%27s_Boat_to_Gaza\&action=edit)]

The [Women's Boat to Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Boat_to_Gaza) (WBG) was an initiative by the [Freedom Flotilla Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flotilla_Coalition) in 2016 to challenge the [Israeli](9282173.md) [naval blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Gaza_Strip_\(2007-present\)) of the [Gaza Strip](12047.md).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] The WBG consisted of an entirely female crew and one ship, *Zaytouna-Oliva*. It started from [Barcelona](4443.md) on 14 September 2016 and visited [Ajaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajaccio), [Corsica](5714828.md) and [Messina](59705.md), [Sicily](27619.md) along the way.[^cite_note-Women's_Boat_to_Gaza_WBG_FAQ-55] On 5 October 2016, the [Israeli Navy](1370788.md) intercepted the Women's Boat to Gaza some 14 miles outside the 20-mile "Security Zone" off the Palestinian coast,[^cite_note-Women's_Boat_to_Gaza_Legal_Cases-56] and detained its crew members, who were taken to the Israeli port of [Ashdod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashdod). The activists were subsequently deported to their home countries.[^cite_note-Women's_Boat_to_Gaza_The_Telegraph_5_Oct_16-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-Women's_Boat_to_Gaza_Legal_Cases-56]

### 2018

The Just Future for Palestine Flotilla, JFP Flotilla or 2018 Gaza Freedom Flotilla was another attempt to challenge the [Israeli](9282173.md) [blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Gaza_Strip_\(2007-present\)) of the [Gaza Strip](12047.md). The flotilla consisted of two ships *Al Awda* (*The Return*) and *Freedom* and two yachts *Mairead* and *Falestine*. On 29 July and 3 August 2018, both *Al Awda* and *Freedom* were seized by the [Israeli Navy](1370788.md). The activists were arrested and some reported they were [tasered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taser), and beaten. Most were deported to their home countries.[^cite_note-NZH_31_Jul_2018-60][^cite_note-TOI_4_Aug_2018-61][^cite_note-62]

#### June

The [June 2025 Gaza Freedom Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2025_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla) was an [aid](434679.md) vessel organized by the [Freedom Flotilla Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flotilla_Coalition) (FFC) with the intention of breaking the [Israeli blockade of the Gaza Strip](75013719.md) and delivering aid in response to the [famine](75828824.md) and [humanitarian crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_humanitarian_crisis_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)) in Gaza.[^cite_note-78] The vessel, *Madleen*, departed from [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania), [Sicily](27619.md), on 1 June 2025 and contained [baby formula](65330.md), 100 kg (220 lb) of flour, 250 kg (550 lb) of rice, diapers, medical kits, and crutches. These paragraphs are an excerpt from [2025 Global Sumud Flotilla](80704925.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2025_Global_Sumud_Flotilla\&action=edit)]

The [Global Sumud Flotilla](80704925.md) (GSF; [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): أسطول الصمود العالمي, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Usṭūl aṣ-Ṣumūd al-ʿĀlamī*), sometimes referred to as the Global Freedom Flotilla[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] (أسطول الحرية العالمي, *Usṭūl al-Ḥurriyya al-ʿĀlamī*), was an international, civil society-led maritime initiative launched in mid-2025, which attempted to break the [Israeli blockade of the Gaza Strip](19224310.md) but was thwarted in an intercept by [Israeli naval forces](1370788.md). It is named after *[ṣumūd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumud)*, [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) for 'steadfastness' or 'resilience'.[^cite_note-2025_Global_Sumud_Flotilla_\:2-95] The initiative emerged in July 2025, organised by coalition of [Freedom Flotilla Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Flotilla_Coalition) (FFC), [Global Movement to Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Movement_to_Gaza), [Maghreb Sumud Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb_Sumud_Flotilla) and [Sumud Nusantara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumud_Nusantara), during the [Gaza war](74998519.md). The flotilla comprised over 40 vessels with 500 participants from more than 44 countries, making it the largest civilian-led convoy of its kind in history. Some attempts to break the Israeli blockade were successful before 2010, but since then ships have been intercepted or attacked by Israeli forces, including an [attack by drones in May 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2025_drone_attack_on_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla) and interceptions in international waters in [June](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_2025_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla) and [July](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2025_Gaza_Freedom_Flotilla) 2025. By early 3 October, Israel had intercepted all vessels of the flotilla, after drone attacks had been reported, and [naval vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_vessels) dispatched to provide assistance.[^cite_note-96] The flotilla was the first unauthorized naval humanitarian mission to come within 70 [nautical miles](21289.md) (130 km) of the Gaza coast since the imposition of the blockade in 2009.

The flotilla began to set sail late August 2025 with delegations and convoys departing [Otranto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otranto), [Genoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Genoa) and [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Barcelona), followed by [Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catania), [Syros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syros) and [Tunis](57659.md) early September. On 3 September, the Italian convoy reached [Sicily](27619.md) and Tunisian vessels began converging on Tunis. Four days later, part of the Spanish convoy arrived in northern Tunisia, where in the early hours of 9 September, a fire broke out on one of the main vessels, suspected to be a drone attack. A second incendiary attack was reported the following night on another vessel. On 19 September, the Spanish and Tunisian convoys, having merged in Sicily, departed heading towards Greece. On 22 September, the Greek convoy then departed from Milos heading towards [Crete](6591.md), arriving the following day. On the night of 24 September, eleven vessels were attacked by drones. On 28 September, with the convoys merged in Crete, the flotilla departed to continue its journey towards Gaza. Between 1 and 2 October, the Israeli Navy intercepted the ships, detaining hundreds of people. In the evening of the interception, spontaneous protest took place across various locations in Europe in response. Three days later, 42 detainees began a [hunger strike](222292.md) in protest, and by 6 October, over a hundred activists had been deported from Israel. Participants reported mistreatment during detention, that was dismissed by the [Israeli foreign ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_foreign_ministry), and the interception was condemned by several political figures. On 8 October, a subsequent flotilla consisting of 9 ships, organised by FFC and [Thousand Madleens to Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Madleens_to_Gaza), was intercepted with participants detained about 120 nautical miles from Gaza.

The initiative received support from over a dozen [foreign ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_ministers), Italian politicians and political parties, MPs in Spain and Portugal, the [President of Colombia](251302.md) [Gustavo Petro](13391962.md), and the [UN Special Rapporteur on the occupied Palestinian territories](70685870.md), [Francesca Albanese](72566699.md). In contrast, Israeli minister of national security [Itamar Ben-Gvir](54504115.md) stated that participants should be imprisoned as [terrorists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist) and the Israeli foreign ministry vowed to stop the flotilla. In response to attacks on the flotilla, the [Italian ministry of defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Italy\)) sent [Italian Navy](1126939.md) ships to assist the flotilla and Spanish Prime Minister [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md) dispatched a [Spanish Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Navy) vessel in case of a rescue operation. With the navy vessels retreating as the flotilla reached closer to Gaza, the [Ministry of National Defense of Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Defense_\(Turkey\)) confirmed it would continue to ensure the safety of the flotilla if required. The [European Commission](9974.md) stated that it does not support aid flotillas to Gaza.

### 2026

Main article: [2026 Global Sumud Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Global_Sumud_Flotilla)

#### Planning

In December 2025, the Global Sumud Flotilla announced their interest in organizing a new flotilla that would take place in the spring of 2026, this time with the aim to include at least 100 ships and 3,000 participants (1,000 of which to be medics that would later stay in the Gaza Strip) from 70 countries..[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-\:1-113]

It was later announced that the first convoy was scheduled to depart from [Barcelona](4443.md) on 29 March 2026 (with departures from Tunisia and Italy also confirmed).&#x20;

#### April



Boats of the flotilla in [Ierapetra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ierapetra) after its interception

During April, boats set out from [Marseille](40888948.md) and [Naples](55880.md), to join others from [Barcelona](4443.md) and form a flotilla of 58 boats carrying humanitarian aid and aiming to break the blockade.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-\:1-113]

In the early hours of April 30, the IDF intercepted 22 boats of the convoy off [Crete](6591.md).[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-\:2-116] 181 people were detained, including [Pablo Giachello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Giachello), an Argentine provincial deputy.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-BBC260519-118] The IDF stated it would transfer the activists to Greece;[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] however two activists, [Saif Abu Keshek](83120959.md) and [Thiago Avila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiago_Avila), were taken to Israel, where they were detained for an additional 10 days.[^cite_note-BBC260519-118][^cite_note-121] Allegations subsequently emerged regarding the mistreatment of Saif Abu Keshek and Thiago Avila.[^cite_note-122]

#### May

Following this first interception, the remaining boats regrouped in Turkey, and 54 vessels sailed again for Gaza, this time from [Marmaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmaris), on 14 May.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] On 18 May, Israeli forces boarded vessels of the flotilla off Cyprus, some 250 nautical miles from Gaza.[^cite_note-BBC260519-118] 430 participants were detained at [Ashdod port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Ashdod).[^cite_note-125]

##### Responses to the interceptions

After the first interception (off Crete) and with Spanish national [Saif Abu Keshek](83120959.md) detained in Israel, the Spanish Ministry of Foreign Affairs summoned the chargé d'affaires of the Israeli embassy in Madrid and strongly condemned the operation. Official sources indicated that around 30 Spanish nationals were among those detained, and the Spanish government stated that consular protection mechanisms had been activated.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127]

Several governments and human rights organizations described the interception as violating international law.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] However, Israeli officials stated that the flotilla was attempting to break the maritime blockade of Gaza and was therefore operating illegally under Israeli interpretation of the blockade.[^cite_note-130]

In May 2026, after Israeli forces intercepted the Global Sumud Flotilla off Cyprus, [Itamar Ben-Gvir](54504115.md) released videos showing him taunting detained activists. The footage showed activists kneeling with their hands tied behind their backs, while Ben-Gvir called for them to be imprisoned for a long period.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] The foreign ministries of several countries, including [France](5843419.md), [Canada](5042916.md), the [Netherlands](21148.md), [Portugal](23033.md), [Spain](26667.md), [Italy](14532.md), [South Korea](27019.md), [Sweden](5058739.md), [Switzerland](26748.md), [Greece](12108.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Poland](22936.md), [Qatar](8238258.md), [Slovenia](27338.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), [Austria](26964606.md), [Belgium](3343.md), [Colombia](5222.md), the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [New Zealand](4913064.md) condemned the treatment of the detained activists.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

Later that month, France banned Ben-Gvir from entering the country, with French Foreign Minister [Jean-Noël Barrot](54388162.md) citing Ben-Gvir's "reprehensible actions towards French and European citizens" who were passengers on the Global Sumud Flotilla.[^cite_note-135] Poland also imposed a five-year entry ban on Ben-Gvir over his treatment of detained activists.[^cite_note-136] Ireland prohibited [Itamar Ben Gvir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itamar_Ben_Gvir) and finance minister [Bezalel Smotrich](46196834.md) from entering the country, citing behavior against [2026 Global Sumud Flotilla](#2026) activists and anti-Palestinian statements.[^cite_note-137]

On 21 May, Israel deported all 420 activists to [Istanbul](3391396.md), Turkey.[^cite_note-138] Following their release, multiple activists alleged they were subjected to systematic physical, psychological, and sexual abuse while in Israeli custody.[^cite_note-Guardian220526-139][^cite_note-TOI220526-140][^cite_note-CNN220526-141][^cite_note-CD290526-142] Human rights organization [Adalah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adalah_\(legal_center\)) and flotilla organizers documented at least 15 cases of sexual violence, detailing incidents of forced strip searches, sexual taunting, groping, and multiple accounts of rape, including forcible penetration with a handgun.[^cite_note-TOI220526-140][^cite_note-CNN220526-141] Activists from approximately 40 countries reported that the most severe abuse occurred inside darkened shipping containers on an Israeli landing craft converted into a makeshift prison ship, where they were denied water and blankets.[^cite_note-TOI220526-140][^cite_note-CNN220526-141][^cite_note-CD290526-142] Volunteers detailed being beaten, repeatedly [tasered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taser), and subjected to [sleep deprivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleep_deprivation) and prolonged stress positions, resulting in severe injuries such as broken bones and fractured vertebrae.[^cite_note-Guardian220526-139][^cite_note-TOI220526-140][^cite_note-CNN220526-141][^cite_note-CD290526-142]

Israeli authorities denied the claims, with the [Israel Prison Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Prison_Service) and the IDF stating that all detainees were treated legally and respectfully, while dismissing the flotilla as a propaganda stunt for Hamas.[^cite_note-Guardian220526-139][^cite_note-TOI220526-140][^cite_note-CNN220526-141] The allegations provoked widespread international condemnation, which was exacerbated when Israeli National Security Minister [Itamar Ben-Gvir](54504115.md) posted a video of himself taunting bound, kneeling detainees, prompting a rare public reprimand from Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu.[^cite_note-CNN220526-141] The United Nations expressed deep concern, and several nations launched legal or diplomatic responses.[^cite_note-TOI220526-140][^cite_note-CNN220526-141][^cite_note-CD290526-142] France instituted an entry ban on Ben-Gvir and referred the abuse of French nationals to its public prosecutor, while Italy, Spain, and Turkey initiated criminal investigations into kidnapping, torture, and sexual assault.[^cite_note-Guardian220526-139][^cite_note-TOI220526-140][^cite_note-CNN220526-141][^cite_note-CD290526-142] Canada and Germany condemned the mistreatment of their nationals, with German officials demanding a full explanation for the serious accusations.[^cite_note-CD290526-142][^cite_note-Guardian220526-139][^cite_note-CNN220526-141] The United States joined in the criticism, with US Ambassador to Israel [Mike Huckabee ](301237.md) stating that Ben-Gvir had "betrayed the dignity" of his nation.[^cite_note-CD290526-142] Malaysia announced plans to initiate formal proceedings against Israel at the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) over the abduction and alleged torture of its citizens.[^cite_note-CD290526-142]

On 21 August, Turkey's [Minister of Justice](10283737.md), [Akın Gürlek](82385461.md), announced that the country was seeking [Interpol](14338129.md) for an international arrest warrant for Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu for the interception of flotilla activists and accusing him of genocide.[^cite_note-143] The ministry also sought the arrest of an IDF soldier named Afek Moskovitch. According to [İhlas News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%B0hlas_News_Agency), Moskovitch was accused of destroying buildings at the [Islamic University of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_University_of_Gaza), including a hospital and medical facility.[^cite_note-144] The ministry also stated that both of them are being prosecuted for "crimes against humanity, genocide, aggravated deprivation of liberty, 'intentional assault and battery,' torture, destruction of property, aggravated looting, and hijacking of transport vehicles."[^cite_note-145]
