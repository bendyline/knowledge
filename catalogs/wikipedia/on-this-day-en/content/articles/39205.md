The **Asian Games**, also known as **Asiad**, is a [continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continent) [multi-sport event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-sport_event) held every four years for [athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athlete) of [Asia](689.md).[^cite_note-1] The Games were regulated by [Asian Games Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Games_Federation) from the [first Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Asian_Games) in [New Delhi](51585.md), [India](14533.md) in 1951, until the [1978 Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Asian_Games).[^cite_note-2] Since the [1982 Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Asian_Games), they have been organized by the [Olympic Council of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Council_of_Asia), after the breakup of the Asian Games Federation.[^cite_note-3] The Games are recognized by the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) and are the second largest multi-sport event after the [Olympic Games](22576.md).[^cite_note-4]

Nine nations have hosted the Asian Games. Forty-six nations have participated in the Games, including [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_at_the_Asian_Games), which was excluded from the Games after its last participation in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Asian_Games). The twentieth edition is being held in [Aichi Prefecture](524618.md) and [Nagoya](61720.md), Japan, from 19 September to 4 October 2026.

Since 2010, it has been common for the host of the Asian Games to host the [Asian Para Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Para_Games) held shortly after the end of the Games. This event is exclusive to athletes with [disabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disability), just like [Paralympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralympic_Games). But unlike the Paralympics where the host city's contract mentions the holding of both events, the case of Asia does not mention the mandatory holding of both. Instead, the exclusion of the Asian Paralympic Games from the Asian Games host city's contract means that both events run independently from one other, and may lead to occasions in the future when the two events are held in different cities and countries.

## History

### Origins

The [Far Eastern Championship Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Eastern_Championship_Games) existed previous to the Asian Games, the former mooted in 1912 for a location set between [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan), the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_Government_of_the_Philippine_Islands), and [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)). The inaugural Far Eastern Games were held in [Manila](184334.md) in [1913](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1913_Far_Eastern_Games) with 6 participating nations. There were ten Far Eastern Games held by [1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1934_Far_Eastern_Games). The [second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War) in 1934, and Japan's insistence on including the [Manchu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchukuo) as a competitor nation in the Games, caused China to announce its withdrawal from participation. The Far Eastern Games scheduled for [1938](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938_Far_Eastern_Games) were cancelled. The organization was eventually discontinued.[^cite_note-5]

### Formation

After World War II, several areas in Asia became [sovereign states](23604120.md). Many of these countries sought to exhibit Asian prowess without violence. During the [1948 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Summer_Olympics) in [London](17867.md), a conversation started between China and the Philippines to restore the idea of the Far Eastern Games, following the [decolonisation of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation_of_Asia) after World War II.[^cite_note-6] [Guru Dutt Sondhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Dutt_Sondhi), the Indian International Olympic Committee representative, believed that the restoration of the Far Eastern Games would sufficiently display the spirit of unity and level of achievement taking place in Asian sports. He proposed the idea of a new competition – which came to be the Asian Games. The Asian Athletic Federation would eventually be formed. A preparatory committee was set up to draft the charter for this new body. On 13 February 1949, the Asian Athletic Federation was formally inaugurated in New Delhi, announced as the inaugural host city to be held in 1950.[^cite_note-FEG-7][^cite_note-8]

### Years of crises



[The first Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Asian_Games) opening ceremony

In [1962](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Asian_Games), the Games were hit by several crises. The host country, Indonesia refused to permit Israel and Taiwan to participate due to [political](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia%E2%80%93Israel_relations) [recognition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia%E2%80%93Taiwan_relations) issues. The IOC terminated its sponsorship of the Games and terminated Indonesia's membership in the IOC.[^cite_note-9] The [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) (AFC),[^cite_note-10] [International Amateur Athletics Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Athletics_Federations) (IAAF), and the [International Weightlifting Federation](3228206.md) (IWF) also removed their recognition of the Games.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

[Seoul](19159283.md) renounced its plan to host the [1970 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Asian_Games) on the grounds of a national security crisis; the main reason was due to a financial crisis. The previous host, Thailand, would host the Games in [Bangkok](56615.md) using funds transferred from South Korea.[^cite_note-13] Japan was asked to host but declined the opportunity as they were already committed to [Expo '70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_'70) in [Osaka](52230.md).[^cite_note-14] This edition marked the Games' inaugural television broadcasting, worldwide.[^cite_note-15] In Tehran, in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Asian_Games), the Games formally recognized the participation of China, North Korea, and Mongolia. Israel was allowed to participate despite the opposition from the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), while Taiwan was permitted to continue taking part (as "[Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei)") although its status was abolished in a general meeting on 16 November 1973 by the Games Federation.[^cite_note-16]

Before the [1978 Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Asian_Games), [Pakistan](23235.md) retracted its plan to host the 1978 Games due to a financial crisis and [political issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_wars_and_conflicts).[^cite_note-17] Thailand offered to host and the Games were held in Bangkok. As in 1962, Taiwan and Israel refused participation by the Games Federation, amid political issues and security fears.[^cite_note-18] Several governing bodies protested the ban. The [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) threatened to bar the participating athletes from the [1980 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-19] Several nations withdrew before the Games opening.[^cite_note-20]

### Reorganization and expansion

These events led the National Olympic Committees in Asia to revise the constitution of the Asian Games Federation. The Olympic Council of Asia was created in November 1981, excluding Israel and Taiwan.[^cite_note-21] India was scheduled to host in [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Asian_Games) and the OCA decided to maintain the old AGF timetable. The OCA formally started to supervise the Games starting with the [1986 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Asian_Games) scheduled for Seoul, South Korea.[^cite_note-22] In the [1990 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Asian_Games) held in [Beijing](18603746.md), Taiwan (Republic of China) was re-admitted, under pressure by the People's Republic of China to compete as *Chinese Taipei*.[^cite_note-23]

The [1994 Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Asian_Games) held in [Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroshima) included the inaugural participation of the former 5 [republics of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union) who were part of [Central Asia](6742.md): Kazakhstan, [Kyrgyzstan](170131.md), Tajikistan, Turkmenistan, and Uzbekistan. It was also the first edition of the Games held in a host country outside its [capital city](181337.md).[^cite_note-24] However, [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee_of_Iraq) was suspended from the Games due to the 1990 [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_War). [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Committee_of_the_Democratic_People's_Republic_of_Korea) boycotted the Games due to political issues with the host country. The Games were hampered during the opening ceremony due to a heart attack that killed Nareshkumar Adhikari, the chief of the Nepalese delegation.[^cite_note-25]

The [1998 Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Asian_Games) marked the fourth time the Games were held in Bangkok, Thailand. This time the city participated in a bidding process. The opening ceremony was on 6 December; the previous three were on 9 December. [King Bhumibol Adulyadej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhumibol_Adulyadej) opened the Games; the closing ceremony was on 20 December (the same date as all the previous games hosted by Thailand).

## Background

### Symbols

The Asian Games Movement uses symbols to represent the ideals embodied in the [Asian Games charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Charter). The Asian Games motto is "Ever Onward" which was designed and proposed by Guru Dutt Sondhi upon the creation of the Asian Games Federation in 1949. The Asian Games symbol is a bright sun in red with 16 rays and a white circle in the middle of its disc which represents the ever glimmering and warm spirit of the Asian people.

### Mascots

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of Asian Games mascots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Asian_Games_mascots).

Since the [1982 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Asian_Games) in [New Delhi](51585.md), India, the Asian Games have had a [mascot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mascot), usually an animal native to the area or occasionally human figures representing the cultural heritage.

### Participation

See also: [Olympic Council of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Council_of_Asia)

All 45 members affiliated with the [Olympic Council of Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Council_of_Asia) (OCA) are eligible to participate in the Games.

According to membership in the OCA, [transcontinental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_transcontinental_countries) [Kazakhstan](16642.md) is an Asian country and could participate in the Asian Games, but this right could not apply to Egypt as the country had 6% of the territory on [Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula), participating in the [African Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Games) instead. Turkey and Russia/Soviet Union — whose major geographical parts are located in the Asian continent — participate in the [European Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Games) rather than the Asian Games. Similarly, Azerbaijan, Georgia, Cyprus, and Armenia are mostly located in Asia but participate in the European Games instead of the Asian Games.

In history, 46 National Olympic Committees (NOCs) have sent competitors to the Games. [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Committee_of_Israel) has been excluded from the Games since 1976, the reason cited as being due to security reasons.[^cite_note-26] Israel requested to participate in the 1982 Games, but the request was rejected by the organizers due to the [Munich massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_massacre).[^cite_note-27] Israel is now a member of the [European Olympic Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Olympic_Committees) (EOC) since 1994 and competes at the [European Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Games).

Taiwan, Palestine, Hong Kong, and Macau participate in the Asian Games according to membership in OCA. Due to its continuing ambiguous political status, Taiwan has participated in the Games under the flag of [Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei) since 1990. [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macau_Sports_and_Olympic_Committee) since 1990 has been allowed to compete as one of the NOCs in the Asian Games, despite not being recognized by the International Olympic Committee (IOC) for participation in the [Olympic Games](22576.md).

In 2007, the President of OCA, Sheikh [Ahmed Al-Fahad Al-Ahmed Al-Sabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Al-Fahad_Al-Ahmed_Al-Sabah), rejected the proposal to allow [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Olympic_Committee) to participate in the Games. He stated that while Australia would add good value to the Asian Games, it would be unfair to the [Oceania National Olympic Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_National_Olympic_Committees) (ONOC).[^cite_note-28] Being members of ONOC, Australia and New Zealand have participated in the [Pacific Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Games) since 2015. This motion was mooted again in 2017 after Australia participated in the [2017 Asian Winter Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Asian_Winter_Games) as they are in discussions to become a full Asian Games member shortly.[^cite_note-29] However, the [Australian Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Olympic_Committee) announced that Australia would be allowed a small contingent of athletes for the 2022 Games, as long as the qualification for Summer Olympics events such as basketball and volleyball are through the Asia-Pacific region.[^cite_note-30] In November 2021, it was announced that athletes from Oceania would be invited to compete in athletics, triathlon, roller skating, weightlifting, and [wushu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wushu_\(sport\)). Athletes were to receive "honorary medals" which would not be counted on the official medal tally.[^cite_note-31] The plan was shelved due to a lack of interest from the relevant federations in Australia and New Zealand.[^cite_note-32]

There are only seven countries, namely [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_at_the_Asian_Games), [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_at_the_Asian_Games), [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_at_the_Asian_Games), the [Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_at_the_Asian_Games), [Sri Lanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_at_the_Asian_Games), [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_at_the_Asian_Games), and [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_at_the_Asian_Games), that have competed in all editions of the games.

## List of sports

Main article: [Asian Games sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Games_sports)

The historical average for the edition of events by the edition of the Asian Games is nearly 260 events with nearly 24 sports by edition. The last edition held in [Hangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou), [China](5405.md) was the edition with the largest number of events when 481 finals in 40 sports were held. Until the 2018 edition, each host country was allowed to set up the program respecting their local demands, which led to a dizzying growth of the event. However, this flexibility rule ruled out the entry of some Olympic sports such as the modern pentathlon and triathlon of the programs of some editions between 1986 and 2006. This flexibility also increased the number of athletes participating in each edition. However, it forced the event to be massive, as there was no calculation of athlete quotas per NOC. To avoid gigantism, OCA established a new policy to be applied starting the [2018 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Asian_Games), which limited the number of sports to be played at the Games to 40. This new rule is by the Olympic program for the subsequent edition of the Summer Olympic Games and if there is any prior change, the entity will necessarily follow it. However, each region that makes up the body can nominate a regional sport according to its demands. Between one and two extra sports could also be included by organizing committees due to local demands.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

## Medal table

Main article: [All-time Asian Games medal table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-time_Asian_Games_medal_table)

Of the 46 National Olympic Committees participating throughout the history of the Games, 43 have won at least a single medal in the competition, leaving three National Olympic Committees without a medal: [Bhutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhutan_at_the_Asian_Games), [Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_at_the_Asian_Games), and [Timor-Leste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor-Leste_at_the_Asian_Games). 38 National Olympic Committees have won at least one gold medal (only [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_at_the_Asian_Games) and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_at_the_Asian_Games) have done so at every Asian Games), while [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_at_the_Asian_Games) and Japan are the only two NOCs to emerge as the top-ranked team in the medal tables.[^cite_note-58]

In the [2022 Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Asian_Games), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_at_the_2022_Asian_Games) became the fourth nation in history after Japan, China, and South Korea to cross the 100-medal mark in one edition.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_at_the_Asian_Games), together with [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_at_the_Asian_Games) and [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_at_the_Asian_Games) became the first three countries to cross the overall 200-medal mark in one edition during the [1986 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Asian_Games).[^cite_note-oca1-61] At the 2022 Games, [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_at_the_Asian_Games) became the first NOC to surpass the threshold of 100 gold medals, 200 gold medals, 300 overall medals and 400 overall medals in a single edition.[^cite_note-\:0-62][^cite_note-China-63][^cite_note-64]

## Centennial Festival

On 8 November 2012, the OCA decided at its 31st General Assembly in [Macau](19068.md) to create a special multi-sport event called the Asian Games Centennial Festival in celebration of the 100th anniversary of the Oriental Games, now known as the Far Eastern Championship Games.[^cite_note-74] OCA awarded the Philippines the hosting rights as it was also the host 100 years previous. The event was originally scheduled to be held in [Boracay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boracay), [Malay, Aklan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay,_Aklan) from 27 to 29 November 2013 but due to the events surrounding [Typhoon Haiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Haiyan), it was moved to January 2014.[^cite_note-75]
