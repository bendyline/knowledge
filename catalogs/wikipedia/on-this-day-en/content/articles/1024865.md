The **Morocco national football team**[^cite_note-4] has represented [Morocco](19291.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) since their first international match in 1957. It is controlled by the [Royal Moroccan Football Federation](1905329.md) (FRMF), the governing body for [football in Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Morocco). It has been affiliated with [FIFA](11049.md) since 1960, with the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) since 1959, and with the [Union of North African Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNAF) since 2005. The team, which is known as the *Atlas Lions*, plays home matches at the [Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Moulay_Abdellah_Stadium) in [Rabat](57400.md), and trains at the Mohammed VI Football Complex in [Salé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sal%C3%A9).

Widely regarded as Africa's most successful national football team and one of the best teams in the world, Morocco has won five continental titles, including the [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_African_Cup_of_Nations) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_African_Cup_of_Nations) editions of the [African Cup of Nations](250680.md). They won the [African Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Nations_Championship) in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_African_Nations_Championship), [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_African_Nations_Championship) and [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_African_Nations_Championship). Morocco has qualified for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) on seven occasions. In [1986](157231.md), they made history as the first African team to win a World Cup group and advance to the [knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage). At the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md), Morocco became the first African and first Arab team to reach a World Cup semi-final. They were also the third World Cup semi-finalist from outside [Europe](32332.md) or [South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL). Over 2023–2026, Morocco broke an international football [world record](66634086.md), achieving the longest winning streak at 19 matches. During this run they also achieved a 33 undefeated match record.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

## History

### Formation and early years

The Moroccan national team was founded in 1928 and played its first game on 22 December of that year against France's B team, losing 2–1. The team, formed of the best footballers of the Moroccan Football League (LMFA), was active in friendly matches against other North African teams such as [Algeria](1029528.md) and [Tunisia](1029953.md).

On 9 September 1954, an [earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Chlef_earthquake) struck the Algerian region of Orléansville (now [Chlef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlef)), destroying the city and killing 1,400 people. On 7 October 1954, the [French Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Football_Association) and the Maghreb inhabitants organized a charity match to raise funds for the families of the victims of the earthquake. In the match, held at the [Parc de Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_de_Princes) in Paris, a team made up of Moroccans, Algerians and Tunisians played against [France](250197.md). Led by star [Larbi Benbarek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larbi_Benbarek), the Maghreb selection managed to win 3–2, a month before the [Toussaint Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toussaint_Rouge) attacks by the [Algerian National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberation_Front_\(Algeria\)) which marked the beginning of the [Algerian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War).

In 1955, the [Royal Moroccan Football Federation](1905329.md) was established, at the end of the [French protectorate of Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_of_Morocco), which had lasted since 1912.

On 19 October 1957, at the [second edition of the Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1957_Arab_Games) in Lebanon, Morocco made its debut as an independent country against Iraq, at the [Camille Chamoun Sports City Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Chamoun_Sports_City_Stadium), and drew 3–3. At the tournament, Morocco recorded their first-ever win, defeating [Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team) 5–1, then beat [Tunisia](1029953.md) 3–1 to reach the semi-finals. After a 1–1 draw with [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team), lots were drawn to decide who would progress to the final, and Syria were selected at Morocco's expense. Morocco withdrew from the third-place play-off against [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team) and finished fourth overall.[^cite_note-7]

&#x20;Between 1957 and 1958, Morocco held numerous friendly matches against the National Liberation Front team, the representative of Algeria before its independence in 1958. In 1959, the team took part for the first time in an international competition, the [qualifying rounds of the 1960 Rome Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1960_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_African_Qualifiers). Drawn into a group with Tunisia and Malta, Morocco finished second on goal difference and failed to progress. That same year, the Royal Moroccan Football Federation joined FIFA.



The Morocco national team in 1942 with [Larbi Benbarek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larbi_Benbarek)

In 1960, Morocco competed in [World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) for the first time. Drawn against Tunisia in the first round, Morocco won the first leg 2–1, while Tunisia won the second leg by the same score. A play-off held in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo), Italy finished in a draw, so a coin toss was used to determine who progressed. Morocco won the toss, and beat Ghana 1–0 on aggregate to reach the [inter-continental play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification#Inter-confederation_play-offs). Drawn against [Spain](609825.md), Morocco lost 4–2 on aggregate and thus failed to qualify.

The following year, Morocco held the [Pan-Arab Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1961_Arab_Games) and won the football tournament, winning all five of their matches. Their third match, against Saudi Arabia, resulted in Morocco's biggest-ever victory, winning 13–1.[^cite_note-8] They also claimed their first two wins against a European team, beating [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany_national_football_team) 2–1 and 2–0.

In 1963, the Moroccan team came close to [qualifying for the African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification); in the decisive play-off against Tunisia, they were defeated 4–1 in [Tunis](57659.md) and won 4–2 at home, therefore being eliminated. At the [1963 Mediterranean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1963_Mediterranean_Games) in [Naples](55880.md), they finished fourth after a 2–1 defeat in the final for third place against Spain's reserve team.[^cite_note-9]

### 1963–1976: First international competitions



[Mohamed Massoun](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed%20Massoun), Morocco's coach in the 1960s

Morocco participated in the final phase of an international competition for the first time at the [1964 Tokyo Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1964_Summer_Olympics). Having qualified under the leadership of manager Mohamed Massoun, the Moroccans were included in a group of three teams due to the withdrawal of [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team). Morocco lost both their matches, against [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungary_national_football_team) (6–0, the team's worst-ever defeat) and Yugoslavia (3–1, despite taking the lead in the second minute via [Ali Bouachra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Bouachra)).

In 1966, the Moroccan Football Association joined the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md), and the team participated in [qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) for the [1970 World Cup](157227.md) in Mexico. Their debut eliminated [Senegal](1017436.md) (1–0) and Tunisia after a draw. In the final round of the preliminaries, against [Sudan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_national_football_team) and [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team), Morocco obtained five points, finishing ahead of Nigeria. Shortly after, Morocco lost the decisive play-off against Algeria to enter the final stage of the [1970 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).

Morocco thus became the first African national team to qualify for the World Cup after having played in an elimination tournament. The Moroccan team, coached by the Yugoslav [Blagoje Vidinić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blagoje_Vidini%C4%87), consisted entirely of players in the Moroccan league, including [Driss Bamous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driss_Bamous) and [Ahmed Faras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Faras).

On 3 June 1970, against [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany_national_football_team), Morocco opened the scoring with a goal in the 21st minute of the game scored by [Houmane Jarir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houmane_Jarir). In the second half, however, the Germans scored twice and won 2–1. The Atlas Lions then played against [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_national_football_team), conceding three goals in ten minutes to lose 3–0. On 11 June 1970, the eliminated Moroccans drew with [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgaria_national_football_team) 1–1. It was the first point obtained by an African national team at the World Cup.[^cite_note-10]

In [1972 Africa Cup of Nations qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Africa_Cup_of_Nations_qualification), the Atlas Lions ousted Algeria, then faced Egypt, defeating them 3–0 in the first leg and suffering a 3–2 defeat on the way back. However, the aggregate win meant they qualified for the final phase of the continental tournament for the first time. In the group stage, they accumulated three 1–1 draws against [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_national_football_team), Sudan and [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire_national_football_team) and were eliminated in the first round. All three Moroccan goals were scored by Ahmed Faras.

Qualifying for the [1972 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1972_Summer_Olympics) in Munich with two wins and two draws, Morocco debuted in Group A with a goalless draw against the [United States](582488.md), then lost 3–0 against hosts West Germany and defeated [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysia_national_football_team) 6–0 with a Faras hat-trick, thereby advancing to the second round. Due to defeats against [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_national_football_team) (3–0), [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_national_football_team) (3–1) and [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team) (5–0), they were eliminated from the tournament; finishing bottom of their group.[^cite_note-11]

In the [1974 World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(CAF\)), Morocco successfully passed three qualifying rounds before entering the final stage alongside [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team) and [Zaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaire_national_football_team). Losing 4–0 away against Zambia, the Moroccans bounced back in the second game, defeating the same opponent 2–0 at home. They then went to Zaire for their third game but lost 3–0, conceding all three goals in the second half, with Faras leaving the field due to injury. Morocco filed an appeal, trying to get the match to be replayed; it was dismissed by FIFA. In protest, Morocco withdrew from the qualifiers causing the Atlas Lions to miss their final game at home against Zaire which had already qualified for the finals, with FIFA awarding Zaire a 2–0 win on walkover. For the same reason, Morocco also decided not to take part in the [1974 African Cup of Nations qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_African_Cup_of_Nations_qualification). As a result, in 1974, Morocco played only two games, both against Algeria, achieving a 2–0 win and a 0–0 draw. Morocco then resumed playing in FIFA and CAF competitions, qualifying for the [1976 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) by eliminating Ghana in the last round, but failed to qualify for the [1976 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1976_Summer_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Men's_African_Qualifiers), being eliminated by Nigeria.

### 1976–1986: Victories and defeats

Morocco, coached by the Romanian [Virgil Mărdărescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgil_M%C4%83rd%C4%83rescu) and captained by Faras, took the continental throne at [1976 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_African_Cup_of_Nations), in only the country's second participation in the competition. Ahmed Makrouh scored in the final to equalize at 1–1, which gave Morocco the first, and to date, only cup in its history.[^cite_note-12]

After failing to qualify for the 1974 FIFA World Cup, they also missed the [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup) and [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup) tournaments. At the [1978 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), they were eliminated in the first round, while in [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) they finished in third place, beating Egypt 2–0.[^cite_note-13] They then won the [1983 Mediterranean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1983_Mediterranean_Games), played at home, after a 3–0 win in the final against [Turkey B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_national_football_B_team).[^cite_note-14]

Morocco did not qualify for either the [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) or [1984 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Africa_Cup_of_Nations). [Two years later](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), they finished fourth, losing 3–2 in the third-place play-off to [Ivory Coast](1039856.md).[^cite_note-15]

Morocco's typical starting line-up at the [1986 FIFA World Cup](157231.md)

Morocco qualified for the [1986 FIFA World Cup](157231.md) in Mexico, and topped a group consisting [Portugal](576328.md), [England](9904.md) and [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_football_team), thanks to two draws against the English and Polish[^cite_note-16] and a 3–1 win against the Portuguese.[^cite_note-17] However, they were narrowly eliminated by [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany_national_football_team) in the first knockout round, thanks to a goal from [Lothar Matthäus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lothar_Matth%C3%A4us) one minute from the end of regulation time. Morocco became the first African or Arab national team to advance from the first round of the World Cup.[^cite_note-18]

Two years later, the Moroccan team presented itself at the [1988 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_African_Cup_of_Nations) as a host country with high expectations. After winning in the first round, they were eliminated in the semi-finals by [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) and finished in fourth place after losing the third-place play-off against [Algeria](1029528.md) 1–1 after extra time, 4–3 after penalties.

Morocco failed to qualify for the [1990 FIFA World Cup](157232.md) and was eliminated in the group stage of the [1992 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_African_Cup_of_Nations). The team also did not qualify for the [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_African_Cup_of_Nations) and [1996 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_African_Cup_of_Nations) tournaments. Despite these setbacks, the team qualified for the [1994 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) in the [United States](3434750.md) and the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) in [France](5843419.md). In both tournaments, Morocco were praised for their attacking style of play, led by key players such as Mustapha Hadji, Noureddine Naybet, and Salaheddine Bassir.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

At the [1998 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), after winning their group, Morocco were defeated by [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_football_team) 2–1.[^cite_note-21]

### 2000–2021: Difficult years



The Morocco national team in 2011

Morocco took part in the [2004 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Cup_of_Nations), drawn into Group D defeating [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) 1–0, defeating [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_national_football_team) 4–0[^cite_note-22] and drawing 1–1 with [South Africa](1018598.md). Morocco qualified to the knockout stages, facing [Algeria](1029528.md); they eventually won 3–1 in extra time,[^cite_note-23] and 4–0 against [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team) in the semi-final.[^cite_note-24] They lost the [2004 African Cup of Nations Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Cup_of_Nations_Final) against [Tunisia](1029953.md) 2–1.[^cite_note-25]

In 2012, the national team won the [Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Arab_Cup), topping their group, defeating [Iraq](1039652.md) in the semi-final and Libya in the final.[^cite_note-arabcup-26]

In [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_African_Nations_Championship), Morocco debuted in the [African Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Nations_Championship) after failing to qualify in the [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_African_Nations_Championship) and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_African_Nations_Championship) editions. Led by coach [Hassan Benabicha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Benabicha), Morocco were eliminated in the second round after losing 4–3 to [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) in the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_African_Nations_Championship#Quarter-finals).[^cite_note-27] They managed to qualify for the [2016 African Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_African_Nations_Championship), but were eliminated in the group stages.[^cite_note-28] Morocco hosted the [2018 African Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_African_Nations_Championship), which included a victory for the home nation, the third North African country to win the competition's title.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

Morocco returned to the World Cup after a 20-year absence in [2018](13327177.md).[^cite_note-32] The North Africans were drawn in [Group B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_B) with World Cup favourites [Spain](609825.md), [Portugal](576328.md), and [Iran](272865.md).[^cite_note-33] In their opening game against Iran, Morocco showed full dedication but lost 1–0 in the final minutes of the match, scored by an own goal.[^cite_note-34] In their second game, Morocco faced Portugal but ended losing 1–0 by a goal scored by [Cristiano Ronaldo](623737.md) and also got eliminated from the tournament.[^cite_note-35] In the last match against Spain they took a 2–1 lead, with goals scored by [Khalid Boutaïb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_Bouta%C3%AFb) and [Youssef En-Nesyri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youssef_En-Nesyri), but the match eventually ended 2–2.[^cite_note-36]

Morocco entered the [2019 AFCON](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFCON) with high confidence and players claiming them to be the favourite to win.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] However, in spite of three straight group stage wins, Morocco were shockingly knocked out by [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_national_football_team) in the round of sixteen.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

At the [2020 African Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_African_Nations_Championship) in Cameroon,[^cite_note-41] Morocco won their second CHAN title, in its second consecutive final appearance. Captained by [Ayoub El Kaabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayoub_El_Kaabi), they defeated [Togo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Togo_national_football_team) (1–0),[^cite_note-42] [Rwanda](1145307.md) (0–0),[^cite_note-43] the [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_national_football_team) (5–2),[^cite_note-44] [Zambia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambia_national_football_team) (3–1),[^cite_note-45] and [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) (4–0)[^cite_note-46] on the way to a final against [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team) in [Yaoundé](57671.md). Morocco won 2–0, with both goals scored late into the second half by [Soufiane Bouftini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soufiane_Bouftini) and [Ayoub El Kaabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayoub_El_Kaabi).[^cite_note-47] Morocco thus became the first team to win back-to-back titles.[^cite_note-48] [Soufiane Rahimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soufiane_Rahimi) went on to be named Total Man of the tournament after an astonishing performance scoring a total of 5 goals.[^cite_note-49]

In December 2021, Morocco started its venture at the [2021 FIFA Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIFA_Arab_Cup)'s [Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIFA_Arab_Cup_Group_C), along with [Jordan](1090065.md), [Palestine](1302455.md) and [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team). Morocco opened the tournament with a 4–0 win against Palestine,[^cite_note-50] and then managed to overcome a highly defensive Jordan with another 4–0 triumph,[^cite_note-51] before winning their final match in a 1–0 victory against Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-52] They were eliminated in the quarter-finals after a penalty-shootout against [Algeria](1029528.md).[^cite_note-53]

### 2021–2026: Comeback

After easily topping their [2021 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) qualification group which consisted of Mauritania, Burundi, and Central African Republic, Morocco entered the tournament in Cameroon.[^cite_note-54] Morocco were drawn into group E alongside [Gabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabon_national_football_team), [Ghana](1029620.md) and [Comoros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comoros_national_football_team), and won their first game against Ghana scored by [Sofiane Boufal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofiane_Boufal) in the final minutes of the game.[^cite_note-55] In their second game against Comoros, they claimed a 2–0 victory.[^cite_note-56] Their final match against Gabon ended in a draw, making Morocco reach the round of 16 after ending up first in the group.[^cite_note-57] They defeated [Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malawi_national_football_team) 2–1 in the round of 16,[^cite_note-58] before being eliminated in the quarter-finals after a 2–1 loss against [Egypt](1022953.md).[^cite_note-59]

Morocco's typical starting line-up at the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md)

After qualifying for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) by winning the [third round of CAF qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_third_round),[^cite_note-60] the team was drawn in [Group F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_F) along with [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_national_football_team), [Belgium](679716.md), and [Canada](723688.md). After holding [previous runners-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_final) Croatia to a 0–0 draw and defeating Belgium 2–0, a 2–1 win over Canada meant they finished top of the group and advanced to the round of 16 for the first time since 1986.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] In the round of 16, they met [Spain](609825.md), drawing 0–0. In the subsequent penalty shootout, goalkeeper [Yassine Bounou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yassine_Bounou) saved two penalties, and [Achraf Hakimi](52004386.md) scored the decisive penalty with a [panenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panenka_\(penalty_kick\)) penalty for Morocco to advance to the quarter-finals for the first time.[^cite_note-63] They advanced further to the semi-finals winning against favourite Portugal, 1–0, with a powerful header by Youssef En-Nesyri.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] Morocco therefore became the first African and Arab team to qualify for the semi-finals.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] However, they lost to [France](250197.md) in the semi-final, 2–0 on 14 December at the [Al Bayt Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Bayt_Stadium) in [Al Khor](19309079.md), putting an end to Morocco's World Cup run.[^cite_note-69] They played Croatia in the third place play-off on 17 December at the [Khalifa International Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalifa_International_Stadium) in [Al-Rayyan](57488319.md), a rematch of the earlier group stage meeting.[^cite_note-70] They lost 2–1 to the latter, and ended their World Cup campaign at fourth place.[^cite_note-71] The team would go on to donate their entire World Cup earnings from the tournament to charities within Morocco that help with poverty that affects children and families in the country.[^cite_note-72]

In October 2025, Morocco broke the world record for the longest winning streak in international football, surpassing Spain's previous mark of 15 consecutive victories set between June 2008 and June 2009. With a 1–0 win over [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_national_football_team) in [Rabat](57400.md), Morocco extended their unbeaten run to 16 straight wins across all competitions, including [World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_qualification) and friendlies.[^cite_note-\:02-73] The streak, which began in June 2024, ultimately reached 19 consecutive wins before ending with a draw in December 2025.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-\:1-75]

Morocco won the [2025 FIFA Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Arab_Cup) with its national [A' team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_A'_national_football_team), defeating [Jordan](1090065.md) 3–2 after extra time in the final. The Moroccan side, composed primarily of players active in domestic and regional leagues, secured the title following a closely contested match in which the score was level at the end of regular time. The victory marked Morocco's second triumph in the [FIFA Arab Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Arab_Cup), after its first title in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Arab_Cup).[^cite_note-76]

Morocco hosted the [2025 Africa Cup of Nations](57061770.md) and advanced from the group stage through the knockout rounds, reaching the [final](81129500.md) for the first time in two decades, where they faced [Senegal](1017436.md). The match remained scoreless after regular and stoppage time. During this period, Morocco was awarded a penalty following a foul on Brahim; however, play was interrupted when the Senegalese team left the pitch upon instruction of then-manager Pape Thiaw before returning over 20 minutes later. Consequently, on 17 March 2026, the CAF Appeal Board ruled that Senegal had forfeited the match by reason of refusing to play and leaving the pitch. In accordance with articles 82 and 84 of the competition regulations, the championship was recorded as a 3–0 victory for Morocco, thereby awarding them the title of 2025 Africa Cup of Nations champions.[^cite_note-77]

Morocco once more finished as the best-performing African side at the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md), whilst becoming the first national team from the continent to reach back-to-back quarter-finals. During their run, they overtook Italy as the nation with the longest undefeated streak in international football before bowing out to France.

## Home stadiums

Morocco’s primary home stadium is [Prince Moulay Abdellah Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Moulay_Abdellah_Stadium) in [Rabat](57400.md), where the national team plays most of its home matches.

&#x20;During [Ezzaki Badou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezzaki_Badou)’s tenure as head coach from 2014 to 2016, Morocco played most of its matches at the [Adrar Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrar_Stadium) in [Agadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agadir), which has a capacity of 45,480.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] Morocco also has several other large venues, including [Fez Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fez_Stadium), [Marrakesh Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marrakesh_Stadium), and [Ibn Battouta Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battouta_Stadium) in [Tangier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tangier).
