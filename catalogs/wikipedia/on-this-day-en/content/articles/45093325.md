**Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes** (French pronunciation: [\[ovɛʁɲ ʁonalp\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/7f/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Auvergne-Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes.wav/LL-Q150\_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Auvergne-Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/7f/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Auvergne-Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes.wav/LL-Q150_%28fra%29-WikiLucas00-Auvergne-Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; **AURA**)[^cite_note-4] is a landlocked [region](58846.md) in [Southern France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_France). It was created by the 2014 territorial reform of French regions; it resulted from the merger of [Auvergne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auvergne) and [Rhône-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes). The new region came into effect on 1 January 2016, after the [regional elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_French_regional_elections) in December 2015.[^cite_note-5]

The region covers an area of 69,711 km<sup>2</sup> (26,916 mi<sup>2</sup>), making it the third largest in [metropolitan France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_France); it had a population of 7,994,459 in 2018, second to [Île-de-France](1716427.md).[^cite_note-Population-6] It consists of twelve [departments](38523.md) and one [collectivity with particular status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_collectivity) (the [Metropolis of Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_of_Lyon)) with [Lyon](8638634.md) as the [prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures_in_France).

This region combines diverse geographical, sociological, economic, and cultural areas, which was already true of Rhône-Alpes, as well as Auvergne, to a lesser extent. While the old Rhône-Alpes and Auvergne regions each enjoyed a unity defined by axes of communication and the pull of their respective metropolitan areas,[^cite_note-7] the new combination is heterogeneous; it sustained lively opposition from some local officials after its creation.[^cite_note-Figaro1-8][^cite_note-CB-9][^cite_note-Figaro2-10][^cite_note-11]

## Toponymy, logo, and symbols



The region Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes and its departments on the background of historical provinces

The text of the territorial reform law gives [interim names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Working_title) for most of the merged regions, combining the names of their constituent regions alphabetically, separated by [hyphens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphen). Permanent [names would be proposed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_renaming) by the new regional councils and confirmed by the [Conseil d'État](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conseil_d'%C3%89tat_\(France\)) by 1 October 2016.[^cite_note-reform_law-12][^cite_note-13]

The interim name of the new administrative region was a [hyphenated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphenated_name) [placename](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placename), composed of the historic region of [Auvergne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auvergne), the river [Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne) and the [French Alps](961317.md) (*Alpes*). The same name has been chosen as the definitive name, which was officialized by the Conseil d'État on 28 September 2016.[^cite_note-Decree_name-14]

According to several online polls from *[Lyon Capitale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Capitale)*, the name "Rhône-Alpes-Auvergne" led voting, ahead of "Alpes-Auvergne" and "AURA" (an acronym for Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes),[^cite_note-15] which was proposed by [Jean-Jack Queyranne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Jack_Queyranne), former president of the [regional council of Rhône-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_council_of_Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes). Schoolchildren were consulted about the name of the new region in February 2016; residents were consulted in March.[^cite_note-16]

After adjusting the votes in proportion to the number of inhabitants of the regions (Rhône-Alpes having five times the population of Auvergne) the name "Rhône-Alpes-Auvergne" was still leading, ahead of "Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes" and the acronym "AURA".[^cite_note-17]

Despite this result, [Laurent Wauquiez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Wauquiez) and his team decided not to follow the preference of the citizens of the new region, and the name Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes was put to the vote by the regional council and adopted unanimously on 23 June 2016;[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] it was made official on 28 September 2016 through a decree appearing in the *[Journal Officiel de la République Française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_Officiel_de_la_R%C3%A9publique_Fran%C3%A7aise)*.[^cite_note-JO-1]

In October 2017, the region was given a coat of arms that combines those of Auvergne, Savoie, Lyonnais and Dauphiné.[^cite_note-20] The region also has a flag, which initially consisted of the coat of arms on a white background, but was replaced by a heraldic flag in January 2018. On 9 February 2018, the region formalised the flag and the coat of arms on its website, as implemented by Mattieu Casali, a historical scholar.[^cite_note-BlasonOffi-21] It was received favourably by the national heraldic commission.[^cite_note-22]

The [blazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blazon) is described on the region's website (in French) as *"Écartelé : au premier d'or au gonfanon de gueules bordé de sinople (Auvergne); au deuxième de gueules à la croix d'argent (Savoie); au troisième de gueules au lion d'argent (Lyonnais); au quatrième d'or au dauphin d'azur, crêté, barbé, loré, peautré et oreillé de gueules (Dauphiné),"* which translates roughly to: "[Quartered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartering_\(heraldry\)): the first quarter, with an [or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Or_\(heraldry\)) (gold) background, containing a [gules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gules) (red) banner fringed with [vert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vert_\(heraldry\)) (green), representing Auvergne; the second quarter, with a gules background, containing an [argent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argent) (silver) cross, representing Savoie; the third quarter, with a gules background, containing an argent lion, representing Lyon; the fourth quarter, with an or background, containing an [azure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azure_\(heraldry\)) dolphin[^cite_note-mistholme2014-23] with gules details, representing the Dauphiné."[^cite_note-BlasonOffi-21]

## Geography

### Location



Map of the region (ML is the [Lyon Metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon_Metropolis))

Marmots in an Alpine Meadow of Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes

The Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes administrative region covers an area of 69 711 km<sup>2</sup> in the centre and east of the south of France. It is a collection of regions of diverse topographies, climates, natural resources, cultures, folklore, architecture, and languages. It is bordered by five other administrative regions: [Bourgogne-Franche-Comté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgogne-Franche-Comt%C3%A9) to the north, [Centre-Val de Loire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-Val_de_Loire) to the northwest, [Nouvelle-Aquitaine](45092904.md) to the west, [Occitanie](45093609.md) to the southwest, and [Provence-Alpes-Côte d'Azur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provence-Alpes-C%C3%B4te_d'Azur) to the southeast. It is also bordered by the [Italian regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_regions) of [Aosta Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aosta_Valley) and [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) to the east and the [Swiss cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_cantons) of [Geneva](292834.md), [Valais](198789.md), and [Vaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaud) to the northeast.

## Economy

The [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the region was 270.0 billion euros (327.0 billion dollars) in 2018, accounting for 11.9% of French economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was 30,200 euros or 100% of the EU27 average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 109% of the EU average.[^cite_note-25]

## Transport

The region's main airport is [Lyon–Saint-Exupéry Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyon%E2%80%93Saint-Exup%C3%A9ry_Airport) which provides most domestic and international destinations, it handles more than 10 million passengers per year.[^cite_note-26] Eastern parts of the regions including the city of [Annecy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annecy) are also served by [Geneva Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Airport) located 45 km (28 mi) away north of the city, taking advantage of the [Schengen Area](16565139.md) laws allowing passage in and out of Switzerland.

There are other airports across the region such as [Chambéry Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamb%C3%A9ry_Airport), [Clermont-Ferrand Auvergne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand_Auvergne_Airport) and [Grenoble Alpes Isère Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpes%E2%80%93Is%C3%A8re_Airport).&#x20;

## Politics

Main article: [Regional Council of Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Auvergne-Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes)

The region is governed by the [Regional Council of Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Auvergne-Rh%C3%B4ne-Alpes), consisting of 204 members. The current regional council was elected in [regional elections on 20 and 27 June 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_regional_elections), with the list of [Laurent Wauquiez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Wauquiez) consisting of [The Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Republicans_\(France\)) (LR) and the [Union of Democrats and Independents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Democrats_and_Independents) (UDI) securing an absolute majority of 136 seats.[^cite_note-27]

## Visual identity



The official pictogram of Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes, designed by Graphéine in 2017.

The visual identity of the Auvergne-Rhône-Alpes region was adopted in 2017 following the [territorial merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_French_regions_reform?action=edit\&redlink=1) of Auvergne and Rhône-Alpes.[^cite_note-brandnew-29][^cite_note-france3-30] The logotype consists of a two-tone pictogram synthesizing the three geographical components of the territory: a peak for the [Alps](981.md), a crater hollow evoking the volcanoes of [Auvergne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auvergne) in the [Massif Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif_Central), and a horizontal line representing the [Rhône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rh%C3%B4ne) river.[^cite_note-france3-30][^cite_note-31]
