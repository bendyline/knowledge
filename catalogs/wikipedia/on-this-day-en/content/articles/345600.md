[Glossop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossop), the second administrative centre of High Peak and the largest settlement in the borough

**High Peak** is a [local government district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_authority_district) with [borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough) status in [Derbyshire](71070.md), England, covering a high [moorland](553383.md) [plateau](538005.md) in the [Dark Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_Peak) area of the [Peak District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_District) National Park. The district stretches from [Holme Moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holme_Moss) in the north to Sterndale Moor in the south, and from [Hague Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Bar) in the west to [Bamford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamford) in the east. The population of the borough taken at the 2011 Census was 90,892.[^cite_note-4] The borough is unusual in having two administrative centres for its council, [High Peak Borough Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Peak_Borough_Council); the offices are in [Buxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buxton) and [Glossop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossop).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Other towns include [Chapel-en-le-Frith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel-en-le-Frith), [Hadfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadfield,_Derbyshire), [New Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mills) and [Whaley Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whaley_Bridge). Under [current local government restructuring plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upcoming_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England) it is planned that the district will be abolished in 2028, with its area forming part of a new [Derbyshire North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbyshire_North) unitary authority.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

High Peak was the name of a [hundred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_\(county_division\)) of the [ancient county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_counties_of_England) of Derbyshire covering a larger area than the current district with the hundred including the town of Bakewell and extending just to the west of Matlock. It may have derived its name from the ancient [Forest of High Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_of_High_Peak), a royal hunting reserve administered by [William Peverel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Peverel), a favourite of [William I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I_of_England), who was based at [Peak Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peveril_Castle). High Peak contains much of the Peak District National Park. The district contains the highest point in Derbyshire, [Kinder Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinder_Scout), which stands at 636m (2,087 ft) above sea level. Its settlements act as [commuter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_town) centres for people who work in the surrounding counties and other parts of Derbyshire, owing to its proximity to [Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire), [Greater Manchester](79819.md), [South Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Yorkshire), [Staffordshire](58733.md) and [West Yorkshire](149139.md).

## Creation

The borough was formed on 1 April 1974, covering the area of six former districts, which were abolished:[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

- [Buxton Municipal Borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Borough_of_Buxton)

- [Chapel-en-le-Frith Rural District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel_en_le_Frith_Rural_District)

- [Glossop Municipal Borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Borough_of_Glossop)

- [New Mills Urban District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mills_Urban_District)

- [Tintwistle Rural District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tintwistle_Rural_District) (which had been in the administrative county of [Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire))

- [Whaley Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whaley_Bridge) Urban District

## Neighbouring districts

The borough adjoins the [metropolitan boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_boroughs) of [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Sheffield) and [Barnsley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Barnsley) in [South Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Yorkshire), [Kirklees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirklees) in [West Yorkshire](149139.md), the districts of [Derbyshire Dales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbyshire_Dales), [Cheshire East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire_East) and [Staffordshire Moorlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staffordshire_Moorlands), and the [Stockport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Stockport), [Tameside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tameside) and [Oldham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Borough_of_Oldham) metropolitan boroughs in [Greater Manchester](79819.md). The Metropolitan Borough of Oldham is only bordered by high moorland near [Black Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hill_\(Peak_District\)) and is not accessible by road.

## Main settlements



Map of High Peak&#x20;

There are five main areas of settlement in the borough: around Buxton in the south west, around New Mills in the west, around Glossop in the north west, around Whaley Bridge and Chapel-en-le-Frith in the central part of the borough, and the [Hope Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_Valley,_Derbyshire) in the east. The northern part of the borough is close to the [Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Manchester_Urban_Area) urban area.

## National Trust

The [National Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Trust) is a major landowner in the district, owning extensive tracts of moorland and a number of farms, including some in Edale. Features of the Trust's [High Peak Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Peak_Estate) include [Kinder Scout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinder_Scout), [Odin Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odin_Mine) and [Mam Tor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mam_Tor).

## Media

For television, the area is served by [BBC North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_North_West) and [ITV Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Granada) broadcasting from the [Winter Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Hill_transmitting_station) transmitter.[^cite_note-16] Some eastern parts of the High Peak such as [Hope Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_Valley,_Derbyshire) are served by [BBC Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Yorkshire) and [ITV Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Yorkshire) broadcasting from the [Emley Moor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emley_Moor_transmitting_station) transmitter.[^cite_note-17]

Radio stations for the area are:

- [BBC Radio Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Manchester) on 95.1 MHz FM (covering [Glossop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossop), [Chapel-en-le-Frith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapel-en-le-Frith), [Hadfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadfield,_Derbyshire), [New Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mills) and [Whaley Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whaley_Bridge)).

- [BBC Radio Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_Derby) on 96.0 MHz FM (covering [Buxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buxton)) and 95.3 MHz FM (for [Hope Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_Valley,_Derbyshire)).

- [Greatest Hits Radio Derbyshire (High Peak)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Peak_Radio) on 103.3 MHz FM, 106.4 MHz FM, and 106.6 MHz FM.

Local newspapers are the *[Buxton Advertiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buxton_Advertiser)*, *Peak Advertiser*[^cite_note-18] and *Glossop Chronicle*.
