The **Houthi–Saudi conflict** is an ongoing armed conflict involving the [Armed Forces of Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Saudi_Arabia) and [Houthi](17240332.md) forces in [Yemen](350939.md) and along the [Saudi–Yemeni border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia%E2%80%93Yemen_border), including parts of [Asir](468723.md), [Jazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazan_Province), and [Najran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najran) in [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and [Saada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada), [Al-Jawf](3604364.md), and [Hajjah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hajjah) in Yemen. The conflict began following the [Saudi-led intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi-led_intervention_in_Yemen) in 2015.

## Background

During their 2004 conflict with the government of Yemeni President Ali Abdullah Saleh, the Houthis accused Saudi Arabia of pressuring Saleh to take action against their community and of providing him with financial support. This accusation was denied by Saleh, and was not acknowledged by Saudi Arabia.Yahya al-Houthi, early in July 2008 indicated Saudi Arabia pressured on Saleh to fight Zaydis and Shia Yemeni tribes[^cite_note-32].&#x20;

Houthi forces and Yemeni Army units aligned with the Houthis deployed teams equipped with anti-tank guided missiles (ATGMs) to target military vehicles and positions.. [BM-21s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-21) and [BM-27s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BM-27) would then target military bases and lay [landmines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landmine) along routes connecting with the [Saudi Arabia–Yemen border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia%E2%80%93Yemen_border). Meanwhile, pro-Houthi special forces would assault the border outposts, while motorcycle mounted teams armed with [RPG-7s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RPG-7) and US-made [M47 Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M47_Dragon) infiltrated the Saudi rear. Columns of [Saudi Arabian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian_Army) (RSLF) forces drive into unknown minefields and ambush places not directly hit by the fighting. Some raiding parties deployed into Saudi Arabia were armed with portable air defense systems ([MANPADS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MANPADS)), as well as [M167 Vulcan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M167_Vulcan) towed anti-aircraft cannons. In the first year of the war, they damaged numerous [attack helicopters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_helicopter) of the RSLF and the [Royal Saudi Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Saudi_Navy) (RSNF), forcing their crews to keep their distance.[^cite_note-33]

## Conflict

### 2015

As a result of the Saudi-led intervention in Yemen, the Houthis shelled the Saudi city of [Najran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najran) on 5 May 2015. Saudi authorities temporarily closed local schools, and the [Najran Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najran_Regional_Airport).[^cite_note-34]

### 2016

Saudi Arabia stated on 25 July 2016 that clashes resulted in the deaths of five Saudi [border guards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_guards).[^cite_note-35]

Regular clashes resumed on 11 August, after a one-year ceasefire between the Saudis and Houthis at the Saudi–Yemeni border expired. The Houthis gained entrance into Najran city.[^cite_note-36] On 16 August, Houthi shelling killed seven civilians in an industrial zone in Najran.[^cite_note-37] On the 26th, a power station near the border was hit by a Houthi rocket. A 3-year-old boy was killed by subsequent debris, and his 9-year-old brother was injured.[^cite_note-38] On the 28th, Saudi officials stated that a rocket fired from Yemen hit a family's home in Saudi Arabia, killing two children and wounding five others.[^cite_note-39]

On 12 September, Saudi Arabia stated that a Houthi mortar killed three Saudi soldiers and wounded two in [Najran Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Najran_Province), and also that there had been over five hundred Houthi casualties since the start of the conflict in the province.[^cite_note-auto-40] On 24 September, Houthi Major-General Hassan Almalsi was killed while his squad was attempting to infiltrate into Najran Province.[^cite_note-41]

On 12 October, Salafist fighters, including Salafist leader Bassam al-Mehbar, took control of [al-Buqa'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Buqa') border crossing in Yemen's [Saada Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saada_Governorate), with the help of Saudi air support.[^cite_note-42] On the 13th, a Saudi spy drone was shot down by Houthis over Al-Qawiya military base in Jizan. On the same day, a Saudi military vehicle came under fire from Houthis, and a Saudi soldier was killed by Houthi snipers in the Al-Rabiah area of Jizan.[^cite_note-43]

On 1 November, the Houthis claimed to have shot down a drone flying over the Ailab area in Asir Province with an anti-aircraft missile. This was the fourth time in two months that the Houthis had shot down a Saudi aircraft.[^cite_note-44]

On 15 November, Saudi Arabia claimed the RSADF intercepted a missile targeting Najran.[^cite_note-45] On the 19th, Saudi Arabia claimed a Saudi soldier was killed by a missile fired by the Houthis across the border into Asir Province.[^cite_note-46] On the 22nd, according to a Civil Defense Directorate spokesman, one Yemeni citizen was killed and seven other expatriates were injured when projectiles fired from Yemen hit a shopping center in Najran.[^cite_note-47]

On 8 December, a Saudi border guard was killed by a landmine near Jazan Province's Yemeni border.[^cite_note-48] On 21 December, 25 Houthis were killed by a coalition airstrike in Najran Province.[^cite_note-49] On the 22nd, Saudi forces carried out a night-time military operation in Jazan and Najran Provinces, killing thirty Houthis.[^cite_note-50]

### 2017



[Ali Abdullah Saleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Abdullah_Saleh), [president of the Republic of Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Republic_of_Yemen) from 1990 to 2012



[Mohammed bin Salman](38689358.md), *[de facto](43594.md)* ruler of Saudi Arabia since 2015

Saudi Arabia stated that at a border guards' post in Najran Province on 14 January 2017, a Saudi corporal was killed as a result of shelling and other intensive fire.[^cite_note-51]

On 13 February, Saudi state media claimed at least seven Saudi soldiers had been killed in the past week while fighting the Houthis.[^cite_note-52] On 24 February, a Jordanian [F-16](11642.md) warplane crashed in Najran Province, reportedly due to a technical error. The pilot survived.[^cite_note-53]

On 23 March, Houthi media claimed several Saudi soldiers were killed by Houthi missiles. According to Yemeni state media, the struck military sites were in Najran, Jazan, and Asir Provinces. However, Saudi Arabia announced the death of just one soldier on a border post in south Dhahran.[^cite_note-54]

On 16 April, Saudi Arabia claimed one Saudi border guard was killed and three others injured in a mine explosion in Jazan Province.[^cite_note-55] On 27 April, Saudi Interior Ministry stated that two soldiers were killed in Al-Rdhaa Sector, Jazan Province, as a result of land mine explosion and projectiles from Yemen.[^cite_note-56]

On 11 May, Houthis captured a Saudi military base in Raboah, Asir Province, killing and wounding several Saudi soldiers.[^cite_note-57] On 28 May, three Saudi personnel were ambushed by Houthis fighters, including a high-ranking officer at Asir.[^cite_note-58]



Protesters against the Saudi-led war on Yemen being handcuffed by the [NYPD](255413.md) outside the [US mission to the UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Mission_to_the_United_Nations) on 11 December 2017

On 5 November, the Houthi missile was launched the Saudi capital of [Riyadh](51554.md), which garnered worldwide media attention. According to a Houthi spokesperson, the missile hit its target, [King Khalid International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Khalid_International_Airport), while Saudi Arabia claimed that it intercepted the missile before it got there.[^cite_note-59]

### 2018

On 11 and 12 January 2018, the Houthis fired missiles at Najran; Saudi Arabia claimed to have intercepted them.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

On 11 and 14 February, Houthi fighters fought the Saudi Armed Forces near Najran city. The Houthis were repelled, with Saudi Arabia claiming to have killed more than twenty of them.[^cite_note-62]

On 26 March, Houthis [fired seven missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Riyadh_missile_strike) towards the Saudi capital of Riyadh, all of which were intercepted by Saudi systems. One person was killed, and two were wounded.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

### 2019

On 14 May 2019, a Houthi attack drone struck the Saudi East–West Crude Oil Pipeline. This temporarily shut down the pipeline, and it was reopened later.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

On 23 June, the Houthis carried out a drone [attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abha_International_Airport_attacks) on [Abha International Airport](9419420.md), killing one person and wounding 21.[^cite_note-67]

On 17 August, the Houthis attacked [an oil and gas field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaybah_oil_field) in [Shaybah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaybah), Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-68]



Victims of Saudi-led [airstrikes on a university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhamar_airstrike) used as a detention center by the Houthis in [Dhamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhamar,_Yemen), 2 September 2019

On 14 September, the Houthis claimed responsibility for the [Abqaiq and Khurais drone attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abqaiq_and_Khurais_drone_attacks), which caused massive damage to [Saudi oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_oil) facilities.[^cite_note-69] On 28 September, a Houthi official stated that three Saudi-led brigades, alongside KSA forces, were besieged and defeated in a 72-hour battle south of Najran. The Houthis claimed the brigades were preparing for a major attack against the Houthis in retaliation to the [attack on Abqaiq and Khurais oil facilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Abqaiq%E2%80%93Khurais_attack), but were entrapped by the Houthis. The Saudis then allegedly conducted airstrikes targeting the Saudi captives but the spokesman assured the families of the captives that they were able to hide and protect them from the Saudi airstrikes.[^cite_note-70] Catherine Shakdam from the Next Century Foundation said that there is no reason to doubt the Houthi statement,[^cite_note-71] yet the Houthis' claims could not be corroborated.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] Saudi Arabia neither confirmed nor denied the attack.[^cite_note-74]

Protest against the war in Yemen on 14 August 2020

On 23 November, a Houthi missile attacked an oil tank at Oil Co. facility in [Jeddah](277718.md), causing a fire.[^cite_note-77] On 25 November, Saudi-led coalition claimed to have destroyed a suicide boat that damaged a nearby ship.[^cite_note-78]

On 9 December, the coalition intercepted a Houthi attack drone launched towards Saudi Arabia.[^cite_note-79]

### 2022

Reportedly, on 21 January 2022, at least 70 people were killed and 138 were wounded in northern Yemen, when the coalition launched airstrikes on Houthis in response to a [Houthi attack on Abu Dhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Abu_Dhabi_attack) earlier that month. One area Saudi Arabia struck was a prison. The coalition also damaged the Internet infrastructure of the entire country.[^cite_note-146] Saudi Arabia denied the air raid on the prison, which killed dozens; on the 27th, The Saudi-led coalition said it will investigate the attack.[^cite_note-147]

On 10 February, after Saudi air defences intercepted and destroyed a drone near the Yemeni borders, at least 12 civilians were hurt by shrapnel.[^cite_note-148]

On 10 March, the Saudi Press Agency reported that a drone attack on an oil refinery in Riyadh, started a small fire that did not cause injuries. They did not specify where the drone strike was launched from, and Houthis later claimed responsibility for it.[^cite_note-149] The attack caused a short dip in output at a refinery.[^cite_note-150] On 20 March, the coalition reported that Houthis fired missiles and drones at Saudi energy and water desalination facilities, causing a temporary drop in output at a refinery. Later on that day, according to the Saudi-led coalition, another Aramco distribution plant was attacked in Jeddah, leading to a fire in one of the tanks. The fire was controlled and did not result in any casualties.[^cite_note-151] On the 26th, the Houthis announced a three-day ceasefire, and offered Saudi Arabia a permanent ceasefire.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] The coalition responded by unleashing a barrage of airstrikes on Sanaa and Hodeidah, killing at least seven.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156]

On 28 March, the coalition said it destroyed numerous Houthi attack drones launched towards areas all over Saudi Arabia. The coalition also reported a fire at an electricity distribution plant in Samtah following a Houthi rocket attack. This was followed by another attack that targeted the National Water Company tanks in [Dhahran Al Janub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhahran_Al_Janub) governorate, Asir Province.[^cite_note-157] Later that day, the coalition said, Aramco's petroleum products distribution station in Jeddah were hit, close to the [Jeddah Corniche Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah_Corniche_Circuit) where the [Formula One](10854.md) [Saudi Arabian Grand Prix](65766701.md) was scheduled to take place the following weekend; this allegedly caused a fire in two storage tanks.[^cite_note-158] The attacks reportedly increased the prices of [Brent Crude](1150532.md) oil to 120 dollars USD per [barrel](594043.md),[^cite_note-159] and [U.S. West Texas Intermediate](797304.md) oil to 113 dollars per barrel.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161] On the 31st, Saudi Arabia added 25 people and organisations from various countries to its terrorist lists for allegedly funding the Houthis.[^cite_note-162]

On 13 April, the U.S. Navy announced the formation of a new multinational task force to combat arms smuggling in the waters surrounding Yemen, in response to Houthi attacks on Saudi Arabia and the UAE.[^cite_note-163] On 28 April, the Saudi-led coalition operating in Yemen announced that it will release 163 Houthi militant prisoners.[^cite_note-164]

On 6 May, the coalition in Yemen said it transported more than a hundred of the inmates to Yemen, in conjunction with the [Red Cross](15487.md).[^cite_note-165]

On 2 June, the warring sides agreed to extend a UN-brokered cease-fire for another two months on the same terms as the previous agreement, which was set to expire on June.[^cite_note-166] On 27 June, following the Iraqi Prime Minister's call for a renewal of negotiations between the regional adversaries, an Iranian official claimed at a news conference that Saudi Arabia wants to begin diplomatic discussions with Iran.[^cite_note-167]
