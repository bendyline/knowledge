**Euboea**,[^cite_note-1] also known as **Evia**,[^cite_note-2] is the second-largest [Greek island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Greece) in area and population, after [Crete](6591.md), and the [sixth largest island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_in_the_Mediterranean#By_area) in the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md). It is separated from [Boeotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeotia) in mainland [Greece](12108.md) by the narrow [Euripus Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripus_Strait) (only 40 m (130 ft) at its narrowest point), the [North Euboean Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Euboean_Gulf), and the [South Euboean Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Euboean_Gulf).[^cite_note-3] In general outline it is a long and narrow island; it is about 180 km (110 mi) long, and varies in breadth from 50 km (31 mi) to 6 km (3.7 mi). Its geographic orientation is from northwest to southeast, and it is traversed throughout its length by a mountain range, which forms part of the chain that bounds [Thessaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaly) on the east, and is continued south of Euboia in the lofty islands of [Andros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andros,_Greece), [Tinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinos) and [Mykonos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykonos).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911865-4]

It forms most of the [regional unit of Euboea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euboea_\(regional_unit\)), which also includes [Skyros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyros) and a small area of the Greek mainland.

## Name

Further information: [Euboea (mythology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euboea_\(mythology\))

Like most of the Greek islands, Euboea was known by other names in [antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece), such as *Macris* (Μάκρις) and *Doliche* (Δολίχη) from its elongated shape, or *Ellopia* (after Ellops the son of [Ion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_\(mythology\))), *Aonia* and *Abantis* from the tribes inhabiting it,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911866-5] or *Ocha/Oche* (Ὄχη), which is also the name of one of the highest mountains on the island.[^cite_note-Strabo-6] The name *Euboea* (Εὔβοια) derives from the words εὖ 'good', and βοῦς 'ox', meaning '(the land of) the well(-fed) oxen'. [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) writes that it took the name *Euboea* either from the heroine Euboea or because of a cave on the island at the Aegaean coast which was called [Boösaule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bo%C3%B6saule) (βοὸς αὐλὴ, meaning 'cow's stall'), where [Io](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Io_\(mythology\)) was said to have given birth to [Epaphus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epaphus).[^cite_note-Strabo-6]

In the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), the island was often referred to by [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) authors as *[Chalcis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcis)* (Χαλκίς), the name of its capital, or [*Euripos*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripus_Strait) (Εὔριπος), the name of the strait that separates the island from the Greek mainland; but the ancient name Euboea remained in use by classicizing authors until the 16th century.

The phrase στὸν Εὔριπον 'to Evripos', [rebracketed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebracketing) as στὸ Νεὔριπον 'to Nevripos', became *Negroponte* ('Black Bridge') in Italian by [folk etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymology), the *ponte* 'bridge' being interpreted as the bridge of Chalcis. This name was most relevant when the island was under [Venetian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Greece).[^cite_note-gibbon-7] That name entered common use in the West in the 13th century,[^cite_note-ODB-8] with other variants being *Egripons*, *Negripo*, and *Negropont*.[^cite_note-gibbon-7]

Under [Ottoman](22278.md) rule, the island and its capital were known as *Eğriboz* or *Ağriboz*, again after the Euripos strait.

## Geography and climate



Topography of Euboea and parts of the Greek mainland.



Landscape near Eretria



View of [Kantili mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantili).

Euboea was believed to have originally formed part of the mainland, and to have been separated from it by an [earthquake](10106.md). This is fairly probable, because it lies in the neighbourhood of a [fault line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics), and both [Thucydides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thucydides) and [Strabo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strabo) write that the northern part of the island had been shaken at different periods.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911866-5] In the neighbourhood of [Chalcis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcis), both to the north and the south, the bays are so confined as to make plausible the story of [Agamemnon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agamemnon)'s fleet having been detained there by contrary winds. The [Euripus Strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripus_Strait) is narrowest, only 40 m, at Chalcis itself. The extraordinary changes of tide that take place in this passage have been a subject of note since classical times, and it was so feared by sailors that the principal line of traffic from the north of the Aegean to Athens used to bypass Chalcis and the Euboic Sea.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911866-5] At one moment the current runs like a river in one direction, and shortly afterwards with equal velocity in the other. A bridge was first constructed here in the twenty-first year of the [Peloponnesian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnesian_War) (410 BC).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911866-5]

Geography and nature divide the island itself into three distinct parts: the fertile and forested north (which suffered major damage in the August 2021 forest fires); the forested mountainous centre, with agriculture limited to the coastal valleys; and the barren south.[^cite_note-ODB-8]

The main mountains include [Dirfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirfi) (1,743 m (5,719 ft)), [Kantili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantili) (1,246 m (4,088 ft)), Pyxaria (1,341 m (4,400 ft)) in the northeast and [Ochi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ochi_Mountains) (1,394 m (4,573 ft)) in the south. The neighboring gulfs are the [Pagasetic Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagasetic_Gulf) in the north, the [Malian Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malian_Gulf) and [North Euboean Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Euboean_Gulf) in the west, and the [South Euboean Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Euboean_Gulf) and [Petalion Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petalion_Gulf) in the south. The [Petalioi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petalioi) archipelago lies to the southwest, while the [Monilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monilia_\(Greece\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) islands lie to the west.

With a total land area of 3,684 km<sup>2</sup> (1,422 sq mi), the island had a population of 198,130 at the 2001 census.

Most of the island features a hot-summer [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*) with hot, dry summers and cool, wet winters.

## History

### Antiquity



Exhibits in the archaeological museum of Chalcis.

Obverse: Head of the [nymph Euboea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euboea_\(mythology\)). Reverse: [Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bull)'s head, [kantharos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantharos) to right EY\[ΒΟΙΕΩΝ] *"of the Euboeans"*.

The archeological site of [Lefkandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lefkandi) is an important site dating to the [Greek Dark Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Dark_Ages) that shows continuous activity on the island during this time period. The classical history of the island of Euboea is largely that of its two principal cities, [Chalcis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcis) and [Eretria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eretria), both mentioned in the [Catalogue of Ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalogue_of_Ships). Both cities were settled by [Ionian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionians) Greeks from [Attica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attica), and would eventually settle numerous colonies in [Magna Graecia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graecia) and [Sicily](27619.md), such as [Cumae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumae) and [Rhegium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_Calabria), and on the coast of [Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonia_\(region\)). This opened new trade routes to the [Greeks](42056.md), and extended the reach of Western Civilization.[^cite_note-9] The commercial influence of these city-states is evident in the fact that the Euboic scale of weights and measures was used among the Ionic cities generally, and in [Athens](1216.md) until the end of the 7th century BC, during the time of [Solon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solon).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911866-5] The classicist [Barry B. Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_B._Powell) has proposed that Euboea may have been where the [Greek alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_alphabet) was first employed, c. 775–750 BC, and that [Homer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer) may have spent part of his life on the island.[^cite_note-10]

Silver tetrobol from Euboia, Histaia

Silver tetrobol from Euboia, Histaia. Wreathed head of the Nymph Histiaia right; \[ΙΣΤΙ] – ΑΕΙΩΝ, Nymph Histiaia seated right on stern of galley, ornamented with wing, holding naval standard; AP monogram and labrys in exergue; BMC 61; BCD 391

Chalcis and Eretria were rival cities, and appear to have been equally powerful for a while. One of the earliest major military conflicts in Greek history took place between them, known as the [Lelantine War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelantine_War), in which many other Greek city-states also took part.[^cite_note-11] In 490 BC, Eretria was utterly ruined by the Persian armies. Eretria, Athens, and other Ionian Greek states had previously burned the Persian city of Sardis and participated in the Ionian revolution. After Eretria was destroyed, its inhabitants were transported as captives to [Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Empire). Though it was restored nearby its original site after the [Battle of Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marathon), the city never regained its former eminence.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911867-12] Following the battles of [Thermopylae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Thermopylae) and [Artemisium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Artemisium) in 480 BC, [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) forces occupied Euboea along with Attica and Boeotia.[^cite_note-L248-13] Following their decisive defeat at the [Battle of Plataea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Plataea) the following year, the Persians withdrew from all of their European possessions, including Euboea.

Both cities gradually lost influence to Athens, which saw Euboea as a strategic territory. Euboea was an important source of [grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain) and [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle), and controlling the island meant Athens could prevent invasion and better protect its trade routes from [piracy](50715.md#Ancient_origins). Athens invaded Chalcis in 506 BC and settled 4,000 Attic Greeks on their lands. After this conflict, the whole of the island was gradually reduced to an Athenian dependency.[^cite_note-14] Another struggle between Euboea and Athens broke out in 446. Led by [Pericles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pericles), the Athenians subdued the revolt, and captured [Histiaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istiaia) in the north of the island for their own settlement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911867-12]

By 410 BC, during the [Peloponnesian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnesian_War), the island succeeded in regaining its independence. Euboea participated in Greek affairs until it fell under the control of [Philip II of Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Macedon) after the [Battle of Chaeronea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Chaeronea_\(338_BC\)) in 338 BC. It was incorporated into the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) in the second century BC.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911867-12] [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle) died on the island in 322 BC soon after fleeing Athens for his mother's family estate in Chalcis. From the early [Hellenistic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period) to well into the [Roman Imperial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), the island was organized into the [Euboean League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euboean_League).

### Middle Ages

Unlike much of [Byzantine Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Greece), Euboea was spared the bulk of the barbarian raids during [late antiquity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_antiquity) and the early medieval period, due to its relatively isolated location. The [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals) raided its shores in 466 and in 475, but the island seems to have been left alone by the [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Avars) and [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs), and it was not until a failed Arab attack on Chalcis in the 870s that the island again came under threat.[^cite_note-ODB-8] As a result, the island preserved a relative prosperity throughout the early medieval period, as attested by finds of mosaics, churches and sculpture throughout the 7th century, "even from remote areas of the island". In the 6th century, the *[Synecdemus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synecdemus)* listed four cities on the island, [Aidipsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidipsos), Chalcis, Porthmos (modern [Aliveri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliveri)) and [Karystos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karystos), and a number of other sites are known as bishoprics in the subsequent centuries ([Oreoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oreoi) and [Avlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avlon,_Euboea)), although their urban character is unclear.[^cite_note-ODB-8] In the 8th century, Euboea formed a distinct fiscal district (*dioikesis*), and then formed part of the [theme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_\(Byzantine_district\)) of [Hellas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellas_\(theme\)).[^cite_note-ODB-8]

In 1157 all the coastal towns of Euboea were destroyed by a [Sicilian](27619.md) force,[^cite_note-15] while Chalcis was burned down by the [Venetians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice) in 1171.[^cite_note-ODB-8] During the 13th century, the Greek element of the island was strengthened by the Byzantine Emperor Michael Palaiologos.[^cite_note-\:1-16]

Euboea came into prominence following the [Fourth Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Crusade). In the partition of the Byzantine Empire by the crusaders after 1204, the island was occupied by a number of [Lombard](43807.md) families, who divided it into three baronies, the [Triarchy of Negroponte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triarchy_of_Negroponte); each barony was split in 1216, giving six [sestiere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sestiere). The island's rulers came early on under the influence of the [Venetian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Republic), which secured control of the island's commerce in the [War of the Euboeote Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Euboeote_Succession) (1256–1258) and gradually expanded its control, until they acquired full sovereignty by 1390.

On 12 July 1470, during the [Ottoman–Venetian War of 1463–1479](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Venetian_War_\(1463%E2%80%931479\)) and after a [protracted and bloody siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Negroponte_\(1470\)), the well-fortified city of Negroponte (Chalcis) was wrested from Venice by [Mehmed II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_II) and the whole island fell into the hands of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md). The Doge [Francesco Morosini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Morosini) besieged the city in 1688, but was forced to withdraw after three months.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911867-12]

[Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians) started settling Euboea gradually, since 1402, encouraged by the [Venetians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Venice). In 1425, a total of 10,000 Albanians from various regions were settled in Euboea. A further indeterminate number of Albanians settled in the island in 1435. These Albanians intermingled with the local Greeks of the island.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] According to [Johann Georg Von Hahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Georg_von_Hahn) (1854), Albanians were present in all of the cities of southern Euboea, excluding the town of [Karystos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karystos), which was inhabited solely by Greeks.[^cite_note-\:2-19][^cite_note-Elsie-20] According to Hahn, the Albanians numbered 25,000 in south Euboea, out of the total population of 72,368 of the island as a whole.[^cite_note-\:2-19]

The Greek inhabitants of south Euboea spoke a certain dialect related to [Old Athenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Modern_Greek#Modern_varieties).[^cite_note-21] This dialect was spoken in Kymi, Avlonari, Konistres, Aliveri, Karystos and other places.[^cite_note-22]

Although the name *Negroponte* remained current in European languages until the 19th century, the Turks themselves called the city and the island *Eğriboz* or *Ağriboz* after the Euripos Strait. Under Ottoman rule, Ağriboz was the seat of a [sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak_of_A%C4%9Friboz) that also encompassed much of [Continental Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Greece_\(geographic_region\)).

At the conclusion of the [Greek War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_War_of_Independence) in 1830, the island returned to Greece and constituted a part of the newly established independent [Greek kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece_\(Wittelsbach\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911867-12]

### Modern period



The Chalcis' Bridge connecting the island with the mainland of [Greece](12108.md).

In 1982, in the Greek-speaking village of Antia in southern Euboea, the entire population knew the local [whistled language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistled_language) called *sfyria* (from the Greek word *sfyrizo* 'whistle'),[^cite_note-Stein2017-23] but only a few whistlers remain in the 21st century.[^cite_note-Meyer2005-24] *Sfyria* is "a whistled version of spoken Greek, in which letters and syllables correspond to distinct tones and frequencies."[^cite_note-Stein2017-23]

Beginning in late 1943, 1,000 Greek Jews were smuggled from [Thessaloniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salonika) and Athens via the island by the [Greek Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Resistance) and British [MI11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MI11) to [Çeşme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%87e%C5%9Fme) in neutral [Turkey](11125639.md), thereby escaping [the Holocaust in Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust_in_Greece).[^cite_note-25]

In the 1980s, the [Dystos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dystos) lake was filled with grass which was set on fire by farmers to make more farmland. This act caused devastation of much of the plants and the environment in that area. A part of the lake later regenerated. Also the municipalities of Anthidona and Avlida in the mid to late 20th century, which once were part of [Boeotia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeotia), reverted to Chalcis. Since then, the postal codes have corresponded with the rest of Euboea, including Skyros.&#x20;

A week-long major [forest fire in 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Greece_wildfires) destroyed over 50,000 hectares of forest and agricultural land in the north of the island, one of the largest forest fires in modern Greek history.

## Mythology

The promontory of Cenaeum, which lies opposite the Malian Gulf, together with the neighbouring coast of [Trachis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trachis), was the scene of the events connected with the death of [Heracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heracles), as described by [Sophocles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sophocles) in the *[Trachiniae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trachiniae)*.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETozer1911865-4]

Based on the records of the 2nd century AD geographer [Pausanias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pausanias_\(geographer\)), it is suspected that the [Titan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titan_\(mythology\)) god [Crius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crius) is an indigenous deity.[^cite_note-26]

## Demographics

The population of the island, according to the census of 2021, was 204,818, making it the second most populous island of Greece. As a whole the Euboeans share a cultural identity similar to that of the people in the rest of [Central Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Greece_\(geographic_region\)) and speak a [southern variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Modern_Greek) of [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language). In the southern part of the island there are [Arvanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvanites) communities. Until the 1970s there were still monolingual speakers of Arvanitika in southern Euboea. As of the late 20th century, in southern Euboea there were about 60 villages inhabited by Arvanites.[^cite_note-27] [Sarakatsani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarakatsani) and [Vlachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlachs) could be found mainly in the mountainous areas in central and northern Euboea respectively, but nowadays they have abandoned the nomadic way of life and live permanently in the towns and villages across the island.

## Economy

The mining areas include [magnesite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesite) in [Mantoudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mantoudi) and [Limni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limni,_Euboea), [lignite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite) in [Aliveri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliveri) and [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) and [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel) from [Dirfys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirfys). [Marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble) is mined 3 km (2 mi) north of [Eretria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eretria) which include *[Marmor Chalcidicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmor_Chalcidicum?action=edit\&redlink=1)* and [asbestos](21492663.md) in the northeastern part of [Carystus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carystus) in the [Okhi mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okhi_mountain). The trees include [chestnuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chestnuts).

## Transport

Euboea is linked to the mainland by [two bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripus_Strait#Bridges), an older sliding bridge that runs west from Chalcis towards [Thebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thebes,_Greece), and a [newer cable-stayed bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euripus_Bridge) which heads south from Chalcis towards Athens.

The [Oinoi–Chalcis railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oinoi%E2%80%93Chalcis_railway) ends just outside the island, at [Chalcis station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcis_railway_station). There are also three national roads in the island: the [EO44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_National_Road_44); the [EO77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_National_Road_77); and the [Chalcis–Vathy–Chalcis National Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcis%E2%80%93Vathy%E2%80%93Chalcis_National_Road), which leads to the [A11 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A11_motorway_\(Greece\)) just outside the island.

## Local administration

The island belongs to [Euboea Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euboea_Prefecture) which also includes two municipalities on the mainland, [Anthidona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthidona) and [Avlida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avlida), as well as the island municipality of [Skyros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyros). At the 2001 census the prefecture had a population of 215,136 inhabitants, whereas the island itself had a population of 198,130. The prefecture's land area is 4,167.449 km<sup>2</sup> (1,609 sq mi), whereas the total land area of the municipalities actually on the island is 3,684.848 km<sup>2</sup> (1,423 sq mi), which includes numerous small offshore islets ([Petalioi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petalioi)) near Euboea's southeastern tip.
