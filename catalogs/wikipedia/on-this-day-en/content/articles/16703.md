[Kyrgyzstan](170131.md) has close relations with other members of the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md), particularly [Kazakhstan](16642.md) and [Russia](25391.md), given the historical legacy of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). It also has close relations with [Turkey](11125639.md) as well, given their shared heritage as [Turkic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages).

While Kyrgyzstan was initially determined to stay in the [ruble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_ruble) zone, the stringent conditions set forth by the Russian Government prompted Kyrgyzstan to introduce its own currency, the [som](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrgyzstani_som), in May 1993. Kyrgyzstan's withdrawal from the ruble zone was done with little prior notification and initially caused tensions in the region. Both Kazakhstan and [Uzbekistan](31853.md) temporarily suspended trade, and Uzbekistan even introduced restrictions tantamount to economic sanctions. Both nations feared an influx of rubles and an increase in inflation. Uzbekistan and Kazakhstan's hostility toward Kyrgyzstan was short-lived, and the three nations signed an agreement in January 1994 creating an economic union. This led to the relaxation of border restrictions between the nations the following month. Kyrgyzstan also has contributed to the CIS peacekeeping forces in [Tajikistan](30108.md).

[Turkey](11125639.md) has sought to capitalize on its cultural and ethnic links to the region and has found Kyrgyzstan receptive to cultivating bilateral relations. The Kyrgyz Republic also has experienced a dramatic increase in trade with the [People's Republic of China](5405.md), its southern neighbor. Kyrgyzstan has been active in furthering regional cooperation, such as joint military exercises with Uzbek and Kazakh troops.

In January 1999, a new [OSCE](22796.md) office opened in [Bishkek](4554.md); on February 18, 2000, the OSCE announced that an additional office would open in Osh to assist Bishkek in carrying out its work. Kyrgyzstan is a member of the OSCE, the CIS, and the [United Nations](31769.md).

## Illegal drug trade

Kyrgyzstan is a limited illicit cultivator of [cannabis](1481886.md) and [opium poppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_poppy), mostly for CIS consumption. There is a limited government eradication program. Kyrgyzstan is used increasingly as a [transshipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transshipment) point for illicit drugs to [Russia](25391.md) and [Western Europe](33800.md) from [Southwest Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Asia).[^cite_note-41] In 2020, with the assistance of the UN Office on Drugs and Crime (UNODC) Program Office in Kyrgyzstan, the Analytical Center of the Counter Narcotic Service of the Ministry of Interior of the Kyrgyz Republic was established to combat drug trafficking.[^cite_note-42]
