**Otjiwarongo** ([Herero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herero_language) for "beautiful place")[^cite_note-woordebook-3] is a city[^cite_note-ALANlist-4] of 49,000 inhabitants in [Namibia](21292.md). It is the capital of [Otjozondjupa Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otjozondjupa_Region), and it also houses the constituency office of the [Otjiwarongo electoral constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otjiwarongo_Constituency).

Otjiwarongo is situated in central-north Namibia on the [TransNamib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransNamib) railway. It is the biggest business centre in the Otjozondjupa Region. Otjiwarongo is located on the [B1 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B1_road_\(Namibia\)) and its links to the capital [Windhoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windhoek), the Golden Triangle of [Otavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otavi), [Tsumeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsumeb) and [Grootfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grootfontein), and [Etosha National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etosha_National_Park). It is one of Namibia's fast-growing towns, with a neat and peaceful quality environment and many excellent facilities, including supermarkets, banks, lodges, and hotels. Some of Namibia's best-known private game farms and nature reserves are located in and around the town.

Otjiwarongo is one of Namibia's towns with a large [population of German-speaking people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Namibians). German influence is also evident in its Germanic buildings. The school "Donatus School Otjiwarongo" (D.S.O.) was once known as "Deutsche Schule Otjiwarongo".

The [San](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_people) and [Damaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damaras) were the first settlers of the area, the Damaras from the Geio-Daman clan lived in the area from as early as 1390. The Damara also named the city ǂKhanubes. [German Namibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Namibians) first settled in Otjiwarongo in 1900. A [bloody war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herero_and_Namaqua_Genocide) was fought in the area between the [Hereros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereros) and the [Germans](152735.md) in 1904 where many of the Herero people died. This happened sometime before Otjiwarongo was established as an administration point for the Germans and officially became a town. A narrow-gauge railway was built from [Swakopmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swakopmund) on the coast, to the [Otavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otavi) copper mine which helped Otjiwarongo become a prosperous agricultural centre. The three tribes where separated like in many of Namibia's towns, where each lived in a separate neighbourhood: *Ovambo Location*, *Damara Location* and *Herero Location* respectively. The three locations together made up the suburb of [Orwetoveni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orwetoveni?action=edit\&redlink=1).

## Economy and infrastructure

### Mining



Pink [fluorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorite) specimen from the Okorusu Mine near Otjiwarongo. Size: 6.4 x 6.0 x 1.1 cm.

Otjiwarongo is one of [Namibia's mining towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_in_Namibia). Mining contributes about 20% of the town's economy.[^cite_note-evolution-6] [B2Gold mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B2Gold_mine?action=edit\&redlink=1), an open-pit gold mine established in 2014 and owned by [B2Gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B2Gold), is located approximately 70 km (43 mi) northwest of town. 48 km (30 mi) to the north there is the Okorusu [fluorspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorspar) mine, a well-known source of [fluorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorite) specimens for [mineral collectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_collector). The mine is a potential resource for [rare-earth elements](145440.md).[^cite_note-7]

### Health facilities

Otjiwarongo District State Hospital is the biggest hospital in the town, it is mostly used by the middle and low income residents. The private hospital, Mediclinic Otjiwarongo, serves the local and surrounding communities, as well as international tourists.

### Tourism

The main interest for tourists is Otjiwarongo's proximity to the [Waterberg Plateau Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterberg_Plateau_Park). Otjiwarongo is home to the [Cheetah Conservation Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheetah_Conservation_Fund), an internationally recognized organization dedicated to ensuring the long-term survival of the cheetah through research, conservation and education. Also about 80 km from Otjiwarongo is [Okonjima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okonjima), the home of the [Africat Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africat_Foundation), a cheetah and leopard rehabilitation centre.[^cite_note-8]

On the edge of town is the Crocodile Ranch, one of the few captive breeding programmes for the [Nile Crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_Crocodile) that has been registered with [CITES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CITES).[^cite_note-9]

Built 15 km outside of town, the [Omatjenne Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omatjenne_Dam) provides artificial recharge of local groundwater.[^cite_note-10]

### Transport



German [Class Hd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_African_Class_Hd) Locomotive no. 41 plinthed at Otjiwarongo railway station

Otjiwarongo has a well-developed road network. It is situated at the junction of the [national road B1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B1_road_\(Namibia\)) that passes north–south through all of Namibia, the [C38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C33_road_\(Namibia\)) to [Outjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outjo) and further into [Kunene Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunene_Region) in Namibia's north-west, and the [C33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C33_road_\(Namibia\)) to [Karibib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karibib), connecting the coastal towns of [Swakopmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swakopmund) and [Walvis Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walvis_Bay). Roads in town are likewise well-maintained, making Otjiwarongo one of the few Namibian towns that has tarred roads even in the townships.

Otjiwarongo is connected to the national railway grid, run by [TransNamib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransNamib). [Otjiwarongo railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otjiwarongo_railway_station) is situated downtown, connecting [Otavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otavi) and the junction at [Kranzberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kranzberg_railway_station), and branching off to [Outjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outjo). In front of the railway station stands the historic *Locomotive No 41*, originally brought from Germany to haul ore between [Tsumeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsumeb) and the port at [Swakopmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swakopmund).

The town has an airstrip, [Otjiwarongo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otjiwarongo_Airport). There are plans to develop an international airport.

## Politics

Otjiwarongo is governed by a municipal council that currently has seven seats.[^cite_note-11]

The [2015 local authority election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibian_local_and_regional_elections,_2015) was won by [SWAPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWAPO) which gained five seats and 3,901 votes. One seat each went to the [Democratic Turnhalle Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Turnhalle_Alliance) (DTA, 454 votes) and the [United Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Democratic_Front_\(Namibia\)) (UDF, 442 votes).[^cite_note-12] SWAPO also won the [2020 local authority election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Namibian_local_and_regional_elections). It obtained 3,128 votes and gained four seats. One seat each went to [Independent Patriots for Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Patriots_for_Change) (IPC, an opposition party formed in August 2020, 1,208 votes), [Popular Democratic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Democratic_Movement) (PDM, the new name of the DTA, 537 votes) and [Landless People's Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landless_People's_Movement_\(Namibia\)) (LPM, an opposition party formed in 2016, 488 votes).[^cite_note-13]

## Education

There are about 15 schools in Otjiwarongo, three private schools and twelve public schools. All schools final exams for grade 10 and 12 are regulated by the Ministry of Education, Arts & Culture. The schools of Otjiwarongo attract more and more non-resident students. The town also has a community library that caters for reading needs of the towns residents.

There are a number of institution for higher education in the town, as well as the MTI and COSDEC [vocational training centres](56551.md) and a [convent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convent). The [University of Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Namibia) and the [Namibia University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_University_of_Science_and_Technology) had plans to build satellite campuses in the town. The two institutions already have their regional centres in the town where [distance students](8997.md) interact with the two institutions respectively.

## Language

About 90% of the town's residents speak and understand [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans). About 75% speaks English and 35% German. Other languages include indigenous languages like [Otjiherero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otjiherero), [Khoe-Khoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damara_people) and [Oshiwambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshiwambo).

## Sport

[Mighty Gunners FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mighty_Gunners_FC) is the town's major football team. [Life Fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_Fighters_F.C.) (Okahirona) also returned to the [Namibia Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namibia_Premier_League) in 2017 after more than a decade. [Mokati Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mokati_Stadium), the smaller of two stadia in Otjiwarongo, is located in Orwetoveni and it is the main football stadium in town. There are also grounds for [basketball](3921.md), [netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netball), and [tennis](29773.md). The largest, [Paresis Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paresis_Park?action=edit\&redlink=1) also known as "The Show Ground", is located in the upper suburb of the town. It is the towns biggest sport ground and one of the biggest in [Namibia](21292.md). It has two soccer fields and two rugby fields. There are also grounds for [hockey](10886.md), [tennis](29773.md), [netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netball), and [cricket](25675557.md). The park is also used to host sport tournaments and business events such as the Otjiwarongo Trade Show. In 2011, it hosted the main event of the 21st Independence Celebrations of Namibia.[^cite_note-17]

## Geography

### Living conditions

In many of Otjiwarongo's townships residents live in [shacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shack). In 2020 the city had a total of 6,251 of these informal housing structures, accommodating more than 50,000 inhabitants,[^cite_note-18] more than the most recent (2011) census reported as total population figure.

### Climate

Otjiwarongo has a [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) (*BSh*, according to the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)), with hot summers and mild winters. The average annual precipitation is 457 mm (18 in).
