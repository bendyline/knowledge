**Cocaine** is a [central nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system) (CNS) [stimulant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulant) and [tropane alkaloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropane_alkaloid), derived primarily from the leaves of two [coca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca) species native to South America: *[Erythroxylum coca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erythroxylum_coca)* and *[E. novogranatense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erythroxylum_novogranatense)*.[^cite_note-EUDA-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-Pom_2012-20][^cite_note-Andean_full_report.pdf-21] The leaves are processed into [cocaine paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_paste), a crude mixture of [coca alkaloids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_alkaloids), from which cocaine base is isolated and then converted to **cocaine hydrochloride**.[^cite_note-Andean_full_report.pdf-21] Although total synthesis is possible, it is complex and not used for production.[^cite_note-22] Historically, cocaine was a standard [topical medication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topical_medication) used as a [local anesthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_anesthetic) with intrinsic [vasoconstrictor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasoconstriction) properties. However, its high abuse potential, adverse effects, and cost have limited its medical use and led to its replacement by alternative medicines.[^cite_note-Latorre_1999-23][^cite_note-Bremner_2019-24][^cite_note-Armbuster_2021-25]

Street cocaine is commonly [snorted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_administration), [injected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injection_\(medicine\)), or smoked as [crack cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine); its effects last up to 90 minutes depending on the route of administration.[^cite_note-Zimmerman_2012-15][^cite_note-smokablecocaine_eng_web-def.pdf-26] Pharmacologically, cocaine acts as a [serotonin–norepinephrine–dopamine reuptake inhibitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin%E2%80%93norepinephrine%E2%80%93dopamine_reuptake_inhibitor) (SNDRI),[^cite_note-Azizi_2022-7][^cite_note-Cheng_2015-27][^cite_note-Pom_2012-20] producing reinforcing effects such as [euphoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria), increased [alertness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alertness), [concentration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attentional_control), [libido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphrodisiac), and reduced fatigue and [appetite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anorectic).[^cite_note-Roque_2022-28]

Cocaine has numerous [adverse effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adverse_effect). Acute use can cause [vasoconstriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasoconstriction), [tachycardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachycardia), [hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension), [hyperthermia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperthermia), or seizures, while overdose may lead to [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke), [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction), or [sudden cardiac death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudden_cardiac_death).[^cite_note-Pom_2012-20][^cite_note-Zimmerman_2012-15][^cite_note-Sordo_2014-29] It also produces a spectrum of [psychiatric symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatric_symptoms), including agitation, [paranoia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranoia), anxiety, irritability, [psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulant_psychosis), hallucinations, delusions, violence, and [suicidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suicidal_ideation) or homicidal thinking.[^cite_note-Morton_1999-30][^cite_note-Pom_2012-20] [Prenatal exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_cocaine_exposure) poses risks to fetal development.[^cite_note-Buckingham-Howes_2013-31][^cite_note-Lambert_2012-32][^cite_note-McCarthy_2014-33][^cite_note-Eiden_2009-34] Chronic use may result in [cocaine dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_dependence), withdrawal symptoms, [neurotoxicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotoxicity), and [nasal damage](#Cocaine_nose), including [cocaine-induced midline destructive lesions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine-induced_midline_destructive_lesions).[^cite_note-Gawin_1989-35][^cite_note-Sofuoglu_2005-36][^cite_note-Clare_2024-37][^cite_note-Berberi_2024-38][^cite_note-Nitro_2022-39][^cite_note-Di_Cosola_2021-40]

Coca cultivation and initial processing occur primarily in [Latin America](18524.md), especially in the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) regions of Bolivia, Peru, and Colombia. Cultivation is expanding into [Central America](6121.md), including Honduras, Guatemala, and Belize.[^cite_note-Andean_full_report.pdf-21][^cite_note-Murillo-Sandoval_2024-41][^cite_note-plantaciones-laboratorios-y-narcorutas-de-coca-en-honduras-LJ17693091-42][^cite_note-uatemala-registra-record-produccion-coca-no-cocaina-43][^cite_note-police-find-half-a-million-coca-plants-in-southern-belize-44] Violence linked to the cocaine trade continues to affect [Latin America and the Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_America_and_the_Caribbean) and is expanding into [Western Europe](33800.md), [Asia](689.md), and [Africa](5334607.md) as [transnational organized crime](5841680.md) groups compete globally.[^cite_note-WDR25_Special_points_of_interest.pdf-45][^cite_note-ow-the-drug-wars-impact-latin-america-and-the-caribbean-development-46] Cocaine remains the world's fastest-growing [illicit drug market](218449.md).[^cite_note-Press_release_WDR_2025_English.pdf-47][^cite_note-48]

Coca chewing dates back at least 8,000 years in South America.[^cite_note-49] Large-scale cultivation occurred in Taiwan and [Java](69336.md) prior to [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-Lu_2024-50][^cite_note-Musto_1998-51] The [cocaine boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_boom)—a sharp rise in illegal production and trade—began in the late 1970s and peaked in the 1980s.[^cite_note-52] Cocaine is regulated under [international drug control conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_drug_control_conventions), though its [legal status varies nationally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_status_of_cocaine): several countries have decriminalized possession of small quantities.[^cite_note-Housego_2004-53][^cite_note-Murphy_2004-54][^cite_note-independent-55][^cite_note-Greenwald_2009-56]

## Uses

Coca leaves have been used by [Andean civilizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_civilizations) since [ancient times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_civilizations).[^cite_note-Goldstein_2009-57] In ancient [Wari culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wari_culture),[^cite_note-Valdez_2015-58] [Inca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_people) culture, and through modern successor [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples) cultures of the [Andes Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes_Mountains), coca leaves are chewed, taken orally in the form of a [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_tea), or alternatively, prepared in a sachet wrapped around alkaline burnt ashes, and held in the [mouth against the inner cheek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buccal_administration); it has traditionally been used as an [anorectic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anorectic) and to combat the effects of cold and [altitude sickness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altitude_sickness),[^cite_note-Martin_1970-59][^cite_note-Plant_2008-60] although its actual effectiveness has never been systematically studied.[^cite_note-Luks_2014-61]

Globally, in 2019, cocaine was used by an estimated 20 million people (0.4% of adults aged 15 to 64 years). The highest prevalence of cocaine use was in [Australia](4689264.md) and [New Zealand](4913064.md) (2.1%), followed by [North America](21139.md) (2.1%), [Western](33800.md) and [Central Europe](5188.md) (1.4%), and [South](26769.md) and [Central America](6121.md) (1.0%).[^cite_note-WDR2021-62] Since 1961, the [Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_Convention_on_Narcotic_Drugs) has required countries to make recreational use of cocaine a [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime).[^cite_note-63] In the United States, cocaine is regulated as a Schedule II drug under the [Controlled Substances Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_Substances_Act), meaning that it has a high potential for abuse but has an accepted medical use.[^cite_note-64] While rarely used medically anymore, its accepted uses include serving as a topical local anesthetic for the upper [respiratory tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_tract) and as an [antihemorrhagic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antihemorrhagic) agent to stop bleeding in the mouth, throat, and nasal cavities.[^cite_note-65]

### Traditional medicine

#### Coca leaves

Main article: [Coca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca)



*Llipta* is used to improve extraction when chewing coca (Museo de la Coca, [Cusco](72735.md), Peru).

It is legal to use [coca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca) leaves in the [Andean Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_Community), such as [Peru](170691.md) and [Bolivia](3462.md), and Argentina, where they are chewed, consumed as tea, or sometimes incorporated into food products.[^cite_note-66] Coca leaves are typically mixed with an alkaline substance (such as [slaked lime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_hydroxide)) and chewed into a wad that is retained in the [buccal pouch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buccal_pouch) (mouth between gum and cheek, much the same as [chewing tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chewing_tobacco) is chewed) and sucked of its juices. The juices are absorbed slowly by the [mucous membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mucous_membrane) of the inner cheek and by the [gastrointestinal tract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastrointestinal_tract) when swallowed.

##### Coca tea

Main article: [Coca tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_tea)



Two cups of coca tea

Coca herbal [infusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infusion) (also referred to as [coca tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_tea)) is used in coca-leaf producing countries much as any herbal medicinal infusion would elsewhere in the world. The free and legal commercialization of dried coca leaves under the form of filtration bags to be used as "coca tea" has been actively promoted by the governments of [Peru](170691.md) and [Bolivia](3462.md) for many years as a drink having [medicinal powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_medicine). In Peru, the [National Coca Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coca_Company), a state-run corporation, sells cocaine-infused teas and other medicinal products and also exports leaves to the U.S. for medicinal use.[^cite_note-67] The effects of drinking coca tea are mild stimulation and mood lift.[^cite_note-68]

In 1986 an article in the *[Journal of the American Medical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journal_of_the_American_Medical_Association)* revealed that U.S. [health food stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_food_store) were selling dried coca leaves to be prepared as an infusion as "Health Inca Tea". While the packaging claimed it had been "decocainized", no such process had actually taken place. The article stated that drinking two cups of the tea per day gave a mild [stimulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulation), increased [heart rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_rate), and [mood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emotional_mood) elevation, and the tea was essentially harmless.[^cite_note-Siegel_1986-69]

##### Ypadu

Main article: [Ypadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ypadu)



Mambe or ypadu is made from toasted and ground coca leaves with ashes

[Ypadú](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ypadu) or ypadu (also known as mambé) is an unrefined, unconcentrated [powder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powder_\(substance\)) made from toasted [coca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca) leaves and the ash of various other plants. It is traditionally prepared and consumed by indigenous tribes in the Northwest Amazon.[^cite_note-70] Like coca teas consumed in [Peru](170691.md) to adapt to sickness induced by high elevation, it has a long [ethnobotanical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnobotanical) history and cultural associations.

### Medical

[Karl Koller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Koller_\(ophthalmologist\))'s groundbreaking discovery of cocaine as a local anesthetic is regarded as the second most significant advance in the history of anesthesia. Although cocaine was once widely preferred for topical anesthesia, the search for replacement agents intensified due to rising costs, strict regulations, and its habit-forming potential.[^cite_note-Armbuster_2021-25] Cocaine is not included on the [WHO Model List of Essential Medicines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Model_List_of_Essential_Medicines); the list formally excludes "cocaine and its combinations" as therapeutic alternatives to [ophthalmological preparations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHO_Model_List_of_Essential_Medicines#Ophthalmological_preparations).[^cite_note-WHO-MHP-HPS-EML-2023.02-71]

The US [Drug Enforcement Administration](146720.md) (DEA) classifies cocaine as a [Schedule II drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_Substances_Act#Schedule_II), recognizing its high potential for abuse but still permitting its limited use for medical purposes. However, current [pharmacoepidemiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacoepidemiology) trends suggest that cocaine may soon reach the point where, in practical terms, it is no longer used medically in health care as a Schedule II substance. As physician boards—but not pharmacy boards—continue to assess knowledge of licit cocaine, attention may shift toward drugs with more contemporary medical use.[^cite_note-Armbuster_2021-25]

Cocaine is rarely prescribed in modern medicine due to its high potential for abuse and significant risk of adverse effects; its use is almost exclusively limited to [health facilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_facility) for specific diagnostic procedures or surgeries.

#### Topical

Cocaine is used in medical practice as a [topical medication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topical_medication).[^cite_note-Armbuster_2021-25] Because it is not absorbed into the bloodstream in significant amounts when used this way, topical application does not produce the psychoactive effects associated with recreational cocaine use.

##### Topical anesthetic

See also: [Moffett's solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moffett's_solution)



Cocaine [hydrochloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrochloride)

Cocaine is sometimes used in [otorhinolaryngology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otorhinolaryngology) as a [topical anesthetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topical_anesthetic) and [vasoconstrictor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasoconstrictor) to help control pain and bleeding during surgery of the nose, mouth, throat, or [lacrimal duct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrimal_duct). It is also used for topical airway anaesthesia for procedures such as awake fibreoptic [bronchoscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchoscopy) or [intubation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intubation). Although some absorption and systemic effects may occur, the use of cocaine as a topical anesthetic and vasoconstrictor is generally safe, rarely causing [cardiovascular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular) toxicity, [glaucoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaucoma), and [pupil dilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mydriasis).[^cite_note-Dwyer_2016-72][^cite_note-Latorre_1999-23] Occasionally, cocaine is mixed with [adrenaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenaline) and [sodium bicarbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_bicarbonate) and used topically for surgery, a formulation called [Moffett's solution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moffett's_solution).[^cite_note-73] It is occasionally used in surgeries involving the [pharynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharynx) or [nasopharynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasopharynx) to reduce pain, bleeding, and [vocal cord spasm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laryngospasm).[^cite_note-74]

Nasal solution cocaine hydrochloride (**Goprelto**), an ester used for intranasal application, was approved for medical use in the United States in December 2017, and is indicated for the introduction of topical anesthesia of the mucous membranes for diagnostic procedures and surgeries on or through the nasal cavities of adults.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-Goprelto_FDA_label-2] Cocaine hydrochloride (**Numbrino**) was approved for medical use in the United States in January 2020.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-Numbrino_FDA_label-3] Headache and [epistaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epistaxis) are the most frequently reported adverse reactions with Goprelto,[^cite_note-Goprelto_FDA_label-2] while hypertension and tachycardia-including [sinus tachycardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinus_tachycardia)-are most common with Numbrino.[^cite_note-Numbrino_FDA_label-3]

##### Ophthalmological use

Cocaine [eye drops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_drop) have traditionally been used by [neurologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurology) when examining people suspected of having [Horner syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horner's_syndrome). In Horner syndrome, [sympathetic innervation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympathetic_innervation) to the eye is blocked. In a healthy eye, cocaine stimulates the [sympathetic nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympathetic_nervous_system) (SNS) by inhibiting norepinephrine reuptake, causing the [pupil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pupil) to dilate. In patients with Horner syndrome, sympathetic innervation to the eye is disrupted, so the affected pupil does not dilate in response to cocaine and remains constricted, or dilates to a lesser extent than the unaffected eye, which also receives the eye drop test. If both eyes dilate equally, the patient does not have Horner syndrome.[^cite_note-77]

However, [apraclonidine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apraclonidine) has largely replaced cocaine as the first-line pharmacologic agent for the diagnosis of Horner syndrome in routine clinical practice.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-Bremner_2019-24]

### Recreational

For Cocaine or crack cocaine combined with other drugs, see [List of polysubstance combinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_polysubstance_combinations).



Peruvian flake cocaine on a metal milligram [scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weighing_scale) tray

Recreational cocaine is typically not taken by mouth due to its poor bioavailability, instead it is usually [snorted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_administration) or [injected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Injection_\(medicine\)). Cocaine hydrochloride can also be chemically converted into its [free base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_base) form, [crack cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine), which can be vaporized.

Cocaine is a [central nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system) [stimulant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulant).[^cite_note-WHO2004-80] Its effects can last from 15 minutes to an hour. The duration of cocaine's effects depends on the amount taken and the route of administration.[^cite_note-WHO2007-81] Cocaine can be in the form of fine white powder and has a bitter taste. [Crack cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine) is a smokeable form of cocaine made into small "rocks" by processing cocaine with [sodium bicarbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_bicarbonate) (baking soda) and water.[^cite_note-Zimmerman_2012-15][^cite_note-Sordo_2014-29]

Cocaine use leads to increases in alertness, feelings of well-being and [euphoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria), increased energy and [motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_control) activity, and increased feelings of competence and [sexuality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sexuality).[^cite_note-Donroe_2017-82]

Analysis of the [correlation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation) between the use of 18 various [psychoactive substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychoactive_substance) shows that cocaine use correlates with the use of other "[party drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_drug)" (e.g., [MDMA](10024.md), [amphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphetamine)), as well as with [heroin](14034.md) and [benzodiazepines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzodiazepine) use, and can be considered as a bridge between the use of different groups of drugs.[^cite_note-83]

#### Insufflation



Lines of cocaine prepared for snorting. [Contaminated currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contaminated_currency) such as banknotes might serve as a [fomite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fomite) of diseases like hepatitis C

[^cite_note-LV-84]

Nasal [insufflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insufflation_\(medicine\)) (known colloquially as "snorting", "sniffing", or "blowing") is a common method of ingestion of recreational powdered cocaine.[^cite_note-85] The drug coats and is absorbed through the mucous membranes lining the [nasal passages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_passages). Cocaine's desired euphoric effects are delayed when snorted through the nose by about five minutes. This occurs because cocaine's absorption is slowed by its constricting effect on the blood vessels of the nose.[^cite_note-Zimmerman_2012-15] Insufflation of cocaine also leads to the longest duration of its effects (60–90 minutes).[^cite_note-Zimmerman_2012-15] When insufflating cocaine, absorption through the nasal membranes is approximately 30–60%[^cite_note-86]

Most [banknotes have traces of cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contaminated_currency) on them; this has been confirmed by studies done in several countries.[^cite_note-87] In 1994, the [US 9th Circuit Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Ninth_Circuit) cited findings that in Los Angeles, three out of four banknotes were tainted by cocaine or another illicit drug.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

[Snuff spoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snuff_spoon), hollowed-out [pens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen), cut [straws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_straw), pointed ends of keys,[^cite_note-90] long [fingernails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingernails) or [artificial nails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_nails), and [tampon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampon) applicators are also used to insufflate cocaine. The cocaine typically is poured onto a flat, hard surface and divided into "bumps", "lines", or "rails", and then insufflated.[^cite_note-91] A 2001 study reported that the sharing of straws used to "snort" cocaine can spread blood diseases such as [hepatitis C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_C).[^cite_note-92]

##### Cocaine spoon



Chinese snuff bottle stopper with a spoon

Historically, [snuff spoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snuff_spoon) were used for cocaine in the 20th century, hence the names "cocaine spoon" and "coke spoon". Some local statutes in the US treat spoons that are too small and thus "unsuited for the typical, lawful uses of a spoon" as [drug paraphernalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_paraphernalia).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

#### Injection

Subjective effects not commonly shared with other methods of administration include a ringing in the ears moments after injection (usually when over 120 milligrams) lasting 2 to 5 minutes including [tinnitus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinnitus) and audio distortion. This is colloquially referred to as a "bell ringer". In a study of cocaine users, the average time taken to reach peak subjective effects was 3.1 minutes.[^cite_note-Volkow_2000-96] The euphoria passes quickly. Aside from the toxic effects of cocaine, there is also the danger of circulatory [emboli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embolism) from the insoluble substances that may be used to cut the drug. As with all injected [illicit substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illicit_substances), there is a risk of the user contracting [blood-borne infections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood-borne_disease) if sterile injecting equipment is not available or used.

#### Inhalation

##### Cocaine paste

Main article: [Cocaine paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_paste)

[Coca paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_paste) (paco, basuco, oxi, pasta) is a crude [extract](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extract) of the [coca leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_leaf) which contains 40% to 91% [cocaine freebase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_freebase) along with companion coca alkaloids and varying quantities of [benzoic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzoic_acid), [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol), and [kerosene](16992.md). The caustic reactions associated with the local application of coca paste prevents its use by oral, intranasal, mucosal, intramuscular, intravenous or subcutaneous routes. Coca paste can only be smoked when combined with a combustible material such as tobacco or cannabis.[^cite_note-Arif_1987-97]

Crude cocaine preparation intermediates are marketed as cheaper alternatives to pure cocaine to local markets while the more expensive end product is exported to United States and European markets. [Freebase cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freebase_cocaine) paste preparations can be smoked. The psychological and physiological effects of the *paco* are quite severe.[^cite_note-Jeri_1984-98][^cite_note-Phillips_2009-99] Media usually report that it is extremely toxic and addictive.[^cite_note-Mcdonnell_2007-100][^cite_note-Legrand_2009-101][^cite_note-Navai_2008-102] According to a study by Intercambios, media appear to exaggerate the effects of *paco*. These stereotypes create a sense that nothing can be done to help a *paco* addict and thus stand in the way of rehabilitation programs.[^cite_note-103]

##### Crack cocaine

Further information: [Crack cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine)



Man smoking crack cocaine in Bogotá, Colombia

Powder cocaine (cocaine hydrochloride) must be heated to a high temperature to be smoked (about 197 °C), and considerable decomposition/burning occurs at these high temperatures. This effectively destroys some of the cocaine and yields a sharp, acrid, and foul-tasting smoke. Cocaine base/crack can be smoked because it vaporizes with little or no decomposition at 98 °C (208 °F).[^cite_note-104]

## Contraindications

For harm caused by the freebase form, see [Crack cocaine § Contraindications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine#Contraindications).

Cocaine should not be used in individuals with a known allergy or [hypersensitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersensitivity) to the drug or any components of its topical formulation. It is also contraindicated in [elderly patients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geriatrics) and those with a history of hypertension or [cardiovascular disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiovascular_disease).[^cite_note-Richards_2023-105]

### Pregnancy

Main articles: [Prenatal cocaine exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_cocaine_exposure) and [Teratology § Cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teratology#Cocaine)

[Prenatal cocaine exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prenatal_cocaine_exposure) (PCE) may occur when a pregnant woman uses cocaine.[^cite_note-Buckingham-Howes_2013-31][^cite_note-Lambert_2012-32][^cite_note-McCarthy_2014-33][^cite_note-Eiden_2009-34]

Under the former FDA [pregnancy category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pregnancy_category) system, cocaine was classified as a Category C drug. Its potential to cause harm to the fetus is not fully known, so it should only be administered to pregnant women if clearly necessary.[^cite_note-Richards_2023-105]

Cocaine can act as a [teratogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teratogen), having various effects on the developing fetus.[^cite_note-ASU-106] Some common teratogenic defects caused by cocaine include [hydronephrosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydronephrosis), [cleft palate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleft_lip_and_cleft_palate), [polydactyly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polydactyly), and [down syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_syndrome).[^cite_note-ASU-106] Cocaine as a drug has a low molecular weight and high water and lipid solubility which enables it to cross the [placenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta) and fetal blood-brain barrier.[^cite_note-107] Because cocaine is able to pass through the placenta and enter the fetus, the fetus' circulation can be negatively affected. With restriction of fetal circulation, the development of organs in the fetus can be impacted, even resulting in [intestines developing outside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastroschisis) of the fetus' body.[^cite_note-ASU-106] Cocaine use during pregnancy can also result in [obstetric labor complications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetric_labor_complication) such as, [placental abruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placental_abruption),[^cite_note-108] [preterm birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preterm_birth) or delivery, [uterine rupture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterine_rupture), [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage), and [stillbirth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stillbirth).[^cite_note-ASU-106][^cite_note-109] Prenatal cocaine exposure may cause subtle cognitive deficits and lower the chance of above-average IQ by age 4, but supportive caregiving can significantly improve outcomes.[^cite_note-110]

### Breastfeeding

Mothers utilizing recreational drugs, such as cocaine, methamphetamines, PCP, and heroin, should not [breastfeed](19347033.md).[^cite_note-CDC-2021e-111][^cite_note-Eglash_2020-112]<sup>: 13 </sup>

The [March of Dimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_of_Dimes) said "it is likely that cocaine will reach the baby through breast milk," and advises the following regarding cocaine use during pregnancy:

> Cocaine use during pregnancy can affect a pregnant woman and her unborn baby in many ways. During the early months of pregnancy, it may increase the risk of [miscarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscarriage). Later in pregnancy, it can trigger [preterm labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preterm_birth) (labor that occurs before 37 weeks of pregnancy) or cause the baby to grow poorly. As a result, cocaine-exposed babies are more likely than unexposed babies to be born with [low birth weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_birth_weight) (less than 5.5 lb or 2.5 kg). Low-birthweight babies are 20 times more likely to die in their first month of life than normal-weight babies, and face an increased risk of [lifelong disabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developmental_disability) such as mental retardation and [cerebral palsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_palsy). Cocaine-exposed babies also tend to have smaller heads, which generally reflect smaller brains. Some studies suggest that cocaine-exposed babies are at increased risk of birth defects, including urinary tract defects and, possibly, heart defects. Cocaine also may cause an unborn baby to have a [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke), irreversible [brain injury](304588.md), or a [myocardial infarction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction).[^cite_note-mod-113]

## Side effects

For harm caused by the freebase form, see [Crack cocaine § Adverse effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine#Adverse_effects).

See also: [Cocaine § Lacing](#Lacing)

### Heart

Cocaine use can cause serious heart problems like sudden death, heart inflammation, [arrhythmias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrhythmia), and heart attacks. It triggers coronary artery spasms, increases blood clot risk, and accelerates [atherosclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atherosclerosis), especially with long-term use. The severity of heart disease often relates to how long and how often cocaine is used.[^cite_note-114] It can also become a serious risk at high doses due to cocaine's blocking effect on cardiac sodium channels.[^cite_note-O'Leary_2010-115]

### Mortality



Death rates cocaine

Persons with regular or problematic use of cocaine have a significantly higher [mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_rate), and are specifically at higher risk of traumatic deaths and deaths attributable to infectious disease.[^cite_note-116] In 2025, the Liberty House Clinic in the United Kingdom noted that chronic cocaine usage in fact had a higher risk of death than [alcoholism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholism).[^cite_note-117]

### Neurotoxicity

Cocaine is considered [neurotoxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotoxic) due to its damaging effects on the brain and nervous system.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119][^cite_note-Clare_2024-37][^cite_note-Nicolucci_2020-120][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-Little_2009-122] Research has shown that both acute and chronic cocaine use can lead to significant reductions in cerebral blood flow, disrupt neurovascular interactions, and impair brain function. These changes are associated with [nerve injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve_injury), cognitive deficits, and an increased risk of cerebrovascular accidents such as strokes. Brain imaging studies consistently report that individuals who misuse cocaine exhibit structural and functional abnormalities compared to non-users, supporting the classification of cocaine as a neurotoxic substance.[^cite_note-Clare_2024-37]

Cocaine use damages [gray matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_matter) in brain regions critical for memory, attention, and emotion, leading to cognitive and behavioral impairments. It also disrupts dopamine levels and blood flow, accelerating [brain aging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aging_brain) and causing long-term neurological harm.[^cite_note-123]

### Psychiatric symptoms

Cocaine produces a spectrum of [psychiatric symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychiatric_symptoms) including agitation, paranoia, anxiety, irritability, [psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosis), hallucinations, delusions, violence, as well as suicidal and homicidal thinking.[^cite_note-Morton_1999-30][^cite_note-Pom_2012-20]

A substantial proportion of cocaine addicts exhibit [hypomanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypomanic) personality traits that are [ego-syntonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ego-syntonic) with their pattern of cocaine abuse.[^cite_note-124]

[Cocaine intoxication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_intoxication) mirrors core traits of [narcissism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcissism)—both involve a dopamine-driven, compulsive drive for reward. Just as cocaine produces a brief high that temporarily enhances the sense of worth, narcissists rely on external [admiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiration) to feed an addiction to their [self-esteem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-esteem), resulting in a [self-reinforcing feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_feedback) cycle.[^cite_note-125]

The misuse of cocaine has a high correlation with [suicide](16831059.md).[^cite_note-Vijayakumar_2011-126][^cite_note-127] In those who use cocaine, the risk is greatest during the [withdrawal phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_withdrawal).[^cite_note-128] Cocaine use has been linked to homicide, with up to 31% of homicide victims testing positive for the drug.[^cite_note-Morton_1999-30] In 1989 Fulton County, 40% of [homicide](123451.md) victims had cocaine metabolites, especially Black and firearm victims.[^cite_note-129]

A 2020 study found that men with cocaine use disorder have greater difficulty identifying [emotional expression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emotional_expression) in female faces, affecting relationships and suggesting a target for intervention.[^cite_note-130] A 2021 study found that cocaine use disorder impairs emotion recognition, especially for happiness and fear, with improvement after long-term abstinence.[^cite_note-131]

[Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(mood\)) is modestly linked to current drug use in cocaine users but does not clearly predict treatment participation or future use.[^cite_note-132] For people who use cocaine, stress and craving can make each other worse. This may help explain why stress can lead to [relapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relapse) in people trying to stop using cocaine.[^cite_note-133]

#### Psychosis

Cocaine has a similar potential to induce temporary psychosis[^cite_note-Brady_1991-134] with more than half of cocaine abusers reporting at least some psychotic symptoms at some point.[^cite_note-Thirthalli_2006-135] Typical symptoms include paranoid delusions that they are being followed and that their drug use is being watched, accompanied by hallucinations that support the delusional beliefs.[^cite_note-Thirthalli_2006-135] [Delusional parasitosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delusional_parasitosis) with [formication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formication) ('cocaine bugs') is also a fairly common symptom.[^cite_note-Elliott_2012-136]

Cocaine-induced psychosis shows [sensitization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_sensitization) toward the psychotic effects of the drug. This means that psychosis becomes more severe with repeated intermittent use.[^cite_note-Thirthalli_2006-135][^cite_note-137]

### Short-term effects

Insufflating (snorting) cocaine commonly causes increased [mucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mucus) production due to irritation and inflammation of the nasal passages. This irritation leads to symptoms such as a [runny nose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinorrhea), [nasal congestion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_congestion), and excessive or thickened mucus.

Acute exposure to cocaine has many effects on humans, including euphoria, increases in heart rate and blood pressure, and increases in cortisol secretion from the adrenal gland.[^cite_note-138] In humans with acute exposure followed by continuous exposure to cocaine at a constant blood concentration, the acute tolerance to the [chronotropic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronotropic) cardiac effects of cocaine begins after about 10 minutes, while acute tolerance to the euphoric effects of cocaine begins after about one hour.[^cite_note-Ambre_1988-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142] With excessive or prolonged use, the drug can cause [itching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itch), [fast heart rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachycardia), and [paranoid delusions or sensations of insects crawling on the skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formication).[^cite_note-Weizhao_2008-143] Cocaine can induce [psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosis) characterized by [paranoia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranoia), impaired [reality testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reality_testing), [hallucinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucinations), irritability, and physical aggression. [Cocaine intoxication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_intoxication) can cause hyperawareness, [hypervigilance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypervigilance), [psychomotor agitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychomotor_agitation), and [delirium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delirium). Consumption of large doses of cocaine can cause violent outbursts, especially by those with preexisting psychosis.[^cite_note-144] Acute exposure may induce [arrhythmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrhythmia), including [atrial fibrillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atrial_fibrillation), [supraventricular tachycardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supraventricular_tachycardia), [ventricular tachycardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventricular_tachycardia), and [ventricular fibrillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventricular_fibrillation). Acute exposure may also lead to [angina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angina), [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction), and [congestive heart failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congestive_heart_failure).[^cite_note-145] Cocaine overdose may cause [seizures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seizures), [abnormally high body temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperthermia) and a marked elevation of blood pressure, which can be life-threatening,[^cite_note-Weizhao_2008-143] [abnormal heart rhythms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_arrhythmia),[^cite_note-O'Leary_2010-115] and death.[^cite_note-O'Leary_2010-115] Anxiety, paranoia, and restlessness can also occur, especially during the [comedown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedown_\(drugs\)). With excessive dosage, [tremors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremor), [convulsions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convulsion), and [increased body temperature](146879.md) are observed.[^cite_note-WHO2004-80]

### Long-term effects



Side effects of chronic cocaine use

Cocaine is highly addictive and has poor bioavailability when taken orally. Individuals often engage in repeated use by either insufflating it [intranasally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intranasally) or converting it to [crack cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine) for vaporization. Cocaine's effects last longest when insufflated (60–90 minutes),[^cite_note-Zimmerman_2012-15] but the drug itself has a short [biological half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_half-life) of about 0.7–1.5 hours.[^cite_note-Treadwell_2007-146] Repeated use raises the risk of developing "[cocaine nose](#Cocaine_nose)," referring to severe nasal tissue damage from intranasal use, as well as "[crack lung](#Crack_lung)," a condition involving lung tissue damage caused by inhaling crack cocaine.

Cocaine use leads to an increased risk of hemorrhagic and ischemic [strokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke).[^cite_note-Sordo_2014-29] Cocaine use also increases the risk of having a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction).[^cite_note-Havakuk_2017-147]

Cocaine use also promotes the [formation of blood clots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrombosis).[^cite_note-Zimmerman_2012-15] This increase in blood clot formation is attributed to cocaine-associated increases in the activity of [plasminogen activator inhibitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasminogen_activator_inhibitor-1), and an increase in the number, activation, and aggregation of [platelets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platelet).[^cite_note-Zimmerman_2012-15]

Cocaine [constricts blood vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasoconstriction), [dilates pupils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mydriasis), and increases body temperature, heart rate, and blood pressure. It can also cause headaches and gastrointestinal complications such as abdominal pain and nausea. Chronic users may lose their [appetite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appetite) and experience severe [malnutrition](258979.md), leading to being [underweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underweight).

A 2014 study found that increased cocaine use is linked to greater [cognitive impairment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_impairment), particularly in working memory, while reduced or ceased use can lead to partial or full recovery of cognitive function. These findings suggest that some cocaine-related cognitive deficits are reversible, especially if use begins later in life.[^cite_note-148] A 2018 review found little evidence that chronic cocaine use causes widespread cognitive impairment.[^cite_note-149] Exposure to cocaine may lead to the breakdown of the [blood–brain barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood%E2%80%93brain_barrier).[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151]

Cocaine use is frequently associated with involuntary tooth grinding, known as [bruxism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruxism), which can cause [dental attrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dental_attrition) and [gingivitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gingivitis).[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] Additionally, stimulants like cocaine, [methamphetamine](11038318.md), and even [caffeine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caffeine) cause dehydration and [dry mouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerostomia).

#### Addiction

See also: [Epigenetics of cocaine addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics_of_cocaine_addiction)

For harm caused by the freebase form, see [Crack cocaine § Addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine#Addiction).

Cocaine can induce [tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_tolerance) after a single dose, and repeated use frequently leads to the development of [addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addiction) and prolonged [craving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_dependence).[^cite_note-Ambre_1988-139][^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] Assessment tools like the Obsessive Compulsive Cocaine Use Scale (OCCUS) may be employed to quantify obsessive and compulsive thoughts related to cocaine consumption.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157]

Withdrawal symptoms include disrupted sleep, [irritability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irritability), [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(mood\)), and reduced ability to experience pleasure ([anhedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhedonia)).[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-Pom_2012-20] Chronic nasal use may cause destructive damage to the nasal septum, including [cocaine-induced midline destructive lesions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine-induced_midline_destructive_lesions) (CIMDL). Illicit cocaine is frequently adulterated with substances such as [fentanyl](141915.md), [levamisole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levamisole), or [local anesthetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_anesthetics), increasing its toxicity.[^cite_note-Goldstein_2009-57][^cite_note-159] Concurrent use with [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(drug\)) produces [cocaethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaethylene), a metabolite that significantly increases the risk of sudden death. According to the [Global Burden of Disease Study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Burden_of_Disease_Study), cocaine use is responsible for approximately 7,300 deaths annually.[^cite_note-160]

Cocaine abuse can trigger [addiction-related structural neuroplasticity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addiction-related_structural_neuroplasticity) in the human brain, although the permanence of such changes remains uncertain.[^cite_note-Hamp_2019-161] [Family history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genealogy) is a known risk factor, as relatives of cocaine users have an increased likelihood of developing [cocaine addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_addiction).[^cite_note-162]

A key mechanism involves the overexpression of [ΔFosB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%94FosB) in the [nucleus accumbens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleus_accumbens), altering [transcriptional regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcriptional_regulation) and reinforcing drug-seeking behavior.[^cite_note-Hope_1998-163] Each dose of cocaine raises ΔFosB levels without a known saturation point. This elevation leads to increased [brain-derived neurotrophic factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain-derived_neurotrophic_factor) ([BDNF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BDNF)) levels, which in turn enhance [dendritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrite) branching and [spine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendritic_spine) density in neurons of the nucleus accumbens and [prefrontal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefrontal_cortex), potentially persisting for weeks after drug cessation. In [transgenic mice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetically_modified_mouse) engineered to express ΔFosB in the nucleus accumbens and [dorsal striatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorsal_striatum), heightened behavioral sensitization to cocaine has been observed.[^cite_note-Kelz_1999-164] These mice self-administer cocaine at lower doses and display a greater propensity for [relapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relapse) after withdrawal[^cite_note-Colby_2003-165][^cite_note-Nestler_2011-166] ΔFosB also enhances sensitivity to reward by upregulating the [AMPA receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMPA_receptor) subunit GluR2[^cite_note-Kelz_1999-164] and downregulating the expression of [dynorphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynorphin).[^cite_note-Nestler_2011-166]

Cocaine use has also been shown to increase [DNA damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_damage_\(naturally_occurring\)) in the brains of rodents.[^cite_note-de_Souza_2014-167][^cite_note-Alvarenga_2010-168] During subsequent [DNA repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_repair), enduring alterations in [chromatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatin) structure may arise, such as [DNA methylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_methylation) and [methylation or acetylation of histones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histone_methylation) at the repair loci.[^cite_note-Dabin_2016-169] These modifications may result in lasting [epigenetic "scars"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics), which are believed to contribute to the persistent [epigenetic changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics_of_cocaine_addiction) observed in cocaine addiction.

#### Dependence and withdrawal

Further information: [Cocaine dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_dependence)

[Cocaine dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_dependence) develops after even brief periods of regular cocaine use.[^cite_note-Gawin_1989-35]

About 25% of adults with [attention deficit hyperactivity disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention_deficit_hyperactivity_disorder) (ADHD) use cocaine, and 10% develop a cocaine use disorder during their lifetime. Because cocaine use can worsen health outcomes, adults with ADHD should be screened for cocaine use disorder and referred for treatment if needed.[^cite_note-170]

Cocaine-dependent patients with high [neuroticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroticism) scores are more likely to experience cocaine-induced psychotic symptoms, regardless of other drug use factors, making [personality assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personality_test) important for risk identification and patient warning.[^cite_note-171]

Cocaine [withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_withdrawal) symptoms group into two types: depressive (e.g., [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(mood\)), craving, insomnia) and somatic (e.g., increased appetite, fatigue). Depressive symptoms are linked to worse outcomes like longer depression, treatment, and risky behaviors.[^cite_note-Sofuoglu_2005-36]

##### Treatment

Because there are no medications with an approved [indication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indication_\(medicine\)) for cocaine use disorder, psychosocial treatments are the current standard. Effective approaches include group and individual counseling, [cognitive behavioral therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_behavioral_therapy) (CBT), and [motivational interviewing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motivational_interviewing) (MI). [Contingency management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contingency_management) (CM)—which rewards patients with vouchers for meeting treatment goals—has proven especially effective, particularly for helping patients achieve initial [abstinence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abstinence) from cocaine.[^cite_note-Kampman_2019-172]



Cocaine Anonymous [sobriety coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobriety_coin)

[Cocaine Anonymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_Anonymous) (CA) is a [twelve-step program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-step_program) formed in 18 November 1982 for people who seek recovery from drug [addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_dependence). It is patterned very closely after [Alcoholics Anonymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholics_Anonymous) (AA), although the two groups are unaffiliated. While many CA members have been addicted to cocaine, crack, speed or similar substances, CA accepts all who desire freedom from "cocaine and all other mind-altering substances" as members.[^cite_note-CAMIND2007-173]

Numerous medications have been investigated for use in cocaine dependence, but as of 2015, none of them were considered to be effective.[^cite_note-Minozzi2015-174] Drugs which help to re-stabilize the glutamate system such as [*N*-acetylcysteine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-acetylcysteine) have been proposed for the treatment of addiction to cocaine, [nicotine](38272.md), and [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(drug\)).[^cite_note-McClure_2014-175] However, none have sufficient evidence or regulatory approval for routine clinical use, so psychosocial interventions remain the mainstay of treatment.[^cite_note-Kampman_2019-172]

##### Cocaine-induced midline destructive lesions

[Cocaine-induced midline destructive lesions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine-induced_midline_destructive_lesions) (CIMDL)[^cite_note-Nitro_2022-39] is the progressive destruction of nasal architecture with the erosion of the [palate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palate), [nasal conchae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_Concha), and [ethmoid sinuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethmoid_sinus) associated with prolonged [insufflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insufflation_\(medicine\)), colloquially 'snorting', of cocaine.[^cite_note-Di_Cosola_2021-40]

Chronic intranasal usage can degrade the [cartilage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartilage) separating the [nostrils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nostrils) (the [septum nasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septum_nasi)), leading eventually to its complete disappearance.[^cite_note-Pagliaro_2004-185]

##### Causes

The cause of "cocaine nose" can be traced to the chemical process that occurs when cocaine hydrochloride is insufflated (snorted). As cocaine is absorbed through the [nasal mucosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_mucosa), the remaining hydrochloride component forms a dilute [hydrochloric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrochloric_acid).[^cite_note-Pagliaro_2004-185] The short half-life of cocaine,[^cite_note-Treadwell_2007-146] combined with binge use, may expose the nasal tissues to this acidic environment more frequently, increasing the risk of irritation and damage.

##### Treatment

For people with cocaine abuse, mild symptoms may resolve completely with total abstinence from cocaine, making early involvement of addiction services essential.[^cite_note-186]

Repair may involve [rhinoplasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhinoplasty), which includes creating a new internal lining with nasolabial flaps and restoring support with costal cartilage grafts.[^cite_note-187]

In 2024, Belgian doctors report a rise in patients needing nose reconstruction due to cocaine use, which damages nasal tissue and cartilage; however, few undergo surgery because it requires at least six months of abstinence from cocaine for proper healing.[^cite_note-188]

Some individuals seek [plastic surgery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_surgery) to repair or reconstruct nasal damage caused by cocaine use, although surgical outcomes can be complicated by ongoing tissue loss and poor healing. When nasal damage is too severe for reconstruction, a [nose prosthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nose_prosthesis) may be used to restore appearance and [quality of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life).[^cite_note-fraser-nose-the-irreparable-damage-cocaine-use-can-do-180][^cite_note-BBC_2023-181]

## Overdose

See also: [Cocaine intoxication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_intoxication)



Death rates from cocaine overdoses



US yearly overdose deaths involving cocaine.

[^cite_note-NIDA-deaths-191]

According to the [European Union Drugs Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Drugs_Agency), the estimated minimum lethal dose is 1.2 grams. However, sensitive individuals have died from as little as 30 milligrams applied to mucous membranes-an amount that is 40 times less than the minimum lethal dose. In contrast, addicts may tolerate doses as high as 5 grams per day.[^cite_note-EUDA-17]

Use of cocaine causes [abnormally fast heart rhythms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_arrhythmia) and a marked elevation of blood pressure ([hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension)), which can be life-threatening. This can lead to death from acute [myocardial infarction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction), acute [respiratory failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_failure) (i.e., [hypoxemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoxemia), with or without [hypercapnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercapnia)), [stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stroke), [cerebral hemorrhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_hemorrhage), and [sudden cardiac arrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_arrest).[^cite_note-192] Overdose can also cause acute [hepatotoxicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatotoxicity)—typically due to toxic metabolites—though most cases resolve quickly; however, fatal outcomes from [multiple organ dysfunction syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_organ_dysfunction_syndrome) are possible, and there is no specific antidote.[^cite_note-193] Cocaine overdose may result in [hyperthermia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperthermia) as stimulation and increased muscular activity cause greater heat production. Heat loss is also inhibited by the cocaine-induced [vasoconstriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasoconstriction).

In 2024, drug-related deaths in England and Wales reached their highest level in three decades, with a notable increase in fatalities involving cocaine and experts urging urgent government intervention to address the crisis. Martin Powell, from the charity Transform, which campaigns for the [legal regulation of drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_liberalization), suggested that the recent rise in cocaine-related deaths in the UK may be due to the increased purity of cocaine, leading users to consume it more frequently and alongside other substances.[^cite_note-194]

## Interactions

See also: [Combined drug intoxication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_drug_intoxication)

### Alcohol

Main article: [Cocaethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaethylene)

See also: [Coca wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_wine)

[Alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(drug\)) interacts with cocaine *in vivo* to produce [cocaethylene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaethylene), another psychoactive substance which may be substantially more [cardiotoxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiotoxic) than either cocaine or alcohol by themselves.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196] In 2024, a systematic review of human studies concluded that, despite some inconsistencies in the findings, the co-use of cocaine and alcohol poses a significantly greater risk of cardiovascular fatalities compared to cocaine use alone. This elevated risk is largely attributed to the formation of cocaethylene, a unique and toxic metabolite produced only when both substances are consumed together. Cocaethylene is associated with an 18- to 25-fold increased risk of sudden death, as well as a higher incidence of myocardial injury and cardiac arrest, underscoring the serious health risks of simultaneous cocaine and alcohol use.[^cite_note-197]

### MAOIs

[Monoamine oxidase inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_oxidase_inhibitor) (MAOIs) should not be combined with other psychoactive substances (antidepressants, painkillers, stimulants, including prescribed, OTC and illegally acquired drugs, etc.) except under expert care.[^cite_note-198]

### Opioids

See also: [Speedball (drug)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speedball_\(drug\)) and [Opioid epidemic in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid_epidemic_in_the_United_States)

The [opioid epidemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid_epidemic) now involves more overdose deaths with both opioids and cocaine, especially among non-Hispanic Blacks who are twice as likely to die from combined opioid-stimulant overdoses compared to non-Hispanic whites. Cocaine-related deaths in Blacks are similar to opioid deaths in whites. Risk factors include young age, education, urban living, mental disorders, and stress. It remains unclear if co-use is intentional. Recent studies expand focus beyond heroin to all opioids, reflecting changing overdose patterns.[^cite_note-199]

## Pharmacology

### Pharmacokinetics

The extent of absorption of cocaine into the [circulatory system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulatory_system) after nasal insufflation is similar to that after oral ingestion. The rate of absorption after nasal insufflation is limited by cocaine-induced vasoconstriction of capillaries in the nasal mucosa. Onset of absorption after oral ingestion is delayed because cocaine is a weak base with a [pKa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PKa) of 8.6, and is thus in an ionized form that is poorly absorbed from the [gastric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastric_acid) and easily absorbed from the alkaline [duodenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duodenum).[^cite_note-Wilkinson_1980-13] The rate and extent of absorption from inhalation of cocaine is similar or greater than with intravenous injection, as inhalation provides access directly to the [capillary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capillary) bed. The delay in absorption after oral ingestion may account for the popular belief that cocaine bioavailability from the stomach is lower than after insufflation. Compared with ingestion, the faster absorption of insufflated cocaine results in quicker attainment of maximum drug effects. Snorting cocaine produces maximum physiological effects within 40 minutes and maximum psychotropic effects within 20 minutes. Physiological and psychotropic effects from nasally insufflated cocaine are sustained for approximately 40–60 minutes after the peak effects are attained.[^cite_note-200]

Cocaine crosses the [blood–brain barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood%E2%80%93brain_barrier) via both a proton-coupled [organic cation antiporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_cation_transport_protein)[^cite_note-How_do_psychostimulants_enter_the_h-201][^cite_note-Structural_Requirements_for_Uptake-202] and (to a lesser extent) via [passive diffusion across cell membranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_transport).[^cite_note-Chapy_2014-203] As of September 2022, the gene or genes encoding the human proton-organic cation antiporter had not been identified.[^cite_note-204]

Cocaine has a short elimination half-life of 0.7–1.5 hours and is extensively [metabolized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolism) by [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_plasma) esterases and also by liver [cholinesterases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholinesterase), with only about 1% excreted unchanged in the urine.[^cite_note-Zimmerman_2012-15] The metabolism is dominated by [hydrolytic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrolysis) [ester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ester) cleavage, so the eliminated metabolites consist mostly of [benzoylecgonine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzoylecgonine) (BE), the major [metabolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolite), and other metabolites in lesser amounts such as ecgonine methyl ester (EME) and [ecgonine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecgonine).[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-Zimmerman_2012-15] Further minor metabolites of cocaine include [norcocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norcocaine), p-hydroxycocaine, m-hydroxycocaine, p-hydroxybenzoylecgonine (pOHBE), and m-hydroxybenzoylecgonine.[^cite_note-206]

Depending on [liver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver) and [kidney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney) functions, cocaine metabolites are detectable in urine between three and eight days. Generally speaking [benzoylecgonine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzoylecgonine) is eliminated from someone's urine between three and five days. In urine from heavy cocaine users, benzoylecgonine can be detected within four hours after intake and in concentrations greater than 150 ng/mL for up to eight days later.[^cite_note-207]

### Detection in the body

#### Body fluids

Cocaine and its major metabolites may be quantified in blood, plasma, or urine to monitor for use, confirm a diagnosis of poisoning, or assist in the forensic investigation of a traffic or other criminal violation or sudden death. Most commercial cocaine [immunoassay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunoassay) screening tests cross-react appreciably with the major cocaine metabolites, but [chromatographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatography) techniques can easily distinguish and separately measure each of these substances. When interpreting the results of a test, it is important to consider the cocaine usage history of the individual, since a chronic user can develop tolerance to doses that would incapacitate a cocaine-naive individual, and the chronic user often has high baseline values of the metabolites in his system. Cautious interpretation of testing results may allow a distinction between passive or active usage, and between smoking versus other routes of administration.[^cite_note-208]

#### Hair

[Hair analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hair_analysis) can detect cocaine [metabolites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metabolite) in regular users until after the sections of hair grown during the period of cocaine use are cut or fall out.[^cite_note-209]

### Pharmacodynamics

Cocaine acts as a [serotonin–norepinephrine–dopamine reuptake inhibitor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin%E2%80%93norepinephrine%E2%80%93dopamine_reuptake_inhibitor) (SNDRI).[^cite_note-Azizi_2022-7][^cite_note-Cheng_2015-27] Cocaine increases levels of [serotonin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin), [norepinephrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norepinephrine), and [dopamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine) in the synaptic cleft, leading to heightened post-synaptic activation, with dopamine contributing to euphoria and arousal, and the other monoamines enhancing additional effects.[^cite_note-Azizi_2022-7][^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212]

The [pharmacodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacodynamics) of cocaine involve the complex relationships of neurotransmitters (inhibiting [monoamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine) uptake in rats with ratios of about: [serotonin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5HT)\:dopamine = 2\:3, serotonin:[norepinephrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norepinephrine) = 2\:5).[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-Pom_2012-20] The most extensively studied effect of cocaine on the [central nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system) is the blockade of the [dopamine transporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine_transporter) protein. Dopamine [neurotransmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotransmitter) released during neural signaling is normally recycled via the transporter; i.e., the transporter binds the transmitter and pumps it out of the synaptic cleft back into the [presynaptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presynaptic) [neuron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron), where it is taken up into storage [vesicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesicle_\(biology\)). Cocaine binds tightly at the dopamine transporter forming a complex that blocks the transporter's function. The dopamine transporter can no longer perform its reuptake function, and thus [dopamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine) accumulates in the [synaptic cleft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synaptic_cleft). The increased concentration of dopamine in the synapse activates post-synaptic dopamine receptors, which makes the drug [rewarding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reward_system) and promotes the compulsive use of cocaine.[^cite_note-214]

Cocaine affects certain serotonin (5-HT) receptors; in particular, it has been shown to [antagonize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Receptor_antagonist) the [5-HT<sub>3</sub> receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-HT3_receptor), which is a [ligand-gated ion channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligand-gated_ion_channel). An overabundance of 5-HT<sub>3</sub> receptors is reported in cocaine-conditioned rats, though 5-HT<sub>3</sub>'s role is unclear.[^cite_note-215] The [5-HT<sub>2</sub> receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-HT2_receptor) (particularly the subtypes [5-HT<sub>2A</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-HT2A_receptor), [5-HT<sub>2B</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-HT2B_receptor) and [5-HT<sub>2C</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-HT2C_receptor)) are involved in the [locomotor-activating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locomotor_activity) effects of cocaine.[^cite_note-216]

Cocaine has been demonstrated to bind as to directly stabilize the [DAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine_transporter) transporter on the open outward-facing conformation. Further, cocaine binds in such a way as to inhibit a [hydrogen bond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_bond) innate to DAT. Cocaine's binding properties are such that it attaches so this hydrogen bond will not form and is blocked from formation due to the tightly locked orientation of the cocaine molecule. Research studies have suggested that the affinity for the transporter is not what is involved in the habituation of the substance so much as the conformation and binding properties to where and how on the transporter the molecule binds.[^cite_note-217]

Conflicting findings have challenged the widely accepted view that cocaine functions solely as a reuptake inhibitor. To induce euphoria an intravenous dose of 0.3-0.6 mg/kg of cocaine is required, which blocks 66-70% of DAT in the brain.[^cite_note-218] Re-administering cocaine beyond this threshold does not significantly increase DAT occupancy but still results in an increase of euphoria which cannot be explained by reuptake inhibition alone. This discrepancy is not shared with other dopamine reuptake inhibitors like [bupropion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bupropion), [sibutramine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibutramine), [mazindol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazindol) or [tesofensine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesofensine), which have similar or higher potencies than cocaine as dopamine reuptake inhibitors. Furthermore, a similar response-occupancy discrepancy has been observed with [methylphenidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methylphenidate), which also stabilizes the dopamine transporter in an open outward-facing conformation.[^cite_note-Heal_2014-219][^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221] These findings have evoked a hypothesis that cocaine may also function as a so-called "DAT inverse agonist" or "negative allosteric modifier of DAT" resulting in dopamine [transporter reversal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_transport), and subsequent dopamine release into the synaptic cleft from the axon terminal in a manner similar to but distinct from [amphetamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphetamine).[^cite_note-Heal_2014-219]

[Sigma receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_receptor) are affected by cocaine, as cocaine functions as a sigma ligand agonist.[^cite_note-222] Further specific receptors it has been demonstrated to function on are [NMDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NMDA) and the D<sub>1</sub> dopamine receptor.[^cite_note-223]

Cocaine also blocks [sodium channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_channel), thereby interfering with the propagation of [action potentials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_potential);[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-O'Leary_2010-115] thus, like [lignocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignocaine) and [novocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novocaine), it acts as a local anesthetic. It also functions on the binding sites to the dopamine and serotonin [sodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium) dependent transport area as targets as separate mechanisms from its reuptake of those transporters; unique to its local anesthetic value which makes it in a class of functionality different from both its own derived [phenyltropanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenyltropane) analogues which have that removed. In addition to this, cocaine has some target binding to the site of the [κ-opioid receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%CE%9A-opioid_receptor).[^cite_note-225] Cocaine also causes [vasoconstriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasoconstriction), thus reducing bleeding during minor surgical procedures. Recent research points to an important role of circadian mechanisms[^cite_note-226] and [clock genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock_genes)[^cite_note-227] in behavioral actions of cocaine.

Cocaine is known to suppress hunger and appetite by increasing co-localization of sigma σ<sub>1</sub>R receptors and [ghrelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghrelin) GHS-R1a [cell surface receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_surface_receptor), thereby increasing ghrelin-mediated signaling of satiety[^cite_note-228] and possibly via other effects on appetitive hormones.[^cite_note-229]

Cocaine effects, further, are shown to be potentiated for the user when used in conjunction with new surroundings and stimuli, and otherwise novel environs.[^cite_note-230]

## Chemistry

See also: [Coca alkaloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_alkaloid) and [List of cocaine analogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cocaine_analogues)

### Forms

See also: [Cocaine paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_paste) and [Crack cocaine § Chemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine#Chemistry)



A pile of [micronized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micronization) cocaine hydrochloride



A piece of compressed cocaine hydrochloride, commonly used for smuggling

In its purest form, cocaine is a white, pearly powder. As a [tropane alkaloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropane_alkaloid), cocaine is a weak base and readily forms salts when combined with acids. The most commonly encountered form is the hydrochloride (HCl) salt, although other salts such as the sulfate (SO<sub>4</sub><sup>2−</sup>) and nitrate (NO<sub>3</sub><sup>−</sup>) are occasionally observed. The solubility of these salts varies depending on their polarity; the hydrochloride salt is highly soluble in water.[^cite_note-231]

### Synthesis

#### Total synthesis

The first [structure elucidation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_structure#Structural_elucidation) and [total synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_synthesis) of the cocaine molecule was accomplished by [Richard Willstätter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Willst%C3%A4tter) in 1898.[^cite_note-Humphrey_2001-232] Willstätter's synthesis involved constructing the cocaine structure from simpler precursors, notably via the intermediate [tropinone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropinone). Subsequent significant contributions to understanding the synthetic pathway and stereochemistry were made by [Robert Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Robinson_\(organic_chemist\)) and Edward Leete.

Cocaine contains four [chiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiral) centers (1*R*, 2*R*, 3*S*, and 5*S*), two of which are [configurationally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_configuration) dependent, resulting in eight possible [stereoisomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereoisomer). The formation of inactive stereoisomers, along with various synthetic by-products, limits both the yield and purity of the final product.[^cite_note-Amara_2021-233][^cite_note-Drake_2018-234]

Although the chemical synthesis of cocaine is technically feasible, it remains impractical due to its high cost, low efficiency, and complex stereoselective synthesis compared to extraction from natural plant sources. While domestic clandestine laboratories could theoretically reduce reliance on offshore production and international smuggling—as seen with illicit [methamphetamine](11038318.md)—manufacture and synthetic production of cocaine remains rare. Large-scale commercial synthesis has not been explored.

#### Biosynthesis

Main article: [Biosynthesis of cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosynthesis_of_cocaine)

The [biosynthesis of cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosynthesis_of_cocaine) is the natural metabolic process by which the [coca plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_plant) (*Erythroxylum* species) produces cocaine, a [tropane alkaloid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropane_alkaloid), through a multi-step enzymatically catalyzed pathway beginning with [ornithine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ornithine) or [arginine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arginine) and culminating in the formation of the cocaine metabolite [benzoylecgonine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzoylecgonine).

Large-scale biosynthesis of cocaine is unexplored.[^cite_note-Kohnen-Johannsen_2019-235]

The biosynthesis of cocaine has long attracted the attention of biochemists and organic chemists. This interest is partly motivated by the strong physiological effects of cocaine, but a further incentive was the unusual [bicyclic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicyclic_molecule) structure of the molecule. The biosynthesis can be viewed as occurring in two phases, one phase leading to the N-methylpyrrolinium ring, which is preserved in the final product. The second phase incorporates a C4 unit with formation of the bicyclic [tropane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropane) core.[^cite_note-Leete_1990-236]

#### GMO synthesis

In 2022, a [GMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMO) produced *[N. benthamiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N._benthamiana)* were discovered that were able to produce 25% of the amount of cocaine found in a coca plant.[^cite_note-237]

However, since *N. benthamiana* also naturally contains [nicotine](38272.md), separating the cocaine from nicotine and related alkaloids would be challenging.

### Field analysis



[Drugwipe test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drugwipe_test) can detect traces of illicit substances, including cocaine, on surfaces such as driver's licenses at [random checkpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Random_checkpoint).



Cocaine under [UV light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UV_light)

[Personal](1845896.md) cards-including ID cards and driver's licenses-are frequently swabbed by inspectors to detect drug residue, as these items are commonly used to prepare lines of cocaine. Swabbing can reveal traces of cocaine or other illicit substances, providing evidence of recent drug handling or use. This practice may be employed during security checks at border crossings.

A *[Newsbeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newsbeat)* investigation found that "cocaine torches" used by UK police to detect cocaine use are ineffective on typical street cocaine, as independent lab tests showed they fail to make the drug [fluoresce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluoresce). Experts and drug charities criticized the devices, warning they can give false positives and waste resources, while police forces defended their use as a deterrent. The manufacturer says the torches only work on much purer forms of cocaine than are found on the street.[^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239]

Cocaine may be detected by law enforcement using the [Scott reagent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_reagent). The test can easily generate [false positives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_positives_and_false_negatives) for common substances and must be confirmed with a laboratory test.[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241]

Approximate cocaine purity can be determined using 1 mL 2% cupric sulfate pentahydrate in dilute HCl, 1 mL 2% potassium thiocyanate and 2 mL of [chloroform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroform). The shade of brown shown by the chloroform is proportional to the cocaine content. This test is not cross sensitive to heroin, methamphetamine, [benzocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzocaine), procaine and a number of other drugs but other chemicals could cause false positives.[^cite_note-242]

## History

Main article: [History of cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cocaine)

Coca leaves have been used by indigenous South Americans for thousands of years, both as a stimulant and for medicinal purposes.[^cite_note-Hesse_2002-243]

When the [Spanish arrived in South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas), they initially banned coca but soon legalized and taxed it after seeing its importance to local labor.[^cite_note-244] The active ingredient was first isolated in 1855 by [Friedrich Gaedcke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Gaedcke) and later refined by [Albert Niemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Niemann_\(chemist\)), who named it "cocaine".[^cite_note-Luch_2009-245][^cite_note-Niemann_1860-246][^cite_note-247] In the late 1800s, cocaine became popular in Western medicine as a local anesthetic and was widely used in various products, including drinks and remedies.[^cite_note-248] and [James Leonard Corning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Leonard_Corning) demonstrated [peridural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peridural) anesthesia.[^cite_note-249] However, due to its toxic effects and potential for abuse, safer alternatives eventually replaced it in medical practice.[^cite_note-Latorre_1999-23]

Large-scale coca cultivation and cocaine production occurred in Asia, on [Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Taiwan) (then known as Formosa) and [Java](69336.md) (today part of Indonesia) before [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-Lu_2024-50][^cite_note-Musto_1998-51]

Since the 1980s, the cocaine trade was dominated by centralized, hierarchical [drug cartels](197015.md) such as [Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn_Cartel) and [Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali_Cartel), along with their successors and early [FARC](26100.md) factions. By the early 2000s, this model fragmented into a diverse network of global trafficking links, allowing South American cocaine production to easily supply markets in Europe, Africa, Asia, and Oceania through various routes.[^cite_note-Lien_2025-250]

## Society and culture

Main article: [Cocaine and society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_and_society)

Both the [pharmaceutical supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_and_society#Pharmaceutical_supply_chain) and the [illicit supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_and_society#Illicit_supply_chain) obtain cocaine from coca cultivated in Latin America, but they operate under very different controls and oversight. In Peru, for example, legal coca cultivation is monopolized by the state company [National Coca Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coca_Company) (ENACO), yet approximately 90% of coca leaves produced in the country are diverted to illegal actors for cocaine manufacturing.[^cite_note-251] As a result, these illicit coca crops are a primary target of ongoing government-led [coca eradication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_eradication) efforts.[^cite_note-Grisaffi_2016-252]

Cocaine is prohibited in competition for athletes by the [World Anti-Doping Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Anti-Doping_Agency) (WADA), which lists it as a stimulant on its [International Standard for the Prohibited List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_drugs_banned_by_the_World_Anti-Doping_Agency).[^cite_note-WADA17-253]<sup>: 6 </sup>

### Slang names

Cocaine is sometimes referred to on the street as blow, coca, coke, crank, flake, snow, or soda cot. Slang terms for free base cocaine include crack or rock.[^cite_note-DEA-254]

### Levamisole syndromes

Further information: [Levamisole-induced necrosis syndrome § Cocaine/levamisole-associated syndromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levamisole-induced_necrosis_syndrome#Cocaine/levamisole-associated_syndromes)

[Levamisole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levamisole) is one of the most common cutting agents used to [lace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacing_\(drugs\)) illicit cocaine, with studies showing that between 2009 and 2016, 50–70% of all cocaine specimens worldwide contained levamisole, reflecting similar high rates of contamination across North America and Europe.[^cite_note-Vonmoos_2018-255] Before trafficking to the United States, the cocaine is frequently adulterated with levamisole.[^cite_note-Buchanan_2010-256] By October 2017, this figure had risen further, with the US [Drug Enforcement Administration](146720.md) (DEA) reporting that 87% of seized and analyzed cocaine bricks in the United States contained levamisole, making it the most common adulterant in cocaine at that time.[^cite_note-DIR-040-17_2017-NDTA.pdf-257]

In the body, levamisole is converted into [aminorex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aminorex), a substance with [amphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphetamine)-like stimulant effects and a long duration of action.[^cite_note-258] Levamisole-adulterated cocaine is associated with cocaine/levamisole-associated [autoimmune syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autoimmune_disease) (CLAAS) and cocaine- and levamisole-induced [vasculitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasculitis) (CLIV).[^cite_note-Gill_2021-259][^cite_note-Cascio_2018-260] [Reagent testing kits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reagent_testing_color_charts) can be used to detect the presence of cocaine and levamisole.

### Legal status

Main article: [Legal status of cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_status_of_cocaine)

The production, distribution, and sale of cocaine products is restricted (and illegal in most contexts) in most countries as regulated by the [Single Convention on Narcotic Drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_Convention_on_Narcotic_Drugs), and the [United Nations Convention Against Illicit Traffic in Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Convention_Against_Illicit_Traffic_in_Narcotic_Drugs_and_Psychotropic_Substances). In the United States the manufacture, importation, possession, and distribution of cocaine are additionally regulated by the 1970 [Controlled Substances Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_Substances_Act).

Some countries, such as Bolivia, Colombia, and Peru, permit the cultivation of coca leaf for traditional consumption by the local [indigenous population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas), but nevertheless, prohibit the production, sale, and consumption of cocaine.[^cite_note-bolivia-cocaine-bar-route-36-261] The provisions as to how much a coca farmer can yield annually is protected by laws such as the Bolivian Cato accord.[^cite_note-262] In addition, some parts of Europe, the United States, and Australia allow processed cocaine for medicinal uses only.

#### Australia

Cocaine is a Schedule 8 controlled drug in Australia under the [Poisons Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_for_the_Uniform_Scheduling_of_Medicines_and_Poisons).[^cite_note-263] It is the second most popular illicit recreational drug in Australia [behind cannabis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_in_Australia).[^cite_note-264]

In [Western Australia](33613.md) under the Misuse of Drugs Act 1981, 4.0g of cocaine is the amount of prohibited drugs determining a court of trial, 2.0g is the amount of cocaine required for the presumption of intention to sell or supply, and 28.0g is the amount of cocaine required for purposes of drug trafficking.[^cite_note-265]

#### United States

See also: [Cocaine in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_in_the_United_States)



President [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) holds up a bag of [crack cocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crack_cocaine) during his Address to the Nation on National Drug Control Strategy on 5 September 1989.

The US federal government instituted a national [drug labelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prescribing_information) requirement for cocaine and cocaine-containing products through the [Pure Food and Drug Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pure_Food_and_Drug_Act) of 1906.[^cite_note-Gootenberg_1999-266]<sup>: 37 </sup> The next important federal regulation was the [Harrison Narcotics Tax Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrison_Narcotics_Tax_Act) of 1914. While this act is often seen as the start of prohibition, the act itself was not actually a prohibition on cocaine, but instead it set up a regulatory and licensing regime.[^cite_note-Madge_2001-267] The Harrison Act did not recognize addiction as a treatable condition and therefore the therapeutic use of cocaine, heroin, or morphine to such individuals was outlawed –  leading a 1915 editorial in the journal *American Medicine* to remark that the addict "is denied the medical care he urgently needs, open, above-board sources from which he formerly obtained his drug supply are closed to him, and he is driven to the underworld where he can get his drug, but of course, surreptitiously and in violation of the law."[^cite_note-268] The Harrison Act left manufacturers of cocaine untouched so long as they met certain purity and labeling standards.[^cite_note-Gootenberg_1999-266]<sup>: 40 </sup> Despite that cocaine was typically illegal to sell and legal outlets were rarer, the quantities of legal cocaine produced declined very little.[^cite_note-Gootenberg_1999-266]<sup>: 40 </sup> Legal cocaine quantities did not decrease until the [Jones–Miller Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcotic_Drugs_Import_and_Export_Act) of 1922 put serious restrictions on cocaine manufactures.[^cite_note-Gootenberg_1999-266]<sup>: 40 </sup>

Before the early 1900s, newspapers primarily portrayed addiction (rather than violence or crime) as the main problem caused by cocaine use, and depicted cocaine users as upper or middle class [White people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_people). In 1914, The New York Times published an article titled "Negro Cocaine 'Fiends' Are a New Southern Menace," portraying [Black people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_people) who used cocaine as dangerous and able to withstand wounds that would normally be fatal.[^cite_note-269] The [Anti-Drug Abuse Act of 1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Drug_Abuse_Act_of_1986) mandated the same prison sentences for distributing 500 grams of powdered cocaine and just 5 grams of crack cocaine.[^cite_note-270] In the [National Survey on Drug Use and Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Survey_on_Drug_Use_and_Health), white respondents reported a higher rate of powdered cocaine use, and Black respondents reported a higher rate of crack cocaine use.[^cite_note-271]

### Prevalence and trends

Cocaine production, seizures, and use all reached record levels in 2023, making it the world's fastest-growing illicit drug market. Seizures rose by 68% from 2019 to 2023, while the number of users increased from 17 million in 2013 to 25 million in 2023, according to the UNODC [World Drug Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Drug_Report) 2025.[^cite_note-Press_release_WDR_2025_English.pdf-47] Concurrently, record levels of cocaine production have enabled traffickers to enter new markets across Asia and Africa, reflecting the expanding global reach of cocaine trafficking.[^cite_note-WDR25_Special_points_of_interest.pdf-45]

The U.S. is disproportionately the world's largest consumer of cocaine,[^cite_note-WFK_Illicit_drugs-272] while South America, collectively as a continent, ranks third in terms of consumer market size.[^cite_note-Andean_full_report.pdf-21] Europe ranks cocaine as the second most commonly used illicit drug.[^cite_note-273]

Cocaine is among the most widely consumed [recreational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_drug_use) stimulants worldwide.[^cite_note-Roque_2022-28]

### Impact

#### Impact of illicit cocaine

##### Impact on impoverished communities

In countries where cocaine is illicitly produced, an intermediate product known as cocaine paste—often referred to as "poor man's cocaine"—is frequently smoked in impoverished communities. This substance is favored in these areas primarily because it is inexpensive and more accessible than refined cocaine. However, the use of cocaine paste poses severe health risks. During its production, various toxic chemicals are used to extract coca alkaloids from the coca leaves. Many of these hazardous substances, such as solvents and acids, remain in the paste after processing. When the paste is smoked, individuals are exposed not only to the addictive effects of the drug itself but also to the dangerous residual chemicals, which can cause significant harm to the lungs, nervous system, and overall health. This combination of affordability, accessibility, and toxicity makes cocaine paste particularly damaging to vulnerable populations in cocaine-producing regions.[^cite_note-smokablecocaine_eng_web-def.pdf-26][^cite_note-Arif_1987-97][^cite_note-Jeri_1984-98][^cite_note-Phillips_2009-99]

##### Environmental impact

See also: [Environmental impact of illicit drug production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_illicit_drug_production)

Most of the world's cocaine is produced in South America, particularly in the [Andean region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes).[^cite_note-UNODC_2019-274] The environmental destruction caused by the production of cocaine has been well documented, with reports made the UN and other government bodies.[^cite_note-McSweeney_2015-275] Due to the illegal nature of [coca production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_production_in_Colombia), farmers make little effort in soil conservation and sustainability practices as seen in the high mobility and short life of coca plots in Colombia.[^cite_note-UNODC_2019-274]

One of the major implications of cocaine production is [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) as large areas of forest are cleared for coca cultivation. The UNODC approximated that 97,622 hectares of [primary forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old-growth_forest) were cleared for coca cultivation during 2001–2004 in the Andean region.[^cite_note-UNODC_2019-274] This further causes [habitat destruction](1707053.md), especially in [biodiversity hotspots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_hotspot), areas rich in a variety of species. Such areas are chosen for coca cultivation due to their remote locations, minimising chances of detection.[^cite_note-Burns-Edel_2016-276] Deforestation impacts [soil erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_erosion) which further inhibits the survival of native species.[^cite_note-UNODC_2019-274]

The use of [pesticides](48340.md) can also severely affect the environment. Farmers are able to use unregulated and highly toxic pesticides due to the clandestine nature of drug production.[^cite_note-Burns-Edel_2016-276] The use of such pesticides can have both direct and indirect effects on the ecosystem. Where lethal levels of exposure directly cause the death of fauna, which is further carried up the [food chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_chain) where secondary feeders who consume the poisoned animals are also impacted. Furthermore, non-lethal levels of exposure can also cause weaker immune system development and neurological issues, further increasing mortality rates.[^cite_note-Burns-Edel_2016-276]

##### Impact of illicit cocaine trade

Cocaine is extremely expensive on the black market, with prices rising sharply at each distribution level—often more than its weight in gold.[^cite_note-277]

###### Latin America

Drug war policies in Latin America and the Caribbean have led to more violence, higher incarceration rates, health crises, and deeper poverty, while undermining trust in institutions and worsening inequality. There is increasing support for shifting toward drug policies that focus on sustainable development and human rights instead of punitive measures.[^cite_note-ow-the-drug-wars-impact-latin-america-and-the-caribbean-development-46]

###### West Africa

Cocaine trafficking in West Africa has become closely linked with the activities of several [terrorist organizations](63578.md).[^cite_note-278][^cite_note-279][^cite_note-280]

#### Impact of enforcement

##### Impact of coca eradication

See also: [Coca eradication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_eradication)

In December 2000, Dutch journalist [Marjon van Royen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjon_van_Royen) found that "because the chemical is sprayed in Colombia from planes on inhabited areas, there have been consistent health complaints \[in humans]. Burning eyes, dizziness and respiratory problems being most frequently reported." In some areas, 80 percent of the children of the indigenous community fell sick with skin rashes, fever, diarrhoea and eye infections.[^cite_note-281] Because the glyphosate is sprayed from the air, there is a much higher chance of human error when spraying suspected illegal coca plantations. In many cases the wrong fields are sprayed, resulting in not only a total loss of the farmer's crop- but the loss of that field altogether as nothing will grow where the herbicide has been sprayed.[^cite_note-Sue_Branford_2005-282] Though official documentation of the health effects of glyphosate spraying in Colombia are virtually non-existent, neighbouring Ecuador has conducted studies to determine the cause of mysterious illnesses amongst people living along the border of Colombia and has since demanded that no aerial sprayings occur within 10 km of the border because of the damages caused to the people, animals and environment in that area.[^cite_note-Sue_Branford_2005-282] In 2015, Colombia announced a ban on using glyphosate in these programs due to concerns about human toxicity of the chemical.[^cite_note-BBCColumbia-283]

##### Impact of interdiction

The Consolidated Counterdrug Database (CCDB) is a U.S. government dataset created in the 1990s that compiles vetted data on cocaine trafficking and seizures in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md) "transit zone," involving 26 U.S. agencies and 20 foreign partners. It provides a highly reliable, conservative record of cocaine movements and interdiction efforts, revealing that despite large seizures, interdiction captures only a small fraction of trafficking events and has minimal impact on U.S. cocaine prices. The CCDB challenges optimistic views of drug interdiction effectiveness and underscores the need for new policy approaches, yet remains underutilized in research despite being unclassified.[^cite_note-284]

### Etymology

The word cocaine derives from [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language)&#x20;*&#x20;Cocaïne*, from [Spanish](26825.md)&#x20;*&#x20;[coca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca)*, ultimately from [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_language)&#x20;*&#x20;kúka*.[^cite_note-285]

## Research

Cocaine [haptens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hapten) are chemically modified derivatives of cocaine that retain key immunogenic features, allowing them to be attached to carrier proteins such as [keyhole limpet hemocyanin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keyhole_limpet_hemocyanin) or [bovine serum albumin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bovine_serum_albumin). This enables the immune system to recognize cocaine and produce anti-cocaine antibodies, which can bind cocaine in the bloodstream and prevent it from reaching the brain, thereby blocking its psychoactive effects.[^cite_note-Janda_2011-286][^cite_note-287][^cite_note-Bremer_2017-288]

The [cocaine esterase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_esterase) enzyme and redesigned versions of it have been studied as a potential treatment for [cocaine addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocaine_dependence) in humans.[^cite_note-289]

[Coca tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca_tea) has been explored as a supportive treatment for cocaine dependence. A study in [Lima](85423.md), Peru, found that using coca leaf infusion along with counseling reduced relapse rates and significantly increased the duration of abstinence among addicted individuals, suggesting that this approach may help prevent relapse during treatment.[^cite_note-Teobaldo_2014-290]

Recent research has also examined the use of prescription psychostimulants for cocaine dependence, following the [Self-Medication Hypothesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-medication#Self-medication_hypothesis). This hypothesis suggests that some individuals use cocaine to address underlying neurochemical or psychological issues. While some studies indicate that psychostimulant therapy may reduce cocaine use and cravings, the evidence is mixed and further research is needed.[^cite_note-291]

In animal studies, nicotine exposure in [mice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laboratory_mouse) increases the likelihood of later cocaine use, with clear molecular changes in the brain.[^cite_note-Kandel_2014-292] These findings mirror human [epidemiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiology) data showing a link between nicotine use and increased risk of later cannabis and cocaine use, as well as other substances.[^cite_note-Keyes_2016-293][^cite_note-Ren_2019-294] Similarly, in rats, alcohol consumption raises the probability of later cocaine addiction and is associated with changes in the brain's reward system.[^cite_note-Griffin_2017-295][^cite_note-Anderson_2019-296] Human studies also show that alcohol use increases the risk of transitioning from cocaine use to addiction.[^cite_note-Lopez-Quintero_2011-297][^cite_note-Bickel_2016-298]

Experimentally, cocaine injections can be delivered to animals such as [fruit flies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drosophila_melanogaster) to study the mechanisms of cocaine addiction.[^cite_note-299]

### Cocaine vaccines

#### Calixcoca

Main article: [Calixcoca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calixcoca)

[Calixcoca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calixcoca) is an experimental [vaccine](32653.md) to treat cocaine and crack cocaine addiction. It has been in development since 2015 by the [Federal University of Minas Gerais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_University_of_Minas_Gerais) (UFMG) in Brazil.[^cite_note-Corrêa_2023-300]

#### TA-CD

Main article: [TA-CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TA-CD)

[TA-CD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TA-CD) is a [vaccine](32653.md) developed by the Xenova Group and designed to negate the effects of cocaine, making it suitable for use in treatment of addiction. It is created by combining [norcocaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norcocaine) with inactivated [cholera](7591.md) toxin.[^cite_note-Martell_2005-301]
