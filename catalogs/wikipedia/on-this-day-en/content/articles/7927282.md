The **Algerian Air Force** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): القُوَّاتُ الجَوِّيَّةُ الجَزَائِرِيَّةُ, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Forces aériennes algériennes*) is the aerial arm of the [Algerian People's National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_People's_National_Army).

Its history begins with the creation by [Saïd Aït Messaoudène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sa%C3%AFd_A%C3%AFt_Messaoud%C3%A8ne), during [the war of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_War), of a first nucleus of air force with the training of Algerian pilots and personnel from friendly countries. This nucleus officially gave birth in 1962, the day after independence, to the Algerian Air Force.

Major General Mahmoud Laraba was appointed Commander of the Air Force in July 2020, succeeding Major General Hamid Boumaïza. On February 23, 2025, Major General Zoubir Ghouila took office as Commander of the Air Force, replacing Mahmoud Laraba.

## History

The Algerian Air Force was created to support the fight of the People's National Army against the French occupying forces. It came as part of the decisions of the Soummam congress held on August 20, 1956, which recommended a long-term plan to form a modern army.[^cite_note-hist-4]

### Before 1962

In 1957, six Algerians were sent for training to the [Syrian Arab Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Arab_Air_Force) flight school at Nayrab near [Aleppo](159244.md). During this period, training also took place with the [Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Air_Force) and [Iraqi](1817852.md) air forces, as well as in the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) and in [China](5405.md).[^cite_note-sws_p34-5][^cite_note-hist-4] During this period, the French army started the Challe and [Morice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morice_Line) lines used to isolate the ALN fighters inside the country and to stop supplies coming from Tunisia and Morocco. Then came the idea to train transport and helicopter pilots to ensure supplying the national liberation army, and to prepare the first core of the military aviation.[^cite_note-hist-4] The Algerian Air Force was officially established on 4 February 1959, as a part of the [FLN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberation_Front_\(Algeria\)).[^cite_note-sws_p34-5]

### From 1962 to 1970

The Algerian Air Force was originally based at [*Maison Blanche* (White House)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houari_Boumediene_Airport). In 1962, Egypt transferred 12 [Helwan Gomhouria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%BCcker_B%C3%BC_181_Bestmann) trainers and five [MiG-15s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-15) (two MiG-15UTIs and three MiG-15bis), together with a group of advisors to help with training. Several [Mil Mi-4s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-4) were also received from the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-6] Two [Beech D.185S](2045174.md) light transports purchased for the personal use of then President [Ben Bella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Bella) in 1963.[^cite_note-MacDonald-7]

Training was one of the major preoccupations of the ALN/FLN leaders. Military aviation had a core of pilots and technicians after independence, who laid the foundations of the present Air Force. The Algerian authorities sent trainees to friendly countries such Egypt, Syria, Iraq, China, and the USSR, while waiting for the creation of Algerian Air Force schools. In 1966, the Air Base of Tafraoui in the 2nd Military Region was built as an air officers' school (EOA) where the first officer students were received to train as pilots and technicians in aeronautics.[^cite_note-hist-4]

The nascent Algerian Air Force participated in the 1963 [Sand War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_War) with Morocco. However, air power did not play a big role during the war. MiG-15s and Mi-4s were used, and aircraft from national company [Air Algérie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Alg%C3%A9rie) served for transport duties.[^cite_note-8] Following the end of the war, the build-up of the Algerian Air Force was intensified.[^cite_note-9] In 1964, 20 additional MiG-15bis fighters were delivered by the USSR. The next year, the first of at least 30 [MiG-17s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-17) arrived in Algeria.[^cite_note-10] In 1965–1966, 24 [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21)F-13s were received, and 14 [Ilyushin Il-28s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin_Il-28) were ordered in the same period, as well as some MiG-21FLs. Despite its growth, at the time of the [Six-Day War](29328.md) the Algerian Air Force was not deemed combat ready. Still, 20 MiG-17Fs, 20 MiG-21s and 12 Il-28s were sent to Egypt, but without any crews.[^cite_note-Cooper_2018_pages=48–49-11] In 1967, the Algerian Air Force also bought its first surface-to-air missile systems, with two battalions of [S-75 Dvinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-75_Dvina). However, these were never put into service.[^cite_note-MiG-23_p17-12]

### From 1970 to 1980



C-130H Hercules

In the late 1960s, thanks to increased oil prices and to Algeria's economic growth, important arms orders were passed with the USSR. Hence, in the early 1970s, the Algerian Air Force received 52 MiG-21MFs, MiG-21Rs and MiG-21UMs, and 40 [Sukhoi Su-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-7)BMKs. During the same period, 28 second-hand [Fouga CM.170 Magisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fouga_CM.170_Magister) were bought from West Germany, as well as some [Aérospatiale SA 330 Puma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_SA_330_Puma) and [Mil Mi-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-6) helicopters.[^cite_note-Cooper_2018_pages=48–49-11]

Thanks to its growth in the previous years, the Algerian Air Force was able to participate directly in the 1973 [October War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War). Two squadrons of MiG-21s, another of Su-7s and a unit comprising 23 MiG-17s were sent to Egypt. This deployment was supported by [Antonov An-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-12) transports. Some Algerian pilots were also assigned to [Egyptian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Air_Force) MiG-17 squadrons. In total, around 500 combat sorties were flown by Algerian pilots. One MiG-21 and one Su-7 were shot down, but no pilot was killed or captured.[^cite_note-13]

In 1975, another big arms deal was passed with Moscow, including 40 [MiG-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-23)BNs, 47 MiG-21bis, and 16 [Mil Mi-8s](957233.md). The first MiG-23BNs arrived in 1976; this type replaced the Su-7BMKs. Another arms order followed in 1978, with the Algerians requesting the delivery of 40 more MiG-21s, 16 MiG-23MFs, 20 [Mil Mi-24s](384591.md), and 12 [S-125 Pechora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-125_Pechora) missile systems. However, while 20 [MiG-25s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-25) were delivered by 1979, negotiations for the remainder of the package proved much more difficult than expected, and took nearly four years. Limited quantities of Western aircraft were bought too: in 1978, six [T-34C Mentors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_T-34_Mentor) and three [Fokker F27s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_F27) were acquired.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-MiG-23_p17-12]

### From 1980 to 2000

&#x20;In 1981, thanks to the deliveries of air defence equipment, a new branch of the air force was created: the *Défense Aérienne du Territoire* (Territorial Air Defence). Through the 1980s, the Algerian Air Force worked to build a fully integrated network of early warning radars. In 1986–1988, the Territorial Air Defence was expanded and reorganised into the *Commandement de la Défense Aérienne du Territoire* (Territorial Air Defence Command). In 1988, this command became a fully independent branch of the Algerian armed forces.[^cite_note-15]



Algerian C-130H on the airport apron



An Ilyushin IL-78

The resulting organisational structure of the air force was as follows:[^cite_note-hist-4]

- A central command assisted by a general staff and an inspectorate, an arms division, a department of support, and specialized offices

- Air commands in the military regions

- Air bases, schools, training centers, support institutions, equipment renovation enterprises & defense, and control units

In the second half of the 1980s, the Algerian Air Force also introduced the [wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_\(military_unit\)) structure.[^cite_note-Cooper_2018_page=56-16] During this period few changes occurred in the combat aircraft inventory of the Algerian Air Force. Ten [Sukhoi Su-24](382335.md)MKs were received from the USSR, while the ageing [MiG-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-17)F was phased out. A new airplane supplier emerged just after the Iranian revolution when Algeria received 18 [C-130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130)H Hercules and 12 Hawker Beechcrafts supplied by USA from 1981 to 1989.

Starting in the second half of the 1980s, the Algerian Air Force saw combat in the [Algerian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algerian_Civil_War).[^cite_note-Cooper_2018_page=56-16]

### From 2000 to 2020



An Algerian [Su-30MKA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-30MKA)

As the Civil War was winding down, the Algerian Air Force began to replace its older combat aircraft. The last MiG-21s were withdrawn from service in 2002. The MiG-23BNs followed in 2005, as did the MiG-23MFs in 2008.[^cite_note-17] The Air Force purchased a large number of [Mikoyan MiG-29s](185653.md) (index 9.13) from [Belarus](3457.md) and [Ukraine](31750.md) from 1999 to 2003. At least 25 [Su-24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-24)MKs were also acquired during the same period. In March 2006, as part of a bigger arms deal, Algeria ordered 28 [Sukhoi Su-30](552808.md)MKA multirole fighters, 16 [Yakovlev Yak-130](1513240.md) trainers, 28 MiG-29SMTs fighters and six MiG-29UB conversion trainers from Russia.[^cite_note-Su-30MKA_red_samovar-18] Shortly after the first MiG-29 9.19 deliveries, the Algerian military discovered that these aircraft were not newly built airframes, but older ones modernised to MiG-29 9.19 standard. The decision was taken to send back the aircraft to Russia, refuse all of the planned subsequent deliveries, and to freeze the payments for these aircraft. After a meeting between Algerian and Russian heads of state in February 2008, the aircraft that had already been delivered were returned to Russia, and the whole batch originally built for Algeria was bought back and delivered to the [Russian Air Force](1526308.md). Instead, Algeria was given the possibility to order another batch of Su-30MKAs at sharply reduced prices. This was done in December 2010, when 16 additional aircraft were ordered. In December 2015, 32 more Su-30MKAs were purchased.[^cite_note-Su-30MKA_red_samovar-18] Algeria ordered 32 SU-30MKA and 4 MIG-29M/M2 multirole fighters in 2019, a massive 10.4-billion-dollar deal.[^cite_note-19]

### Since 2020

In late 2020 Algerian Air Force modernized its SU-24M/MK/MR to the M2/MR2 format.[^cite_note-su-20]

In August 2022, the final MIG-25 were retired, but in late 2022 they were brought back to service.

Since 2025, the Algerian Air Force has entered a new phase of modernization. In 2025, a deal was signed with Russia for the delivery of [Sukhoi Su-35](314292.md) multirole fighters, continuing the long-standing partnership between the two countries. The delivery of these aircraft started in March 2025.[^cite_note-21]

In addition to fighter jets, Algeria has also improved its helicopter fleet. In 2022, the Air Force received several [AW139](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AgustaWestland_AW139) utility helicopters from the Italian company Leonardo. These helicopters are used for search and rescue missions, transport, and other support roles.

## Incidents

- In November 2012, a [C-295M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EADS_CASA_C-295) cargo plane returning from Paris crashed in southeastern France, killing all six people on board.

  [^cite_note-sd-71]

- In December 2012, two [MiG-29s](185653.md) conducting routine training operations collided in midair in northwestern Algeria, killing both pilots.

  [^cite_note-am-72]

  [^cite_note-73]

- On 11 February 2014, a [C-130H-30 Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130_Hercules) transport plane [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Algerian_Air_Force_C-130_crash) in Oum El Bouaghi, Algeria. It resulted in 77 deaths.

  [^cite_note-sd-71]

- On 11 October 2014, a [Sukhoi Su-24](382335.md) crashed in a military zone during training, killing its two occupants.

  [^cite_note-am-72]

  [^cite_note-aeronautique.ma-74]

- On 11 November 2014, a [MiG-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-25) or MiG-29 fighter came down near a military range in the Hassi Bahbah Military Region in the central part of Algeria. No casualties were reported at the time as the pilot was alone and ejected safely out of the plane.

  [^cite_note-am-72]

  [^cite_note-aeronautique.ma-74]

- On 20 April 2015, a [Mil Mi-24](384591.md) helicopter crashed a few minutes after taking off from [Illizi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illizi_Airport), killing both crew members.

  [^cite_note-75]

- On 27 March 2016, a [Mi-171](1493374.md) helicopter crashed near [Reggane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggane) due to technical problems, killing 12 soldiers and leaving two others injured.

  [^cite_note-am-72]

- On 11 April 2018, an [Il-76](229943.md) strategic airlifter [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Algerian_Air_Force_Ilyushin_Il-76_crash) in a field shortly after taking off from [Boufarik Airport](34913697.md). It resulted in 257 deaths.

  [^cite_note-76]

- In February 2019, a [Sukhoi Su-24](382335.md) crashed in the province of [Tiaret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiaret), resulting in 2 deaths.

  [^cite_note-77]

- On 13 March 2019, a [L-39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_L-39_Albatros) crashed at [Tafraoui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafraoui), resulting in 2 deaths.

  [^cite_note-78]

- On 28 January 2018, a [Sukhoi Su-30MKA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-30MKA) fighter jet crashed in the mountains resulting in 2 deaths.

  [^cite_note-79]

- On 25 June 2020, a [CH-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASC_Rainbow#CH-4) [medium-altitude long-endurance UAV](9309406.md) crashed due to some unknown issues.

  [^cite_note-80]

- On 29 March 2022, a [MiG-29UB](185653.md) crashed near the city of [Oran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oran), due to a technical malfunction, resulting in 1 death.

  [^cite_note-81]

- On 23 January 2023, a [Mi-171](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi-171) helicopter crashed near [El Attaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Attaf). All three of its crew died.

  [^cite_note-Ouali-82]

- On February 7, 2024, a [Mi-171](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi-171) helicopter crashed near [El Menia Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Menia_Airport) during a night training flight. All three of its crew died.

  [^cite_note-Ouali-82]

- On 19 March 2025, a [Sukhoi Su-30MKA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-30MKA) fighter jet crashed near Timokten, Adrar after takeoff from [Reggane Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggane_Airport), resulting in 1 pilot fatality.

  [^cite_note-83]

- On 5 March 2026, a [Beechcraft 1900D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechcraft_1900D) operated by the Algerian Air Force crashed shortly after takeoff from [Boufarik Airport](34913697.md), near Blida, Algeria. Out of six people on board, four people were killed. An investigation into the cause of the crash was launched by Algerian authorities.

  [^cite_note-84]
