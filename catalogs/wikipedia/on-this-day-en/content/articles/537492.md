The **Antonov An-72** ([NATO reporting name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_reporting_name): **Coaler**) is a [transport aircraft](2375692.md), developed and produced by the [Ukrainian](31750.md) aircraft manufacturer [Antonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov). The An-72 and the related [An-74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-74) get their nickname, *[Cheburashka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheburashka)*, from the large engine intake ducts, which resemble the oversized ears of the popular [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) animated character of the same name.

Design work on the An-72 commenced during the early 1970s, likely as the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)'s response to the American [Advanced Medium STOL Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Medium_STOL_Transport) (AMST) programme that was ultimately terminated. It was developed in relative secrecy as a [short takeoff and landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STOL) (STOL) transport aircraft and was intended to serve as a jet-powered replacement for the [Antonov An-26](908173.md). The An-72 made the use of the [Coandă effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coand%C4%83_effect) to improve its STOL performance by directing engine exhaust gases over the upper surface of the wing to boost lift. This performance directed numerous other design features across the aircraft, including its use of a [T-tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-tail) and the specifics of its flight control system. It was also equipped with an automatic navigation system and a robust [undercarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear), the latter feature facilitated the use of austere airstrips.

The An-72's [maiden flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_flight) took place on 31 August  1977. Quantity production did not proceed until the late 1980s, although the type did see active service with the [Soviet Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Force) prior to its dissolution. The An-72's export prospects had been hampered by the airlifter's development being concealed even from Western certifying bodies. Following the dissolution of the Soviet Union, various successor states, including Russia and Ukraine, inherited the type and continued to operate it. In addition to its use as a military airlifter, some An-72 variants have found success as commercial freighters with various [airlines](1942.md) operating the type. An An-72 derivative, the An-74, is designed to operate under the harsh weather conditions of the [polar regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_regions_of_Earth).

## Design and development

The origins of the An-72 can be traced back to the 1970s; early work on the programme appears to have been performed under a high level of secrecy.[^cite_note-sweetman_1978-2] It has been speculated that the An-72's development had been initiated as a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) response to the American [Advanced Medium STOL Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Medium_STOL_Transport) (AMST) that had been initiated during the 1960s, but ultimately never resulted in any production aircraft.[^cite_note-3] Aviation author [Bill Sweetman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Sweetman) observed that the An-72 bore a close resemblance to the [Boeing YC-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_YC-14),[^cite_note-sweetman_1978-2] one of the AMST designs that had been submitted to the [United States Air Force](32090.md) as a prospective [tactical airlifter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airlift#Tactical_airlift).[^cite_note-4] Officially, the primary purpose of the An-72 programme was to produce a successor to the [turboprop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboprop)-powered [Antonov An-26](908173.md).[^cite_note-sweetman_1978-2][^cite_note-5]

The An-72 was powered by a pair of [Lotarev D-36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotarev_D-36) [turbofan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbofan) engines (these have previously been used on the [Yakovlev Yak-42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakovlev_Yak-42));[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-AFM_73-7] these were mounted in a relatively high over-wing position that was relatively close to the centreline, which allowed the aircraft to make use of the [Coandă effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coand%C4%83_effect) (another benefit was to reduce instances of [foreign object damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_object_damage)).[^cite_note-AFM_73-7] A substantial portion of the aircraft's design was shaped by this use of jet deflection across the upper surface of the wing; beyond the positioning of the engines, a [T-tail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-tail) configuration was also necessary in order to keep the tail plane clear of the engine wake.[^cite_note-sweetman_1978-2] This T-tail was equipped with a swept [fin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fin), which helped to lengthen the tail moment arm and to accommodate [trim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trim_tab) changes. The wing of the AN-72, unlike the straight wing of the YC-14, is [swept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swept_wing) and a noticeable [anhedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihedral_\(aeronautics\)#Anhedral); the centre section is unswept.[^cite_note-sweetman_1978-2] It is furnished with multiple [flaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flap_\(aeronautics\)), which move on tracks; the outboard flaps are double-slotted while [slats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading-edge_slat) are also fitted on the [leading edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leading_edge).[^cite_note-sweetman_1978-2][^cite_note-AFM_73-7]

The fuselage of the An-72 was sized to provide slightly greater internal width than that of the preceding An-26.[^cite_note-sweetman_1978-2] A large nose [radome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radome) is usually fitted. The [landing gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear) comprises four independent single-wheel main gear legs that retract inwards into sizable [fairings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_fairing).[^cite_note-sweetman_1978-2] The automatic flight control system was believed to be particularly challenging to develop due to the aircraft's STOL capabilities.[^cite_note-sweetman_1978-2] An automatic navigation system that incorporated a [Doppler radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doppler_radar) unit was also installed. The An-72 is capable of operating from austere airstrips even under icy conditions;[^cite_note-AFM_73-7] specifically, the combination of a robust [undercarriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear) and high-flotation tyres permit operations on [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand), [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass), or other [unpaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unpaved) surfaces. The rear fuselage of the aircraft has a hinged loading ramp with a rear fairing that slides backwards and up to clear the opening. Up to 7.5 tonnes (7.4 long tons; 8.3 short tons) can be airdropped. The cabin featured side-mounted folding seats for up to 52 passengers, as well as provisioned for up to 24 casualties in [litters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litter_\(rescue_basket\)) along with 12 seated personnel and an attendant in a [medical evacuation](146346.md) configuration.[^cite_note-AFM_73-7]



The unusual engine placement on the An-72, which permits the use of the [Coandă effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coand%C4%83_effect) to achieve improved [STOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STOL) performance

On 31 August  1977, the prototype An-72 performed its [maiden flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_flight); its STOL capabilities were not used during the initial flight.[^cite_note-sweetman_1978-2] At the time, there was no other aircraft in the same class as the An-72 on account of its capabilities and the limited perceived market for such a specialised airlifter in Western nations. Despite this, its export prospects were hindered from the onset due to the type having been developed in isolation from non-Soviet certificating authorities.[^cite_note-sweetman_1978-2] Various refinements and modifications were made between the first prototype and production aircraft; these included the lengthening of the fuselage and an increase in wing span.[^cite_note-AFM_73-7] By 1990, quantity production of the An-72 had commenced, 20 aircraft were being completed each year prior to the dissolution of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in the early 1990s.[^cite_note-AFM_73-7]

Produced in tandem with the An-72 was the An-74 derivative. This aircraft is oriented around operations under the inhospitable weather conditions in polar regions, for which it can be outfitted with wheel-skis landing gear, [de-icing equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_protection_system), and a number of other upgrades, allowing the aircraft to support activities in [Arctic](36971.md) or [Antarctic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antarctic) environments.[^cite_note-AFM_73-7] Other An-72 versions include the An-72S VIP transport and An-72P [maritime patrol aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_patrol_aircraft). The An-72's STOL performance includes a take-off roll of 620 m (2,000 ft) and a landing distance of 420 m (1,400 ft).[^cite_note-8]

## Variants



Russian Border Guard An-72P at [Vladivostok Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladivostok_Airport)

- **[An-71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-71)** "Madcap": Prototype AWACS aircraft developed from the An-72.

- **An-72** "Coaler-A": Preproduction aircraft. Two flying prototypes, one static test airframe and eight preproduction machines.

- **An-72A** "Coaler-C": Initial production STOL transport with a longer fuselage and increased wingspan.

- **An-72AT** – "Coaler-C": Freight version of the An-72A compatible with standard international shipping containers.

- **An-72S** – "Coaler-C": Executive [VIP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Very_Important_Person) transport fitted with a galley in a front cabin, work and rest areas in a central cabin, and 24 armchairs in a rear cabin, can also be reconfigured for transporting freight or 38 passengers or as an air ambulance carrying eight stretchers.

- **An-72P**: Patrol aircraft. Armed with one 23 millimetres (0.91 in) [GSh-23L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSh-23L) cannon plus bombs and/or rockets.

  [^cite_note-10]

- **An-72R** (also known as **An-72BR**): Prototype electronic intelligence (ELINT) aircraft, with conformal antenna fairings running up each side of the fuselage.

  [^cite_note-AirVectors-11]

  &#x20;Known as 'Aircraft 88' during development and erroneously known as **An-88**.

- **An-72-100**: Demilitarized An-72.

  [^cite_note-AirVectors-11]

- **An-72-100D**: Demilitarized An-72S.

  [^cite_note-AirVectors-11]

- **[An-74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-74)**: Arctic/Antarctic support model with room for five crew, increased fuel capacity, larger radar in bulged nose radome, improved navigation equipment, better de-icing equipment, and can be fitted with wheel-skis [landing gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_gear).

## Accidents and incidents

As of May 2022, there had been 24 known accidents and incidents involving the An-72 or An-74; of these, seven were reported to involve fatalities:[^cite_note-18]

- 10 February  1995 — An Antonov An-72 [chase plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chase_plane) had a [mid-air collision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Borodianka_mid-air_collision) while following the [Antonov An-70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-70) prototype aircraft during a test flight. The collision caused the An-70 to crash into a forested area, killing all seven An-70 crew members.

  [^cite_note-An70Crash-19]

  &#x20;The An-72 lost a right [wing flap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_flap),

  [^cite_note-An70Blame-20]

  &#x20;but it was able to return to base safely.

  [^cite_note-An70Crash-19]

- 22 December  1997 — ER-ACF, an Antonov An-72 [disappeared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Renan_Antonov_An-72_disappearance) on a cargo flight from [Port Bouet Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Bouet_Airport), [Côte d'Ivoire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%B4te_d'Ivoire) to [Rundu Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rundu_Airport), [Namibia](21292.md). The aircraft and its five crew members disappeared without a trace over the [South Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic). The cause of the incident remains undetermined.

  [^cite_note-Renan-21]

- 21 April 2002 — ES-NOP, an Antonov An-72 leased by [Enimex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enimex) and operated by [Trigana Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigana_Air) was damaged in a hard landing at [Wamena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wamena_Airport), [Indonesia](14579.md) and a minor fire broke out. Due to the nearest fire truck being inoperable, some firefighters ran to the accident scene with hand-held fire extinguishers. The delayed response led to unrecoverable damage to the aircraft, which had to be written off. There were no fatalities.

  [^cite_note-22]

- 25 December  2012 — An An-72 carrying Kazakhstani border patrol officials [crashed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Kazakhstan_Antonov_An-72_crash) in [Shymkent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shymkent), killing all 27 people on board.

  [^cite_note-23]

  [^cite_note-24]

- 10 October  2019 — An An-72 operated for the [DRC Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_of_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) with 4 crew and 4 passengers crashed in [Congo](76762.md); none survived.

  [^cite_note-25]

  [^cite_note-26]

- 12 December  2024 — An An-72 operated by the [Russian Navy](383258.md) exploded at the [Ostafyevo airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostafyevo_International_Business_Airport) in the Moscow region. The explosion occurred from the detonation of the main power unit. The value of the destroyed aircraft was approximately US\$4.5 million.

  [^cite_note-27]

- 2 April 2026 — An An-72P variant in [Kirovske](59753781.md) operated by the Russian military was hit with a Ukrainian FP-2 missile by the [Unmanned Systems Forces](76045069.md).

  [^cite_note-28]
