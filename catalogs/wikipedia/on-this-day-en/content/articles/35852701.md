The **Musée du Pays Châtillonnais**, or **Trésor de Vix**, formerly called the musée archéologique de [Châtillon-sur-Seine](1070333.md) (Côte-d'Or), was created in the late nineteenth century and is managed by the community of communes of the [Pays Châtillonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pays_Ch%C3%A2tillonnais?action=edit\&redlink=1). The museum houses the finding of the [Vix Grave](8081269.md), and especially the famous [Vix krater](8081269.md), dated to circa 500 BCE and testifying to the links between the Gauls and the Greeks at that period.[^cite_note-1]

## History



The old museum building, July 2008

Throughout France the second half of the nineteenth century was an era of growing interest in archeology. The subject was particularly popular in the [Côte-d'Or](90544.md) region since major excavations had been organized by [Napoleon III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon_III) on the site of [Alesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alesia_\(city\)), 80 kilometres (50 mi) from Châtillon. In 1882, the Châtillon Archaeological and Historical Society opened the first museum, to be a museum of archaeology. The region is rich in archaeological relics. The excavations of the Gallo-Roman [Vertillum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertillum), about fifteen miles away, and those of Mount Lassois, produced a large number of objects.

In 1928 the society acquired a Renaissance hotel downtown, the hotel Philandrier, named after William Philandrier, an architect born in Chatillon in the 16th century. In 1950 the museum expanded with a move into the hotel, which provided an exhibition area of 400 square metres (4,300 sq ft). Three years later the discovery of the [Vix Grave](8081269.md), a burial of prime importance for its richness and historical interest, brought international recognition to the museum. It was able to collect all the treasures of the site, which otherwise would probably have been taken to the [National Archaeological Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Archaeological_Museum_\(France\)) at [Saint-Germain-en-Laye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Germain-en-Laye).

In 2009, the museum left the hotel Philandrier, which had become too cramped, and moved to the old abbey of Notre-Dame de Châtillon. Founded in the 12th century by [Bernard of Clairvaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_of_Clairvaux) and rebuilt in the 17th and 18th centuries, the chateau had until recently been a hospital and nursing home. The design of the new museum was assigned to the architect [Antoine Stinco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antoine_Stinco). The new museum site has 2,000 square metres (22,000 sq ft) in a prestigious setting.[^cite_note-2] Taking advantage of the move, the museum reorganized its collection, which now covers the entire prehistory and history of the region up to the 18th century.

## Collections

- Prehistory: Numerous chance finds, excavations of mount Lassois Mount and the Cave of La Grande Baume at [Balot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balot,_C%C3%B4te-d'Or).

- Early History: The [graves of Sainte-Colombe-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graves_of_Sainte-Colombe-sur-Seine), the Treasure of Vix with the famous [Cratère de Vix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crat%C3%A8re_de_Vix). Deposits from the source of Douix.

- The Gallo-Roman world: The city of Vertillum, the sanctuary of Essarois and the sanctuary of Tremblois at [Villiers-le-Duc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villiers-le-Duc).

- The Middle Ages: The Art of Fontenay Abbey and the Abbey of Notre-Dame de Châtillon, the relics of St. Vorles.

- From the Renaissance to the eighteenth century through many objects of daily life, business and the metal industry.

- [Marshal Marmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshal_Marmont), a native of Chatillon, for whom there is a memory room.

The museum opened its doors to the new site on 4 July 2009. There are experiments in the DIVINE system, a visitor assistance program with interactive multimedia content delivered by Wi-Fi.[^cite_note-3]
