# Law of Serbia

&#x20;The **Law of Serbia** is the [system of legal rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law) in force in [Serbia](29265.md), and in the international community it is a member of. Serbian [legal system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_legal_systems) belongs mainly to the Germanic branch of continental legal culture ([civil law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\))). Major areas of [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_law) and [private law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_law) are divided into branches, among them civil, criminal, administrative, family and labour law.

Serbia is the fourth modern-day European country, after [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_France), [Austria](25135167.md), and the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_the_Netherlands), to have a codified legal system.[^cite_note-1]

Written law is the basis of the legal order, and the most important [source of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_of_law) are: [Constitution of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Serbia), [legal regulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation) ([acts of parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Parliament)), [international treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) (once they have been [ratified](429650.md) by the parliament and [promulgated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promulgation)), and such findings of the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Serbia), in which a law or its part has been nullified as [unconstitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionality).[^cite_note-2]

## Sources of law

Sources of Serbian law are (in this hierarchical order):

- the Constitution ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): Устав, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Serbian): *Ustav*) and constitutional acts ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): уставни закон, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Serbian): *ustavni zakon*)

- international treaties ratified by the [National Assembly](619753.md) ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): ратификовани међународни уговори, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Serbian): *ratifikovani međunarodni ugovori*)

- laws adopted by the National Assembly ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): закони, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Serbian): *zakoni*)

- published decisions of the Constitutional Court ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): одлуке Уставног суда, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Serbian): *odluke Ustavnog suda*)

- derived legislation: government decrees ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): уредбе Владе, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Serbian): *uredbe Vlade*) and ministerial decisions ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): одлуке министарстава, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Serbian): *odluke ministarstava*); legislative acts of territorial self-government bodies: provincial government decrees ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): уредбе Покрајинске владе, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Serbian): *uredbe Pokrajinske vlade*) and city/municipal ordinances ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): градске/општинске уредбе, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Serbian): *gradske/opštinske uredbe*)

Acts of parliament and other legal regulations enter into force on the day they are [promulgated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promulgation) (published) in the "Official Gazette of the Republic of Serbia" ([Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language): Службени гласник Републике Србије, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Serbian): *Službeni glasnik Republike Srbije*), although they may take effect at a later date. International treaties are similarly published in the "Official Gazette of the Republic of Serbia".[^cite_note-about-3]
