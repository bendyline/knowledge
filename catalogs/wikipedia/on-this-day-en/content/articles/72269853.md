**Pieter Gerard** "**Gian**" **van Veen**[^cite_note-5] (born 23 April 2002) is a Dutch professional [darts](397713.md) player who competes in [Professional Darts Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Darts_Corporation) (PDC) events, where he is ranked [world number three](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDC_Order_of_Merit). He won the [2025 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_European_Championship_\(darts\)) and is the reigning back-to-back [PDC world youth champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDC_World_Youth_Championship). He was also the runner-up at the [2026 PDC World Championship](80462029.md).

In youth darts, Van Veen won the [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_PDC_World_Youth_Championship) and [2025 PDC World Youth Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_PDC_World_Youth_Championship), as well as nine [PDC Development Tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDC_Development_Tour), including six titles in 2023 to top the [2023 Development Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_PDC_Development_Tour_series) rankings. His first deep run at a major tournament came at the [2023 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_European_Championship_\(darts\)), where he reached the semi-finals. In 2025, he won his first PDC ranking title at [Players Championship 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_PDC_Players_Championship_series#Players_Championship_6) and his first PDC major title at the 2025 European Championship, defeating [Luke Humphries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Humphries) in the final on both occasions. The following year, having lost his opening match in his two prior [World Championship](6920895.md) appearances, Van Veen reached the 2026 PDC World Championship final, losing to [Luke Littler](70129559.md).

## Career

### 2022

Van Veen attended [2022 PDC Pro Tour Qualifying School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_PDC_Pro_Tour#Q-School). He reached the final stage but lost his opening games on the first two days, losing 6–3 to [Mario Vandenbogaerde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Vandenbogaerde) in the last 16 in event three[^cite_note-6] and to [Ricardo Pietreczko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Pietreczko) 6–4 in the last 32 in event four.[^cite_note-7] This meant he failed to gain a [PDC Tour Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDC_Tour_Card) but gained access to the secondary tour, the [2022 Challenge Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_PDC_Challenge_Tour_series).

On 15 July 2022, Van Veen won his only [PDC Challenge Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDC_Challenge_Tour) (CT) title to-date at [CT12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_PDC_Challenge_Tour_series), defeating [David Pallett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Pallett) 5–4 in a deciding leg in the final.[^cite_note-CT11&12-8]

While participating in the [Challenge Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDC_Challenge_Tour), Van Veen suffered with [dartitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartitis), a neurological condition which makes it difficult to release a dart. Van Veen has spoken out about it, stating that dartitis actually made him play better, and that an opponent had accused him of cheating while he suffered with it.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

On 4 November 2022, Van Veen reached the final of the [Players Championship 29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_PDC_Players_Championship_series#Players_Championship_29) event, losing 4–8 to [Gerwyn Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerwyn_Price) in the final.[^cite_note-11]

### 2023

In January 2023, he attended [2023 PDC Pro Tour Qualifying School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_PDC_Pro_Tour#Q-School) (Q–School) and won a [PDC Tour Card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDC_Tour_Card) by finishing ninth on the European Q–School Order of Merit.[^cite_note-DayFour-12]

On 27 August, he reached his second Pro Tour final. He finished as the runner-up at [Players Championship 18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_PDC_Players_Championship_series#Players_Championship_18), being defeated by [Gerwyn Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerwyn_Price) 8–1 in the final.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

He qualified for the [2023 European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Championship_\(darts\)), held in late-October, and reached his first PDC major semi-final. He achieved victories against [Damon Heta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damon_Heta), [Daryl Gurney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daryl_Gurney) and [Michael van Gerwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_van_Gerwen). He lost to [James Wade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Wade) 11–9 in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-15]

Van Veen won 6 titles and topped the [2023 Development Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_PDC_Development_Tour_series) Order of Merit, ahead of [Luke Littler](70129559.md).[^cite_note-DT23&24-16] On 26 November, Littler beat Van Veen 6–4 in the [2023 PDC World Youth Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_PDC_World_Youth_Championship) final.[^cite_note-17]

### 2024

Van Veen made his [PDC World Championship](6920895.md) debut in the [2024 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_PDC_World_Darts_Championship). He was eliminated in the first round by Hong Kong qualifier [Man Lok Leung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man_Lok_Leung), who overturned a 2–0 deficit to defeat Van Veen 3–2 in a major upset.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

Van Veen won two 2024 Development Tour (DT) titles: [DT5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_PDC_Development_Tour_series#Development_Tour_5) on 25 February, with a 5–0 [whitewash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitewash_\(sport\)) of [Dylan Slevin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_Slevin) in the final;[^cite_note-2024DT5-20] and [DT7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_PDC_Development_Tour_series#Development_Tour_7) on 3 May, with a 5–1 win against [Nathan Rafferty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Rafferty).[^cite_note-DT6-21]

On 22 September 2024, he reached his third Pro Tour final at the [Hungarian Darts Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Hungarian_Darts_Trophy). In his first [PDC European Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDC_European_Tour) final, he finished as runner-up after missing a [match dart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossary_of_darts#Match_dart) at the [bullseye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullseye_\(target\)#darts). He was defeated by [Michael van Gerwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_van_Gerwen) 8–7.[^cite_note-22] In November, he reached his second PDC major quarter-final at the [Grand Slam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Grand_Slam_of_Darts). He was defeated by [Gary Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Anderson_\(darts_player\)) 16–14.[^cite_note-23] Having been defeated by [Luke Littler](70129559.md) in the previous year's final, Van Veen defeated fellow Dutchman [Jurjen van der Velde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurjen_van_der_Velde) 6–5 to win the [2024 World Youth Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_PDC_World_Youth_Championship).[^cite_note-24]

### 2025



Van Veen (right) and his Dutch teammate [Danny Noppert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Noppert) at the [2025 PDC World Cup of Darts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_PDC_World_Cup_of_Darts)

Van Veen entered the second round of the [2025 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_PDC_World_Darts_Championship) as the 28th seed, but lost 3–1 to [Ricardo Pietreczko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Pietreczko).[^cite_note-25] Van Veen made his third [UK Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Open) appearance at the [2025 event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UK_Open). He reached the quarter-finals for the first time, before losing there to world champion [Luke Littler](70129559.md) 10–4.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] He reached the final of [Players Championship 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_PDC_Players_Championship_series#Players_Championship_5) by defeating Luke Littler 7–6 in the semi-finals – ending Littler's 19-match winning streak. In the final, he led 7–4 against [Joe Cullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Cullen_\(darts_player\)) but missed six match darts on his way to an 8–7 loss.[^cite_note-28] The following day, he won his first ranking PDC title by winning [Players Championship 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_PDC_Players_Championship_series#Players_Championship_6), defeating world number one [Luke Humphries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Humphries) 8–3 in the final.[^cite_note-29] He reached a second European Tour final at the [German Darts Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_German_Darts_Grand_Prix), where he lost 8–5 to [Michael van Gerwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_van_Gerwen).[^cite_note-30]

Van Veen represented the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_at_the_PDC_World_Cup) at the [World Cup of Darts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_PDC_World_Cup_of_Darts) for the first time, teaming with [Danny Noppert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Noppert).[^cite_note-31] The pair reached the semi-finals, where they were defeated 8–5 by runners-up [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_at_the_PDC_World_Cup).[^cite_note-32] Following [Players Championship 30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_PDC_Players_Championship_series#Players_Championship_30), he entered the top 16 in the world on the [PDC Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDC_Order_of_Merit) for the first time.[^cite_note-33] At the [European Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_European_Championship_\(darts\)), Van Veen defeated Michael van Gerwen 11–9 to progress to his first PDC major final against Luke Humphries. The match went to a deciding leg, where Humphries missed a match dart and Van Veen completed a 100 checkout to win 11–10 and capture his first PDC major title, resulting in him rising to world number seven.[^cite_note-34]

Van Veen faced Humphries in a rematch in the first round of the [Players Championship Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Players_Championship_Finals), where Van Veen was once again victorious in a deciding leg.[^cite_note-35] He was eliminated by [Krzysztof Ratajski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krzysztof_Ratajski) in the second round. On 23 November, Van Veen won his second [World Youth Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_PDC_World_Youth_Championship) by defeating [Beau Greaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beau_Greaves) 6–3 in the final, becoming the second player to defend the title.[^cite_note-36]

### 2026



Van Veen on night eight of [2026 Premier League Darts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Premier_League_Darts)

At the [2026 World Championship](80462029.md), Van Veen achieved his first win at the event by beating [Cristo Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristo_Reyes) 3–1 in the first round.[^cite_note-37] He followed this with wins over [Alan Soutar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Soutar), [Madars Razma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madars_Razma), [Charlie Manby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Manby), [Luke Humphries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Humphries) and [Gary Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Anderson_\(darts_player\)) to reach the final for the first time in his career,[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] before losing 7–1 to [Luke Littler](70129559.md).[^cite_note-40] Van Veen's success at the tournament saw him rise to a career-high [third in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PDC_Order_of_Merit) and replace [Michael van Gerwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_van_Gerwen) as the Dutch number one.[^cite_note-41] His rise in the world rankings also saw him qualify for the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Premier_League_Darts) for the first time in his career.[^cite_note-42]

Van Veen reached his maiden [World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Series_of_Darts) final at the [Bahrain Darts Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Bahrain_Darts_Masters), where he lost 8–6 to Michael van Gerwen.[^cite_note-43] He faced Luke Littler in a World Championship rematch at the inaugural [Poland Darts Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Poland_Darts_Open), where he hit a [nine-dart finish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine-dart_finish) but was beaten 8–4 in the final.[^cite_note-44] In his first Premier League campaign, Van Veen finished seventh in the league table on 18 points.[^cite_note-45] At the [World Cup of Darts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_PDC_World_Cup_of_Darts), he partnered Van Gerwen for the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_at_the_PDC_World_Cup) and the pair reached the final, where they were defeated 10–5 by [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_at_the_PDC_World_Cup).[^cite_note-46] In July, Van Veen reached his first [World Matchplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_World_Matchplay) semi-final, where he was beaten 17–10 by [Gerwyn Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerwyn_Price).[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

## Personal life

Van Veen was born Pieter Gerard van Veen[^cite_note-49] on 23 April 2002 in [Poederoijen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poederoijen), a village in the Dutch province of [Gelderland](57160.md).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] His parents gave him the name "Gian" in honour of a young boy from their neighbourhood named Gian who had died.[^cite_note-52] He is [colourblind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_blindness).[^cite_note-53] He is a supporter of the Dutch football club [FC Den Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Den_Bosch).[^cite_note-54]

Van Veen studied [aerospace engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineering) and earned a [bachelor's degree in aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Aviation) in 2023.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] He worked a part-time office job near [Amsterdam Airport Schiphol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Airport_Schiphol) before pursuing darts full-time.[^cite_note-57] In 2026, Van Veen underwent surgery to remove [kidney stones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_stones), which caused him to miss a night of the [2026 Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Premier_League_Darts) in [Dublin](8504.md).[^cite_note-58]
