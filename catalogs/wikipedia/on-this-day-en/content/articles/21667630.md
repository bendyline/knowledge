# Holy See–Lebanon relations

**Holy See–Lebanon relations** are foreign relations between the [Holy See](13393.md) and [Lebanon](17771.md). Both countries established [diplomatic relations](28165911.md) in 1947. The Holy See has a [nunciature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nunciature) in [Harissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harissa,_Lebanon). Lebanon has an embassy in [Rome](25458.md).

The Holy See has played a major role in the peace negotiations of Lebanon. It has sought to unify Christian factions that were separated after the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War). At the same time, it sought to reduce Christian-Muslim tensions and to preserve Christian communities that have been declining in many parts of Lebanon and elsewhere in the [Middle East](19323.md).

Cardinal [Nasrallah Sfeir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasrallah_Sfeir), a close friend of the Pope, has had a significant influence in local Lebanese politics. [John Paul II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_II) was widely regarded as a political ally of Lebanon, which he called the *message-country*. He also had a great interest in the nation, stating that it is a "message of freedom and example of [pluralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluralism_\(political_philosophy\))" in 1989.[^cite_note-1]

In April 2021, prime minister-designate [Saad al-Hariri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saad_al-Hariri) visited the [Holy See](13393.md) and met with [Pope Francis](1687680.md), who promised to visit Lebanon, after political differences were set aside. Francis reiterated his support for the country and urged the international community to help Lebanon "to get back on its feet."[^cite_note-2]

In 2025, it was announced by the Holy See that [Pope Leo XIV](54520372.md) would [visit Lebanon](81282363.md) from November 30 to December 2, 2025.[^cite_note-3]
