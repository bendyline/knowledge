The **Ukrainian Premier League** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Українська Прем'єр-ліга [\[ʊkrɐˈjinʲsʲkɐ preˈmjɛr ˈl⁽ʲ⁾iɦɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian)) or **UPL** is a professional [association football](10568.md) league in [Ukraine](31750.md) and the highest level of the [Ukrainian football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_football_league_system).

Originally known as the **Vyshcha Liha** (Вища ліга [\[ˈwɪʃtʃɐ ˈl⁽ʲ⁾iɦɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian), lit. 'Higher League') it was formed in 1991, after the [fall of the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), and the consequent discontinuation of the [Soviet Top League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Top_League).[^cite_note-hgcfdu-1] In the first season in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Vyshcha_Liha), the Ukrainian league included the Ukraine-based clubs that competed previously in the Soviet top three tiers competitions, as well as better clubs of the [Ukrainian republican competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Championship_of_the_Ukrainian_SSR). The initial season of the league featured six former [Soviet Top League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Top_League) clubs among which were [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv), [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk), [Chornomorets Odesa](3435061.md), [Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro), [Metalist Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalist_Kharkiv), [Metalurh Zaporizhzhia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalurh_Zaporizhzhia), as well as four more clubs that previously also competed at the top league. The first edition was played during the first semester of the year, as it was decided to synchronise the Ukrainian football calendar with most European leagues. That first tournament was won by [Tavriya Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Tavriya_Simferopol), a club based in [Crimea](163045.md). That was the only edition of the league not won by either Dynamo or Shakhtar.

The Ukrainian Premier League is also a public organization of professional clubs. In 1996 along with the other professional football leagues of Ukraine, a council of the Vyshcha Liha (Top League) clubs became a member of the [Professional Football League of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Football_League_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-hgcfdu-1][^cite_note-2] In 2008[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] was withdrawn from Professional Football League of Ukraine and reformed into a separate self-governed entity of the [Ukrainian Association of Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Association_of_Football) (previously Football Federation of Ukraine), officially changing its name to the current one.

As a leading club of the [Soviet Top League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Top_League), Dynamo Kyiv dominated the league in the 1990s, winning nine consecutive titles from 1993 to 2001, while since the mid-2000s the league has been contested between Dynamo and Shakhtar Donetsk. Three Ukrainian clubs have reached the finals of European club competitions: Dynamo (as Soviet club), Shakhtar and [Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro). Among Ukrainian fans the most popular Ukrainian clubs are [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) and [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk).[^cite_note-UCPUI27813-5] Other popular clubs include [Karpaty Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Karpaty_Lviv), [Metalist Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalist_Kharkiv), [Chornomorets Odesa](3435061.md) and [Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro).[^cite_note-UCPUI27813-5] The league was ranked the [12th highest in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_coefficient#Current_ranking) by [UEFA](32332.md) in 2021, however since the Russian invasion, the league has fallen to 23rd.

Since 2014, the operation of the league has been disrupted greatly on account of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), worsening with the full scale [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in 2022. The league has been affected by destruction of its sports infrastructure, many Ukrainian players choosing to join the military, and disruption to match attendances with games played behind closed doors, and many other facets of the league's operation.

## General overview and format

The [2023–24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Ukrainian_Premier_League) season is the league's sixteenth after the restructuring of professional club football in 2008 and the 33rd season since the establishing of professional club competitions independent from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). As of 2024, [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk) is the reigning Ukrainian Premier League champion. To summarise, [Tavriya Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Tavriya_Simferopol) won the first championship, while all the subsequent titles have gone to either [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) or [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk). Only 2 teams, [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) and [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk), have participated in all previous 33 Ukrainian Top League competitions. The central feature of the league is a game between the same Dynamo and Shakhtar, which developed into the [Klasychne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klasychne_derby) (*Classic*).

On 15 April 2008 the new Premier-Liha (*Premier League*) was formed. It consists of 12 football clubs that take control of the league's operations under the statues of [Football Federation of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_Ukraine), [UEFA](32332.md), and [FIFA](11049.md). With the new reorganization the format of the League was preserved, while the changes that were made were exclusively administrative. Competitions continued to be conducted in a [double round robin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_round_robin) format among 16 clubs. There were a couple of seasons when the league experimented with a 14 club composition.

In 2014, the league was reduced to 12 members, while its format has changed. The season is still being played in a double round robin in the first half of a season, after which the league splits in half into two groups of six teams. Both the top six and the bottom six play another a double round robin tournament with the clubs of their grouping. For 2019-20 a post-season play-off for qualification for the European club competitions was introduced.

The teams that reach the top ranks of the competition table at the end of each season, gain the chance to represent [Ukraine](31750.md) internationally in several prestigious tournaments (continental club tournaments). At the end of the season, the bottom clubs (usually two) are relegated to the [First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_First_League), part of the lower [Professional Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Football_League_of_Ukraine), and are replaced by the top clubs from that league. All the participants of the Premier League enter the [National Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Cup) competition and enter it at the round of 32 (1/16th of the final) or Round of 16 stage.

The winner of the league at the beginning of every next season plays against the winner of the National Cup for the [Ukrainian Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Super_Cup), under administration of the Premier Liha. Besides the Super Cup game and the Premier Liha itself, the league conducts competitions among junior teams, including under 21s and under 19s. The champion of the under 19 championship qualifies for the [UEFA Youth League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Youth_League).

As with the old emblem, the new emblem contains 16 stars. For the 2016–17 season, the sponsor's name was added.

### Season's format and regulations

Season regulations are one of the two most important documents (other being the competition calendar) that are adopted by the Premier League prior to each season.

The Premier League directly organizes and conducts competitions among member clubs. Competitions are conducted on the principle of "Fair play" and according to the competition calendar which is approved by the Premier League General Assembly and the FFU Executive Committee 30 days before start of competitions. Until 2019 all advertisement, commercial rights and rights on TV and radio broadcasting of games of championship and cup belong to the club that hosts them (the Super Cup of Ukraine and the "Gold game"). All advertisement, commercial rights and rights on TV and radio broadcasting of the game of Super Cup and the "Gold game". Before 2014 Premier League was also administering some rounds of the [Ukrainian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Cup) (round of 8, quarterfinals, and semifinals). The earlier rounds were administered by the Professional League and the final by the Federation. Since 2014 the organization of Ukrainian Cup competitions in full belongs exclusively to the Federation.

There are currently 12 club members of the league. All participants get approved by the Premier League General Assembly. Each club fields each team for senior competitions, and competitions for under 21 and under 19 teams (three teams). A club is required to have a stadium (registered with FFU) and an education and training facility (or center). A club is also obligated to finance its own youth sports institution and a complex scientific-methodical group as well as to own and finance a number of youth teams. A Premier League club needs to ensure the participation of at least four youth teams (ages groups between 14 and 17) in the Youth Football League of Ukraine. A club cannot field more than one team for a certain competition.

All club's staff members (coaches, physicians, massage specialists) have to be contracted and be UEFA licensed. All coaches should have A-diploma, while head coaches – PRO-diploma. Football players are listed in "A" and "B" rosters. "A" roster contains no more than 25 players, while "B" roster has unlimited number of players no older than 21 who have professional contracts or agreements for sports training. The 25-players "A" roster includes the number of slots allotted for players developed by the club.

During breaks in competitions in summer and winter there are two periods for registering players.

Beside the main championship among senior teams, the Premier League also organizes youth championship which was adopted from the previous Vyshcha Liha championship of doubles (reserves). Since 2012 there was added another competition for junior teams, so the original youth championship was renamed into the Championship of U-21 teams and the new competition was named as the Championship of U-19 teams. Unlike the Championship of U-21 teams, in the Championship of U-19 teams beside all of the Premier League clubs' junior teams, there also compete teams of some lower leagues' clubs.

The league's championship among senior teams is conducted by manner of the [round robin](939742.md) system in two cycles "fall-spring" with one game at home and another at opponent's field with each participant. A competition calendar is formed after a draw that is conducted based on the Premier League club rankings. The calendar of the second cycle repeats the first, while hosting teams are switched. There should be no less than two calendar days between official games of a club. All games take place between 12\:00 and 22\:00 local time. Any game postponement is allowed only in emergencies and on decision of the Premier League Administration (Dyrektsiya). Game forfeitures are controlled by technical win/loss nominations and fines, followed by additional sanctions of the FFU Control-Disciplinary Committee, and possible elimination from the league.

### Competition calendar

Clubs play each other twice (once at home and once away) in the 26-match season. The league begins in mid-July and ends in mid-June. After 13 rounds of fixtures, there is a winter break that lasts for three months (from early December to early March). Thus, the winter break is significantly longer than the interval between seasons. This schedule accounts for climatic conditions and matches that of most European leagues in terms of the beginning and the end of the season.

The [first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Ukrainian_Premier_League) of the League in 1992 was an exception, as it lasted only half a year. This was because the last [Soviet league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Top_League) season ended in the autumn of 1991, and the [Football Federation of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_Ukraine) decided to shift the calendar from "spring-fall" to "fall-spring" football seasons. In the inaugural season, 20 clubs were divided into two 10-team groups. In both groups, each club played each other twice, and the championship was decided by a play-off match between the group winners, in which [Tavriya Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Tavriya_Simferopol) surprised the pre-season favorite [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv).

After the first season, in each of the following seasons each team played each other team in the League twice. The number of participating teams fluctuated between 14 and 18, stabilizing since [2002–03 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_Ukrainian_Premier_League) at 16.

As of the [2005–06 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_Ukrainian_Premier_League), the golden match rule was introduced. According to the rule, if the first two teams obtain the same number of points, the championship is to be decided by an additional "golden" match between the two teams. In fact, in that season [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) and [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk) had earned the same number of points and Shakhtar won the championship by winning the golden match (2–1 after extra time).

## History

See also: [Football in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Ukraine), [Soviet Top League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Top_League), [Championship of the Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Championship_of_the_Ukrainian_SSR), and [Football Federation of the Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_the_Ukrainian_SSR)

### Creation

Before 1992, Ukrainian teams played in the Soviet league system, and Dynamo Kyiv enjoyed great success, with the team being the team with the most Soviet league titles at 13. The only other Ukrainian teams that won the Soviet league were Dnipro and [Zorya Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Zorya_Luhansk). Shakhtar Donetsk was never able to win the Soviet Top League.

With the Soviet Union falling apart in late 1991,[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] discussion arose about the creation of a separate Ukrainian league which would only include the top Ukrainian clubs. Following the failed [1991 coup d'état attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Soviet_coup_attempt) against [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev), the [Ukrainian parliament](844993.md) [declared independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine) and set a date for an [independence referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum) to confirm the decision, which was ratified by the Ukrainian people.

Despite the failed putsch and declaration of independence by number of Soviet union republics, the [Football Federation of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_the_Soviet_Union) continued planning the 1992 football season.[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] In September 1991, the Soviet magazine "Futbol" published some comments from head coaches of Ukrainian clubs playing in the [Soviet First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Soviet_First_League), with those being Tavriya and Bukovyna.[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] Tavriya head coach [Anatoliy Zayaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoliy_Zayaev) said that the club was strongly against participation in Ukrainian championship and intended to continue to play in Soviet championship.[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] The Bukovyna head coach [Yukhym Shkolnykov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yukhym_Shkolnykov) said that the club did not have any wishes to return to the Ukrainian group as planned by the republican federation and no one should let politics transverse football.[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] On May 1, 2024, Ukrainian journalist [Ihor Tsyhanyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ihor_Tsyhanyk?action=edit\&redlink=1) released a video-interview where he claimed that one of motivations to conduct the championship in the spring of 1992 was a financial situation of [FC Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv). He pointed to the fact that Dynamo won the [1990 Soviet Top League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Soviet_Top_League) and qualified for the [1991–92 European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991%E2%80%9392_European_Cup) where it received prize money by participating in the last season of the European Cup before the competition rebranded as the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md). Tsyhanyk also mentioned that Dynamo was losing money due to poor business management of player transfers, but did not mention that in the Soviet period, when transferred of players was finally allowed, it was carried through a special department of the [Football Federation of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_the_Soviet_Union), and Soviet clubs would receive only a portion of the transfer money. According to Tsyhanyk the administration of Dynamo along with the [Football Federation of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_Ukraine), members of which were former players of the club saw an opportunity to gain financial support by fielding Ukrainian club which had a high ranking in the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md).

In October 1991 some Moscow press took a big interview from [FC Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro) head coach [Yevhen Kucherevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevhen_Kucherevsky) titled "How to live on?" His direct speech had started with a phrase "Dnipro is definitely for the Soviet championship".[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] Next Yevhen Mefodiyich told about possible isolation of Ukrainian football, because if Ukraine would not be recognized by the world, there would be no membership in FIFA or UEFA.[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] After that recalling some kind of World Basket League, Kucherevsky discussed the topic that "people are uniting, but we..."[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] When questioned "what is the mood among coaches of other Ukrainian teams", he firmly answered: "Almost all are for the united championship and against separate Ukrainian".[^cite_note-uaf160414-26]

In particular, Kucherevsky mentioned his talks with head coach of Shakhtar [Valeriy Yaremchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeriy_Yaremchenko).[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] According to Kucherevsky, the majority of Dnipro's fans, judging by their letters and telephone calls also consider that conducting of Ukrainian championship was not in time.[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] Ended his interview Kucherevsky with a phrase that "he wants to hope that the situation when they have to play in a separate championship will never come".[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] The coach even allowed the thought that Ukraine could be recognized as an independent state, but proposes an idea of the "Soviet open championship", referring to... the case with NHL.[^cite_note-uaf160414-26]

In general, Kucherevsky was speaking of true situation.[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] Among all Ukrainian teams of the [Soviet Top League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Soviet_Top_League), only Dynamo was clearly and firmly for its own independent championship. Other clubs took position from "strongly against" to "possibly for, but".[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] For example, Metalurh Zaporizhzhia that was playing its first season at such level was for the Soviet championship.[^cite_note-uaf160414-26] Yet, Metalist that was struggling to stay in, took a tricky position: "If we are would relegate to the First Union League, we will be for Ukrainian championship, if we would stay at the top, we will be for Soviet championship".[^cite_note-uaf160414-26]

In September 1991 there took place a session of the Football Federation of the Ukrainian SSR Executive Committee ([ispolkom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ispolkom)), which started with raising of blue-and-yellow flag that was given by a member of parliament [Vyacheslav Chornovil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Chornovil).[^cite_note-fanatmotornyi-27] On proposition of [Viktor Bannikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Bannikov) who at time was heading the football federation, the struggle for independent championship had to take place under national colors.[^cite_note-fanatmotornyi-27] The executive committee decided that blue-and-yellow flags had to flown over all stadiums where were playing Ukrainian teams.[^cite_note-fanatmotornyi-27] Some members of the executive committee have spoken about the independent Ukrainian championship, but did not rush with a decision.[^cite_note-fanatmotornyi-27] For that it was decided to wait until the Federation's [plenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plenum_\(meeting\)) on 13–14 December 1991.[^cite_note-fanatmotornyi-27]

### Vyshcha Liha and Professional Football League (1992–1999)

After the [fall of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Soviet_Union), the inaugural independent championship took place hastily at the start of spring 1992 after the creation of the Ukrainian Higher League ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Вища Ліга, Vyshcha Liha). The League was created out of the six teams that took part in the [Soviet Top League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Top_League), two teams from the [Soviet First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_First_League), and nine out of the eleven Ukrainian teams from the [Soviet Second League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Second_League). The other two of that eleven were placed in the [Ukrainian First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_First_League) as they were to be relegated anyway. The two best teams of the [Soviet Second League B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Second_League_B) of the *Ukrainian Zone* were also placed in the Higher League along with the winner of the 1991 Ukrainian Cup which finished ninth in the same group (Soviet Second League B).

The 20 participants were split into two groups with the winners playing for the championship title and the runners-up playing for third place. Three teams from each group were to be relegated. As expected, the five favorites, *[Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv)*, *[Dnipro Dnipropetrovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro_Dnipropetrovsk)*, *[Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk)*, *[Chornomorets Odesa](3435061.md)*, and *[Metalist Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalist_Kharkiv)* finished at the top of each group. In the championship play-off game in [Lviv](49968.md), a sensation took place as [Tavriya Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Tavriya_Simferopol) beat [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) 1–0. The Crimeans earned the first Ukrainian title, the only one in their history, with Tavriya only losing once, to [Temp Shepetivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Temp_Shepetivka). To this date, this is the only title that was not won either by Dynamo or Shakhtar. Tavriya went extinct in 2014.

After being stunned in the first championship by Tavriya Simferopol in [Lviv](49968.md), [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) were anxious to earn their first title at the second opportunity. In the second Ukrainian championship, which had a regular League format of 16 teams, their main rivals were [Dnipro Dnipropetrovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro_Dnipropetrovsk), who were top after the first half of the season. By the end of the season both teams were neck and neck and they finished with the same number of points, and [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) won the title on goal difference.

The next eight years were known as the total domination of [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv). During this period 'the main Soviet protagonists' had changed as some of the best teams were facing a crisis. After the [1993–94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_Ukrainian_Premier_League) season [Metalist Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalist_Kharkiv) were surprisingly relegated to the [First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_First_League). In the [1995–96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_Ukrainian_Premier_League) season [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk) had the worst year in the club's history, coming tenth. [Chornomorets Odesa](3435061.md) were relegated twice during that first decade after which manager [Leonid Buryak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Buryak) was sacked. A few newly created teams have since emerged such as [Arsenal Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Arsenal_Kyiv) and [Metalurh Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalurh_Donetsk), as well as [Vorskla Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Vorskla_Poltava), who surprisingly came third in the club's first season at the Top Level in the [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_Ukrainian_Premier_League).

### Dynamo–Shakhtar rivalry and Premier League (2000–2010)

See also: [Ukrainian derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_derby)

The next decade was marked by fierce competition between [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) and [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk). Since 2000, [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk) has proved to be the real challengers to Dynamo. In 2000, Shakhtar earned their first qualification to the Champions League earning a place in the group stage. Nonetheless, [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) is still considered to be the benchmark of excellence in the country and the primary feeder to the Ukraine national football team. 2002 became the real cornerstone in the *miners* history when they earned their first national title under the management of the newly appointed Italian specialist, [Nevio Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevio_Scala), who managed to secure the [Ukrainian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Cup) title as well. Since that time the issue of foreign players has become particularly acute and brought a series of court cases. The [FFU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_Ukraine) and [PFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Football_League_of_Ukraine) worked together to solve that issue, coming up with a plan to force the *transitional* limitation of foreign players over time.

The clubs such as [Dnipro Dnipropetrovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro_Dnipropetrovsk) and [Chornomorets Odesa](3435061.md), who were recent contenders for the title, had to put up a fierce fight against the newly established contenders [Metalurh Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalurh_Donetsk) and [Metalist Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalist_Kharkiv) to qualify for the European competitions. [Metalist Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalist_Kharkiv) shone brightly in the late 2000s (decade) by consistently finishing right behind [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) and [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk) in third place. Their most remarkable feat was their participation in the 2009 European season when they had to face [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) to earn a place in the quarter-finals of the [2008–09 UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_UEFA_Cup), but lost on the away goals rule. That same [2008–09 UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_UEFA_Cup_Final) competition was won for the first time by [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk), the first club of independent Ukraine to win the title. It was also the last UEFA cup title before it changed its name to the Europa league. In the 2008–09 season the league earned the highest [UEFA league coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_coefficient#Current_ranking) in Europe for that season.



Aerial duel between players of Shakhtar and Metalist in September 2009 including [Fernandinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernandinho_\(footballer,_born_May_1985\)) and [Marko Devic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marko_Devic)

On 15 November 2007 clubs' presidents of the Vyshcha Liha adopted a decision to create the Premier League (Premier Liha).[^cite_note-aftwad-28] At the same meeting session there was created a supervisory board that consisted of [Ravil Safiullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravil_Safiullin) (Professional Football League), [Vitaliy Danilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaliy_Danilov) (FC Kharkiv), [Petro Dyminskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro_Dyminskyi) (FC Karpaty), and [Vadym Rabinovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadym_Rabinovych) (FC Arsenal).[^cite_note-aftwad-28] During the next three months that body curated a process on creation of the Premier League's regulation and statute as well as a procedure of launching the championship starting from the 2008–09 season.[^cite_note-aftwad-28] On 15 April 2008 at one of the meetings among the presidents of clubs there was signed a protocol about establishing the Association of Professional Football Clubs of Ukraine "Premier-Liha"[^cite_note-aftwad-28] as an autonomous entity, parting away from the PFL.

The Premier League has been split since the moment it was created in regards to its president. The dispute went as far as even canceling the 13th round of [2009–10 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Ukrainian_Premier_League) and moving it to the spring half, while having the 14th round still playing in the fall. The representatives of five clubs: [Arsenal Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Arsenal_Kyiv), [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv), [Dnipro Dnipropetrovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro_Dnipropetrovsk), [Kryvbas Kryvyi Rih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Kryvbas_Kryvyi_Rih_\(1959%E2%80%932013\)), and [Metalist Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalist_Kharkiv) have been boycotting most of the League meetings, not complying with its financial obligations and giving the broadcasting rights to TV-channels other than the League official supplier. They justified their actions due to what they deem to be the illegal election of the Premier League president. The representatives of the above-mentioned clubs did not recognize the election in 2008 of [Vitaliy Danilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaliy_Danilov) as the president and believed that the elections should have been won by [Vadim Rabinovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadim_Rabinovich).

To resolve this conflict [Vitaliy Danilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaliy_Danilov) instigated the re-election of the Premier League president in September 2009, and on 1 December 2009 won the election again with 11 clubs voting for his candidature, 3 were against, 1 abstained, and 1 was absent. This time most club presidents of the Premier League of Ukraine acknowledged [Vitaliy Danilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaliy_Danilov) legality. In the subsequent elections on 9 December 2011 [Vitaliy Danilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaliy_Danilov) was challenged by Andriy Kurhanskyi (through the proposal of [Karpaty Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Karpaty_Lviv)). The other available candidates, Miletiy Balchos (president of the Professional Football League of Ukraine) and Yuriy Kindzerskyi, were not picked by any members of the Premier League. [Vitaliy Danilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaliy_Danilov) managed to retain his seat with nine votes for him.

Starting from [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009%E2%80%9310_Ukrainian_Premier_League) and to [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Ukrainian_Premier_League) season, FC Shakhtar led by Romanian coach [Mircea Lucescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_Lucescu) obtained five national league titles in a row, making Lucescu the most successful manager in the history of the league with 9 titles. At the same time, in the beginning of the 2010s the so-called *"Big Four"* of clubs eventually formed, consisting from [Shakhtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk), [Dynamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv), [Metalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalist_Kharkiv) and [Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro).[^cite_note-29] These four clubs consecutively took all the top 4 places for five seasons from 2009–10 to 2013–14 and displayed the biggest financial abilities in the league.[^cite_note-30]

In [2012–13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Ukrainian_Premier_League), Metalist Kharkiv finished second and qualified for the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) for the first time, the achievement which was repeated by Dnipro in the next season. In the same 2013–14 season Dynamo Kyiv for the first time since Ukrainian independence placed as low as fourth in league's season ranking, which led to dismissal of former national team coach and the legend of Soviet football [Oleh Blokhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleh_Blokhin) as the club's manager. In European football, new club achievements were set in these years for Shakhtar in [2010–11 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_UEFA_Champions_League) quarter-finals and for Metalist in [2011–12 UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_UEFA_Europa_League) quarter-finals.

### The league during the war in Donbas (2014-2022)



The 2017 Liha Pari-Match champions [FC Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk) with a pennant (Hrayemo Chesno, We Play Fair)

After the start of the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas) in 2014, the number of teams participating in the league was cut from 16 in the [2013–14 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Ukrainian_Premier_League) to 14 in the following two seasons.[^cite_note-ColriU-31] Teams from the Donbas would be forced to play outside the region as a consequence. Both of the seasons were won by Dynamo Kyiv with [Serhii Rebrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serhii_Rebrov) as manager. With the continuation of the [military conflict in the eastern oblasts of Ukraine](42085878.md) since 2014 and its economic impact, the league was forced to change its format again and started to be contested by 12 teams after being cut from 14 after the [2015–16 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Ukrainian_Premier_League), introducing the two stages of the competition: after the standard two rounds of games the league would split into two 6-team groups according to their positions.

Under the new format, Shakhtar Donetsk under the manager [Paulo Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Fonseca) managed to win three league titles in a row from [2016–17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Ukrainian_Premier_League) to [2018–19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%9319_Ukrainian_Premier_League), runner-up in all the three seasons being Dynamo Kyiv. In [2019–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Ukrainian_Premier_League) season, Shakhtar set the record of the earliest title win in the history, with 5 rounds remaining.[^cite_note-32] In 2019, the decision was adopted to expand the league to 14 teams from the [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Ukrainian_Premier_League) and to 16 teams from the 2021–22 season.[^cite_note-33]

### After the Russian invasion of Ukraine (2022-present)

On 24 February 2022, [Russia invaded Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), and the result of this for Ukrainian football was the suspension of football competitions in the country, and on 26 April 2022, it was announced that the [2021-22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Ukrainian_Premier_League) season would be abandoned due to the extension of martial law in Ukraine.[^cite_note-\:0-34] The football clubs of the UPL also expressed their support for the termination, since it would not possible to end the championship due to the state of war in the country. Thus, it was concluded that the standings as of 24 February 2022 would be the final standings of the 2021–22 season, and there would be no champion for the season.[^cite_note-\:0-34]

In the [2022-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Ukrainian_Premier_League) season, the competition restarted, with matches being played behind closed doors and sometimes interrupted by alert sirens. That season ended with Shakhtar Donetsk winning the tournament, which they also won in the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Ukrainian_Premier_League). Dynamo Kyiv won the league in the [2024-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Ukrainian_Premier_League) season.

## Broadcasting

From the spring part of the 2023–24 season, the Ukrainian Premier League launched a new sports channel — UPL.TV, which will be distributed by 1+1 Media distribution. The broadcast of the TV channel includes pre-match and post-match studios, reviews, interviews with football players, coaches and leaders of Ukrainian football.

### International broadcasters

The main international broadcaster of the league in [Western Europe](33800.md) and some countries of [Africa](5334607.md) is the French Ma Chaîne Sport providing coverage for such countries like [France](5843419.md), and many other countries like [Andorra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andorra), [Switzerland](26748.md), [Belgium](3343.md), [Luxembourg](17515.md), [Monaco](19261.md), [Algeria](358.md), [Morocco](19291.md), [Tunisia](30188.md). Another broadcaster [Sport Klub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Klub) provides coverage in all countries of former [Yugoslavia](34244.md) including [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md), [Croatia](5573.md), [North Macedonia](23564616.md), [Serbia](29265.md), and [Slovenia](27338.md). Other broadcasters of the Ukrainian league in other countries include 12 TV ([Armenia](10918072.md)), CBC Sport ([Azerbaijan](746.md)), [Polsat Futbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polsat_Futbol) ([Poland](22936.md)), and Dolce Sport ([Romania](25445.md)).

## UEFA ranking and European competitions

See also: [Ukrainian football clubs in European competitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_football_clubs_in_European_competitions)



Shakhtar Donetsk against [Arsenal](2174.md) in the [2010–11 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_UEFA_Champions_League)

Ukrainian clubs have been competing in European competitions since the 1960s, when the country was part of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). In fact the first Soviet club that took part in European competitions was Ukrainian club, [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv), which took part in the [1965–66 European Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965%E2%80%9366_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup). Before the [fall of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Soviet_Union), the following Ukrainian clubs participated in European competitions: [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv), [Karpaty Lviv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Karpaty_Lviv), [Zorya Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Zorya_Luhansk), [Chornomorets Odesa](3435061.md), [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk), [FC Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro), and [Metalist Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalist_Kharkiv).

At least five clubs participated in top continental competitions the European Cup and the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) among which are [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv), [Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro), [Metalist Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalist_Kharkiv), [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk), and [Tavriya Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Tavriya_Simferopol).

Both Dynamo and Shakhtar were able to win European trophies. Dynamo won two [European Cup Winners' Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) ([1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup) and [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup)) and one UEFA Super Cup in [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_European_Super_Cup), Shakhtar Donetsk won the [UEFA Cup](232175.md) in [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_UEFA_Cup). Dnipro also reached the [2015 UEFA Europa League Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_UEFA_Europa_League_Final), but lost to [Sevilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevilla_FC).

## International relations

In 2009, the Ukrainian Premier League joined the [European Professional Football Leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Professional_Football_Leagues).[^cite_note-47] Also in 2009 the league signed a partnership with [IMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMG_\(company\)) of which during the first month of cooperation sold broadcasting rights for the [Ukrainian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Cup) to Poland and Armenia. On its own initiative, the Ukrainian Premier League sold broadcasting rights to Romania and Russia as well.

### Honored teams

A representative star is placed above the team's badge to indicate 10 league titles.[^cite_note-51] [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) became the first Ukrainian team to achieve the prestigious honor of winning the [Soviet Top League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Top_League) for the 10th time in 1981. [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) after having entered the Ukrainian championship has become the same dominant leader as during the Soviet times by earning its 20th national title at the top level in 1999. The two stars were added to the club's logo in 2007.[^cite_note-52] Earning its 10th national title in 2017, Shakhtar Donetsk has not yet adopted a star on its crest. After winning the 2024–25 season, Dynamo Kyiv added a star to the club's badge.

Currently (as of 2025) the following clubs earned the star element to be added to their crest.

-    [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) (13 in Soviet Union; 17 in Ukraine).

-  [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk) (16 in Ukraine)

- [Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro) (2 in Soviet Union)

- [Zorya Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Zorya_Luhansk) (1 in Soviet Union)

- [Tavriya Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Tavriya_Simferopol) (1 in Ukraine)

### Prestige trophy

From [2016–17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Ukrainian_Premier_League) to [2019–20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Ukrainian_Premier_League) seasons, the league conducted season competition in two rounds, where after the first double round robin tournament the league is split in half into two groups of six teams. Then, top six play second double round robin for the title, while the bottom six play to determine teams to be relegated (and Europa League playoff participants in the 2019–20 season). The team that won the relegation group receives a consolation-type honorary award, the Prestige trophy.[^cite_note-53]

## Premier League players

Further information: [Football records in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_records_in_Ukraine)

Ex-[Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) strikers [Maksim Shatskikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksim_Shatskikh) and [Serhiy Rebrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serhiy_Rebrov) hold the record for most Ukrainian Premier League goals with 123, with Shatskikh winning the top single season scorer title twice in 1999–2000 and 2002–03, Rebrov once in 1997–98. Since the first Ukrainian Premier League season in 1992, 22 different players have won or shared the top scorer's title. Only five players have won the title more than once, [Tymerlan Huseynov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tymerlan_Huseynov), [Maksim Shatskikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksim_Shatskikh), [Yevhen Seleznyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevhen_Seleznyov), [Alex Teixeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Teixeira) and [Júnior Moraes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BAnior_Moraes).

[Henrikh Mkhitaryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrikh_Mkhitaryan) holds the record for most goals in a season (25), [Serhiy Rebrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serhiy_Rebrov) and [Maksim Shatskikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maksim_Shatskikh) are the only two players to score at least 20 goals twice. The most prolific all-time scorers are [Ivan Hetsko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Hetsko) and [Viktor Leonenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Leonenko), respectively attaining 0.59 and 0.57 goals per game.

The league's record holder for winnings is [Mircea Lucescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mircea_Lucescu).

## Post-season play-offs

There were several instances when the games outside of regular double [round-robin tournament](939742.md) and split group seasons were scheduled or required. They were held either for determining the league position (golden and third place matches), international competitions qualification (Europa League play-off) or promotion or relegation (relegation play-off).

### Golden matches

League finals for Premier League took place on two occasions. In the inaugural [1992 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Vyshcha_Liha), the league was conducted in two groups of 10 teams due to transition to the autumn-spring competition calendar. The teams in each group played a double [round-robin tournament](939742.md), after which the winners of both groups faced each other in the one-match league final at neutral field. The [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Vyshcha_Liha_final) was played on 21 June 1992, crowning [Tavriya Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Tavriya_Simferopol) as the first [champions of independent Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ukrainian_football_champions) after their 1–0 win over [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv).

Starting from the [2005–06 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_Ukrainian_Premier_League), if multiple teams finish tied on points on the top of the table, Golden match is required to determine the champion. In the same season, this rule came into effect for the first and only to the moment time: [Shakhtar Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Shakhtar_Donetsk) and [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv) both finished with 75 points after the regular season. In the following final game, Shakhtar managed to win the title after the 100th-minute goal from [Julius Aghahowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Aghahowa) which concluded their 2–1 [extra-time](633242.md) win.

### Relegation play-offs

For the first time, a play-off to determine the participants of the next Vyshcha Liha season was held unplanned at the end of the [1998–99 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_Ukrainian_Premier_League). Third-placed team of [1998–99 First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998%E2%80%9399_Ukrainian_First_League), [Torpedo Zaporizhzhia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Torpedo_Zaporizhzhia), which was to be promoted, filed for bankruptcy at the end of the season. The league regulations at the time did not specify what actions are needed to be taken in such situation, so [PFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Football_League_of_Ukraine) made a decision to held a play-off game between the highest-ranked relegated team, [Prykarpattia Ivano-Frankivsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Spartak_Ivano-Frankivsk), and highest-ranked not promoted team, [FC Cherkasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro_Cherkasy) at neutral field in Kyiv. The game ended with Prykarpattia defending their league place 3–1.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-rppo12-117]

In the [2001–02 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Ukrainian_Premier_League), due to league enlargement play-off was held between the second-lowest Vyshcha Liha team, [Polihraftekhnika Oleksandriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Oleksandriya), and the fourth [First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_Ukrainian_First_League) team, [Polissya Zhytomyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Polissya_Zhytomyr). The game on a neutral field in Kyiv ended 1–0 in favour of Oleksandriya.[^cite_note-rppo12-117]

Since [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Ukrainian_Premier_League), the play-offs have been held in a home-and-away format between the 10th and 11th teams from the Premier League, and the 2nd and 3rd from the First League. During this time, 3 teams were promoted by play-offs, and another 1 managed to defend its place. However, in the [2019–20 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_Ukrainian_Premier_League) and [2020–21 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_Ukrainian_Premier_League) there were no playoffs because of the league enlargement, and three teams were promoted from the First League directly.[^cite_note-118] In the [2021–22 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Ukrainian_Premier_League) play-offs were not conducted as the championship ceased halfway due to the [2022 Russian full-scale invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).

## Rivalries and city derbies

### Klasychne derby

See also: [Klasychne derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klasychne_derby) and [List of sports derbies in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sports_derbies_in_Ukraine)

The central feature of the league is a rivalry between Shakhtar Donetsk and Dynamo Kyiv which has adopted its name as [Klasychne derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klasychne_derby). The rivalry started ever since the end of the 1990s when both teams started consistently to place the top two places from season to season. The rivalry became really established when Shakhtar obtained its first national title in 2002.

### Other championship contenders

The surprising win of the first season by [SC Tavriya Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Tavriya_Simferopol) has never turned the club into a real contender for another title and the club was not always successful to secure a place among the top five. In the beginning of the 1990s, [FC Chornomorets Odesa](3435061.md) and the two-time Soviet champions [FC Dnipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dnipro) were also among the main contenders. The 1972 Soviet champions [FC Zorya Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Zorya_Luhansk) until 2013 really struggled to stay in the top league. Among other contenders there were [FC Metalist Kharkiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalist_Kharkiv) that were the league's runners-up in 2012–13 and [FC Metalurh Donetsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Metalurh_Donetsk) that showed some consistent form in the early 2000s.

### Other rivalries

There are few smaller regional rivalries such between Karpaty and Volyn, Metalist and Dnipro, Zorya and Shakhtar.

Among city derbies, there were no running city derbies in the league for the 2017–18 season. Among the most notable previously there were [Zaporizhzhia derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_derby?action=edit\&redlink=1) between Metalurh and Torpedo, [Kyiv derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_derby) between Dynamo and Arsenal (CSKA), [Donetsk derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donetsk_derby) between Shakhtar and Metalurh. Other derbies existed in Lviv, Odesa, Kharkiv, [West Ukrainian football derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ukrainian_football_derby) and others.
