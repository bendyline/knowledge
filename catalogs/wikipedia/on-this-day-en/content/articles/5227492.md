The **mile run** (1,760 [yards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yard),[^cite_note-2] 5,280 [feet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_\(unit\)), or exactly 1,609.344 [metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre)) is a [middle-distance](89280.md) [foot race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_race).

The one [mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile) run was a common athletic event in 18th and 19th century [England](9316.md), primarily for [gambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling) purposes.[^cite_note-JB-3] It survived [track and field](194904.md)'s switch to [metric distances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_system) in the 1900s and retained its popularity, with the chase for the [four-minute mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-minute_mile) in the 1950s a high point for the race.

In spite of the roughly equivalent [1500 metres](2707254.md) race, which is used instead of the mile at the [World Championships](218926.md) and [Olympic Games](22576.md) and is sometimes referred as the foremost middle-distance track event in athletics, the mile run is present in all fields of [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_\(sport\)). Since 1976, it is the only [imperial distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_unit) [World Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics) has on its books for official [world records](3443107.md).[^cite_note-4]

Although the mile is not featured at any major championships, the [Wanamaker Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanamaker_Mile), [Dream Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Mile), [Emsley Carr Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emsley_Carr_Mile) and [Bowerman Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowerman_Mile) races are among the foremost annual middle-distance races. The [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games) reintroduced the mile race to its championship athletics program from [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_2026_Commonwealth_Games), in tribute to the tradition of the distance in [Commonwealth nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_nations), and particularly the rivalry between the Englishman [Roger Bannister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Bannister) and his Australian rival [John Landy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Landy) to run the first [four-minute mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-minute_mile).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

The current [mile world record](854191.md) holders are [Josh Kerr](54894149.md) of [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Great_Britain) with a time of 3\:42.66 and [Faith Kipyegon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Kipyegon) of [Kenya](188171.md) with the women's record of 4\:07.64.

Despite being only 109.344 metres longer, the mile is distinctly different from its much more common 1500 metres counterpart. Former world record holder [Hicham El Guerrouj](492488.md) considers the mile to be his more challenging event.[^cite_note-7]

> “My favourite is 1500m. It’s part of my heart. I competed in it a lot and I know every metre of this race. The mile is completely different. If you are not strong physically and mentally, you cannot run it well.”

— Hicham El Guerrouj

Each lap during Kerr's world record run averaged 55.34 seconds per 400 m.[^cite_note-8] Along with Kerr, only four other men in history have broken the 3\:44 barrier in the mile: Hicham El Gerrouj, [Noah Ngeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Ngeny), [Jakob Ingebrigtsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakob_Ingebrigtsen), and [Yared Nuguse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yared_Nuguse).[^cite_note-9]

When the mile is run, the time is often taken at 1500 metres, in effect when entering the home straight. As in the case with Kerr's world record run, runners approaching national, area or world records in the mile distance can break an official 1500 metre record en route; in Kerr's case, breaking his own national 1500 metres record on the way to his mile world record.[^cite_note-10]

## History

Although a [statute mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mile) today is equal to a length of 5,280 feet, the distance of the English mile gained its current definition of 1,760 yards through a statute of the [Parliament of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_England) in 1593.[^cite_note-11] Thus, the history of the mile run began in [England](9316.md) and it initially found usage within the [wagered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling) running contests of the 18th and 19th century. Such contests would attract large numbers of spectators and gamblers – so many that the activity became a [professional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_sports) one for its more-established participants.[^cite_note-JB-3]

By 1790, mile footraces were being wagered on. In September 1790, Wood of [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire) and Harper of [Cheshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheshire) ran a mile in 4 minutes 51 seconds.[^cite_note-12] Later that month, a huntsman improved the time to 4 minutes 39 seconds running [barefoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barefoot_running).[^cite_note-13]



[Gunder Hägg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunder_H%C3%A4gg) (right) defeats [Arne Andersson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arne_Andersson) with a world record time of 4\:06.2 in [Gothenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothenburg), 1942.

The mile run was at the heart of the divide between professional and [amateur sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateur_sports) in the late 19th century, as running was beginning to gain popularity in the sports world. Separate [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_record) categories were kept for amateurs and professionals, with professional runners providing the faster times. High-profile contests between Britons [William Cummings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cummings_\(athlete\)) and [Walter George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_George_\(athlete\)) brought much publicity to the sport, as did George's races against the American [Lon Myers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lon_Myers). The mile run was also one of the foremost events at the amateur [AAA Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AAA_Championships).[^cite_note-JB-3] Although the spotlight was shining on the running scene, the categories remained distinct but the respective rise in amateurism and decline of the professional sector saw the division become irrelevant in the 20th century.[^cite_note-McMillan-14]

Prior to [metrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrication), many tracks in the United States and the [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) were constructed to the specifications of one quarter of a mile, 440 yards (402.336 m). Thus, when the mile was run, the race was four laps. The [Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games) officially converted to metric in the mid-1960s. The [United States adopted metric rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrication_in_the_United_States#Sports) in the mid to late 1970s, though some tracks are still constructed to be a quarter of a mile in length requiring calibrated painted lines to run metric races.



A statue commemorating Roger Bannister and [John Landy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Landy)'s [Miracle Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1954_British_Empire_and_Commonwealth_Games) in 1954

The mile run continued to be a popular distance in spite of the [metrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrication) of [track and field](194904.md) and [athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_\(sport\)) in general, replacing the imperial distance for the metric mile (1500 meters). It was the [1500 metres](2707254.md) – sometimes referred to as the [metric mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metric_mile) – which was featured on the [Olympic athletics programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_Summer_Olympics). The [International Amateur Athletics Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Amateur_Athletics_Federation) formed in 1912 and confirmed the first officially recognised [world record in the mile](854191.md) the following year (4\:14.4 minutes run by [John Paul Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_Jones_\(athlete\))).[^cite_note-IAAFWR-15]

The fact that the mile run was the only [imperial distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_unit) to retain its official world record status after 1976 reflects its continued popularity in the international (and principally metric) era.[^cite_note-16] Decades later, the distance remains widespread, and is often used as a benchmark for distance running performance.

The top men's middle-distance runners continued to compete in the mile run in the first half of the 1900s – [Paavo Nurmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paavo_Nurmi), [Jack Lovelock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Lovelock) and [Sydney Wooderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Wooderson) were all world record holders over the distance.[^cite_note-IAAFWR-15] In the 1940s, [Swedish](5058739.md) runners [Gunder Hägg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunder_H%C3%A4gg) and [Arne Andersson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arne_Andersson) pushed times into a new territory, as they set three world records each during their rivalry over the decade.[^cite_note-Intro-17]

The goal of completing a sub-[four-minute mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-minute_mile) sparked further interest in the distance in the 1950s and to this day, many competitive runners are still chasing the ambitious barrier. Englishman [Roger Bannister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Bannister) became the first person to achieve the feat in May 1954 and his effort, conducted with the help of [Chris Brasher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Brasher) and [Chris Chataway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Chataway), was a key moment in the rise of the use of [pacemakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacemaker_\(running\)) at the top level of the sport – an aspect which is now commonplace at non-championship middle and [long-distance races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_running).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] In fact, pacemakers, if performing effectively, can earn generous sums of money for their performances and accurate pacing duties.

The 1960s saw American [Jim Ryun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Ryun) set world records near the 3\:50-minute mark and his achievements popularised [interval workout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interval_workout) techniques which are still heavily used today, especially for collegiate distance runners.[^cite_note-Intro-17] Jim Ryun was the first person to run a sub-four minute mile in high school.[^cite_note-20] From this period onwards, African runners began to emerge, breaking the largely white, [Western](21208200.md) dominance of the distance; Kenya's [Kip Keino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kip_Keino) won the mile at the [1966 British Empire and Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_1966_British_Empire_and_Commonwealth_Games) (which was among the last mile races to be held at a major [multi-sport event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-sport_event) as of 2021).[^cite_note-21]

[Filbert Bayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filbert_Bayi) of Tanzania became Africa's first world record holder over the distance in 1975, although New Zealander [John Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Walker_\(runner\)) further broke Bayi's record a few months later to become the first man under 3\:50 minutes for the event. The 1980s were highlighted by the rivalry between British runners [Sebastian Coe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Coe) and [Steve Ovett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Ovett), who improved the record five times between them, including two records at the [Oslo Dream Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Dream_Mile) race. [Noureddine Morceli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noureddine_Morceli) brought the mile record back into African hands in 1993 and Morocco's [Hicham El Guerrouj](492488.md) set a world record of 3\:43.13, which has stood from 1999 to 2026.[^cite_note-IAAFWR-15]

Mile run contests remain a key feature of many annual track and field meetings, including recreational, high school, and collegiate meets.

In the United States, particularly in many high school ([NFHS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Federation_of_State_High_School_Associations)) competitions, the [1600 meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1600_meters) is a substitute for the mile run.

On the professional level, races such as the [Wanamaker Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wanamaker_Mile) at the [Millrose Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millrose_Games), the [Dream Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Mile) at the [Bislett Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bislett_Games), the British [Emsley Carr Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emsley_Carr_Mile), and the [Bowerman Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowerman_Mile) at the [Prefontaine Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefontaine_Classic) are among the most prominent. Aside from track races, mile races are also occasionally contested in [cross country running](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_country_running), and mile runs [on the road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_running) include the [Fifth Avenue Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue_Mile) in [New York City](645042.md). However, in high school and collegiate cross country running, races are often measured in kilometers, with 5K and 8K being the most common.

In 2019, [World Athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics) President and former athlete [Sebastian Coe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Coe) organized the World Athletics Heritage Mile Night in [Monaco](19261.md), which brought together eleven mile world record holders, either indoors or outdoors: [Ron Delany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Delany), [Michel Jazy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Jazy), [Jim Ryun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Ryun), [Filbert Bayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filbert_Bayi), [Paola Pigni-Cacchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paola_Pigni), [John Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Walker_\(runner\)), [Eamonn Coghlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eamonn_Coghlan), Coe, [Steve Cram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Cram), [Noureddine Morceli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noureddine_Morceli), and [Hicham El Guerrouj](492488.md). The event posthumously honored [Roger Bannister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Bannister) and [Diane Leather Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diane_Leather), who were the first to break the four-minute and five-minute mile barriers, for men and women respectively.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

In February 2025, it was announced that the mile run would be revived at the [2026 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Commonwealth_Games), replacing the 1500 metres.[^cite_note-\:42-24]

In June 2025, in a [special event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking4) organized by [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.), [Faith Kipyegon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faith_Kipyegon) attempted to become the first woman to break four minutes for the mile. She fell 6.91 seconds short with an unadjusted time of 4\:06.91. Given the use of male pacemakers, the event was not record eligible.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

### Men (road)

Note: World Athletics only accepts times achieved on World Athletics certified courses that are conducted according to World Athletics rules. The elevation gradient must not exceed one meter per kilometer.[^cite_note-\:4-94] For instance, the famous [Fifth Avenue Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Avenue_Mile) in New York City, or New Zealand's Queen Street Golden Mile,[^cite_note-\:6-95] are too steep to be record eligible by World Athletics. Downhill miles such as Craig Wheeler's 3\:24 clocking in the 1993 Meltham Mile[^cite_note-96] and Mike Boit's 3\:28 in the 1983 Queen Street Mile,[^cite_note-97] were achieved on ineligible courses and as such are not recognized by World Athletics. The road mile became an official world record event on 1 September 2023 which is why most performances prior to this date were not previously considered records. The winning times from the 2023 U.S. Road Mile Championships, on 25 April, were ratified by World Athletics as the inaugural road mile world records.[^cite_note-\:5-98]
