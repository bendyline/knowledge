**Comedy horror** (also called **horror comedy**) is a [literary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_genre), [television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_genre) and [film genre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_genre) that combines elements of [comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy) and [horror fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_fiction). Comedy horror has been described as having three types: "[black comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_comedy), [parody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parody) and spoof."[^cite_note-Miller_2004_p._1-1] Comedy horror can also parody or subtly spoof horror [clichés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clich%C3%A9) as its main source of humour or use those elements to take a story in a different direction. Examples of comedy horror films include *[Abbott and Costello Meet Frankenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbott_and_Costello_Meet_Frankenstein)* (1948), *[An American Werewolf in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_American_Werewolf_in_London)* (1981), the *[Evil Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_Dead)* franchise (1981–present), *[Gremlins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gremlins)* (1984), *[Shaun of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaun_of_the_Dead)* (2004) and *[The Cabin in the Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cabin_in_the_Woods)* (2011).

## In literature

Horror and comedy have been associated with each other since the early days of horror novels. Author Bruce G. Hallenbeck cites the 1820 short story "[The Legend of Sleepy Hollow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Legend_of_Sleepy_Hollow)" by [Washington Irving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Irving) as "the first great comedy horror story".[^cite_note-hallenbeck1-2] The story made readers "laugh one moment and scream the next" and its premise was based on mischief typically found during the holiday [Halloween](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halloween).[^cite_note-hallenbeck1-2]

Shortly after the publication of [Mary Shelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Shelley)'s *[Frankenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankenstein)*, comedic parodies appeared. [Edgar Allan Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe) put humor and horror on the same continuum, and many nineteenth century authors used black humor in their horror stories. Author [Robert Bloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bloch) called them "opposite sides of the same coin".[^cite_note-\:0-3]

## In film

In comedy horror films, [gallows humor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_comedy) is a common element. While comedy horror films provide scares for audiences, they also provide something that dramatic [horror films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horror_film) do not: "the permission to laugh at your fears, to whistle past the cinematic graveyard and feel secure in the knowledge that the monsters can't get you".[^cite_note-hallenbeck1-2]

In the era of [silent film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_film), the source material for early comedy horror films came from stage performances instead of literature. One example, *[The Ghost Breaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ghost_Breaker_\(1914_film\))* (1914), was based on a 1909 play, though the film's horror elements were more interesting to the audience than the comedy elements. In the United States following the trauma of [World War I](4764461.md), film audiences sought to see horror on screen but tempered with humor. The "pioneering" comedy horror film was *[One Exciting Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Exciting_Night_\(1922_film\))* (1922), written, directed and produced by [D. W. Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._W._Griffith), who noticed the stage success of the genre and foresaw a cinematic translation. The film included [blackface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackface) performances and footage of a hurricane for a climactic storm. As an early experiment, the various genres were not well-balanced with horror and comedy, and later films improved the balance and took more sophisticated approaches.[^cite_note-4] Charles Bramesco of [Vulture.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulture.com) identifies *[Abbott and Costello Meet Frankenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbott_and_Costello_Meet_Frankenstein)* as the first commercially successful comedy horror film. Its success legitimized the genre and established it as commercially viable.[^cite_note-\:1-5]

Following the success of [Universal’s classic monster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Monsters) films, horror comedies in the mid-20th century often combined slapstick humor with supernatural elements. Films like *[The Fearless Vampire Killers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fearless_Vampire_Killers)* (1967) blended parody with gothic horror.[^cite_note-6] This era established the foundation for later films that would more evenly mix horror and comedy.&#x20;

### Slapstick and gore in horror comedy (1980s–1990s)

The 1980s marked a shift toward more extreme and graphic horror comedies. Films like *[An American Werewolf in London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_American_Werewolf_in_London)* (1981), *[Evil Dead II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil_Dead_II)* (1987) and *[Braindead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braindead_\(film\))* (1992) featured elaborate practical effects and exaggerated gore, merging [body horror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_horror) with absurd humor.[^cite_note-7] Other notable entries, such as *[Gremlins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gremlins)* (1984) and *[Beetlejuice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beetlejuice)* (1988), embraced fantasy-horror elements while maintaining a comedic tone suitable for wider audiences.[^cite_note-\:0-3]

The 1990s saw a continuation of self-aware horror comedies, as seen in *[Scream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scream_\(1996_film\))* (1996), which satirized slasher tropes while still functioning as a horror film.[^cite_note-8] This approach influenced later films that incorporated [meta-humor.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-referential_humor)

### Meta-horror and satirical trends (2000s–present)

Horror comedies in the 21st century frequently incorporate meta-commentary on the horror genre itself. *[Scary Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Movie)* (2000) and its sequels directly parodied popular horror films, such as *[Scream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scream_\(1996_film\))* (1996) and *[I Know What You Did Last Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Know_What_You_Did_Last_Summer)* (1997).[^cite_note-9] Unlike traditional horror-comedies, *[Scary Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scary_Movie)* primarily functions as a comedy with horror references. Films such as *[Shaun of the Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaun_of_the_Dead)* (2004) offered a comedic but heartfelt homage to zombie cinema.[^cite_note-\:1-5] *[Cabin in the Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cabin_in_the_Woods)* (2012) took a self-aware approach by deconstructing horror archetypes.[^cite_note-10]

In the 2010s and 2020s, horror comedies also began incorporating social satire. *[Get Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Out)* (2017) blended psychological horror with dark humor to critique racial dynamics, while *[Ready or Not](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ready_or_Not_\(2019_film\))* (2019) and *[Bodies Bodies Bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodies_Bodies_Bodies)* (2022) used horror-comedy to comment on class and generational divides.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## In television

Horror comedy on television dates back to classic sitcoms like *[The Munsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Munsters)* and *[The Addams Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Addams_Family_\(1964_TV_series\))* and has since expanded to include a variety of styles.[^cite_note-13] Notable examples range from the gory slapstick of *[Ash vs Evil Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_vs_Evil_Dead)* and *[Stan Against Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stan_Against_Evil)* to mockumentaries like the *[What We Do in the Shadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_We_Do_in_the_Shadows_\(franchise\))* franchise and *[Wellington Paranormal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellington_Paranormal)*.[^cite_note-14] Other comedic horror series include *[Todd and the Book of Pure Evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_and_the_Book_of_Pure_Evil)*, *[Shining Vale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shining_Vale)*, and *[Santa Clarita Diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clarita_Diet)*, while animated entries feature *[Beetlejuice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beetlejuice_\(TV_series\))*, *[Invader Zim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invader_Zim)*, *[School for Vampires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_for_Vampires)*, *[Scooby-Doo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scooby-Doo)*, and *[Courage the Cowardly Dog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courage_the_Cowardly_Dog)*.[^cite_note-15] More recent additions to the genre include *[The Ghost and Molly McGee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ghost_and_Molly_McGee)*, *[Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wednesday_\(TV_series\))*, *[Don't Hug Me I'm Scared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don't_Hug_Me_I'm_Scared_\(TV_series\))*, *[Gravity Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_Falls)*, *[Hazbin Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazbin_Hotel)*, *[Helluva Boss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helluva_Boss)*, and *[Bunsen Is a Beast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunsen_Is_a_Beast)*.
