**Al-Shati** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مخيم الشاطئ), also known as **Shati** or **Beach camp**, is a [Palestinian refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_refugee_camp) along the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), located in [Gaza City](55387.md) within [Gaza Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Governorate) in the northern part of [Gaza Strip](12047.md).

Al-Shati was established in 1948 for about 23,000 Palestinians who [fled or were expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_expulsion_and_flight) from the cities of [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa), [Lod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lod), and [Beersheba](312755.md) as well as surrounding villages during the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War). The camp's total land area consists of 520 [dunums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam).[^cite_note-UNRWA-2][^cite_note-3]

According to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (PCBS), al-Shati had a population of 40,734 inhabitants in 2017.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1] As of July 2023, the [United Nations Relief and Works Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Relief_and_Works_Agency) (UNRWA) reports a population of 90,173 registered [refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_refugee).[^cite_note-UNRWA-2] The camp is the third largest [refugee camp in the Palestinian Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_refugee_camps).

## History

Al-Shati was established in 1948 for about 23,000 Palestinians who [fled or were expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_expulsion_and_flight) by Zionist militias from the cities of [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa), [Lod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lod), and [Beersheba](312755.md) as well as surrounding villages during the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War).



Israeli blockade of the camp during the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada), 1991

In 1971, Israeli authorities demolished over 2,000 shelters for the purpose of widening the roads for security reasons. About 8,000 refugees were forced to leave the camp to the nearby housing project in [Sheikh Radwan](21912219.md) in [Gaza City](55387.md).[^cite_note-UNRWA-2][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

In 2023, Al-Shati was [hit by multiple airstrikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Shati_Camp_airstrike) during the [Gaza war](74998519.md). On November 10, during the [Israeli invasion of the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_invasion_of_the_Gaza_Strip_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)), [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) said they had killed around 150 [Hamas](13913.md) militants during battles in the Al-Shati area.[^cite_note-7] In November 2023 the Israel Defense Forces took control of the camp completely.[^cite_note-8]

## Economy

Before September 2000, when Israel closed off its border with the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) due to the violence of the [Second Intifada](71717.md), the majority of al-Shati's work force were laborers in [Israel](9282173.md) or worked in agriculture. Today, some refugees work in workshops and sewing factories. A sizable number of the camp's 2453 families depend on fishing for income. It contains a sewage system, a health center, and 23 schools (17 primary, 6 secondary).[^cite_note-UNRWA-2]
