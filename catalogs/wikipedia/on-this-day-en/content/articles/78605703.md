The **2025 North Indian Ocean cyclone season** was the costliest season on record and the deadliest since [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_North_Indian_Ocean_cyclone_season). The season featured several deadly [tropical cyclones](8282374.md), including cyclones [Senyar](81689422.md) and [Ditwah](77002119.md). Senyar was the first recorded cyclone to form in the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca), producing deadly floods across [Indonesia](14579.md), [Thailand](30128.md), and [Malaysia](3607937.md) and claiming over 1,501 lives, with Cyclone Ditwah meandered near [Sri Lanka](26750.md) for several days, killing 647 people in the country in November 2025. The season itself saw an above-average number of depressions forming, and an average amount of cyclonic storms forming. Seasons have no official bounds, but cyclones tend to form between April and December, with the peak from May to November. However, this year, the first cyclone attained that strength on October 3rd. These dates conventionally delimit the period of each year when most tropical cyclones form in the northern [Indian Ocean](14580.md).

The scope of this article is limited to the Indian Ocean in the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere), east of the [Horn of Africa](62142.md) and west of the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula). There are two main seas in the North Indian Ocean—the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) to the west of the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), abbreviated *ARB* by the [India Meteorological Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Meteorological_Department) (IMD); and the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) to the east as *BOB*. The systems that form over land are abbreviated as *LAND*.

The official [Regional Specialized Meteorological Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Specialized_Meteorological_Centre) in this basin is the IMD, while the [Joint Typhoon Warning Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Typhoon_Warning_Center) (JTWC) releases unofficial advisories. On average, four to five cyclonic storms form in this basin every season.[^cite_note-1]

## Season summary

A depression formed on 24 May in the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) off the coast of Konkan, named ARB 01. It made a landfall on India soon after, bringing heavy rain to the region after degrading into a tropical low. It peaked with 30 mph 3-min winds and a minimum barometric pressure of 997 hPa (mbar). On 29 May, Depression BOB 01 formed near [West Bengal](34040.md). It strengthened into a Deep Depression three hours later on the IMD scale. It made landfall on the border of West Bengal and [Bangladesh](3454.md) 6 hours later at its peak of 35 mph 3-min winds and a minimum barometric pressure of 988 hPa (mbar). It weakened and dissipated over Bangladesh over the next 36 hours, bringing heavy rain to the region. 65 people died from the resulting landslides and flooding.

After a lull in activity, Depression BOB 02 formed over West Bengal on 14 July from a well-marked low that moved inland a day prior. The next day, another depression was designated LAND 01. It quickly dissipated after forming over Northwestern India. BOB 02 dissipated but soon regenerated, causing it to be assigned a new name, LAND 03, despite there being no recorded evidence of a LAND 02. Soon after it dissipated for good, a new Depression emerged in the extreme northern [Bay of Bengal](47892.md), designated as BOB 04, but, similar to BOB 02/LAND 03, there was no evidence of a BOB 03. It moved inland and soon dissipated, but brought extreme rain and flooding to the affected areas.

## Systems

### Depression ARB 01

| Duration | 24 May – 25 May |

On 21 May, a [tropical disturbance](8282374.md) designated as Invest 93A formed in the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) near the west coast of India, approximately 165 nautical miles (306 km) south-southwest of [Mumbai](19189.md). Favorable environmental conditions including warm sea surface temperatures and moderate wind shear allowed the system to gradually consolidate. The [India Meteorological Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Meteorological_Department) (IMD) forecasted its intensification into a cyclonic storm within 2 to 3 days, prompting orange and yellow weather warnings along the Konkan and Gujarat coasts, as well as widespread rainfall alerts across Kerala. This system marked the onset of tropical cyclone activity in the North Indian Ocean basin for the 2025 season.[^cite_note-2]

Under the influence of the tropical disturbance, a [low pressure area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_pressure_area) formed over the eastern Arabian Sea off the southern [Konkan-Goa coasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkan) by 05\:30 IST on 22 May.[^cite_note-3] By 05\:30 IST on 23 May, it became a well marked low pressure area off the southern Konkan coast and persisted over the same region as of 08\:30 IST.[^cite_note-4] The system organised into a [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_depression) by 05\:30 IST on 24 May about 40 km northwest of [Ratnagiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratnagiri).[^cite_note-5] Over the next 6 hours, the depression moved slowly eastward at a speed of 5 km/h and between 11\:30 and 12\:30 IST it made [landfall](5016402.md), crossing the south Konkan coast near Ratnagiri. The maximum sustained windspeed at the time was 25 knots (46 km/h) gusting to 35 knots (65 km/h).[^cite_note-6] The system then moved eastwards inland,[^cite_note-7] and by 05\:30 IST on 25 May, the system had weakened back into a well marked low pressure area over southern Madhya Maharastra and the adjoining regions of [Marathwada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathwada) and [Karnataka](16880.md).[^cite_note-8]

### Deep Depression BOB 01

| Deep depression (IMD) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | 29 May – 30 May |
| Peak intensity | 55 km/h (35 mph) (3-min); 986 [hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\)) ([mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\))) |

A low pressure area formed over northwest [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) off the coast of [Odisha](250724.md) at 08\:30 IST on 27 May.[^cite_note-9] Favorable environmental conditions including warm sea surface temperatures, moderate vertical [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear) over the system and the presence of warm air over Gangetic [West Bengal](34040.md) and [Bangladesh](3454.md) allowed it to gradually consolidate. However, the strong wind shear in the region due to the advancing [southwest monsoon](57630.md) and insufficient time over sea before landfall limited its intensification. It became well marked at 05\:30 IST on 28 May and persisted over the same region.[^cite_note-10] By 05\:30 IST on 29 May, the system coalesced into a depression near the coasts of West Bengal and Bangladesh.[^cite_note-11] Thereafter, the depression moved nearly northwards and by 08\:30 IST on the same day, it intensified into a deep depression.[^cite_note-12] Over the next 6 hours, the system continued to move nearly northwards and made landfall on the West Bengal–Bangladesh coast between [Sagar Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagar_Island) and [Khepupara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalapara_Upazila), close to [Raidighi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raydighi_Abad). Its maximum sustained windspeed at the time was 30 knots (56 km/h) gusting to 40 knots (74 km/h).[^cite_note-13] It then tracked north-northeastward inland,[^cite_note-14] and by 05\:30 IST on 30 May the system had weakened back into a depression over Bangladesh.[^cite_note-15] By 17\:30 IST on the same day, it had degenerated into a well marked low over [Meghalaya](326019.md) due to interaction with rugged terrain.[^cite_note-16]

As a result of the system, [Noakhali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noakhali_District) received 168 mm (6.6 in)[^cite_note-17] and [Dhaka](56656.md) 88 mm (3.5 in) of rainfall on 29 May.[^cite_note-18] The same day, [Kolkata](47905.md) received light to moderate rainfall with Jinjirabazar receiving 23 mm (0.91 in) of rainfall in a timespan from noon to 20\:00 IST. [^cite_note-19] It caused floods and landslides which killed 61, left 10 missing and affected 6.1 million people in India, with 27 deaths in [Assam](186162.md),[^cite_note-20] 12 in [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh), 6 each in [Meghalaya](326019.md) and [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram), 4 in [Manipur](422642.md), 3 in [Sikkim](140372.md), 2 in [Tripura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripura) and 1 in [Nagaland](408677.md).[^cite_note-21] Four additional deaths occurred in [Bangladesh](3454.md), including 2 in [Dhaka](56656.md) and 1 in [Moheshkhali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moheshkhali_Upazila), while 8 others went missing when a [trawler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_trawler) sank near [Kutubdia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kutubdia_Upazila). In Moheshkhali and Kutubdia, over 100 homes and farmland were flooded.[^cite_note-22] Another person was killed by a collapsing wall, 11 were injured by lightning strikes, over 1,400 homes were damaged and 53 landslides were observed across 33 [Rohingya](53544313.md) [refugee camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugee_camps).[^cite_note-23] The system brought heavy rainfall and strong winds to [Myanmar](19457.md) as well. As a result, 26 homes were destroyed and 22 others were damaged in [Yegyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yegyi_Township) and [Dedaye Townships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedaye_Township).[^cite_note-24]

### Depression LAND 01

| Depression (IMD) |  |
| --- | --- |
|  |  |
| Duration | 15 July – 16 July |
| Peak intensity | 35 km/h (25 mph) (3-min); 998 [hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\)) ([mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\))) |

On 13 July, an upper air cyclonic circulation developed over parts of north [Madhya](47945.md). The IMD forecasted a low-pressure area to form under its influence over northwest Madhya during the next 24 hours.

Sometime during the late hours of 13 July or the early hours of 14 July, the forecasted low-pressure area formed over northwest Madhya, the active monsoon trough contributing to its rapid development.[^cite_note-28]

On 15 July, the low pressure area became well-marked as of 08\:30 IST (03\:00 UTC) over parts of north [Rajasthan](26291.md) and concentrated into a depression.[^cite_note-29] The IMD forecasted the newly-formed depression to move west-northwestwards during the next 24 hours across parts of north Rajasthan. On 16 July, the depression weakened into a well-marked low over northwest Rajasthan at 05\:30 IST (00\:00 UTC) due to loss of moisture and topographical interaction.[^cite_note-30]

The IMD issued red alerts across Rajasthan due to heavy forecasted rain. Isolated extremely heavy rainfall (over 21 cm) was forecasted by the IMD in southwest Rajasthan and north Gujarat. The highest rainfall recorded as a result of the depression was 234 mm in [Nainwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nainwa) of the [Bundi district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundi_district).[^cite_note-31] 10 casualties were reported.[^cite_note-32] The depression contributed to widespread flooding and financial losses. Many villages were cut off as rivers, drains, and dams overflowed. 17 people became stranded in the [Banas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banas_River) of [Tonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonk,_India), but were fortunately rescued by authorities.[^cite_note-18_dead-33] Flash floods across cities like [Kota](1324777.md) and [Jaipur](166537.md) affected Rajasthan's economy, damaging roads and submerging farmlands.[^cite_note-34]

### Severe Cyclonic Storm Shakhti

| Severe cyclonic storm (IMD) |  |
| --- | --- |
| Category 1 tropical cyclone (SSHWS) |  |
|  |  |
| Duration | 1 October – 7 October |
| Peak intensity | 110 km/h (70 mph) (3-min); 987 [hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\)) ([mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\))) |

Cyclone Shakhti originated from a well-marked area of low pressure, associated with the remnants of BOB 06, at the north-east Arabian Sea on 30 September 2025. By 1 October, the system developed into a [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(weather\)) due to favourable conditions such as the mid-level wind shear being low to moderate (5–10 knots \[5.8–11.5 mph]) and warm sea surface temperatures (28–29 °C \[82–84 °F]). These suitable conditions remained, causing the system to intensify into a deep depression at 23\:30 IST on 2 October and to intensify into a [Cyclonic Storm](8282374.md) named *Shakhti* on 3 October 11\:30 IST.[^cite_note-78] Shakhti moved west-southwestward due to prevailing east-northeasterlies in the middle-upper tropospheric levels, with cyclone-force winds. On 7 October 2025, Shakhti dissipated off the coast of Oman. The remnants persisted for a time, sporadically generating convective bursts throughout the next few days.

Further information: [List of tropical cyclones near the Equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tropical_cyclones_near_the_Equator)

On 21 November, the Joint Typhoon Warning Center marked a tropical disturbance in the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca) as Invest 95B as it moved little over the next few days. Four days later, the IMD began monitoring the system, designating it as Depression BOB 09. The JTWC followed suit the same day, issuing a Tropical Cyclone Formation Alert on the system, giving the designation 04B. Later that same day it would make landfall on Indonesia's Sumatra.[^cite_note-92] The system tracked along the coast of Sumatra and weakened, with a small low-level circulation center.[^cite_note-93] On 26 November, the system was renamed Cyclonic Storm Senyar as it recurved back into the Strait of Malacca the next day,[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] with Senyar weakening into a deep depression as it moved east.[^cite_note-97]

The system brought heavy landfall to the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula). Flooding in [Southern Thailand](1453076.md) killed 33 people and affected 2.7 million others,[^cite_note-98] with the worst effects seen in [Hat Yai](482222.md), where floodwaters reached up to 2 m (6.6 ft) deep, stranding 7,000 foreign tourists as a result.[^cite_note-99] Flooding also occurred in [Peninsular Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Malaysia), where 21,000 people were evacuated across 10 states in Malaysia.[^cite_note-100]

As the system traversed through the Strait of Malacca, it also brought widespread [flash flood](244391.md) and landslides to the Indonesian island of [Sumatra](28679.md) affecting 52 [cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_status_in_Indonesia) and [regencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regency_\(Indonesia\)) in 3 [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia): [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh), [North Sumatra](453595.md), and [West Sumatra](453590.md). A total of 3.3 million people were affected, with at least 1.5 million displaced.[^cite_note-101] The Indonesian Government reported at least 1,201 deaths and 142 missing persons in the 3 affected provinces.[^cite_note-102]

Main article: [Cyclone Ditwah](77002119.md)

The IMD estimated that a depression formed at 18\:00 UTC on November 26 from a well marked low just offshore the southeastern coast of [Sri Lanka](26750.md).[^cite_note-103] Amidst favorable conditions, the depression would intensify, first to a deep depression then into a cyclonic storm, being named *Ditwah*.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] Ditwah would slowly cross [Sri Lanka](26750.md) over the next few days, maintaining cyclonic storm status before moving off into the [Bay of Bengal](47892.md) to the north on 29 November. Ditwah would slightly intensify for a brief time before weakening back to a deep depression on 30 November as it paralleled the east coast of Southern India.[^cite_note-106]

Heavy rains from the cyclone caused flooding and landslides in Sri Lanka, causing at least 640 fatalities in the nation.[^cite_note-107] Additionally, a helicopter belonging to the [Sri Lanka Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Lanka_Air_Force) crashed during relief operations, killing the pilot and injuring 4 others.[^cite_note-108] Meanwhile, in the Indian State of [Tamil Nadu](29918.md), at least 3 people were killed by the storm, 2 from collapsing walls, and one from electrocution.[^cite_note-109]

## Storm names

Within this basin, a tropical cyclone is assigned a name when it is judged to have reached [cyclonic storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclone_scales#North_Indian_Ocean) intensity with winds of 65 km/h (40 mph). The names were selected by a new list from the Regional Specialized Meteorological Center in [New Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Meteorological_Department) by mid year of 2020.[^cite_note-110] There is no retirement of tropical cyclone names in this basin as the list of names is only scheduled to be used once before a new list of names is drawn up. Should a named tropical cyclone move into the basin from the Western Pacific, then it will retain its original name. This season mainly had the last 5 cyclones as the main storms. (except ARB 03)
