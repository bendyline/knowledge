**Rotterdam** ([/ˈrɒtərdæm/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*ROT-ər-dam*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), [UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English) also [/ˌrɒtərˈdæm/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*ROT-ər-DAM*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Dutch: [\[ˌrɔtərˈdɑm\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/51/Nl-Rotterdam.ogg/Nl-Rotterdam.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/51/Nl-Rotterdam.ogg/Nl-Rotterdam.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>)[^cite_note-10] is the second-largest [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_the_Netherlands_by_province) in the [Netherlands](21148.md) by population and the largest by area (319.4 km<sup>2</sup>). It is in the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_the_Netherlands) of [South Holland](28687.md), part of the [North Sea](21179.md) mouth of the [Rhine–Meuse–Scheldt delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine%E2%80%93Meuse%E2%80%93Scheldt_delta), via the [New Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwe_Maas) inland shipping channel, dug to connect to the [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse) at first and now to the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine).

Rotterdam's history goes back to 1270, when a [dam](51518.md) was constructed in the [Rotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotte_\(river\)). In 1340, Rotterdam was granted city rights by [William IV, Count of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_II,_Count_of_Hainaut). The [Rotterdam–The Hague metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam%E2%80%93The_Hague_metropolitan_area), with a population of approximately 2.7 million, is the [10th-largest in the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_the_European_Union) and the most populous in the country.

A major logistic and economic centre, Rotterdam is [Europe's largest seaport](4410985.md). In 2022, Rotterdam had a population of 655,468 and is home to over 180 different nationalities.[^cite_note-11]

Rotterdam is known for its [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_University_Rotterdam), riverside setting, lively cultural life, maritime heritage and modern [architecture](#Architecture). The near-complete destruction of the city centre during the [World War II](32927.md) [German bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_bombing_of_Rotterdam) has resulted in a varied architectural landscape, including [skyscrapers](29485.md) designed by architects such as [Rem Koolhaas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rem_Koolhaas), [Piet Blom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Blom) and [Ben van Berkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_van_Berkel).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-Academy_of_Urbanism-13][^cite_note-14]

The [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse) and [Scheldt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheldt) give waterway access into the heart of Western Europe, including the highly industrialized [Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr). The extensive distribution system including rail, roads, and waterways have earned Rotterdam the nicknames "Gateway to Europe" and "Gateway to the World".[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Rotterdam).

### Early history



Map of Rotterdam by [Frederick de Wit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_de_Wit) (c. 1690)

The settlement at the lower end of the [fen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fen) stream *[Rotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotte_\(river\))* (or *Rotta*, as it was then known, from *rot*, "muddy", and *a*, "water", thus "muddy water") dates from at least the year 950.[^cite_note-18] Around 1150, large [floods](50482.md) in the area ended development, leading to the construction of protective [dikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levee) and dams, including *Schielands Hoge Zeedijk* ("Schieland's High Sea Dike") along the northern banks of the present-day [Nieuwe Maas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwe_Maas) river. A dam on the Rotte was built in the 1260s and was located at the present-day *Hoogstraat*.[^cite_note-Bokern-19]

On 7 July 1340, Count [Willem IV of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_II_of_Hainaut) granted [city rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_rights_in_the_Low_Countries) to Rotterdam, which then had a population of only a few thousand.[^cite_note-20] Around the year 1350, a shipping canal (the *[Rotterdamse Schie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schie)*) was completed, which provided Rotterdam access to the larger towns in the north, allowing it to become a local trans-shipment centre between the Netherlands, [England](9316.md) and [Germany](11867.md), and to [urbanize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urbanization).[^cite_note-21] However, the economy of Rotterdam was mainly based on herring fishing until the [Eighty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighty_Years'_War), when it turned out that it is one of the few Dutch towns with an easy sea access. As a result, [William the Silent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Silent) built new fortifications around Rotterdam.[^cite_note-Bokern-19]

Beginning in the 1600s, Rotterdam was involved in the [Atlantic slave trade](49005.md). According to historian Gerhard de Kok, "Rotterdam merchants were the pioneers of the [Dutch slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Dutch_slavery)". From the 17th century until 1814, when the [United Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_Principality_of_the_United_Netherlands) abolished the Netherlands' involvement in the slave trade at the request of the [British government](25318118.md), Dutch [slave ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_ship) from Rotterdam sailed to [Africa](5334607.md) and the [Americas](29833.md) as part of the [triangular trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangular_trade). Rotterdam merchants also sold significant quantities of [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder) to [Zeeland](52229.md)-based slave ships.[^cite_note-22]



The Delftsevaart, c. 1890–1905



Nieuwe Markt, 1915

The port of Rotterdam grew steadily in importance, the new harbours were built, and Rotterdam became the seat of one of the six chambers of the Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie (VOC), the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company), and one of the five chambers of the West-Indische Compagnie (WIC), the [Dutch West India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_West_India_Company). In the 17th century, the city grew beyond the fortifications, and by the 1670s, its population was around 30,000.[^cite_note-Bokern-19]

The greatest spurt of growth, both in port activity and population, followed the completion of the [Nieuwe Waterweg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwe_Waterweg) in 1872. The city and harbour started to expand on the south bank of the river. The *[Witte Huis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witte_Huis),* or *White House* skyscraper,[^cite_note-23] inspired by American office buildings and built in 1898 in the French [Art Nouveau style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau), is evidence of Rotterdam's rapid growth and success. When completed, it was the tallest office building in Europe, with a height of 45 m (147.64 ft).

### 20th century



Rotterdam centre after the [1940 bombing of Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Blitz). The ruined [St. Lawrence Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grote_or_Sint-Laurenskerk_\(Rotterdam\)) has been restored.



[Tower blocks](118767.md) in the [Kop van Zuid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kop_van_Zuid) neighbourhood

During [World War I](4764461.md), the city was the world's largest spy centre because of Dutch neutrality and its strategic location between Britain, Germany and German-occupied Belgium. Many spies who were arrested and executed in Britain were led by German secret agents operating from Rotterdam. [MI6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MI6) had its main European office on de Boompjes. From there the British coordinated espionage in Germany and occupied Belgium. During World War I, an average of 25,000 Belgian refugees lived in the city, as well as hundreds of German deserters and escaped Allied prisoners of war.[^cite_note-24]

During [World War II](32927.md), the German army [invaded the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_invasion_of_the_Netherlands) on 10 May 1940.[^cite_note-25] [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md) had hoped to conquer the country in just one day, but his forces met unexpectedly fierce resistance. The Dutch army was forced to capitulate on 15 May 1940, following the [bombing of Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Blitz) on 14 May and the threat of bombing other Dutch cities.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAmersfoort2005369-27][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGötzel1980149,_150-28][^cite_note-29] The heart of Rotterdam was almost completely destroyed by the [Luftwaffe](17885.md). Some 80,000 civilians were made homeless, and 900 were killed; a relatively low number since many had fled the city because of the warfare and bombing going on in Rotterdam since the start of the invasion three days earlier. The City Hall survived the bombing. [Ossip Zadkine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossip_Zadkine) later attempted to capture the event with his statue *[De Verwoeste Stad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Verwoeste_Stad)* ('The Destroyed City'). The statue stands near the Leuvehaven, not far from the [Erasmusbrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmusbrug) in the centre of the city, on the north shore of the river Nieuwe Maas. In 1941, 11,000 Jews still lived in Rotterdam. Before the war there were 13,000. Between 30 July 1942 and 22 April 1943, 6,790 people were deported in 8 transports via Loods 24. The vast majority of the Jews who were deported via Loods 24 were murdered in [Sobibór](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobibor_extermination_camp) and [Auschwitz-Birkenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp). Research in 2000 showed that 144 people survived the deportations. In 2013 the [Jewish Children's Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Children's_Monument?action=edit\&redlink=1) was unveiled.

In January 1948, [Queen Wilhelmina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelmina_of_the_Netherlands) presented the motto 'Sterker door strijd' (Stronger through effort) as part of the [coat of arms of Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Rotterdam) to the city government:

"...to serve as a record of the wartime experiences of Rotterdam residents and their role in national liberation..."

—Wilhelmina of the Netherlands

Rotterdam was gradually rebuilt from the 1950s through to the 1970s. Because the city centre was largely destroyed, new spatial infrastructure could be built, making it an open and modern city. In 1953 the [Lijnbaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijnbaan) was opened, the first car-free shopping street in Europe. The progressive design attracted a lot of international attention, in which film and television played an important role.[^cite_note-30] The new [Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Centraal_station) was completed in 1957, with the [Groothandelsgebouw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groothandelsgebouw) from 1953 next to it. The [Euromast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromast) was erected in 1960 on the occasion of the [Floriade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floriade_\(Netherlands\)). From the 1980s onwards, the city councils began developing an active architectural policy. The harbours were moving westwards, and the old environment had to be reshaped. Daring and new styles of [apartments](265567.md), office buildings and recreation facilities resulted in a more '[livable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_of_life)' city centre with a new skyline. In the 1990s, the [Kop van Zuid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kop_van_Zuid) was built on the south bank of the river as a new business centre. Rotterdam was voted 2015 European City of the Year by the Academy of Urbanism.[^cite_note-Academy_of_Urbanism-13] A profile of Rem Koolhaas in *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* begins, "If you put the last 50 years of architecture in a blender, and spat it out in building-sized chunks across the skyline, you would probably end up with something that looked a bit like Rotterdam".[^cite_note-Wainwright_2013-31]

## Geography



Topographic map image of Rotterdam (city), as of September 2014

Rotterdam is divided into a northern and a southern part by the river Nieuwe Maas, connected by (from west to east): the Beneluxtunnel; the [Maastunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastunnel); the [Erasmusbrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmusbrug); a subway tunnel; the Willemsspoortunnel ('Willems railway tunnel'); the [Willemsbrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willemsbrug) ('Willems Bridge') together with the Koninginnebrug ('Queen's Bridge'); and the [Van Brienenoordbrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Brienenoordbrug) ('Van Brienenoord Bridge'). The former railway lift bridge [De Hef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Hef) ('the Lift') is preserved as a [Rijksmonument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijksmonument) (national heritage site) in lifted position between the [Noordereiland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noordereiland) ('North Island') and the south of Rotterdam.



View of Rotterdam from the Euromast

The city centre is located on the northern bank of the Nieuwe Maas, although recent urban development has extended the centre to parts of southern Rotterdam known as [Kop van Zuid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kop_van_Zuid) ('the Head of South', i.e., the northern part of southern Rotterdam). From its inland core, Rotterdam reaches the [North Sea](21179.md) by a swathe of predominantly harbour area.

Built mostly behind dikes, large parts of Rotterdam are below sea level. For instance, the Prins Alexander Polder in the northeast of Rotterdam extends 6 m (20 ft) below sea level, or rather below [Normaal Amsterdams Peil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normaal_Amsterdams_Peil) (NAP) or 'Amsterdam Ordnance Datum'. The lowest point in the Netherlands (6.76 m (22.2 ft) below NAP) is situated just to the east of Rotterdam, in the municipality of [Nieuwerkerk aan den IJssel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwerkerk_aan_den_IJssel).



Satellite image of Rotterdam and its port

&#x20;The [Rotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotte_\(river\)) river no longer joins the Nieuwe Maas directly. Since the early 1980s, when the construction of Rotterdam's second underground line interfered with the Rotte's course, its waters have been pumped through a pipe into the [Nieuwe Maas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuwe_Maas) via the Boerengat.&#x20;



The 24 municipalities of the [Rotterdam–The Hague metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam%E2%80%93The_Hague_metropolitan_area)

Between the summers of 2003 and 2008, an artificial [beach](40630.md) was created at the Boompjeskade along the Nieuwe Maas, between the Erasmus Bridge and the Willems Bridge. [Swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_swimming) was not possible; digging pits was limited to the height of the layer of [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand), about 50 cm (20 in). Alternatively, people go to the beach of [Hook of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hook_of_Holland) (which is a Rotterdam district) or one of the beaches in [Zeeland](52229.md): [Renesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renesse) or the Zuid-Hollandse Eilanden: [Ouddorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouddorp), [Oostvoorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oostvoorne).

Rotterdam forms the centre of the [Rijnmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijnmond) conurbation, bordering the conurbation surrounding [The Hague](30269.md) to the northwest. The two conurbations are close enough to be a single conurbation. They share the [Rotterdam The Hague Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_The_Hague_Airport) and a light rail system called [RandstadRail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RandstadRail). Consideration is being given to creating an official Metropolitan region Rotterdam The Hague (*Metropoolregio Rotterdam Den Haag*), which would have a combined population approaching 2.5 million.

In its turn, the Rijnmond conurbation is part of the southern wing (the [Zuidvleugel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuidvleugel)) of the [Randstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randstad), which is one of the most important economic and densely populated areas in the northwest of Europe. Having a population of 7.1 million, the Randstad is the [sixth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_Europe) [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_area) in [Europe](9239.md) (after Moscow, London, Paris, Istanbul, and the Rhein-Ruhr Area). The Zuidvleugel, situated in the province of [South Holland](28687.md), has a population of around 3 million.

### Climate

Rotterdam experiences a temperate [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfb*) similar to all of the coastal areas in the Netherlands. Located near to the coast, its climate is slightly milder than locations further inland. Winters are cool with frequent cold days, while the summers are mild to warm, with occasional hot temperatures. Temperature rises above 30 °C on average 4 days each summer, while (night) temperatures can drop below −5 °C during winter for short periods of time, mostly during periods of sustained easterly (continental) winds. Precipitation is generally moderate throughout the year, although spring and summer (particularly before August) are relatively drier and sunnier, while autumn and winter are cloudier with more frequent rain (or snow). The following climate data is from the airport, which is slightly cooler than the city, being surrounded by water canals which make the climate milder and with a higher [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity). The city has an [urban heat island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_heat_island), especially inside the city centre.[^cite_note-32]

Rotterdam is diverse, with the demographics differing by neighbourhood. The city centre has a disproportionately high number of single people when compared to other cities, with 70% of the population between the ages of 20 and 40 identifying as single.[^cite_note-39] Those with higher education and higher income live disproportionately in the city centre, as do foreign-born citizens.

### Composition

See also: [Districts and neighbourhoods of Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_and_neighbourhoods_of_Rotterdam)

The [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_the_Netherlands) of Rotterdam is part of the [Rotterdam–The Hague metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam%E2%80%93The_Hague_metropolitan_area), which, as of 2015, covers an area of 1,130 km<sup>2</sup>, of which 990 km km<sup>2</sup> is land, and has a population of approximately 2,563,197. As of 2019, the municipality itself occupies an area of 325.79 km<sup>2</sup>, 208.80 km<sup>2</sup> of which is land, and is home to 638,751 inhabitants.[^cite_note-40] Its population peaked at 731,564 in 1965, but the dual processes of [suburbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburbanization) and [counterurbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterurbanization) saw this number steadily decline over the next 2 decades, reaching 560,000 by 1985.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-Pijbes_2016-42] Although Rotterdam has experienced population growth since then, it has done so at a slower pace than comparable cities in the Netherlands, like Amsterdam, The Hague and Utrecht.[^cite_note-Pijbes_2016-42]

Rotterdam consists of 14 submunicipalities: [Centrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Centrum), [Charlois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlois) (including Heijplaat), [Delfshaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delfshaven), [Feijenoord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feijenoord), [Hillegersberg-Schiebroek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillegersberg-Schiebroek), [Hook of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hook_of_Holland), [Hoogvliet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoogvliet), [IJsselmonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IJsselmonde,_Rotterdam), [Kralingen-Crooswijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kralingen-Crooswijk), [Noord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noord,_Rotterdam), [Overschie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overschie), [Pernis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pernis,_Netherlands), and [Prins Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prins_Alexander) (the most populous submunicipality with around 85,000 inhabitants). One other area, [Rozenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozenburg), has had an official submunicipality status since 18 March 2010. Since the status of a [submunicipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deelgemeente) was lifted on 19 March 2014, it has become an integral part of the municipality of Rotterdam.

### Origin background makeup

In the Netherlands, Rotterdam has the highest percentage of residents with a recent [migration background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_background) from non-industrialised nations. They form a large part of Rotterdam's multi-ethnic and multicultural diversity. 52.9% of the population have at least one parent born outside the country. There are 80,000 Muslims, constituting 13% of the population in 2010.[^cite_note-44] The former mayor of Rotterdam, [Ahmed Aboutaleb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Aboutaleb) (2009–2024), is of Moroccan descent and is a practicing Muslim.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] The city is home to the largest Dutch Antillean community in the Netherlands.[^cite_note-47] The city also has its own [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown) at the West-Kruiskade, close to Rotterdam Centraal.[^cite_note-48]

The second- and third-largest religions are [Islam](6037917.md) (13.1%) and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) (3.3%), while about half of the population has [no religious affiliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion).

Since 2010 the city has been home to the largest mosque in the Netherlands, the [Essalam Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essalam_Mosque?action=edit\&redlink=1) (capacity 1,500).

## Economy



[Gebouw Delftse Poort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gebouw_Delftse_Poort), one of the tallest office buildings in the Netherlands

Rotterdam has always been one of the main centres of the shipping industry in the Netherlands. From the Rotterdam Chamber of the [VOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company), the world's first multinational, established in 1602, to the merchant shipping leader Royal [Nedlloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nedlloyd), established in 1970, with its corporate headquarters located in the landmark building the 'Willemswerf' in 1988.[^cite_note-51] In 1997, Nedlloyd merged with the British shipping industry leader P\&O, forming the third largest merchant shipping company in the world. The Anglo-Dutch [P\&O Nedlloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P\&O_Nedlloyd) was bought by the Danish giant corporation 'AP Moller [Maersk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._P._Moller-Maersk_Group)' in 2005, and its Dutch operations are still headquartered in the 'Willemswerf'. Nowadays, well-known companies with headquarters in Rotterdam are consumer goods company [Unilever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unilever) (since 2020 London), asset management firm [Robeco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robeco), energy company [Eneco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eneco_Energie), dredging company [Van Oord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Oord), oil company [Royal Dutch Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_plc) (since 2021 [London](17867.md)), terminal operator [Vopak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vopak), commodity trading company [Vitol](17518274.md) and architecture firms [MVRDV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MVRDV) and [Office for Metropolitan Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_for_Metropolitan_Architecture).

It is also home to the regional headquarters of chemical company [LyondellBasell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LyondellBasell), commodities trading company [Glencore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glencore), pharmaceutical company [Pfizer](62304.md), logistics companies [Stolt-Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolt-Nielsen), electrical equipment company [ABB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABB) and consumer goods company [Procter & Gamble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procter_&_Gamble). Furthermore, Rotterdam has the Dutch headquarters of [Allianz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz), [Maersk](723005.md), [Petrobras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrobras), [Samskip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samskip?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Louis Dreyfus Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Dreyfus_Group), and [Aon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aon_\(company\)). The City of Rotterdam makes use of the services of semi-government companies [Roteb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roteb) (to take care of [sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitation), [waste management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_management) and assorted services) and the Port of Rotterdam Authority (to maintain the [Port of Rotterdam](4410985.md)). Both these companies were once municipal bodies; now they are autonomous entities, owned by the city.



Unmanned vehicles handle containers at Europe Container Terminals (ECT), the largest container terminal operator in Europe.



The Waalhaven

Being the largest port and one of the largest cities of the country, Rotterdam attracts many people seeking jobs, especially in the cheap labour segment. The city's unemployment rate is 12%, almost twice the national average.[^cite_note-52] Rotterdam is the largest [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) in [Europe](9239.md), with the rivers [Maas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse_\(river\)) and [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) providing excellent access to the [hinterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinterland) upstream, reaching to [Basel, Switzerland](4911.md), and into France. In 2004 [Shanghai](27643.md) took over as the world's [busiest container port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_container_ports). In 2006, Rotterdam was the world's seventh largest [container port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Containerization) in terms of [twenty-foot equivalent units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-foot_equivalent_unit) (TEU) handled.[^cite_note-53] The port's main activities are [petrochemical](67728.md) industries and general [cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo) handling and [transshipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transshipment). The harbour functions as an important transit point for [bulk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulk_material_handling) materials between the European continent and overseas. From Rotterdam, goods are transported by ship, river barge, train or road. In 2007, the *[Betuweroute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betuweroute)*, a new fast freight [railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport) from Rotterdam to [Germany](11867.md), was completed. the port also allows cruiseships to dock[^cite_note-54].

Well-known streets in Rotterdam are the [Lijnbaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijnbaan) (the first set of [pedestrian streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrian_mall) of the country, opened in 1953), the Hoogstraat, the [Coolsingel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolsingel) with the city hall, which was renovated between 2018 and 2021 giving cyclists and pedestrians more space, meaning that car traffic was reduced from 4 lanes (2 in each direction) to 2 lanes (1 in each direction). Another main street is the [Weena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weena_\(Rotterdam\)), which runs from the Central Station to the [Hofplein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofplein) (square). A modern shopping venue is the Beurstraverse ("Stock Exchange Traverse"), better known by its informal name '[Koopgoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koopgoot?action=edit\&redlink=1)' ('Buying/Shopping Gutter', after its subterranean position), which crosses the Coolsingel below street level. The Kruiskade is a more upscale shopping street, with retailers like [Michael Kors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Kors), [7 For All Mankind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_For_All_Mankind), [Calvin Klein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Klein), [Hugo Boss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Boss), [Tommy Hilfiger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Hilfiger) and the Dutch men's clothier [Oger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oger_\(clothing_retailer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1). Another upscale shopping venue is a flagship store of department store [De Bijenkorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Bijenkorf). Located a little more to the east is the [Markthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_hall_\(Rotterdam\)), with lots of small retailers inside. This hall is also one of Rotterdam's famous architectural landmarks. The main shopping venue in the south of Rotterdam is Zuidplein, which lies close to [Rotterdam Ahoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Ahoy), an accommodation centre for shows, exhibitions, sporting events, concerts and congresses. Another prominent shopping centre called Alexandrium lies in the east of Rotterdam. It includes a large kitchen and furniture centre.

## Education



Bronze statue of [Erasmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus), created by [Hendrick de Keyser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendrick_de_Keyser) in 1622

Rotterdam has one major university, the [Erasmus University Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_University_Rotterdam) (EUR), named after one of the city's famous former inhabitants, [Desiderius Erasmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desiderius_Erasmus). The Woudestein campus houses (among others) the [Rotterdam School of Management, Erasmus University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_School_of_Management,_Erasmus_University). In the [Financial Times](136566.md)' 2005 rankings, it placed 29th globally and 7th in Europe. In the 2009 rankings of Masters of Management, the school reached first place with the [CEMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Alliance_in_Management_Education) Master in Management and tenth place with its RSM Master in Management.[^cite_note-55] The university is also home to Europe's largest student association, STAR Study Association Rotterdam School of Management, Erasmus University, and the world's largest student association, [AIESEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIESEC), has its international office in the city.

The Hoboken campus of EUR houses the Dijkzigt (general) hospital, the Sophia Hospital (for children), the [Daniel den Hoed clinic](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel%20den%20Hoedkliniek) (cancer institute) and the medical department of the university. They are known collectively as the [Erasmus Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_MC). This centre is ranked third in Europe by CSIC[^cite_note-56] as a hospital and is also ranked within top 50 universities of the world in the field of medicine (clinical, pre-clinical & health, 2017).[^cite_note-57]

The [Willem de Kooning Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_de_Kooning_Academy) is Rotterdam's main art school, which is part of the Hogeschool Rotterdam. It is regarded as one of the most prestigious art schools in the Netherlands and the number 1 in Advertising and Copywriting. Part of the Willem de Kooning Academy is the [Piet Zwart Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Zwart_Institute) for postgraduate studies and research in Fine Art, Media Design and Retail Design. The Piet Zwart Institute boasts a selective roster of emerging international artists.

Three *[hogescholen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_university)* (universities of applied sciences) exist in Rotterdam. These schools award their students a professional [bachelor's degree](188874.md) and a postgraduate or [master's degree](170346.md). The three *Hogescholen* are [Hogeschool Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogeschool_Rotterdam), [Hogeschool Inholland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inholland_University_of_Applied_Sciences) and [Codarts University for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codarts) (Codarts hogeschool voor de kunsten), a vocational university that teaches music, dance and circus.

Unique to the city is the [Shipping & Transport College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STC_Group), which offers master's, bachelor's and vocational diplomas at all levels.

## Culture



Rotterdam waterfront, with spotlights shining into the air to commemorate the [Rotterdam Blitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Blitz)

Once primarily a city of labour for its harbour and related industries, Rotterdam has developed into a city of culture, with various museums, cultural centres and activities, offering a stage for [architecture](#Architecture), music, visual arts, poetry, cinema, theatre, and culture more generally, with a range of festivals and other events and a buzzing nightlife. The city has its own orchestra, the [Rotterdam Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Philharmonic_Orchestra), directed by [Lahav Shani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahav_Shani), which plays at the concert venue and convention center *[De Doelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Doelen)*.[^cite_note-58] The [Ahoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Ahoy) complex in the south of the city is used for pop concerts, exhibitions, tennis tournaments and other activities. There are also several theatres and cinemas, including [LantarenVenster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans_\(Rotterdam\)) and [Cinerama.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinerama_\(Rotterdam\))[^cite_note-59]

Alongside [Porto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porto), Rotterdam was [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) in 2001.

### Museums, libraries and archives

Rotterdam has various cultural institutions. Well-known [museums](37585.md) are the [Museum Boijmans Van Beuningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Boijmans_Van_Beuningen), [Het Nieuwe Instituut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Het_Nieuwe_Instituut), the [Wereldmuseum Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wereldmuseum_Rotterdam), the [Kunsthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunsthal), [Kunstinstituut Melly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunstinstituut_Melly)[^cite_note-60] and the [Maritime Museum Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Museum_Rotterdam).[^cite_note-61] The Historical Museum Rotterdam has changed into [Museum Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Rotterdam), which aims to exhibit the development of Rotterdam as a contemporary transnational city, and not merely the city's past.[^cite_note-62] Other museums include the [Tax & Customs Museum](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belasting%20&%20Douane%20Museum), the [Netherlands Marine Corps Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Marine_Corps_Museum), the [Rotterdam Public Transport Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Public_Transport_Museum) and the [Natural History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_History_Museum_Rotterdam). The [FENIX Museum of Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FENIX_Museum_of_Migration) opened in May 2025.[^cite_note-63]

The first municipal library of Rotterdam was founded in 1604. The current [Bibliotheek Rotterdam](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibliotheek%20Rotterdam) (public library) was established in 1869 and is currently the largest cultural organization in Rotterdam, with fifteen branches across the city.

The Rotterdam City Archives (*Stadsarchief Rotterdam*) was established in 1857. Here one can find administrative records and sources about the city's historical development.[^cite_note-64] The archival holdings include, among others, general archives, notarial deeds, an audiovisual collection, and a library.

### Popular music, film, festivals

Rotterdam has a long tradition of popular music, including the city's jazz scene before and after WWII.[^cite_note-65] A major historical moment in the city's music history is the legendary [Kralingen Pop Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kralingen_Music_Festival), which took place in Rotterdam in 1970 (featuring, among others, [Pink Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Floyd), [Jefferson Airplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Airplane), [The Byrds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Byrds), [Canned Heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canned_Heat), [It's a Beautiful Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_a_Beautiful_Day), and [Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santana_\(band\))). The festival was also made into a film, *Stamping Ground* (dir. George Sluizer).[^cite_note-66] Alternative (music) culture became prominently present in the city in these days. From the 1960s until the 2000s, Rotterdam had a thriving [squatters' movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squatting), which not only accommodated thousands of people but also created social centres and cultural venues.[^cite_note-67] From this movement came clubs like Boogjes, Eksit, Nighttown, Vlerk and Waterfront. A major reference was [Poortgebouw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poortgebouw), which was squatted in 1980 and quickly legalised.

Rotterdam also became the home of [Gabber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabber), a type of hardcore electronic music popular in the mid-1990s, with hard beats and samples. Groups like [Neophyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neophyte_\(band\)) and [Rotterdam Terror Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Terror_Corps) (RTC) started in Rotterdam, playing at clubs like Parkzicht. In the years 2005–2011, the city struggled with keeping venues for pop music; many of them suffered severe financial problems.[^cite_note-68] This resulted in the disappearance of the major music venues Nighttown and WATT and smaller stages such as Waterfront, Exit, and Heidegger. The city today has a few stages for [pop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pop_music), like [Rotown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotown), [Poortgebouw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poortgebouw) and Annabel. Additionally, the venue [WORM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WORM_\(Rotterdam\)) focuses on [experimental music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_music), as well as various other cultural activities.

WORM also screens films and hosts a film lab, *Filmwerkplaats*.[^cite_note-69] In fact, Rotterdam has an extensive film history,[^cite_note-70] ranging from avant-garde classics, such as [The Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_brug) ([Ivens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joris_Ivens), 1928), to internationally acclaimed documentaries from the post-war era, such as [Steady!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houen_zo!) (Van der Horst, 1952), and all kinds of fiction films. Of major importance within this context has also been the [International Film Festival Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Film_Festival_Rotterdam) (IFFR), an annual event that lasts more than ten days (end of January, beginning of February), which has been organized since 1972. Besides the IFFR, several smaller film festivals take place in Rotterdam too, such as the [Architecture Film Festival Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_Film_Festival_Rotterdam) (AFFR).



[Summer Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Carnival) in Rotterdam

Throughout the year, many different festivals take place in Rotterdam. There are the summer festivals celebrating the city's multicultural population and identity, such as the Caribbean-inspired "[Summer Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Carnival)", the Dance Parade, Rotterdam 666, and the Metropolis pop festival. There are also [Poetry International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry_International_Web) (in June), the [North Sea Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_Jazz_Festival) (in July), the Valery Gergiev Festival (in September), and, also in September, the festival 'September in Rotterdam', the festival 'World of the Witte de With Quaret', and the [World Port Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Port_Days).

### Rivalry

There is a healthy competition with [Amsterdam](844.md), which is often viewed as the cultural capital of the Netherlands. This rivalry is most common amongst the city's football supporters, [Feyenoord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feyenoord) (Rotterdam) and [Ajax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Ajax) (Amsterdam), and it is so severe that football games are only played with the home crowd present due to out-of-control riots in the past. There is a saying: "Amsterdam to party, Den Haag (The Hague) to live, Rotterdam to work". Another one, more popular with Rotterdammers, is "Money is earned in Rotterdam, distributed in The Hague and spent in Amsterdam".[^cite_note-75] Another saying that reflects both the rivalry between Rotterdam and Amsterdam is "Amsterdam has it, Rotterdam doesn't need it". Bright magazine editor Erwin van der Zande notes that this phrase is on T-shirts in Rotterdam.[^cite_note-76]

## Architecture

See also: [List of tallest buildings in Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Rotterdam)



The Wilhelmina pier at the [Kop van Zuid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kop_van_Zuid) in the distance, on the left the [Erasmus Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_Bridge) can be seen

Rotterdam has become world famous because of its modern and groundbreaking architecture. Throughout the years the city has been nicknamed *[Manhattan](45470.md) at the Meuse*[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] and *The architectural capital of the Netherlands*[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] both for its skyline and because it is home to internationally leading architectural firms involved in the design of famous buildings and bridges in other big cities. Examples include [OMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_for_Metropolitan_Architecture) ([Rem Koolhaas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rem_Koolhaas)), [MVRDV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MVRDV), [Neutelings & Riedijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutelings_Riedijk_Architects) and [Erick van Egeraat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erick_van_Egeraat).[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] It has the reputation of being a platform for architectural development and education through the NAi ([Netherlands Architecture Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Architecture_Institute)), which is open to the public and has a variety of exhibitions on architecture and urban planning issues, and previously the [Berlage Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlage_Institute), a postgraduate laboratory of architecture. The city has 38 skyscrapers and 352 high-rises and has many skyscrapers planned or under construction.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] The top 5 highest buildings in the Netherlands consist entirely of buildings in Rotterdam.[^cite_note-90] It is home to the two tallest buildings in the Netherlands, the [Maastoren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastoren) with a height of 165 meters and the [Zalmhaven Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Zalmhaven) (completed in 2021) with a height of 215 meters.

### History

In 1898, the 45 m (148 ft) high-rise office building the White House (in Dutch [Witte Huis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witte_Huis)) was completed, at that time the tallest office building in Europe. In the first decades of the 20th century, some influential architecture in the modern style was built in Rotterdam. Notable are the [Van Nelle fabriek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Nelle_Factory) (1929) a monument of modern factory design by [Brinkman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Brinkman) and [Van der Vlugt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leendert_van_der_Vlugt), the [Jugendstil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau) clubhouse of the [Royal Maas Yacht Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Maas_Yacht_Club) designed by Hooijkaas jr. en Brinkman (1909), and [Feyenoord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feyenoord)'s football stadium [De Kuip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Kuip) (1936), also by Brinkman and Van der Vlugt. The architect [J. J. P. Oud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobus_Oud) was a famous Rotterdammer in those days. The Van Nelle Factory obtained the status of UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 2014. During the early stages of [World War II](32927.md), the centre of Rotterdam was [bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Blitz) by the German [Luftwaffe](17885.md), destroying many of the older buildings in the centre of the city. After an initial crisis of reconstruction, the centre of Rotterdam has become the site of the ambitious new architecture.[^cite_note-91]



The [Cube Houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_house), popularly known as the *Blaak-forest* in 2014



The [Markthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_hall_\(Rotterdam\)) at night as seen from the Binnenrotte

Rotterdam is also famous for its [Lijnbaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijnbaan) 1952 by architects Broek en Bakema, Peperklip by architect Carel Weeber, and the Kubuswoningen or [cube houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_house) designed by architect [Piet Blom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piet_Blom) in 1984.

&#x20;The newest landmark in Rotterdam is the [Markthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_hall_\(Rotterdam\)), designed by architect firm [MVRDV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MVRDV). In addition to that, there are many internationally well-known architects based in Rotterdam, like [O.M.A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_for_Metropolitan_Architecture) ([Rem Koolhaas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rem_Koolhaas)), [Neutelings & Riedijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutelings_Riedijk_Architects) and [Erick van Egeraat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erick_van_Egeraat), to name a few. Two architectural landmarks are located in the Lloydkwartier: the STC college building and the [Schiecentrale 4b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schiecentrale). The construction of the Depot of the [Museum Boijmans Van Beuningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Boijmans_Van_Beuningen) was started in 2003 and was officially opened by King Willem-Alexander on 5 November 2021. It is the world's first fully accessible art depot.[^cite_note-92]

- The [Erasmusbrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmusbrug) (1996) is a 790-meter (2,600 ft) cable-stayed bridge linking the north and south of Rotterdam. It is held up by a 138 m (453 ft) tall pylon with a characteristic bend, earning the bridge its nickname 'De Zwaan' ('the Swan').

- Rotterdam has the tallest residential building in the Netherlands: the [De Zalmhaven Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Zalmhaven) (215 m (705.4 ft)).

- Rotterdam is also home to the tallest office building in the Netherlands, the '[Maastoren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastoren)' (164.75 m or 540.5 ft), which houses [Deloitte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte). This office tower surpassed the 'Delftse Poort' (160 m or 520 ft), which houses the Nationale-Nederlanden insurance company, part of [ING Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ING_Group), as the tallest office tower in 2009.

### Parks

&#x20;Within Rotterdam's urban structure, parks and greenery play an important role. A number of well-known parks in Rotterdam are:



Arboretum Trompenburg

- *Het Park* ([Het Park bij de Euromast](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Het%20Park%20\(Rotterdam\))) is a 70-acre park on the Maas, south of the Westzeedijk, at the [Euromast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromast). The eastern half of the park was constructed between 1852 and 1863 to a design by the firm Jan David Zocher. The western part was added in 1866 with some modifications. [National Heritage site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijksmonument) since 2011. Originally, the park continued across the Westzeedijk, where the Medical Faculty was built in the 1960s (now Erasmus MC). See also: Museumpark.

Witteveen, who also designed [Museum Boijmans Van Beuningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Boijmans_Van_Beuningen). Today, several museums have clustered around the park.

- [Diergaarde Blijdorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diergaarde_Blijdorp), which is situated on the northwest side of Rotterdam, complete with a walkthrough sea aquarium called the [Oceanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanium).

#### Green activities

Since 28 May 1994, Rotterdam has had the phenomenon [*Opzoomeren*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/opzoomeren). 15% of Rotterdam residents (about 100,000 residents) say they participate in this phenomenon.[^cite_note-97] At the end of 2020, the city has a record number of 2,503 Opzoomer streets,[^cite_note-98] which is mainly reflected in the construction of facade gardens.



Dakpark Rotterdam

The municipality of Rotterdam is encouraging the construction of [green roofs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_roof). There is an attractive subsidy for roof owners, and the city has now provided a number of municipal buildings with a green roof. As of 1 January 2020, the water storage capacity requirement has been increased to 30 liters of water storage capacity per square metre. This reduces the burden on the sewer system during heavy rainfall and reduces the risk of flooding on the street.[^cite_note-99]

- The city's largest green roof is located on top of the [Groothandelsgebouw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groothandelsgebouw) next to Central Station.

- The *Dakakker* is the largest roof farm in Europe on top of the Schieblok.

- The *Dakpark* is an elongated, narrow park in the district [Bospolder-Tussendijken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bospolder-Tussendijken) in Rotterdam-West. It has been built at a height of about nine meters, is about 85 meters wide and extends for about a kilometer from Hudson Square to near Marconi Square.

## Sports

Rotterdam calls itself *Sportstad* (City of Sports). The city annually organises several world-renowned sporting events. Some examples are the [Rotterdam Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Marathon), the [World Port Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Port_Tournament), and the [Rotterdam World Tennis Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Open). Rotterdam has also hosted a race of the [Red Bull Air Race World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Air_Race_World_Championship) and the car racing event [Monaco aan de Maas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monaco_aan_de_Maas) (Monaco at the Meuse).

The city is also the home of many sports clubs and some historic and iconic athletes.

### Football



[Robin van Persie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_van_Persie) began his career with [Excelsior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excelsior_Rotterdam) and broke through in Feyenoord.



De Kuip, Feyenoord home stadium

Rotterdam is the home of three professional [football](10568.md) clubs, being [first-tier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eredivisie) clubs [Feyenoord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feyenoord), [Sparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparta_Rotterdam), and [Excelsior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excelsior_Rotterdam).

Feyenoord, founded in 1908 and the dominant of the three professional clubs, has won sixteen national titles since the introduction of professional football in the Netherlands. It won the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) as the first Dutch club in 1970 and won the World Cup for club teams in the same year. In 1974, they were the first Dutch club to win the [UEFA Cup](232175.md), and in 2002, Feyenoord won the UEFA Cup again. In 2008, the year of their 100-year anniversary, Feyenoord won the [KNVB Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KNVB_Cup).

Seating 51,480, its 1937 stadium, called *Stadion Feijenoord* but popularly known as [De Kuip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Kuip) ('the Tub'), is the second-largest in the country, after the [Amsterdam Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Arena). De Kuip, located in the southeast of the city, has hosted many international football games, including the final of [Euro 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2000), and has been awarded a FIFA 5-star ranking. There were concrete plans to build a new stadium with a capacity of at least 63,000 seats; these fell through after supporter backlash and lack of funds.

Sparta, founded in 1888 and situated in the northwest of Rotterdam, won the national title six times; Excelsior (founded 1902), in the northeast, has never won any.

### Marathon



Runners during the marathon in Rotterdam

Rotterdam has its own annual international [marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Marathon), which offers one of the fastest courses in the world. From 1985 until 1998, the [world record](310762.md) was set in Rotterdam, first by [Carlos Lopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Lopes) and later in 1988 by [Belayneh Densamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belayneh_Densamo).

In 1998, the world record for women was set by [Tegla Loroupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tegla_Loroupe), in a time of 2\:20.47. Loroupe won the Rotterdam Marathon three consecutive times, from 1997 to 1999.

The track record for men is held by [Bashir Abdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashir_Abdi), who ran a time of 2\:03.36 in 2021. The female record was set in 2012 when [Tiki Gelana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiki_Gelana) finished the race in 2\:18.58. Gelana went on to become the 2012 Olympic champion in London a few months later.

The marathon starts and ends on the *[Coolsingel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coolsingel)* in the heart of Rotterdam. It attracts a total of 900,000 visitors.

### Tennis



Arthur Ashe at the [1975 ABN World Tennis Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_ABN_World_Tennis_Tournament)

Since 1972, Rotterdam has hosted the indoor hard court [ABN AMRO World Tennis Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Open) (part of the [ATP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Tennis_Professionals) Tour) ever since the event was first organised in 1972, when it was won by [Arthur Ashe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ashe). Ashe went on to win the tournament two more times, making him the singles title record holder.

Former [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Championships,_Wimbledon) winner [Richard Krajicek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Krajicek) became the tournament director after his retirement in 2000. The latest edition of the tournament attracted a total of 116,354 visitors.[^cite_note-102]

### Tour de France

In November 2008 Rotterdam was chosen as the host of the [Grand Départ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_D%C3%A9part) of the [2010 Tour de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Tour_de_France). Rotterdam won the selection over the Dutch city of [Utrecht](50333.md). Germany's Düsseldorf had previously also expressed interest in hosting. The [Amaury Sport Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amaury_Sport_Organisation) (ASO), the organizer of the Tour de France, said in a statement on its website that it chose Rotterdam because, in addition to it being another big city, like London, to showcase the use of bikes for urban transportation, it provided a location well-positioned considering the rest of the route envisioned for the 2010 event. The start in Rotterdam was the fifth to take place in the Netherlands. The [prologue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prologue) was a 7 km (4.35 mi) individual time trial crossing the centre of the city. The first regular stage left the [Erasmusbrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmusbrug) and went south, towards Brussels.[^cite_note-103]

The second stage of the [2015 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Tour_de_France) took the riders through Rotterdam on their way to [Neeltje Jans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neeltje_Jans) in Zeeland.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

The 2024 edition of the [Tour de France Femmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tour_de_France_Femmes) began in Rotterdam, with three stages in the Netherlands.[^cite_note-106]

### Rowing

Members of the student rowing club [Skadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skadi_\(rowing_club\)) were part of the '[Holland Acht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland_Acht)', winning a gold medal at the [Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rowing_at_the_Summer_Olympics) in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Summer_Olympics).[^cite_note-107] Since the opening in April 2013, Rotterdam hosts the rowing venue [Willem-Alexander Baan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem-Alexander_Baan) that hosted the [2016 World Rowing Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_World_Rowing_Championships) for Seniors, U23 and Juniors.

### Field hockey

In [field hockey](10886.md), Rotterdam has the largest hockey club in the Netherlands, [HC Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HC_Rotterdam), with its own stadium in the north of the city and nearly 2,400 members. The first men's and women's teams both play on the highest level in the Dutch *Hoofdklasse*.

### Baseball

Rotterdam is home to the most successful European [baseball](3850.md) team, [Neptunus Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DOOR_Neptunus), winning the most [European Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Cup_\(baseball\)).

### Boxing



Bep van Klaveren

Rotterdam has a long boxing tradition starting with [Bep van Klaveren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bep_van_Klaveren) (1907–1992), aka 'The Dutch Windmill', Gold medal winner of the 1928 [Amsterdam Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Olympics), followed by professional boxers like [Regilio Tuur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regilio_Tuur) and Don Diego Poeder.

### Swimming

Rotterdam's swimming tradition started with [Marie Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Braun), also known as Zus (sister) Braun, who was coached to a gold medal at the 1928 Amsterdam Olympics by her mother, Ma Braun, and 3 European titles three years later in Paris. In her career as a 14-time national champion, she broke 6 world records. Ma Braun later also coached the Rotterdam-born, three-times Olympic champion [Rie Mastenbroek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rie_Mastenbroek) during the Berlin Olympics in 1936. In later years, [Inge de Bruijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inge_de_Bruijn) became a Rotterdam sports icon as a triple Olympic gold medal winner in 2000 and a triple European gold medal winner in 2001.

### Sailing

Olympic gold medalist, in the [O-Jolle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O-Jolle) during the [1936 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing_at_the_1936_Summer_Olympics), [Daan Kagchelland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daan_Kagchelland) was born in Rotterdam and was a member of the [Rotterdamsche Zeil Vereeniging](https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdamsche%20Zeil%20Vereeniging). The Kralingse Plas was and is still a source of Olympic sailors like [Koos de Jong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koos_de_Jong), [Ben Verhagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Verhagen), [Henny Vegter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henny_Vegter), [Serge Kats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serge_Kats) and [Margriet Matthijsse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margriet_Matthijsse).

### Motorcycle racing

Motorcycle speedway was staged in the Feyenoord Stadium after the Second World War. The team which raced in a Dutch league was known as the Feyenoord Tigers. The team included Dutch riders and some English and Australian riders.

## Transportation

See also: [Rotterdam Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Metro)



Rotterdam's new [Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Centraal_railway_station) reopened in March 2014, designed to handle up to 320,000 passengers daily.



A Bombardier 5700 series EMU between [Vlaardingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlaardingen) and [Hoek van Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hook_of_Holland)

Main article: [Transport in Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Rotterdam)

Rotterdam offers connections by international, national, regional and local public transport systems, as well as by the Dutch [motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorway) network.

Several motorways form part of the Rotterdam ring road; the [A20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A20_motorway_\(Netherlands\)) (Ring North), the [A16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A16_motorway_\(Netherlands\)) (Ring East), [A15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A15_motorway_\(Netherlands\)) (Ring South), and the [A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4_motorway_\(Netherlands\)). The following two other motorways also serve Rotterdam: the [A13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A13_motorway_\(Netherlands\)) and the [A29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A29_motorway_\(Netherlands\)). Rotterdam is also home to several tunnels like the Beneluxtunnel and several bridges.

Rotterdam is served by one international airport. [Rotterdam The Hague Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_The_Hague_Airport) (formerly known as *Zestienhoven*) is the third-largest [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) in the country. The much larger [Amsterdam Airport Schiphol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Airport_Schiphol) is more frequently used by people travelling to and from Rotterdam. The airport is located 58 km (36 mi) north east of Rotterdam. [NS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Spoorwegen) operates direct train services between Rotterdam and Schiphol.

The main [Rotterdam Centraal station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_Centraal_station) is well connected to the Dutch railway network and has several international connections. Domestic destinations include [Dordrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dordrecht), [Breda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breda), [Eindhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven) and further south. [Flushing (Vlissingen)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing,_Netherlands) in Zeeland. [The Hague](30269.md), [Amsterdam](844.md) to the north west. [Utrecht](50333.md) towards [Groningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groningen) and [Leeuwarden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeuwarden) in the North East.

Passengers can also board direct international trains to Belgium (mostly Antwerp and Brussels) and [France](5843419.md) (mostly Paris sometimes beyond) and London [UK](31717.md).

Ferries also leave daily from the [Europoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europoort) to [Kingston Upon Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingston_Upon_Hull) in the [UK](31717.md) daily, courtesy of [P\&O Ferries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P\&O_Ferries), as well as from [Hoek van Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoek_van_Holland) to [Harwich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harwich).

### City level public transport

In Rotterdam, public transport services are provided by trains operating [Nederlandse Spoorwegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nederlandse_Spoorwegen) services between local stations. [Rotterdamse Elektrische Tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdamse_Elektrische_Tram) operating metro, tram and bus services and bus services by [Arriva Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arriva), Connexxion, Qbuzz and [Transdev Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connexxion).

In 1968, Rotterdam was the first Dutch city to operate a [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system. The metro system consists of three main lines, each of which has its own variants. The metro network has 78.3 km (48.7 mi) of rail tracks, and there are 70 stations, which makes it the largest of the Benelux. 5 lines operate the system: 3 lines (A, B and C) on the east–west line and two (D and E) on the north–south line. Opened in 2006, Line E (RandstadRail) connects Rotterdam with The Hague.

The [Rotterdam tramway network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Rotterdam) offers 9 regular tram lines and 4 special tram lines with a total length of 93.4 km (58.0 mi).

The Waterbus network consists of seven lines. The mainline (Line 20) stretches from Rotterdam to Dordrecht. The ferry carries about 130 passengers, and there is space for 60 [bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle).

Rotterdam has city and port connections throughout the world. Rotterdam features in [Edgar Allan Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allan_Poe)'s short story "[The Unparalleled Adventure of One Hans Pfaall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Unparalleled_Adventure_of_One_Hans_Pfaall)" (1835).[^cite_note-131]

Rotterdam features in J.T. [Sheridan Le Fanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheridan_Le_Fanu)'s "Strange Event in the Life of Schalken the Painter" (1839).

In 1996, the [British](31717.md) band [the Beautiful South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beautiful_South) recorded a song named after this region titled "[Rotterdam (or Anywhere)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_\(or_Anywhere\))".[^cite_note-132]

Part of [Jackie Chan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackie_Chan)'s 1998 film *[Who Am I?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Am_I%3F_\(1998_film\))* is set in Rotterdam.

*[Ender's Shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ender's_Shadow)*, part of the series *[Ender's Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ender's_Game),* is partially set in Rotterdam.

In the 2004 video game *[Hitman: Contracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitman:_Contracts)*, the missions "Rendezvous in Rotterdam" and "Deadly Cargo" are both set in Rotterdam.

The [2017 Laurence Olivier Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Laurence_Olivier_Awards)-winning play *[Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotterdam_\(play\))*, written by [Jon Brittain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jon_Brittain), is set in the city.

In *[Battlefield V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battlefield_V)*, this city appears in two launch maps, Rotterdam and Devastation, featuring battles between the [British Army](4887.md) and the [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht). Historically, the [Witte Huis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witte_Huis) was almost untouched by the WWII bombings, and this building can be seen both in-game and in the real world.
