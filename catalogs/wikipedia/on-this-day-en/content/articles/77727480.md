The [Gaza war](74998519.md), which began as a result of the Hamas-led [October 7 attacks](74998317.md), has resulted in significant destruction and damage to numerous religious sites including [mosques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosques) and [churches](6325.md) in Gaza, particularly by the [Israeli Defense Forces](39237.md).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

On 19 October 2023, an [Israeli airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Porphyrius_airstrike) hit the [Church of Saint Porphyrius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Porphyrius), where 500 people were sheltering.[^cite_note-4] On 8 November 2023, Israel bombed and destroyed the Khalid bin al-Walid Mosque.[^cite_note-5] By 13 November 2023, at least sixty mosques had been destroyed by Israeli bombs.[^cite_note-6] In December 2023, an Israeli bombing destroyed the [Great Mosque of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Mosque_of_Gaza).[^cite_note-7] At least seven people were killed in an Israeli airstrike on a [Rafah](584373.md) mosque full of displaced people on 23 February 2024.[^cite_note-8] Five people were killed in a mosque in northern Gaza that was bombed without warning.[^cite_note-9] The al-Riad mosque in [Khan Younis](101330.md) was heavily damaged by an Israeli bombing on 9 March 2024.[^cite_note-10] On 24 August 2024, Israeli forces attacked and destroyed a 96-year-old historic Bani Saleh Mosque in [Khan Yunis](101330.md), where they also insulted the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) and then [set it on fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran_desecration).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

By 10 March 2024, more than 1,000 mosques had been destroyed by Israeli attacks.[^cite_note-13] In May 2024, an Israeli bombing on a mosque in Gaza City reportedly killed at least 10 people.[^cite_note-14]

## Background

See also: [Gaza war](74998519.md) and [October 7 attacks](74998317.md)

The invasion of Gaza, which began on October 7, 2023, was triggered by a violent attack by Hamas on Israeli communities on the outskirts of Gaza. Hamas dubbed the operation "Al Aqsa flood", a reference to the [Al Aqsa mosque](65759.md) in the [old city of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Jerusalem\)), and cited Israeli encroachments on the that site as one of the reasons for its attack.[^cite_note-Usher-15] Israel's military response caused widespread destruction in Gaza, with significant damage to religious sites.

According to a 2009 report published by [The Washington Institute for Near East Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Washington_Institute_for_Near_East_Policy), Hamas made wide use of civilian facilities as a cover for its military activities. The report said the "schools, mosques, hospitals, and civilian housing became weapons storage facilities, Hamas headquarters, and fighting positions".[^cite_note-16] Hamas denies such allegations. According to international humanitarian law, the deliberate targeting of religious buildings during conflict is considered a [war crime](48626.md), unless such sites are used for military purposes.[^cite_note-auto1-17]

The [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) recorded that there were 1,230 mosques in the Gaza Strip in 2021.[^cite_note-18] According to the Gaza local authorities, 1,000 mosques have been destroyed in Israeli attacks on the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) since October 7, 2023, with reconstruction costs estimated at \$500 million.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Other reports indicate that 378 mosques were targeted during the conflict, although this number has not been independently verified. By January 2024, the [BBC](19344654.md) had confirmed 72 incidents of mosques being damaged or destroyed between 7 October and 31 December 2023.[^cite_note-auto1-17][^cite_note-auto2-21]

Reports also stated that three churches were damaged during the conflict. In January 2024, the [BBC](19344654.md) confirmed two incidents of churches being damaged or destroyed between 7 October and 31 December 2023.[^cite_note-auto1-17][^cite_note-auto2-21]

## Impact

See also: [Gaza genocide](75668427.md)

The destruction of religious places has had a profound impact on the local community. As many of these sites served as shelters for displaced Palestinians, their destruction resulted in countless casualties.[^cite_note-42] The loss of these cultural and religious landmarks has been described as a form of [cultural genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_genocide).[^cite_note-\:1-24] The damage to these sites has also affected the religious and cultural practices of the local population, further exacerbating the humanitarian crisis in Gaza.[^cite_note-auto1-17]

Hundreds of [imams](15240.md) and religious scholars have also been killed as a result of the campaign in Gaza. The Palestinian [Ministry of Awqaf and Religious Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Awqaf_and_Religious_Affairs_\(Palestine\)) has said that about 300 Islamic scholars, including Quran teachers, Islamic preachers and imams, have been killed in military strikes.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]
