**Terrorism in Russia** has a long history starting from the time of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). [Terrorism](30636.md), in the modern sense,[^cite_note-1] means [violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence) against civilians to achieve political or [ideological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideological) objectives by creating extreme fear.[^cite_note-2]

Terrorism was an important tool used by revolutionaries in the early 20th century to disrupt the social, political, and economic system and enable rebels to bring down the Tsarist government of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).[^cite_note-3]

Starting from the end of the 20th century, significant terrorist activity has taken place in Russia, most notably the [Budyonnovsk hospital hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budyonnovsk_hospital_hostage_crisis), the [1999 apartment bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_apartment_bombings), the [Moscow theater hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_theater_hostage_crisis), the [Beslan school siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beslan_school_siege), and most recently the [Crocus City Hall attack](76418995.md) and the [2024 Dagestan attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Dagestan_attacks). Many more acts of terrorism have been committed in major Russian cities, as well as the regions of [Chechnya](6095.md) and [Dagestan](751099.md).

## 19th century

German Social Democrat [Karl Kautsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Kautsky) traces the origins of [terrorism](30636.md), including the terrorism seen in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), to the "[Reign of Terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reign_of_Terror)" of the [French Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution).[^cite_note-Kautsky-4][^cite_note-5] Others emphasize the role of Russian revolutionary movements during the 19th century, especially [Narodnaya Volya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narodnaya_Volya_\(organization\)) ("People's Will") and the [Nihilist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nihilist_movement), which included several thousand followers. "People's Will" organized one of the first [political terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_terrorism) campaigns in history. In March 1881, it assassinated the Emperor of Russia [Alexander II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_II_of_Russia), who twenty years earlier had [emancipated the Russian serfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emancipation_reform_of_1861_in_Russia).[^cite_note-Radzinsky-6]

Important ideologists of these groups were [Mikhail Bakunin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Bakunin) and [Sergey Nechayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergey_Nechayev), who was described in [Fyodor Dostoevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Dostoevsky)'s novel *[The Possessed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Possessed_\(novel\))*.[^cite_note-Radzinsky-6] Nechayev argued that the purpose of [revolutionary terror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_terror) is not to gain the support of the masses, but on the contrary, to inflict misery and fear on the common population. According to Nechayev, a revolutionary must terrorize civilians in order to incite rebellions. He wrote:[^cite_note-Radzinsky-6]

"A revolutionary must infiltrate all social formations including the police. He must exploit rich and influential people, subordinating them to himself. He must aggravate the miseries of the common people, so as to exhaust their patience and incite them to rebel. And, finally, he must ally himself with the savage word of the violent criminal, the only true revolutionary in Russia".

"The Revolutionist is a doomed man. He has no private interests, no affairs, sentiments, ties, property nor even a name of his own. His entire being is devoured by one purpose, one thought, one passion – the revolution. Heart and soul, not merely by word but by deed, he has severed every link with the social order and with the entire civilized world; with the laws, good manners, conventions, and morality of that world. He is its merciless enemy and continues to inhabit it with only one purpose – to destroy it."

According to historian and writer [Edvard Radzinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Radzinsky), Nechayev's ideas and tactics were widely used by [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) and other Russian revolutionaries.[^cite_note-Radzinsky-6]

The [SR Combat Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SR_Combat_Organization) was founded in 1902 and operated as an autonomous branch of the [Socialist Revolutionary Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Revolutionary_Party) responsible for assassinating government officials, was led by [Grigory Gershuni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigory_Gershuni) and operated separately from the party so as not to jeopardize its political actions. SRCO agents assassinated two Ministers of the Interior, [Dmitry Sipyagin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Sergeyevich_Sipyagin) and [V. K. von Plehve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_von_Plehve), [Grand Duke Sergei Aleksandrovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duke_Sergei_Alexandrovich_of_Russia), the Governor of [Ufa](321112.md) N. M. Bogdanovich, and many other high-ranking officials.[^cite_note-7] It has been estimated that all together in the last twenty years of the Tsarist regime (1897–1917) more than 17,000 people were killed or wounded in terror attacks.[^cite_note-8]

## Soviet Union

### 1977 Moscow bombings

Main article: [1977 Moscow bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Moscow_bombings)

A series of three bombings in [Moscow](19004.md) on 8 January 1977 killed seven people and seriously injured 37 others. No one claimed responsibility for the bombings, although three members of an [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_nationalism) organization were [executed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_the_Soviet_Union) early in 1979 after a [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB) investigation and a secret trial. Some [Soviet dissidents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_dissidents) said that the bombings were allegedly organized by KGB to frame-up Armenian nationalists who were executed.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-Nekrich-11][^cite_note-12]

## Russian Federation



Photos of killed hostages in the gym of school # 1 in Beslan



A victim of [2010 Moscow metro bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Moscow_metro_bombing)

The [First Chechen War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Chechen_War) (1994–1996) and the [Second Chechen War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Chechen_War) (2000–2009) saw [Chechen nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechen_Republic_of_Ichkeria) transformed into [jihadism](7826589.md). In later years, [the conflict extended beyond Chechnya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurgency_in_the_North_Caucasus), inspiring jihadist movements in [Dagestan](751099.md) and [Ingushetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingushetia). Since the First Chechen war, there has been a number of attacks by jihadists in various Russian cities, with the [Doubrovka Theatre hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_theater_hostage_crisis) in [Moscow](19004.md) (over 150 dead, including 130 hostages, in 2002) and the [Beslan school siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beslan_school_siege) in [North Ossetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Ossetia) (334 dead, including 186 children, in 2004) peaking. There have been numerous other bloody jihadist terrorist attacks, notably at airports and in the Moscow and [St Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Petersburg) metros, with dozens of deaths.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

### 1999 Russian apartment bombings

Main article: [Russian apartment bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_apartment_bombings)

The [Russian apartment bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_apartment_bombings) were a series of bombings in Russia that killed 300 & injured over 1,700. And, together with the [Dagestan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagestan_War), led the country into the [Second Chechen War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Chechen_War). The four bombings took place in the Russian cities of [Buinaksk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buinaksk), [Moscow](19004.md) and [Volgodonsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgodonsk) during early days of September 1999.[^cite_note-\:0-15]

The bombings were followed by a controversial episode when a suspected bomb was found and defused in an apartment block in the Russian city of [Ryazan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryazan) on 22 September, which was then explained to be an exercise by the Russian security services, the FSB.[^cite_note-16]

An official investigation of the bombings was completed only three years later, in 2002. Seven suspects were killed, six have been convicted on terrorism-related charges, and one remains a fugitive. According to the investigation, the Moscow and Volgodonsk bombings were organized and led by [Achemez Gochiyaev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achemez_Gochiyaev), who headed a group of "Karachai Wahhabis", while the Buinaksk bombing was organized and perpetrated by a different group of "Dagestani Wahhabis".[^cite_note-Pokalova-17]

The Russian [Duma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duma) rejected two motions for parliamentary investigation of the Ryazan incident. An independent public commission to investigate the bombings chaired by Duma deputy [Sergei Kovalev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Kovalev) was rendered ineffective because of government refusal to respond to its inquiries. Two key members of the Kovalev Commission, [Sergei Yushenkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Yushenkov) and [Yuri Shchekochikhin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Shchekochikhin), both Duma members, have since died in assassinations in April 2003 and July 2003 respectively. The commission's lawyer [Mikhail Trepashkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Trepashkin) was arrested in October 2003 to become one of the better-known political prisoners in Russia.[^cite_note-\:0-15][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### 21st century

**2002**

The [Moscow theater hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_theater_hostage_crisis) (also known as the 2002 Nord-Ost siege) was the seizure of a crowded Dubrovka Theater by 40 to 50 armed Chechens on 23 October 2002 that involved 850 hostages and ended with the deaths of at least 170 people.

**2004**

A suicide [bombing in downtown Moscow Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2004_Moscow_Metro_bombing) killed 41 people on 6 February.

&#x20;\
&#x20;Simultaneous [suicide bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Russian_aircraft_bombings) brought down two passenger aircraft within one hour of leaving from the Domedodovo airport, Moscow, killing 90 people total on 24 August.

&#x20;\
&#x20;Chechen terrorists seized over 1,000 hostages at a [school in Beslan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beslan_school_siege), North Ossetia on 1 September. The siege ended on 3 September, with more than 300 people dead, most of them children.

&#x20;\
&#x20;**2006**

The [2006 Moscow market bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Moscow_market_bombing) occurred on 21 August 2006, when a [self-made bomb](265112.md) with the power of more than 1 kg of [TNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinitrotoluene) exploded at [Moscow](19004.md)'s [Cherkizovsky Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherkizovsky_Market) frequented by foreign merchants.[^cite_note-20] The bombing killed 13 people and injured 47. In 2008, eight members of the [neo-Nazi](54361.md) organization [The Saviour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Savior_\(paramilitary_organization\)) were sentenced for their roles in the attack.[^cite_note-21]

#### 2010

Main article: [2010 Moscow Metro bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Moscow_Metro_bombings)

In March 2010 suicide bombings were carried out by two women who were aligned with [Caucasus Emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Emirate) and [Al-Qaeda](1921.md). The terrorist attack happened during the morning rush hour of 29 March 2010, at two stations of the Moscow Metro ([Lubyanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubyanka_\(Moscow_Metro\)) and [Park Kultury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Kultury_\(Koltsevaya_Line\))), with roughly 40 minutes interval between. At least 38 people were killed, and over 60 injured.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

#### 2011

Main article: [Domodedovo International Airport bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domodedovo_International_Airport_bombing)

The Domodedovo International Airport bombing was a [suicide bombing](18835454.md) in the international arrival hall of Moscow's [Domodedovo International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domodedovo_International_Airport), in [Domodedovsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domodedovsky_District), [Moscow Oblast](372315.md), on 24 January 2011.

The bombing killed 37 people[^cite_note-37deaths-24] and injured 173 others, including 86 who had to be hospitalised.[^cite_note-BBC-25] Of the casualties, 31 died at the scene, three later in hospitals, one en route to a hospital,[^cite_note-26] one on 2 February after having been put in a coma, and another on 24 February after being hospitalised in grave condition.[^cite_note-37deaths-24]

Russia's [Federal Investigative Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigative_Committee_of_Russia) later identified the suicide bomber as a 20-year-old from the North Caucasus, and said that the attack was aimed "first and foremost" at foreign citizens.[^cite_note-27]

#### 2013

Main articles: [December 2013 Volgograd bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_2013_Volgograd_bombings) and [October 2013 Volgograd bus bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2013_Volgograd_bus_bombing)

In December 2013, two separate suicide bombings a day apart targeted mass transportation in the city of Volgograd, in the Volgograd Oblast of Southern Russia, killing 34 people overall, including both perpetrators who were aligned to [Caucasus Emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Emirate) and [Vilayat Dagestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilayat_Dagestan). The attacks followed a bus bombing carried out in the same city two months earlier.[^cite_note-28]

On 21 October 2013, a suicide bombing took place on a bus in the city of [Volgograd](32585.md), in the [Volgograd Oblast](478748.md) of [Southern Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Russia). The attack was carried out by a female perpetrator named Naida Sirazhudinovna Asiyalova (Russian: Наида Сиражудиновна Асиялова) who was converted to Islam by her husband, she detonated an explosive belt containing 500–600 grams of TNT inside a bus carrying approximately 50 people, killing seven civilians and injuring at least 36 others.[^cite_note-29]

#### 2014

Main articles: [2014 Grozny bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Grozny_bombing) and [2014 Grozny clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Grozny_clashes)

On 5 October 2014 a 19-year-old man named Opti Mudarov went to the town hall where an event was taking place to mark Grozny City Day celebrations in [Grozny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grozny) coinciding with the birthday of [Chechen President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechen_President) [Ramzan Kadyrov](647880.md). Police officers noticed him acting strangely and stopped him. The officers began to search him and the bomb which Mudarov had been carrying exploded. Five officers, along with the suicide bomber, were killed, while 12 others were wounded.[^cite_note-30]

On 4 December 2014, a group of Islamist militants, in three vehicles, killed three traffic policemen, after the latter had attempted to stop them at a checkpoint in the outskirts of [Grozny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grozny).[^cite_note-Guardian-31] The militants then occupied a press building and an abandoned school, located in the center of the city. Launching a [counter-terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-terrorism) operation, security forces, with the use of armored vehicles, attempted to storm the buildings and a firefight ensued.[^cite_note-itartass-thanks-32]

14 policemen, 11 militants and 1 civilian were killed. Additionally 36 policemen were wounded in the incident. The Press House was also burned and severely damaged in the incident.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

#### 2015

Main article: [Metrojet Flight 9268](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrojet_Flight_9268)

Metrojet Flight 9268 was an international chartered passenger flight operated by Russian airline [Kogalymavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kogalymavia) (branded as Metrojet). On 31 October 2015 at 06\:13 local time EST (04\:13 UTC), an Airbus A321-231 operating the flight disintegrated above the northern Sinai following its departure from Sharm El Sheikh International Airport, Egypt, in route to Pulkovo Airport, Saint Petersburg, Russia. All 217 passengers and seven crew members who were on board were killed.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

Shortly after the crash, the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) (ISIL)'s [Sinai Branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_of_Iraq_and_the_Levant_%E2%80%93_Sinai_Province), previously known as [Ansar Bait al-Maqdis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_Bait_al-Maqdis), claimed responsibility for the incident, which occurred in the vicinity of the [Sinai insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_insurgency).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-auto3-39] ISIL claimed responsibility on [Twitter](9988187.md), on video, and in a statement by [Abu Osama al-Masri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Osama_al-Masri), the leader of the group's Sinai branch.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-leaderpost.com-41] ISIL posted pictures of what it said was the bomb in *[Dabiq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dabiq_\(magazine\))*, its online magazine.

By 4 November 2015, British and American authorities suspected that a bomb was responsible for the crash. On 8 November 2015, an anonymous member of the Egyptian investigation team said the investigators were "90 percent sure" that the jet was brought down by a bomb. Lead investigator Ayman al-Muqaddam said that other possible causes of the crash included a [fuel](1205435.md) [explosion](18993857.md), [metal fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatigue_\(material\)), and [lithium batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) overheating.[^cite_note-auto4-42] The Russian [Federal Security Service](385990.md) announced on 17 November that they were sure that it was a terrorist attack, caused by an [improvised bomb](265112.md) containing the [equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_equivalent) of up to 1 kilogram (2.2 lb) of [TNT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinitrotoluene) that detonated during the flight. The Russians said they had found explosive residue as evidence. On 24 February 2016, [Egyptian President](335341.md) [Abdel Fattah el-Sisi](36703624.md) acknowledged that terrorism caused the crash.[^cite_note-el-sisi-43]

#### 2017

Main article: [2017 Saint Petersburg Metro bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Saint_Petersburg_Metro_bombing)

### Accusations of state terrorism

See also: [Russia–European Union relations § Russian destabilization of EU states](3666797.md#Russian_destabilization_of_EU_states), and [Active measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_measures)

Since the dissolution of the Soviet Union in 1991, the government of the Russian Federation has been frequently accused of sponsoring or inspiring terrorist activities inside the country and in other countries in order to achieve its political goals.

Former [FSB](385990.md) officer [Alexander Litvinenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Litvinenko), [Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) and [Hoover Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoover_Institute) scholar [David Satter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Satter),[^cite_note-Satter-70] Russian lawmaker [Sergei Yushenkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Yushenkov), historian [Yuri Felshtinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Felshtinsky), politologist [Vladimir Pribylovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Pribylovsky) and former KGB general [Oleg Kalugin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleg_Kalugin) asserted that [Russian apartment bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Russian_apartment_bombings) were in fact a "[false flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_flag)" attack perpetrated by the FSB (successor to the [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB)) in order to legitimize the resumption of military activities in Chechnya and bring Vladimir Putin and the FSB to power. FSB operatives were actually briefly arrested in the case, but their presence at the crime scene was explained as "training".[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] This view was disputed by philosopher [Robert Bruce Ware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bruce_Ware) and [Richard Sakwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Sakwa),[^cite_note-sakwa2-73][^cite_note-bennett-74][^cite_note-sobell_western-75][^cite_note-sobell_expert-76][^cite_note-bowker-77] but supported by historians [Amy Knight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amy_Knight)[^cite_note-amyknight2012-78][^cite_note-79] and [Karen Dawisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_Dawisha)[^cite_note-80]

Aleksander Litvinenko and investigator [Mikhail Trepashkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Trepashkin) alleged that a [Chechen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chechen_people) FSB agent directed the [Moscow theater hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_theater_hostage_crisis) in 2002.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-cp1-12-82]

In May 2016, [Reuters](18998750.md) published a Special Report titled "How Russia allowed homegrown radicals to go and fight in Syria" that, based on first-hand evidence, said that at least in the period between 2012 and 2014 the Russian government agencies ran a programme to facilitate and encourage Russian radicals and militants to leave Russia and go to Turkey and then on to [Syria](7515849.md); the persons in question had joined jihadist groups, some fighting with the [ISIL](9087364.md). According to the report, the goal has been to eradicate the risk of Islamic terrorism at home; however Russian security officials deny that terrorists were encouraged to leave Russia.[^cite_note-83]

## Investigation and prosecution of alleged terrorism

Russian authorities routinely extort confessions from suspected terrorists using torture, instead of engaging in genuine investigative efforts.[^cite_note-astrakhan-84] According to [Vyacheslav Izmailov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Izmailov), the terrorist kidnappings of journalists and members of international NGOs in 2005 in Chechnya, along with [Andrei Babitsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Babitsky) from [Radio Free Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe), [Arjan Erkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arjan_Erkel) and Kenneth Glack from [Doctors Without Borders](20498.md) were organized by FSB agents.[^cite_note-delivery-85]

Investigative journalist [Yulia Latynina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Latynina) has accused the Russian security services of staging fake terrorist attacks to report false successes in solving those cases, instead of investigating the actual terrorist attacks.[^cite_note-Latyn-86]

Russia reportedly abuses its anti-terrorism and anti-extremism laws.[^cite_note-87] On 10 February 2020, seven Russian anarchists and anti-fascist activists were sentenced to six to eighteen years in prison, based on fabricated terrorism charges. The activist were accused to be members of "The Set" an alleged terrorist organization from [Penza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penza) that aimed to "overthrow the Russian government".[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]
