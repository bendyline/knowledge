**Lin King** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 金翎; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Jīn Líng*; born December 6, 1993) is a Taiwanese and American writer and translator. In 2024, King and writer [Yang Shuang-zi](78414363.md) won the [National Book Award for Translated Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Award_for_Translated_Literature) for the English translation of *[Taiwan Travelogue](80179283.md),* before winning the [International Booker Prize](1526491.md) for it in 2026.

## Early life and education

King was born in New York to Taiwanese parents. Both her parents, Kwang-Yu King and Erin C. Shih, worked in architecture firms in New York City for 10 and 8 years, respectively.[^cite_note-1] When King was one year old, she and her parents returned to Taiwan and settled in the capital, [Taipei](57648.md).[^cite_note-2] King grew up in Taiwan roughly seven years after the [end of martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_in_Taiwan) in Taiwan.[^cite_note-\:2-3] She holds both Taiwan (ROC) and United States citizenship. She is fluent in Mandarin Chinese, Japanese, and English, with some proficiency in Taiwanese Hokkien.[^cite_note-\:1-4]

In Taiwan, King attended the [Taipei American School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_American_School) and graduated in 2012. After high school, she returned to the U.S. to attend [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University). As an undergraduate at Princeton, she became a writer for *[The Daily Princetonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Daily_Princetonian).* She graduated from Princeton with a [Bachelor of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_of_Arts) in English literature, [Creative Writing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Writing), and [East Asian studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Asian_studies) in 2016. She then earned a [Master of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Fine_Arts) (M.F.A.) from [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) in fiction and literary translation in 2022 under [Elissa Schappell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elissa_Schappell) and [Jeremy Tiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremy_Tiang).[^cite_note-\:32-5]

## Career

King's own writing in English has appeared in numerous publications including [*Joyland*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joyland_\(magazine\)), *[Boston Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Review)*, and others.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] In 2018, King received the PEN/Robert J. Dau Short Story Prize for Emerging Writers for her short story, "Appetite", published in *SLICE*.[^cite_note-8] It was subsequently published in that year's edition of *The PEN America Best Debut Short Stories*.[^cite_note-9]

In 2023, King released an English translation of *The Boy from Clearwater: Book 1* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 來自清水的孩子) by Yu Pei-Yun and Zhou Jian-Xin, published by Levine Querido. She had read and worked with its translations in Taiwanese, Mandarin Chinese, and Japanese.[^cite_note-\:2-3] *[Booklist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Booklist)*, in a starred review, called it "a triumph of translation by gifted polyglot King, who artfully rendered the Taiwanese Hoklo, Mandarin Chinese, and Japanese in the original."[^cite_note-10] It later won a [Freeman Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeman_Awards) in 2023.[^cite_note-11] *The Boy from Clearwater: Book 2* received the same award in 2024.[^cite_note-12]

As a graduate student at Columbia University, King worked on *Taiwan Travelogue*, after which it was published in 2024 by [Graywolf Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graywolf_Press).[^cite_note-13] King had met Yang through the [Asian American Writers' Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_American_Writers'_Workshop) where King's translation of an excerpt from Yang's novel *Seasons of Bloom* appeared.[^cite_note-\:3-14] Yang then asked King to read and mull over *Taiwan Travelogue* in Mandarin Chinese for a possible translation, after which King pitched its English translation to numerous publishers before landing at Graywolf Press. There, King worked with [Yuka Igarashi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuka_Igarashi) on calibrating the book's execution as a "meta-novel".[^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-15]

In 2024, King and Yang won the [National Book Award for Translated Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Book_Award_for_Translated_Literature) for the English translation of *Taiwan Travelogue.*[^cite_note-\:0-16] The following year, King and Yang received the Baifang Schell Book Prize for Outstanding Literature Translated from Chinese Language, presented by the [Asia Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Society)'s *China Books Review*.[^cite_note-17] Later in 2025, King's translation won the [American Literary Translators Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Literary_Translators_Association)'s First Translation Prize and was shortlisted for the [National Translation Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Translation_Award).[^cite_note-18] In 2026, King's translation became the first literary work translated from [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese) to win the [International Booker Prize](1526491.md).[^cite_note-19]
