**Dutywa**, also known as **Idutywa**,[^cite_note-2] is a town in [Mbashe Local Municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbhashe_Local_Municipality), [Eastern Cape](313645.md) province, South Africa, that was founded in 1858 as a military fort after a dispute between a [Natal Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natal_Colony) raiding party and its local people.[^cite_note-wildcoasttowns-3] It is named after the [Dutywa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutywa_River?action=edit\&redlink=1), a tributary of the [Mbhashe River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbhashe_River).[^cite_note-\:0-4] The name means "place of disorder" in the [Xhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_language) language;[^cite_note-routes-5] its spelling was officially changed from "Idutywa" to "Dutywa" on 16 July 2004.[^cite_note-\:1-6] The settlement was laid out in 1884 and was made a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality) in 1913.[^cite_note-routes-5] The town is the birthplace of former South African President, [Thabo Mbeki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thabo_Mbeki).[^cite_note-\:2-7]

Dutywa is home to 11,076 people, 96.6% of who are Black African (Xhosa).[^cite_note-\:2-7]
