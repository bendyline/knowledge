# Opera house

This article is about the type of entertainment venue. For other uses, see [Opera House (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_House_\(disambiguation\)).



[Teatro di San Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_di_San_Carlo) in [Naples](55880.md), the world's oldest working opera house.



The [Sydney Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sydney_Opera_House) is one of the world's most recognisable opera houses and landmarks.

An **opera house** is a [theater building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_\(structure\)) used for performances of [opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera). Like many theaters, it usually includes a [stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_\(theatre\)), an [orchestra pit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestra_pit), audience seating, backstage facilities for [costumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costumes) and building sets, as well as offices for the institution's administration.

While some venues are constructed specifically for operas, other opera houses are part of larger [performing arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performing_arts) centers. Indeed, the term *opera house* is often used as a term of prestige for any large performing arts center.

## History

### Greco-Roman antiquity

Based on [Aristoxenus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristoxenus)'s musical system,[^cite_note-1] and paying homage to the architects of [ancient Greek theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_ancient_Greece), [Vitruvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitruvius) described, in the 1st century BC, in his treatise *[De architectura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_architectura)*, the ideal acoustics of theaters. He explained the use of brazen vases that [Mummius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucius_Mummius_Achaicus) had brought to Rome after having had the theater of [Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Corinth) demolished, and as they were probably used in the [Theater of Pompey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_of_Pompey). As wooden theaters were naturally sonorous, these vases, placed between the seats on the stands, served as resonators in the stone buildings: "By means of this arrangement, the voice, which will come from the stage as from a center, will extend in circles, will strike in the cavities of the vases, and will be made stronger and clearer, according to the relationship of consonance that it will have with one of these vases."[^cite_note-2] The [odeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odeon_of_Pericles) built by [Pericles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pericles) near the [Theater of Dionysus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_of_Dionysus) in [Athens](1216.md) was, according to the *[Suda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suda)*, intended for the rehearsal of music that was to be sung in the grand theater or, according to [Plutarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutarch), for the jury to audition musicians competing for a prize.[^cite_note-3]

Ancient theaters provided the ideal conditions, but it was not yet time for opera: the aim was to worship the deities, not to venerate the [muses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muses). The subject was religious, it was accompanied by singing and instrumental music. Worship was public, and the audience was made up of citizens as well as other categories of the population. Four centuries later, the [Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Church) abandoned spectacles as practiced in Antiquity. [Histrions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histrion?action=edit\&redlink=1), representative of [Greco-Roman civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_civilization), gradually disappeared.

### Middle Ages

The [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) saw the abandonment of ancient theaters, which were transformed into gigantic stone [quarries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quarry), like many other ancient buildings, public and private. Music still had its place in worship. It continued to bring audiences together, but its content was completely renewed. The *[Jeu de Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Play_of_Daniel)* ("Play of Daniel") was a sung play, characteristic of the medieval [Renaissance of the 12th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_of_the_12th_century). The subject, taken from the [biblical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible) [Book of Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Daniel), deals with [Israel's captivity in Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_captivity).[^cite_note-The_European_Music_Series-4] The play was written and performed by students of the Episcopal School of [Beauvais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beauvais), located in northern France.[^cite_note-5] In the 15th century, sung theater of a religious nature found a special place in the [mystery plays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mystery_play) performed on cathedral squares. As before, they dealt with sacred subjects, but they were not about worship per se. [Secular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularism) musical theater also existed, but had a more popular and intimate aspect (see, for example, [Adam de la Halle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_de_la_Halle)'s *[Jeu de Robin et Marion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeu_de_Robin_et_Marion)* ("Play of Robin and Marion"), in the 13th century).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-DixonSinclair2008-7]

### Modern period

At the beginning of the 17th century, in Italy, singing underwent yet another renewal, with the emergence of [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) art at the height of the [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance). Italy continues to have many working opera houses,[^cite_note-8] such as the [Teatro Massimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Massimo) in [Palermo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo) (the biggest in the country), the [Teatro di San Carlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_di_San_Carlo) in [Naples](55880.md) and the [Teatro alla Scala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_alla_Scala) in [Milan](36511.md). The [Teatro San Cassiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_San_Cassiano) in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) was the world's first public opera house, inaugurated as such in 1637.[^cite_note-9]

In the 17th and 18th centuries, opera houses were often financed by rulers, nobles, and wealthy people who used [patronage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patronage) of the arts to endorse their political ambitions and social position. There was no opera house in London when [Henry Purcell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Purcell) was composing and the first opera house in Germany, the [Oper am Gänsemarkt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oper_am_G%C3%A4nsemarkt), was built in [Hamburg](13467.md) in 1678, followed by the [Oper am Brühl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oper_am_Br%C3%BChl) in [Leipzig](17955.md) in 1693, and the [Opernhaus vorm Salztor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opernhaus_vorm_Salztor) in [Naumburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naumburg) in 1701. With the rise of [bourgeois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourgeois) and [capitalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) social forms in the 19th century, European culture moved away from its patronage system to a publicly supported system.

Early United States opera houses served a variety of functions in towns and cities, hosting community dances, fairs, plays, and [vaudeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaudeville) shows as well as operas and other musical events. In the 2000s, most opera and theatre companies are supported by funds from a combination of government and institutional [grants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_\(money\)), ticket sales, and private donations.

## Other uses of the term

In the 19th-century United States, many theaters were given the name "opera house", even ones where opera was seldom if ever performed. Opera was viewed as a more respectable art form than [theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater); calling a local theater an "opera house" therefore served to elevate it and overcome objections from those who found the theater morally objectionable.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]
