**Lower Manhattan**, also known as **Downtown Manhattan**, is the southernmost part of the [New York City borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughs_of_New_York_City) of [Manhattan](45470.md). The area is [the historical birthplace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_New_York_City) of New York City[^cite_note-LowerManhattanNYCBirthplace-1] and in the 17th and 18th centuries composed the entirety of the city. Lower Manhattan serves as [the seat of government](531637.md) of both Manhattan and the entire City of New York.[^cite_note-LowerManhattanNYCBirthplace-1] Because there are no municipally defined boundaries for the area, a precise population cannot be quoted, but several sources have suggested that it was one of the fastest-growing locations in New York City between 2010 and 2020, related to the influx of young adults and significant development of new housing units.[^cite_note-LowerManhattanBooming1-2][^cite_note-LowerManhattanBooming2-3]

Despite various definitions of Lower Manhattan, they generally include all of [Manhattan Island](45470.md) south of [14th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Street_\(Manhattan\)), with the [ Bowling Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Green_\(New_York_City\)) and [the Battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battery_\(Manhattan\)) near the southern end. Anchored by [Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street) and the [Financial District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_District,_Manhattan) in Lower Manhattan, New York City is the leading [global center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) for [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_center) and [fintech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fintech).[^cite_note-NYCFinancialAndFintechCapitalWorld-4][^cite_note-5] The Financial District houses the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md), the [Federal Reserve Bank of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_New_York), and other major financial institutions. A center of [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_New_York_City) and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_New_York_City), Lower Manhattan is home to many of New York City's most iconic structures, including [New York City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Hall), the [Woolworth Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolworth_Building), the [Stonewall Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewall_Inn), the *[Charging Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charging_Bull)* of Wall Street, and [One World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_World_Trade_Center), the [tallest skyscraper in the Western Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings).&#x20;

## Geography and neighborhoods

Lower Manhattan is delineated on the north by [14th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Street_\(Manhattan\)), on the west by the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River), on the east by the [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River), and on the south by [New York Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Harbor). Its northern border is designated by thoroughfares about a mile-and-a-half south of [14th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Street_\(Manhattan\)) and a mile north of [Manhattan](45470.md)'s southern tip around [Chambers Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambers_Street_\(Manhattan\)) near the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River) east of the entrances and overpass to the [Brooklyn Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Bridge).[^cite_note-6] Two other major arteries to Lower Manhattan are [Canal Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_Street_\(Manhattan\)), roughly half a mile north of Chambers Street, and [23rd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Street_\(Manhattan\)), roughly half a mile north of 14th Street.

Lower Manhattan's central business district forms the core of the area below Chambers Street and includes the [Financial District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Financial_District_\(Manhattan\)), commonly known as [Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street) after the name of its primary artery, and the [World Trade Center site](67173.md). At the island's southern tip is [Battery Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_Park), near the [Bowling Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Green_Park); [City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Hall) is north of the Financial District. South of Chambers Street are [Battery Park City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_Park_City) and [South Street Seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Street_Seaport). [TriBeCa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TriBeCa) straddles Chambers Street on the west side; at the street's east end is the giant [Manhattan Municipal Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Municipal_Building). North of Chambers Street and the Brooklyn Bridge and south of Canal Street is the [Chinatown neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Manhattan), home to the largest concentration of [Chinese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people_in_New_York_City) in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md).[^cite_note-ManhattanChinatownLargestConcentrationChineseWesternHemisphere-7][^cite_note-fact-sheet-8] Many [court buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Courts) and [other government offices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foley_Square) are located in this area.

The [Lower East Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_East_Side,_Manhattan) neighborhood straddles Canal Street. North of Canal Street and south of 14th Street are [SoHo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoHo), the [Meatpacking District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meatpacking_District,_Manhattan), the [West Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Village), [Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village), [Little Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Italy,_Manhattan), [Nolita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NoLIta,_Manhattan), and the [East Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Village,_Manhattan). Between 14th and [23rd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Street_\(Manhattan\)) Streets are lower [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea,_Manhattan), [Union Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Square_\(New_York_City\)), the [Flatiron District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatiron_District), [Gramercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramercy,_Manhattan), and [Stuyvesant Town–Peter Cooper Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuyvesant_Town%E2%80%93Peter_Cooper_Village).

## History

See also: [History of New York City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_New_York_City) and [History of Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Manhattan)



[New Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam), centered in what eventually became Lower Manhattan, in 1664, the year [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonization_of_the_Americas) took control and renamed it New York

### Lenape and New Netherland

Main article: [History of New York City (prehistory–1664)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_New_York_City_\(prehistory%E2%80%931664\))

The area that would eventually encompass modern-day New York City was inhabited by the [Lenape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenape) people. These groups of culturally and linguistically identical [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas) who spoke an [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_languages) language now referred to as *[Unami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unami_language)*. European settlement began with the founding of a [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) [fur trading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) post in Lower Manhattan, later called [New Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam) ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Nieuw-Amsterdam*) in 1626.[^cite_note-LowerManhattanHistory-9][^cite_note-10] The first fort was built at [The Battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Battery_\(Manhattan\)) to protect [New Netherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland).[^cite_note-NYCPR-11]



[Peter Stuyvesant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Stuyvesant)

In approximately 1626, construction of [Fort Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Amsterdam) began.[^cite_note-NYCPR-11] The [Dutch West Indies Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_West_Indies_Company) subsequently [imported African slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery_in_New_York) to serve as laborers; they helped to build [the wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street) that defended the town against English and native attacks. Early directors included [Willem Verhulst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Verhulst) and [Peter Minuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Minuit). [Willem Kieft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Kieft) became a [director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_New_Netherland) in 1638 but five years later was embroiled in [Kieft's War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kieft's_War) against the Native Americans. The [Pavonia Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavonia_Massacre), across the Hudson River in present-day [Jersey City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jersey_City,_New_Jersey), New Jersey resulted in the death of 80 natives in February 1643. Following the massacre, [Algonquian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_peoples) tribes joined forces and nearly defeated the Dutch. The [Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) sent additional forces to the aid of Kieft, leading to the overwhelming defeat of the Native Americans and a peace treaty on August 29, 1645.[^cite_note-EllisEpic1966-12]<sup>: 37–40 </sup>

On May 27, 1647, [Peter Stuyvesant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Stuyvesant) was inaugurated as [director general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director-General_of_New_Netherland) upon his arrival. The colony was granted self-government in 1652, and New Amsterdam was formally incorporated as a city on February 2, 1653.[^cite_note-EllisEpic1966-12]<sup>: 57 </sup> The first mayors (*[burgemeesters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgemeesters)*) of New Amsterdam, [Arent van Hattem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arent_van_Hattem) and [Martin Cregier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Cregier), were appointed in that year.[^cite_note-13]

### 17th and 18th centuries

Main article: [History of New York City (1665–1783)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_New_York_City_\(1665%E2%80%931783\))

In 1664, the English [conquered the area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Dutch_War) and [renamed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_renaming) it "[New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_New_York)" after the [Duke of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_II_of_England) and the city of [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York) in Yorkshire.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] At that time, people of African descent made up 20% of the population of the city, with European settlers numbering approximately 1,500,[^cite_note-HarrisSlavery2003-16]<sup>: 14 </sup> and people of African descent numbering 375 (with 300 of that 375 enslaved and 75 free).[^cite_note-HarrisSlavery2003-16]<sup>: 22 </sup> While it has been claimed that African slaves comprised 40% of the small population of the city at that time,[^cite_note-Spencer-17] this claim has not been substantiated. During the mid-1600s, farms of free blacks covered 130 acres (53 ha) where [Washington Square Park](745249.md) later developed.[^cite_note-Rothstein-18] The Dutch [briefly regained the city in 1673](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Dutch_War), renaming the city "[New Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orange)", before permanently ceding the colony of [New Netherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland) to the English for what is now [Suriname](26828.md) in November 1674. The new English rulers of the formerly Dutch New Amsterdam and New Netherland renamed the settlement back to New York. As the colony grew and prospered, sentiment also grew for greater autonomy. In the context of the [Glorious Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glorious_Revolution) in England, [Jacob Leisler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Leisler) led [Leisler's Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leisler's_Rebellion) and effectively controlled the city and surrounding areas from 1689 to 1691, before being arrested and executed.



[New York Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Harbor), 1727

By 1700, the Lenape population of New York had diminished to 200.[^cite_note-gothamcenter.org-19] By 1703, 42% of households in New York had slaves, a higher percentage than in [Philadelphia](50585.md) or [Boston](24437894.md).[^cite_note-hh_slavery-20] The 1735 [libel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libel) trial of [John Peter Zenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Peter_Zenger) in the city was a seminal influence on [freedom of the press](105323.md) in North America. It would be a standard for the basic articles of freedom in the [United States Declaration of Independence](31874.md). By the 1740s, with expansion of settlers, 20% of the population of New York were slaves, totaling about 2,500 people.[^cite_note-Rothstein-18] After a series of fires in 1741, the city became panicked that blacks [planned to burn the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Conspiracy_of_1741) in a conspiracy with some poor whites. Historians believe their alarm was mostly fabrication and fear, but officials rounded up 31 blacks and 4 whites, all of whom were convicted of arson and executed. City officials executed 13 blacks by burning them alive and hanged 4 whites and 18 blacks.[^cite_note-21]

In 1754, [Columbia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_University) was founded under [charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter) by [George II of Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_II_of_Great_Britain) as King's College in Lower Manhattan.[^cite_note-22] The [Stamp Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamp_Act) and other British measures fomented dissent, particularly among the [Sons of Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_Liberty), who maintained a long-running skirmish with locally stationed British troops over [Liberty Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Pole) from 1766 to 1776. The [Stamp Act Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamp_Act_Congress) met in New York City in 1765 in the first organized resistance to British authority across the colonies. After the major defeat of the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army) in the [Battle of Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Long_Island), General [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) withdrew to [Manhattan Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Island), but with the subsequent defeat at the [Battle of Fort Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fort_Washington) the island was effectively left to the British. The city became a haven for [loyalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyalist_\(American_Revolution\)) refugees, becoming a British stronghold for the entire war. Consequently, the area also became the focal point for Washington's [espionage and intelligence-gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligence_in_the_American_Revolutionary_War) throughout the war. In 1771, [Bear Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_Market_\(Manhattan\)) was established along the Hudson River shoreline on land donated by [Trinity Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Church_\(Manhattan\)), and replaced by [Washington Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Market) in 1813.[^cite_note-23]

New York City was greatly damaged twice by [fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_New_York_\(1776\)) of suspicious origin during British military rule. The city became the political and military center of operations for the British in North America for the remainder of the war and a haven for Loyalist refugees. [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army) officer [Nathan Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Hale) was hanged in Manhattan for espionage. In addition, the British began to hold the majority of captured American [prisoners of war](25008.md) aboard [prison ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prison_Ship_Martyrs'_Monument) in [Wallabout Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallabout_Bay), across the [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River) in [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn). More Americans died from neglect aboard these ships than died in all the battles of the war. British occupation lasted until November 25, 1783. George Washington triumphantly returned to the city that day, as [the last British forces left the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evacuation_Day_\(New_York\)).

Starting in 1785, the [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Confederation) met in New York City under the [Articles of Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation). In 1789, New York City became the first [national capital of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_in_the_United_States#United_States) under the new [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution). The Constitution also created the current [Congress of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_United_States), and its first sitting was at [Federal Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Hall) on Wall Street. The first [United States Supreme Court](31737.md) sat there. The [United States Bill of Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bill_of_Rights) was drafted and ratified there. George Washington was inaugurated at Federal Hall.[^cite_note-24] New York City remained the capital of the U.S. until 1790, when the role was transferred to Philadelphia.

### 19th century

Main articles: [History of New York City (1784–1854)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_New_York_City_\(1784%E2%80%931854\)) and [History of New York City (1855–1897)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_New_York_City_\(1855%E2%80%931897\))



*Sidney's Map Twelve Miles Around New York*, 1849 lithograph by James Charles Sidney



[Cooper Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Union) at [Astor Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astor_Place), one of Lower Manhattan's most storied buildings, where [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) gave his famed [Cooper Union speech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Union_speech) on February 27, 1860

New York grew as an economic center, first as a result of [Alexander Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton)'s policies and practices as the first [Secretary of the Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Treasury) and, later, with the opening of the [Erie Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_Canal) in 1825, which connected the [Atlantic port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey) to the vast agricultural markets of the North American interior.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-lankevich-p67-26] [Immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_immigration_to_the_United_States) resumed after being slowed by wars in Europe, and a new [street grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_grid) system, the [Commissioners' Plan of 1811](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioners'_Plan_of_1811), expanded to encompass all of Manhattan. Early in the 19th century, the [landfill](211285.md) was used to expand Lower Manhattan from the natural Hudson shoreline at [Greenwich Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Street) to [West Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Street_\(Manhattan\)).[^cite_note-27]

In 1898, the modern [City of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Greater_New_York) was formed with the consolidation of [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Brooklyn) (until then an independent city), Manhattan and outlying areas.[^cite_note-28] The borough of [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn) incorporated the independent City of Brooklyn, recently joined to Manhattan by the [Brooklyn Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Bridge) in Lower Manhattan. Municipal governments contained within the boroughs were abolished, and the county governmental functions, housed in Lower Manhattan after unification, were absorbed by the city or each borough.[^cite_note-29]

Washington Market was located between [Barclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclay_Street_station) and Hubert Streets, and from [Greenwich Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Street) to [West Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Street_\(Manhattan\)).[^cite_note-30] It was demolished in the 1960s and replaced by a new Independence Plaza, [Washington Market Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Market_Park), and other developments.

#### Construction boom

Lower Manhattan retains the most irregular street [grid plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_plan) in the borough. Throughout the early decades of the 1900s, the area experienced a construction boom, with major towers such as [40 Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40_Wall_Street), the [American International Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_International_Building), [Woolworth Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolworth_Building), and [20 Exchange Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20_Exchange_Place) being erected. Many new water crossings into Lower Manhattan were built at this time, including the [Williamsburg Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg_Bridge) in 1903[^cite_note-31] and the [Manhattan Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhattan_Bridge) in 1909.[^cite_note-32] The [Holland Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland_Tunnel) to New Jersey opened in 1927,[^cite_note-33] while the [Brooklyn–Battery Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn%E2%80%93Battery_Tunnel) to [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn) opened in 1950 and was the last major fixed crossing to be built to Lower Manhattan.[^cite_note-34]

Despite these road connections opening, the economic center of New York City began to shift from Lower Manhattan to [Midtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan) with the opening of many commuter rail terminals at the turn of the 20th century. The original [Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(1910%E2%80%931963\)) opened in 1910,[^cite_note-35] the [Hudson and Manhattan Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_and_Manhattan_Railroad) (now PATH) extension to [33rd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33rd_Street_station_\(PATH\)) was completed in 1910,[^cite_note-36] and [Grand Central Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Terminal) opened in 1913.[^cite_note-37]

On March 25, 1911, the [Triangle Shirtwaist Factory fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triangle_Shirtwaist_Factory_fire) in [Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village) took the lives of 146 garment workers, which would eventually lead to great advancements in the city's fire department, building codes, and workplace regulations.

Throughout the first half of the 20th century, New York became a world center for industry, commerce, and [communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_New_York_City). [Interborough Rapid Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interborough_Rapid_Transit_Company), the first [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway) company, began operating in 1904. The area's demographics stabilized, labor unionization brought new protections and affluence to the working class, the city's government and infrastructure underwent a dramatic overhaul under [Fiorello La Guardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiorello_La_Guardia), and his controversial parks commissioner, [Robert Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Moses), ended the 'blight' of many tenement areas, by demolishing slums, factories, and working-class neighborhoods through public works such as the [High line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Line#History), the [West Side Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_Elevated_Highway) and [FDR Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDR_Drive), built housing projects, expanded new parks, rebuilt streets, and [zoning controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoning_in_the_United_States), especially in Lower Manhattan. The zoning changes were intended to displace the industrial workforce by removing zoning protection for industrial space and incentivizing upscale residential and clerical redevelopment. The port of New York, despite its physical suitability for berthing and its close proximity to Europe, began to deteriorate due to the city's unwillingness to invest or modernise the port and the deindustrialization zoning policy. However a large number of small scale, dynamic, and highly specialized industries persisted despite the city's efforts such as the garment industry which was closely tied to the fashion industry in Midtown, or the printing industry; linked with the publishing industry.

In the 1950s, a few new buildings were constructed in Lower Manhattan, including an 11-story building at 156 William Street in 1955.[^cite_note-nyt-12251960-38] A 27-story office building at 20 Broad Street, a 12-story building at 80 Pine Street, a 26-story building at 123 William Street, and a few others were built in 1957.[^cite_note-nyt-12251960-38] By the end of the decade, Lower Manhattan had become economically depressed, in comparison with [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan), which was booming with the continued [march uptown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_uptown). [David Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rockefeller) spearheaded widespread [urban renewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_renewal) efforts in Lower Manhattan, beginning with constructing [One Chase Manhattan Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Chase_Manhattan_Plaza), the new headquarters for his bank. He established the Downtown-Lower Manhattan Association (DLMA) which drew up plans for broader revitalization of Lower Manhattan, with the development of a proposed [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(1973%E2%80%932001\)) at the heart of these plans. The original DLMA plans called for the "world trade center" to be built along the [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_River), between Old Slip and [Fulton Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulton_Street_\(Manhattan\)). After negotiations with [New Jersey](21648.md) Governor [Richard J. Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_J._Hughes), the [Port Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey) decided to build the World Trade Center on a site along the [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River) and the [West Side Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_Highway), rather than the East River site.



View from an airplane in 1981 prior to the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) when the Lower Manhattan skyline was dominated by the [Twin Towers of the former World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(1973%E2%80%932001\))

When [building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_of_the_World_Trade_Center) the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(1973%E2%80%932001\)), 1.2 million cubic yards (917,000 m<sup>3</sup>) of material was excavated from the site.[^cite_note-gillespie-p71-39] Rather than dumping the spoil at sea or in landfills, the fill material was used to expand the Manhattan shoreline across West Street, creating [Battery Park City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_Park_City,_Manhattan).[^cite_note-iglauer-40] The result was a 700-foot (210-m) extension into the river, running six blocks or 1,484 feet (452 m), covering 92 acres (37 ha), providing a 1.2-mile (1.9 km) riverfront esplanade and over 30 acres (12 ha) of parks.[^cite_note-41]

Through much of its history, the area south of Chambers Street was mainly a commercial district, with a small population of residents—in 1960, it was home to about 4,000.[^cite_note-nyt-01311960-42] Construction of [Battery Park City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_Park_City), on landfill from construction of the World Trade Center, brought many new residents to the area. Gateway Plaza, the first Battery Park City development, was finished in 1983. The project's centerpiece, the [World Financial Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookfield_Place_\(New_York_City\)), consists of four luxury highrise towers. By the turn of the century, Battery Park City was mostly completed, with the exception of some ongoing construction on West Street. Around this time, Lower Manhattan reached its highest population of business tenants and full-time residents. These developments struggled to become fully occupied at desirable rents, with relatively high vacancy rates.[^cite_note-43]

In 1993, the Downtown Lower Manhattan Association contributed to a city plan calling for the revitalization of Lower Manhattan. The plan included recommended zoning changes, tax incentives to encourage new tenants, and the conversion of commercial buildings into apartments. It also called for the creation of a business improvement district, called The Alliance for Downtown New York, to help spur the area's renewal. Between 1995 and 2014, 15.8 million square feet of office space was converted to residential or hotel use. As a result, Lower Manhattan's residential population rose from 14,000 to 60,000.[^cite_note-44]

#### September 11 attacks

During the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md) in 2001, two of four hijacked planes were flown into the Twin Towers of the original [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(1973%E2%80%932001\)), and the towers collapsed. The [7 World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7_World_Trade_Center_\(1987%E2%80%932001\)) was not struck by a plane but uncontrolled fires that were caused by falling debris resulted in the building's collapse; a first in the history of steel framed skyscrapers.[^cite_note-46] The 3, 4, 5, and 6 World Trade Center buildings were damaged beyond repair or destroyed, and soon after demolished. The collapse of the Twin Towers also caused extensive damage to surrounding buildings and skyscrapers in Lower Manhattan. A total of 2,753 people, including those on the planes, were killed in New York.[^cite_note-47] About 400,000 people, including rescue workers and residents of the area were exposed to toxic dust and debris; many developed [serious respiratory illnesses, cancers, and other harms](7770119.md) arising from the attack, and 3,496 died.[^cite_note-48]

#### Post-9/11 rebuilding

Following September 11, Lower Manhattan lost much of its economy and office space but has since rebounded significantly. Private sector employment reached 233,000 at the end of 2016, the highest levels since the end of 2001. This was largely due to growth and diversification in the local workforce with gains in employment sectors like Technology, Advertising, Media and Information, as well as Hotel, Restaurants, Retailing, and Health care.[^cite_note-49] As of 2016, Lower Manhattan's business district is home to approximately 700 retail stores and 500 bars and restaurants.[^cite_note-50]

The [Lower Manhattan Development Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Manhattan_Development_Corporation) has consummated plans to rebuild downtown Manhattan by adding new streets, buildings, and office space. The [National September 11 Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_September_11_Memorial_&_Museum) at the site was opened to the public on September 11, 2011, while the National September 11 Museum was officially inaugurated by President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) on May 15, 2014.[^cite_note-51] As of the time of its opening in November 2014, the new [One World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_World_Trade_Center), formerly known as the *Freedom Tower*, is the tallest skyscraper in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md)[^cite_note-52] and the [seventh-tallest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_the_world#Tallest_buildings_in_the_world), at 1,776 feet (541 m);[^cite_note-53] while other skyscrapers are under construction at the site.

The [Occupy Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupy_Wall_Street) protests in [Zuccotti Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuccotti_Park), formerly known as Liberty Plaza Park, began in the [Financial District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_District_\(Manhattan\)) on September 17, 2011, receiving global attention and spawning the [Occupy movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupy_movement) against [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inequality) and [economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality) worldwide.[^cite_note-54]

On October 29 and 30, 2012, [Hurricane Sandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Sandy) ravaged portions of Lower Manhattan with record-high [storm surge](449744.md) from New York Harbor, severe flooding, and high winds, causing [power outages](296636.md) for hundreds of thousands of Manhattanites and leading to gasoline shortages and disruption of [mass transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_transit) systems. The storm and [its effects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_Hurricane_Sandy_in_New_York) have prompted the discussion of constructing [seawalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawall) and other [coastal barriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_management) around the shorelines of Manhattan and the New York City metropolitan region to minimize the risk of destructive consequences from another such event in the future.[^cite_note-55]

Lower Manhattan has been experiencing a [baby boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_boom), well above the overall birth rate in Manhattan, with the area south of [Canal Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal_Street_\(Manhattan\)) witnessing 1,086 births in 2010, 12% greater than 2009 and over twice the number born in 2001.[^cite_note-56] The Financial District alone saw its population grow to approximately 43,000 as of 2014, nearly double the 23,000 recorded at the 2000 Census.[^cite_note-57]

There are currently 61,000 residents in the [Financial District of Lower Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_District,_Manhattan) south of [Chambers Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambers_Street_\(Manhattan\))[^cite_note-58] and more than 62 percent of the population is between 18 and 44. Lower Manhattan is home to more young professionals than [Greenpoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenpoint,_Brooklyn), the [East Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Village,_Manhattan), and [Downtown Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Brooklyn) and on par with [Downtown Jersey City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Jersey_City) and [Williamsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsburg,_Brooklyn).[^cite_note-59]

In June 2015, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* wrote that Lower Manhattan's dining scene was experiencing a renaissance.[^cite_note-60] There are over 400 casual dining and more than 100 full-service dining restaurants in the area.[^cite_note-61] *[The Village Voice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Village_Voice)*, based at 80 [Maiden Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiden_Lane_\(Manhattan\)) in the Financial District and historically the largest [alternative newspaper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_newspaper) in the United States, announced in 2017 that it would cease publication of its print edition and convert to a fully [digital venture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_media).[^cite_note-VillageVoiceDigital-62]

Since 2010, a Lower Manhattan community known as [Little Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Australia) has emerged and is growing in the [Nolita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nolita,_Manhattan) neighborhood.[^cite_note-LittleAustraliaNYC-63]

### Historical sites

Before the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), the Twin Towers were iconic of Lower Manhattan's global significance as a [financial center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_center). The new office towers built since the attack (including [One World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_World_Trade_Center)) have transformed the skyline of Lower Manhattan. The [9/11 Memorial & Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_September_11_Memorial_&_Museum) at the former [World Trade Center site](67173.md) has become a popular draw for visitors. New York City has been described as the [gay capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_culture_in_New_York_City) of the world, and the epicenter of [LGBT culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_culture) and its catalyst as a continuing cultural force in modern society has been the [Stonewall Inn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonewall_Inn) in [Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village).[^cite_note-NYCGayCapitalOfTheWorld1-64] Similarly, [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Manhattan), which was spawned just east of the original [Five Points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Points,_Manhattan) neighborhood of Lower Manhattan, was born in the 1850s and continues to be the epicenter of culture for the [Chinese diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese).

Lower Manhattan contains many more historical buildings and sites, including [Castle Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Clinton), [Bowling Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling_Green_\(New_York_City\)), the old [United States Customs House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Hamilton_U.S._Custom_House) (now the [National Museum of the American Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_the_American_Indian)), [Federal Hall National Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Hall_National_Memorial) commemorating the site where [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) was [inaugurated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_presidential_inaugurations) as the first U.S. President, [Fraunces Tavern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraunces_Tavern), [New York City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Hall), the [Museum of American Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_American_Finance), the [New York Stock Exchange Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Stock_Exchange_Building), [South Street Seaport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Street_Seaport), the [Brooklyn Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Bridge), [South Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Ferry,_Manhattan) (the embarkation point for the [Staten Island Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staten_Island_Ferry)), and [Trinity Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Church_\(Manhattan\)). Lower Manhattan is home to some of New York City's most spectacular skyscrapers, including the [Woolworth Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolworth_Building), [40 Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/40_Wall_Street) (also known as the Trump Building), [26 Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26_Wall_Street) (also known as the [Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil) Building), and [70 Pine Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/70_Pine_Street) (also known as the [American International Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_International_Group)).

In 1966, the commercial district of [Radio Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Row) on [Cortlandt Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortlandt_Street_\(Manhattan\)) was demolished to make way for construction of the former World Trade Center.

## Culture



[Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Manhattan) is home to the highest concentration of [Overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese) in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md)

Further information: [Chinese people in Lower Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people_in_Lower_Manhattan), [LGBTQ culture of Lower Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_culture_of_Lower_Manhattan), [Transgender culture of Lower Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_culture_of_Lower_Manhattan), and [Greenwich Village Halloween Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village_Halloween_Parade)



The annual [Village Halloween Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Halloween_Parade) in [Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village) is the world's largest Halloween parade, with millions of spectators annually.

[^cite_note-NewYorkCityQueerHalloweenParade-65]

Since the early-20th century, Lower Manhattan has been an important center for the arts and leisure activities. [Greenwich Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwich_Village) was a locus of [bohemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemianism) culture from the first decade of the century through the 1980s. Several of the city's leading jazz clubs are still located in Greenwich Village, which was also one of the primary bases of the [American folk music revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_folk_music_revival) of the 1960s. Many art galleries were located in SoHo between the 1970s and early 1990s; today, the downtown Manhattan gallery scene is centered in Chelsea. From the 1960s onward, Lower Manhattan has been home to many alternative theater companies, constituting the heart of the [Off-Off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-Off-Broadway) community.

[Punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock) and its musical derivatives emerged in the mid-1970s largely at two Lower Manhattan venues, [CBGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBGB) on [Bowery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowery) in the western edge of the East Village, and [Max's Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max's_Kansas_City) on [Park Avenue South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_Avenue_\(Manhattan\)). At the same time, the area's surfeit of appropriated industrial lofts, played an integral role in the development and sustenance of the minimalist composition, [free jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_jazz), [disco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disco), and [electronic dance music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_dance_music) subcultures. The area's many nightclubs and bars, though mostly shorn of the freewheeling iconoclasm, pioneering spirit, and [do-it-yourself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do-it-yourself) mentality that characterized the pre-gentrification era, still draw patrons from throughout the city and the surrounding region.

### Denotation of *Downtown*

See also: [Downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown)



[Union Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Square,_Manhattan) and its surrounding neighborhood, located between [14th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Street_\(Manhattan\)) and [17th Streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_Street_\(Manhattan\)), may be considered a part of either Lower or [Midtown Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan).

*Downtown* in the context of Manhattan, and of New York City generally, has different meanings to different people, especially depending on where in the city they reside. Residents of the island or of [The Bronx](3338.md) generally speak of *going* "downtown" to refer to any southbound excursion to any Manhattan destination.[^cite_note-NYCV-66] A declaration that one is going to *be* "downtown" may indicate a plan to be anywhere south of [14th Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Street_\(Manhattan\))—the definition of downtown according to the city's official tourism marketing organization[^cite_note-NYCV-66]—or even [23rd Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Street_\(Manhattan\)).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] The full phrase *Downtown Manhattan* may also refer more specifically to the area of Manhattan south of Canal Street.[^cite_note-nyt-01311960-42] Within business-related contexts, many people use the term *Downtown Manhattan* to refer only to the Financial District and the corporate offices in the immediate vicinity. For instance, the [Business Improvement District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Improvement_District) managed by the Alliance for Downtown New York defines Downtown as south of Murray Street (essentially South of [New York City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Hall)), which includes the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)) area and the [Financial District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_District,_Manhattan). The phrase *Lower Manhattan* may apply to any of these definitions: the broader ones often if the speaker is discussing the area in relation to the rest of the city; more restrictive ones, again, if the focus is on business matters or on the colonial and early post-colonial history of the island.

As reflected in popular culture, "Downtown" in Manhattan has historically represented a place where one could "forget all your troubles, forget all your cares, and go Downtown," as the lyrics of [Petula Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petula_Clark)'s 1964 hit "[Downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_\(Petula_Clark_song\))" celebrate (although [Tony Hatch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Hatch), the songwriter of the track, later clarified that he naively believed [Times Square](31389.md) to be "downtown", and was the actual inspiration for the hit single). The protagonist of [Billy Joel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Joel)'s 1983 hit "[Uptown Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uptown_Girl)" contrasts himself (a "downtown man") with the purportedly staid uptown world.[^cite_note-69] Likewise, the chorus of [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young)'s 1995 single "Downtown" urges "Let's have a party, downtown all right."

The headquarters of the [Port Authority of New York and New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_of_New_York_and_New_Jersey) is located in [4 World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4_World_Trade_Center) of the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)) complex.[^cite_note-97]

The city hall and related government infrastructure of the City of New York are located in Lower Manhattan, next to City Hall Park. The [Jacob K. Javits Federal Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_K._Javits_Federal_Building) is located in [Civic Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Center,_Manhattan). It includes the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) New York [field office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FBI_field_office).[^cite_note-98]

Many [New York City Subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_Subway) routes converge downtown. The largest hub, [Fulton Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulton_Center), was completed in 2014 after a \$1.4 billion reconstruction project necessitated by the September 11, 2001, attacks, and involves six separate stations. This transit hub was expected to serve 300,000 daily riders as of late 2014.[^cite_note-99] The [World Trade Center Transportation Hub and PATH station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(PATH_station\)) opened in 2016.[^cite_note-100] Ferry services are also concentrated downtown, including the [Staten Island Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staten_Island_Ferry) at the [Whitehall Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staten_Island_Ferry_Whitehall_Terminal), [NYC Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NYC_Ferry) at [Pier 11/Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pier_11/Wall_Street) (and [Battery Park City Ferry Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_Park_City_Ferry_Terminal) starting in 2020), and service to [Governors Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governors_Island) at the [Battery Maritime Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_Maritime_Building).

### Vehicles

Lower Manhattan is accessible by vehicle through several major thoroughfares. From [New Jersey](21648.md), Lower Manhattan is accessible through the [Holland Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holland_Tunnel) from [Interstate 78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_78_in_New_Jersey). From [Queens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens), [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island), and points east, it is accessible through the [Queens–Midtown Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queens%E2%80%93Midtown_Tunnel) from the [Long Island Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_495_\(New_York\)). From [Midtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midtown_Manhattan) and [Upper Manhattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Manhattan), it is accessible from the [West Side Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_Highway).
