**Financial risk management** is the practice of protecting [economic value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_\(economics\)) in a [firm](39206.md) by managing exposure to [financial risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_risk) - principally [credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_risk) and [market risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_risk), with more specific variants as listed aside - as well as some aspects of [operational risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_risk). As for [risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_management) more generally, financial risk management requires identifying the sources of risk, measuring these, and crafting plans to [mitigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitigation) them.[^cite_note-Christoffersen2011-1][^cite_note-2] See [Finance § Risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance#Risk_management) for an overview.

Financial risk management as a "science" can be said to have been born[^cite_note-\:0-3] with [modern portfolio theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_portfolio_theory), particularly as initiated by Professor [Harry Markowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Markowitz) in 1952 with his article, "Portfolio Selection";[^cite_note-4] see [Mathematical finance § Risk and portfolio management: the P world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_finance#Risk_and_portfolio_management:_the_P_world).

The discipline can be qualitative and quantitative; as a specialization of [risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk) management, however, financial risk management focuses more on when and how to [hedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_\(finance\)),[^cite_note-Welch-5] often using financial instruments to manage costly exposures to risk.[^cite_note-Malz2011-6]

## Economic perspective

[Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_economics) [finance theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_economics) prescribes that (1) a firm should take on a project only if it increases [shareholder](146081.md) value.[^cite_note-14] Further, the theory suggests that (2) [firm managers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management) cannot create value for shareholders or [investors](63121.md) by taking on projects that shareholders could do for themselves at the same cost; see [Theory of the firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_the_firm) and [Fisher separation theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_separation_theorem).

Given these, there is therefore a fundamental debate relating to "Risk Management" and [shareholder value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shareholder_value).[^cite_note-Welch-5][^cite_note-Lewellen-15][^cite_note-16] The discussion essentially weighs the value of risk management in a market versus the cost of [bankruptcy](4695.md) in that market: per the [Modigliani and Miller framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modigliani%E2%80%93Miller_theorem), hedging is irrelevant since diversified shareholders are assumed to not care about firm-specific risks, whereas, on the other hand hedging is seen to create value in that it reduces the [probability of financial distress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_of_default).&#x20;

When applied to financial risk management, this implies that firm managers should not hedge risks that investors can hedge for themselves at the same cost.[^cite_note-Welch-5] This notion is captured in the so-called "hedging irrelevance proposition":[^cite_note-CHANDRASEKHAR-17] "In a [perfect market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_market), the firm cannot create value by hedging a risk when the price of bearing that [risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk) within the firm is the same as the [price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price) of bearing it outside of the firm."

In practice, however, financial markets are not likely to be perfect markets.[^cite_note-Hampton1982-18][^cite_note-Hoque2005-19][^cite_note-Butler2012-20][^cite_note-Franzen2012-21] This suggests that firm managers likely have many opportunities to create value for shareholders using financial risk management, wherein they are able to determine which risks are cheaper for the firm to manage than for shareholders. Here, [market risks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_risk) that result in unique risks for the firm are commonly the best candidates for financial risk management.[^cite_note-\:1-22]

## Application

As outlined, businesses are exposed, in the main, to market, credit and operational risk. A broad distinction[^cite_note-PRMIA-13] exists though, between [financial institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_institution) and non-financial firms - and correspondingly, the application of risk management will differ. Respectively:[^cite_note-PRMIA-13] For Banks and Fund Managers, "credit and market risks are taken intentionally with the objective of earning returns, while operational risks are a [byproduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byproduct) to be controlled". For non-financial firms, the priorities are reversed, as "the focus is on the risks associated with the business" - ie the production and marketing of the services and products [in which expertise is held](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competitive_advantage) - and their impact on revenue, costs and cash flow, "while market and credit risks are usually of secondary importance as they are a byproduct of the main business agenda". (See related discussion re [valuing financial services firms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_\(finance\)#Valuing_financial_services_firms) as compared to other firms.) In all cases, as above, risk capital is the last "[line of defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_lines_of_defence)".

### Banking

Further information: [Bank § Capital and risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank#Capital_and_risk), [Shadow banking system § Risks or vulnerability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow_banking_system#Risks_or_vulnerability), and [Banking regulation and supervision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_regulation_and_supervision)

[Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banks) and other [wholesale institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wholesale_banking) face various [financial risks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_risk) in conducting their business, and how well these risks are managed and understood is a key driver[^cite_note-OCC-23] behind profitability, as well as of the [quantum of capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_requirement) they are required to hold.[^cite_note-Zaher-24] Financial risk management in banking has thus grown markedly in importance since the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis).[^cite_note-CRO_article-25] (This has given rise[^cite_note-CRO_article-25] to various [dedicated degrees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Master_of_Quantitative_Finance) and several [professional certifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_certification_in_financial_services#Risk_management_and_quantitative_finance).)

The broad distinction between [Investment Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_bank), on the one hand, and [Commercial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_bank) and [Retail Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retail_bank) on the other, carries through to the management of risk at these institutions. Investment Banks profit from trading - [proprietary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_trading) and [flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flow_trading) - and earn fees from [structuring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structured_product) and [deal making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_banking#Front_office); the latter [includes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_banking#Corporate_finance) listing securities so as to raise funding in the [capital markets](41914.md) (and [supporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bookmaking) these thereafter), as well as directly providing debt-funding for [large corporate "projects"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_finance#Investment_banking). The major focus for risk managers here is therefore on [market-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_risk) and ([corporate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_bond#Default_risk)) [credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_risk). Commercial and Retail Banks, as [deposit taking institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_institution), profit from the spread between [deposit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deposit_\(finance\)) and loan rates. The focus of risk management is then on [loan defaults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_default) from individuals or businesses ([SMEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_and_medium_enterprises)), and on having enough [liquid assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_assets) to meet [withdrawal demands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deposit_risk); market risk concerns, mainly, the impact of interest rate changes on [net interest margins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_interest_margin). All banks will focus also on operational risk, impacting here (at least) through [regulatory capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_capital); (large) banks are also exposed to [Macroeconomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomics) [systematic risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Systematic_risk) - risks related to the aggregate economy the bank is operating in[^cite_note-26] (see [Too big to fail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Too_big_to_fail)).

Central to both commercial and investment banking is the function of [maturity transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maturity_transformation), [^cite_note-27] where institutions fund long-term assets using short-term liabilities. Commercial banks typify this by issuing [demand deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demand_deposit) (which can be withdrawn at any time) to fund long-dated assets like [mortgages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortgage), while investment banks often finance longer-term [ trading inventory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proprietary_trading) or [structured products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structured_product) through short-term [repurchase agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repurchase_agreement) (repos). While this "borrowing short and lending long" strategy is profitable — normally capturing the spread between [lower short-term rates and higher long-term rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yield_curve#Construction_of_the_full_yield_curve_from_market_data) — it creates an inherent mismatch on the balance sheet. This structural mismatch generates the primary risks that banks must manage - outlined in the preceding paragraph - but here especially: liquidity risk (the inability to meet short-term obligations without selling assets) and [interest rate risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate_risk) (changes in the [yield curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yield_curve) affecting asset and liability values [differently](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duration_gap)), making [Asset and liability management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_and_liability_management) (ALM) a critical discipline.

The [Basel Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_Accords) mandate the predominant risk management framework. Under ["Pillar I"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_II#The_first_pillar:_Minimum_capital_requirements) regulators define the minimum [regulatory capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_capital) requirements for quantifiable risks — principally [credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_risk), [market risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_risk), and [operational risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_risk) as outlined — using either standardised or approved internal‑model approaches. Under ["Pillar II"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_II#The_second_pillar:_Supervisory_review_process), banks must conduct an internal capital adequacy assessment ([ICAAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_II#ICAAP)) to capture all material risks, holding sufficient "[economic capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_capital)" for those.[^cite_note-Pillar_2-28] Some jurisdictions (or banks) complement these with additional buffers, stress testing, and [supervisory review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_regulation_and_supervision).

For [investment banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_banks) - as outlined - the major focus is on credit and market risk. Credit risk is inherent in the business of banking, but additionally, these institutions are exposed to [counterparty credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterparty_credit_risk). Both are [to some extent offset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_risk#Mitigation) by [margining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margin_\(finance\)) and [collateral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collateral_\(finance\)); and the management is of the [net-position](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_\(economics\)).

Risk management here[^cite_note-Haugh-29][^cite_note-DeMeo-30][^cite_note-Van_Deventer_Imai-7][^cite_note-Drumond-8] is, as discussed, simultaneously concerned with (i) managing, and as necessary [hedging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_\(finance\)), the various positions held by the institution - both [trading positions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_book) and [long term exposures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_book); and (ii) calculating and monitoring the resultant [economic capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_capital), as well as the [regulatory capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_capital) under [Basel III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_III) — which, importantly, covers also [leverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage_\(finance\)) and [liquidity](56121.md) — with regulatory capital as a floor.

Correspondingly, and broadly, the analytics[^cite_note-DeMeo-30][^cite_note-Haugh-29] are based as follows: For (i) on [the "Greeks"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_\(finance\)), the sensitivity of the price of a derivative to a change in its underlying factors; as well as on [the various other measures of sensitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_\(finance\)#Related_measures), such as [DV01](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DV01) for the sensitivity of a [bond](60737.md) or [swap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swap_\(finance\)) to interest rates, and [CS01](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CS01) or [JTD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_default_swap#Risk) for exposure to [credit spread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_spread_\(bond\)). For (ii) on [value at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_at_risk), or "VaR", an estimate of how much the investment or area in question might lose as market and credit conditions deteriorate, with a given probability over a set time period, and with the bank then holding ["economic"-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_capital) or "[risk capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_capital)" correspondingly; [common parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_at_risk#Details) are 99% and 95% worst-case losses - i.e. 1% and 5% - and one day and two week ([10 day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_count_convention)) horizons.[^cite_note-Pearson-31] These calculations are mathematically sophisticated, and within [the domain of quantitative finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analysis_\(finance\)#Risk_management).

The regulatory capital quantum is calculated via specified formulae: risk weighting the exposures per highly standardized asset-categorizations, applying the aside frameworks, and the resultant capital — at least 12.9%[^cite_note-32] of these [Risk-weighted assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk-weighted_asset) (RWA) — must then be held [in specific "tiers"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_requirement#Regulatory_capital) and is measured correspondingly via the [various capital ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_requirement#Common_capital_ratios). In certain cases, banks are allowed to use their own estimated risk parameters here; these ["internal ratings-based models"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_ratings-based_approach_\(credit_risk\)) typically result in less required capital, but at the same time [are subject to](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_III:_Finalising_post-crisis_reforms#Requirements) strict minimum conditions and disclosure requirements. As mentioned, additional to the capital covering RWA, the aggregate [balance sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_sheet) will require capital for [leverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage_\(finance\)) and [liquidity](56121.md); this is monitored via[^cite_note-LR_etc-33] the [LR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage_ratio), [LCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_Coverage_Ratio), and [NSFR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_stable_funding_ratio) ratios.

The [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) exposed holes in the mechanisms used for hedging (see [Fundamental Review of the Trading Book § Background](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_Review_of_the_Trading_Book#Background), [Tail risk § Role of the 2007–2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tail_risk#Role_of_the_2007%E2%80%932008_financial_crisis), [Value at risk § Criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_at_risk#Criticism), and [Basel III § Criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_III#Criticism)). As such, the methodologies employed [have had to evolve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_economics#Departures_from_normality), both from a modelling point of view, and in parallel, from a regulatory point of view.

Regarding the modelling, changes corresponding to the above are: (i) For the daily [direct analysis of the positions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PnL_Explained#Sensitivities_method) at the [desk level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_desk), as a standard, measurement of the Greeks [now inheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_of_options#Post_crisis) the [volatility surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatility_surface) — through [local-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_volatility) or [stochastic volatility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_volatility) models — while re interest rates, discounting [and analytics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_income_analysis) are under a "[multi-curve framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-curve_framework)".[^cite_note-CQF-34] Derivative pricing [now embeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVA#Context) considerations re [counterparty risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterparty_risk) and [funding risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margin_\(finance\)#Types_of_margin_requirements), amongst others,[^cite_note-iacpm-35] through the [CVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_valuation_adjustment) and [XVA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVA) "valuation adjustments"; these [also carry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVA#Accounting_impact) regulatory capital. (ii) For Value at Risk, the traditional [parametric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_at_risk#Computation_methods) and ["Historical"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_simulation_\(finance\)) approaches, are now supplemented[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-DeMeo-30] with the more sophisticated [Conditional value at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_value_at_risk) / [expected shortfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expected_shortfall), [Tail value at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tail_value_at_risk), and [Extreme value theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_value_theory)[^cite_note-McNeil-37][^cite_note-Embrechts-38]. For the underlying mathematics, these may utilize [mixture models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixture_model#A_financial_model), [PCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_component_analysis#Quantitative_finance), [volatility clustering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatility_clustering), [copulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copula_\(probability_theory\)#Quantitative_finance), and other techniques.[^cite_note-39] Extensions to VaR include [Margin-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margin_at_risk), [Liquidity-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_at_risk), [Earnings-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earnings_at_risk) and [Cash flow at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_flow_at_risk), as well as [Liquidity-adjusted VaR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_risk#Liquidity-adjusted_value_at_risk). For both (i) and (ii), [model risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_risk) is addressed[^cite_note-40] through regular [validation of the models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analysis_\(finance\)#Model_validation) used by the bank's various divisions; for VaR models, [backtesting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backtesting#Financial_analysis) is especially employed.

Regulatory changes, are also twofold. The first change, entails an [increased emphasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_III#Summary_of_originally-proposed_changes_\(2010\)_in_Basel_Committee_language)[^cite_note-Investopdia_bank_stress_test-41] on [bank stress tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_test_\(financial\)#Bank_stress_test).[^cite_note-bcbs155-42] These tests, essentially [a simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_simulation) of the [balance sheet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balance_sheet) for a [given scenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scenario_planning#Finance), are typically linked to the macroeconomics, and provide an indicator of how sensitive the bank is to changes in economic conditions, whether it is [sufficiently capitalized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_Capital_Analysis_and_Review), and of its ability to respond to market events. The second set of changes, sometimes called "[Basel IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_IV)", entails the modification of several regulatory capital standards ([CRR III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_Requirements_Regulation_2013) is the EU implementation). In particular [FRTB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRTB) addresses market risk, and [SA-CCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SA-CCR) addresses counterparty risk; other modifications [are also being phased in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_III:_Finalising_post-crisis_reforms#Requirements).

To [operationalize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_manager) the above, [Investment banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_bank), particularly, employ dedicated ["Risk Groups"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_bank#Risk_management), i.e. [Middle Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_office) teams monitoring the firm's risk-exposure to, and the profitability and structure of, its various [business units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_unit), [products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank#Products), [asset classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_class), desks, and / [or geographies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_risk#Geopolitical_risk).[^cite_note-iacpm_2-43] By increasing order of aggregation and time-horizon:

1. Financial institutions will set

   [^cite_note-MAR12-44]

   [^cite_note-Haugh-29]

   [^cite_note-45]

   &#x20;[limit values for each of the Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black%E2%80%93Scholes_model#The_Options_Greeks), or other sensitivities, that their [traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trader_\(finance\)) must not exceed, and traders [will then hedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_\(finance\)#Risks), offset, or reduce periodically if not daily; see the techniques [listed below](#Investment_management). These limits are set given a range

   [^cite_note-46]

   &#x20;of plausible changes in prices and rates, coupled with the board-specified [risk appetite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_appetite)

   [^cite_note-47]

   &#x20;re overnight-losses.

   [^cite_note-48]

2. Desks, or areas, will similarly be [limited as to their VaR quantum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_at_risk#VaR_risk_management) (total or incremental, and under various calculation regimes), corresponding to their allocated

   [^cite_note-Guo-49]

   &#x20;economic capital; a loss which exceeds the VaR threshold is termed a "VaR breach".

3. RWA - the key Pillar I result - is correspondingly monitored from desk level

   [^cite_note-MAR12-44]

   &#x20;and upward. Market-risk RWA will be monitored more frequently, with credit-risk RWA less so. While VaR is a risk-taking constraint (front office) RWA is a capital supply constraint (management + regulators); it does provide the denominator for the below ratios and feeds into other planning.

4. Each area's (or desk's) [concentration risk will be checked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_risk#Monitoring_and_management)

   [^cite_note-50]

   [^cite_note-iacpm_2-43]

   [^cite_note-51]

   &#x20;against thresholds set for various types of risk, and / or re a single counterparty, [sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_sector) or geography.

5. [Leverage will be monitored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage_\(finance\)#Risk), at very least [re regulatory requirements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage_\(finance\)#Banking) via LR, the [Leverage Ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage_ratio), as leveraged positions [could lose large amounts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_\(finance\)#Leverage) for a relatively small move in the price of the underlying.

6. Relatedly,

   [^cite_note-LR_etc-33]

   &#x20;[liquidity risk is monitored](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_risk#Management): LCR, the [Liquidity Coverage Ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_Coverage_Ratio), measures the ability of the bank to survive a short-term stress, covering its total net cash outflows over the next 30 days with "[high quality liquid assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_quality_liquid_assets)"; NSFR, the [Net Stable Funding Ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_stable_funding_ratio), assesses its ability to finance assets and commitments within a year (addressing also, [maturity transformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maturity_transformation) risk). Any ["gaps"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duration_gap), also, [must be managed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duration_gap#Management).

   [^cite_note-IRRBB-52]

7. [Systemically Important Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_systemically_important_banks) hold additional capital such that their [total loss absorbency capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_Loss_Absorbency_Capacity), TLAC, is sufficient

   [^cite_note-53]

   &#x20;given both RWA and leverage. (See also "MREL"

   [^cite_note-54]

   &#x20;for EU institutions.)

Periodically,[^cite_note-55] these all are estimated under a given stress scenario — [regulatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bank_stress_tests) and,[^cite_note-56] often, [internal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scenario_planning#Finance) — designed to assess whether the bank is "sufficiently capitalized to absorb losses during stressful conditions while meeting obligations to creditors and counterparties and continuing to be able to lend to households and businesses". [^cite_note-57] Risk capital,[^cite_note-OCC-23] [together with these limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_appetite#Levels) if indicated,[^cite_note-OCC-23][^cite_note-RAF-58] is correspondingly revisited (or optimized[^cite_note-59]). The approaches taken center either on a hypothetical or [historical scenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_stock_market_crashes_and_bear_markets),[^cite_note-Investopdia_bank_stress_test-41][^cite_note-DeMeo-30] and may apply increasingly sophisticated mathematics[^cite_note-IMF_Stress_Testing-60][^cite_note-DeMeo-30] to the analysis. More generally, these tests [provide estimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_at_risk#VaR_risk_management) for scenarios beyond the VaR thresholds, thus “preparing for anything that might happen, rather than worrying about precise likelihoods".[^cite_note-Aldous-61] A reverse stress test, in fact, starts from the point at which "the institution can be considered as failing or likely to fail... and then explores scenarios and circumstances that might cause this to occur".[^cite_note-62]

[Economic Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Capital) (EC) goes further, reflecting the *total* [risk capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_capital) that the bank requires to cover "all" its risks as a [going concern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Going_concern) assessed on a realistic basis, including survival in a worst-case scenario.[^cite_note-ecb_icaap-63] It is calibrated to a specific confidence level (e.g., 99.9%) over a one-year horizon, a further distinction re regulatory capital. The modelling - at least once annually - must be such that[^cite_note-ecb_icaap-63] any material risks are adequately and conservatively quantified: banks typically deploy detailed (long run) simulations and coupled stress testing, aggregating credit, market, operational, and business risk, while accounting for diversification. The [balance-sheet composition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_requirement#Regulatory_capital) is naturally revisited as part of the assessment. Although essentially an internal measure, EC will be (re)viewed by the Regulator under Pillar II and, as above, is governed by [ICAAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel_II#ICAAP), the framework for the bank's "internal capital adequacy assessment process".[^cite_note-Pillar_2-28]

A key practice,[^cite_note-Crouhy-64] incorporating and assimilating much of the above, is to assess the [Risk-adjusted return on capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk-adjusted_return_on_capital), RAROC, of each area (or product). Here,[^cite_note-Skoglund-65] ["economic profit"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_value_added) is divided by allocated-capital; and this result is then compared[^cite_note-Skoglund-65][^cite_note-Zaher-24] to the target-return for the area — usually, at least the [equity holders' expected returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_of_equity) on the bank stock[^cite_note-Skoglund-65] — and identified under-performance can then be addressed. (See similar [below](#Corporate_finance) re. DuPont analysis.) The numerator, risk-adjusted return, is realized trading-return less a [term and risk appropriate funding cost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funds_transfer_pricing#Banking_and_finance) as charged [by Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_management#Banks) to the business-unit under the bank's [funds transfer pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funds_transfer_pricing) (FTP) framework;[^cite_note-66] [direct costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_costs) are (sometimes) also subtracted.[^cite_note-Crouhy-64] The denominator is the area's allocated capital, as above, increasing as a function of position risk;[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-Crouhy-64] several allocation techniques exist.[^cite_note-Guo-49] RAROC is calculated both *[ex post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_post)* as discussed, used for performance evaluation (and related [bonus calculations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance-related_pay#Financial_sector)), and *[ex ante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_ante)* - i.e. [expected return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expected_return) less [expected loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expected_loss) - to decide whether a particular business unit should be expanded or contracted.[^cite_note-69]

Other teams, overlapping the above Groups, are then also involved in risk management. [Corporate Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_management#Banks) is responsible for monitoring overall funding and capital structure; it shares responsibility for monitoring liquidity risk, and for maintaining the FTP framework. Middle Office [maintains the following functions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_analyst#Middle_office) also: [Product Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_control) is primarily responsible for ensuring traders [mark their books to fair value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark-to-market_accounting) — a key protection against [rogue traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_trader) — and for ["explaining" the daily P\&L](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P\&L_Explained); with the ["unexplained" component](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PnL_explained#PnL_unexplained), of particular interest to risk managers. Credit Risk [monitors the bank's debt-clients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_analyst#Credit_analysts) on an ongoing basis, re both exposure [and performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt_service_coverage_ratio); while (large) exposures are initially approved by an "[investment committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_committee)". In the [Front Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_office) — since counterparty and funding-risks span assets, products, and desks — specialized [XVA-desks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_valuation_adjustment#Function_of_the_CVA_desk) are tasked with centrally [monitoring and managing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVA#Accounting_impact) overall CVA and XVA exposure and capital, typically with oversight from the appropriate Group.[^cite_note-iacpm-35] "Stress Testing" is similarly centralized.[^cite_note-bcbs155-42]

Performing the above tasks — while simultaneously ensuring that computations are consistent[^cite_note-70] [over the various](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OLAP_cube#Operations) areas, products, teams, [and measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coherent_risk_measure) — requires that banks maintain a significant investment[^cite_note-71] in [sophisticated infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_banking#Technology), [finance / risk software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Financial_software_companies), and [dedicated staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analysis_\(finance\)#Quantitative_developer). Risk software often deployed is from [FIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_\(company\)), [Kamakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamakura_Corporation), [Murex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murex_\(financial_software\)), [Numerix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerix) (FINCAD) and [Refinitiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refinitiv). Large institutions may prefer systems developed entirely ["in house"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In-house_software) - notably[^cite_note-72] [Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs) ([SecDB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SecDB)), [JP Morgan](231001.md) (Athena), [Jane Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Street_Capital) ([Core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Street_Capital#Technology)), [Barclays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barclays) (BARX), [BofA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BofA_Securities) (Quartz), [ Citadel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citadel_LLC) (Apollo), [Morgan Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Stanley) (SecMaster) - while, more commonly, the [pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_\(finance\)#Valuation) [library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_\(computing\)) will be [developed internally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analysis_\(finance\)#Library_quantitative_analysis), especially as this allows for currency re new products or market features.

#### Commercial and retail banking



&#x20;[Risk taxonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxonomy) for retail and commercial banks

Further information: [Financial institution § Regulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_institution#Regulation), and [Volcker Rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcker_Rule)

See also: [Bank failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_failure)

[Commercial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_bank) and [retail banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retail_bank)[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-FC-75][^cite_note-HKIB-76] are, by nature, more conservative than Investment banks, earning steady income from lending and deposits; their focus is more on the "[banking book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_book)" than the "[trading book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_book)". The biggest concern here - as mentioned - is the credit risk due to [loan defaults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loan_default) from individuals or businesses. Liquidity risk - in this context [deposit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deposit_risk), i.e. not having enough liquid assets to meet [withdrawal demands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deposit_risk) - is also a major focus; while interest rate risk concerns the impact of interest rate changes on [net interest margins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_interest_margin) (the spread between deposit and loan rates).

For these banks, regulatory oversight is often tighter due to their direct impact on the financial system. Thus they are also [highly regulated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_regulation_and_supervision) under Basel III and [national banking laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Banking_legislation), and will also be subject to regular stress testing [by central banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank#Banking_supervision_and_other_activities); and all regulations above then apply (with local exceptions; e.g. an LCR "threshold" in the US[^cite_note-77]). Additional to these, however, they must maintain high capital and liquidity ratios to [protect depositors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deposit_protection); see [CAMELS rating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAMELS_rating_system).

Given their business model and risk appetite,[^cite_note-FC-75] as outlined, various differences result vs risk management at investment banks.

- Banks here maintain specific (and often additional) [capital buffers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_adequacy_ratio) to cover potential loan losses; reflected also in the fact that retail and commercial loans usually attract [higher RWA results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standardized_approach_\(credit_risk\)#The_summary_of_risk_weights_in_standardized_approach)

  [^cite_note-78]

  &#x20;than for assets typical in investment banking. See, e.g., the [ALLL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allowance_for_Loan_and_Lease_Losses) and [NPL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-performing_loan) ratios.

- At the same time, credit exposure for these banks is to significantly more clients than at investment banks. For retail banks, "[consumer credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_credit_risk)" is often diversified across a vast number of borrowers, and these employ statistical models for (ongoing ["behavioral"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_credit_risk#Scorecards)) [credit scoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_scoring) and [probability of default](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_of_default). Commercial banks deal with mid-sized [corporate loans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_loan#Bank_loan) and [bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_bond), and apply [accounting-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accounting_analyst) and [financial analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_analysis) to determine creditworthiness; the approach differs re investment banking in that the broad client base allows for (necessitates) automation, with [close monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_analyst#Credit_analysts) on [an exception basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_by_exception). [AI](1164.md) / [ML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning) is increasingly employed at all stages.

  [^cite_note-Vyas-79]

  [^cite_note-Boukherouaa-80]

  [^cite_note-Nassr-81]

- Concentration risk, relatedly, differs in [its management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_risk#Monitoring_and_management): the concern is [sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_sector) concentration as opposed to "name concentration". Here, in calculating VaR for a credit portfolio,

  [^cite_note-82]

  &#x20;banks will incorporate a [joint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_probability_distribution) default probability for the various sectors and / or industries.

- Both retail and commercial banks employ strict [liquidity management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_risk#Management) to ensure enough cash for [customer withdrawals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_run): at a minimum meeting the above NSFR and LCR requirements; but also complying with their regulator's [reserve requirement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_requirement). See also [liquidity at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_at_risk).

- Both use [interest rate hedging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate_risk#At_banks) (e.g., swaps) but here, in the main, to protect their profit margin against rate fluctuations, and the resultant "margin compression";

  [^cite_note-83]

  &#x20;i.e., as opposed to addressing market risk *per se*. Re the latter, they will often employ the abovementioned [cash flow at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_flow_at_risk) and [earnings at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earnings_at_risk) models. They also hold specific capital for [interest rate risk in the banking book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate_risk_in_the_banking_book),

  [^cite_note-IRRBB-52]

  [^cite_note-84]

  &#x20;"IRRBB", which deals with the risks associated with a change in interest rates, including [interest rate gaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duration_gap), [basis risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basis_risk), yield curve risk, and [option risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pin_risk).

- Banks' [Economic Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Capital) models, here, are focused more on [credit-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_risk) and [operational risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_risk). ICAAP applies; although allows for modelling which may be simpler, and with less stringent review by regulators.

The Risk Management function typically exists [independent of operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_lines_of_defence) - although may sit in Treasury - and reports directly to the board.[^cite_note-HKIB-76] Its scope often extends to non-financial [operational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_risk) and [reputational risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reputational_risk) (monitoring for any consequent [run on the bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run_on_the_bank)). Specialised software is employed here, [both operationally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_software#Retail_banks) and [for risk management and modelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_management_system).

### Corporate finance



[Contribution analytics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break-even_\(economics\)): Profit and Loss for units sold at current fixed costs.



The same, [for varying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break-even_\(economics\)#Break-even_analysis) (scenario-based) Revenue levels, at current Fixed and Total costs

Further information: [Asset and liability management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_and_liability_management), [Treasury management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_management), [Strategic financial management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_financial_management), and [Managerial risk accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managerial_risk_accounting)

See also: [Corporate finance § Financial risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_finance#Financial_risk_management)

In [corporate finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_finance), and [financial management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_management) more generally,[^cite_note-Dahlquist-85][^cite_note-chron.com-10] financial risk management, as above, is concerned with [business risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_risk) - risks to the [business’ value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valuation_\(finance\)), within the context of its [business strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_strategy) and [capital structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_structure).[^cite_note-Business_Risk_Investopedia-86] The scope here - ie in non-financial firms[^cite_note-PRMIA-13] - is thus broadened[^cite_note-Hampton-9][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-Saylor-88] (re banking) to overlap [enterprise risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_risk_management), and financial risk management then addresses risks to the firm's overall [strategic objectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_management), incorporating various (all) financial aspects[^cite_note-CIMA-89] of the exposures and opportunities arising from business decisions, and their link to the firm’s [appetite for risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_appetite), as well as their impact on [share price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_price). In many organizations, risk executives [are therefore involved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_risk_management#Implementing_an_ERM_program) in strategy formulation: "the choice of which risks to undertake through the allocation of its scarce resources is the key tool available to management."[^cite_note-Chance_Edleson-90] Relatedly,[^cite_note-91] [strategic projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project#Corporate_finance) and [major corporate investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equity_method) [must first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_finance#Corporate_governance) undergo [thorough analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_finance#Investment_and_project_valuation), with approval by an [Investment Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_committee).

Re the standard framework,[^cite_note-CIMA-89] then, the discipline largely focuses on operations, i.e. business risk, as outlined. Here, the management is ongoing[^cite_note-chron.com-10] — see following description — and is coupled with [the use of insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_interruption_insurance),[^cite_note-Vernimmen-92] managing the net-exposure as above: [credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_risk) is usually addressed via [provisioning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_debt#Doubtful_debt_reserve) and [credit insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_credit_insurance); likewise, [where this treatment is deemed appropriate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_management#Potential_risk_treatments), [specifically identified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_management#Assessment) operational risks are also insured.[^cite_note-Saylor-88] [Market risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_risk), in this context,[^cite_note-PRMIA-13] is concerned mainly with changes in [commodity prices](48190.md#Traded_commodity_classes), [interest rates](163115.md), and [foreign exchange rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_rate), and any adverse impact due to these on [cash flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_flow) and [profitability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Profitability), and hence share price.

Correspondingly, the practice here covers two perspectives; these are shared with corporate finance more generally:

1. Both risk management and corporate finance share the goal of enhancing, or at least preserving, firm [value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_\(economics\)).

   [^cite_note-Dahlquist-85]

   &#x20;Here,

   [^cite_note-Hampton-9]

   [^cite_note-CIMA-89]

   &#x20;businesses [devote much time and effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_analysis#Firm-level_analysis) to (short term) [liquidity-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_risk), [cash flow-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_flow_forecasting#Function) and [performance monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managerial_finance#Managerial_accounting_techniques), and Risk Management then also overlaps [cash-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_management) and [treasury management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_management#Corporates), especially as impacted by capital and funding as above. More specifically re business-operations, management focuses on their [break even dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break-even_\(economics\)#Margin_of_safety), [safety margins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margin_of_safety_\(financial\)#Application_to_accounting), [contribution margin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contribution_margin) and [operating leverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_leverage); along with the [corresponding monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue_management#Dynamic_re-evaluation) and [management of revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue_management#Levers), [of costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_analyst), and [of other budget elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variance_\(accounting\)#Variance_analysis). The [DuPont analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DuPont_analysis) entails a "decomposition" of the firm's [return on equity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_on_equity), ROE, allowing management [to identify and address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Return_on_equity#The_DuPont_formula) specific areas of concern,

   [^cite_note-Hargrave-93]

   &#x20;preempting any underperformance vs [shareholders' required return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_asset_pricing_model#Asset-specific_required_return).

   [^cite_note-94]

   &#x20;In larger firms, specialist [Risk Analysts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_Analyst) complement this work with [model-based analytics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_modeling#Accounting) more broadly;

   [^cite_note-SiegelShim1997_2-95]

   [^cite_note-Shimko-96]

   &#x20;in some cases, employing [sophisticated statistical models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_modeling#Quantitative_finance),

   [^cite_note-Shimko-96]

   [^cite_note-97]

   &#x20;in, for example, [financing activity prediction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_and_liability_management#Managing_gaps) problems, and for [risk analysis ahead of a major investment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_finance#Quantifying_uncertainty).

2. Firm exposure to long term market (and business) risk is a direct result of previous [capital investment decisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_budgeting). Where applicable here

   [^cite_note-PRMIA-13]

   [^cite_note-CIMA-89]

   [^cite_note-Dahlquist-85]

   &#x20;— usually in large corporates and [under guidance from](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_banking#Sales_and_trading)

   [^cite_note-98]

   &#x20;their investment bankers — risk analysts will manage and hedge

   [^cite_note-Vernimmen-92]

   &#x20;their exposures using traded [financial instruments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_instruments) to create [commodity-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_\(finance\)#Categories_of_hedgeable_risk),

   [^cite_note-99]

   [^cite_note-100]

   &#x20;[interest rate-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_\(finance\)#Categories_of_hedgeable_risk)

   [^cite_note-101]

   [^cite_note-102]

   &#x20;and [foreign exchange hedges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_hedge)

   [^cite_note-Garrett-103]

   [^cite_note-104]

   &#x20;(see further below). Because company specific, "[over-the-counter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-the-counter_\(finance\))" (OTC) [contracts](19280537.md) tend to be costly to create and monitor — i.e. using [financial engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_engineering) and / or [structured products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structured_product) — ["standard" derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_\(finance\)) that trade on well-established [exchanges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange_\(organized_market\)) are often preferred.

   [^cite_note-Lewellen-15]

   [^cite_note-CIMA-89]

   &#x20;These comprise [options](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_\(finance\)), [futures](242855.md), [forwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_contract), and [swaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swap_\(finance\)); the "second generation" [exotic derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exotic_derivatives) usually trade OTC. Complementary to this hedging, periodically, Treasury may also [adjust the capital structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_finance#Capitalization_structure), reducing [financial leverage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage_\(finance\)#Corporate_finance) - i.e. repaying debt-funding - so as to accommodate increased business risk; they may also suspend [dividends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dividend).

   [^cite_note-105]

[Multinational corporations](214491.md) are faced with additional challenges, particularly as relates to [foreign exchange risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_risk), and the scope of financial risk management modifies significantly in the international realm[^cite_note-Garrett-103] (see [below](#Investment_management) re [geopolitical risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopolitical_risk) generally). Here, dependent on [time horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_horizon) and risk sub-type — [transactions exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_risk#Transaction_risk)[^cite_note-106] (essentially that discussed above), [accounting exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_risk#Translation_risk),[^cite_note-107] and [economic exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_risk#Economic_risk)[^cite_note-108] — so the corporate [will manage its risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_analytics) differently. The forex risk-management discussed here and above, is additional to the per transaction ["forward cover"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_exchange_market) that [importers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Importer) and [exporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exporter) purchase from their bank (alongside other [trade finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_finance) mechanisms).

Hedging-related transactions will attract their own [accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accounting) treatment, and corporates (and banks) may then require changes to systems, processes and documentation;[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110] see [Hedge accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_accounting), [Mark-to-market accounting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark-to-market_accounting), [Hedge relationship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_relationship), [Cash flow hedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_flow_hedge), [IFRS 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFRS_7), [IFRS 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFRS_9), [IFRS 13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFRS_13), [FASB 133](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FASB_133), [IAS 39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAS_39), [FAS 130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accumulated_other_comprehensive_income).

It is common for large corporations to have dedicated risk management teams — typically within [FP\&A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FP\&A) or [corporate treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_treasury) — reporting to the [CRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Risk_Officer); often these overlap the [internal audit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_audit) function (see [Three lines of defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_lines_of_defence)). For small firms, it is impractical to have a formal risk management function, but these typically apply the above practices, at least the first set, informally, as part of the [financial management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_management) function; see [discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_analyst#Financial_planning_and_analysis) under [Financial analyst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_analyst).

The discipline relies on a [range of software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_management#Financial_management_systems),[^cite_note-111] correspondingly, from [spreadsheets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spreadsheet) (invariably as a starting point, and frequently in total[^cite_note-112]) through commercial [EPM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_performance_management) and [BI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_intelligence) tools, often [BusinessObjects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BusinessObjects) ([SAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAP)), [OBI EE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oracle_Business_Intelligence_Suite_Enterprise_Edition) ([Oracle](22591.md)), [Cognos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognos) ([IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM)), and [Power BI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_BI) ([Microsoft](19001.md)).[^cite_note-113]

### Insurance

Further information: [Insurance § Insurers' business model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance#Insurers'_business_model)

See also: [IFRS 17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFRS_17)



Actuaries use [Extreme Value Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_value_theory)

[^cite_note-Embrechts-38]

&#x20;to model rare events such as "[100-year floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100-year_flood)". Pictured is [Kaskaskia, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaskaskia,_Illinois), entirely submerged during the [Great Flood of 1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Flood_of_1993).

[Insurance companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurer) [make profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loss_ratio#Insurance_loss_ratio)[^cite_note-Goldfarb-114][^cite_note-Nissim-115] through [underwriting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underwriting#Insurance_underwriting) — selecting which risks to insure, charging a risk-appropriate [premium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_premium), and then [paying claims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance#Claims) as they occur — [and by investing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance#Insurers'_business_model) the premiums they collect from insured parties. They will, in turn, manage their own risks[^cite_note-Grondin-11][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-Nissim-115] with a focus on solvency and the ability to pay claims: [Life Insurers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_insurer)[^cite_note-Farr-118] are concerned more with longevity risk and [interest rate risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate_risk#Insurers); Short-Term Insurers ([Property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_insurance), [Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance), [Casualty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casualty_insurance))[^cite_note-Goldfarb-114] emphasize [catastrophe-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_God) and claims volatility risks.

Fundamental here, therefore, are risk selection and [pricing discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_premium), which as outlined, prevent insurers from taking on unprofitable business. For *expected* claims — i.e. those covered, on average, by the [pricing model’s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuarial_science) assumptions re claim [frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_rate) and severity — [reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserve_\(accounting\)) are set aside ([actuarial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuarial_reserve), with [statutory reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutory_reserve) as a floor). These will cover both known claims, reported but unpaid, as well as those which are [incurred but not reported](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Incurred_but_not_reported) (IBNR). To absorb *unexpected* losses, insurance companies maintain a minimum level of capital plus an additional [solvency margin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvency_ratio). Capital requirements are based on [the risks an insurer faces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_risk_and_solvency_assessment), such as underwriting risk, market risk, credit risk, and operational risk, and [are governed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Insurance_regulation) by frameworks such as [Solvency II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvency_II) (Europe) and Risk-Based Capital[^cite_note-119] (U.S.). To further mitigate large-scale risks — i.e. to reduce exposure to catastrophic losses — insurers transfer portions of their risk to [Reinsurers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinsurance). Here, analogous to VaR for banks, [insurers use simulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_modelling_\(insurance\)) to estimate potential losses at various thresholds, while stress tests assess how extreme events might impact capital and reserves under various[^cite_note-SOA_scenarios-120] scenarios. ([Dynamic financial analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_financial_analysis) (DFA) and the [Wilkie model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkie_investment_model) are used generally in scenario analytics, and may underpin the VaR engine.) In parallel with all these, as above, premiums collected [are invested](30857467.md) to generate returns which will supplement [underwriting profits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance#Underwriting_and_investing), and the fund is then risk-managed as follows:[^cite_note-Heywood-121] ALM must ensure that investments [align with the timing and amount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liability-driven_investment_strategy) of expected claim payouts; while returns ("float") are defended using the techniques[^cite_note-Ang-122] [discussed in the next section](#Investment_management). As for banks, all models are [regularly reviewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuarial_science#Technological_advances),[^cite_note-123] comparing,[^cite_note-124] i.a., "Actual versus Expected".

Specific treatments will, as outlined, differ by insurer-profile:

- Life Insurers

  [^cite_note-Farr-118]

  &#x20;deal with long-term risks tied to [mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_rate), [longevity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longevity), and interest rates. Policies (e.g., [whole life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_life_insurance), [annuities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_annuity)) can span decades, making them sensitive to long-term economic and [demographic shifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_shift). Reserves are large and complex due to the long duration of liabilities, with capital models emphasizing [longevity risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longevity_risk), [interest rate risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate_risk#Insurers), and [lapse risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancellation_\(insurance\)). Stress tests, correspondingly, focus on long-term scenarios (e.g. sustained low interest rates, or a [pandemic](24255.md) related spike in [mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortality_rate)). Reinsurance is often used for [excess death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excess_deaths) claims. ALM here is critical, and investments will be in long-term, stable assets ([bonds as well as equities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_fund#Balanced_fund)) to match these [long-duration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duration_\(finance\)) liabilities.

- Short-Term Insurers

  [^cite_note-Goldfarb-114]

  &#x20;[face more volatility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_cycle) relative to Life companies, while claims are typically resolved within a year or two (although [tail events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tail_event) - e.g. [asbestos litigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asbestos_litigation) - can linger). Thus, reserves are shorter-term but must account for high uncertainty in claim frequency and severity; IBNR may be significant, especially after large events. Capital requirements focus on underwriting risk (e.g., mispricing policies) and catastrophe risk (e.g., [hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane), [earthquakes](10106.md)). Stress tests therefore emphasize short-term catastrophic scenarios, and specialized [catastrophe models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catastrophe_modeling) are often used. Reinsurance is widely utilized to cap exposure to catastrophes; as are [quota-share](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinsurance_sidecar#Precedents) or [excess-of-loss treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinsurance#Non-proportional) re single events. Rapid claims settlement reduces reserving duration compared to life insurance, and portfolios lean toward liquid, shorter-term assets (e.g., [cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_market_fund), [short-term bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_bill)).

In a typical insurance company, Risk Management and the [Actuarial Function](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuary#Traditional_employment) are separate but closely related departments, each with distinct responsibilities. In smaller companies, the lines might blur, with actuaries taking on some risk management tasks, or vice versa. Regardless, the Head Actuary (or Chief Actuary or Appointed Actuary) has specific responsibilities, typically requiring formal "sign-off": Reserve Adequacy and Solvency and Capital Assessment, as well as Reinsurance Arrangements.

The relevant calculations are usually performed with specialized software — provided e.g. by [WTW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_Towers_Watson) and [Milliman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milliman) — and often using [R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_\(programming_language\)) or [SAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAS_\(software\)).

### Investment management

Further information: [Portfolio optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_optimization), [Active management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_management), and [Passive management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_management)

See also: [Modern portfolio theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_portfolio_theory), [Post-modern portfolio theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-modern_portfolio_theory), and [Financial economics § Portfolio theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_economics#Portfolio_theory)



[Modern portfolio theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_portfolio_theory) suggests a diversified portfolio of [shares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shares) and other [asset classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_classes) (such as debt in [corporate bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_bonds), [treasury bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_bond), or [money market funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Money_market_funds)) will realise more predictable returns. Illustrated is a typical [diversified fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_fund#Asset_allocation_fund), where [asset allocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_allocation) is between asset classes; within each, managers may further select specific securities.



Efficient Frontier. The [hyperbola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperbola) is sometimes referred to as the "Markowitz bullet", and its upward sloped portion is the efficient frontier if no risk-free asset is available. With a risk-free asset, the straight [capital allocation line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_allocation_line) is the efficient frontier.



Here maximizing return and minimizing risk such that the portfolio is [Pareto efficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pareto_efficiency) (Pareto-optimal points in red)

[Fund managers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fund_manager), classically,[^cite_note-Drake_Fabozzi-125] define the risk of a [portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_\(finance\)) as its [variance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variance)[^cite_note-Kenton-12] (or [standard deviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_deviation)) — the extent to which the portfolio's [return is uncertain](11930108.md#Volatility_for_investors) — and through [diversification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diversification_\(finance\)) the [portfolio is optimized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_optimization) so as to achieve the lowest risk for a given targeted return,[^cite_note-126] or equivalently the highest return for a given level of risk: this approach is known as [mean-variance optimization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_portfolio_theory#Efficient_frontier_with_no_risk-free_asset). (The collection of these [risk-efficient portfolios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_variance_efficiency) form the "[efficient frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_frontier)"; see [Markowitz model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markowitz_model).) The logic here is that returns from different assets are highly unlikely to be perfectly [correlated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correlation), and in fact the correlation may sometimes be negative. In this way, market risk particularly, and other financial risks such as [inflation risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_risk) (see below) can at least partially be moderated by forms of diversification.

A key issue, however, is that the (assumed) relationships are (implicitly) forward looking. As observed in the [late-2000s recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late-2000s_recession), historic relationships can break down, resulting in losses to market participants believing that diversification would provide sufficient protection (in that market, including funds that had been explicitly set up to avoid being affected in this way[^cite_note-127]). A related issue is that [diversification has costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markowitz_model#Demerits_of_the_HM_model): as correlations are not constant it may be necessary to [regularly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_management) [rebalance the portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebalancing_investments), incurring [transaction costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transaction_costs), negatively impacting [investment performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_performance);[^cite_note-128] and as the fund manager diversifies, so this problem compounds (and a large fund may also exert [market impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_impact)). See [Modern portfolio theory § Criticisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_portfolio_theory#Criticisms).

The above mean-variance optimization is implemented[^cite_note-129] (more or less) directly[^cite_note-130] by [asset allocation funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_allocation). At the same time - in part given the issues outlined - alternative methods for portfolio construction have been developed,[^cite_note-Meucci-131][^cite_note-132] [including](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_economics#Portfolio_theory) new approaches to [defining risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_measure), and to the [optimization itself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_optimization#Improving_portfolio_optimization).[^cite_note-Meucci-131] Notably, managers will employ [factor models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_factor_models)[^cite_note-Practitioner's_Guide-133] — generically [APT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbitrage_pricing_theory) — using [time series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_series) regression[^cite_note-Kempthorne-134] to design portfolios[^cite_note-Ang-122] with [the desired exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_beta) to macroeconomic, market and / or fundamental [risk factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_factor_\(finance\));[^cite_note-Sharpe-135] respectively: macro-, [factor-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_investing), and [style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_investing) portfolios. The optimization, under both the mean-variance and [^cite_note-136] [^cite_note-137] factor model approaches, may be with respect to [(tail)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tail_risk_parity) [risk parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_parity), focusing on allocation of risk, rather than allocation of capital, and employ, e.g. the [Black–Litterman model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black%E2%80%93Litterman_model) which modifies the above "Markowitz optimization", to incorporate the "views" of the portfolio manager.[^cite_note-138]

Alongside these, [Discretionary investment management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discretionary_investment_management) funds,[^cite_note-Dicretionary_investopedia-139][^cite_note-Dicretionary_fastercapital-140] instead, lean heavily on traditional [stock picking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_picking), employing [company specific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_valuation) ("bottom-up") [fundamental analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_analysis) in preference to advanced[^cite_note-141] mathematical approaches. (These Managers are then [the major consumers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buy_side) of [securities research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_research).) The specific concerns will, in turn, differ[^cite_note-ActiveInvesting101-142] as a function of the Manager's [investment philosophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_philosophy) and [active strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_strategy), preferring, e.g., [value-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_investing), [growth-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_investing) or [defensive stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-volatility_investing) within her fund; in all cases, reaching a [target price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_valuation) [^cite_note-143] can trigger a review or sale. Portfolios here are managed, also, using qualitative and subjective considerations, which include evaluations [of company management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_analyst#Securities_firms), [industry dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter's_five_forces_analysis), and [macro/political factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_financial_system#Unique_financial_risks). Risk Management here, to be discussed below, will correspondingly, be largely pragmatic and heuristic, as opposed to quantitative.

An overarching consideration - i.e regardless of approach - is that the Manager *must* ensure[^cite_note-Drake_Fabozzi-125][^cite_note-ActiveInvesting101-142] that the portfolio's risk level matches [the investor's objectives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investor_profile) and comfort zone, i.e. must ensure [risk tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_tolerance) alignment. Correspondingly, the fund's ([advertised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospectus_\(finance\))) [investment strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_strategy) will, almost necessarily, define its own risk tolerance and [appetite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_appetite), and hence selection and application of optimization-criteria and risk management techniques (and investors). Here, for both individuals and Funds, generally, longer time horizons allow for greater tolerance of short-term volatility, while shorter horizons require more conservative strategies. A further generalization: portfolios constructed using mathematical-approaches are more exposed to market risk and the [stock market cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market_cycle); while those constructed by [stock picking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_picking) are exposed, more, to [firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiosyncratic_risk) and sector specific risks.

In measuring risk quantitatively, the Manager will employ a variety[^cite_note-Heywood-121] of [financial risk modeling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_risk_modeling) techniques — including [value at risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_at_risk),[^cite_note-144] [historical simulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_simulation_\(finance\)), [stress tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_test_\(financial\)), [^cite_note-145] and[^cite_note-McNeil-37][^cite_note-Embrechts-38] [extreme value theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_value_theory) — to analyze the portfolio and to forecast the likely losses incurred for a selection of exposures and scenarios (see [§ Investment banking](#Investment_banking) for discussion).

Guided by the analytics, and / or the above considerations, fund managers (and [traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trader_\(finance\))) will implement specific [risk hedging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_\(finance\)) techniques and [strategies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_\(finance\)#Hedging_strategies).[^cite_note-Drake_Fabozzi-125][^cite_note-Kenton-12] As appropriate, these are applied to the portfolio as a whole ("top-down") or to individual holdings ("bottom-up"):

- To protect the overall portfolio, fund managers

  [^cite_note-Clarke-146]

  &#x20;[may sell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_\(finance\)#Futures_and_options_contracts) the [stock market index future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market_index_future) or [buy puts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Put_option#Buying_a_put) on the [stock market index option](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_market_index_option);

  [^cite_note-147]

  [^cite_note-148]

  &#x20;the respective sensitivities, [portfolio beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_\(finance\)) and [option delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_\(finance\)#Delta), determine the number of hedge-contracts required.

  [^cite_note-Clarke-146]

  &#x20;For both, the logic is that the (diversified) portfolio is likely highly correlated with the [stock index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_index) it is part of: thus if the portfolio-value declines, the index will have declined likewise with the derivative holder profiting correspondingly.

  [^cite_note-Clarke-146]

  &#x20;Fund managers may (instead) engage in "[portfolio insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_insurance)", a dynamic hedging process that involves selling index futures during periods of decline and using the proceeds to offset portfolio losses.

* Fund managers, or traders, may also wish to [hedge a specific stock's price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_\(finance\)#Hedging_a_stock_price). Here, they may likewise

  [^cite_note-Clarke-146]

  &#x20;[buy a single-stock put](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Options_strategy#Bearish_strategies), [or sell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_\(finance\)#Hedging_equity_and_equity_futures) a [single-stock future](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-stock_futures). Alternative strategies may rely on assumed relationships between related stocks, employing, for example, a ["long/short" strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long/short_equity).

- [Bond portfolios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_fund), when e.g. a component of an [asset-allocation fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stock_fund#Asset_allocation_fund) or other [diversified portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_fund), are typically managed similar to equity above: the fund manager will hedge her bond allocation with [bond index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_index) futures or options; with the number of contracts, a function of [duration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duration_\(finance\)).

  [^cite_note-149]

  [^cite_note-150]

  [^cite_note-Clarke-146]

  &#x20;Managers may also employ [duration management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duration_\(finance\)#Hedging_and_portfolio_construction) more generally to ["immunize"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunization_\(finance\)) the fund against undesired [interest rate risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate_risk) (often guided by a "[DV01 target"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duration_\(finance\)#Hedging_and_portfolio_construction)), or against failing to meet a target return objective (e.g. a [target date fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_date_fund) has an implicit liability to its investors). See below re liquidity management; also [liability-driven investment strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liability-driven_investment_strategy), [duration gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duration_gap), [laddering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laddering).

  [^cite_note-151]

* For individual [bonds](60737.md) and other [fixed income securities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_income), specific [credit and interest rate risks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_income#Risks) can be hedged

  [^cite_note-Clarke-146]

  &#x20;using [interest rate-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate_derivative) and [credit derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_derivative) (although [with care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-curve_framework#Risk_management), under multi-curves

  [^cite_note-CQF-34]

  &#x20;). Sensitivities [re interest rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interest_rate_risk) are measured using [duration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_duration) and [convexity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_convexity) for bonds, and [DV01](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DV01) and [key rate durations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_rate_duration) generally, and an offsetting derivative-position is purchased. For [credit risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_risk),

  [^cite_note-152]

  &#x20;sensitivities are measured via [CS01](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CS01), while analysts use models such as [Jarrow–Turnbull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarrow%E2%80%93Turnbull_model) and [KMV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KMV_model) to estimate the ([risk neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rational_pricing#The_risk_neutrality_assumption)

  [^cite_note-153]

  ) [probability of default](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability_of_default), hedging where appropriate, usually [via credit default swaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_default_swap#Hedging). Probabilities ([actuarial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathematical_finance#History:_Q_versus_P)) may also be obtained from [Bond credit ratings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_credit_rating); then, often at a portfolio level — e.g. for credit-VaR — analysts will use a [transition matrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stochastic_matrix) of these

  [^cite_note-154]

  &#x20;to estimate the probability [and impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bond_valuation#Relative_price_approach) of a "credit migration",

  [^cite_note-155]

  [^cite_note-156]

  &#x20;aggregating the bond-by-bond result. Interest rate- and credit risk together, may be hedged via a [Total return swap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_return_swap). See [Fixed income analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed_income_analysis)

- For [derivative portfolios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_strategy), and positions, [the Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greeks_\(finance\)#Use_of_the_Greeks) are a vital risk management tool: as above, these measure sensitivity to a small change in a given underlying price, rate, [or parameter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatility_risk), and the portfolio [is then rebalanced accordingly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Option_\(finance\)#Risks)

  [^cite_note-Clarke-146]

  &#x20;by including additional derivatives with offsetting characteristics, or by purchasing or selling [specified units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_neutral#Creating_the_position) of the [underlying security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underlying).

Further, and more generally, various safety-criteria may also inform overall portfolio composition, both at initial construction and, in this context, as a risk overlay. The [Kelly criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion)[^cite_note-157] [will suggest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_criterion#Application_to_the_stock_market) - i.e. limit - the size of a position that an investor should hold in her portfolio. [Roy's safety-first criterion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy's_safety-first_criterion)[^cite_note-158] [minimizes the probability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy's_safety-first_criterion#Example) of the portfolio's return falling below a minimum desired threshold. [Chance-constrained portfolio selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chance-constrained_portfolio_selection) similarly seeks to ensure that the probability of final wealth falling below a given "safety level" is acceptable.

Managers likewise employ the abovementioned [factor models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_factor_models) on an ongoing basis to measure exposure to the relevant [risk factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_factor_\(finance\)).[^cite_note-Sharpe-135] Ahead of an anticipated movement in any of these, the Manager may then,[^cite_note-Practitioner's_Guide-133][^cite_note-Heywood-121] [as indicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_investing), reduce holdings, hedge, or purchase offsetting exposure. Thus a factor-based fund may "tilt" from [momentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_investing#Momentum_factor) to [value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factor_investing#Value_factor), a style-based fund from [cyclical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclical_tactical_asset_allocation) to [defensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necessity_good). Risk management for [asset allocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_allocation) funds is, similarly, both proactive and reactive: guided by [economic forecasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_forecast), a diversified fund could,[^cite_note-Ibbotson-159] [allocation-strategy dependent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_allocation#Allocation_strategy) ([tactical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactical_asset_allocation), [dynamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_allocation#Dynamic_asset_allocation), or [strategic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_allocation#Strategic_asset_allocation)) [rebalance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebalancing_investments#Rebalancing_to_control_risk) its [asset allocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_allocation) from e.g. equities to bonds. In all cases here, the Risk Manager may impose a limit on the relevant factor-, asset-, or country exposure.

Parallel to the aforementioned concerns,[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-McMillan-161] managers — active and [passive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_management) — periodically monitor and manage [tracking error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracking_error),[^cite_note-Ibbotson-159] i.e. [underperformance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_management#Risk-adjusted_performance_measurement) vs a ["benchmark"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benchmark_\(finance\)). Here, they will use [attribution analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_attribution) preemptively so as to diagnose the source early, and to take corrective action: realigning, often factor-wise, on the basis of this "feedback".[^cite_note-McMillan-161][^cite_note-162] [As relevant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_drift#Implications), they will similarly use [style analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Returns-based_style_analysis) to address [style drift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Style_drift). See also [Fixed-income attribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixed-income_attribution) and [Benchmark-driven investment strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benchmark-driven_investment_strategy).

In preference to the approaches above, Managers of [Discretionary Funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discretionary_investment_management), will, as mentioned, rely largely [^cite_note-Dicretionary_investopedia-139][^cite_note-Dicretionary_fastercapital-140] [on insight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_analyst#Securities_firms) when managing portfolio risk. Here, they will closely monitor [company-level risks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiosyncratic_risk), [industry dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industry_analyst), and [macro-factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_cycle#Identifyin), and will then reduce exposure, or hedge, based on any perceived risks. The weight attached to the various concerns will differ given the strategy employed: [value funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_investing), for example, focus on changes in [firm fundamentals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_analysis) (but otherwise will "[buy and hold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buy_and_hold)"); while [growth funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_investing) are exposed to both market ([beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_\(finance\))) [and sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Stock_market_indices_by_industry) returns. In parallel, these Managers apply ([practice derived](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Techne)) position-level [stop loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_\(exchange\)#Stop_orders) rules, as well as portfolio-level construction limits re max position size, sector exposure, country or currency exposure, and benchmark-relative [tracking error](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracking_error). As a *supplement*, Managers (at [larger institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_asset_management_firms)) may use various of the above quantitative tools to monitor risk exposures and potential losses.

Regardless of approach, all Managers - especially those with long horizons - must ensure a positive [real growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_and_nominal_value#Real_growth_rate), i.e. that their [portfolio-returns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investment_performance) at least match [inflation](38286.md) (and regardless of market returns). Since this phenomenon impacts all securities,[^cite_note-163] [inflation risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_risk) will typically be managed[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] at the portfolio level. Here the manager will programmatically[^cite_note-166] (or heuristically) increase exposure[^cite_note-ssga-167] to inflation-sensitive stocks (e.g. [consumer staples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Industry_Classification_Standard)) and / or invest in [tangible assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset#Tangible_assets) and [commodities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodity_\(type_of_good\)), as well as [inflation swaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_swap) and [inflation-linked bonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation-linked_bond) (ILBs). The latter [inflation derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_derivative) can, in fact, provide a direct [inflation hedge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_hedge): to fully offset inflation, [^cite_note-168] the proportion of the portfolio in ILBs, for example, will correspond to its [inflation beta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_beta) [^cite_note-169] [^cite_note-170] [^cite_note-ssga-167] (sensitivity of portfolio return to increases in inflation, measured using regression). See [Inflation hedge § Portfolio construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_hedge#Portfolio_construction).

Newer and broader, and often qualitative[^cite_note-New_York_Life-171] risks, are similarly managed industry-wide. These include ESG risks (financially material risks related to the broader [environmental, social, and governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental,_social,_and_governance) contexts in which the firm operates),[^cite_note-Morningstar-172] [cybersecurity risks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyber_risk_quantification) (a material drop in share prices caused, e.g., by a significant [ransomware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransomware) incident)[^cite_note-RLAM-173] and [geopolitical risks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopolitical_risk).[^cite_note-New_York_Life-171] These risks are often less tangible and less immediately visible than traditional financial risks,[^cite_note-Morningstar-172][^cite_note-174] and quantifying these can be challenging.[^cite_note-New_York_Life-171] Managers may then employ techniques such as scenario analysis, and, sometimes, [approaches from game theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_theory#Political_science). Based on this, in the case of geopolitical risks they will then diversify geographically and / or increase exposure (possibly factor-wise) to [macro-sensitive assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safe-haven_currency) such as [gold](2697304.md#Investment_strategies), [oil](5137675.md#Speculative_trading_and_crude_oil_futures), and [Bitcoin](28249265.md). (See [Global macro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_macro).) ESG and cybersecurity risks are dealt with by diversification, and (for bottom-up portfolios) proactive screening,[^cite_note-Morningstar-172] with [direct management engagement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shareholder_activism)[^cite_note-RLAM-173] as necessary. The rise of [alternative investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_investments) (e.g., [cryptocurrencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrencies), [private equity](191280.md)) introduces unique risks that must also be addressed.[^cite_note-175][^cite_note-176]

Beyond market volatility, investment management requires rigorous control of liquidity, operational, and [leverage risks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leverage_\(finance\)#Risk) [^cite_note-177] [^cite_note-178] (the concerns mirroring those discussed above re banking). [Liquidity risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquidity_risk#Management) ensures that a fund can meet [redemption requests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_fund#Open-end_funds) or rebalance portfolios without incurring excessive [transaction costs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transaction_cost), typically by maintaining [cash buffers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_operating_assets#Calculation_-_Operating_Approach) or limiting holdings in illiquid assets; this is closely tied to leverage concerns, where borrowed capital magnifies losses (as outlined above) and introduces the risk of [margin calls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margin_call) or [forced liquidation](565034.md#Compulsory_liquidation). Operational and counterparty risks—the potential for failure in internal systems, trade execution, or default by a trading partner—are mitigated through robust [reconciliation processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconciliation_\(accounting\)), [segregation of duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segregation_of_duties), and [collateral agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collateral_management). Note that the controls here, will, in their turn, [impose constraints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constrained_optimization) on any portfolio holdings; see [Portfolio optimization § Optimization constraints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_optimization#Optimization_constraints).

These concerns differ significantly by fund structure. [Hedge funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_fund) [often employ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_fund#Risk_management) high leverage and hold illiquid assets to boost returns, managing the associated risks through ["lock-up" periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hedge_fund#Risks_shared_with_other_investment_types) that restrict withdrawals; in contrast, [mutual funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_funds) and [ETFs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exchange-traded_fund) typically face [regulatory limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_fund#Regulation_and_operation) on leverage and must provide daily liquidity, necessitating stricter risk controls to prevent [asset-liability mismatches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset-liability_mismatch). Unlike institutional managers, discretionary managers and retail brokers [^cite_note-ActiveInvesting101-142] manage liquidity by holding cash and [blue-chip assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_chip_\(stock_market\)) for immediate access, and avoiding or (strictly) limiting leverage; operational and counterparty risks are largely outsourced to [clearing houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clearing_house_\(finance\)) and protected by, e.g., [SIPC insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Securities_Investor_Protection_Corporation), prioritizing regulatory safety over complex hedging strategies. [Pension funds](43615.md) are, as above, specifically concerned re any asset–liability mismatch, and will employ [interest rate immunization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunization_\(finance\)) or [cashflow matching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cashflow_matching), respectively attempting to offset changes in liabilities with corresponding changes in asset value, or by matching cash outflows - i.e., financial obligations - with cash inflows and asset growth over a given time horizon; see [dedicated portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedicated_portfolio_theory).&#x20;

While portfolio risks are managed day-to-day by the fund manager, the [Chief Risk Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Risk_Officer) - often[^cite_note-179] [Chief Investment Officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Investment_Officer) - is responsible for overall risk.[^cite_note-180][^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182] The Risk Function ("Group" at an IB, as above) thus monitors aggregate firm-level risks (exposure across funds, as well as, e.g., [reputational risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reputational_risk)) ensuring alignment with the firm's [risk appetite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_appetite) and [regulatory obligations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_regulation); it will, relatedly, be involved in scenario generation - economic and geopolitical - and stress testing. This team also provides [independent challenge and escalation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_lines_of_defence) if a fund breaches its Risk Budget [^cite_note-183] (e.g. VaR, stress losses and sector concentration). The CRO typically signs off on stress testing, liquidity risk reviews, and [model validation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quantitative_analysis_\(finance\)#Model_validation).

Given the complexity of these analyses and techniques, Fund Managers - and [Risk Analysts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_analyst) - typically rely on [sophisticated software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_software#Fund_management) (as do banks, above). Widely used platforms are provided by [BlackRock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlackRock) ([Aladdin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aladdin_\(BlackRock\))), [Refinitiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refinitiv) ([Eikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eikon)), [Finastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finastra), [Murex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murex_\(financial_software\)), [Numerix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerix), MPI, [Morningstar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morningstar,_Inc.#Morningstar_Direct), [MSCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSCI) ([Barra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barra,_Inc.)), [SimCorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimCorp) ([Axioma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SimCorp#Acquisitions)), [SS\&C Advent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS\&C_Advent) and [State Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Street_Corporation) ([Charles River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Street_Corporation#21st_century)).
