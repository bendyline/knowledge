# Higher education in Iran



The College of Humanities of the [University of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tehran)



The [University of Tabriz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tabriz)



First Iranian women entered university

[^cite_note-1]

[Iran](14653.md) has a network of [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_University), [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_University), and state-affiliated universities offering degrees in higher education. State-run universities of Iran are under the direct supervision of Iran's [Ministry of Science, Research and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Science,_Research_and_Technology) (for non-medical universities) and [Ministry of Health and Medical Education](24234168.md) (for medical schools). According to article 3 of the Constitution of the Islamic Republic of Iran, Iran guarantees "free education and physical training for everyone at all levels, and the facilitation and expansion of higher education." [IANI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Against_Nuclear_Iran) representatives say that academics in Iran are "ultimately directed by the regime and military when it comes to specific areas of research". Rana Dadpour, who taught at an Iranian university, said that certain areas of research are directed by the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) and could be employed for "surveillance or military purposes".[^cite_note-2]

## History

### Pre-Islamic era

See also: [Academy of Gondishapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Gondishapur)

The existence of pre-Islamic era universities such as the [School of Nisibis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Nisibis), [Sarouyeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarouyeh), [Reishahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reishahr), and The [Academy of Gondishapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Gondishapur) provide examples of precedence of academic institutions of science that date back to ancient times.[^cite_note-3]

### Islamic era

See also: [Hawza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawza) and [Nizamiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizamiyya)



An academic library in [Basra](57580.md)

[^cite_note-4]

&#x20;depicted in the 13th century by [Yahya ibn Mahmud al-Wasiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_ibn_Mahmud_al-Wasiti), found in the *[Maqamat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maqamat_al-Hariri)* of Hariri.

The traditions and heritage of these centers of higher learning were later carried on to schools such as Iran's [Nizamiyyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizamiyya), and Baghdad's [House of Wisdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Wisdom), during the Islamic era. The Nizamiyyas were among the first well organized [institutions of higher learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_higher-learning_institutions) in the [Muslim world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_world). The quality of education was among the highest in the Islamic world, and they were even renowned in Europe. They were supported financially, politically, and spiritually by the royal establishment and the elite class. Some scholars have suggested that the establishment of the Nizamiyya madrasas was in fact an attempt to thwart the growing influence of another group of Muslims, the [Ismailis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismailis), in the region. Indeed, Nizam al-Mulk devoted a significant section in his *[Siyasatnama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siyasatnama)* (*Books of Politics*) to refuting the Ismaili doctrines.[^cite_note-5]

The most famous and celebrated of all the Nizamiyyas was [Nizamiyya of Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizamiyya_of_Baghdad) (established 1065), where [Nizam al-Mulk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizam_al-Mulk) appointed the distinguished philosopher and theologian, [al-Ghazali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ghazali), as a professor. Persian poet [Sa'di](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadi_\(poet\)) was a student of the Baghdad Nizamiyya. Other Nizamiyyas were located in [Nishapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nishapur), [Amol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amol), [Balkh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balkh), [Herat](14128.md) and [Isfahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isfahan_\(city\)).

### Modern

[Abbas Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_Mirza) was who first dispatched Iranian students to Europe for a Western education.[^cite_note-6] The history of the establishment of Western style academic universities in Iran (Persia) dates back to 1851 with the establishment of [Darolfonoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_ul-Funun_\(Persia\)) – which was founded as a result of the efforts of the royal vizier *[Mirza Taghi Khan Amir Kabir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Kabir)*, aimed at training and teaching Iranian experts in many fields of science and technology.[^cite_note-7]

In 1855, The Ministry of Sciencewas first established, and Ali Gholi Mirza I'tizad al-saltaneh (علیقلی میرزا اعتضاد السلطنه) was appointed Iran's first Minister of Science by [Nasereddin Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasereddin_Shah). By the 1890s [Darolfonoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_ul-Funun_\(Persia\)) was competing with other prominent institutions of modern learning. The *Military College of Tehran* (Madraseh-ye Nezam), established in 1885 with a budget of 10,000–12,000 tomans, was its first rival; and in 1899 the [*College of political sciences* (Madraseh-ye olum-e siyasi)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_School_of_Political_Sciences) was organized within the Foreign ministry."[^cite_note-8]

The Ministry of Higher Education, which oversees the operation of all institutes of higher education in Iran, was established in 1967. However, it was back in 1928 that Iran's first university, as we know it today, was proposed by an Iranian physicist, [Mahmoud Hessaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Hessaby). The University of Tehran (a.k.a. Tehran University) was designed by French architect [Andre Godard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andre_Godard), and built in 1934. Today, [Tehran University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Tehran) is Iran's largest university with over 32,000 students.

In the medical field, it was [Joseph Cochran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Cochran) who first founded a professional school in Iran in 1878, and who is often credited for founding Iran's "first contemporary medical college",[^cite_note-9] as well as founding Westminster Hospital, one of Iran's first modern hospitals, in [Urmia](615663.md) formerly known as Rezaieh in honor of Shia Islam's 8th imam. The medical faculty Cochran established at [Urmia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urmia_University) was joined by several other Americans, namely Drs. Wright, Homlz, van Nourdon, and Miller. They were all buried in Urmia as their resting place after serving the area for many years.

&#x20;In Tehran, [Samuel M. Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_M._Jordan) (the namesake of "Jordan Ave." in Tehran), also was directly responsible for the expansion of the [American College in Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz_High_School). The school received a permanent charter from the Board of Regents of the [State University of New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_New_York) in 1932.[^cite_note-10]



*McCormick Hall, [American College of Tehran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz_High_School)*, circa 1930.

By the end of the first Pahlavi period in 1941, the University of Tehran was still the only modern university in the country. Hence, the ministry of science commenced the establishment of other universities in [Isfahan](223149.md), [Tabriz](439791.md), [Ahvaz](599079.md), and [Shiraz](39622.md), with special emphasis given to the medical and veterinary sciences.[^cite_note-11] Dr. Charles Oberling was highly instrumental in this regard.[^cite_note-12]

In 1953, there were four universities with 14,500 undergraduate students whereas in 1977 there were 16 universities with 154,315 undergraduate students.[^cite_note-13]

[The Shah](20065598.md) soon initiated projects to build Iranian universities modeled after American schools. Thus *Pahlavi University* ([Shiraz University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiraz_University) today), [Sharif University of Technology](453155.md), and [Isfahan University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isfahan_University_of_Technology), three of Iran's top academic universities were all directly modeled on American institutions such as the [University of Illinois at Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Illinois_at_Chicago), [MIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT), and the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania).[^cite_note-autogenerated2-14][^cite_note-15] The Shah in return was generous in awarding American universities with financial gifts. For example, the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) received a gift from the Shah in the form of an endowed chair of [petroleum engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_engineering), and a million dollar donation was given to the [George Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington_University) to create an [Iranian Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranistics) program.[^cite_note-autogenerated2-14]

[The Iranian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_revolution) put an end to the massive US-Iran academic relations. In 1980, a major overhaul in the academia and higher education system of Iran initiated by [Ayatollah Khomeini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayatollah_Khomeini) led to what is referred to in Iran as "Iran's Cultural Revolution". However, all universities in the country were closed down from 1980 to 1983.[^cite_note-parsi12-16] In addition, Islamic curricula and Islamic educational setting were introduced when the universities were reopened.[^cite_note-parsi12-16]

In 1986, the ministry of higher education handed over supervision and overseeing of education in the medical sciences in Iran to the ministry of health, treatment and medical education.

After the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), some new universities were founded and doctoral programs were developed in the previous universities. The number of university students is now more than six times as many as in 1979 (when Shah was overthrown), so that critics debate whether the national entrance exam is useful anymore or not.

## Academic system of Iranian universities

See also: [Science and technology in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_Iran) and [Islamic Azad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Azad_University)



[Tehran University of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_University_of_Medical_Sciences)' *Tehran Heart Center*

In 2008, Iran had over 3.5 million students enrolled in universities;[^cite_note-17] about 1.7 million in various programs in the [Islamic Azad University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Azad_University) system and the remainder in state universities. In addition the new enrollment numbers for the academic year 2004 were 290,000 in Azad universities and 250,000 in state universities. Iran has 54 state operated universities and 42 state medical schools. These are primarily the top choice for students in national entrance exams. There are 289 major [private universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_university) in Iran.[^cite_note-18] In all these schools, except for private universities such as the Islamic Azad University system, tuition and room and board is mostly paid for by the government. The universities themselves largely operate on [state budgets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_budget).[^cite_note-19]

Some schools offer degrees in conjunction with European universities. The [International University of Chabahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_University_of_Chabahar) for example in 2007 offered programs under the guidance of [London School of Economics and Political Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_School_of_Economics_and_Political_Science) [Goldsmiths University of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldsmiths,_University_of_London), and [Royal Holloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Holloway).[^cite_note-20] Other schools such as the [Institute for Advanced Studies in Basic Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Advanced_Studies_in_Basic_Sciences) in [Zanjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanjan_\(city\)), have close collaboration with The [International Centre for Theoretical Physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Centre_for_Theoretical_Physics) in [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste), [Italy](14532.md) for workshops, seminars, and summer schools. The Iranian government also in 2017 offered scholarships for successful applicants to pursue PhD-level studies in foreign countries, mostly [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain).[^cite_note-21]

By early 2000, Iran allocated around 0.4% of its [gross domestic product](12594.md) to [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development); that ranks it "far behind industrialized societies" and the world average of 1.4%.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-22] By 2009 this ratio of research to GDP reached 0.87% and the set target was 2.5%.[^cite_note-23]

## Prominent libraries in Iran

Large libraries existed in Iran both before and after the advent of Islam and throughout many periods in Iran's history, including the libraries at [Gondeshapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondeshapur), [School of Nisibis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_of_Nisibis), and [Sarouyeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarouyeh) during the pre-Islamic era of Iran.

During the Middle Ages, many schools of [Nizamiyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizamiyya) harbored large collections of manuscripts and treatises. In [Maragheh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maragheh), [Nasīr al-Dīn al-Tūsī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nas%C4%ABr_al-D%C4%ABn_al-T%C5%ABs%C4%AB) built a library that reportedly contained some 40,000 volumes which was well financed[^cite_note-27] and the royal library of the [Samanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samanid) court in which [Avicenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avicenna) was granted special access to, is yet another fine example.[^cite_note-28]

The first prototype of a modern national library in Iran was the Library of [Dar al-Funun College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar_ul-Funun_\(Persia\)) established in 1851. In 1899 another library called the Nation's Library was inaugurated in [Tehran](57654.md). Finally, the [National Library of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Library_of_Iran) was inaugurated in 1937.

Iran's major national libraries today are:

## Ideology and politics in higher education

See also: [Government of Iran](50913538.md)

Under the rule of the Islamic [theocracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theocracy) in [Iran](14653.md) since 1979 revolution, the status of science and education has been dramatically affected in the country. In particular, following the so-called [Iranian Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Cultural_Revolution) and Islamization of universities after a shutdown period,[^cite_note-parsi12-16] the quality of science and technology has since been revived, so much so that Iran ranked 40th in science production and first in scientific growth in the world in 2011.[^cite_note-31]

### Exclusion of students

#### Religious

See also: [Persecution of Baháʼís](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%ADs)

Students of unrecognized minority religions have been barred from entering tertiary education institutions in Iran, particularly those of the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith).[^cite_note-32] Since the Iranian Revolution of 1979 Baháʼí students have been excluded from universities regardless of their national university examination results on basis of their religion. See [Baháʼí Institute for Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Institute_for_Higher_Education).

#### Gender-based

See also: [Iranian restrictions on women's education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_restrictions_on_women's_education)

Additionally, in August 2012, 36 universities declared that 77 BA and BSc courses in 2012–2012 academic year would be "single gender" and effectively exclusive to men, limiting the options of female undergraduate students.[^cite_note-tt2012-33]

## Brain drain and students abroad

Main articles: [Iran's brain drain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran's_brain_drain), [Iranian citizens abroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_citizens_abroad), [Demography of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demography_of_Iran), and [Economy of Iran](64704.md)

Iran tops the world countries in the brain drain phenomenon. In 2002, the CIA estimated that 77% of Iran's population aged 15 and over can read and write.[^cite_note-35] By 2008, the adult literacy rate had reached 80.6%.[^cite_note-36] A significant majority of this population is at or approaching collegiate levels. Of this population, nearly 150,000 are estimated to exit Iran every year.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

According to the Iranian government, in 2013, 12,000 foreign students were studying at Iranian universities while 55,686 Iranian students were studying abroad.[^cite_note-39] Out of this number, 8,883 students were studying in Malaysia, 7,341 in the United States, 5,638 in Canada, 3,504 in Germany, 3,364 in Turkey, 3,228 in Britain, and the rest in other countries.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] But according to a newer estimate by the Minister of education, between 350 and 500 thousand Iranians were studying outside of the country in 2014.[^cite_note-42] The difference remains unexplained. As of 2015, 42% of recent/young graduates were unemployed. As of 2020, 40,000 foreign nationals are studying in 43 Iranian universities, 22,000 of whom are studying at the universities affiliated to the Ministry of Science, 8,000 at the Azad University, 3,000 at the Ministry of Health, and the rest at other universities. Some 50 percent of these students are studying for a bachelor's degree and 16 percent of whom are educating to receive a Ph.D.[^cite_note-tehrantimes.com-43]
