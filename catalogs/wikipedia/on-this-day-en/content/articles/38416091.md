The **2026 Winter Olympics** ([Italian](14708.md): *Olimpiadi invernali del 2026*), officially the **XXV Olympic Winter Games** and commonly known as **Milano Cortina 2026**, were an international winter [multi-sport event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-sport_event) held from 6 to 22 February 2026, at multiple sites across [Lombardy](43807.md), [Veneto](43780.md) and [Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol](246766.md), with competition in selected events beginning on 4 February 2026. Featuring the debut of [ski mountaineering](424477.md) as a [Winter Olympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_Olympic) event,[^cite_note-10] Milano Cortina 2026 was the first [Olympic Games](22576.md) under the [IOC presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_International_Olympic_Committee) of [Kirsty Coventry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirsty_Coventry).[^cite_note-11]

A joint bid by [Milan](36511.md) and [Cortina d'Ampezzo](568829.md) was awarded on 24 June 2019, at the [134th IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/134th_IOC_Session) in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne), Switzerland, in preference to another joint bid made by [Stockholm](26741.md) and [Åre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%85re), Sweden. The 2026 Olympic Games were the first to be officially co-hosted by two cities.[^cite_note-12] Milan primarily hosted the ice events, while the remaining events were hosted in clusters around Cortina, [Livigno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livigno), and [Fiemme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiemme_Valley). These were the third Winter Olympics, and the fourth Olympics overall, to be hosted by Italy, as Cortina d'Ampezzo had previously hosted the [1956 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Winter_Olympics) and [Torino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin) hosted the [2006 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Winter_Olympics).[^cite_note-13]

[Norway](79531438.md) finished at the top of the [medal table](82241843.md) for the fourth successive Winter Olympics with 18 gold and 41 medals overall, setting a new record for the largest number of gold and total medals won at a single Winter Olympics. The [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) finished second with 12 gold and 33 medals overall, making this the team's most successful Winter Olympics in terms of gold medals. The [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) finished in a tie for third with 10 gold and 20 medals overall, also marking this the team's most successful Winter Olympics in terms of gold medals. The host nation [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) finished in a tie for third with 10 gold, but finished fourth due to having fewer silvers and 30 medals overall, setting a new record for number of medals won. [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) won the first medal, already a gold medal, in their Winter Olympic history; also the first [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_nations_at_the_Winter_Olympics), [Latin American](18524.md), and [South American](26769.md) National Olympic Committee to win a medal at the Winter Olympics.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] [Georgia](79463437.md) also won the first medal in their Winter Olympic history.[^cite_note-17] Despite early concerns and controversies relating to logistics, the Games were considered a success by the press and observers upon their conclusion.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### Host city selection

Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo were selected as the host cities on 24 June 2019, at the [134th IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/134th_IOC_Session) in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne), Switzerland. The three Italian International Olympic Committee (IOC) members ([Franco Carraro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Carraro), [Ivo Ferriani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivo_Ferriani), and [Giovanni Malagò](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Malag%C3%B2)) and two Swedish IOC members ([Gunilla Lindberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunilla_Lindberg) and [Stefan Holm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Holm)) were ineligible to vote as stated in the [Olympic Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Charter).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

[Stadio San Siro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Siro) in [Milan](36511.md) (top) and [Verona Arena](703367.md) (bottom) are the venues of opening and closing ceremonies, respectively.

The Games primarily utilised existing venues across [Lombardy](43807.md), [Veneto](43780.md) and [Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol](246766.md), including those used by the [1956 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Winter_Olympics) previously held in Cortina d'Ampezzo, and by the [2013 Winter Universiade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Winter_Universiade) hosted by the province of [Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino). Most ice events, aside from [curling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curling_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics), were held in the Milan cluster, while sliding and snow events were held in clusters around [Cortina](568829.md), [Valtellina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valtellina), and the [Fiemme Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiemme_Valley). A new 12,000-seat multi-use arena designed by [David Chipperfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Chipperfield) was constructed in Milan's [Santa Giulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milano_Santa_Giulia) district and hosted ice hockey.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] [Stadio San Siro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_San_Siro) in Milan hosted the opening ceremony, and the historic [Verona Arena](703367.md) in Verona hosted the closing ceremony.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] Athletes stayed in several [Olympic villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_village), depending on the location of their sport: [Milan Olympic Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Olympic_Village), [Cortina d'Ampezzo Olympic Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortina_Airport), or in hotels.[^cite_note-28]

### Speed skating venue selection

During the bidding process, the bidding committee proposed that the speed skating events could be held at the existing [Ice Rink Piné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Rink_Pin%C3%A9) in [Baselga di Piné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baselga_di_Pin%C3%A9). Despite the infrastructure being ready, it required a roof, which impact and cost studies indicated would be costly, potentially exceeding the budget. The committee deliberated three choices: building an ice rink in the pavilions of [Fiera Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiera_Milano) (with a possibility to be permanent or temporary), options that would require significant structural work, or moving the events to the [Oval Lingotto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oval_Lingotto) in the city of [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), which required no structural changes.[^cite_note-Ridley-29]

The Turin venue was constructed to host speed skating during the [2006 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Winter_Olympics); after the Games, it hosted events such as exhibitions, fairs, and conferences. The venue hosted the same sport in the [2007 Winter Universiade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Winter_Universiade), but it has remained iceless since. In April 2023, it was estimated that the temporary ice rink in Fiera Milano would cost nearly €20 million, which would be paid for with private funds. The proposal to use Turin's Oval Lingotto received opposition from Milan-area officials, as Turin was part of the initial stages of the project but later withdrew. One of the spokespersons to reject this proposal was [Giuseppe Sala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Sala_\(politician\)) (the mayor of Milan) and officials from the host regions of Lombardy and Veneto.[^cite_note-30] Fiera Milano was confirmed as the speed skating venue in April 2023.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-Ridley-29]

### Olympic torch

See also: [2026 Winter Olympics torch relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Winter_Olympics_torch_relay)

The [Olympic torch relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_torch_relay) started on 26 November 2025, with the flame lighting in [Olympia, Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia,_Greece), and concluded on 6 February 2026, in Milan, Italy, coinciding with the opening ceremony at [Stadio San Siro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_San_Siro). Along with the 13 regional units and seven regions in Greece, the flame visited the 110 [provinces of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Italy), making 60 stops over 63 days across 12,000 kilometres (7,500 mi).[^cite_note-unveils-32] The Olympic torch was unveiled on 14 April 2025, in parallel events held in Milan and [Osaka](52230.md), Japan.[^cite_note-france24_20250414-33] It was simultaneously revealed at the [Triennale di Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triennale_di_Milano) and at the Italian pavilion at [Expo 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2025) as a way of connecting the two [sister cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_cities), as Milan had previously hosted [Expo 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2015).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] The Olympic torch is light blue, whereas the corresponding [Paralympic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralympic) torch is bronze coloured.[^cite_note-france24_20250414-33] Named "Essential"; developed by [Eni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eni) and its subsidiary [Versalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versalis), designed by Studio [Carlo Ratti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Ratti) Associati and produced in Italy by Cavagna Group, the torches are made primarily of an alloy of recycled aluminium and bronze. They run bio-LPG, a fuel made from renewable materials, produced at the [Enilive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enilive?action=edit\&redlink=1) biorefinery in [Gela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gela), and have been designed to be refilled up to ten times in order to cut down on the number of torches produced.[^cite_note-37]

Following the lighting of the [Olympic flame](74217.md) in Olympia, there was a low-key handover ceremony in [Athens](1216.md) on 4 December 2025.[^cite_note-38] The flame then arrived in [Rome](25458.md) to visit all 110 provinces of Italy, involving 10,001 torchbearers.[^cite_note-unveils-32][^cite_note-39] The torch was in [Naples](55880.md) for [Christmas](6237.md), in [Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari) for [New Year's Eve](277922.md), and in Cortina d'Ampezzo on 26 January to commemorate the 70th anniversary of it hosting the [1956 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Winter_Olympics).[^cite_note-unveils-32] While the torch was in [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont), a tribute was planned for skier [Matilde Lorenzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilde_Lorenzi), who died while training in October 2024.[^cite_note-40] The torch relay visited every [UNESCO World Heritage Site in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_World_Heritage_Site_in_Italy).[^cite_note-41] On 29 November 2024, Italian comedy trio [Gli Autogol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gli_Autogol?action=edit\&redlink=1) were announced as official narrators for the torch relay.[^cite_note-42]

### Medals



&#x20;The 2026 Winter Olympics gold medal

On 15 July 2025, the official medals of the Games were unveiled in [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), designed as two halves that symbolise the culmination of an athlete and Para athlete's journey and of all those who have walked beside them along the way; it was created by the [Istituto Poligrafico e Zecca dello Stato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Poligrafico_e_Zecca_dello_Stato) (IPZS).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] The medals featured a design that placed emotion and teamwork at its core. They had the traditional Olympic five-ring symbol on one side, with an inscription on the reverse that detailed the event and commemorated the venue.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] The medals for several athletes broke after they received them.[^cite_note-q800-47]

## The Games

### Opening ceremony

Main article: [2026 Winter Olympics opening ceremony](75430691.md)



A scene from the opening ceremony; the unveiling of the [Olympic rings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_rings)



The [Olympic cauldron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_cauldron) in Milan

The opening ceremony was held on 6 February 2026, at Stadio San Siro in Milan, titled *Armonia* ("Harmony").[^cite_note-48] The ceremony was produced by [Banijay Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banijay_Entertainment) (Balich Wonder Studio). Marco Balich, Creative Lead of the Opening Ceremony, explained that the word harmony derives from [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek): "It means 'bringing together' in musical terms, different elements."[^cite_note-49] The opening ceremony also featured special performances from American singer [Mariah Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariah_Carey),[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] as well as Italian artists [Laura Pausini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Pausini) and [Andrea Bocelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Bocelli).[^cite_note-53] Two [Olympic cauldrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_cauldron) were lit in both Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo, a first in Games' history.[^cite_note-54]

### Sports

The 2026 Winter Olympics featured 116 medal events in 16 disciplines, an increase of seven events and one discipline over [Beijing 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Olympics). New medal events included [ski mountaineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_mountaineering_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics), men's and women's dual [moguls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mogul_skiing) in [freestyle skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freestyle_skiing_at_the_Winter_Olympics), the return of the men's and women's doubles in [luge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luge_at_the_Winter_Olympics) (replacing the open doubles event), men's and women's team [combined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_skiing_combined) events in [alpine skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_skiing_at_the_Winter_Olympics), women's large hill individual in [ski jumping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_jumping_at_the_Winter_Olympics), and a mixed relay team event in [skeleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skeleton_at_the_Winter_Olympics). The Games had the highest percentage of women's participation in Winter Olympic history until that point ([Nordic combined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_combined_at_the_Winter_Olympics) remained the only Winter Olympic sport in which only men compete), at 47%.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-\:3-56][^cite_note-Inside_the_Games-57]

The alpine mixed team parallel event was dropped, while team ski jumping, [alpine combined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_skiing_combined), and Nordic combined all switched to being pairs events.[^cite_note-Inside_the_Games-57] Team combined replaced the individual alpine combined due to evolving technical and training requirements that had diminished its popularity; it was dropped from the [FIS Alpine Ski World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup) circuit in 2020, and contested at the Olympics for the final time in 2022.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] Nordic combined switched to a large hill/2×7.5 km (4.7 mi) course,[^cite_note-Inside_the_Games-57] while women raced the same distances as men in [cross-country skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-country_skiing_at_the_Winter_Olympics).[^cite_note-Inside_the_Games-57]

At the 138th IOC Session on 20 July 2021, the IOC approved a proposal by the Fondazione Milano Cortina 2026 to add [ski mountaineering](424477.md) as a debuting optional sport.[^cite_note-61] It consisted of three medal events: men's sprint, women's sprint, and mixed relay.[^cite_note-\:3-56]

In February 2024, the [International Ice Hockey Federation](16760449.md) (IIHF) reached an agreement with the [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL) to allow the league's professional players to participate in the men's hockey tournament for the first time since [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Winter_Olympics). NHL players were originally scheduled to return to Olympic hockey in 2022, but the league and its [players' union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hockey_League_Players'_Association) opted out due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-62]

### Closing ceremony

Main article: [2026 Winter Olympics closing ceremony](76159922.md)

The closing ceremony was held at the [Verona Arena](703367.md) in Verona on 22 February 2026, entitled "Beauty in Action".[^cite_note-63] Along with cultural segments, the ceremony featured closing remarks and the formal handover to the [French Alps](961317.md) as the host region of the [2030 Winter Olympics](57658858.md).[^cite_note-64] The first name confirmed to perform at the closing ceremony was world-renowned ballet dancer [Roberto Bolle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Bolle), the principal dancer at [La Scala Theatre Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Scala_Theatre_Ballet). He and director Alfredo Accatino had previously worked together on the [2006 Winter Olympics opening ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Winter_Olympics_opening_ceremony). Some sequences of the ceremony were held at the [Teatro Filarmonico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Filarmonico).[^cite_note-65]

## Participating National Olympic Committees

A total of 92 [National Olympic Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Olympic_Committee) (NOCs) had qualified athletes. The [Russian](25045856.md) and [Belarusian](1927547.md) NOCs remained suspended for violating the [Olympic Truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Truce) due to the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine). As with the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) in Paris, athletes from Russia and Belarus could compete at the 2026 Games as [independent Olympians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Olympians_at_the_Olympic_Games) without national identification, under the banner of "[Individual Neutral Athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_Neutral_Athletes_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics)" (AIN).[^cite_note-ReutersMay2025-66][^cite_note-67] The individual neutral athletes had to be approved by their sport's [international federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_federation), and then by an IOC panel.[^cite_note-themoscowtimes-68]

As individual athletes, AIN were not considered a delegation during the opening ceremony or in the medal tables.[^cite_note-69] They were also not allowed to compete in team events like [ice hockey](75053831.md) and [curling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curling_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics).[^cite_note-ReutersMay2025-66] International federations that were allowing Russian and Belarusian athletes to compete under the AIN banner included the [International Skating Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Skating_Union) (ISU) for the various skating events,[^cite_note-themoscowtimes-68] as well as the [International Ski Mountaineering Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Ski_Mountaineering_Federation) (ISMF) for the [ski mountaineering events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ski_mountaineering_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics).[^cite_note-70] In all, 20 athletes (13 from Russia and seven from Belarus) competed at the Games.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] [Benin](3459.md), [Guinea-Bissau](12186.md), and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) (UAE) all made their Winter Olympic debuts.[^cite_note-73]

## Marketing

Main article: [2026 Winter Olympics marketing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Winter_Olympics_marketing)

The overall branding of the 2026 Winter Olympics was influenced by [Italian gesticulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesticulation_in_Italian), "harmony", and the "new Italian Spirit"; one of the main visual elements were "Vibes"—abstract designs consisting of curved lines intended to convey the themes of "creativity", "energy", "imagination", "passion", and "style".[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-\:0-91] Five celebrity ambassadors were chosen to respectively symbolise the themes in promotion for the Games, including musician [Dardust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dario_Faini), Paralympic fencer [Bebe Vio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatrice_Vio), comedian [Federico Basso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Basso?action=edit\&redlink=1), chef [Davide Oldani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Oldani?action=edit\&redlink=1), and dancer [Nicoletta Manni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicoletta_Manni).[^cite_note-92] The event [pictograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pictogram) were similarly inspired by [gesture drawing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesture_drawing).[^cite_note-\:0-91]

For the first time, an Olympic emblem was determined via a public vote: two designs by [Landor Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landor_Associates) were unveiled on 6 March 2021, during the [Sanremo Music Festival final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanremo_Music_Festival_2021), with the winning design—"Futura"—announced on 30 March 2021.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] The emblem consists of a stylised "26" written in a single stroke, representing the impact of "small gestures" and "sport, solidarity and sustainability".[^cite_note-95] During the [2022 Sanremo Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Sanremo_Music_Festival) finals on 5 February, the two final candidates for the official anthem of the event were presented, with a poll opening afterward. On 7 March 2022, "Fino all'alba" ("Until the dawn")—composed by the youth music group La Cittadina of the San Pietro Martire in [Seveso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seveso), and performed during Sanremo by [Arisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arisa)—was announced as the winner.[^cite_note-96]

The Games' official slogan, "IT's Your Vibe", was announced on 23 February 2025. It used "IT" as both an abbreviation for Italy and as the [contraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_auxiliary_verbs#Unstressed_and_contracted_forms) "It's", with variants of the slogan used in other contexts to reflect upon the Games and its host country.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] [Official art posters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_poster) were developed in collaboration with the [Triennale Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triennale_di_Milano) exhibition,[^cite_note-triennale-99][^cite_note-designboom-100] including 10 works commissioned from emerging Italian artists (five representing the Olympics and five representing the Paralympics),[^cite_note-olympicsart-101] and two "iconic" posters for the Olympics and Paralympics by Olimpia Zagnoli and Carolina Altavill, respectively.[^cite_note-ap-102][^cite_note-creativebloq-103][^cite_note-triennale-99]

### Mascot

Main article: [Tina and Milo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_and_Milo)



Tina and Milo

An online vote closing on 28 February 2023, was held to select the two mascots for the event. The winning candidates, designed by the students of a school in [Taverna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taverna,_Calabria) and inspired by [stoats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoat),[^cite_note-104] were presented during the second night of the [2024 Sanremo Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Sanremo_Music_Festival) on 7 February.[^cite_note-105] Their names were revealed to be Tina and Milo (derived from the names of the host cities) and are portrayed as sister and brother.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-mascots-107][^cite_note-\:mascots2-108]

The choice of stoats was explained as being due to these animals' embodiment of "the contemporary Italian spirit" of curiosity, ability to change according to the seasons, and capacity of adaptation to challenging habitats.[^cite_note-mascots-107][^cite_note-\:mascots2-108] The two main mascots were accompanied by six [snowdrop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowdrop) flowers, called "The Flo".[^cite_note-109]

Milo, a brown stoat, and Tina, a white stoat, were brother and sister "born in the mountains of Italy", who "decided to move to the city". Tina, the main Olympic mascot, symbolised art, music, and the transformative force of beauty. Milo, the Paralympic mascot, was born without a leg but used his tail to help him lead a normal life with some ingenuity, willpower, and creativity.[^cite_note-mascots-107]

## Broadcasting

Main article: [List of 2026 Winter Olympics broadcasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2026_Winter_Olympics_broadcasters)



[RAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAI)'s Winter Olympics studio at [Porta Sempione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Sempione).

[Olympic Broadcasting Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Broadcasting_Services) (OBS) produced over 6,500 hours of content during the Games, ranging from unilateral broadcast feeds to other forms of video content usable by rightsholders.[^cite_note-\:1-110][^cite_note-\:2-111]

In Europe, the 2026 Winter Olympics were the first covered by a renewal of [Warner Bros. Discovery](67824355.md)'s pan-European rights to the Olympics through 2032, encompassing rights across digital and pay television in 49 European territories. As part of the agreement, WBD sublicensed a package of free-to-air rights to the [European Broadcasting Union](10380.md) (EBU) and its members—including Italy's state-owned broadcaster [RAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAI)[^cite_note-RAI-112]—covering at least 100 hours of coverage of each Winter Olympics.[^cite_note-Roxborough-113] This was in contrast to the previous contract, where corporate predecessor [Discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discovery_Inc.) was responsible for selling the free-to-air rights.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

After being used for the first time at the [2024 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) in mountain biking, OBS expanded its use of [FPV drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FPV_drone) for new camera angles during skiing and sliding events. [Omnidirectional cameras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnidirectional_camera) were also used to enable stroboscopic "freeze-frame" and multi-angle [instant replays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instant_replay).[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] OBS expanded its use of [artificial intelligence](1164.md) (AI) to transcribe and tag scenes from events using [computer vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_vision) and semantic data, as well as the ability to automatically curate tailored highlights for media distribution.[^cite_note-\:1-110][^cite_note-\:2-111] Rai and the EBU conducted trials of [5G Broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5G_Broadcast) during the Games.[^cite_note-118]

## Concerns and controversies

Main article: [Concerns and controversies at the 2026 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concerns_and_controversies_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics)

The Olympics were accompanied by various controversies related to [corruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_Italy) among Italian organisers, which included scandals involving host personnel and delays in building venues;[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121] and the participation of athletes from Iran, Israel, and Russia amidst ongoing military conflicts.[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] During the Games, other incidents included restrictions on athlete expression by the IOC,[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127] along with allegations of cheating in some sports.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]
