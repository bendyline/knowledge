# Eastern Catholic Churches

This article is about Eastern Churches in full communion within the Catholic Church. For the Eastern Christian churches as a whole, see [Eastern Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity).

Not to be confused with [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md) or [Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East).

The **Eastern Catholic Churches** or **Oriental Catholic Churches**, also known as the **Eastern-Rite Catholic Churches**, **Eastern Rite Catholicism**, or simply the **Eastern Churches**,[^cite_note-2] are 23 [Eastern Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christian) autonomous (*[sui iuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_iuris)*) [particular churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular_church) of the [Catholic Church](606848.md) in [full communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_communion) with the [pope](23056.md) in [Rome](13393.md). Although they are distinct theologically, liturgically, and historically from the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church), they are all in full communion with it and with each other. Eastern Catholics are a minority within the Catholic Church; of the 1.3 billion Catholics in communion with the pope, approximately 18 million are members of the Eastern churches. The largest numbers of Eastern Catholics are found in [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe), [Eastern Africa](260987.md), the [Middle East](19323.md), and [India](14533.md). As of 2022, the [Syro-Malabar Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syro-Malabar_Church) is the largest Eastern Catholic church, followed by the [Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church).[^cite_note-\:0-3]

With the exception of the [Maronite Church](66391078.md), the Eastern Catholic Churches are groups that, at different points in the past, used to belong to the [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md), the [Oriental Orthodox Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches), or the [Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East); these churches underwent various [schisms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schism_in_Christianity) through history. Eastern Catholic churches that were formerly part of other communions have been points of controversy in [ecumenical relations with the Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic%E2%80%93Eastern_Orthodox_relations) and other non-Catholic churches. The five historic [liturgical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgical) traditions of Eastern Christianity, namely the [Alexandrian Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandrian_Rite), the [Armenian Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Rite), the [Byzantine Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Rite), the [East Syriac Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Syriac_Rite), and the [West Syriac Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Syriac_Rite), are all represented within [Eastern Catholic liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_liturgy).[^cite_note-Yurkus-4] On occasion, this leads to a conflation of the liturgical word "rite" and the institutional word "church".[^cite_note-LaBanca-5] Some Eastern Catholic jurisdictions admit members of churches not in communion with Rome to the [Eucharist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucharist_in_the_Catholic_Church) and the other sacraments.[^cite_note-7]

Full communion with the bishop of Rome constitutes mutual sacramental sharing between the Eastern Catholic Churches and the Latin Church and the recognition and acceptance of [papal supremacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_supremacy) and [infallibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_infallibility).[^cite_note-\:1-8][^cite_note-9] Provisions within the [1983 Latin canon law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Code_of_Canon_Law) and the 1990 *[Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches](3547385.md)* govern the relationship between the Eastern and Latin churches. Historically, pressure to conform to the norms of the [Western Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Christianity) practiced by the majority Latin Church led to a degree of encroachment ([Latinization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgical_Latinization)) on some of the Eastern Catholic traditions. The [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) document, *[Orientalium Ecclesiarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientalium_Ecclesiarum)*, built on previous reforms to reaffirm the right of Eastern Catholics to maintain their distinct practices.[^cite_note-10]

The 1990 *Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches* was the first codified body of [canon law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_law_of_the_Catholic_Church) governing the Eastern Catholic Churches collectively,[^cite_note-11] although each church also has its own internal [canons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_\(canon_law\)) and laws on top of this. Members of Eastern Catholic churches are obliged to follow the norms of their particular church regarding celebration of church feasts, marriage, and other customs. Notable distinct norms include many of the Eastern Catholic Churches regularly allowing the [ordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_orders_in_the_Catholic_Church) of married men to the [priesthood](8655005.md) (although not as bishops to the [episcopacy](8799424.md)), in contrast to the stricter [clerical celibacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clerical_celibacy_in_the_Catholic_Church) of the Latin Church. Both Latin and Eastern Catholics may freely attend a Catholic liturgy celebrated in any rite.[^cite_note-12]

## Terminology

See also: [Catholic (term)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_\(term\)) and [Roman Catholic (term)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_\(term\))

Although Eastern Catholics are in [full communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_communion) with the [pope](23056.md) and members of the worldwide [Catholic Church](606848.md),[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-16] they are not members of the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church), which uses the [Latin liturgical rites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_liturgical_rites), among which the [Roman Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Rite) is the most widespread.[^cite_note-18] The Eastern Catholic churches are instead distinct [particular churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular_church) *[sui iuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_iuris)* (autonomous), although they maintain full and equal, mutual sacramental exchange with members of the Latin Church.

### *Rite* or *church*

There are different meanings of the word *[rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rite_\(disambiguation\))*. Apart from its reference to the liturgical patrimony of a [particular church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular_church), the word has been and is still sometimes, even if rarely, officially used of the particular church itself. Thus the term *Latin rite* can refer either to the Latin Church or to one or more of the [Latin liturgical rites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_liturgical_rites), which include the [Roman Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Rite), [Ambrosian Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambrosian_Rite), [Mozarabic Rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozarabic_Rite), and others.

&#x20;In the 1990 *[Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches](3547385.md)* (CCEO),[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] the terms *autonomous Church* and *rite* are thus defined:

> A group of Christian faithful linked in accordance with the law by a hierarchy and expressly or tacitly recognized by the supreme authority of the Church as autonomous is in this Code called an autonomous Church (canon 27).[^cite_note-CCEO-27-21]
>
> 1. A rite is the liturgical, theological, spiritual and disciplinary patrimony, culture and circumstances of history of a distinct people, by which its own manner of living the faith is manifested in each autonomous \[*_sui iuris_*] Church.
>
> 2. The rites treated in CCEO, unless otherwise stated, are those that arise from the Alexandrian, Antiochene, Armenian, Chaldean and Constantinopolitan traditions" (canon 28)
>
>    [^cite_note-intratext-22]
>
>    &#x20;(not just a liturgical heritage, but also a theological, spiritual and disciplinary heritage characteristic of peoples' culture and the circumstances of their history).

When speaking of Eastern Catholic Churches, the Latin Church's [1983 Code of Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Code_of_Canon_Law) (1983 CIC) uses the terms "ritual Church" or "ritual Church *_sui iuris_*" (canons 111 and 112), and also speaks of "a subject of an Eastern rite" (canon 1015 §2), "Ordinaries of another rite" (canon 450 §1), "the faithful of a specific rite" (canon 476), etc. The Second Vatican Council spoke of Eastern Catholic Churches as "particular Churches or rites".[^cite_note-OE-23]<sup>: n. 2 </sup>

In 1999, the [United States Conference of Catholic Bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Conference_of_Catholic_Bishops) stated: "We have been accustomed to speaking of the Latin (Roman or Western) Rite or the Eastern Rites to designate these different Churches. However, the Church's contemporary legislation as contained in the *Code of Canon Law* and the *Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches* makes it clear that we ought to speak, not of rites, but of Churches. Canon 112 of the Code of Canon Law uses the phrase 'autonomous ritual Churches' to designate the various Churches."[^cite_note-24] And a writer in a periodical of January 2006 declared: "The Eastern Churches are still mistakenly called 'Eastern-Rite' Churches, a reference to their various liturgical histories. They are most properly called Eastern Churches, or Eastern Catholic Churches."[^cite_note-Zagano2006-25] However, the term "rite" continues to be used. The 1983 CIC forbids a Latin bishop to ordain, without permission of the Holy See, a subject of his who is "of an Eastern [rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular_Church#Autonomous_particular_Churches_or_Rites)" (not "who uses an Eastern [rite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgy)", the faculty for which is sometimes granted to Latin clergy).[^cite_note-26]

### *Uniate*

Further information: [Ruthenian Uniate Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenian_Uniate_Church)

The term *Uniat* or *Uniate* has been applied to Eastern Catholic churches and individual members whose church hierarchies were previously part of Eastern Orthodox or Oriental Orthodox churches. The term is sometimes considered derogatory by such people,[^cite_note-OCA_Uniate-27][^cite_note-CNEWA2011-28] though it was used by some Latin and Eastern Catholics prior to the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) of 1962–1965.[^cite_note-31] Official Catholic documents no longer use the term due to its perceived negative overtones.[^cite_note-32]

## History

### Background

Eastern Catholic Churches have their origins in the [Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_the_Middle_East), [North Africa](21714.md), [East Africa](260987.md), [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe) and [South India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_India). However, since the 19th century, [diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaspora) has spread to [Western Europe](33800.md), the [Americas](29833.md) and [Oceania](22621.md) in part because of [persecution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persecution_of_Christians), where [eparchies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eparchy) have been established to serve adherents alongside those of Latin Church [dioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese). [Latin Catholics in the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church_in_the_Middle_East), on the other hand, are traditionally cared for by the [Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem](231524.md).[^cite_note-33]

The communion between Christian churches has been broken over matters of faith, whereby each side accused the other of [heresy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heresy) or departure from the true faith ([orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodoxy)). Communion has been broken also because of disagreement about questions of authority or the legitimacy of the election of a particular bishop. In these latter cases, each side accused the other of [schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schism_\(religion\)), but not of heresy.

The following ecumenical councils are major breaches of communion:

#### Council of Ephesus (AD 431)

In 431, the churches that accepted the teaching of the [Council of Ephesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ephesus) (which condemned the views of [Nestorius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nestorius)) were classified as heretics by those who rejected the council's statements. The [Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East), which was mainly under the [Sassanid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid_Empire), never accepted the council's views. It later experienced a period of great expansion in Asia before collapsing after the [Mongol invasion of the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquests) in the 14th century.

Monuments of their presence still exist in China. Now they are relatively few in number and have divided into three churches: the Chaldean Catholic Church—an Eastern Catholic church in full communion with Rome—and two Assyrian churches which are not in communion with either Rome or each other. The [Chaldean Catholic Church](698821.md) is the largest of the three. The groups of Assyrians who did not reunify with Rome remained and are known as the [Assyrian Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_Church_of_the_East), which experienced an internal schism in 1968 which led to the creation of the [Ancient Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Church_of_the_East).

The [Syro-Malabar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syro-Malabar_Church) and [Syro-Malankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syro-Malankara_Catholic_Church) churches are the two Eastern Catholic descendants of the Church of the East in the Indian subcontinent.

#### Council of Chalcedon (AD 451)

In 451, those who accepted the [Council of Chalcedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon) similarly classified those who rejected it as [Monophysite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monophysitism) heretics. The Churches that refused to accept the Council considered instead that it was they who were orthodox; they rejected the description *Monophysite* (meaning *only-nature*) preferring instead *[Miaphysite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miaphysitism)* (meaning *one-nature*). The difference in terms may appear subtle, but it is theologically very important. "Monophysite" implies a single divine nature alone with no real human nature—a heretical belief according to [Chalcedonian Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcedonian_Christianity)—whereas "Miaphysite" can be understood to mean one nature as God, existing in the person of Jesus who is both human and divine—an idea more easily reconciled to Chalcedonian doctrine. They are often called, in English, [Oriental Orthodox Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches), to distinguish them from the [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md).

This distinction, by which the words *oriental* and *eastern* that in themselves have exactly the same meaning but are used as labels to describe two different realities, is impossible to translate in most other languages, and is not universally accepted even in English. These churches are also referred to as *pre-Chalcedonian* or now more rarely as *non-Chalcedonian* or *anti-Chalcedonian*. In languages other than English other means are used to distinguish the two families of churches. Some reserve the term "Orthodox" for those that are here called "Eastern Orthodox" churches, but members of what is called "[Oriental Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox)" churches consider this illicit.

The [East–West Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism) came about in the context of [cultural differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_identity) between the Greek-speaking East and Latin-speaking West, and of rivalry between the churches in Rome—which claimed a primacy not merely of honour but also of authority—and in [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), which claimed parity with Rome.[^cite_note-34] The rivalry and lack of comprehension gave rise to controversies, some of which appear already in the acts of the [Quinisext Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinisext_Council) of 692. At the [Council of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Florence) (1431–1445), these controversies about Western theological elaborations and usages were identified as, chiefly, the insertion of "[Filioque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filioque)" into the [Nicene Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicene_Creed), the use of [unleavened bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flatbread) for the [Eucharist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucharist), [purgatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purgatory), and the authority of the pope.[^cite_note-36]

The schism is generally considered to have started in 1054, when the [Patriarch of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople), [Michael I Cerularius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_I_Cerularius), and the [Papal Legate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_legate), [Humbert of Silva Candida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humbert_of_Silva_Candida), issued mutual [excommunications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excommunication). (These excommunications would eventually be revoked by both Rome and Constantinople in 1965.) In spite of that event, for many years both churches continued to maintain friendly relations and seemed to be unaware of any formal or final rupture.[^cite_note-37]

However, estrangement continued. In 1190, Eastern Orthodox theologian [Theodore Balsamon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Balsamon), who was [patriarch of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarch_of_Antioch), wrote that "no [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) should be given Communion unless he first declares that he will abstain from the doctrines and customs that separate him from us".[^cite_note-38]

Later in 1204, [Constantinople was sacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Constantinople_\(1204\)) by the Catholic armies of the [Fourth Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Crusade), whereas two decades previously the [Massacre of the Latins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre_of_the_Latins) (i.e., Catholics) had occurred in Constantinople in 1182. Thus, by the 12th–13th centuries, the two sides had become openly hostile, each considering that the other no longer belonged to the church that was orthodox and catholic. Over time, it became customary to refer to the Eastern side as the [Greek Orthodox Church](10186.md) and the Western as the Catholic Church, without either side thereby renouncing its claim of being the truly orthodox or the truly catholic church.

#### Attempts at restoring communion



Major Archbishop [Sviatoslav Shevchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatoslav_Shevchuk) of the [Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church)

Parties within many non-Latin churches repeatedly sought to organize efforts to restore communion. In 1438, the [Council of Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Florence) convened, which featured a strong dialogue focused on understanding the theological differences between the East and West, with the hope of reuniting the Catholic and Orthodox churches.[^cite_note-39] Several eastern churches associated themselves with Rome, forming Eastern Catholic churches. The See of Rome accepted them without requiring that they adopt the customs of the Latin Church, so that they all have their own "liturgical, theological, spiritual and disciplinary heritage, differentiated by peoples' culture and historical circumstances, that finds expression in each *_sui iuris_* Church's own way of living the faith".[^cite_note-intratext-22]

### Emergence of the churches



[Monastery of Qozhaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monastery_of_Qozhaya) in [Kadisha Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadisha_Valley), [Lebanon](17771.md), the historical stronghold of the [Maronite Church](66391078.md)

Most Eastern Catholic churches arose when a group within an ancient church in disagreement with the See of Rome returned to full communion with that see. The following churches have been in communion with the Bishop of Rome for a large part of their history:

- &#x20;The [Maronite Church](66391078.md), which has no counterpart in [Byzantine](10186.md), nor [Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy), Orthodoxy. The Maronite Church has historical connections to the [Monothelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monothelite) controversy in the 7th century. It re-affirmed unity with the [Holy See](13393.md) in 1154 during [the Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Crusades).

  [^cite_note-Attwater-40]

  - The Maronite Church has historically been treated as never having fully schismed with the Holy See, despite a dispute over [Christological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christology) doctrine that concluded in 1154; most of the other Eastern Catholic churches came into being from the 16th century onwards.

    [^cite_note-Attwater-40]

    : 165–167&#x20;

    &#x20;However, the [Melkite Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Church), the [Syro-Malabar Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syro-Malabar_Church) and the [Italo-Albanian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Albanian_Catholic_Church) also claim perpetual communion.

- The [Albanian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Greek_Catholic_Church) and Italo-Albanian Catholic Church, which, unlike the Maronite Church, use the same liturgical rite as the [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md).

- The former [Melkite Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite) considered itself in dual communion with Rome and Constantinople until [an exclusively Orthodox body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite#Orthodox_Melkites) was formed in the 18th century, leaving a remainder unified exclusively with Rome as the [Melkite Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Church).

- The Oriental Orthodox [Armenian Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church) had included a long-standing minority that accepted Roman primacy until the [Armenian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church) was officially established in the 18th century.

The [canon law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_law) shared by all Eastern Catholic churches, CCEO, was codified in 1990. The [dicastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery) that works with the Eastern Catholic churches is the [Dicastery for the Eastern Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Eastern_Churches), which by law includes as members all Eastern Catholic patriarchs and major archbishops.

The largest six churches based on membership are, in order, the [Syro-Malabar Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syro-Malabar_Church) (East Syriac Rite), the [Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church) (UGCC; Byzantine Rite), the Maronite Church (West Syriac Rite), the Melkite Greek Catholic Church (Byzantine Rite), the [Chaldean Catholic Church](698821.md) (East Syriac Rite), and the [Armenian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church) (Armenian Rite).[^cite_note-cnewa17-41] These six churches account for about 85% of the membership of the Eastern Catholic Churches.[^cite_note-42]

### *Orientalium dignitas*



[Pope Leo XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII) issued the [apostolic constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_constitution) *[Orientalium dignitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientalium_dignitas)*. Photogram of the 1896 film *[Sua Santità papa Leone XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sua_Santit%C3%A0_papa_Leone_XIII)*, the first time a pope appeared on film.

On 30 November 1894, [Pope Leo XIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_XIII) issued the [apostolic constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_constitution) *[Orientalium dignitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientalium_dignitas)*, in which he stated:

> The Churches of the East are worthy of the glory and reverence that they hold throughout the whole of Christendom in virtue of those extremely ancient, singular memorials that they have bequeathed to us. For it was in that part of the world that the first actions for the redemption of the human race began, in accord with the all-kind plan of God. They swiftly gave forth their yield: there flowered in first blush the glories of preaching the True Faith to the nations, of martyrdom, and of holiness. They gave us the first joys of the fruits of salvation. From them has come a wondrously grand and powerful flood of benefits upon the other peoples of the world, no matter how far-flung. When blessed Peter, the Prince of the Apostles, intended to cast down the manifold wickedness of error and vice, in accord with the will of Heaven, he brought the light of divine Truth, the Gospel of peace, freedom in Christ to the metropolis of the Gentiles.[^cite_note-OrientaliumDignitas1894-43]

[Adrian Fortescue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Fortescue) wrote that Leo XIII "begins by explaining again that the ancient Eastern rites are a witness to the Apostolicity of the Catholic Church, that their diversity, consistent with unity of the faith, is itself a witness to the unity of the Church, that they add to her dignity and honour. He says that the Catholic Church does not possess one rite only, but that she embraces all the ancient rites of Christendom; her unity consists not in a mechanical uniformity of all her parts, but on the contrary, in their variety, according in one principle and vivified by it."[^cite_note-44]

Leo XIII declared still in force [Pope Benedict XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV)'s [encyclical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclical) *[Demandatam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demandatam)*, addressed to the Patriarch and the Bishops of the [Melkite Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Catholic_Church), in which Benedict XIV forbade Latin Church clergy to induce Melkite Catholics to transfer to the Roman Rite, and he broadened this prohibition to cover all Eastern Catholics, declaring: "Any Latin rite missionary, whether of the secular or religious clergy, who induces with his advice or assistance any Eastern rite faithful to transfer to the Latin rite, will be deposed and excluded from his benefice in addition to the *ipso facto* suspension *a divinis* and other punishments that he will incur as imposed in the aforesaid Constitution *Demandatam*."[^cite_note-OrientaliumDignitas1894-43]

### Second Vatican Council



[Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) presiding over the introductory ingress of the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council), flanked by [Camerlengo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camerlengo) [Benedetto Aloisi Masella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedetto_Aloisi_Masella) and two [Papal gentlemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_gentlemen)

There had been confusion on the part of Western clergy about the legitimate presence of Eastern Catholic Churches in countries seen as belonging to the West, despite firm and repeated papal confirmation of these churches' universal character. The [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) brought the reform impulse to visible fruition. Several documents, from both during and after the Second Vatican Council, have led to significant reform and development within Eastern Catholic Churches.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] The view was expressed by several Eastern Catholic participants at the Council that their presence "represented" the absent voice of the Eastern Orthodox Church.[^cite_note-47]

#### *Orientalium Ecclesiarum*

Main article: [Orientalium Ecclesiarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientalium_Ecclesiarum)



Bishops, including Eastern Catholic ones as seen in their distinctive vestments, assisting at the Second Vatican Council

The Second Vatican Council directed, in *[Orientalium Ecclesiarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientalium_Ecclesiarum)*, that the traditions of Eastern Catholic Churches should be maintained. It declared that "it is the mind of the Catholic Church that each individual Church or Rite should retain its traditions whole and entire and likewise that it should adapt its way of life to the different needs of time and place" (n. 2), and that they should all "preserve their legitimate liturgical rite and their established way of life, and ... these may not be altered except to obtain for themselves an organic improvement" (n. 6; cf. n. 22).[^cite_note-OE-23]

It confirmed and approved the ancient discipline of the sacraments existing in the Eastern churches, and the ritual practices connected with their celebration and administration, and declared its ardent desire that this should be re-established if circumstances warranted (n. 12). It applied this in particular to administration of sacrament of [Confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation) by priests (n. 13). It expressed the wish that, where the permanent [diaconate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deacon) (ordination as deacons of men who are not intending afterwards to become priests) had fallen into disuse, it should be restored (n. 17).

Paragraphs 7–11 are devoted to the powers of the patriarchs and major archbishops of the Eastern Churches, whose rights and privileges, it says, should be re-established in accordance with the ancient tradition of each of the churches and the decrees of the [ecumenical councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_council), adapted somewhat to modern conditions. Where there is a need, new patriarchates should be established either by an ecumenical council or by the Bishop of Rome.

#### *Lumen gentium*

The Second Vatican Council's Dogmatic Constitution on the Church, *[Lumen gentium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumen_gentium)*, deals with Eastern Catholic Churches in paragraph 23, stating:

> By [divine Providence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Providence) it has come about that various churches, established in various places by the apostles and their successors, have in the course of time coalesced into several groups, organically united, which, preserving the unity of faith and the unique divine constitution of the universal Church, enjoy their own discipline, their own liturgical usage, and their own theological and spiritual heritage. Some of these churches, notably the ancient patriarchal churches, as parent-stocks of the Faith, so to speak, have begotten others as daughter churches, with which they are connected down to our own time by a close bond of charity in their sacramental life and in their mutual respect for their rights and duties. This variety of local churches with one common aspiration is splendid evidence of the catholicity of the undivided Church. In like manner the Episcopal bodies of today are in a position to render a manifold and fruitful assistance, so that this collegiate feeling may be put into practical application.[^cite_note-48]

#### *Unitatis redintegratio*

The 1964 decree *[Unitatis redintegratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitatis_redintegratio)* deals with Eastern Catholic Churches in paragraphs 14–17.[^cite_note-UR-49]

### Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches

Main article: [Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches](3547385.md)

The First Vatican Council discussed the need for a common code for the Eastern churches, but no concrete action was taken. Only after the benefits of the Latin Church's [1917 Code of Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_Code_of_Canon_Law) were appreciated was a serious effort made to codify the Eastern Catholic Churches' canon laws.[^cite_note-Beal2000-50]<sup>: 27 </sup> This came to fruition with the promulgation of the 1990 *[Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches](3547385.md)*, which took effect in 1991. It is a framework document that contains canons that are a consequence of the *common* patrimony of the churches of the East: each individual *_sui iuris_* church also has its own canons, its own particular law, layered on top of this code.

### Joint International Commission

In 1993 the [Joint International Commission for Theological Dialogue Between the Catholic Church and the Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_International_Commission_for_Theological_Dialogue_Between_the_Catholic_Church_and_the_Orthodox_Church) submitted the document *Uniatism, method of union of the past, and the present search for full communion*, also known as the [Balamand declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balamand_declaration), "to the authorities of the Catholic and Orthodox Churches for approval and application,"[^cite_note-Balamand1993-51] which stated that initiatives that "led to the union of certain communities with the See of Rome and brought with them, as a consequence, the breaking of communion with their Mother Churches of the East ... took place not without the interference of extra-ecclesial interests".[^cite_note-Balamand1993-51]<sup>: n. 8 </sup>

Likewise the commission acknowledged that "certain civil authorities \[who] made attempts" to force Eastern Catholics to return to the Orthodox Church used "unacceptable means".[^cite_note-Balamand1993-51]<sup>: n. 11 </sup> The missionary outlook and [proselytism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proselytism) that accompanied the Unia[^cite_note-Balamand1993-51]<sup>: n. 10 </sup> was judged incompatible with the rediscovery by the Catholic and Orthodox churches of each other as sister churches.[^cite_note-Balamand1993-51]<sup>: n. 12 </sup> Thus the commission concluded that the "missionary apostolate, ... which has been called 'uniatism', can no longer be accepted either as a method to be followed or as a model of the unity our Churches are seeking."[^cite_note-Balamand1993-51]<sup>: n. 12 </sup>

At the same time, the commission stated:

- that Eastern Catholic Churches, being part of the Catholic Communion, have the right to exist and to act in response to the spiritual needs of their faithful;

  [^cite_note-Balamand1993-51]

  : n. 3&#x20;

- that Oriental Catholic Churches, which desired to re-establish full communion with the See of Rome and have remained faithful to it, have the rights and obligations connected with this communion.

  [^cite_note-Balamand1993-51]

  : n. 16&#x20;

These principles were repeated in the 2016 [Joint Declaration of Pope Francis and Patriarch Kirill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Declaration_of_Pope_Francis_and_Patriarch_Kirill), which stated that 'It is today clear that the past method of “uniatism”, understood as the union of one community to the other, separating it from its Church, is not the way to re-establish unity. Nonetheless, the ecclesial communities which emerged in these historical circumstances have the right to exist and to undertake all that is necessary to meet the spiritual needs of their faithful, while seeking to live in peace with their neighbours. Orthodox and Greek Catholics are in need of reconciliation and of mutually acceptable forms of co–existence.'[^cite_note-52]

### Liturgical prescriptions

Main article: [Eastern Catholic liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_liturgy)



Inside a [Syriac Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Catholic_Church) building in [Damascus](8914.md), the capital city of [Syria](7515849.md)

The 1996 *Instruction for Applying the Liturgical Prescriptions of the Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches* brought together, in one place, the developments that took place in previous texts,[^cite_note-Instruction1996-53] and is "an expository expansion based upon the canons, with constant emphasis upon the preservation of Eastern liturgical traditions and a return to those usages whenever possible—certainly in preference to the usages of the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church), however much some principles and norms of the [conciliar constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacrosanctum_Concilium) on the Roman rite, "in the very nature of things, affect other [rites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_liturgy) as well."[^cite_note-Beal2000-50]<sup>: 998 </sup> The *Instruction* states:

> The liturgical laws valid for all the Eastern Churches are important because they provide the general orientation. However, being distributed among various texts, they risk remaining ignored, poorly coordinated and poorly interpreted. It seemed opportune, therefore, to gather them in a systematic whole, completing them with further clarification: thus, the intent of the Instruction, presented to the Eastern Churches which are in full communion with the [Apostolic See](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_See), is to help them fully realize their own identity. The authoritative general directive of this Instruction, formulated to be implemented in Eastern celebrations and liturgical life, articulates itself in propositions of a juridical-pastoral nature, constantly taking initiative from a theological perspective.[^cite_note-Instruction1996-53]<sup>: n. 5 </sup>

Past interventions by the Holy See, the Instruction said, were in some ways defective and needed revision, but often served also as a safeguard against aggressive initiatives.

> These interventions felt the effects of the mentality and convictions of the times, according to which a certain subordination of the non-Latin liturgies was perceived toward the Latin-Rite liturgy which was considered "[ritus ](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ritus%20#Latin)[praestantior](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/praestantior#Latin)".[^cite_note-54] This attitude may have led to interventions in the Eastern liturgical texts which today, in light of theological studies and progress, have need of revision, in the sense of a return to ancestral traditions. The work of the commissions, nevertheless, availing themselves of the best experts of the times, succeeded in safeguarding a major part of the Eastern heritage, often defending it against aggressive initiatives and publishing precious editions of liturgical texts for numerous Eastern Churches. Today, particularly after the solemn declarations of the Apostolic Letter *[Orientalium dignitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orientalium_dignitas)* by Leo XIII, after the creation of the still active special Commission for the liturgy within the Congregation for the Eastern Churches in 1931, and above all after the Second Vatican Council and the Apostolic Letter *[Orientale Lumen](https://www.vatican.va/holy_father/john_paul_ii/apost_letters/documents/hf_jp-ii_apl_02051995_orientale-lumen_en.html)* by John Paul II, respect for the Eastern liturgies is an indisputable attitude and the Apostolic See can offer a more complete service to the Churches.[^cite_note-Instruction1996-53]<sup>: n. 24 </sup>

### Papal supreme authority



[Pope Pius XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XI) in an audience with [Demetrius I Qadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrius_I_Qadi), [Patriarch of Antioch and All the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Catholic_Patriarchate_of_Antioch) and other bishops of the [Melkite Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Church) in 1923

Under the [Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches](3547385.md), the pope has [supreme, full, immediate and universal ordinary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_supremacy) in the whole Catholic Church, which he can always freely exercise, including the Eastern Catholic churches.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-57]

### Eastern patriarchs and major archbishops



[Patriarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Antioch) [Bechara Boutros al-Rahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bechara_Boutros_al-Rahi) is the head of the [Maronite Church](66391078.md), and also a [Cardinal](6221.md).

The Catholic [patriarchs](175409.md) and [major archbishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_archbishop) derive their titles from the sees of [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/See_of_Alexandria) ([Coptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Catholic_Church)), [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/See_of_Antioch) (Syriac, Melkite, Maronite), [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chaldean_Catholic_patriarchs_of_Baghdad) (Chaldean), [Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Patriarchate_of_Cilicia) (Armenian), [Kyiv-Halych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Catholic_Major_Archeparchy_of_Kyiv%E2%80%93Galicia) (Ukrainian), [Ernakulam-Angamaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syro-Malabar_Catholic_Major_Archeparchy_of_Ernakulam-Angamaly) (Syro-Malabar), [Trivandrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trivandrum) ([Syro-Malankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syro-Malankara_Catholic_Church)), and [Făgăraş-Alba Iulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_F%C4%83g%C4%83ra%C5%9F_and_Alba_Iulia) (Romanian). The Eastern Catholic churches are governed in accordance with Code of Canons of the Eastern Churches and their particular laws.[^cite_note-canon_1-58]

Within their proper *_sui iuris_* churches there is no difference between patriarchs and major archbishops. However, differences exist in the [order of precedence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_precedence_in_the_Catholic_Church) (i.e. patriarchs take precedence over major archbishops) and in the mode of accession: The election of a major archbishop has to be confirmed by the pope before he may take office.[^cite_note-59] No papal confirmation is needed for newly elected patriarchs before they take office. They are just required to request as soon as possible that the pope grant them [full ecclesiastical communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communion_\(Christian\)).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-62]

### Variants of organizational structure

There are significant differences between various Eastern Catholic churches, regarding their present organizational structure. Major Eastern Catholic churches, that are headed by their patriarchs, major archbishops or metropolitans, have fully developed structure and functioning internal autonomy based on the existence of ecclesiastical provinces. On the other hand, minor Eastern Catholic churches often have only one or two hierarchs (in the form of eparchs, apostolic exarchs, or apostolic visitors) and only the most basic forms of internal organization if any, like the [Belarusian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Greek_Catholic_Church) or the [Russian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Greek_Catholic_Church).[^cite_note-63] Individual eparchies of some Eastern Catholic churches may be suffragan to Latin metropolitans. For example, the [Greek Catholic Eparchy of Križevci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Catholic_Eparchy_of_Kri%C5%BEevci) is suffragan to the [Roman Catholic Archdiocese of Zagreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Zagreb).[^cite_note-64] Also, some minor Eastern Catholic churches have Latin prelates. For example, the [Macedonian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_Greek_Catholic_Church) is organized as a single [Eparchy of Strumica-Skopje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_Catholic_Eparchy_of_the_Assumption_of_the_Blessed_Virgin_Mary_in_Strumica-Skopje), whose present ordinary is the Roman Catholic bishop of [Skopje](29618.md).[^cite_note-65] The organization of the [Albanian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Greek_Catholic_Church) is unique in that it consists of an "Apostolic Administration".[^cite_note-66]

### Juridical status

See also: [Eastern Catholic canon law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_canon_law)

Although every diocese in the Catholic Church is considered a [particular church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particular_church), the word is not applied in the same sense as to the 24 *_sui iuris_* particular churches: the Latin Church and the 23 Eastern Catholic Churches.

[Canonically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_law_\(Catholic_Church\)), each Eastern Catholic Church is *_[sui iuris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_iuris)_* or autonomous with respect to other Catholic churches, whether Latin or Eastern, though all accept the spiritual and juridical [supreme authority of the pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_supremacy). Thus a Maronite Catholic is normally directly subject only to a Maronite bishop. However, if members of a particular church are so few that no hierarchy of their own has been established, their spiritual care is entrusted to a bishop of another ritual church. For instance, members of the Latin Church in [Eritrea](17238590.md) are under the care of the Eastern rite [Eritrean Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eritrean_Catholic_Church), whereas the other way around may be the case in other parts of the world.

Theologically, all the particular churches can be viewed as "sister churches".[^cite_note-67] According to the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) these Eastern Catholic churches, along with the larger Latin Church, share "equal dignity, so that none of them is superior to the others as regards rite, and they enjoy the same rights and are under the same obligations, also in respect of preaching the [Gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gospel) to the whole world (cf. [Mark 16\:15](https://www.biblegateway.com/passage/?search=Mark+16\:15\&version=rsvce)) under the guidance of the [Roman Pontiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_of_Rome)."[^cite_note-OE-23]<sup>: n. 3 </sup>



[Syro-Malankara Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syro-Malankara_Catholic_Church) Major Archbishop-Catholicos celebrating [Qurbono Qadisho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurbono_Qadisho) in [West Syriac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Syriac_Rite)

The Eastern Catholic churches are in [full communion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_communion) with the whole Catholic Church. While they accept the canonical authority of the Holy See of Rome and share belief in the same [dogmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogma_in_the_Catholic_Church) as the [Latin church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church),[^cite_note-\:1-8][^cite_note-68] they retain their distinctive [liturgical rites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgy), laws, customs and traditional devotions, and have their own theological emphases. Terminology may vary: for instance, *diocese* and *eparchy*, *vicar general* and *[protosyncellus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protosyncellus)*, *[confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation)* and *[chrismation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrismation)* are respectively Western and Eastern terms for the same realities. The mysteries (sacraments) of [baptism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism) and chrismation are generally administered, according to the ancient tradition of the church, one immediately after the other. Infants who are baptized and chrismated are also given the [Eucharist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eucharist_in_the_Catholic_Church).[^cite_note-69]

The Eastern Catholic churches are represented in the [Holy See](13393.md) and the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia) through the [Dicastery for the Eastern Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Eastern_Churches), which is "made up of a Cardinal Prefect (who directs and represents it with the help of a Secretary) and 27 cardinals, one archbishop and 4 bishops, designated by the pope *_ad quinquennium_* (for a five-year period). Members by right are the Patriarchs and the Major Archbishops of the Oriental Churches and the President of the [Pontifical Council for the Promotion of Unity among Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Council_for_Promoting_Christian_Unity)."[^cite_note-70]

### Bi-ritual faculties



A [bishop](4092.md) celebrating [Divine Liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Liturgy) in a [Greek Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Catholic_Church) church in [Prešov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre%C5%A1ov), eastern [Slovakia](26830.md). Another bishop stands to his immediate right (white [omophorion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omophorion) visible), and two married priests stand to the side (facing camera).

While "clerics and members of [institutes of consecrated life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_order) are bound to observe their own rite faithfully",[^cite_note-71] priests are occasionally given permission to celebrate the liturgy of a rite other than the priest's own rite, by what is known as a grant of "biritual faculties". The reason for this permission is usually the service of Catholics who have no priest of their own rite. Thus priests of the [Syro-Malabar Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syro-Malabar_Catholic_Church) working as missionaries in areas of India in which there are no structures of their own church, are authorized to use the Roman Rite in those areas, and Latin priests are, after due preparation, given permission to use an Eastern rite for the service of members of an Eastern Catholic church living in a country in which there are no priests of their own particular church. Popes are permitted to celebrate a Mass or Divine Liturgy of any rite in testament to the Catholic Church's universal nature. John Paul II celebrated the Divine Liturgy in Ukraine during his pontificate.

For a just cause, and with the permission of the local bishop, priests of different autonomous ritual churches may concelebrate; however, the rite of the principal celebrant is used whilst each priest wears the [vestments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vestments) of his own rite.[^cite_note-72] No indult of bi-ritualism is required for this.

Biritual faculties may concern not only clergy but also [religious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_\(Catholicism\)), enabling them to become members of an institute of an autonomous Church other than their own.[^cite_note-73]

### Clerical celibacy

See also: [Clerical marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clerical_marriage)



[Romanian Greek Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Greek_Catholic_Church) priest from Romania with his family.

Eastern and Western Christian churches have different traditions concerning [clerical celibacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clerical_celibacy) and the resulting controversies have played a role in the relationship between the two groups in some [Western countries](21208200.md).

In general, Eastern Catholic Churches have always allowed ordination of married men as priests and deacons. Within the lands of the [Ukrainian Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic_Church), the second largest Eastern Catholic Church, 90% of the [diocesan priests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocesan_priests) in Ukraine are [married](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clerical_marriage).[^cite_note-Galadza_2010_303-74]

Most Eastern Churches distinguish between monastic and non-monastic clergy. [Monastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieromonk) do not necessarily live in monasteries, but have spent at least part of their period of training in such a context. Their [monastic vows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_vows) include a vow of celibate chastity.

Bishops are normally selected from the monastic clergy, and in most Eastern Catholic Churches a large percentage of priests and deacons also are celibate, while a large portion of the parish priests are married, having taken a wife when they were still laymen.[^cite_note-Subtelny2009-75] If someone preparing for the diaconate or priesthood wishes to marry, this must happen before ordination.

In territories where Eastern traditions prevail, married clergy caused little controversy, but aroused opposition inside traditionally Latin Church territories to which Eastern Catholics migrated; this was particularly so in the United States.[^cite_note-76] In response to requests from the Latin bishops of those countries, the [Congregation for the Propagation of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Propagation_of_the_Faith) set out rules in an 1890 letter to [François-Marie-Benjamin Richard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois-Marie-Benjamin_Richard), [archbishop of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Paris),[^cite_note-77] which the Congregation applied on 1 May 1897 to the United States, stating that only celibates or widowed priests coming without their children should be permitted in the United States.[^cite_note-78]

This celibacy mandate for Eastern Catholic priests in the United States was restated with special reference to [Ruthenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenian_Greek_Catholic_Church) by the 1 March 1929 decree *Cum data fuerit*, which was renewed for a further ten years in 1939. Dissatisfaction by many Ruthenian Catholics in the United States gave rise to the [American Carpatho-Russian Orthodox Diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Carpatho-Russian_Orthodox_Diocese).[^cite_note-79] The mandate, which applied in some other countries also, was removed by a decree of June 2014.[^cite_note-80]

While most Eastern Catholic Churches admit married men to ordination as priests, some have adopted mandatory clerical celibacy, as in the Latin Church. These include the India-based Syro-Malankara Catholic Church and Syro-Malabar Catholic Church,[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] and the [Coptic Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Catholic_Church).[^cite_note-Galadza_2010_303-74]

In 2014, [Pope Francis](1687680.md) approved new norms for married clergy within Eastern Catholic Churches through CCEO canon 758 § 3. The new norms abrogated previous norms and now allow those Eastern Catholic Churches with married clergy to ordain married men inside traditionally Latin territories and to grant [faculties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faculty_\(instrument\)) inside traditionally Latin territories to married Eastern Catholic clergy previously ordained elsewhere.[^cite_note-AAS_106-6-83] This latter change will allow married Eastern Catholic priests to follow their faithful to whatever country they may immigrate to, addressing an issue which has arisen with the migration of Christians from Eastern Europe and the Middle East in recent decades.[^cite_note-VaticanInsider_2014-11-15-84]

## Persecution

### Eastern Europe

Main article: [Eastern Catholic victims of Soviet persecutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholic_victims_of_Soviet_persecutions)

A study by Methodios Stadnik states: "The Georgian Byzantine Catholic Exarch, Fr. Shio Batmanishviii \[*[sic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sic)*], and two Georgian Catholic priests of the Latin Church were executed by the Soviet authorities in 1937 after having been held in captivity in Solovki prison and the northern gulags from 1923."[^cite_note-102] Christopher Zugger writes, in *The Forgotten*: "By 1936, the [Byzantine Catholic Church of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Byzantine-Rite_Catholics) had two communities, served by a bishop and four priests, with 8,000 believers", and he identifies the bishop as Shio Batmalashvili.[^cite_note-103] [Vasyl Ovsiyenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasyl_Ovsiyenko?action=edit\&redlink=1) mentions, on the [Ukrainian Helsinki Human Rights Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Helsinki_Human_Rights_Union) website, that "the Catholic administrator for Georgia Shio Batmalashvili" was one of those executed as "anti-Soviet elements" in 1937.[^cite_note-104]

See also: [Orthodox Church in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Church_in_America) and [Alexis Toth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Toth)

While not subject to the kind of physical dangers or persecution from government authorities encountered in Eastern Europe or the Middle East, adherents of Eastern Catholic Churches in United States, most of whom were relatively new immigrants from Eastern Europe, encountered difficulties due to hostility from the Latin Church clergy who dominated the Catholic hierarchy in United States who found them alien. In particular, immigration of Eastern Catholic priests who were married, common in their churches but extremely rare in Latin churches, was forbidden or severely limited and some Latin Church bishops actively interfered with the pastoral work of those who did arrive. Some bishops sought to forbid all non-Latin Catholic priests from coming to United States at all. Many Eastern Catholic immigrants to United States were thus either assimilated into the Latin Church or joined the Eastern Orthodox Church. One former Eastern Catholic priest, Alexis Toth, left the Catholic Church following criticism and sanctions from Latin authorities including [John Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ireland_\(bishop\)), the Bishop of [Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Saint_Paul_and_Minneapolis), and joining the Orthodox Church. Toth has been canonized as an Eastern Orthodox saint for having led as many as 20,000 disaffected former Eastern Catholics to the Eastern Orthodox Church, particularly the [American Carpatho-Russian Orthodox Diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Carpatho-Russian_Orthodox_Diocese).
