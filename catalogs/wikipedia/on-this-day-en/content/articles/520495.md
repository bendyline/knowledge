**Santa Cruz Province** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Provincia de Santa Cruz*, Spanish pronunciation: [\[ˈsanta ˈkɾus\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish), "Holy Cross") is a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Argentina) of [Argentina](18951905.md), located in the southern part of the country, in [Patagonia](65433.md). It borders [Chubut Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chubut_Province) to the north, and [Chile](5489.md) to the west and south, with the [Atlantic](698.md) coast to the east. Santa Cruz is the second-largest province of the country (after [Buenos Aires Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires_Province)), and the least [densely populated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) in mainland Argentina. It covers a land area of 243,943 km<sup>2</sup> and had a population of 337,226 at the 2022 Census.[^cite_note-Census_2022-1]

The indigenous people of the province are the [Tehuelches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehuelches), who despite European exploration from the 16th century onwards, retained independence until the late 19th century. Soon after the [Conquest of the Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_the_Desert) in the 1870s, the area was organised as the Territory of Santa Cruz, named after its original capital in [Puerto Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Santa_Cruz). The capital moved to [Rio Gallegos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Gallegos) in 1888 and has remained there ever since. Immigrants from various European countries came to the territory in the late 19th and early 20th century [during a gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego_gold_rush). Santa Cruz became a province of Argentina in 1957.

## History

The [Tehuelches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehuelche_people) inhabited these lands before the arrivals of the [Spanish colonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonisation_of_the_Americas). In 1520 [Ferdinand Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Magellan) arrived to what is currently known as *San Julián Bay*. 15 years later Martín de Alcazaba explored the area near the Chico River, which he named [Gallegos River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallegos_River). Because of the attacks of [British](31717.md) [privateers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privateer), and after the visit of [Francis Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Drake) in 1578, the [Spaniards](26667.md) sent [Pedro Sarmiento de Gamboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Sarmiento_de_Gamboa) to fortify and map the [Strait of Magellan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Magellan) and prevent access to Spanish posts in the [Pacific](23070.md).

In the middle of the 18th century, the [Jesuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) settled in the area, establishing a few [missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_\(Christian\)). When the [Viceroyalty of the Río de la Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_the_R%C3%ADo_de_la_Plata) was created in 1776, the region was set under the rule of [Buenos Aires](255919.md). Antonio de Biedma founded *Nueva Colonia* near present [Puerto Deseado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Deseado) and [*Floridablanca*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floridablanca_\(Patagonia\)) not far from [Puerto San Julián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_San_Juli%C3%A1n), both of them shut down later by Viceroy Vertíz.

Between 1825 and 1836 there were a series of explorations of the regions, including that of [Charles Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin) in 1834. In 1860 commander [Luis Piedrabuena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Piedrabuena) established a base on [Isla Pavón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isla_Pav%C3%B3n) in the estuary of Puerto Deseado.

In 1878 the Government of [Patagonia](65433.md) was created, with capital in [Viedma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viedma), but six years later it was split into smaller entities, with the territory declared *National Government of Santa Cruz*, whose capital was the city of Santa Cruz. In 1901 the capital was moved to its current location at the city of [Río Gallegos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_Gallegos).

At the beginning of the 20th century, a large [European](9239.md) immigration began to arrive to the almost uninhabited zone; Spanish, [Germans](11867.md), British and [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) were the most numerous among them. They came mainly to escape the growing conflicts of [World War I](4764461.md), and were attracted by the wool industry of the area. The beginning of the war meant a sharp reduction in the amount of exports, bringing a serious economic crisis to Santa Cruz.



Scene from *[La Patagonia rebelde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Patagonia_rebelde)*. Based on an ill-fated local strike in 1922, the 1974 epic was made with the encouragement of Santa Cruz's governor.

The ideals of [progressivism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism), brought by the Spanish immigrants, grew among the workers who, working in Santa Cruz's harsh environment under often sub-human conditions, decided to [strike](155134.md) in 1922. The strike was severely and harshly repressed by the government, culminating in the events of the *[Patagonia Trágica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patagonia_rebelde)* ("Tragic Patagonia"), the execution of dozens of strikers.

In 1944, the *Military zone of Comodoro Rivadavia* was created, which encompassed the northern part of the *National Government of Santa Cruz* and the southern part of [Chubut Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chubut_Province). This jurisdiction lasted until the abolition of the measures in 1955. The Territory of Santa Cruz acquired province status in 1957.&#x20;

In 1973, voters in Santa Cruz elected [Jorge Cepernic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Cepernic), a [Peronist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peronist). An advocate of labor rights, Gov. Cepernic worked with film maker [Osvaldo Bayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osvaldo_Bayer) to make *[La Patagonia Rebelde](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/La%20Patagonia%20Rebelde)* ('"Rebellion in Patagonia"), a documentary drama on the ill-fated 1922 sheep ranch laborers' strike. For this, Gov. Cepernic was imprisoned following the March 1976 [coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reorganization_Process).[^cite_note-5]



Néstor and [Cristina Kirchner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Kirchner) (*center*) in conference with fellow [Patagonia](65433.md)-area lawmakers.

The return to democracy in Argentina in 1983 brought new, mostly young leadership to Santa Cruz's elected posts, among them a well-known local [country lawyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_lawyer) named [Néstor Kirchner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9stor_Kirchner), elected that year to the [Río Gallegos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_Gallegos) City Council. Elected mayor in 1987 and governor in 1991, Kirchner helped negotiate a US\$535 million payout for his province following the 1993 privatization of the state-owned oil concern [YPF](715706.md). Earning plaudits for his careful administration of the funds, Kirchner was elected president of Argentina in April 2003, following the withdrawal of [Carlos Menem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Menem) from a runoff which Kirchner was projected to win handily.

Presiding over four years of expansion totalling 42% (the best performance for the Argentine economy since the 1880s),[^cite_note-6] Pres. Kirchner steered record spending into public works (particularly those in his province, as is customary for Argentine presidents).

&#x20;In the Province there is a [border dispute with Chile in the Southern Patagonian Ice Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Patagonian_Ice_Field_dispute),[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] in 1898 the border was defined and wasn't objected during the [1902 Arbitral award of the Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1902_Arbitral_award_of_the_Andes_between_Argentina_and_Chile) which defined most of the border on the current Province territory. Both experts, [Francisco Pascasio Moreno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Moreno) from Argentina and [Diego Barros Arana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Barros_Arana) from Chile agreed on the border between [Mount Fitz Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Fitz_Roy) and [Cerro Daudet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Daudet?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-11] However the border started being questioned by Argentina later on which started the dispute between both countries. In 1998 a border redraw is agreed, being pending to this day the part between Mount Fitz Roy and [Cerro Murallón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Murall%C3%B3n),[^cite_note-12] however a new border was drawn between [Cerro Murallón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Murall%C3%B3n) and [Cerro Daudet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Daudet?action=edit\&redlink=1).

In the 20th century both countries had another [dispute over the Del Desierto Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_del_Desierto_incident) which was resolved in favor of Argentina in 1994 and had its climax in 1965 when Lieutenant [Hernán Merino Correa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Merino_Correa) was killed by [Argentine Gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Gendarmerie).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-ALaSombra-14]

## Geography



The windswept [Patagonian](65433.md) landscape of Santa Cruz.

The province is generally divided into two distinct regions: The [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) in the west and the plateaux in the centre and east.[^cite_note-relieve-15] In the Andes, there are numerous lakes such as [Buenos Aires Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buenos_Aires/General_Carrera_Lake) (2,240 km<sup>2</sup>, 881 km<sup>2</sup> in Argentina), [Cardiel Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lago_Cardiel) (460 km<sup>2</sup>), [Viedma Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Viedma) (1,082 km<sup>2</sup>), [Argentino Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentino_Lake) (1,560 km<sup>2</sup>), [Pueyrredón Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pueyrred%C3%B3n/Cochrane_Lake), [Belgrano Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgrano_Lake) and [San Martín Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Higgins/San_Mart%C3%ADn_Lake) (1,013 km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-relieve-15] One characteristic of the Andean region is the presence of the [Southern Patagonian Ice Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Patagonian_Ice_Field) that covers the central part of the Andes.[^cite_note-relieve-15]

From the centre to the [Atlantic](698.md) coast in the east, the landscape is dominated by plateaux.[^cite_note-relieve-15] These plateaux are made up of [basalt rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basalt) occurring in stepped sequences.[^cite_note-Mazrab2010-16] The plateaux are of different ages, with the older –of [Neogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neogene) and [Paleogene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleogene) age– being located at higher elevations than [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) and [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene) lava plateaus and outcroppings.[^cite_note-Mazrab2010-16] There are isolated pockets of hills and depressions within this region.[^cite_note-relieve-15] In *Gran Bajo de San Julián*, the *[Laguna del Carbón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_del_Carb%C3%B3n)* is 105 meters below sea level, and is the lowest point in the Western and Southern Hemispheres.[^cite_note-relieve-15] On the Atlantic coast, it is characterised by cliffs.[^cite_note-relieve-15]

### Hydrography

The main rivers in the province are the [Deseado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deseado_River) ([Spanish](26825.md): ***Río Deseado***), [Chico River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chico_River_\(Santa_Cruz\)) ([Spanish](26825.md): ***Río Chico***), [Santa Cruz River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_River_\(Argentina\)) ([Spanish](26825.md): ***Río Santa Cruz***), [Coig River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coig_River) ([Spanish](26825.md): ***Río Coig***), and the [Gallegos River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallegos_River) ([Spanish](26825.md): ***Río Gallegos***).[^cite_note-relieve-15] These rivers all originate in the Andes and drain into the lakes before moving eastwards to empty into the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md).[^cite_note-relieve-15]

### Climate

See also: [Climate of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Argentina) and [Climatic regions of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climatic_regions_of_Argentina)



Köppen climate map of Santa Cruz, Argentina

The [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) block most of the incoming frontal systems and as a result, most of the rainfall occurs on the western side of the mountains, with [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) rapidly decreasing as one moves eastward.[^cite_note-IRN-17] As a result, except for the westernmost parts of the province where precipitation is abundant as the area comes under greater influence from prevailing winds from the Pacific, much of the province is dry.[^cite_note-IRN-17] Unlike the Northern Hemisphere where there are large land masses to allow cold temperatures, [cold fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_fronts), which usually originate from the southwest and move northeast and are moderated by the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md), resulting in less intense cold temperatures.[^cite_note-IRN-17] Similar to much of [Patagonia](65433.md), the climate of the province is characterized by strong [westerlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westerlies), which also enhances [evapotranspiration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evapotranspiration).[^cite_note-IRN-17] In general, the southern sections of the province are colder than those to the north.

#### Temperatures

The mean temperatures for the province are relatively cold for its latitude due to the cold [Falkland Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Current).[^cite_note-IRN-17] The extreme northeast coast is by far the mildest area, with annual temperatures around 8 to 9 °C (46.4 to 48.2 °F) with temperatures inland being 1 °C to 2 °C lower.[^cite_note-IRN-17] The coldest temperatures are found in the southern parts of the province with an annual mean of 6 °C (42.8 °F) although in the western parts of the province which are at higher altitudes, the mean can be below 5 °C (41.0 °F) and even below 0 °C (32.0 °F) at the highest elevations.[^cite_note-IRN-17] In the summer, mean temperatures range from 16 °C (60.8 °F) in the North to around 12 °C (53.6 °F) in the south. However, temperatures can reach up to 35 °C (95.0 °F) in all parts of the province except at the highest elevations.[^cite_note-IRN-17] Winters are cold throughout the province, with more uniform temperatures than during the summer; July temperatures average 4 °C (39.2 °F) in the North to 2 °C (35.6 °F) in the southern parts.[^cite_note-IRN-17] Temperatures can plummet below −20 °C (−4.0 °F) in the interior while at the coast, it infrequently drops below −15 °C (5.0 °F).[^cite_note-IRN-17]

#### Precipitation

As the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) block most precipitation of the incoming frontal systems, most of the rainfall occurs on the western side of the mountains, with [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) rapidly decreasing as one moves eastward.[^cite_note-IRN-17] Much of the province receives less than 200 millimetres (8 in) of precipitation a year, while the Andean region, which is under greater influence from the Pacific, it can receive more than 1,000 millimetres (39 in).[^cite_note-IRN-17] Precipitation mainly falls in the form of light rain with heavy rain and thunderstorms being rare, with these two only occurring during the summer.[^cite_note-IRN-17] In all parts of the province, more precipitation falls in the winter than in the summer.[^cite_note-IRN-17]

#### Wind

The prevalent wind directions are from the west, southwest and northwest and together, these occur 60% of the time.[^cite_note-IRN-17] Northern parts of the province tend to have more northwest winds than the southern parts of the province. With few landmasses south of 46°S, the province is dominated by strong winds throughout the year. Summers are windier than winters.[^cite_note-IRN-17] The strongest winds are found in the Andean region while the weakest are found in the coastal areas.[^cite_note-IRN-17] With a mean wind speed of 10 metres per second (33 ft/s), the province is the windiest in Argentina.[^cite_note-IRN-17] [Gusts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind) can reach up to 200 kilometres per hour (120 mph) in some places.[^cite_note-IRN-17]

#### Sunshine

Although most of the province is dry, the province is relatively cloudy.[^cite_note-IRN-17] The percent of cloudy days ranges from 50% in the north to 60% in the south.[^cite_note-IRN-17] Average daily sunshine hours range from 6 hours/day in the North to 4 hours/day in the south with the lowest in the coastal areas in the south.[^cite_note-IRN-17]

## Economy



Oceanographic craft ARA *Puerto Deseado*

Santa Cruz, with a small population and rich in natural resources, has long had one of Argentina's most prosperous economies. Its 2006 output was estimated at US\$3.3 billion or a per capita income of US\$16,553.[^cite_note-pbg-22] In 2011 Santa Cruz had the highest per capita income of Argentina, US\$36,550.[^cite_note-23]

Its economy, with the possible exception of [Neuquen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuquen_Province)'s, is the country's least diversified, however. Fully half its output is accounted for by the extractive sector (petroleum, gas and mining), with an annual production of 4.5 million m<sup>3</sup> of [petroleum](23195.md) and 3 million m<sup>3</sup> of [gas](22131.md), mainly in the *Pico Truncado*, *Cañadón seco* and *Cerro Redondo* extracting facilities. [Cerro Negro mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Negro_mine) by Gold Corp. has in recent years contributed to the growth of population of Santa Cruz through its [Gold](12240.md) mining operations and subsidies to local public education and private sector.[^cite_note-GoldCorp-24]

The [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) production at *Río Turbio*, Argentina's only active coal mine, is of around one million m<sup>3</sup> per year. Mining includes [gold](12240.md) (*[Cerro Vanguardia Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Vanguardia_Mine)*), [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay), [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum), [salt](1605200.md) and others.

The second most important productive activity is that associated with [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep). With 7 million heads, Santa Cruz is the second main producer of wool and meat after the Province of Chubut, most of which is designated for export. Sheep farming revived in 2002 with the devaluation of the peso and firmer global demand for wool (led by China and the EU). Still there is little investment in new abattoirs (mainly in Rio Gallegos), and often there are [phytosanitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytosanitary_regulation) restrictions to the export of sheep meat. Livestock also includes small numbers of [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle), and in lesser numbers pigs and horses.

Sea fishing, and its later industrialization at the fishing ports of [Puerto Deseado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Deseado), [Puerto San Julián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_San_Juli%C3%A1n), [Puerto Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Santa_Cruz) and [Río Gallegos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_Gallegos) produces [prawn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prawn), [squid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squid), [hake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hake) and dozens of others. Most of the production is frozen and exported.

There is little agriculture due to the arid nature of the soil. There is a small timber industry fed by both forests and planted trees, of which the wood of the [lenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenga_Beech) is the most exploited.

## Tourism



[Mount Fitz Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Fitz_Roy), near [El Chaltén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Chalt%C3%A9n)

Santa Cruz's most visited destination is [Los Glaciares National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Glaciares_National_Park) and a number of glaciers of which the [Perito Moreno Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perito_Moreno_Glacier) is the most famous. Nearby [El Calafate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Calafate) has an airport that connects the area with [Buenos Aires](255919.md) and [Trelew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trelew).

Some 200 kilometres north of El Calafate is the village of [El Chaltén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Chalt%C3%A9n) at the feet of the [Cerro Torre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Torre) and [Mount Fitz Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Fitz_Roy). Still not very developed, El Chaltén serves as a hub for various trekking routes including walks on the [Viedma Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viedma_Glacier).



Tourists trekking by the [Perito Moreno Glacier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perito_Moreno_Glacier), near [El Calafate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Calafate)

600 kilometres further north of El Chaltén, by the dirt road [Ruta 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruta_40), the [Cueva de las Manos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cueva_de_las_Manos) near the town of [Perito Moreno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perito_Moreno,_Santa_Cruz) allows the few tourists who venture to this point to see the prehistoric wall paintings in the caves near the [Pinturas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinturas_River).[^cite_note-25]

[Perito Moreno National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perito_Moreno_National_Park) and its lakes, north of Los Glaciares, are rarely visited. Besides trekking, other sports practised on the west side of the province are sport fishing, rafting and climbing.

In the east, the [National Route 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Route_3_\(Argentina\)) follows the [Atlantic](698.md) coastline, by which buses connect the coastal cities, and take passengers both south to [Tierra del Fuego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego_Province,_Argentina) and north to [Chubut Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chubut_Province) and [Buenos Aires](255919.md). The most visited places are the cities of [Río Gallegos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_Gallegos), the [Bosques Petrificados National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosques_Petrificados_National_Monument?action=edit\&redlink=1) petrified forest, and the depression of *[Laguna del Carbón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_del_Carb%C3%B3n)* near [Puerto San Julián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_San_Juli%C3%A1n).

## Government

The provincial government is divided into three branches: the executive, headed by a popularly elected governor, who appoints the cabinet; the [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_branch); and the [judiciary](2627860.md), headed by the Supreme Court.

Main articles: [Chamber of Deputies of Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies_of_Santa_Cruz) and [List of Governors of Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Governors_of_Santa_Cruz)

The Constitution of Santa Cruz Province, Argentina forms the formal law of the province.

In Argentina, the most important law enforcement organization is the [Argentine Federal Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Federal_Police) but the additional work is carried out by the [Santa Cruz Provincial Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz_Provincial_Police?action=edit\&redlink=1).
