[Indonesia](14579.md) and the [Netherlands](21148.md) share a special relationship, rooted in their shared, multicentury [history of colonial interactions](7299.md).[^cite_note-1] This began during the [spice trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spice_trade) in the early 17th century with the establishment of trading posts by the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) in the [Indonesian archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Indonesia), and continued until the [decolonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decolonisation) of the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) in the mid-20th century. Indonesia was the largest [former Dutch colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_colonial_empire). Since the 2010s, the Dutch government has been committed to improving bilateral ties with Indonesia, with an emphasis on strengthening economic, political, and interpersonal contacts.[^cite_note-2]

## History

See also: [Company rule in the Dutch East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_rule_in_the_Dutch_East_Indies), [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md), and [Netherlands-Indonesia Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands-Indonesia_Union)



Stadhuis of [Batavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Dutch_East_Indies), the former Asian headquarters of the VOC, now housing the [Jakarta History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakarta_History_Museum)



[Mohammad Hatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Hatta) and Queen Juliana sign the [Hague Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hague_Agreement_Between_Netherlands-Indonesia) in 1949

Brothers [Cornelis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelis_de_Houtman) and [Frederick de Houtman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_de_Houtman)[^cite_note-3] were explorers who led the [first Dutch expedition to the East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Dutch_expedition_to_the_East_Indies), which discovered shipping routes from the [Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) to the Indonesian archipelago and successfully started the Dutch spice trade. In 1592, Cornelis de Houtman was sent by the merchants of [Amsterdam](844.md) to [Lisbon](18091.md) to find as much information as possible about the Spice Islands (the present day [Maluku Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands)). By the time De Houtman returned to Amsterdam, the traders confirmed that [Banten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banten) was the most appropriate place to buy spices. In 1594, they established the *[Compagnie van Verre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compagnie_van_Verre)* (lit. 'long-distance company'), and on 2 April 1595, four ships left Amsterdam: *[Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_\(ship\))*, *[Hollandia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollandia_\(1742_ship\))*, *[Mauritius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauritius_\(1612\))* and *[Duyfken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duyfken)*. Cornelis de Houtman and his fleet arrived off the coast of the [Banten Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banten_Sultanate) on 27 June 1596 and departed on 14 August 1597, bringing with them 240 bags of [pepper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_pepper), 45 tonnes of [nutmeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutmeg), and 30 bales of [mace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutmeg#Mace).[^cite_note-4]

In 1603, the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Vereenigde Oostindische Compagnie*, VOC) commenced operations in the Indonesian archipelago, where it waged wars to expand its trade monopoly.[^cite_note-5] Though Indonesian history featured other European colonial powers, it was the Dutch who consolidated their hold on the archipelago. Following the bankruptcy of the VOC and the [French and British interregnum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_British_interregnum_in_the_Dutch_East_Indies), the [Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands) assumed control of the colony in 1816.[^cite_note-6] The Dutch government embarked on the conquest of the remaining independent indigenous polities in the region and enforced the exploitative *[Landrentestelsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landrentestelsel)* (lit. 'land tax system') and [Cultivation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultivation_System) until 1870. The [Liberal Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Period_\(Dutch_East_Indies\)) that followed resulted in the adoption of the [Ethical Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Ethical_Policy) in 1901, which included hesitant political reforms and provided modest improvements in economic and educational opportunities for the native population, but ended with the onset of the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression). Even so, this short period of liberalisation inadvertently led to the [Indonesian National Awakening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Awakening) and the development of [Indonesian nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_nationalism) among the new, educated indigenous elite.

Following the [end of World War II in Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_World_War_II_in_Asia) and the [Indonesian declaration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Indonesian_Independence) in 1945, the Netherlands attempted to re-establish colonial rule, resulting in a [bitter armed and diplomatic struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution).[^cite_note-7] International pressure forced the Dutch to formally recognise Indonesian independence in December 1949.[^cite_note-8] Headed by [Queen Juliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliana_of_the_Netherlands), a loose union continued to exist between the Netherlands and Indonesia until 1956, when the growing [West New Guinea dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_New_Guinea_dispute) pitted the two countries against each other once more. In 1960, the Indonesian government under President [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno) severed all diplomatic ties with the Netherlands and forcefully pursued their claim to [Dutch New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_New_Guinea). Once again under international pressure, the Dutch yielded and agreed to a diplomatic compromise, whereby Indonesia gained control of the territory in May 1963. Diplomatic relations were restored in 1968 by the [New Order](2864794.md) government under second Indonesian President [Suharto](217513.md).

## Political relations



Queen Juliana and President Suharto in Jakarta, during her royal visit in 1971



President Prabowo Subianto with Queen Máxima and King Willem-Alexander in 2025

In the past, relations between the two have been marred by the separatist intentions of the [Free Papua Movement](541409.md)[^cite_note-9] and the [Republic of South Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_South_Maluku). In this vein, they have attacked targets[^cite_note-10] in the Netherlands in the 1970s and 1980s, seeking to force the country to pressure Indonesia into allowing for the secession of their nation. Political ties were then strained as Indonesian officials refused to visit the Netherlands while the group was allowed to bring cases to court against them.[^cite_note-11] However, the visit of the Dutch Minister of Foreign Affairs [Bernard Bot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Bot) to Indonesia in 2005 to celebrate its 60th independence day anniversary was claimed by the Netherlands to have "marked a\[n] historic moment in the relations between the two countries. After this visit, the relations between Indonesia and the Netherlands was further intensified and strengthened by the extension of the cooperation in a wide range of fields."[^cite_note-12]

In 2010, Indonesian President [Susilo Bambang Yudhoyono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susilo_Bambang_Yudhoyono) cancelled a visit to the Netherlands after the group's activists asked a Dutch court to issue an arrest warrant for him.[^cite_note-13] The move was condemned by pro-Indonesia Moluccan activists in Jakarta.[^cite_note-14]

### High-level visits

In 1970, Indonesian President [Suharto](217513.md) paid an official visit to the Netherlands, which was reciprocated by Dutch [Queen Juliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Juliana) and [Prince Bernhard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Bernhard) royal visit to Indonesia in 1971.[^cite_note-15] [Queen Beatrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Beatrix) and [Prince Claus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_Claus) also paid a royal visit to Indonesia in 1995.[^cite_note-16] On 22 April 2016, President [Joko Widodo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joko_Widodo) paid an official visit to the Netherlands.[^cite_note-17] On 10 March 2020, King [Willem-Alexander](100384.md) paid a royal visit to Indonesia and made surprise apologies for excessive violence used during [the early years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution) of Indonesian independence.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] On 25 September 2025, President [Prabowo Subianto](2829084.md) visited the Netherlands and held meetings with King Willem-Alexander and Queen [Máxima Zorreguieta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1xima_Zorreguieta) to maintain the positive relations between the two nations, especially their collaboration in science and culture.[^cite_note-20]

## Economic relations



[Garuda Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garuda_Indonesia) [Boeing 777-300ER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_777-300ER) in [Schiphol Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schiphol_Airport). The Netherlands has been a traditional gate for Indonesians to access Europe

The Netherlands is one of Indonesia's most important trade partners in Europe. Trade between the two countries in 2024 reached approximately US\$ 5.7 billion.[^cite_note-\:1-21] In terms of exports, Indonesia exports a variety of commodities such as [palm oil](57561.md), [manufactured goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_good), [footwear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Footwear), [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile), and [fishery products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_products), while imports from the Netherlands are dominated by [machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine), [chemical products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Product_\(chemistry\)), [pharmaceuticals](180121.md), and [high-tech goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tech).[^cite_note-22] In addition to trade, economic ties between the two countries are also strengthened through investment, with the Netherlands consistently ranking among the top ten foreign investors in Indonesia, particularly in the manufacturing, logistics, and energy sectors.[^cite_note-\:1-21]

The Port of [Rotterdam](26049.md) and [Schiphol Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schiphol_Airport) have been the main entry points for Indonesian products into the [European Union](9317.md). Similarly, Dutch companies have seen Indonesia as their gateway to the larger [ASEAN](106539.md) market, which is home to more than 500 million people.

### Development assistance

For over 25 years, from 1966 to 1992, development assistance was provided by the Netherlands to Indonesia within the arrangements of the [Inter-Governmental Group on Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-Governmental_Group_on_Indonesia) (IGGI).[^cite_note-23] IGGI had been established in the late 1960s to help coordinate the flow of foreign aid to Indonesia, and was convened and chaired by the Dutch government for over two decades throughout the 1970s and 1980s. However, in the early 1990s, the then-Minister for Development Cooperation in the Netherlands, [Jan Pronk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Pronk), became increasingly critical of domestic policy in Indonesia. In response, in early 1992, the Indonesian government indicated that it no longer wished to participate in the annual IGGI meetings in The Hague and preferred that a new donor consultative group, the [Consultative Group on Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consultative_Group_on_Indonesia) (CGI), be established and be chaired by the World Bank.[^cite_note-24]

## Military relations

See also: [Military Mission for Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Mission_for_Indonesia)



[*Martadinata*-class frigate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martadinata-class_frigate) made by [Damen Schelde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damen_Schelde_Naval_Shipbuilding) and [PT PAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAL_Indonesia)

Following a period of conflict during the [Indonesian National Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution) and the [West Irian dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_New_Guinea_dispute), defense cooperation was suspended until the normalization of diplomatic relations in the 1970s. This cooperation has developed through various formal mechanisms, such as the 2014 MoU, as well as industrial projects and multinational exercises.[^cite_note-25] Today, the Indonesian military procures naval vessels from the Dutch, such as [Van Speijk-class frigates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Speijk-class_frigate) and [Sigma-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-class_design) corvettes.[^cite_note-26] The two countries also exchanged experiences in [peacekeeping operations](15652027.md) under the mandate of the [United Nations](31769.md).[^cite_note-27]

## Cultural ties



[Rijsttafel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijsttafel), the culinary link between the Netherlands and Indonesia

Traces of Dutch influences in Indonesia include [Dutch origin loanwords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_loan_words_in_Indonesian#From_Dutch) in [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language) and [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_cuisine#Foreign_influences). Some Indonesian dishes have been adopted and, in turn, influenced [Dutch cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_cuisine#Non-native_influences). The Dutch influence on [Indonesian cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_cuisine) is evident in various dishes such as [lapis legit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapis_legit) (spekkoek), [meses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagelslag), [nastar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple_tart), [kastengel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaasstengels) and [klappertaart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klappertaart). Conversely, in the Netherlands, the influence of Indonesian cuisine is very strong and has become part of the national culinary culture, particularly through dishes such as [rijsttafel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijsttafel)—which evolved from the tradition of serving Indonesian colonial dishes—as well as the popularity of [satay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satay) and [nasi goreng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasi_goreng). Indonesian restaurants are also easy to find in various cities across the Netherlands.[^cite_note-28]

[Christianity in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Indonesia) was a result of proselytisation by mainly Dutch missionaries.[^cite_note-29] There is also a sizable [Indonesian population in the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo_people). Many have set up their own churches in what has been termed as a "reverse mission," referring to the Dutch missionaries in the colonies.[^cite_note-30]

Another legacy of colonial rule in Indonesia is the legal system that was inherited from the Dutch. In 2009, the Dutch Minister of Justice [Ernst Hirsch Ballin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Hirsch_Ballin) visited Indonesia in what some considered a stepping stone to reforming its legal system.[^cite_note-31]

Through centuries of colonial relations, numbers of cultural institutions in the Netherlands—such as [Tropenmuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wereldmuseum_Amsterdam) and [Rijksmuseum voor Volkenkunde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wereldmuseum_Leiden) in [Leiden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden)—have extensive collections of Indonesian archaeology and [ethnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnology) artefacts. Both are the leading centres of Indonesian studies in Europe, specialised in its culture, history, archaeology and ethnography. The [Erasmus Huis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_House_\(Jakarta\))—the Netherlands' cultural centre—was established in 1970 in Jakarta. It was meant as cultural cooperation to promote art and cultural exchanges between Indonesia and the Netherlands. Besides many exhibitions, music performances and films screenings, some lectures on Dutch and Indonesian culture are being held on a regular basis in their auditorium and gallery.[^cite_note-32] Several universities in the Netherlands, such as [Leiden University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiden_University), have Indonesian studies centers that actively conduct research on Indonesian history, language, and culture.[^cite_note-33] In 2024, the Indonesian government officially opened Indonesia House Amsterdam in [Amsterdam](844.md) as a hub for promoting Indonesia's economy, trade, and culture in the Netherlands and Europe.[^cite_note-34]
