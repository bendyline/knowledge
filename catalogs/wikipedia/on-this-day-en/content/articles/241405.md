**Palestine**,[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-naming-32] officially the **State of Palestine**,[^cite_note-33] is a [Southern Levantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Levantine) country in [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia) comprising the [Israeli-occupied West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli-occupied_West_Bank) (including [East Jerusalem](343508.md)) and [Gaza Strip](12047.md), collectively known as the [occupied Palestinian territories](2781576.md). Israel has occupied the Palestinian territories since the [1967 war](29328.md); the West Bank is currently divided into 165 [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves) [enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves) under partial civilian rule by the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md), and [Israeli settlements](15123.md) under [Israeli law in the West Bank settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_law_in_the_West_Bank_settlements). Palestine share the vast majority of their borders with [Israel](9282173.md), with the West Bank bordering [Jordan](7515964.md) to the east and the Gaza Strip bordering [Egypt](8087628.md) to the southwest. It has a total land area of 6,020 square kilometers (2,320 sq mi) while [its population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Palestine) exceeds five million. Its [proclaimed capital](4024423.md) is [Jerusalem](16043.md), while [Ramallah](44132.md) serves as its *[de facto](43594.md)* administrative center. [Gaza](55387.md) was its largest city prior to [Israel's forced evacuations in 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_evacuations).[^cite_note-flux1-4][^cite_note-flux2-5]

Situated at [a continental crossroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levantine_corridor), the [Palestine region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)) was ruled by various empires and experienced [various demographic changes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographic_history_of_Palestine_\(region\)) from antiquity to the modern era. The region [has religious significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land). The ongoing [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md) dates back to the rise of the [Zionist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism), supported by the United Kingdom during [World War I](4764461.md). The war saw Britain occupying Palestine from the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), where it set up [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) under the auspices of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations). Increased [Jewish immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_immigration_to_Palestine) led to [intercommunal conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercommunal_conflict_in_Mandatory_Palestine) between Jews and [Palestinian Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Arab), which escalated into [a civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947%E2%80%931948_civil_war_in_Mandatory_Palestine) in 1947 after [a proposed partitioning by the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine) was rejected by the Palestinians and other [Arab nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_nations).

The [1948 Palestine war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestine_war) saw the [forcible displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_expulsion_and_flight) of a majority of the Arab population, and consequently the establishment of Israel; these events are referred to by Palestinians as the [Nakba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakba) ('catastrophe'). In the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, Israel [occupied the West Bank](59076253.md) and the [Gaza Strip](76465951.md), which had been held by [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) and [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Republic) respectively. The [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO) [declared independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Declaration_of_Independence) in 1988. In 1993, the PLO signed the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) with Israel, creating limited PLO governance in the West Bank and Gaza Strip through the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) (PA). Israel withdrew from Gaza in [its unilateral disengagement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_disengagement_from_Gaza) in 2005. In 2007, [internal divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah%E2%80%93Hamas_conflict) between political factions led to a [takeover of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gaza_\(2007\)) by [Hamas](13913.md). Since then, the West Bank has been governed in part by the [Fatah](11104.md)-led PA, while the Gaza Strip has remained under the [control of Hamas](38626632.md).

The Gaza Strip has long been subject to Israeli military occupation and an illegal [blockade](19224310.md). Israel has also constructed [large settlements](15123.md) in the [occupied West Bank](59076253.md) and East Jerusalem since 1967, which currently house more than 737,000 Israeli settlers, which are [illegal under international law](25408080.md).[^cite_note-34] Following the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) by [Hamas](13913.md) in Israel in 2023 which killed 1195 people, Israel launched [a large-scale military operation in Gaza](74998519.md) that has killed over 72,000 [Palestinians](23267.md), caused [mass population displacement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_evacuations), [a humanitarian crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_humanitarian_crisis_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)), and [a famine](75828824.md) in the Gaza Strip;[^cite_note-Dannenbaum_Dill_2024-35][^cite_note-IPC_22_August_2025-36][^cite_note-\:1-37] these acts have been recognised as constituting [genocide against the Palestinian people](75668427.md) by a growing scholarly consensus.[^cite_note-genocide2-38][^cite_note-genocide_int_law_scholars2-39][^cite_note-\:2-40]

Palestine is a permanent [non-member observer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_observers) at the [United Nations](31769.md) (UN) and is recognized as a sovereign state by [157 of the UN's 193 member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine).[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] The questions of [Palestine's borders](12999306.md), legal and diplomatic status of Jerusalem, and the [right of return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_right_of_return) of [Palestinian refugees](74681.md) remain unsolved. Some of the other challenges to Palestine include [restrictions on movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_freedom_of_movement), ineffective government and [Israeli settlements](15123.md) and [settler violence](47246246.md), as well as an overall poor security situation. Despite these challenges, the country maintains [an emerging economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Palestine) and sees frequent [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Palestine). [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic) is the official language of the country. While the majority of Palestinians [practice Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Palestine), [Christianity](5211.md) also has [a presence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Christians). Palestine is also a member of several [international organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_organizations), including the [Arab League](52625.md) and the [Organization of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md), [UNESCO](21786641.md),[^cite_note-44] and a delegation of parliamentarians sit at the [Parliamentary Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe) of the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe).[^cite_note-45]

## Etymology

Further information: [Palestine (region) § Etymology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)#Etymology), and [Timeline of the name *Palestine*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_name_Palestine)

The term "Palestine" (in Latin, *Palæstina*) comes via ancient Greek from a Semitic [toponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponym) for the general area dating back to the late second millennium BCE, a reflex of which is also to be found in the Biblical [ethnonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnonym) [Philistines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philistines). The term "Palestine" has been used to refer to the area at the southeast corner of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) beside [Syria](7515849.md). In the 5th century BCE, [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus), in his work *[The Histories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histories_\(Herodotus\))*, used the term to describe a "district of Syria, called *Palaistínē*" ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Συρίη ἡ Παλαιστίνη καλεομένη),[^cite_note-46] in which [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) interacted with other maritime peoples.[^cite_note-Masalha2018-47]

Currently, the terms "Palestine", "State of Palestine", and "occupied Palestinian territory (oPt or OPT)" are interchangeable depending on context. Specifically, the term "occupied Palestinian territory" refers as a whole to the geographical area of the Palestinian territory occupied by Israel since 1967. Palestine can, depending on contexts, be referred to as a country or a state, and its authorities can generally be identified as the [Government of Palestine](2436248.md).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

## History

Main articles: [History of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Palestine), [History of Palestinian statehood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Palestinian_statehood), and [Timeline of the name Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_the_name_Palestine)

In 1947, the UN adopted a [partition plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine) for a two-state solution in the remaining territory of the mandate. The plan was accepted by the Jewish leadership but rejected by the Arab leaders, and Britain refused to implement the plan. On the eve of final British withdrawal, the [Jewish Agency for Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Agency_for_Israel), headed by [David Ben-Gurion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ben-Gurion), declared the establishment of the [State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Israel) according to the proposed UN plan. The [Arab Higher Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Higher_Committee) did not declare a state of its own and instead, together with [Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Transjordan), [Egypt](8087628.md), and the other members of the [Arab League](52625.md) of the time, commenced military action resulting in the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War). During the war, Israel gained additional territories that were designated to be part of the Arab state under the UN plan. [Egypt occupied the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_the_Gaza_Strip_by_the_United_Arab_Republic), and [Transjordan occupied and then annexed the West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank). [Egypt](8087628.md) initially supported the creation of an [All-Palestine Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Palestine_Government) but disbanded it in 1959. Transjordan never recognized it and instead decided to incorporate the West Bank with its own territory to form [Jordan](7515964.md). The annexation was ratified in 1950 but was rejected by the international community. The [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, when Israel fought against Egypt, Jordan, and [Syria](7515849.md), ended with [Israel occupying the West Bank](59076253.md) and the Gaza Strip, besides other territories.[^cite_note-50]

In 1964, when the West Bank was controlled by Jordan, the Palestine Liberation Organization was established there with the goal to confront Israel. The [Palestinian National Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Charter) of the PLO defines the boundaries of Palestine as the whole remaining territory of the mandate, including Israel. Following the Six-Day War, the PLO moved to Jordan, but later relocated to [Lebanon](17771.md) in 1971.[^cite_note-51]

The October [1974 Arab League summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Arab_League_summit) designated the PLO as the "sole legitimate representative of the Palestinian people" and reaffirmed "the right of the Palestinian people to establish an independent national authority"[^cite_note-52] In November 1974, the PLO was recognized as competent on all matters concerning the question of Palestine by the [UN General Assembly](31957.md) granting them [observer status](31969.md#Observers_and_non-members) as a "non-state entity" at the UN.[^cite_note-GA32372-53][^cite_note-54] After the 1988 Declaration of Independence, the UN General Assembly officially acknowledged the proclamation and decided to use the designation "Palestine" instead of "Palestine Liberation Organization" in the UN.[^cite_note-GA431772-55][^cite_note-56] In spite of this decision, the PLO did not participate at the UN in its capacity of the State of Palestine's government.[^cite_note-57]

In 1978 , through the [Camp David Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_David_Accords), Egypt signaled an end to any claim of its own over the Gaza Strip. In July 1988, Jordan [ceded its claims to the West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan's_disengagement_from_the_West_Bank)—with the exception of guardianship over [Haram al-Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haram_al-Sharif)—to the PLO. In November 1988, the PLO legislature, while in exile, declared the establishment of the "State of Palestine". In the month following, it was quickly recognised by many states, including Egypt and Jordan. In the Palestinian Declaration of Independence, the State of Palestine is described as being established on the "Palestinian territory", without explicitly specifying further. Because of this, some of the countries that recognised the State of Palestine in their statements of recognition refer to the "1967 borders", thus recognizing as its territory only the [occupied Palestinian territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories), and not Israel. The UN membership application submitted by the State of Palestine also specified that it is based on the "1967 borders".[^cite_note-only1967-58] During the negotiations of the Oslo Accords, the PLO recognised Israel's right to exist, and Israel recognised the PLO as representative of the Palestinian people. The 1988 [Palestinian Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Declaration_of_Independence) included a PNC call for multilateral negotiations on the basis of [UN Security Council Resolution 242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council_Resolution_242) later known as "the Historic Compromise",[^cite_note-59] implying acceptance of a two-state solution and no longer questioning the legitimacy of the [State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Israel).[^cite_note-Quigleyp212-60]

After Israel captured and occupied the West Bank from Jordan and Gaza Strip from Egypt, it began to establish [Israeli settlements](15123.md) there. Administration of the Arab population of these territories was performed by the [Israeli Civil Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Civil_Administration) of the [Coordinator of Government Activities in the Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinator_of_Government_Activities_in_the_Territories) and by local [municipal councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_\(Palestinian_Authority\)) present since before the Israeli takeover. In 1980, Israel decided to freeze elections for these councils and to establish instead Village Leagues, whose officials were under Israeli influence. Later this model became ineffective for both Israel and the Palestinians, and the Village Leagues began to break up, with the last being the Hebron League, dissolved in February 1988.[^cite_note-61]



[Bill Clinton](3356.md), [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin) and [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat) during [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords)

In 1993, in the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords), Israel acknowledged the PLO negotiating team as "representing the Palestinian people", in return for the PLO recognizing Israel's right to exist in peace, acceptance of [UN Security Council resolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council_resolution) [242](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_242) and [338](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_338), and its rejection of "violence and terrorism".[^cite_note-62] As a result, in 1994 the PLO established the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) (PNA or PA) territorial administration, that exercises some governmental functions[^cite_note-control-16] in [parts of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_Areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord) the [West Bank](33209.md) and the [Gaza Strip](12047.md).[^cite_note-GA522502-71][^cite_note-72] In 2007, the [Hamas](13913.md) [takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gaza_\(2007\)) of Gaza Strip politically and territorially divided the Palestinians, with [Abbas](232595.md)'s [Fatah](11104.md) left largely ruling the West Bank and recognized internationally as the official [Palestinian Authority](24093.md),[^cite_note-alarabiya07012-73] while Hamas secured its [control over the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance_of_the_Gaza_Strip). In April 2011, the Palestinian parties signed an agreement of [reconciliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah%E2%80%93Hamas_conflict#Reconciliation_attempts), but its implementation had stalled[^cite_note-alarabiya07012-73] until a [unity government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_government_of_2014) was formed on 2 June 2014.[^cite_note-74]



Demonstration against road block, [Kafr Qaddum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_Qaddum), March 2012

As envisioned in the Oslo Accords, Israel allowed the PLO to establish interim administrative institutions in the Palestinian territories, which came in the form of the PNA. It was given civilian control in [Area B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves) and civilian and security control in Area A, and remained without involvement in [Area C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_C_\(West_Bank\)). In 2005, following the implementation of Israel's unilateral disengagement plan, the PNA gained full control of the Gaza Strip with the exception of its borders, airspace, and [territorial waters](496983.md).[^cite_note-control-16] Following the [inter-Palestinian conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah%E2%80%93Hamas_conflict) in 2006, Hamas took over control of the Gaza Strip (it already had majority in the PLC), and Fatah took control of the West Bank. From 2007, the Gaza Strip was [governed by](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governance_of_the_Gaza_Strip) Hamas, and the West Bank by Fatah.[^cite_note-Guardian2-75]

The Hamas led [October 7 attacks](74998317.md) on Israel in 2023 were followed by the [Gaza war](74998519.md). The war has caused widespread destruction, a [humanitarian crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_humanitarian_crisis_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)), and a [famine](75828824.md) in the Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-Dannenbaum_Dill_20242-76][^cite_note-IPC_22_August_20252-77][^cite_note-\:12-78] Most of the Palestinian population in Gaza was [forcibly displaced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Strip_evacuations).[^cite_note-Dannenbaum_Dill_20242-76] Since the start of the war, over 72,000 Palestinians in Gaza [have been killed](75130785.md), more than half of them are [women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_the_Gaza_war#Gaza_Strip) and [children](75987631.md), and more than 148,000 injured.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] Israel [has been committed genocide](75668427.md) against the [Palestinian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) during [its ongoing invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_invasion_of_the_Gaza_Strip_\(2023%E2%80%93present\)) and [bombing of the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_the_Gaza_Strip).[^cite_note-genocide22-82][^cite_note-genocide_int_law_scholars22-83][^cite_note-\:23-84] A study in *[The Lancet](463899.md)* estimated 64,260 deaths in Gaza from [traumatic injuries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traumatic_injuries) by June 2024, while noting a potentially larger death toll when "indirect" deaths are included.[^cite_note-Lancet2025-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] As of May 2025, a comparable figure for traumatic-injury deaths would be 93,000.[^cite_note-89] There was also a [spillover of the war occurring in the West Bank](75337172.md).

In an attempt to keep alive the possibility of peace and a two-state solution following the Gaza war, the UK, Australia, Canada and France have recognized a Palestinian state in late 2025.[^cite_note-90]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Palestine)



Mandatory Palestine in 1946



Located around [Nablus](233369.md), [Mount Gerizim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Gerizim) is the holiest site for [Samaritans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritans).

Areas claimed by the country, known as the Palestinian territories, lie in the [Southern Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Levant) of the [Middle East](19323.md) region. Palestine is part of the [Fertile Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertile_Crescent), along with Israel, Jordan, Lebanon, Iraq and Syria. The [Gaza Strip](12047.md) borders the Mediterranean Sea to the west, Egypt to the south, and Israel to the north and east. The West Bank is bordered by [Jordan](7515964.md) to the east, and Israel to the north, south, and west. Palestine shares its maritime borders with [Israel](9282173.md), [Egypt](8087628.md) and [Cyprus](5593.md). Thus, the two enclaves constituting the area claimed by the State of Palestine have no geographical border with one another, being separated by Israel.[^cite_note-United_Nations-2-91] These areas would constitute the world's [163rd largest country by land area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area).[^cite_note-UN_Stats-2012-19][^cite_note-United_Nations-2-91][^cite_note-Worldometers-2020-92]

Three terrestrial ecoregions are found in the area: [Eastern Mediterranean conifer–sclerophyllous–broadleaf forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean_conifer%E2%80%93sclerophyllous%E2%80%93broadleaf_forests), [Arabian Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabian_Desert), and [Mesopotamian shrub desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamian_shrub_desert).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-93] Palestine has a number of environmental issues; issues facing the Gaza Strip include [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification); [salination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosalinity) of fresh water; [sewage treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment); [water-borne diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water-borne_disease); [soil degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_degradation); and depletion and contamination of underground water resources. In the West Bank, many of the same issues apply; although fresh water is much more plentiful, access is restricted by the ongoing [dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_status_of_Palestine).[^cite_note-al_Haq-94]

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Palestine)

Palestine, which experiences hot, dry summers and cool, rainy winters, is located in the subtropical Mediterranean region on the edge of the desert.[^cite_note-\:5-95] The climate in the West Bank is mostly [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate), slightly cooler at elevated areas compared with the shoreline, west to the area. In the east, the West Bank includes much of the Judean Desert including the western shoreline of the Dead Sea, characterized by dry and hot climate. Gaza has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): BSh) with mild winters and dry hot summers.[^cite_note-96] Along the coast and mountains, the average annual rainfall ranges from 25 to 27 inches. Gaza has an annual average of roughly 16 inches of rain.[^cite_note-\:5-95]

It is one of the countries most vulnerable to climate change despite its negligible contribution to global emissions. Palestine is facing substantial environmental challenges due to its delicate environmental resources, lack of sovereignty and its limited financial assets.[^cite_note-97]

### Biodiversity

Main article: [Biodiversity of Israel and Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_of_Israel_and_Palestine)

Palestine connects [Africa](5334607.md) and [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) and features a rich [flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora) and [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna) shaped by its geological history and varied topography. It encompasses 5 ecological zones (Central Highlands, Semi-coastal Region, Eastern Slopes, [Jordan Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Rift_Valley), and Coastal Regions) and 5 phytogeographical areas (Coastal, Mediterranean, [Irano-Turanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irano-Turanian_Region), [Saharo-Arabian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharo-Arabian_region), and Sudanese/Ethiopian). A total of 373 bird species belonging to 23 orders, 69 families, 21 subfamilies, and 172 genera have been recorded in the occupied Palestinian areas. Historically, these mild climate conditions and rich natural resources allowed early human populations to transition from hunter-gatherers to agricultural and nomadic shepherd communities.[^cite_note-\:4-98]

Despite its ecological significance, local biodiversity faces severe pressure from habitat fragmentation, desertification, industrial pollution, and ongoing colonization. 81% of designated protected areas lie within Area C under Israeli military rule. The annual flow of the [Jordan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_River) has plummeted from 1,250 million cubic meters (mcm) per year to less than 20 mcm. Factors such as settlement expansion, military zoning, wastewater dumping, and land confiscation have fragmented habitats and compromised native ecosystems.[^cite_note-\:4-98]

Palestine operates a [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) and [semi-presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system) system of government.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] The country consists of the institutions that are associated with the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO), which includes the [president of the State of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_State_of_Palestine),[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-PLOchair-15] who is appointed by the [Palestinian Central Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Council),[^cite_note-p2008-104] the [Palestinian National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Council), and the [Executive Committee of the Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Committee_of_the_Palestine_Liberation_Organization), which performs the functions of a government in exile,[^cite_note-A67L28-102][^cite_note-GiE-103][^cite_note-unescogie-105][^cite_note-106] maintaining an extensive foreign-relations network. The PLO is combination of several political parties.

These should be distinguished from the [president of the Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Palestinian_National_Authority), Palestinian Legislative Council, and [PNA Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Government), all of which are instead associated with the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) (PNA). Palestine's founding document is the Palestinian Declaration of Independence,[^cite_note-declaration1988-13] which should be distinguished from the unrelated PLO [Palestinian National Covenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Covenant) and PNA [Palestine Basic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Basic_Law).

The State of Palestine is divided into [sixteen administrative divisions](5811262.md). The governorates in the West Bank are grouped into [three areas per the Oslo II Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_Areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord). More than 99% of Area C is off-limits to Palestinians, due to security concerns, and is a point of ongoing negotiation.[^cite_note-WBAUS2922-109][^cite_note-110] In the occupied West Bank, including East Jerusalem, there are over 750,000 Israeli settlers living in 147 settlements and outposts.[^cite_note-111] Although Area C is under [martial law](50477.md), Israelis living there are entitled to [full civic rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_law_in_the_West_Bank_settlements).[^cite_note-CERD1416-112] [Palestinian enclaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves) currently under Palestinian administration in red (Areas A and B; not including Gaza Strip, which is under Hamas rule).

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Palestine)

See also: [List of diplomatic missions of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_Palestine) and [List of diplomatic missions in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_in_Palestine)



Nations with which Palestine has diplomatic relations.

Representation of the State of Palestine is performed by the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO). In states that recognise the State of Palestine it maintains [embassies](8970.md). The Palestine Liberation Organization is represented in various international organizations as member, associate or observer. Because of inconclusiveness in sources in some cases it is impossible to distinguish whether the participation is executed by the PLO as representative of the State of Palestine, by the PLO as a non-state entity or by the PNA.

On 15 December 1988, the State of Palestine's [declaration of independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Declaration_of_Independence) of November 1988 was acknowledged in the General Assembly with [Resolution 43/177](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolution_43/177).[^cite_note-117]

On 29 November 2012,[^cite_note-GA11317-118] [UN General Assembly resolution 67/19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_General_Assembly_resolution_67/19) passed, upgrading Palestine to "[non-member observer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observer_status)" status in the United Nations.[^cite_note-A67L282-119][^cite_note-AljazeeraNmChng-120] The change in status was described as "*[de facto](43594.md)* recognition of the [sovereign state](23604120.md) of Palestine".[^cite_note-UNStatehoodBid2012accepted2-121]

In 2013 the Swedish Parliament upgraded the status of the Palestinian representative office in the country to full embassy status.[^cite_note-122] On 3 October 2014, new [Swedish](5058739.md) Prime Minister [Stefan Löfven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_L%C3%B6fven) used his inaugural address in parliament to announce that Sweden would recognise the state of Palestine. The official decision to do so was made on 30 October, making Sweden the first EU member state outside of the former communist bloc to recognise the state of Palestine. In February 2015, Mahmoud Abbas visited Sweden to open the new embassy, and Löfven said "According to our view, Palestine is from now on a state."[^cite_note-123] As of 2025, 16 of the EU's 27 member states recognise Palestine.[^cite_note-124]

On 13 October 2014, the UK House of Commons voted by 274 to 12 in favour of recognising Palestine as a state.[^cite_note-125] The House of Commons backed the move "as a contribution to securing a negotiated two-state solution"—although less than half of MPs took part in the vote. However, the UK government is not bound to do anything as a result of the vote: its current policy is that it "reserves the right to recognise a Palestinian state bilaterally at the moment of our choosing and when it can best help bring about peace".[^cite_note-126]

On 2 December 2014, the [French parliament](299616.md) voted by 339 to 151 in favour of urging their government to recognise Palestine as a state. The text, proposed by the ruling Socialists and backed by left-wing parties and some conservatives, asked the government to "use the recognition of a Palestinian state with the aim of resolving the conflict definitively".[^cite_note-127]

On 31 December 2014, the United Nations Security Council voted down a resolution demanding the end of Israeli occupation and statehood by 2017. Eight members voted for the Resolution (Russia, China, France, Argentina, Chad, Chile, Jordan, Luxembourg), however following strenuous US and Israeli efforts to defeat the resolution,[^cite_note-128] it did not get the minimum of nine votes needed to pass the resolution. Australia and the United States voted against the resolution, with five other nations abstaining.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]



Children waving a Palestinian flag, West Bank.

On 16 January 2015, the [International Criminal Court](14880.md) announced that, since Palestine was granted observer State status in the UN by the UNGA, it must be considered a "State" for the purposes of accession to the [Rome Statute](68762.md).[^cite_note-132]

On 13 May 2015, the [Vatican](13393.md) announced it was shifting recognition from the PLO to the State of Palestine, confirming recognition of Palestine as a state after the UN vote of 2012.[^cite_note-133] Monsignor Antoine Camilleri, Vatican foreign minister, said the change was in line with the evolving position of the Holy See, which has referred unofficially to the State of Palestine since [Pope Francis](1687680.md)'s visit to the Holy Land in May 2014.[^cite_note-134]

On 23 December 2015, the UN General Assembly adopted a resolution demanding Palestinian sovereignty over the natural resources in the Palestinian territories under Israeli occupation. It called on Israel to desist from the exploitation, damage, cause of loss or depletion and endangerment of Palestinian natural resources, the right of Palestinians to seek restitution for extensive destruction. The motion was passed by 164 votes to 5, with Canada, [Federated States of Micronesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_States_of_Micronesia), Israel, [Marshall Islands](19147.md), and the United States opposing.[^cite_note-135]

As of 23 September 2025, 157 (81.3%) of the 193 member states of the United Nations have recognised the State of Palestine. Many of the countries that do not recognise the State of Palestine nevertheless recognise the PLO as the "representative of the [Palestinian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people)". The PLO's [executive committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Committee_of_the_Palestine_Liberation_Organization) is empowered by the PNC to perform the functions of government of the State of Palestine.[^cite_note-GiE2-136]

### Status and recognition

Main articles: [Legal status of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_status_of_Palestine) and [Palestine and the United Nations](13444990.md)

The Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO) declared the establishment of the State of Palestine on 15 November 1988. There is a wide range of views on the legal status of the State of Palestine, both among international states and legal scholars.[^cite_note-137] The existence of a state of Palestine is recognized by the states that have established bilateral diplomatic relations with it.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139] In January 2015, the International Criminal Court affirmed Palestine's "State" status after its UN observer recognition,[^cite_note-140] a move condemned by Israeli leaders as a form of "diplomatic terrorism".[^cite_note-141]

In December 2015, the UN General Assembly passed a resolution demanding Palestinian sovereignty over natural resources in the occupied territories.[^cite_note-142] It called on Israel to cease exploitation and damage while granting Palestinians the right to seek restitution. In 1988, the State of Palestine's declaration of independence was acknowledged by the General Assembly with [Resolution 43/177](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolution_43/177).[^cite_note-143] In 2012, the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) passed [Resolution 67/19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolution_67/19), granting Palestine "[non-member observer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-member_observer_state)" status, effectively recognizing it as a [sovereign state](23604120.md).[^cite_note-A67L28-102][^cite_note-GA113172-144]

In August 2015, Palestine's representatives at the United Nations presented a draft resolution that would allow the non-member observer states Palestine and the Holy See to raise their flags at the United Nations headquarters. Initially, the Palestinians presented their initiative as a joint effort with the Holy See, which the Holy See denied.[^cite_note-i24_un_flag-145] In a letter to the Secretary General and the President of the General Assembly, Israel's Ambassador at the UN [Ron Prosor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Prosor) called the step "another cynical misuse of the UN ... in order to score political points".[^cite_note-146] After the vote, which was passed by 119 votes to 8 with 45 countries abstaining,[^cite_note-cnnUN-147][^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] the US Ambassador [Samantha Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Power) said that "raising the Palestinian flag will not bring Israelis and Palestinians any closer together".[^cite_note-150] US Department of State spokesman [Mark Toner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Toner) called it a "counterproductive" attempt to pursue statehood claims outside of a negotiated settlement.[^cite_note-aj_un_flag-151]

At the ceremony itself, UN Secretary-General [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) said the occasion was a "day of pride for the Palestinian people around the world, a day of hope",[^cite_note-152] and declared "Now is the time to restore confidence by both Israelis and Palestinians for a peaceful settlement and, at last, the realization of two states for two peoples."[^cite_note-cnnUN-147]

### International recognition

Main article: [International recognition of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine)



&#x20; State of Palestine

&#x20; Countries that have recognized Palestine

[^cite_note-153]

&#x20; Countries that have not recognized Palestine

The State of Palestine has been [recognized by 157 of the 193 UN members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine) and since 2012 has had a status of a [non-member observer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_observers) in the United Nations.[^cite_note-UNStatehoodBid2012accepted-154][^cite_note-Charbonneau-155][^cite_note-Lederer-156] This limited status is largely due to the fact that the [United States](3434750.md), a permanent member of the [UN Security Council](31956.md) with veto power, has consistently used its veto or threatened to do so to block Palestine's full UN membership.[^cite_note-United_Nations-1][^cite_note-Associated_Press_News-157]

On 29 November 2012, in a 138–9 vote (with 41 abstentions and 5 absences),[^cite_note-GA113172-144] the United Nations General Assembly passed [resolution 67/19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_resolution_67/19), upgrading Palestine from an "observer entity" to a "[non-member observer state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observer_status)" within the [United Nations System](216034.md), which was described as recognition of the PLO's sovereignty.[^cite_note-Charbonneau-155][^cite_note-Lederer-156][^cite_note-158][^cite_note-A67L28-102][^cite_note-AljazeeraNmChng2-159] Palestine's UN status is equivalent to that of the [Holy See](13393.md).[^cite_note-Hume-160]  The UN has permitted Palestine to title its representative office to the UN as "The Permanent Observer Mission of the State of Palestine to the United Nations".[^cite_note-un.int-161] Palestine has instructed its diplomats to officially represent "The State of Palestine"—no longer the Palestinian National Authority.[^cite_note-AljazeeraNmChng2-159]

On 17 December 2012, UN Chief of Protocol Yeocheol Yoon declared that "the designation of 'State of Palestine' shall be used by the Secretariat in all official United Nations documents",[^cite_note-Gharib-162] thus recognizing the title 'State of Palestine' as the state's official name for all UN purposes. On 21 December 2012, a UN memorandum discussed appropriate terminology to be used following GA 67/19. It was noted therein that there was no legal impediment to using the designation Palestine to refer to the geographical area of the Palestinian territory. At the same time, it was explained that there was also no bar to the continued use of the term "Occupied Palestinian Territory including East Jerusalem" or such other terminology as might customarily be used by the Assembly.[^cite_note-163]

As of 23 September 2025, 157 (81.3%) of the 193 [member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_state) of the United Nations have [recognized the State of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_Palestine).[^cite_note-A67L28-102][^cite_note-164] Many of the countries that do not recognize the State of Palestine nevertheless recognize the PLO as the "representative of the [Palestinian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people)". The PLO's [Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Committee_of_the_Palestine_Liberation_Organization) is empowered by the [Palestinian National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Council) to perform the functions of government of the State of Palestine.[^cite_note-GiE-103]

On 2 April 2024, [Riyad Mansour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riyad_Mansour), the Palestinian ambassador to the UN, requested that the Security Council consider a renewed application for membership. As of April, seven UNSC members recognize Palestine but the US has indicated that it opposes the request and in addition, US law stipulates that US funding for the UN would be cut off in the event of full recognition without an Israeli–Palestinian agreement.[^cite_note-165] On 18 April, the US vetoed a widely supported UN resolution that would have admitted Palestine as a full UN member.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]

A May 2024 [UNGA resolution came into force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_ES-10/23) with the 2024 general assembly. The resolution, which recognized the Palestinian right to become a full member state, also granted the right to Palestinians to submit proposals and amendments and Palestine was permitted to take a seat with other member states in the assembly.[^cite_note-169][^cite_note-170]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Palestine)

The State of Palestine's overall gross-domestic-product (GDP) declined by 35% in the first quarter of 2024, due to the ongoing war in Gaza, the Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics (PCBS) reports.[^cite_note-pcbs.gov.ps-174][^cite_note-175]

### Agriculture

Main article: [Agriculture in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Palestine)



Palestinian farmers [harvesting olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_production_in_Palestine) using traditional methods.

Agriculture is a mainstay in the [economy of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Palestine). The production of agricultural goods supports the population's sustenance needs and fuels Palestine's export economy. According to the Council for European Palestinian Relations, the agricultural sector formally employs 13.4% of the population and accounts for 90% of informal employment[^cite_note-176] In the late 2000s [unemployment rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment_rate) in Palestine increased, and the agricultural sector became the most impoverished sector in Palestine. Unemployment rates peaked in 2008 when they reached 41% in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md).[^cite_note-177]

According to [World Bank](36752.md), Palestinian agriculture suffers from widespread use of land for nature reserves as well as military and [settler](15123.md) use.[^cite_note-178] Because the root of the conflict is with land, the disputes between Israel and Palestine are well-manifested in the agriculture of Palestine.

### Water supply and sanitation

Main article: [Water supply and sanitation in Palestine](21378125.md)

See also: [Water, Sanitation and Hygiene Monitoring Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water,_Sanitation_and_Hygiene_Monitoring_Program)

Water supply and sanitation in the Palestinian territories are characterized by severe water shortage and are highly influenced by the Israeli occupation. The water resources of Palestine are partially controlled by Israel, due in part to historical and geographical complexities, with Israel granting partial autonomy in 2017.[^cite_note-179] The division of groundwater is subject to provisions in the [Oslo II Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II_Accord), agreed upon by both Israeli and Palestinian leadership.[^cite_note-180] Israel provides the Palestinian territories water from its own water supply and desalinated water supplies, in 2012 supplying 52 [MCM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Million_cubic_metre).[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182]

Generally, the water quality is considerably worse in the Gaza Strip when compared to the West Bank. About a third to half of the delivered water in the Palestinian territories is [lost in the distribution network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-revenue_water). The lasting blockade of the Gaza Strip and the [Gaza War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%9309\)) have caused severe damage to the infrastructure in the Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-UN_Gaza-183][^cite_note-184] Concerning wastewater, the existing treatment plants do not have the capacity to treat all of the produced wastewater, causing severe water pollution.[^cite_note-185] The development of the sector highly depends on external financing.[^cite_note-186]

### Energy

Main article: [Energy in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_Palestine)

Palestine produces no oil or natural gas, and is predominantly dependent on the [Israel Electric Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Electric_Corporation) (IEC) for electricity.[^cite_note-hen-187][^cite_note-good-188] Between 2000 and 2023, Palestine's energy consumption grew from 6TWh to 24TWh. In the same period, generation of energy in Palestine grew from 0.03TWh (approximately 0.5% of consumption) to 1.02TWh (4%).[^cite_note-\:22-189] The only domestic source of energy is the disputed [Gaza Marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Marine) gas field, which has not yet been developed.[^cite_note-good-188] According to [UNCTAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNCTAD), the Palestinian Territory "lies above sizeable reservoirs of oil and natural gas wealth" but "occupation continues to prevent Palestinians from developing their energy fields so as to exploit and benefit from such assets."[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191] In 2012, [electricity](9540.md) available in the [West Bank](33209.md) and [Gaza](12047.md) was 5,370 [GW-hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt_hour) (3,700 in the West Bank and 1,670 in Gaza), while the annual per capita consumption of electricity (after deducting transmission loss) was 950 kWh.[^cite_note-192] National sources only produce 445 GWh of electricity, supplying less than 10% of demand.[^cite_note-Pal-193]

### Oil and gas

Main article: [Natural gas in the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_in_the_Gaza_Strip)

The reserves of [natural gas](22131.md) were found offshore in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) in 2000, within the framework of licensing to [British Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Gas) by the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md).[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195] The discovered Gaza Marine gas field, though mediocre in size, had been considered at the time as one of the possible drives to boost Palestinian economy and promote regional cooperation.

With the [Hamas takeover of the Gaza Strip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gaza_\(2007\)) in 2007, the chances for developing the gas field reduced, due to a stand-off with the Palestinian administration and Israel's refusal to deal with Hamas.[^cite_note-196] The chances further diminished with the discovery of major gas fields in the Israeli economic waters in 2009 and 2010, making Israel an unlikely customer for the Palestinian gas. In 2023 Israel has given preliminary approval for the development of the Gaza Marine gas field, with the involvement of the Palestinian Authority and Egypt,[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198] but, as of 2024, Gaza's natural gas was still underwater.

### Transport

Main article: [Transport in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Palestine)



King Faisal Street in [Hebron](38577.md)

The transportation consists of two non-contiguous territories, the [West Bank](33209.md) and the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), different parts of which are administered by [Palestinian Authority](24093.md), [Gaza Strip](12047.md) and [Israel](9282173.md).

In 2010, the West Bank and Gaza Strip together had 4,686 km (2,912 mi) of roadways.[^cite_note-CIA-199]

The [Port of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Gaza) is a small port near the [Rimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimal) district of [Gaza City](55387.md).[^cite_note-200] It is the home port of Palestinian fishing-boats and the base of the [Palestinian Naval Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Naval_Police), a branch of the [Palestinian National Security Forces](5844475.md). Under the [Oslo II Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II_Accord), the activities of the Palestinian Naval Police are restricted to 6 nautical miles from the coast.[^cite_note-201] The Port of Gaza has been under [naval blockade](19224310.md#Naval_blockade) since 2007, and activities at the port have been restricted to small-scale fishing.

There were three defunct airports in the Palestinian territories. The [Yasser Arafat International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat_International_Airport) (previously called Gaza International Airport), located in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md). The airport was closed in October 2000 by Israeli order after the breakout of the [Second Intifada](71717.md).[^cite_note-202]

West Bank Palestinians traveling abroad can use the [Allenby Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allenby_Bridge) to enter Jordan and then use the [Queen Alia International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Alia_International_Airport) in [Amman](45007.md), or the [Eilat-Ramon Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramon_Airport) in [Be'er Ora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Be'er_Ora) to fly abroad.[^cite_note-203] The bridge is currently the sole designated exit/entry point for West Bank Palestinians traveling abroad.[^cite_note-IAA-204]

The [Erez Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erez_Crossing) is the only crossing on the [Gaza–Israel barrier](478337.md) between the Gaza Strip and Israel, and has been affected by the Israeli [blockade of the Gaza Strip](19224310.md).

The [Rafah Crossing](10475618.md) is the only land crossing between the Gaza Strip and [Egypt](8087628.md). There exists also the [Kerem Shalom border crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerem_Shalom_border_crossing), managed by Israel, but used exclusively for the crossing of goods from Egypt into Gaza. The [Karni crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karni_crossing) was a [cargo terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Container_terminal) on the [Israel-Gaza Strip barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel-Gaza_Strip_barrier) located in the north-eastern end of the Gaza Strip to allow Palestinian merchants to export and import goods. This was done as a 'back-to-back' transfer, meaning that Palestinian products meant for export were removed from a Palestinian truck and placed in an Israeli truck, or vice versa for incoming goods.[^cite_note-205] Israel closed the Karni crossing after Hamas took over the Gaza Strip in June 2007, and the closure was made permanent at the end of March 2011.[^cite_note-206]

### Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Palestine)



Al Mashtal Hotel in Gaza, before the [Gaza war](74998519.md).



Jerusalem is holy for three of the world's major religions — Christianity, Islam and Judaism.

In 2010, 4.6 million people visited the Palestinian territories, compared to 2.6 million in 2009. Of that number, 2.2 million were foreign tourists, while 2.7 million were domestic.[^cite_note-PCBS-207] Most tourists come for only a few hours or as part of a day trip itinerary. In the last quarter of 2012 over 150,000 guests stayed in West Bank hotels. 40% were European and 9% were from the United States and Canada.[^cite_note-208] [Lonely Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonely_Planet) travel guide writes that "the West Bank is not the easiest place in which to travel but the effort is richly rewarded."[^cite_note-209]

In 2013, Palestinian Authority Tourism minister Rula Ma'aya stated that her government aims to encourage international visits to Palestine, but the occupation is the main factor preventing the tourism sector from becoming a major income source to Palestinians.[^cite_note-210] There are no visa conditions imposed on foreign nationals other than those imposed by the visa policy of Israel. Access to Jerusalem, the West Bank, and Gaza is completely controlled by the [government of Israel](5006115.md). Entry to the occupied Palestinian territories requires only a valid [international passport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_passport).[^cite_note-211]

### Communications

Main article: [Communications in Palestine](26308793.md)

The high tech industry in Palestine, have experienced good growth since 2008. In 2020, the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (PCBS) and the [Ministry of Telecom and Information Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Telecommunications_and_Digital_Economy_\(Palestine\)) said there were 4.2 million cellular mobile subscribers in Palestine compared to 2.6 million at the end of 2010. The number of ADSL subscribers in Palestine increased to about 363 thousand by the end of 2019, from 119 thousand over the same period. In 2020, 97% of Palestinian households had at least one cellular mobile line. At least one smartphone is owned by 86% of households (91% in the West Bank and 78% in Gaza Strip). About 80% of the Palestinian households have access to the internet in their homes and about a third have a computer.[^cite_note-4-2020-212]

In June 2020, the World Bank approved a US\$15 million grant for the Technology for Youth and Jobs (*TechStart*) Project aiming to help the Palestinian IT sector upgrade the capabilities of firms and create more high-quality jobs. Kanthan Shankar, World Bank Country Director for West Bank and Gaza said "The IT sector has the potential to make a strong contribution to economic growth. It can offer opportunities to Palestinian youth, who constitute 30% of the population and suffer from acute unemployment."[^cite_note-213]

### Financial services

Main article: [Taxation in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Palestine)

The [Palestine Monetary Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Monetary_Authority) has issued guidelines for the operation and provision of electronic payment services including e-wallet and prepaid cards.[^cite_note-214]

### Impact of Israeli policies

The [Gaza War](74998519.md) and [Israeli incursions in the West Bank during the Gaza war](75337172.md) caused a severe economic contraction. In the West Bank, the gross domestic product (GDP) shrank by more than 17%, regressing to 2014 levels, with per capita GDP returning to 2008 levels. Gaza's economy collapsed with GDP contracting to one-eighth of its levels in 2022. The cumulative losses incurred as of 2024 were estimated by [UNCTAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNCTAD) to be \$170 billion, representing 17 times West Bank's 2024 GDP.[^cite_note-215]

### Ethnicity

Main article: [Palestinians](23267.md)

Palestinians represent a highly homogenized community who share a [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_culture) and [ethnic identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_identity),[^cite_note-221] and close [linguistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Arabic) and cultural ties with other [Levantine Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levantine_Arabic).

In 1919, [Palestinian Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Palestine) and [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Christians) constituted 90 percent of the population of Palestine, just before the [third wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Aliyah) of [Jewish immigration](351513.md) and the setting up of British [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) after [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-222][^cite_note-223] Opposition to Jewish immigration spurred the consolidation of [a unified national identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_identity), though Palestinian society was still fragmented by regional, class, religious, and family differences.[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225] The history of the Palestinian national identity is a disputed issue amongst scholars.[^cite_note-Likhovski-226][^cite_note-Gelvin2014-227] For some, the term "[Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Definitions_of_Palestinian)" is used to refer to the nationalist concept of a Palestinian people by Palestinian Arabs from the late 19th century and in the pre-World War I period, while others assert the Palestinian identity encompasses the heritage of all eras from pre-[biblical times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_ancient_Israel_and_Judah) up to the [Ottoman period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria).[^cite_note-palestineeb-228][^cite_note-Lewis-229][^cite_note-Khalidip18-230] After the [Israeli Declaration of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence), the [1948 Palestinian expulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_expulsion_and_flight), and more so after the [1967 Palestinian exodus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Palestinian_exodus), the term "Palestinian" evolved into a sense of a shared future in the form of aspirations for a [Palestinian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_State_of_Palestine).[^cite_note-palestineeb-228] Though the concept of Palestinian citizenship for the purpose of international law has been revived, the *[in fieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_fieri)* [realization of self-determination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_self-determination) is still insufficient, thus Palestinians remain over the threshold of eligibility to receive international protection as refugees and stateless persons.[^cite_note-231]

### Language

Main article: [Languages of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Palestine)

[Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) is the official language of the State of Palestine, specifically the [Palestinian Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Arabic) dialect which is commonly spoken by the local population. [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language) and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) are also widely spoken. Around 16% of the population consists of Israeli settlers, whose primary language is typically Hebrew. Many Palestinians use Hebrew as a second or third language.[^cite_note-232]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Palestine)

The culture has influenced by the many diverse cultures and religions which have existed in the historical [region of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)). The cultural and linguistic heritage of [Palestinian Arabs](23267.md) along with Lebanese, Syrians, and Jordanians is integral part of Levantine Arab culture.[^cite_note-246] Palestinians also have their own dialect of Arabic, the [Palestinian dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Arabic).

Cultural contributions to the fields of [art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art), [literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literature), [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music), [costume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costume) and [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine) express the Palestinian identity despite the geographical separation between the Palestinians from the [Palestinian territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_territories), [Palestinian citizens of Israel](492331.md) and [Palestinians in the diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_diaspora).[^cite_note-Elmokadem-247][^cite_note-Moran-248]

### Architecture

Main article: [Architecture of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Palestine)



The [Dome of the Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock) in [Jerusalem](16043.md).

The architecture which covers a vast historical time frame and a number of different styles and influences over the ages. The urban architecture of the region of [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)) prior to 1850 was relatively sophisticated. While it belonged to greater geographical and cultural context of the [Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant) and the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), it constituted a distinct tradition, "significantly different from the traditions of [Syria](7515849.md), [Jordan](7515964.md), [Lebanon](17771.md) and [Egypt](8087628.md)." Nonetheless Palestinian townhouse shared in the same basic conceptions regarding the arrangement of living space and apartment types commonly seen throughout the [Eastern Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Mediterranean). The rich diversity and underlying unity of the architectural culture of this wider region stretching from the [Balkans](4829.md) to [North Africa](21714.md) was a function of the exchange fostered by the [caravans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravan_\(travellers\)) of the [trade routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_routes), and the extension of [Ottoman rule](22278.md) over most of this area, beginning in the early 16th century through until the end of [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-Fuchsp173-249]

### Art, music, and clothing

Main articles: [Palestinian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_art), [Music of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Palestine), and [Palestinian traditional costumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_traditional_costumes)



Dome of the Rock mosaic art

Palestinian art have been originating from [historic Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)), as well as [paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Painting), [posters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poster), [installation art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Installation_art), [costumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_traditional_costumes), and [handcrafts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handicraft) produced by [Palestinian](23267.md) artists in modern and contemporary times.

Similar to the structure of Palestinian society, the Palestinian art field extends over four main geographic centers: the modern-day nation of Palestine (consisting of the [West Bank](33209.md) and [Gaza Strip](12047.md)), [Israel](9282173.md), the [Palestinian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_diaspora) in the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), and [Europe](9239.md) and the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-Zvi-250]

[Contemporary Palestinian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_Palestinian_art) finds its roots in [folk art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_art) and traditional [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_art) and [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic) painting popular in Palestine over the ages. After the [Nakba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakba) of 1948, [nationalistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalistic) themes have predominated as Palestinian artists use diverse media to express and explore their connection to identity and land.[^cite_note-Ankori-251]

The most popular recorded musicians at the time were in [Arab classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_classical_music), especially [Umm Kulthum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_Kulthum) and [Sayed Darwish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayed_Darwish). The centers for Palestinian music were in the [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) towns of [Nazareth](21702.md) and [Haifa](55606.md), where performers composed in the classical styles of [Cairo](6293.md) and [Damascus](8914.md). A shared Palestinian identity was reflected in a new wave of performers who emerged with distinctively Palestinian themes, relating to the dreams of statehood and the burgeoning [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism) sentiment.

In the 1970s, a new wave of popular Palestinian stars emerged, including [Sabreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabreen), Mustafa Al-Kurd and Al Ashiqeen. After the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada) (1987), a more hard-edged group of performers and songwriters emerged, such as al- Funoun, Suhail Khoury, Jameel al-Sayih, Thaer Barghouti's Doleh song and Sabreen's Mawt a'nabi.

In the 1990s, the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) was established, and Palestinian cultural expression began to stabilize. Other performers to emerge later in the included Yuad, Washem, [Mohsen Subhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohsen_Subhi), Adel Salameh, [Issa Boulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issa_Boulos), [Wissam Joubran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wissam_Joubran), Samir Joubran, and Basel Zayed with his new sound of Palestine and Turab group founded in 2004 with the CD *Hada Liel*.

The Diaspora Palestinian [Reem Kelani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reem_Kelani) is one of the foremost present day researchers and performers of music with a specifically Palestinian narrative and heritage.[^cite_note-252] Her 2006 debut solo album *Sprinting Gazelle – Palestinian Songs from the Motherland and the Diaspora* comprised Kelani's research and arrangement of five traditional Palestinian songs, whilst the other five songs were her own musical settings of popular and resistance poetry by the likes of Mahmoud Darwish, [Salma Khadra Jayyusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salma_Khadra_Jayyusi), [Rashid Husain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Hussein) and Mahmoud Salim al-Hout.[^cite_note-253] All the songs on the album relate to [pre-1948 Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine).

Although the popular music was limited to the genre of folk music that served the needs of ritual and social events varied, but the beginnings of a serious musical phenomenon began to form in Palestine with the presence of profound composers of the first generation, such as Augustin Lama, Yousef Khasho, Salvador Arnita and others. The second generation of composers included among others, Patrick Lama, Amin Nasser, Nasri Fernando Dueri, and Saleem Zoughbi. The third generation includes younger musicians such as, [Habib Hasan Touma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habib_Hasan_Touma), Mounir Anastas, Bichara El Khail and Sam Gebran.&#x20;

At the same time, Palestinian classical music continued to develop, with the emergence of new composers and performers and the establishment of major institutions and ensembles. These include the [Edward Said National Conservatory of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Said_National_Conservatory_of_Music), which supports the Palestinian Youth Orchestra; and the [West–Eastern Divan Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West%E2%80%93Eastern_Divan_Orchestra), founded by [Daniel Barenboim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Barenboim) and [Edward Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Said). Alongside these, the growth of chamber ensembles, such as string quartets and quintets, gave the Palestinian Territories the highest number of orchestras among Arab countries.

Palestinian [war songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_songs) prominently feature references to resistance against the occupation of Palestine, calling on Palestinians to fight the occupation and stay in their land, and describing historic events.[^cite_note-254][^cite_note-255][^cite_note-256]

The biggest moments in Palestinian music is [Mohammad Assaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Assaf) (born 1989) won the [second season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Idol_season_2) of the competition [Arab Idol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Idol) in 2013. With one of the biggest voter turn outs of all time, he earned support from Palestinians and the Arab world. He would go on to make huge records such as *Ana Dammi Falastini* (my blood is Palestinian), a national and international treasure. It was one of the highest selling Palestinian artists of all time.

Modern couching stitch from [Beit Jalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Jalla) traditionally used on panels of *malak* wedding dress

The traditional clothing is historically and still presently worn by [Palestinians](23267.md) in the 19th and early 20th centuries often have the rich variety of the costumes worn, particularly by the [fellaheen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellaheen) or village women. Many of the handcrafted garments were richly embroidered and the creation and maintenance of these items played a significant role in the lives of the region's women.

In 1940s, traditional Palestinian clothing reflected a woman's economic and marital status and her town or district of origin, with knowledgeable observers discerning this information from the [fabric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabric), colours, [cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_\(clothing\)), and [embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery) motifs (or lack thereof) used in the apparel.[^cite_note-Grutz-257]

In 2021, The [art of embroidery in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatreez), practices, skills, knowledge and rituals was inscribed on the UNESCO [Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_Lists).[^cite_note-embroi-258]

### Cinema

Main article: [Cinema of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Palestine)

Palestinian cinema began in 1935 when Ibrahim Hassan Sirhan produced a twenty-minute film that chronicled Saudi Prince [Saud's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Saud) visit to [Jerusalem](16043.md) and [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa). Four major periods that correspond to the main stages of the Palestinian national struggle are used by historians to trace the development of Palestinian filmmaking.[^cite_note-\:6-261]

- **The First Period (1935–1948)**: The early stages of Palestinian filmmaking before the 1948 war ([Nakba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakba)) and the Palestinian people's mass exodus.

  [^cite_note-\:6-261]

* **The Second Period (1948–1967)**: Known as the "Epoch of Silence" this nearly two-decade period saw a near-complete cessation of domestic film production.

  [^cite_note-\:6-261]

- **The Third Period (1968–1982)**: Also referred to as the "Cinema of the Palestinian Revolution" or "Cinema of the Palestinian Organizations" it was produced in exile, mostly in Beirut, Lebanon, with support from the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO) and other Palestinian institutions after the [1967 war](29328.md).

  [^cite_note-\:6-261]

* **The Fourth Period (1980–present)**: Known as "Individualistic Cinema" or "Independent Cinema," it is defined by the individual efforts of filmmakers working independently in the homeland and diaspora without institutional support.

  [^cite_note-\:6-261]
