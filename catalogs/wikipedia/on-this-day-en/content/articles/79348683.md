The **Sudan Founding Alliance**[^cite_note-1] also known as **Tasis** or **Tasees**,[^cite_note-2] is an alliance of [Sudanese](27421.md) anti-governmental political factions and paramilitary forces formed in February 2025 during the [Sudanese civil war (2023–present)](73556699.md).

## Background

Following the [ousting](60469131.md) of longtime [president](450116.md) [Omar al-Bashir](393625.md) in April 2019, Sudan entered a period of [political transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudanese_transition_to_democracy_\(2019%E2%80%932021\)) which abruptly ended with a further [coup](69110231.md) by the [Sudanese Armed Forces](27429.md) led by [Abdel Fattah al-Burhan](60480545.md) in October 2021. A [civil war](73556699.md) between the [Sudanese Armed Forces](27429.md) (SAF) led by al-Burhan and the paramilitary [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) (RSF) led by [Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Hamdan_Dagalo) (Hemedti) broke out in April 2023. As Chairman of the [Transitional Sovereignty Council](61549039.md), Al-Burhan announced plans to form a new [transitional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_government_of_Sudan_\(2025%E2%80%93present\)) and amended Sudan's transitional constitution to remove references to the RSF and the [Forces of Freedom and Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forces_of_Freedom_and_Change) in February 2025.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Sudan Founding Charter

### Drafting

The Chairman of the Transitional Sovereignty Council, Abdel Fattah al-Burhan, announced plans for the formation of a civilian led [transitional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transitional_government_of_Sudan_\(2025%E2%80%93present\)) on 10 February 2025.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] In response, the RSF held a meeting at the [Kenyatta International Convention Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyatta_International_Convention_Centre) in [Nairobi](21482.md), [Kenya](188171.md) on 18 February 2025 to establish a Sudan Founding Alliance and draft a **Sudan Founding Charter**[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] to pave the way for the formation of a rival [Government of Peace and Unity](79260861.md) to administer territory under RSF control.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] The signing of the charter was later postponed, initially to 21 February 2025 [^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] and it was finally signed at 2am on 23 February 2025 at the Edge Convention Centre in Nairobi.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

### Signatories

In addition to the Rapid Support Forces, the charter was also supported by 23 political factions including [Abdelaziz al-Hilu](32370982.md) of the [SPLM-N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPLM-N) (al-Hilu), Fadlallah Baramah Nasser of the [National Umma Party](1184790.md), Al-Hadi Idris of the [Sudan Revolutionary Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudan_Revolutionary_Front), and Ibrahim al-Mirghani of the [Original Democratic Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Unionist_Party_\(Sudan\)).[^cite_note-thekenyatimes.com-16]

The [National Umma Party](1184790.md) subsequently removed [Fadlallah Burma Nasir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadlallah_Burma_Nasir?action=edit\&redlink=1) as its acting leader following him signing the Sudan Founding Charter.[^cite_note-17] However, Nasir rejected his dismissal and ordered the dissolution of the party's Presidential Institution instead.[^cite_note-18]

The [Original Democratic Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Unionist_Party_\(Sudan\)) also denied signing the Charter and disavowed [Ibrahim al-Mirghani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_al-Mirghani).[^cite_note-19]

### Provisions

The charter calls for “a secular, democratic, decentralised state based on freedom, equality, and justice, without bias toward any cultural, ethnic, religious, or regional identity”, and outlines plans for a “new, unified, professional, national army”.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

## Transitional constitutional framework

On 4 March 2025, the Sudan Founding Alliance announced that it had adopted a transitional constitutional framework at a ceremony held at the Mercure Upper Hill Hotel in Nairobi.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-\:0-25][^cite_note-\:1-26] The transitional constitutional framework establishes the structure of the [Government of Peace and Unity](79260861.md) and defines the powers of national, regional and local authorities. It also contains provisions for the division of Sudan into eight administrative regions.[^cite_note-\:1-26][^cite_note-\:0-25] Rapid Support Forces leader Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo officially announced the establishment of the Government of Peace and Unity on 15 April 2025.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

## Member parties and factions

The alliance includes 23 political and military factions including:[^cite_note-thekenyatimes.com-16]

- [Rapid Support Forces](43053874.md) ([Janjaweed](683270.md))

- [Sudan People's Liberation Movement–North (al-Hilu)](30884032.md)

- [National Umma Party](1184790.md) (Fadlallah Burma Nasir faction)

- [Sudan Liberation Movement/Army](2031543.md) (Transitional Council faction)

- [Democratic Unionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Unionist_Party_\(Sudan\)) (Ibrahim al-Mirghani faction)

- [Justice and Equality Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Equality_Movement) (Suleiman Sandal faction)

- Sudan Liberation Forces Alliance&#x20;

## Leadership

A Leadership Council was established on 1 July 2025:[^cite_note-29]

- President: Admiral-General [Mohamed Hamdan Dagalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_Hamdan_Dagalo)

- Vice-president: Commander [Abdelaziz al-Hilu](32370982.md)

- Spokesperson: Aladdin Awad Nugud

- Rapporteur: Makin Hamed Tirab
