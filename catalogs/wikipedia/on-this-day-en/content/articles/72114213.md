A [regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Spain) was held in [Extremadura](68204.md) on 21 December 2025 to elect the 12th [Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Extremadura) of the [autonomous community](55002.md). All 65 seats in the Assembly were up for election. This marked the first time that an [Extremaduran president](32304517.md) exercised the legal prerogative to call a [snap election](462915.md).

The [2023 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Extremaduran_regional_election) had seen the [People's Party](53445935.md) (PP) and the [far-right](221220.md) [Vox](41928488.md) party form a [coalition](6036.md) under the presidency of [María Guardiola](72221033.md), but not before she was forced to [backtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flip-flop_\(politics\)) after having pledged not to let the latter into [her cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_government_of_Mar%C3%ADa_Guardiola). The [Spanish Socialist Workers' Party](52982859.md) (PSOE), who was ousted from power only for the second time in history, saw a leadership change from [Guillermo Fernández Vara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Fern%C3%A1ndez_Vara)—who died from [stomach cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach_cancer) in October 2025—to the then-president of the [provincial deputation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_deputation_\(Spain\)) of [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Badajoz), [Miguel Ángel Gallardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_%C3%81ngel_Gallardo_\(politician\)). The PP–Vox coalition collapsed in July 2024 over a strategic movement from the latter's national leadership, with Guardiola leading a [minority government](271434.md) from that point onwards. Amid growing tensions with Vox, unable to pass her 2026 budget through parliament and seeking to capitalize on various corruption probes affecting the national government under [Prime Minister](203556.md) [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md), Guardiola called an early election in what became the first of a [series of regional elections](81810102.md) held in PP-controlled regions.[^cite_note-1]

Political stability and the continuity of the [Almaraz Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almaraz_Nuclear_Power_Plant) were seen as key themes going into the election and in ensuing negotiations.[^cite_note-2] The campaign was shaken by a string of [sexual misconduct allegations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932026_Spanish_sexual_misconduct_allegations)—particularly the [Salazar affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salazar_affair) affecting the ruling PSOE in Spain, and accusations against Guardiola's official driver and the PP's local mayor in [Navalmoral de la Mata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navalmoral_de_la_Mata)—as well as by a theft in a postal office that saw the PP spread suspicions of [electoral fraud](382165.md).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] The election resulted in the PSOE's worst historical result in the region, with the demobilization of part of its electorate as it lost 10 of its 28 seats, in what was attributed both to a punishment of Gallardo and to growing discontent with Sánchez's national government.[^cite_note-5] Guardiola's PP, while having a strong performance, fell well short of its goal of securing an overall majority of 33, remaining dependant on support from a resurgent Vox, which doubled its results. The left-wing [United for Extremadura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidas_por_Extremadura) alliance, comprising [Podemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podemos_Extremadura) and allies of [Sumar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumar_\(electoral_platform\)) (PSOE's ruling partner in Spain), benefitted from the Socialist collapse and obtained its best historical result. The combined vote for right-wing parties was 60%, which was the highest in history, albeit under the lowest [voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout) since the first regional election in [1983](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Extremaduran_regional_election).[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

A [political deadlock](3786577.md) ensued as government formation negotiations between PP and Vox failed to deliver a deal,[^cite_note-8] resulting in a failed [investiture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investiture) attempt by Guardiola in March 2026.[^cite_note-Ara060326-9] As a result, the scenario of a repeat election taking place in 28 June—if no government is elected before 4 May—remains a possibility.[^cite_note-Ara060326-9]

## Background

María Guardiola being sworn into office as president of Extremadura

Government negotiations in 2023 saw the regional [PP](53445935.md) leader, [María Guardiola](72221033.md), [backtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flip-flop_\(politics\)) on her pledge not to form a [coalition](6036.md) with the [far-right](221220.md) [Vox](41928488.md) party.

The [2023 regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Extremaduran_regional_election) saw a parliamentary majority for the [People's Party](53445935.md) (PP) and the [far-right](221220.md) [Vox](41928488.md) party, despite the [Spanish Socialist Workers' Party](52982859.md) (PSOE) remaining the largest overall.[^cite_note-10] PP candidate [María Guardiola](72221033.md)'s initial refusal to form a [coalition government](6036.md) with Vox led to several weeks of public clashes[^cite_note-RTVE300523-11][^cite_note-12]—seeing Guardiola herself proclaiming that she could not allow "those who deny [gender-based violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender-based_violence), who use bold strokes, [dehumanize immigrants](6491626.md) and display a banner on which they [throw the LGTBI flag into a trash can](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobia)" into her government[^cite_note-13]—and in the PSOE and [United for Extremadura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unidas_por_Extremadura) (the alliance between [Podemos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podemos_Extremadura) and [United Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Left_Extremadura)) retaining control of the Assembly's bureau as the PP and Vox voted for their own candidates.[^cite_note-14] Ultimately, Guardiola was forced to [U-turn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flip-flop_\(politics\)) and form a [PP–Vox cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_government_of_Mar%C3%ADa_Guardiola) by her own party's national leadership,[^cite_note-EC260623-15] then embroiled in the [23 July 2023 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Spanish_general_election) campaign.[^cite_note-EP300623-16][^cite_note-EC300623-17] This political episode was said to have contributed to the PP's failure to meet expectations in the general election by evidencing the party's willingness to allow the far-right into government despite public pledges to the contrary.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

[Guillermo Fernández Vara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Fern%C3%A1ndez_Vara), who had been president of Extremadura from 2007 to 2011 and again from 2015 to 2023, announced his withdrawal from regional politics after failing to secure [investiture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investiture),[^cite_note-RTVE300523-11][^cite_note-21] being proposed as a [senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Spain) instead.[^cite_note-22] In December 2023, Vara announced that he was suffering from [stomach cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach_cancer),[^cite_note-23] which ultimately led to his death in October 2025.[^cite_note-24] [Miguel Ángel Gallardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_%C3%81ngel_Gallardo_\(politician\)), president of the [provincial deputation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_deputation_\(Spain\)) of [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Badajoz) and regarded as a critic of [Prime Minister](203556.md) [Pedro Sánchez](43297512.md)'s leadership, was elected in March 2024 as Vara's successor at the helm of the regional PSOE branch.[^cite_note-EP020324-25][^cite_note-26] In May 2025, Gallardo was indicted on charges of [perverting the course of justice](405736.md) and [influence peddling](630570.md) related to the allegedly illegal hiring in 2017 of Sánchez's brother in the deputation he presided over.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] He denied any wrongdoing and suggested that he was the target of a [lawfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawfare) campaign,[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] but his public standing was damaged by his seeking of [parliamentary immunity](320344.md), in a move that the [High Court of Justice of Extremadura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_Justice_of_Extremadura) deemed to be [law evasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evasion_\(law\)).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

On 11 July 2024, Vox's national leader [Santiago Abascal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Abascal) forced the break up of all PP–Vox regional governments over a controversy regarding the nationwide distribution of [unaccompanied migrant minors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unaccompanied_minor) among the autonomous communities.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] Vox's single regional minister in Guardiola's cabinet, [Ignacio Higuero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacio_Higuero), quit the party in order to preserve his office,[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] though his involvement in a [resume padding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resume_padding) scandal eventually led to his resignation in August 2025.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] Budget negotiations in the autumn of 2025 hinted at the possibility of Guardiola calling a [snap election](462915.md) for early next year in the event of a parliamentary deadlock,[^cite_note-EP290925-41][^cite_note-42] which she ultimately did in an attempt to take advantage of internal polling showing a weak opposition.[^cite_note-EP271025-43][^cite_note-44]

## Overview

Under the [2011 Statute of Autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_autonomy), the [Assembly of Extremadura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_Extremadura) was the [unicameral legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameral_legislature) of the [homonymous](68204.md) [autonomous community](55002.md), having legislative power in [devolved matters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution), as well as the ability to grant or withdraw confidence from a [regional president](32304517.md).[^cite_note-45] The electoral and procedural rules were supplemented by [national law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Spain#Electoral_procedures) provisions.[^cite_note-46]

### Date

The term of the Assembly of Extremadura expired four years after the date of its previous election, unless it was [dissolved](652293.md) earlier. The election [decree](322949.md) was required to be issued no later than 25 days before the scheduled expiration date of parliament and published on the following day in the Official Journal of Extremadura (DOE), with [election day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_day) taking place 54 days after the decree's publication.[^cite_note-47] The [previous election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Extremaduran_regional_election) was held on 28 May 2023, which meant that the chamber's term would have expired on 28 May 2027. The election decree was required to be published in the DOE no later than 4 May 2027, setting the latest possible date for election day on 27 June 2027.

The regional president had the prerogative to dissolve the Assembly of Extremadura at any given time and call a [snap election](462915.md), provided that no [motion of no confidence](208101.md) was in process and that dissolution did not occur before one year after a previous one.[^cite_note-48] In the event of an [investiture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investiture) process failing to elect a regional president within a two-month period from the first ballot, the Assembly was to be automatically dissolved and a fresh election called.[^cite_note-49]

The political deadlock resulting from the 2023 election result, in which no single party secured an absolute majority of seats, was briefly commented as potentially leading to a repeat election as PP's María Guardiola opposed letting the far-right Vox into government.[^cite_note-50] Guardiola eventually bowed to political pressure from her party at the national level and signed a coalition agreement with Vox,[^cite_note-EC260623-15][^cite_note-EP300623-16][^cite_note-EC300623-17] being elected as [regional president](32304517.md) on 14 July 2023 and dashing any prospect of an imminent early election.[^cite_note-51] The possibility of an early election call was again floated in late 2024, after Vox's split with her government left Guardiola in a minority and unable to get her 2025 budget passed through parliament, but this attempt ultimately failed to materialize.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

Guardiola threatened in September 2025 with a snap election if opposition parties blocked her government's 2026 budget.[^cite_note-EP290925-41] Speculation then emerged that the national leadership of the [People's Party](404727.md) (PP) was planning to advance the elections in Aragon and Extremadura (and possibly the [Balearic Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Balearic_regional_election)) to make them take place near or concurrently with the [Castilian-Leonese election scheduled for early 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Castilian-Leonese_regional_election).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] While the alleged justification would be the regional governments' failure to approve their 2026 budgets, the true motive was attributed to PP plans—not without risk—to turn the simultaneous election call into a referendum on the [national government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_government_of_Pedro_S%C3%A1nchez) of Prime Minister Pedro Sánchez.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] The regional PP ruled out any plans for a joint election call and defended their president's "full freedom" to make her independent decisions.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] After PSOE and Vox hinted at voting down Guardiola's budget,[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] she announced the parliament's dissolution on 27 October, with the election being scheduled for 21 December 2025.[^cite_note-EP271025-43][^cite_note-63]

The Assembly of Extremadura was officially dissolved on 28 October 2025 with the publication of the corresponding decree in the DOE, setting election day for 21 December.[^cite_note-64]

### Electoral system

Voting for the Assembly was based on [universal suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_suffrage), comprising all [Spanish nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_national) over 18 years of age, registered in Extremadura and with full [political rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_rights), provided that they had not been [deprived of the right to vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disfranchisement) by a final [sentence](870351.md).[^cite_note-65]

The Assembly of Extremadura had a maximum of 65 seats, with electoral provisions fixing its size at that number. All were elected in two [multi-member constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-member_district)—corresponding to the [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Spain) of [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Badajoz) and [Cáceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_C%C3%A1ceres), each of which was assigned an initial minimum of 20 seats and the remaining 25 distributed in proportion to population—using the [D'Hondt method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method) and [closed-list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closed-list) [proportional voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_voting), with a five percent-[threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) of valid votes (including [blank ballots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blank_ballot)) in each constituency. Alternatively, parties could also enter the seat distribution as long as they ran candidates in both constituencies and reached five percent regionally.[^cite_note-66]

As a result of the aforementioned allocation, each Assembly constituency was entitled the following seats:[^cite_note-67]

| Seats | Constituencies |
| --- | --- |
| **36** | [Badajoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badajoz_\(Assembly_of_Extremadura_constituency\)) |
| **29** | [Cáceres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1ceres_\(Assembly_of_Extremadura_constituency\)) |

The law did not provide for [by-elections](155434.md) to fill [vacant seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casual_vacancy); instead, any vacancies arising after the proclamation of candidates and during the legislative term were filled by the next candidates on the [party lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_list) or, when required, by designated [substitutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitute_\(elections\)).[^cite_note-68]

## Parties and candidates

The electoral law allowed for [parties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) and [federations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_alliance) registered in the [interior ministry](21238851.md), [alliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_alliance) and [groupings of electors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouping_of_electors_\(Spain\)) to present [lists of candidates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_list). Parties and federations intending to form an alliance were required to inform the relevant electoral commission within 10 days of the election call, whereas groupings of electors needed to secure the signature of at least two percent of the electorate in the constituencies for which they sought election, disallowing electors from signing for more than one list.[^cite_note-71] Amendments in 2024 required a balanced composition of men and women in the electoral lists through the use of a [zipper system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipper_system).[^cite_note-72]

## Campaign

### Timetable

The key dates are listed below (all times are [CET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Time)):[^cite_note-82]

- 27 October: The election decree is issued with the countersign of the [president](32304517.md), after deliberation in the [Regional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Government_of_Extremadura).

- 28 October: Formal [dissolution of parliament](652293.md) and start of [prohibition period on the inauguration of public works, services or projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purdah_\(pre-election_period\)).

- 31 October: Initial constitution of provincial and zone [electoral commissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_commission) with judicial members.

- 3 November: Division of constituencies into [polling sections and stations](763947.md).

- 7 November: Deadline for parties and federations to report on their [electoral alliances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_alliance).

- 10 November: Deadline for [electoral register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_register) consultation for the purpose of possible corrections.

- 17 November: Deadline for parties, federations, alliances, and groupings of electors to present [electoral lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_list).

- 19 November: Publication of submitted electoral lists in the Official Journal of Extremadura (DOE).

- 24 November: Official proclamation of validly submitted electoral lists.

- 25 November: Publication of proclaimed electoral lists in the DOE.

- 26 November: Deadline for the selection of polling station members by [sortition](19288053.md).

- 4 December: Deadline for the appointment of non-judicial members to provincial and zone electoral commissions.

- 5 December: Official start of [electoral campaigning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_campaign).

  [^cite_note-Decr825Art4-83]

- 11 December: Deadline to apply for [postal voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postal_voting).

- 16 December: Start of legal ban on electoral [opinion polling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling) publication; deadline for [non-resident citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-resident_citizen_voting) (electors residing abroad (CERA) and citizens temporarily absent from Spain) to vote by mail.

- 17 December: Deadline for postal and temporarily absent voting.

- 18 December: Deadline for CERA voting.

- 19 December: Last day of electoral campaigning.

  [^cite_note-Decr825Art4-83]

- 20 December: Official [election silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_silence) ("reflection day").

- **21 December**: [Election day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_day) (polling stations open at 9 am and close at 8 pm or once voters present in a queue at/outside the polling station at 8 pm have cast their vote); provisional [vote counting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vote_counting).

- 26 December: Start of general vote counting, including CERA votes.

- 29 December: Deadline for the general vote counting.

- 7 January: Deadline for the proclamation of elected members.

- 20 January: Deadline for the reconvening of parliament.

  [^cite_note-84]

- 16 February: Deadline for the publication of definitive election results in the DOE.
