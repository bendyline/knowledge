**Pennsylvania**,[^cite_note-9] officially the **Commonwealth of Pennsylvania**,[^cite_note-10] is a [state](18618239.md) located in the [Mid-Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_\(United_States\)), [Northeastern](431669.md), [Appalachian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachia), and [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_region) regions of the United States. The state borders [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware) to its southeast, [Maryland](18858.md) to its south, forming the [Mason–Dixon line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason%E2%80%93Dixon_line), [West Virginia](32905.md) to its southwest, [Ohio](22199.md) to its west, the [Canadian province](75763.md) of [Ontario](22218.md) to its northwest via [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie), [New York](8210131.md) to its north, and the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) and [New Jersey](21648.md) to its east. Pennsylvania is the [fifth-most populous U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population), home to over 13 million residents as of the [2020 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), and the [ninth-highest state by population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_states_and_territories_of_the_United_States_by_population_density). It is the [33rd-largest state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area) by land area. The largest [metropolitan statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) is the [Philadelphia metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_metropolitan_area)/[Delaware Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Valley), centered on [Philadelphia](50585.md), the state's [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Pennsylvania) and [sixth-most populous U.S. city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population), and an expanding [biopharmaceutical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biopharmaceutical), commercial, educational, [entrepreneurial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entrepreneur)/[venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital), industrial, and transportational hub.[^cite_note-PhiladelphiaAmericanAirlinesTransatlanticHub-11] Pennsylvania's second-largest metropolitan area, [Greater Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Pittsburgh), is centered in and around [Pittsburgh](25101.md), also a biopharmaceutical hub and the Commonwealth's second-largest city. Geographically situated between the two larger metros lies [Harrisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg), the capital city of Pennsylvania.

[Pennsylvania's geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Pennsylvania) is highly diverse. The [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains) run through the center of the state, the [Allegheny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_Mountains) and [Pocono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocono_Mountains) mountains span much of [Northeastern Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_Pennsylvania), and approximately [59% of the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover_by_state_and_territory_in_the_United_States) is [forested](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest). Although Pennsylvania technically has no oceanic [shoreline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoreline), it has 140 miles (225 km) of [waterfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfront_\(area\)) along Lake Erie and along the [tidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_estuary) Delaware River, the latter of which reaches the [Delaware Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Bay), a [marginal bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_sea) of the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md).[^cite_note-NOAA_Office_of_Coastal_Management-12]

Pennsylvania was founded in 1681 through a royal [land grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_grant) to [William Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn), the son of the [commonwealth's namesake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn_\(Royal_Navy_officer\)). Before that, between 1638 and 1655, a southeast portion of the state was part of [New Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Sweden), a [Swedish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire) colony. Established as a haven for religious and political tolerance, the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) [colonial-era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_colonization_of_the_Americas) [Province of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pennsylvania) was known for its relatively peaceful relations with native tribes, [innovative government system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_commission), and [religious pluralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_tolerance). Pennsylvania played a vital historical role in the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution) and the ultimately successful quest for independence from the British Empire, hosting the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Continental_Congress) and [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress) in [Philadelphia](50585.md), the latter of which formed the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army) commanded by [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) in 1775, during the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), unanimously adopted the [Declaration of Independence](31874.md) the following year.[^cite_note-13] On December 12, 1787, Pennsylvania was the second state to ratify the [U.S. Constitution](31644.md) after [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware).

In the 21st century, Pennsylvania has garnered the nickname "Cellicon State" for the central role of Philadelphia and Pittsburgh in the development of [technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_technology) and [immunotherapies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunotherapy) to treat different cancers.[^cite_note-University_of_Pennsylvania_-_Penn_Medicine-2025-14] [AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI) and [STEM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STEM) education are recognized to hold high-priority status for Pennsylvania's long-term mission and future.

### Indigenous settlement

Pennsylvania's history of human habitation extends thousands of years before the foundation of the colonial [Province of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pennsylvania) in 1681. [Archaeologists](18951655.md) believe the first [settlement of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peopling_of_the_Americas) occurred at least 15,000 years ago, during the [Last Glacial Period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_Glacial_Period), though it is unclear when humans first inhabited present-day Pennsylvania. Between 10,000 and 16,000 years ago, [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) crossed the two continents, arriving in [North America](21139.md).[^cite_note-15] [Meadowcroft Rockshelter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowcroft_Rockshelter) in [Jefferson Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_Township,_Washington_County,_Pennsylvania) includes the earliest known signs of human activity in Pennsylvania and perhaps all of North America,[^cite_note-CBS_News-2013-16] including the remains of a civilization that existed over 10,000 years ago and possibly pre-dated the [Clovis culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovis_culture).[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Historical_and_Museum_Commission-17][^cite_note-CBS_News-2013-16] By 1000 AD, in contrast to their nomadic [hunter-gatherer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter-gatherer) [ancestors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleo-Indians), the native population of Pennsylvania had developed [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) techniques and a mixed food economy.[^cite_note-18]

By the time [European colonization of the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) began, at least two major Native American tribes inhabited Pennsylvania.[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Historical_and_Museum_Commission-17] The first, the [Lenape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenape), spoke an [Algonquian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algonquian_languages) and inhabited the eastern region of the state, then known as [Lenapehoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenapehoking). It included most of present-day [New Jersey](21648.md) and the [Lehigh Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley) and [Delaware Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Valley) regions in eastern and southeastern Pennsylvania. The Lenape's territory ended somewhere between the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) in the east and the [Susquehanna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susquehanna_River) in central Pennsylvania. The second tribe, the [Susquehannock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susquehannock), spoke an [Iroquoian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroquoian_language) and were based in [Eastern Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Pennsylvania) along the Susquehanna River.[^cite_note-19] European disease and constant warfare with several neighboring tribes and groups of Europeans weakened these two tribes, and they were grossly outpaced financially as the [Wendat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendat_people) and [Haudenosaunee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haudenosaunee) blocked them from proceeding west into [Ohio](22199.md) during the [Beaver Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver_Wars). As they lost numbers and land, the Wendat abandoned much of their western territory and moved closer to the Susquehanna River, and the Haudenosaunee and Mohawk tribes moved further north. The Iroquoian [Petun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petun) was northwest of the Allegheny River.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

### 17th century

Main article: [Province of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pennsylvania)



[William Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn), a [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers) and son of a [prominent admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn_\(Royal_Navy_officer\)), founded the colonial [Province of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pennsylvania) in 1681.

In the 17th century, the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland) and the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginia_Company) claimed both sides of the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) as part of their colonial lands in America.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-Van_Zandt-1976-24] The Dutch were the first to take possession.[^cite_note-Van_Zandt-1976-24] By June 3, 1631, the Dutch began settling the [Delmarva Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delmarva_Peninsula) by establishing the [Zwaanendael Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zwaanendael_Colony) on the site of present-day [Lewes, Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewes,_Delaware).[^cite_note-25] In 1638, Sweden established the [New Sweden Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Sweden) in the region of [Fort Christina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Christina) on the site of present-day [Wilmington, Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilmington,_Delaware). New Sweden claimed and, for the most part, controlled the lower Delaware River region, including parts of present-day Delaware, New Jersey, and Pennsylvania, but settled few colonists there.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

On March 12, 1664, [King Charles II of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) gave [James, Duke of York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_II_of_England) a [grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_grant) that incorporated all lands included in the original Virginia Company of Plymouth Grant and other lands. This grant was in conflict with the Dutch claim for [New Netherland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Netherland), which included parts of today's Pennsylvania.[^cite_note-28]

On June 24, 1664, the Duke of York sold the portion of his large grant that included present-day [New Jersey](21648.md) to [John Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Berkeley,_1st_Baron_Berkeley_of_Stratton) and [George Carteret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Carteret) for a proprietary colony. The land was not yet in British possession, but the sale boxed in the portion of New Netherland on the West side of the Delaware River. The British conquest of New Netherland began on August 29, 1664, when [New Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam) was coerced to surrender while facing cannons on British ships in [New York Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Harbor).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] The conquest was completed in October 1664, when the British captured [Fort Casimir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Casimir) in what today is [New Castle, Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Castle,_Delaware). The [Treaty of Breda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Breda_\(1667\)) between England, France, and the Netherlands confirmed the English conquest on July 21, 1667,[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] although there were temporary reversions.

On September 12, 1672, during the [Third Anglo-Dutch War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Dutch_War), the Dutch reconquered [New York Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Colony)/[New Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Amsterdam), establishing three County Courts, which went on to become original Counties in present-day [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware) and Pennsylvania. The one that later transferred to Pennsylvania was Upland.[^cite_note-33] This was partially reversed on February 9, 1674, when the [Treaty of Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Westminster_\(1674\)) ended the Third Anglo-Dutch War and reverted all political situations to the *status quo ante bellum*. The British retained the Dutch Counties with their Dutch names.[^cite_note-34] By June 11, 1674, New York reasserted control over the outlying colonies, including Upland, and the names started to be changed to British names by November 11, 1674.[^cite_note-35] Upland was partitioned on November 12, 1674, producing the general outline of the current border between Pennsylvania and Delaware.[^cite_note-36]

On February 28, 1681, Charles II granted a land [charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter)[^cite_note-37] to [Quaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers) leader William Penn to repay a debt of £16,000[^cite_note-38] (around £2,100,000 in 2008, adjusting for retail inflation)[^cite_note-39] owed to William's father. The transaction represents one of the largest land grants to an individual in history.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-Pym.org-2006-41] Penn proposed that the land be called New Wales, but there were objections to that name, so he recommended Sylvania (from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *silva*: "forest, woods"). The King named it Pennsylvania (literally "Penn's Woods") in honor of Admiral Penn. The younger Penn was embarrassed at this name, fearing that people would think he had named it after himself, but King Charles would not rename the grant.[^cite_note-42] Penn established a government with two innovations, the [county commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_commission) and [freedom of religion](46006.md), which were subsequently replicated in many of the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies).[^cite_note-Pym.org-2006-41]

What had been Upland on the Pennsylvania side of the Pennsylvania-Delaware border was renamed [Chester County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_County,_Pennsylvania) when Pennsylvania instituted its colonial governments on March 4, 1681.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] Penn signed a peace treaty with [Tamanend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamanend), leader of the Lenape, which began a long period of friendly relations between the Quakers and the Indians.[^cite_note-45] Additional treaties between Quakers and other tribes followed. The [Treaty of Shackamaxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Shackamaxon) of William Penn was never violated.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

### 18th century

See also: [Pennsylvania in the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_in_the_American_Revolution)

Further information: [George Washington's crossing of the Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington's_crossing_of_the_Delaware_River), [Philadelphia campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_campaign), and [Constitutional Convention (United States)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_\(United_States\))



[Shelter House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelter_House) in [Emmaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmaus,_Pennsylvania), constructed in 1734 by [Pennsylvania German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Dutch) settlers, is the oldest continuously occupied building structure in the [Lehigh Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley) and one of the oldest in Pennsylvania

[^cite_note-49]



[Independence Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Hall) in [Philadelphia](50585.md), where the [Declaration of Independence](31874.md) and [United States Constitution](31644.md) were adopted in 1776 and 1787–88, respectively

Between 1730 and when the Pennsylvania Colony was shut down by [Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Great_Britain) with the [Currency Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency_Act) in 1764, the Pennsylvania Colony made its own [paper money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paper_money) to account for the shortage of actual [gold](12240.md) and [silver](27119.md). The paper money was called [Colonial Scrip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Scrip).

The Colony issued bills of credit, which were as good as gold or silver coins because of their [legal tender](267793.md) status. Since they were issued by the government and not a banking institution, they were interest-free, largely defraying the expense of the government and therefore taxation of the people. It also promoted general employment and prosperity, since the government used discretion and did not issue excessive amounts that inflated the currency. [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin) had a hand in creating this currency, whose utility, he said, was never to be disputed. The currency also met with "cautious approval" by [Adam Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Smith).[^cite_note-50]

In 1740, Franklin also founded the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) in [Philadelphia](50585.md). The university, one of nine [colonial colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_colleges), was the first college established in Pennsylvania and one of the first in the nation. Today, it is an [Ivy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League) university, which is routinely ranked among the world's best universities.[^cite_note-Classroom-51] [Dickinson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickinson_College) in [Carlisle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlisle,_Pennsylvania), founded by [Benjamin Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Rush) and named after John Dickinson, was the first college founded after [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) and the unification of the states.[^cite_note-Classroom-51] Established in 1773, it was ratified on September 9, 1783, five days after the [Treaty of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Paris_\(1783\)).

[James Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Smith_\(frontiersman\)) wrote that in 1763, "the Indians again commenced hostilities, and were busily engaged in killing and scalping the frontier inhabitants in various parts of Pennsylvania. This state was then a Quaker government, and at the first of this war the frontiers received no assistance from the state."[^cite_note-52] The ensuing hostilities became known as [Pontiac's War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac's_War).

After the [Stamp Act Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamp_Act_Congress) of 1765, delegate John Dickinson of Philadelphia wrote the [Declaration of Rights and Grievances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Rights_and_Grievances). The Congress was the first meeting of the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies), which convened at the request of the [Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Massachusetts_Bay) assembly; nine of the 13 colonies sent delegates.[^cite_note-53] Dickinson then wrote *[Letters from a Farmer in Pennsylvania, To the Inhabitants of the British Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letters_from_a_Farmer_in_Pennsylvania)*, which were published in the Pennsylvania Chronicle between December 2, 1767, and February 15, 1768.

When the [Founding Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Fathers_of_the_United_States) convened in Philadelphia in 1774, 12 colonies sent representatives to the [First Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Continental_Congress).[^cite_note-54] The [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress), which began in May 1775, authored and signed the [Declaration of Independence](31874.md) in Philadelphia,[^cite_note-55] but when Philadelphia fell to the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_during_the_American_Revolutionary_War) in the [Philadelphia campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_campaign), the Continental Congress moved west, where it met at the [Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster,_Pennsylvania) courthouse on Saturday, September 27, 1777, and then to [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York,_Pennsylvania). In York, the Second Continental Congress adopted the [Articles of Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation), largely authored by Pennsylvania delegate John Dickinson, that formed 13 independent States[^cite_note-56] into a new union. Later, the [Constitution](31644.md) was written, and Philadelphia was once again chosen to be cradle to the new nation.[^cite_note-57] The Constitution was drafted and signed at the [Pennsylvania State House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_House) in Philadelphia, now known as [Independence Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Hall), the same building where the Declaration of Independence was previously adopted and signed in 1776.[^cite_note-58]

On December 12, 1787, Pennsylvania was the second state to ratify the [U.S. Constitution](31644.md),[^cite_note-59] five days after [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware) became the first. At the time, Pennsylvania was the most ethnically and religiously diverse of the thirteen colonies. Because a third of Pennsylvania's population spoke [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), the Constitution was presented in German so that. those citizens could participate in the discussion about it. [Reverend Frederick Muhlenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Muhlenberg), a [Lutheran](23371382.md) minister and the [first Speaker of the U.S. House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives), acted as chairman of Pennsylvania's ratifying convention.[^cite_note-60]

For about half a century, the [Pennsylvania General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_General_Assembly) met in the Philadelphia area before it began meeting regularly in Independence Hall in Philadelphia for 63 years.[^cite_note-Legis.state.pa.us-61] However, events such as the [Paxton Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paxton_Boys) massacres of 1763 had made the legislature aware of the need for a central capital. In 1799, the General Assembly moved to the [Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster,_Pennsylvania) Courthouse.[^cite_note-Legis.state.pa.us-61]

### 19th century

See also: [Pennsylvania in the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_in_the_American_Civil_War), [Battle of Gettysburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gettysburg), [Gettysburg Address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_Address), and [Industrial Revolution in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution_in_the_United_States)



The *Battle of Gettysburg*, an 1887 portrait by [Thure de Thulstrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thure_de_Thulstrup) depicting the [Battle of Gettysburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gettysburg), fought over three days from July 1 to 3, 1863, in [Gettysburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_Battlefield). It was the [deadliest battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_costliest_American_Civil_War_land_battles) ever fought on American soil and the bloodiest battle in all of American military history. But the [Union army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_army)'s victory at Gettysburg proved the [Civil War's turning point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_point_of_the_American_Civil_War), paving the way for the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\))'s ultimate victory two years later and the nation's preservation.



On November 19, 1863, [President Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) (center, facing camera) traveled to present-day [Gettysburg National Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_National_Cemetery) and delivered the [Gettysburg Address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_Address), a 271-word address considered one of the most famous speeches in American history.

[^cite_note-62]

[^cite_note-63]

The [Pennsylvania General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_General_Assembly) met in the old [Dauphin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dauphin_County,_Pennsylvania) Court House until December 1821[^cite_note-Legis.state.pa.us-61] when the [Federal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_architecture)-style Hills Capitol, named for Lancaster architect [Stephen Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Hills), was constructed on a hilltop land grant of four acres set aside for a seat of state government in [Harrisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg,_Pennsylvania) by the son and namesake of [John Harris, Sr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Harris,_Sr.), a [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire) native who founded a trading post and ferry on the east shore of the [Susquehanna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susquehanna_River) in 1705.[^cite_note-64] The Hills Capitol burned down on February 2, 1897, during a heavy snowstorm, presumably because of a faulty [flue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flue).[^cite_note-Legis.state.pa.us-61]

While awaiting the construction of a new capitol, the General Assembly temporarily convened at a nearby [Methodist Church](20119.md). Following an architectural selection contest, Chicago-based architect [Henry Ives Cobb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Ives_Cobb) was commissioned to design and build the replacement structure, a project hindered by strict financial limitations imposed by its legislature. When they dubbed the roughly finished, somewhat industrial Cobb Capitol building complete, the General Assembly refused to occupy the building. In 1901, political and popular indignation prompted a second contest that was restricted to Pennsylvania architects; [Joseph Miller Huston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Miller_Huston) of Philadelphia was chosen to design the present [Pennsylvania State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_Capitol) that incorporated Cobb's building into a magnificent public work, finished and dedicated in 1907.[^cite_note-Legis.state.pa.us-61]

From 1857 to 1861, [James Buchanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Buchanan), a [Franklin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_County,_Pennsylvania) native, served as the 15th U.S. president, the first president to be born in Pennsylvania.[^cite_note-65]

Over three days, from July 1 to 3, 1863, during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), the [Battle of Gettysburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gettysburg) was fought near [Gettysburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg,_Pennsylvania). The Battle of Gettysburg was the bloodiest battle of both the Civil War and of any battle in American history. The [Union army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_army)'s victory at Gettysburg also proved the [Civil War's turning point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turning_point_of_the_American_Civil_War), leading to the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\))'s victory two years later and the nation's preservation. Several months later, on November 19, 1863, then [U.S. president](24113.md) [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln) traveled to Gettysburg, where he participated in a ceremonial consecration of present-day [Gettysburg National Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_National_Cemetery) and delivered the [Gettysburg Address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gettysburg_Address), a 271-word speech that is one of the most famous [speeches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_speaking) in [American history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States).[^cite_note-66] During the Civil War, an estimated 350,000 Pennsylvanians served in the Union army, including 8,600 African American [military volunteers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_volunteer).[^cite_note-67]

The politics of Pennsylvania were for decades dominated by the financially conservative [Republican](32070.md)-aligned [Cameron machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_machine), established by [U.S. Senator Simon Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Cameron),[^cite_note-68] who later served as Lincoln's [Secretary of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_War). Control of the machine was subsequently passed on to Cameron's son [J. Donald Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Donald_Cameron), whose ineffectiveness resulted in a transfer of power to [Matthew Quay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Quay) and later to [Boies Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boies_Penrose).

The post-Civil War era, known as the [Gilded Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilded_Age), saw the continued rise of industry in Pennsylvania. Pennsylvania was home to some of the largest steel companies in the world. [Andrew Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Carnegie) founded the [Carnegie Steel Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Steel_Company) in [Pittsburgh](25101.md) and [Charles M. Schwab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_M._Schwab) founded [Bethlehem Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_Steel) in [Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_Pennsylvania). Other titans of industry, including [John D. Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._Rockefeller) and [Jay Gould](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Gould), also operated in Pennsylvania. In the latter half of the 19th century, the U.S. [oil industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_industry) was born in [Western Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Pennsylvania), which supplied the vast majority of [kerosene](16992.md) for years thereafter. As the [Pennsylvania oil rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_oil_rush) developed, Pennsylvania's oil boom towns, such as [Titusville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titusville,_Pennsylvania), rose and later fell. Coal mining, primarily in the state's [Coal Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_Region) in the northeast region of the state, also was a major industry for much of the 19th and 20th centuries. In 1903, [Milton S. Hershey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_S._Hershey) began construction on a chocolate factory in [Hershey, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hershey,_Pennsylvania); [The Hershey Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hershey_Company) grew to become the largest chocolate manufacturer in North America. [Heinz Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Company) was also founded during this period. These huge companies exercised a large influence on the politics of Pennsylvania; as [Henry Demarest Lloyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Demarest_Lloyd) put it, oil baron John D. Rockefeller "had done everything with the Pennsylvania legislature except refine it".[^cite_note-Explore_PAHistory.com-69] Pennsylvania created a Department of Highways and engaged in a vast program of road-building, while railroads continued to see heavy usage.[^cite_note-70]

The growth of industry eventually provided middle class incomes to working-class households after the development of labor unions helped them gain living wages. However, the rise of unions also led to a rise of [union busting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_busting) with several private police forces springing up.[^cite_note-Explore_PAHistory.com-69] Pennsylvania was the location of the first documented organized strike in North America, and Pennsylvania was the location of two hugely prominent strikes, the [Great Railroad Strike of 1877](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Railroad_Strike_of_1877) and the [Coal Strike of 1902](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_Strike_of_1902). The eight-hour day was eventually adopted, and the coal and iron police were banned.[^cite_note-71]

### 20th century



[Bethlehem Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem_Steel) in [Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_Pennsylvania) was one of the world's leading steel manufacturers for most of the 20th century. In 1982, however, it discontinued most of its operations, declared bankruptcy in 2001, and was dissolved in 2003.

In the beginning of the 20th century, Pennsylvania's economy was centered on steel manufacturing, [logging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_logging_in_Pennsylvania), [coal mining](208413.md), [textile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile) production, and other forms of industrial [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing). A surge in immigration to the U.S. during the late 19th and early 20th centuries provided a steady flow of cheap labor for these industries, which often employed children and people who could not speak English from [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe) and [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe). Thousands of Pennsylvanians volunteered during the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War). Pennsylvania was an important industrial center during [World War I](4764461.md). The state provided over 300,000 soldiers during the war. On May 31, 1918, the [Pittsburgh Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Agreement) was signed in [Pittsburgh](25101.md), signed by [Tomáš Masaryk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Masaryk) and established [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) as an independent nation.

In 1922, 310,000 Pennsylvania miners joined the [UMW General coal strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UMW_General_coal_strike_\(1922\)), which lasted 163 days and shut down most of the state's coal mines.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

In 1923, [President Calvin Coolidge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Coolidge) established the [Allegheny National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_National_Forest) under the authority of the [Weeks Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weeks_Act) of 1911.[^cite_note-74] The forest is located in the northwest part of the state in [Elk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elk_County,_Pennsylvania), [Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_County,_Pennsylvania), [McKean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKean,_Pennsylvania), and [Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_County,_Pennsylvania) Counties for the purposes of timber production and watershed protection in the [Allegheny River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_River) basin. The Allegheny is the state's only national forest.[^cite_note-75]

In 1922, the [Benjamin Franklin Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin_Bridge) began being constructed for the [Sesquicentennial Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesquicentennial_Exposition) in Philadelphia being held in 1926. The chief engineer of the bridge was Polish-born [Ralph Modjeski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Modjeski), the design engineer was [Leon Moisseiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Moisseiff), the supervising architect was [Paul Philippe Cret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Philippe_Cret), and the construction engineer was [Montgomery B. Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_Case). Work began on January 6, 1922. At the peak of construction, 1,300 people worked on the bridge, and 15 died during its construction. The bridge was originally painted by a commercial painting company owned by David A. Salkind, of Philadelphia, which also painted the Golden Gate Bridge. The bridge opened to traffic on July 1, 1926, three days ahead of its scheduled opening on the nation's 150th anniversary. At completion, its 1,750-foot (533-meter) span was the [world's longest for a suspension bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_longest_suspension_bridge_spans), a distinction it held until the opening of the [Ambassador Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_Bridge) connecting [Detroit, Michigan](8687.md), with [Windsor, Ontario](33596.md), Canada, in 1929.[^cite_note-76]

During [World War II](32927.md), Pennsylvania manufactured 6.6 percent of total U.S. military armaments for the war, the sixth-most of the 48 states.[^cite_note-77] [Philadelphia Naval Shipyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Naval_Shipyard) served as an important naval base during the war, and Pennsylvania produced many of the war's most important military leaders, including [George C. Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_C._Marshall), [Hap Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_H._Arnold), [Jacob Devers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_L._Devers), and [Carl Spaatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Spaatz). During World War II, over a million Pennsylvanians served in the armed forces, and more [Medals of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Honor) were awarded to Pennsylvanians than to individuals from any other state.[^cite_note-78]

On March 28, 1979, the [Three Mile Island accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Mile_Island_accident) was the most significant [nuclear accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_and_radiation_accidents_and_incidents) in U.S. commercial nuclear power plant history.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] The state was hard-hit by the decline and restructuring of the steel industry and other heavy industries during the late 20th century. With job losses came heavy population losses, especially in the state's largest cities. Pittsburgh lost its place among the [top ten most populous cities in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_populous_cities_in_the_United_States_by_decade) in 1950, and Philadelphia dropped to the fifth and is currently the [sixth-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) after decades of ranking among the top three.

After 1990, as information-based industries became more important in the economy, state and local governments put more resources into the old, well-established public library system. Some localities, however, used new state funding to cut local taxes.[^cite_note-81] New ethnic groups, especially [Hispanics and Latinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans), began entering the state to fill low-skill jobs in agriculture and service industries. For example, in [Chester County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester_County,_Pennsylvania), [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Americans) immigrants brought the [Spanish language](26825.md), increased [Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism), high birth rates, and cuisine when they were hired as agricultural laborers; in some rural localities, they made up half or more of the population.[^cite_note-82]

[Stateside Puerto Ricans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stateside_Puerto_Ricans) have built a large community in the state's third-largest city, [Allentown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allentown,_Pennsylvania), where they comprise over 40% of the city's population as of 2000.[^cite_note-83]

In the late 20th century, as Pennsylvania's historical national and even global leadership in [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) largely ceased and its [steelmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steelmaking) and other heavy manufacturing sectors slowed, the state sought to grow its service and other industries to replace the jobs and economic productivity lost from the downturn of these industries. Pittsburgh's concentration of universities has enabled it to be a leader in technology and healthcare. Similarly, Philadelphia has a concentration of university expertise. Healthcare, retail, transportation, and tourism are some of the state's growing industries of the postindustrial era. As in the rest of the nation, most residential population growth has occurred in suburban rather than central city areas, although both major cities have had significant revitalization in their downtown areas.[^cite_note-84] Philadelphia anchors the [seventh-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Metropolitan_Statistical_Areas) [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) in the country and one of the largest metropolitan areas in the world, and Pittsburgh is the center of the nation's 27th-largest metropolitan areas. As of 2020, the [Lehigh Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley) in eastern Pennsylvania is the nation's 69th-largest metropolitan area.[^cite_note-85] Pennsylvania also has six additional metropolitan areas that rank among the nation's 200-most populous metropolitan areas. Philadelphia forms part of the [Northeast megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_megalopolis) and is associated with the [Northeastern United States](431669.md). Pittsburgh is part of the [Great Lakes megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_megalopolis) and is often associated with the [Rust Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_Belt).

### 21st century

Further information: [Pittsburgh synagogue shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_synagogue_shooting) and [United Airlines Flight 93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines_Flight_93)



The [Stonycreek Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonycreek_Township,_Somerset_County,_Pennsylvania) crash site of [Flight 93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines_Flight_93), one of four planes hijacked in the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md); the site is now a [national memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_93_National_Memorial). Flight 93 passengers wrestled with [al-Qaeda terrorist hijackers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hijackers_in_the_September_11_attacks) for control of the plane, preventing it from being likely flown into the [U.S. Capitol](31979.md) or the [White House](33057.md).

[^cite_note-Sources_and_detailed_information-86]

During the [September 11, 2001, terrorist attacks on the United States](5058690.md), the small town of [Shanksville, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanksville,_Pennsylvania), received worldwide attention after [United Airlines Flight 93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines_Flight_93) crashed into a field in [Stonycreek Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonycreek_Township,_Somerset_County,_Pennsylvania), located 1.75 miles (2.82 km) north of the town. All 40 civilians and 4 [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) hijackers on board were killed. The hijackers had intended to fly the plane to [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) to crash it likely into either the [United States Capitol](31979.md) or [The White House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_White_House).[^cite_note-Sources_and_detailed_information-86] After learning from family members via air phone of the earlier attacks on the [World Trade Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Center_\(1973%E2%80%932001\)) in [New York City](645042.md) and the [Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) in [Arlington County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia), however, Flight 93's passengers revolted against the hijackers and fought for control of the plane, causing it to crash. It was the only one of the four aircraft hijacked that day that never reached its intended target and the heroism of the passengers has been commemorated.[^cite_note-87]

In October 2018, the [Tree of Life – Or L'Simcha Congregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_of_Life_%E2%80%93_Or_L'Simcha_Congregation), a [conservative Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Judaism) synagogue, experienced the [Pittsburgh synagogue shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_synagogue_shooting), which resulted in 11 fatalities.[^cite_note-88] On July 13, 2024, near [Butler, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butler,_Pennsylvania), [an assassination attempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attempted_assassination_of_Donald_Trump_in_Pennsylvania) on the 45th President of the United States [Donald Trump](4848272.md) occurred. Since the inception of the 21st century, Pennsylvania has garnered the additional nickname of "Cellicon State" for the central role of Philadelphia and Pittsburgh in the development of [immunotherapies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunotherapy) to treat different cancers.[^cite_note-University_of_Pennsylvania_-_Penn_Medicine-2025-14]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Pennsylvania)

Further information: [List of counties in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Pennsylvania)

Pennsylvania is 170 miles (274 km) north to south and 283 miles (455 km) east to west.[^cite_note-89] Of a total 46,055 square miles (119,282 km<sup>2</sup>),[^cite_note-geo_census_2010-90] 44,817 square miles (116,075 km<sup>2</sup>) are land, 490 square miles (1,269 km<sup>2</sup>) are inland waters, and 749 square miles (1,940 km<sup>2</sup>) are waters in [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie).[^cite_note-91] It is the [33rd-largest state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_area) in the United States.[^cite_note-92] Pennsylvania has 51 miles (82 km)[^cite_note-93] of coastline along Lake Erie and 57 miles (92 km)[^cite_note-NOAA_Office_of_Coastal_Management-12] of shoreline along the [Delaware Estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River). Of the original [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies), Pennsylvania is the only state that does not border the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md). With 86,000 miles of rivers and streams, Pennsylvania has the highest density of waterways of any state in the continental United States.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

The boundaries of the state are the [Mason–Dixon line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason%E2%80%93Dixon_line) (39°43' N) to the south, [Twelve-Mile Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelve-Mile_Circle) on the Pennsylvania-[Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware) border, the [Delaware River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_River) to the east, 80°31' W to the west, and the [42° N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_parallel_north) to the north, except for a short segment on the western end where a [triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_Triangle) extends north to Lake Erie. The state has five geographical regions: [Allegheny Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_Plateau), [Ridge and Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge-and-Valley_Appalachians), [Atlantic Coastal Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Coastal_Plain), [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(plateau\)), and [Erie Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_Plain).

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Pennsylvania)



The [Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) of Pennsylvania based on 1991–2020 [climate normals](42778673.md)



Autumn in [North Branch Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Branch_Township,_Pennsylvania) in [Wyoming County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming_County,_Pennsylvania) in October 2011

Pennsylvania's diverse topography produces a variety of climates, though the entire state experiences cool to cold winters and very warm, humid summers. Straddling two major zones, much of the state has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfa&#x20;*&#x6F;r *Dfb*). The southern portion of the state has a [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical) climate. The largest city, [Philadelphia](50585.md), has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (Köppen *Cfa*).

Summers are generally hot and humid. Moving toward the mountainous interior of the state, the winter climate becomes colder, the number of cloudy days increases, and snowfall amounts are greater. Western areas of the state, particularly locations near Lake Erie, can receive over 100 inches (250 cm) of snowfall annually, and the entire state receives plentiful precipitation throughout the year. The state may be subject to severe weather from spring through summer into autumn. Tornadoes occur annually in the state, sometimes in large numbers, such as 30 recorded tornadoes in 2011; violent tornadoes, however, are rarer than they are in states further west.[^cite_note-96]

The state's three-most populated cities, in order of size, are Philadelphia, Pittsburgh, and Allentown.

### Place of origin

Among Pennsylvania residents, as of 2020, nearly three out of four, 74.5%, are native to the state and were born in Pennsylvania, 18.4% were born in a different U.S. state, 1.5% were born in [Puerto Rico](23041.md), U.S. Island areas, or born abroad to American parent(s), and 5.6% were foreign born.[^cite_note-110] Foreign-born Pennsylvanians are largely from Asia (36.0%), Europe (35.9%), and Latin America (30.6%) with the remainder from Africa (5%), North America (3.1%), and Oceania (0.4%). The state's largest ancestry groups, expressed as a percentage of total people who responded with a particular ancestry for the 2010 census, were [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans) 28.5%, [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans) 18.2%, [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Americans) 12.8%, [African Americans](2154.md) 9.6%, [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Americans) 8.5%, [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Americans) 7.2%, and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Americans) 4.2%.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] As of 2018, the top countries of origin for Pennsylvania's immigrants were [India](14533.md), the [Dominican Republic](8060.md), [China](5405.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), and [Vietnam](202354.md).[^cite_note-113]

### Race and ethnicity



Ethnic origins of Pennsylvanians

Map of counties in Pennsylvania by racial and ethnic plurality, per the 2020 U.S. census

The vast majority of Pennsylvania's population is made up of Whites, Blacks, Hispanics, and Asian Americans. Non-Hispanic Whites make up the majority of Pennsylvania; they are mostly descended from German, Irish, Scottish, Welsh, Italian, and English immigrants. Rural portions of South Central Pennsylvania are recognized nationally for their [Amish communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amish#Population_and_distribution). [Wyoming Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming_Valley), including [Scranton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scranton,_Pennsylvania) and [Wilkes-Barre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkes-Barre,_Pennsylvania), has the highest percentage of white residents of any metropolitan area with a population of 500,000 or above in the U.S.; in Wyoming Valley, 96.2% of the population claim to be white with no Hispanic background. [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) have been present in the state since the mid-1800s.[^cite_note-115]

The state's Hispanic or Latino American population grew by 82.6% between 2000 and 2010, marking one of the largest increases in a state's Hispanic population. The significant growth of the Hispanic or Latino population is due to migration to the state mainly from [Puerto Rico](23041.md), a U.S. territory, and to a lesser extent immigration from countries such as the [Dominican Republic](8060.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), and various [Central](6121.md) and [South American](26769.md) nations and a wave of Hispanic and Latinos leaving [New York City](645042.md) and [New Jersey](21648.md) for more affordable living.[^cite_note-116]

The majority of Hispanic or Latino Americans in Pennsylvania are of [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_American) descent.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] Most of the remaining Hispanic or Latino population is made up of [Mexicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_American) and [Dominicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_American), and the majority of Hispanics and Latinos are concentrated in Philadelphia, the [Lehigh Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley), and South Central Pennsylvania.[^cite_note-119] The Hispanic or Latino population is greatest in [Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_Pennsylvania), [Allentown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allentown,_Pennsylvania), [Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading,_Pennsylvania), [Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster,_Pennsylvania), [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York,_Pennsylvania), and around Philadelphia. As of 2010, the vast majority of Hispanics and Latino Americans in Pennsylvania, about 85%, live within a 150-mile (240 km) radius of Philadelphia, and about 20% live in the city itself.

Pennsylvania's Asian population more than doubled between 2000 and 2020, comprising mostly Indian, Chinese, Vietnamese, Korean, and Filipino immigrants, and many Asians moving to [Philadelphia](50585.md) from New York City. The rapid growth of this community has given Pennsylvania one of the largest Asian populations in the nation.

Among the state's black population, the vast majority in the state are African American. There are also a growing number of black residents of [West Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indian_American), [recent African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_immigration_to_the_United_States), and [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hispanic) origins.[^cite_note-120] Most Blacks live in the Philadelphia area, Pittsburgh, or South Central Pennsylvania. Additionally, in 2020, 31,052 identified as being Native American alone, and 158,112 did in combination with one or more other races.[^cite_note-Census.gov-121]

### Age and poverty

As of the 2010 census, Pennsylvania had the fourth-highest proportion of elderly (65+) citizens in the nation at 15.4%, compared to a national average of 13.0%.[^cite_note-147] According to U.S. Census Bureau estimates, the state's poverty rate was 12.5% in 2017 compared to 13.4% for the U.S. as a whole.[^cite_note-148]

### Languages



An [Amish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amish) family riding in a traditional [Amish buggy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amish_buggy) in [Lancaster County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster_County,_Pennsylvania). As of 2024, Pennsylvania has an Amish population of 92,660, the [largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_Amish_population) of any state in the nation.

As of 2010, 90.2% (10,710,239) of Pennsylvania residents age five and older spoke [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) at home as a [primary language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_language) while 4.1% (486,058) spoke [Spanish](26825.md), 0.9% (103,502) spoke [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), including [Pennsylvania Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Dutch_language), and 0.5% (56,052) spoke [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language), including [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese). In total, 9.9% (1,170,628) of Pennsylvania's population age 5 and older spoke a [mother tongue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_tongue) other than English.[^cite_note-149]

#### Pennsylvania Dutch language

Main article: [Pennsylvania Dutch language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Dutch_language)

[Pennsylvania German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Dutch_language), spoken by nearly one percent of Pennsylvania's population as of 2010, is often misleadingly called Pennsylvania Dutch. The term Dutch used to mean German,[^cite_note-150] (including, at the time, from the [Netherlands](21148.md)) before the Latin derived "German" replaced it. When referring to the language spoken by the [Pennsylvania Dutch people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Dutch), Pennsylvania German, it means [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) - the modern German language word for "German" is "Deutsch" - cognate with the Pennsylvania German word "Deitsch". Pennsylvania German is a descendant of German from the [West Central German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Central_German) dialect family and is closest to [Palatine German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatine_German_language). Pennsylvania German is still very vigorous as a first language among [Old Order Amish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amish) and [Old Order Mennonites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Order_Mennonite), principally in the [Lancaster County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster_County,_Pennsylvania) and [Berks County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berks_County,_Pennsylvania) areas; it is almost extinct as an everyday language outside the [plain communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain_people), though a few words have passed into English usage.

### Religion

Religious self-identification in Pennsylvania (April 2023 *[Franklin & Marshall College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_&_Marshall_College)* poll)

[^cite_note-Yost-2023-151]

1. [Unaffiliated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion_in_the_United_States) (32.0%)

2. [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_the_United_States) (29.0%)

3. [Catholicism](410868.md) (24.0%)

4. Other (14.0%)

Of the original [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies), Pennsylvania and [Rhode Island](25410.md) had the most religious freedom.[^cite_note-152] [Voltaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voltaire), writing of [William Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn) in 1733, observed: "The new sovereign also enacted several wise and wholesome laws for his colony, which have remained invariably the same to this day. The chief is, to ill-treat no person on account of religion, and to consider as brethren all those who believe in one God."[^cite_note-153] One result of this uncommon freedom was a wide religious [diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism), which continues to the present.

Pennsylvania's population in 2010 was 12,702,379; of these, 6,838,440 (53.8%) were estimated to belong to some sort of organized religion. According to the [Association of Religion Data Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Religion_Data_Archives) (ARDA) at [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University), the largest religious bodies in Pennsylvania by adherents were the [Catholic Church](606848.md) with 3,503,028 adherents, the [United Methodist Church](32304.md) with 591,734 members, and the [Evangelical Lutheran Church in America](248255.md) with 501,974 members. Since 2014, among the state's religious population, 73% were [Christian](5211.md), according to [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center).[^cite_note-154] In 2020, the [Public Religion Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Religion_Research_Institute) estimated 68% of the population identified with Christianity.[^cite_note-155] As of 2014, 47% of all Pennsylvanians identified as [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_the_United_States), making [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) far and away the most prominent religious affiliation among Pennsylvanians. Among all self-identified Christians in the state, however, 24% identified as Catholics, the most of any Christian religious affiliation. In April 2023, a *[Franklin & Marshall College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_&_Marshall_College)* poll found that a plurality of Pennsylvania residents were [unaffiliated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion_in_the_United_States), with the rest predominately being Protestant or Catholic.[^cite_note-Yost-2023-151]

Pennsylvania, especially the [Greater Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Pittsburgh) area, has one of the largest communities of [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism) in the nation, the third-highest by percentage of population and the largest outright in membership as [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) Christians.[^cite_note-156] The [American Presbyterian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_\(USA\)), with about 250,000 members and 1,011 congregations, is the largest Presbyterian denomination, and the [Presbyterian Church in America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_in_America) is also significant, with 112 congregations and approximately 23,000 adherents; the [EPC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Presbyterian_Church_\(United_States\)) has around 50 congregations, including the [ECO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Covenant_Order_of_Presbyterians), according to 2010 estimates. The fourth-largest Protestant denomination, the [United Church of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Church_of_Christ), has 180,000 members and 627 congregations in the state. The [American Baptist Churches USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Baptist_Churches_USA), also referred to as the Northern Baptist Convention is based in [King of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Prussia,_Pennsylvania).

Pennsylvania was the center state of the [German Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_and_Reformed_Church) denomination from the 1700s.[^cite_note-157] [Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_Pennsylvania) is one of the headquarters of the [Moravian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_Church) in the U.S. Pennsylvania also has a very large [Amish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amish) population, second only to [Ohio](22199.md) among U.S. states.[^cite_note-158] As of 2000, there was a total Amish population of 47,860 in Pennsylvania and an additional 146,416 [Mennonites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mennonite) and 91,200 [Brethren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwarzenau_Brethren). The total [Anabaptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anabaptism) population including [Bruderhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruderhof_Communities) was 232,631, about two percent of the population.[^cite_note-159] While Pennsylvania owes its existence to [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers), and much of the historic character of Pennsylvania is ideologically rooted in the teachings of the [Religious Society of Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Society_of_Friends) (as they are officially known), practicing Quakers are a small minority of about 10,000 adherents as of 2010.[^cite_note-160]

## Economy

See also: [List of Pennsylvania counties by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pennsylvania_counties_by_per_capita_income)



A graph of unemployment in Pennsylvania vs United States average

In 2025, Pennsylvania's [gross state product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product) (GSP) was \$1.056 trillion, the [sixth-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_GDP) among all U.S. states, behind [California](5407.md), [Texas](29810.md), [New York](8210131.md), [Florida](18933066.md), and [Illinois](14849.md).[^cite_note-161] If Pennsylvania [were an independent country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)), its economy, as of 2023, would rank as the 20th-largest in the world.[^cite_note-162] On a per capita basis, Pennsylvania's per capita personal income was \$74,231 in 2025, ranked 22nd among the 50 states and the District of Columbia.[^cite_note-163] As of 2016, there were 5,354,964 people in employment in Pennsylvania with 301,484 total employer establishments. In 2025, 99.6% of businesses in Pennsylvania were [small businesses](276495.md), and employed 45.2% of the state's work force.[^cite_note-164] As of May 2025, the state's unemployment rate is 3.9%.[^cite_note-165]

The state has five manufacturing centers: [Philadelphia](50585.md) in the southeast, [Pittsburgh](25101.md) in the southwest, [Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie,_Pennsylvania) in the northwest, [Scranton-Wilkes-Barre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming_Valley) in the northeast, and the [Lehigh Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley) in the east.[^cite_note-166] Since the inception of the 21st century, Pennsylvania has garnered the additional nickname of "Cellicon State" for the central role of Philadelphia and Pittsburgh in the development of [immunotherapies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunotherapy) to treat different cancers.[^cite_note-University_of_Pennsylvania_-_Penn_Medicine-2025-14]

Pennsylvania is home to 23 of the nation's 500 largest companies that comprise the [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500), including two that rank in the top 100, [Cencora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cencora) (formerly AmeriSource Bergen) in [Conshohocken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conshohocken,_Pennsylvania), which is the nation's 11th-largest company, and [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast) in Philadelphia, which is the 29th-largest.[^cite_note-167] Philadelphia is home to six of the *Fortune* 500 companies,[^cite_note-CNN-2007-168] with more located in suburbs like [King of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_of_Prussia,_Pennsylvania); it is a leader in the financial[^cite_note-169] and insurance industries. Pittsburgh is home to eight *Fortune* 500 companies, including [U.S. Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Steel), [PPG Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PPG_Industries), [Heinz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz), and [GE Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Transportation).[^cite_note-CNN-2007-168] Hershey is home to [The Hershey Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hershey_Company), one of the world's largest chocolate manufacturers. In eastern Pennsylvania, the Lehigh Valley has become an epicenter for the growth of the U.S. [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) industry, including [warehousing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warehousing) and the [intermodal transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermodal_freight_transport) of goods.[^cite_note-170]

Like many U.S. states, [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart) is the largest private employer in Pennsylvania. The state's second-largest employer is the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), an [Ivy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League) private [research university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university) in Philadelphia.[^cite_note-171] Pennsylvania is home to the oldest investor-owned utility company in the U.S., [The York Water Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_York_Water_Company).

As of 2018, Pennsylvania ranks first in the nation in a few economic sectors and niches, including barrels of [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer) produced annually (3.9 million), [farmers' markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmers'_market) (over 6,000), [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing) companies (2,300), hardwood [lumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumber) production (a billion board feet annually), [mushroom farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungiculture) (68), [natural gas](22131.md) production, [potato chip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato_chip) manufacturing (24 facilities manufacturing one-fourth of the nation's total), and [pretzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretzel) manufacturing (80 percent of the nation's total).[^cite_note-172]

Other notable Pennsylvania companies include [C. F. Martin & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._F._Martin_&_Company), based in [Nazareth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazareth,_Pennsylvania), which manufacturers Martin Guitars, used by some of the world's most prominent [guitarists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guitar), including [Johnny Cash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Cash), [Elvis Presley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvis_Presley), [Bob Dylan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Dylan), [Neil Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Young), [John Lennon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennon), [Kurt Cobain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Cobain), [Eric Clapton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Clapton), and others.[^cite_note-173]

### Agriculture

Main article: [Agriculture in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_Pennsylvania)

Pennsylvania ranks 19th overall among U.S. states in agricultural production.[^cite_note-174] Its leading agricultural products are [mushrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fungiculture), apples, [Christmas trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_tree), [layer chickens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_\(food\)), [nursery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nursery_\(horticulture\)), [sod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sod), milk, [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) for [silage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silage), grapes (including [juice grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape_juice)), and horses production. Pennsylvania ranks eighth in the nation in [winemaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winemaking).

The [Pennsylvania Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Department_of_Agriculture) worked with private companies to establish "PA Preferred" as a way to brand agricultural products grown or made in the state.[^cite_note-175] The financial impact of agriculture in Pennsylvania[^cite_note-176] includes employment of more than 66,800 people employed by the food [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) industry and over \$1.7 billion in food product [export](307592.md) as of 2011.

### Banking

The first nationally chartered bank in the U.S., the [Bank of North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_North_America), was founded in 1781 in Philadelphia. After a series of mergers, the Bank of North America is now part of [Wells Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo). Pennsylvania is home to the first nationally chartered bank under the 1863 [National Banking Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Banking_Act). That year, the Pittsburgh Savings & Trust Company received a national charter and renamed itself the First National Bank of Pittsburgh as part of the National Banking Act. That bank is still in existence today as [PNC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNC_Financial_Services) and remains based in Pittsburgh. PNC is currently the state's largest and the nation's sixth-largest bank.

### Mining

Coal mining in Pennsylvania dates back to the mid-1700s. Since then, over 15 billion tons of coal were removed from the state. Production peaked in 1918. As mine output decreased, some 250,000 acres of mine land were abandoned.[^cite_note-179][^cite_note-180] As of 2024, the state has the largest inventory of abandoned mines in the United States, creating environmental problems such as water pollution and ground subsidence which damages above-ground buildings. [Allegheny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_County,_Pennsylvania) and [Westmoreland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmoreland_County,_Pennsylvania) counties alone account for over 550 abandoned sites. The Pennsylvania Department of Environmental Protection is responsible for remediation of such problems.[^cite_note-181]

### Healthcare

### Judiciary

Main article: [Judiciary of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_Pennsylvania)

Pennsylvania is divided into 60 judicial districts.[^cite_note-Aopc.org-191] With the exception of [Philadelphia County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_County,_Pennsylvania), most have district justices and justices of the peace who preside over most preliminary hearings in felony and misdemeanor offenses, all minor (summary) criminal offenses, and small civil claims.[^cite_note-Aopc.org-191] Most criminal and civil cases originate in the Courts of Common Pleas, which also serve as [appellate court](643.md).[^cite_note-Aopc.org-191] The [Superior Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Court_of_Pennsylvania) hears all appeals from the Courts of Common Pleas not expressly designated to the [Commonwealth Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Court_of_Pennsylvania) or [Supreme Court](31737.md). The Superior Court also has original jurisdiction to review [probable cause](146751.md) governmental requests for [warrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrant_\(law\)) in [wiretap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telephone_tapping) surveillance.[^cite_note-Aopc.org-191] The Commonwealth Court is limited to appeals from final orders of certain state agencies and certain designated cases from the Courts of Common Pleas.[^cite_note-Aopc.org-191] The [Supreme Court of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Pennsylvania) is the state's final appellate court. All judges in Pennsylvania are elected, and the [chief justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_justice) of the state's Supreme Court is determined by seniority.[^cite_note-Aopc.org-191]

### Local government

Main article: [Local government in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Pennsylvania)



Map of [Pennsylvania's 67 counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Pennsylvania)



[Pennsylvania Municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Pennsylvania)

Pennsylvania is divided into 67 [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-The_Pennsylvania_Manual-192] Counties are further subdivided into municipalities that are either incorporated as cities, [boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_\(Pennsylvania\)), or [townships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Township_\(Pennsylvania\)).[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Manual-193] The most populous county in Pennsylvania and [24th-most populous county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_most_populous_counties_in_the_United_States) in the United States is [Philadelphia County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_County,_Pennsylvania), which includes the city of [Philadelphia](50585.md), with a 2020 population of 1,603,797; the state's least populous county is [Cameron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron_County,_Pennsylvania) with a population of 4,547.[^cite_note-194]

There are a total of 56 cities in Pennsylvania, which are classified by population as either first-class, second-class, or third-class cities.[^cite_note-The_Pennsylvania_Manual-192][^cite_note-195] Philadelphia, the state's largest city with a population exceeding 1.6 million, is Pennsylvania's only first-class city.[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Manual-193] [Pittsburgh](25101.md) (303,000) and [Scranton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scranton,_Pennsylvania) (76,000) are second-class and second-class 'A' cities, respectively.[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Manual-193] All of the state's remaining cities including [Allentown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allentown,_Pennsylvania), the state's third-largest city, and [Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading,_Pennsylvania), its fourth-largest, to [Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker,_Pennsylvania), the state's smallest city with a population of only 820, are designated as third-class cities.[^cite_note-196] First- and second-class cities are governed by a "strong mayor" form of [mayor–council government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor%E2%80%93council_government), whereas third-class cities are governed by either a "weak mayor" form of government or a [council–manager government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council%E2%80%93manager_government).[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Manual-193]

Pennsylvania boroughs are generally smaller in population than the state's cities, and most of the state's cities were incorporated as boroughs prior to being designated cities.[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Manual-193] There are 958 boroughs in Pennsylvania, all of which are governed by the "weak mayor" form of mayor-council government.[^cite_note-The_Pennsylvania_Manual-192][^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Manual-193] The largest borough in Pennsylvania is [State College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_College,_Pennsylvania) (40,501) and the smallest is [Centralia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralia,_Pennsylvania).

Townships are the third type of municipality in Pennsylvania and are classified as either first-class or second-class townships. There are 1,454 second-class townships and 93 first-class townships.[^cite_note-The_Pennsylvania_Manual-2-197] Second-class townships can become first-class townships if they have a population density greater than 300 inhabitants per square mile (120/km<sup>2</sup>) and a [referendum](51582.md) is passed supporting the change.[^cite_note-The_Pennsylvania_Manual-2-197] Pennsylvania's largest township is [Upper Darby Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Darby_Township,_Delaware_County,_Pennsylvania) (85,681), and the smallest is [East Keating Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Keating_Township,_Clinton_County,_Pennsylvania).

There is one exception to the types of municipalities in Pennsylvania: [Bloomsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomsburg,_Pennsylvania) was incorporated as a town in 1870 and is, officially, the only town in the state.[^cite_note-198] In 1975, [McCandless Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCandless_Township,_Allegheny_County,_Pennsylvania) adopted a home-rule charter under the name of "Town of McCandless", but is, legally, still a first-class township.[^cite_note-199] The state has 56 cities, 958 boroughs, 93 first-class townships, 1,454 second-class townships, and one town (Bloomsburg) for a total of 2,562 municipalities.

### Taxation

Pennsylvania had the 15th-highest state and local tax burden in the nation as of 2012, according to the [Tax Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_Foundation).[^cite_note-200] Residents paid a total of \$83.7 billion in state and local taxes with a per capita average of \$4,589 annually. Residents share 76% of the total tax burden. Many state politicians have tried to increase the share of taxes paid by out-of-state sources. Suggested revenue sources include taxing natural gas drilling as Pennsylvania is the only state without such a tax on gas drilling.[^cite_note-201] Additional revenue prospects include trying to place tolls on interstate highways; specifically [Interstate 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_80_in_Pennsylvania), which is used heavily by out of state commuters with high maintenance costs.[^cite_note-202]

[Sales taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) provide 39% of Pennsylvania's state revenue; [personal income taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_income_tax) 34%; motor vehicle taxes about 12%, and taxes on [cigarettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cigarette_tax#Taxation) and alcoholic beverages 5%.[^cite_note-203] The personal income tax is a flat 3.07%. An individual's taxable income is based on the following eight types of income: compensation (salary); interest; dividends; net profits from the operation of a business, profession or farm; net gains or income from the dispositions of property; net gains or income from rents, royalties, patents and copyrights; income derived through estates or trusts; and gambling and [lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lottery) winnings (other than [Pennsylvania Lottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Lottery) winnings).[^cite_note-204]

Counties, municipalities, and [school districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district) levy taxes on real estate. In addition, some local bodies assess a [wage tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) on personal income. Generally, the total wage tax rate is capped at 1% of income but some municipalities with [home rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_rule) charters may charge more than 1%. Thirty-two of Pennsylvania's sixty-seven counties levy a [personal property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) on stocks, bonds, and similar holdings. With the exception of the city of [Philadelphia](50585.md), Pennsylvania, municipalities and school districts are allowed to enact a local earned income tax within the purview of Act 32. Residents of these municipalities and school districts are required to file a local income tax return in addition to federal and state returns. This local return is filed with the local income tax collector, a private collection agency appointed by a particular county to collect the local earned income and local services tax (the latter a flat fee deducted from salaried employees working within a particular municipality or school district).[^cite_note-205]

[Philadelphia](50585.md) has its own local income taxation system. Philadelphia-based employers are required to withhold the Philadelphia wage tax from the salaries of their employees. Residents of Philadelphia working for an employer are not required to file a local return as long as their Philadelphia wage tax is fully withheld by their employer. If their employer does not withhold the Philadelphia wage tax, residents are required to register with the Revenue Department and file an Earnings Tax return. Residents of Philadelphia with self-employment income are required to file a Net Profits Tax (NPT) return, while those with business income from Philadelphia sources are required to obtain a Commercial Activity License (CAL) and pay the Business Income and Receipts Tax (BIRT) and the NPT. Residents with unearned income except interest from checking and savings accounts are required to file and pay the School Income-tax (SIT).[^cite_note-206]

The complexity of Pennsylvania's local tax filing system has been criticized by experts, who note that the outsourcing of collections to private entities is akin to [tax farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_farming) and that many new residents are caught off guard and end up facing failure to file penalties even if they did not owe any tax. Attempts to transfer local income tax collections to the state level by having a separate local section on the state income tax return, currently the method used to collect local income taxes in [New York](8210131.md), [Maryland](18858.md), [Indiana](21883857.md), and [Iowa](26810748.md), have been unsuccessful.[^cite_note-207]

Since the latter half of the 20th century, Pennsylvania has been perceived as a powerful [swing state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_state), and winning Pennsylvania has since been deemed as essential to [U.S. presidential candidates](24113.md). Only thrice between [1932](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_United_States_presidential_election) and [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_United_States_presidential_election) (1932, 1948, and [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_United_States_presidential_election), with [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt), [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman), and [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon), respectively) has a presidential candidate been able to win the White House while losing Pennsylvania.

Between 1992 and 2016, Pennsylvania trended [Democratic](5043544.md) in presidential elections; [Bill Clinton](3356.md) won the state twice by large margins and [Al Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gore) won it by a slightly closer margin in 2000. In the [2004 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_presidential_election_in_Pennsylvania), [John F. Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kerry) beat President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) in Pennsylvania, 2,938,095 (51%) to 2,793,847 (48%). In the [2008 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_United_States_presidential_election_in_Pennsylvania), Democrat [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) defeated [Republican](32070.md) [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain) in Pennsylvania, 3,276,363 (54%) to 2,655,885 (44%).

In the [2016 United States presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_States_presidential_election), however, Republican [Donald Trump](4848272.md) broke the Democratic streak in the state, winning by 2,970,733 (48%) votes to 2,926,441 (47%) votes.[^cite_note-209] The state returned to the Democratic column in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_election_in_Pennsylvania) by voting for [Joe Biden](145422.md) over Trump, 3,458,229 (50%) to 3,377,674 (49%). In [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_Pennsylvania), Pennsylvania swung back to Trump, with the former president winning 3,543,308 (50%) to Vice President [Kamala Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Harris)'s 3,423,042 (49%). The state holds 19 [electoral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Electoral_College) votes.[^cite_note-210]

Despite voting for the Democratic ticket for president in every election between 1992 and 2012, Pennsylvania has a history of electing Republican U.S. senators. From 2009 to 2011, the state was represented by two Democratic senators for the first time since 1947 after Republican Senator [Arlen Specter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlen_Specter) switched party affiliation. In 2010, Republicans recaptured a U.S. Senate seat and a majority of the state's congressional seats, control of both chambers of the state legislature, and the governorship. Democrats won back the governorship, however, four years later in the [2014 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_gubernatorial_election,_2014). It was the first time since a governor became eligible for reelection that an incumbent governor had been defeated in a reelection bid.

Historically, Democratic strength was concentrated in Philadelphia in the southeast, the Pittsburgh, and [Johnstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnstown,_Pennsylvania) areas in the southwest, and [Scranton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scranton,_Pennsylvania) and [Wilkes-Barre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkes-Barre,_Pennsylvania) in the northeast. Republican strength was concentrated in the Philadelphia suburbs and the more rural areas in the state's central, northeastern, and western portions, some of which have long been considered among the nation's most conservative areas. Since 1992, however, the Philadelphia suburbs have swung Democratic; the brand of Republicanism there was traditionally moderate. In the 21st century, however, Pittsburgh suburbs, which historically had been Democratic strongholds, have swung more Republican.

Democratic political consultant [James Carville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Carville) once pejoratively described Pennsylvania as "Philadelphia in the east, Pittsburgh in the west, and Alabama in the middle", suggesting that political power in the state was based in its two largest cities, which have been reliably Democratic, offset by the state's large rural power base, which has proven equally reliably Republican. Political analysts and editorials refer to central Pennsylvania as the "T" in statewide elections. The state's three valleys ([Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Valley), [Lehigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley), and [Wyoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming_Valley) valleys) and [Greater Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Pittsburgh) generally vote Democratic, while the majority of the counties in the central part of the state vote Republican. As a result, maps showing the results of statewide elections invariably form a shape that resembles a "T".

Pennsylvania retains the [death penalty](5902.md), although there is currently a gubernatorial hold on executions.[^cite_note-211]

### Higher education

See also: [List of colleges and universities in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_Pennsylvania)



The *[Statue of Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statue_of_Benjamin_Franklin_\(University_of_Pennsylvania\))* on the campus of the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania) in [West Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Philadelphia), which pays tribute to [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin), a [Founding Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Father_of_the_United_States) who founded the university, now an [Ivy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League) institution and one of the world's top universities, in 1740

[^cite_note-218]

"Pennsylvania has the fourth most higher education institutions of any state", according to *[Inside Higher Ed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inside_Higher_Ed)*, with 250 universities and colleges.[^cite_note-219] The state is ranked second among the nation's top destinations for freshman out-of-state college students, according to [NPR](29697232.md)/[PBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBS) affiliate [WHYY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WHYY-TV), which cites a study by the [Association of Independent Colleges and Universities of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Independent_Colleges_and_Universities_of_Pennsylvania) (AICUP).[^cite_note-220] Pennsylvania is third in the nation for the quantity of "Best Colleges", according to *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)*.[^cite_note-221]

The [Pennsylvania State System of Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_System_of_Higher_Education) (PASSHE), which includes 14 state-owned universities and colleges, is Pennsylvania's [public university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university) system. [West Chester University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Chester_University) is by far the largest of the 14 with nearly 15,000 students. The [Commonwealth System of Higher Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_System_of_Higher_Education) is the organizing body of Pennsylvania's four state-related schools, which include [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University), [Lincoln University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_University_\(Pennsylvania\)), the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh), and [Temple University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_University). There are 15 publicly funded two-year [community colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_colleges_in_the_United_States) and technical schools in Pennsylvania that are separate from the PASSHE system, and many private two- and four-year technical schools, colleges, and universities.

[Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), [Pennsylvania State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_University), the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania), and the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) are members of the [Association of American Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_American_Universities), an invitation-only organization of leading research universities. [Lehigh University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_University), founded in 1865 and located in [Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_Pennsylvania), is a private research university. The Pennsylvania State University is Pennsylvania's [land-grant university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land-grant_university), [Sea Grant College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Sea_Grant_College_Program), and [Space Grant College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Space_Grant_College_and_Fellowship_Program).

The University of Pennsylvania, founded in [West Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Philadelphia) in 1740 by [Benjamin Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Franklin), a [Founding Father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_Father_of_the_United_States) of the nation, is Pennsylvania's only [Ivy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivy_League) university, and is the geographically most southern of the nation's eight Ivy League universities. The University of Pennsylvania was one of the [first universities established in the nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_university_in_the_United_States); its medical school, [Perelman School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perelman_School_of_Medicine), founded in 1765, was the first [medical school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_medical_schools_in_the_United_States) established in the nation.

The [Lake Erie College of Osteopathic Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie_College_of_Osteopathic_Medicine) (LECOM) is a private graduate school of medicine, dentistry, and pharmacy with a main campus in Erie, a branch campus in [Greensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensburg,_Pennsylvania), and two additional campuses outside Pennsylvania. It is the largest medical school in the United States.[^cite_note-222] The [Pennsylvania Academy of the Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Academy_of_the_Fine_Arts) is the first and oldest [art school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_school) in the United States.[^cite_note-223] [Philadelphia College of Pharmacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_College_of_Pharmacy), now part of [University of the Sciences in Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Sciences), was the first [pharmacy school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacy_school) in the United States.[^cite_note-224]

## Recreation

See also: [Gambling in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gambling_in_Pennsylvania), [List of festivals in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_festivals_in_Pennsylvania), and [List of Pennsylvania state parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pennsylvania_state_parks)



[Dorney Park & Wildwater Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorney_Park_&_Wildwater_Kingdom)'s [Steel Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Force) and [Thunderhawk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderhawk_\(Dorney_Park\)) roller coasters in [Allentown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allentown,_Pennsylvania); Steel Force is the eighth-longest [steel roller coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_roller_coaster) in the world with a first drop of 205 feet (62 m) and a top speed of 75 miles per hour (121 km/h).

[^cite_note-225]

Pennsylvania is home to the nation's first zoo, the [Philadelphia Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Zoo).[^cite_note-226] Other zoos also accredited by the [Association of Zoos and Aquariums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Zoos_and_Aquariums#Accreditation) (AZA) are the [Pittsburgh Zoo & PPG Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Zoo_&_PPG_Aquarium), [Lehigh Valley Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley_Zoo) and [ZooAmerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZooAmerica).[^cite_note-227] [Erie Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_Zoo) was formerly accredited by AZA, but lost its accreditation in 2021.[^cite_note-228]

Pennsylvania is home to some of the most notable museums in the nation, including the [Allentown Art Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allentown_Art_Museum) in [Allentown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allentown,_Pennsylvania), [Carnegie Museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Museums) in [Pittsburgh](25101.md), the [Philadelphia Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Museum_of_Art) in [Philadelphia](50585.md), and [several others](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Museums_in_Pennsylvania). One unique museum is the [Houdini Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houdini_Museum) in Scranton, the only building in the world devoted to the legendary magician.[^cite_note-229] Pennsylvania is also home to the [National Aviary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aviary), located in Pittsburgh.

All 121 [state parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pennsylvania_state_parks) in Pennsylvania feature free admission.

Pennsylvania's notable amusement parks include [Conneaut Lake Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conneaut_Lake_Park), [Dorney Park & Wildwater Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorney_Park_&_Wildwater_Kingdom), [Dutch Wonderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Wonderland), [DelGrosso's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DelGrosso's_Park), [Great Wolf Lodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wolf_Lodge), [Hersheypark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hersheypark), [Idlewild and Soak Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idlewild_and_Soak_Zone), [Kalahari Resorts Poconos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalahari_Resorts), [Kennywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennywood), [Knoebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knoebels), [Lakemont Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakemont_Park), [Sandcastle Waterpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandcastle_Waterpark), [Sesame Place Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame_Place_Philadelphia), and [Waldameer & Water World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldameer_&_Water_World). The largest indoor waterpark resort on the [U.S. East Coast](89126.md) is [Splash Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Splash_Lagoon) in [Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie,_Pennsylvania).

The state's notable music festivals include [Musikfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musikfest), the nation's largest free music festival held annually each August in [Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_Pennsylvania),[^cite_note-230] the [Philadelphia Folk Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Folk_Festival), [Creation Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creation_Festival), and Purple Door. The [Great Allentown Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Allentown_Fair), held annually at the [Allentown Fairgrounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allentown_Fairgrounds) since the 19th century, is one of the nation's longest-running annual fairs.

There are nearly one million licensed hunters in Pennsylvania. [White-tail deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-tail_deer), [black bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_black_bear), [cottontail rabbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottontail_rabbit), [squirrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squirrel), [turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkey_\(bird\)), and [grouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouse) are common game species. Pennsylvania is considered one of the finest [wild turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_turkey) hunting states in the nation, alongside [Texas](29810.md) and [Alabama](303.md). Sport hunting in Pennsylvania provides a massive boost for the state's economy. A report from The Center for Rural Pennsylvania, a legislative agency of the [Pennsylvania General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_General_Assembly), reported that hunting, fishing, and furtaking generated a total of \$9.6 billion statewide.

The [Boone and Crockett Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boone_and_Crockett_Club) reports that five of the ten largest [black bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_black_bear) entries came from the state.[^cite_note-Reilly-2007-231] The state also has a tied record for the largest hunter shot black bear in the Boone and Crockett record books at 733 lb (332 kg) and a [skull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skull) of 23 3/16, tied with a bear shot in [California](5407.md) in 1993.[^cite_note-Reilly-2007-231] As of 2007, Pennsylvania has the second-highest number of Boone and Crockett-recorded record black bears at 183, behind [Wisconsin](33127.md)'s 299.[^cite_note-Reilly-2007-231]

The [Pennsylvania Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Department_of_Transportation), abbreviated as PennDOT, is responsible for transport issues in Pennsylvania.

### Air

See also: [List of airports in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Pennsylvania)



[Philadelphia International Airport](262559.md), the busiest airport in the state and the [21st-busiest airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_the_United_States) in the nation with over 13 million passengers in 2023

Pennsylvania has seven major airports: [Philadelphia International](262559.md), [Pittsburgh International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_International_Airport), [Lehigh Valley International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley_International_Airport), [Harrisburg International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg_International_Airport), [Wilkes-Barre/Scranton International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkes-Barre/Scranton_International_Airport), [Erie International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_International_Airport), and [State College Regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_College_Regional_Airport). Intercity bus service is provided between cities in Pennsylvania and other major points in the Northeast by [BoltBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BoltBus), [Fullington Trailways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fullington_Trailways), [Greyhound Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines), [Martz Trailways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martz_Trailways), [Megabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabus_\(North_America\)), [OurBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OurBus), [Trans-Bridge Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Bridge_Lines), and various [Chinatown bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown_bus_lines) companies. ### Highways and roads

See also: [List of Interstate Highways in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Interstate_Highways_in_Pennsylvania), [List of state routes in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_routes_in_Pennsylvania), and [Pennsylvania Turnpike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Turnpike)

PennDOT owns 39,861 miles (64,150 km) of the 121,770 miles (195,970 km) of roadway in the state, making it the fifth-largest state highway system in the United States.[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Department_of_Transportation_Fact_Book-233] The [Pennsylvania Turnpike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Turnpike) system is 535 miles (861 km) long, with the mainline portion stretching from [Ohio](22199.md) to Philadelphia and [New Jersey](21648.md).[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Department_of_Transportation_Fact_Book-233] It is overseen by the [Pennsylvania Turnpike Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Turnpike_Commission). Another major east–west route is [Interstate 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_80_in_Pennsylvania), which runs primarily in the northern tier of the state from Ohio to New Jersey at the [Delaware Water Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware_Water_Gap). [Interstate 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_90_in_Pennsylvania) travels the relatively short distance between Ohio and New York through [Erie County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_County,_Pennsylvania), in the extreme northwestern part of the state.

Primary north–south highways are [Interstate 79](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_79_in_Pennsylvania) from its terminus in Erie through [Pittsburgh](25101.md) to [West Virginia](32905.md), [Interstate 81](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_81_in_Pennsylvania) from [New York state](8210131.md) through [Scranton, Lackawanna County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scranton,_Pennsylvania) and [Harrisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg,_Pennsylvania) to [Maryland](18858.md) and [Interstate 476](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_476), which begins 7 miles (11 km) north of the [Delaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delaware) border, in [Chester, Delaware County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chester,_Pennsylvania) and travels 132 miles (212 km) to [Clarks Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarks_Summit,_Pennsylvania), where it joins I-81. All but 20 miles (32 km) of I-476 is the Northeast Extension of the [Pennsylvania Turnpike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Turnpike). The highway south of the Pennsylvania Turnpike is officially called the "Veterans Memorial Highway", but is commonly referred to colloquially as the "Blue Route".

### Rail

See also: [List of Pennsylvania railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pennsylvania_railroads) and [List of public transit authorities in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_transit_authorities_in_Pennsylvania)



[Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak)'s [*Pennsylvanian*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvanian_\(train\)) on [Horseshoe Curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe_Curve_\(Pennsylvania\)) in [Logan Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logan_Township,_Blair_County,_Pennsylvania)

[SEPTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEPTA) is the sixth-largest transit agency in the United States and operates the [commuter](6286.md), [heavy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_rail) and [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) transit, and [transit bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_bus) service in the Philadelphia metropolitan area. [Pittsburgh Regional Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Regional_Transit) is the 25th-largest transit agency and provides transit bus and light rail service in and around Pittsburgh.[^cite_note-234]

Intercity passenger rail transit is provided by [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak), with the majority of traffic occurring on the *[Keystone Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_Service)* in the high-speed [Keystone Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_Corridor) between Harrisburg and Philadelphia's [30th Street Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30th_Street_Station) before heading north to New York City, and the *[Northeast Regional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Regional)*, which provides regular high-speed service up and down the [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor). The *[Pennsylvanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvanian_\(train\))* follows the same route from New York City to Harrisburg, but extends out to Pittsburgh. The *[Capitol Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Limited_\(Amtrak_train\))* also passes through Pittsburgh, as well as [Connellsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connellsville,_Pennsylvania), on its way from Chicago to [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Department_of_Transportation_Fact_Book-2-232] Traveling between Chicago and New York City, the *[Lake Shore Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Shore_Limited)* passes through Erie once in each direction.[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Department_of_Transportation_Fact_Book-2-232] There are 67 [short-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-line_railroad), freight railroads operating in Pennsylvania, the highest number in any U.S. state.[^cite_note-Pennsylvania_Department_of_Transportation_Fact_Book-2-232] With more than four million [inter-city rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-city_rail) passengers in 2018, Philadelphia's 30th Street Station is Amtrak's third-busiest train station in the nation after [Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(New_York\)) in [Manhattan](45470.md) and [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Union_Station) in Washington, D.C.,[^cite_note-235] and North America's [12th-busiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_railway_stations_in_North_America) train station overall.

### Food



[Pat's King of Steaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat's_King_of_Steaks) in [South Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Philadelphia) is widely credited with inventing the [cheesesteak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheesesteak) in 1933.

[^cite_note-238]



[The Hershey Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hershey_Company) in [Hershey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hershey,_Pennsylvania)

In 2008, author Sharon Hernes Silverman wrote in the *[Pittsburgh Tribune-Review](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Tribune-Review)* that Pennsylvania was the [snack food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snack_food) capital of the world.[^cite_note-239] It leads all other states in the manufacture of [pretzels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretzel) and [potato chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato_chip). In 1861, as the [Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) was beginning, [Sturgis Pretzel House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturgis_Pretzel_House) in [Lititz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lititz,_Pennsylvania) was first to introduce the pretzel to American consumers. Two other Pennsylvania-based companies, Immergut Hand-Rolled Soft Pretzels in [Intercourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercourse,_Pennsylvania) and [Snyder's of Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snyder's_of_Hanover) in [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover,_Pennsylvania), are leading national pretzel manufacturers. Two of the nation's three leading potato chip companies are based in Pennsylvania: [Utz Brands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utz_Brands), which started making chips in Hanover in 1921, and [Wise Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wise_Foods), which started making chips in [Berwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwick,_Pennsylvania) the same year; the third, [Frito-Lay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frito-Lay) is owned by [Plano, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plano,_Texas)-based [PepsiCo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PepsiCo). Additional Pennsylvania-based companies, including [Herr's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herr's) in [Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham,_Pennsylvania), [Martin's Potato Chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin's_Potato_Chips) in [Thomasville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomasville,_Pennsylvania), are popular chip manufacturers.

[The Hershey Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hershey_Company) in [Hershey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hershey,_Pennsylvania) is a nearly \$9 billion a year company and one of the world's leading manufacturers of [chocolate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chocolate); the company was founded in Hershey by [Milton S. Hershey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_S._Hershey) in 1894.[^cite_note-240] [Gertrude Hawk Chocolates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_Hawk_Chocolates) is based in [Dunmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunmore,_Pennsylvania). Other notable companies include [Just Born](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Born) in [Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_Pennsylvania), makers of [Hot Tamales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Tamales), [Mike and Ikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_and_Ike), the Easter favorite marshmallow [Peeps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peeps), and [Boyer Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyer_Brothers) of [Altoona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altoona,_Pennsylvania), which manufacturers Mallo Cups. The pretzel company [Auntie Anne's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auntie_Anne's) began as a market-stand in [Downingtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downingtown,_Pennsylvania), and now has corporate headquarters in [Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster,_Pennsylvania).[^cite_note-241] Traditional Pennsylvania Dutch foods include chicken pot pie, ham pot pie, schnitz un knepp (dried apples, ham, and dumplings), [fasnachts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasnacht_\(doughnut\)) (raised doughnuts), scrapple, pretzels, bologna, chow-chow, and [Shoofly pie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoofly_pie). [Martin's Famous Pastry Shoppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin's_Famous_Pastry_Shoppe), based in [Chambersburg, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambersburg,_Pennsylvania), specializes in potato bread, another [traditional Pennsylvania Dutch food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_the_Pennsylvania_Dutch). [D.G. Yuengling & Son](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.G._Yuengling_&_Son), America's oldest brewery, has been brewing beer in [Pottsville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottsville,_Pennsylvania) since 1829.

Among the regional foods associated with Philadelphia are [cheesesteaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheesesteaks), [hoagies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoagies), [soft pretzels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_pretzels), [Italian water ice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_water_ice), [Irish potato candy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_potato_candy), [scrapple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrapple), [Tastykake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tastykake), and [strombolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stromboli_\(food\)). In Pittsburgh, tomato ketchup was improved by [Henry John Heinz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._J._Heinz_Company) from 1876 to the early 20th century. Famous to a lesser extent than Heinz ketchup is the Pittsburgh's [Primanti Brothers Restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primanti_Brothers_Restaurant) sandwiches, [pierogies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierogies), and [city chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_chicken). In northeastern Pennsylvania, Italian heritage has popularized a variety of pizza styles. Outside of [Scranton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scranton), in [Old Forge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Forge,_Lackawanna_County,_Pennsylvania), there are dozens of Italian restaurants specializing in pizza made with thick, light crust, and American cheese. [New York–style pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York%E2%80%93style_pizza) is popular in [Wilkes-Barre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkes-Barre,_Pennsylvania). [Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie) also has its share of foods, including Greek sauce and [sponge candy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponge_candy). [Sauerkraut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sauerkraut) along with pork and mashed potatoes is a traditional meal on New Year's Day in Pennsylvania; its tradition began with the [Pennsylvania Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Dutch) who believe the meal leads to good luck in the new year to come. Among other [foods and drinks commonly found in Pennsylvania Dutch Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_the_Pennsylvania_Dutch) are chicken pot pie (a type of soup made with egg noodles and unrelated to the more commonly found baked pie), [shoofly pie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoofly_pie), scrapple, sand tarts (a type of thin cookie), and [birch beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch_beer). [Whoopie pies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whoopie_pie), an American favorite, are also said to trace their roots back to [Pennsylvania Dutch Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Dutch_Country).

Pennsylvania is home to eight major league professional sports teams: the [Philadelphia Phillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Phillies) and [Pittsburgh Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pirates) of [Major League Baseball](38776.md), the [Philadelphia 76ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_76ers) of the [NBA](22093.md), the [Philadelphia Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Eagles) and [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers) of the [NFL](21211.md), the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) and [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) of the [NHL](21809.md), and the [Philadelphia Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Union) of [Major League Soccer](71802.md). Among them, these teams have accumulated seven [World Series](23489161.md) championships (with the Pirates winning five and Phillies winning two), 16 [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_\(baseball\)) pennants (with the Pirates winning nine and Phillies winning seven), three pre-[Super Bowl](27718.md) era NFL championships (all won by the Eagles), eight Super Bowl championships (with the Steelers winning six and the Eagles two), two NBA championships (both won by the 76ers), and seven [Stanley Cup](66968.md) championships (with the Penguins winning five and Flyers winning two).

With [five professional sports teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Philadelphia) and some of the most passionate sports fans in the nation, [Philadelphia](50585.md) is often described as the "nation's best sports city".[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243]

In addition to its two Major League Baseball franchises, Pennsylvania is home to two [Triple-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple-A_\(baseball\))-level teams, the highest level of [Minor League Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_League_Baseball) play. The [Lehigh Valley IronPigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley_IronPigs), affiliated with the Philadelphia Phillies, are based in [Allentown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allentown,_Pennsylvania), where they play at [Coca-Cola Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola_Park). The [Scranton/Wilkes-Barre RailRiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scranton/Wilkes-Barre_RailRiders), affiliated with the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees), are based in [Moosic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moosic,_Pennsylvania), where they play at [PNC Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNC_Field).

Pennsylvania is home to four [Double-A level baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-A_\(baseball\)) teams: the [Altoona Curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altoona_Curve), [Erie SeaWolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_SeaWolves), [Harrisburg Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg_Senators), and [Reading Fightin Phils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_Fightin_Phils). Pennsylvania has two [collegiate summer baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_summer_baseball) teams affiliated with the [MLB Draft League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_Draft_League): the [State College Spikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_College_Spikes) and [Williamsport Crosscutters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsport_Crosscutters). In independent baseball, the state has three teams, the [Lancaster Stormers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancaster_Stormers), [Washington Wild Things](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Wild_Things), and [York Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Revolution).

In addition to its two National Hockey League teams, Pennsylvania has three [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) [ice hockey](14790.md) teams: the [Hershey Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hershey_Bears) affiliated with the [Washington Capitals](73125.md), the [Lehigh Valley Phantoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley_Phantoms) affiliated with the Philadelphia Flyers, and the [Wilkes-Barre/Scranton Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilkes-Barre/Scranton_Penguins) affiliated with the Pittsburgh Penguins. It also has an [ECHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHL)-level ice hockey team, the [Reading Royals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading_Royals), and an [Arena Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Football_League) team, the [Philadelphia Soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Soul). These Pennsylvania-based developmental-level professional teams have accumulated 12 [Triple-A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triple-A_National_Championship_Game) and Double-A baseball league titles (Altoona Curve (1) Reading Fightin Phils (4), and Scranton/Wilkes-Barre Senators (6)), 3 [ArenaBowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArenaBowl) championships (Soul), and 11 [Calder Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calder_Cup) (Bears).

In addition to the Philadelphia Union of Major League Soccer, Pennsylvania has two [lower level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_soccer_league_system) professional soccer teams: [Philadelphia Union II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Union_II) of [MLS Next Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLS_Next_Pro) and the [Pittsburgh Riverhounds SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Riverhounds_SC) of the [USL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Championship).[^cite_note-244]

Since 1959, the [Little League World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_League_World_Series) has been held annually in August in [South Williamsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Williamsport,_Pennsylvania) near where [Little League Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_League_Baseball) was founded in [Williamsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williamsport,_Pennsylvania).[^cite_note-245]

In professional golf, [Arnold Palmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Palmer), one of the 20th century's most accomplished professional golfers, comes from [Latrobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latrobe,_Pennsylvania), and [Jim Furyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Furyk), a current [PGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_Golfers'_Association_of_America) player grew up near in [Lancaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manheim_Township,_Lancaster_County,_Pennsylvania). PGA tournaments in Pennsylvania include the 84 Lumber Classic played at Nemacolin Woodlands Resort in [Farmington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farmington,_Pennsylvania) and the Northeast Pennsylvania Classic played at Glenmaura National Golf Club in [Moosic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moosic,_Pennsylvania).

Philadelphia is home to [Love Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Love_Park), located across from [City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_City_Hall), which is a popular [skateboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skateboard) location that hosted [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN)'s [X Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X_Games) in 2001 and 2002.[^cite_note-246]

#### Motorsports

In motorsports, the [Mario Andretti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Andretti) dynasty of race drivers hails from [Nazareth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazareth,_Pennsylvania) in the [Lehigh Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Valley). Pennsylvania racetracks include [Jennerstown Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennerstown_Speedway) in [Jennerstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennerstown,_Pennsylvania), [Lake Erie Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie_Speedway) in [North East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East,_Pennsylvania), [Lernerville Speedway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lernerville_Speedway) in [Sarver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarver,_Pennsylvania), and [Pocono Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pocono_Raceway) in [Long Pond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Pond,_Pennsylvania), which is home to two [NASCAR Cup Series](56571945.md) races and an [IndyCar Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndyCar_Series) race. The state is also home to [Maple Grove Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_Grove_Raceway), near [Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reading,_Pennsylvania), which hosts major [National Hot Rod Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Hot_Rod_Association)-sanctioned drag racing events each year.

There are also two [motocross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motocross) race tracks that host a round of the AMA Toyota Motocross Championships in Pennsylvania. [High Point Raceway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Point_Raceway) is located in [Mount Morris, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Morris,_Pennsylvania), and Steel City is located in [Delmont, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delmont,_Pennsylvania).

[Horse racing](74711.md) tracks in Pennsylvania include [The Meadows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Casino_at_The_Meadows) in [North Strabane Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Strabane_Township,_Pennsylvania), [Mohegan Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohegan_Pennsylvania) in Wilkes-Barre, [Penn National](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Casino_at_Penn_National_Race_Course) in [Grantville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grantville,_Pennsylvania), [Presque Isle Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presque_Isle_Downs_&_Casino) in [Summit Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summit_Township,_Erie_County,_Pennsylvania), and [Parx Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parx_Casino_and_Racing), [Harrah's Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrah's_Philadelphia) in Chester, which was the home course of [Smarty Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smarty_Jones), winner of the [2004 Kentucky Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Kentucky_Derby) and [2004 Preakness Stakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Preakness_Stakes). Harrah's Philadelphia also hosts [harness racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harness_racing) and Presque Isle Downs also hosts [thoroughbred racing](67745.md).

#### College sports

In [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football), three Pennsylvania universities compete in [NCAA Division I](669931.md), the highest level of sanctioned collegiate play in the sport: [Penn State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_State_Nittany_Lions_football) in the [Big Ten Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Ten_Conference), [Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Panthers_football) in the [Atlantic Coast Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Coast_Conference), and [Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Owls_football) in the [American Athletic Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Athletic_Conference).

Over their respective college football histories, Penn State claims two [national championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football_national_championships_in_NCAA_Division_I_FBS) (1982 and 1986) and seven undefeated seasons (1887, 1912, 1968, 1969, 1973, 1986, and 1994) and Pitt has won nine national championships (1915, 1916, 1918, 1929, 1931, 1934, 1936, 1937, and 1976) and had eight undefeated seasons (1904, 1910, 1915, 1916, 1917, 1920, 1937, and 1976).[^cite_note-247] Penn State plays its home games at [Beaver Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver_Stadium), a 106,572-capacity stadium that is the [second-largest stadium in the nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_North_American_stadiums_by_capacity); the team is coached by [James Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Franklin_\(American_football_coach\)). Pitt plays its home games at [Acrisure Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrisure_Stadium), a 68,400-capacity stadium it shares with the [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers); the team is coached by [Pat Narduzzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Narduzzi). Over their respective histories, four additional Pennsylvania universities and colleges have won national college football championships: [Lafayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lafayette_Leopards_football) in [Easton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easton,_Pennsylvania) (1896), [Villanova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanova_Wildcats_football) in [Villanova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanova,_Pennsylvania) (2009), [Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_Quakers_football) in Philadelphia (1895, 1897, 1904, and 1908),[^cite_note-248] and [Washington & Jefferson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_&_Jefferson_Presidents) in [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_Pennsylvania) (1921).

In [college basketball](768856.md), six [Philadelphia-area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_area) universities (Drexel, La Salle, Penn, St. Joseph's, Temple, and Villanova), collectively known as the [Big 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Big_5), have a rivalry tradition. National titles in college basketball have been won by [La Salle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Salle_Explorers_men's_basketball) (1954), [Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_Owls_men's_basketball) (1938), [Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_Quakers_men's_basketball) (1920 and 1921), [Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Panthers_men's_basketball) (1928 and 1930), and [Villanova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanova_Wildcats_men's_basketball) (1985, 2016, and 2018).

Pennsylvania has several universities and colleges known as national leaders in [college wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_wrestling). [Penn State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_State_Nittany_Lions_wrestling), coached by [Cael Sanderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cael_Sanderson), has won ten [NCAA Division I Wrestling Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Wrestling_Championships) in its history, second most among all universities and colleges after [Oklahoma State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oklahoma_State_Cowboys_wrestling). [Lehigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lehigh_Mountain_Hawks) in [Bethlehem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bethlehem,_Pennsylvania) has had 28 NCAA Division I individual champions over its history.

## Nicknames

Since 1802, Pennsylvania has been known as the **Keystone State**, which remains the state's most popular and widely used nickname.[^cite_note-249] The nickname "Keystone State" originates with the agricultural and architectural term "[keystone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keystone_\(architecture\))", and is based on the central role that Pennsylvania played geographically and functionally among the original [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) from which the nation was established, the important founding documents, including the Declaration of Independence and U.S. Constitution, that were signed and ratified in Pennsylvania, and the central role that Pennsylvania played in the nation's early manufacturing and agricultural development.[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251]

Less often, Pennsylvania is referred to as **the Coal State**, **the Oil State**, and **the Steel State**, in recognition of the important role these respective industries played in the state in the 19th and 20th centuries.[^cite_note-252] **The State of Independence** appears on several current day road signs entering Pennsylvania from bordering states.

Pennsylvania residents and those of surrounding states sometimes refer to Pennsylvania by the state's abbreviation, **PA**.[^cite_note-253]

Pennsylvania was historically referred to by the nickname **Quaker State** during the [colonial era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pennsylvania)[^cite_note-254] based on the influential role that [William Penn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn) and other [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers) played in establishing the [first frame of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frame_of_Government_of_Pennsylvania) constitution for the Province of Pennsylvania that guaranteed [liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_\(political\)) of [conscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience), which was a reflection of Penn's knowledge of the hostility Quakers confronted when they opposed religious rituals, taking oaths, violence, war, and military service, and what they viewed as ostentatious [frippery](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/frippery).[^cite_note-255][^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257] In the 21st century, Pennsylvania has garnered the additional nickname of "Cellicon State" for the central role of Philadelphia and Pittsburgh in the development of [immunotherapies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunotherapy) to treat different cancers.[^cite_note-University_of_Pennsylvania_-_Penn_Medicine-2025-14]
