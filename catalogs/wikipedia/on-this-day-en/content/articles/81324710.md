**Saleh al-Jafarawi** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): صالح الجعفراوي, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Ṣāliḥ al-Jaʻfarāwī*; 22 November 1997 – 12 October 2025) was a Palestinian [citizen journalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen_journalist), [social media influencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_media_influencer) and [table tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_tennis) player from [Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_city). He was recognised during the [Gaza war](74998519.md) for his frontline video reporting and wide social media reach. During a period when [a ceasefire was in place](81215079.md) between [Hamas](13913.md) and [Israel](9282173.md),[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] Saleh was shot and killed by members of an "armed militia"[^cite_note-shot-3] of [Doghmush clan](12081036.md) members amid [clashes with Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Hamas-Doghmush_conflict) in the [Sabra](36311846.md) neighborhood of [Gaza City](55387.md) on 12 October 2025.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Early life

Saleh al-Jafarawi was born in [Gaza City](55387.md) in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), on 22 November 1997. He attended the [Islamic University of Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_University_of_Gaza), where he received a bachelor's degree in media and journalism in 2019. He began producing content around 2018 on the [Great March of Return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018%E2%80%932019_Gaza_border_protests), during which he covered the situation on the group as a freelance journalist for local media outlets.[^cite_note-9] He also worked independently as a freelance photographer and journalist on social media platforms. His reporting style often involved documenting civilian suffering and destruction during Israeli military operations in Gaza.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-\:0-11][^cite_note-\:1-12]

Prior to his journalism role, Al-Jafarawi was a [table tennis player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_table_tennis_players) and represented Palestine in international table competitions, including the [WTT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Table_Tennis_\(ITTF\)) Feeder in [Doha, Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doha,_Qatar) in 2023.[^cite_note-13]

## Career

Al-Jafarawi became well-known during the [Gaza war](74998519.md), when his videos from the conflict zones went viral on platforms such as Instagram, YouTube, and TikTok. He amassed a large following, and his content was widely shared.[^cite_note-\:0-11][^cite_note-\:1-12][^cite_note-\:2-14]

In November 2023, pro-Israel influencers falsely claimed that al-Jafarawi had faked a scene of being hospitalized and labelled the video as "[Pallywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallywood)." [Voice of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_America) reported that a video alleging to show Jafarawi faking hospitalization was in fact a misidentification.[^cite_note-15]

In March 2025, al-Jafarawi's [Instagram](31591547.md) account was suspended "indefinitely", forcing him to use a backup account.[^cite_note-16]

In late September 2025, al-Jafarawi's followers raised alarm after the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) Arabic-language spokesperson [Avichay Adraee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avichay_Adraee) alleged that al-Jafarawi was affiliated with Hamas. His followers feared that, like other Palestinian journalists in Gaza accused of associations with Hamas, al-Jafarawi could be targeted in the near future. They drew comparison to the journalist [Anas al-Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anas_Al-Sharif), who was targeted and killed in Gaza by Israel with similar allegations.[^cite_note-17]

## Death

On 12 October 2025, al-Jafarawi was shot and killed by [Doghmush clan](12081036.md) members amid [clashes with Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Hamas-Doghmush_conflict) in [Sabra](36311846.md), [Gaza City](55387.md).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-shot-3][^cite_note-19] Palestinian sources have indicated that there were confrontations in Hasher Sabra involving Hamas security personnel and members of the Daghmush tribe; however, local authorities have yet to verify this information.[^cite_note-shot-3]

After his death, [Instagram](31591547.md) removed al-Jafarawi's account which had 4.5 million followers at the time of his death as well as its digital archives from the [Wayback Machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayback_Machine).[^cite_note-20] According to [Meta](62420226.md), they justified the erasure of al-Jafarawi's account under its policy on "dangerous organizations and individuals." TRT World wrote that this justification has repeatedly been used to purge pro-Palestinian journalists and activists from its platforms.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]
