# Southern Syria

This article is about the Ottoman designation. For the southern part of the modern Arab Republic of Syria, see [As-Suwayda Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Suwayda_Governorate). For the Southern part of [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition) territories, see [Southern Front (Syrian rebel group)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Front_\(Syrian_rebel_group\)). For the newspaper, see [Suriyya al-Janubiyya (newspaper)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suriyya_al-Janubiyya_\(newspaper\)).

In the yellow area, corresponding to the Levant, the medium sized bold text at the far right says “برشام” (“Bar Sham” or "Outskirts of Damascus/Syria"), and at bottom, it says "ارض فلاستان" ("Land of Palestine"). Near the top of the yellow area, it also says "الشام" (*Al-Sham*, meaning "Damascus/Syria". At the bottom right outside the yellow area in large text it says "بادية العرب" ("The Arab Desert", *[badiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badia_\(region\)) il-Arab*)

**Southern Syria** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): سوريا الجنوبية, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Sūriyā al-Janūbiyya*) is a geographical term referring to the southern portion of either the Ottoman-period [Vilayet of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_vilayet),[^cite_note-1] or the modern-day [Arab Republic of Syria](7515849.md).

The term was used in the Arabic language primarily from 1919 until the end of the [Franco-Syrian war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Syrian_War) in July 1920, during which the [Arab Kingdom of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria) existed.[^cite_note-Gerber2008-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Zachary Foster, in his [Princeton University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princeton_University) [doctoral dissertation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesis), has written that in the decades prior to [World War I](4764461.md), the term “Southern Syria” was the least frequently used out of ten different ways to describe the [region of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_of_Palestine) in Arabic, noting it was so rare that “it took me nearly a decade to find a handful of references”.[^cite_note-6]

## Background

Throughout the Ottoman period, prior to 1888, the Levant was viewed administratively as part of one province called the [Vilayet of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_vilayet) and was divided into districts known as "[Sanjaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak)".

[Ottoman Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Palestine) was, by the end of 19th and early 20th centuries divided into the [Mutasarrifate of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutasarrifate_of_Jerusalem), the [Nablus Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Sanjak), and the [Acre Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre_Sanjak) (under [Beirut Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut_Vilayet) from 1888, and previously under [Syria Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_Vilayet)), and a short-lived [Mutasarrıfate of Karak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karak_Sanjak) in [Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transjordan_\(region\)) (split as a new administrative unit from [Syria Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_Vilayet) in 1894/5). In 1884, the governor of Damascus proposed the establishment of a new Vilayet in southern Syria, composed of the regions of Jerusalem, [Balqa'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balqa_\(region\)) and Ma'an though nothing came out of this.[^cite_note-Rogan2002p52-7]

In the beginning of Faisal’s reign in the [Arab Kingdom of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria), particularly after the San Remo Conference of March 1920, the term "Southern Syria" emerged as a political [neologism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neologism) synonymous with Palestine,[^cite_note-Gerber2008-2] and it would take on an increased political significance as a way of rejecting the separation of Palestine from the Kingdom.[^cite_note-8]

## Usage during British and French occupation

Further information: [Greater Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Syria), [Occupied Enemy Territory Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied_Enemy_Territory_Administration), and [Arab Kingdom of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria)

In the early 20th century, the term "Southern Syria" was a slogan that implied support for a [Greater Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Syria) nationalism associated with the [kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Kingdom_of_Syria) promised to the [Hashemite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashemites) dynasty of the [Hejaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz) by the British during World War I.



First Palestine Arab Congress resolution in February 1919, addressed to the Paris Peace Conference

After the war, the [Hashemite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashemites) prince [Faisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_I_of_Iraq) attempted to establish Pan-Levantine state —a united kingdom that would comprise all of what eventually became [Syria](7515849.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), [Israel](9282173.md), [Jordan](7515964.md), and [Palestine](241405.md), but he was stymied by conflicting promises made by the British to different parties (see [Sykes–Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes%E2%80%93Picot_Agreement)), leading to the [French](5843419.md) creation of the [mandate of Syria and Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon) in 1920.

One of the resolutions adopted at the first [Palestinian Arab Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Arab_Congress) in 1919, Jerusalem, was:

> "We consider Palestine nothing but part of Arab Syria, and it has never been separated from it at any stage. We are tied to it by national, religious, linguistic, moral, economic, and geographic bounds."[^cite_note-9]

&#x20;Yoav Gelber notes that the Historians of [Palestinian Nationalism](47246228.md) such as [Yehoshua Porath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yehoshua_Porath) and Muhammad Y. Muslih consider the declaration to have been ideologically disingenuous, pointing out that there was a split among Palestinians regarding the resolutions' implications. The younger generation was eager to pursue political opportunities in a unified Levantine state under [Faisal I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_I_of_Iraq), and the older generation wished for Palestine's Independence to maintain their autonomous power in the post-Ottoman world order. Nonetheless, the resolution was mainly supported in the hopes of thwarting support for the [Jewish National Homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_for_the_Jewish_people) that was introduced in the [1917 Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration) on the occasion of the upcoming [San Remo Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Remo_conference) by presenting that Palestine already belongs to another [nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation) than [Jews](25955086.md).[^cite_note-Muslih1988-10][^cite_note-11] The notables in Faisal's government in Damascus, such as Iraqis and Damascenes, including the Palestinians, had some conflict, and each sought to place their interests above others.[^cite_note-Muslih1988-10]

According to the Minutes of the Ninth Session of the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations)' [Permanent Mandates Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Mandates_Commission), held in 1926, "Southern Syria" was suggested by some as the name of [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) in the Arabic language. The reports say the following:

> "Colonel Symes explained that the country was described as 'Palestine' by Europeans and as 'Falestin' by the Arabs. The Hebrew name for the country was the designation 'Land of Israel', and the Government, to meet Jewish wishes, had agreed that the word "Palestine" in Hebrew characters should be followed in all official documents by the initials that stood for that designation. As a set-off to this, certain Arab politicians suggested that the country should be called 'Southern Syria' in order to emphasize its close relation with another Arab State".[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

&#x20;In 1932, a Palestinian Arab party named whose name "Hizb Al-Istiqlal" ([Independence Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Party_\(Mandatory_Palestine\)) ) was established in [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) by the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorbonne_University) educated lawyer [Awni Abd al-Hadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awni_Abd_al-Hadi), whom Daniel Pipes says it the reaffirmed support for the incorporation of Palestine and its people into a Pan-Levantine state.[^cite_note-14]

After when Mandatory Palestine ceased to exist in the aftermath of the Palestine War: the term has been used by Syrian Baathists who sought to expand Syria's borders and justify aggression against Israel, such as Hafiz Al Assad in 1974. The Baathists later went on to create Baathist Palestinian groups such as Al-Saqia in an effort to dominate the [PLO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) and wrest it from [Fatah](11104.md). As of the 1990s [Israeli-Syrian peace process](23533046.md), and the [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md) in the early 2010s: the Baathist party of Syria stopped claiming [Israel](9282173.md) and the [Palestinian territories](241405.md) as "Southern Syria".

The Arabs described "the area that became Israel," as Meir put it, in at least ten different ways in the decades prior to World War I, roughly in this order of frequency: Palestine; Syria; Sham; the Holy Land; the Land of Jerusalem; the District of Jerusalem + the District of Balqa + the District of Acre; southern Sham; the southern part of Sham; the Land of Jerusalem + the land of Gaza + the land of Ramla + the land of Nablus + the land of Haifa + the land of Hebron (i.e. cities were used, not regions); "the southern part of Syria, Palestine"; and southern Syria. The Arabic term "southern Syria" so rarely appeared in Arabic sources before 1918 that I've included every reference to the phrase I've ever come across in the footnote at the end of this paragraph (it did appear more often in Western languages). Golda Meir, Mikhaʼel Asaf and my Shabbat hosts were right about Southern Syria, but by focusing only on the facts that supported their arguments and ignoring all the others, they got the story completely wrong. This led some folks to start calling the place southern Syria. The decision was born out of the preference of some of Palestine's Arabs to live under Arab rule from Damascus rather than under British rule from Jerusalem—the same British who, only a few months earlier, in 1917, had declared in the Balfour Declaration their intention to make a national home for the Jews in Palestine.
