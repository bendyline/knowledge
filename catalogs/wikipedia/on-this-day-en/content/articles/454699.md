The **Argentina national football team**,[^cite_note-19] nicknamed ***la Albiceleste***, [^cite_note-20] represents [Argentina](18951905.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) and is administered by the [Argentine Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Football_Association), the governing body of [football in Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Argentina). It has been a member of [FIFA](11049.md) since 1912 and was a founding member of [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) in 1916. It was also a member of [PFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panamerican_Championship#Panamerican_Football_Confederation), the unified confederation of the Americas, from 1946 to 1961. Argentina has won three [FIFA World Cups](11370.md), in [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup_final), [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_final) and [2022](17742072.md). The team has appeared in seven World Cup [finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_finals), including the inaugural one in [1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup_final).

Argentina has won the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) a record 16 times, most recently in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica). With victories in [1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_South_American_Championship), [1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_South_American_Championship) and [1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_South_American_Championship), it is the only nation to have won the tournament three consecutive times. Argentina won the [Panamerican Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panamerican_Championship) in [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Panamerican_Championship), the [inaugural FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_King_Fahd_Cup) in 1992, and the [CONMEBOL–UEFA Cup of Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL%E2%80%93UEFA_Cup_of_Champions) in [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Artemio_Franchi_Cup) and [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Finalissima). Argentina holds the record for most official international titles, with 23.[^cite_note-24] Argentina players have won the [FIFA World Cup Golden Ball](3482503.md#Golden_Ball) three times: [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) in 1986, and [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) in [2014](656933.md) and 2022. Messi has the most appearances for the team (207) and has scored the most goals (125). As of 20 July 2026,[^cite_note-25] Argentina ranks second in the [FIFA Men's World Ranking](719095.md). Argentina is known for having rivalries [with Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Brazil_football_rivalry), [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93England_football_rivalry), [Germany](250204.md), [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Netherlands_football_rivalry), and [Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Uruguay_football_rivalry).

## History

Main article: [History of the Argentina national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Argentina_national_football_team)

Further information: [Football in Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Argentina)

[The first ever match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1902_Uruguay_v_Argentina_football_match) Argentina played was against [Uruguay](679782.md) on June 20, 1902, in [Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montevideo).[^cite_note-albion-27] It was the first international match for both sides, and Argentina won 6–0.[^cite_note-First_match-7][^cite_note-barnade-10] During the first years of Argentina's existence, the team only played [friendly matches](977090.md) against other South American teams. The reasons for this varied, including long travel times between countries and the outbreak of [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-28]

Argentina has appeared in the [FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_World_Cup_finals) seven times, including the [first ever final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup_final) on 30 July 1930, which they lost 4–2 to Uruguay. Argentina won their [next final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup_final) on 25 June [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup), beating the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) 3–1 on [extra time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra-time). In [1986](157231.md), [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) led Argentina to their second title with a 3–2 victory over [West Germany](250204.md). Maradona led Argentina to the final again in [1990](157232.md), but the team lost 1–0 to West Germany by a much-disputed penalty. With [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) as captain, Argentina reached the final in [2014](656933.md), where they were defeated 1–0 by [Germany](250204.md) in extra time. Argentina won [their third FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) in 2022 by defeating [France](250197.md) in a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md), after a 3–3 draw. In the [2026 World Cup](36581929.md), Argentina reached the final again but lost 1–0 to Spain, finishing as runners-up.[^cite_note-29] This match was very controversial as many football fans deemed the Argentine team's behavior after losing the game to be unprofessional.[^cite_note-30] Argentina's World Cup-winning managers are [César Luis Menotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Luis_Menotti) (1978), [Carlos Bilardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Bilardo) (1986) and [Lionel Scaloni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lionel_Scaloni) (2022).

Argentina has won the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica) a record 16 times, most recently in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Copa_Am%C3%A9rica). The team also won the [inaugural FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_King_Fahd_Cup) in 1992 and the [CONMEBOL–UEFA Cup of Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL%E2%80%93UEFA_Cup_of_Champions) in [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Artemio_Franchi_Cup) and [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Finalissima). In March 2007, Argentina reached the top of the [FIFA Men's World Ranking](719095.md) for the first time.[^cite_note-31]

## Stadiums



Estadio Monumental, Argentina's most frequent home venue

Argentina plays most of its home matches at [Estadio Monumental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Monumental_\(Buenos_Aires\)), the stadium of [Club Atlético River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Atl%C3%A9tico_River_Plate) in [Buenos Aires](255919.md).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] The team frequently uses other venues as well, including [Estadio Único Madre de Ciudades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_%C3%9Anico_Madre_de_Ciudades)[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-tycoct20-35] and [La Bombonera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Bombonera).[^cite_note-tycoct20-35]

[Estadio GEBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_GEBA) was the first stadium Argentina used for home matches, including the [Copa Newton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Newton) match against Uruguay on 13 September 1908.[^cite_note-Pelayes-36] The 1908 match marked the official debut of Argentina's white and light blue striped jersey, which has been the team's defining uniform up to the present day.[^cite_note-KitArchive-37] GEBA was also used for the [Copa Centenario Revolución de Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Centenario_Revoluci%C3%B3n_de_Mayo), which was organized by the [Argentine Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Football_Association) (AFA) in 1910. It was the first competition between South American national teams, and is considered the predecessor of the [Copa América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica).[^cite_note-38]

[Estadio Sportivo Barracas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Sportivo_Barracas) was often used by Argentina from 1920 to 1932. Playing in the stadium on 2 October 1924, Argentina forward [Cesáreo Onzari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ces%C3%A1reo_Onzari) scored directly from a [corner kick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corner_kick), the first reported instance of this feat in football. Because Uruguay had recently won the gold medal at the [1924 Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1924_Summer_Olympics), this type of goal is now often referred to as an *[Olimpico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corner_kick#Scoring_a_goal_directly_from_a_corner:_Olimpico_goal)*.[^cite_note-42]

### Kit

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [Argentina national football team kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Argentina%20national%20football%20team%20kits).



The classic light blue and white-striped jersey first worn on 9 July 1906 in Buenos Aires

The first kit ever worn by Argentina, in their official debut against [Uruguay](679782.md) in 1902, included a light blue shirt.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-nostalgia-9]

On 9 July 1906, Argentina wore the lightblue and white shirt for the first time in a match v [South Africa](1018598.md) during their [tour of South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_football_clubs_tours_to_South_America). The match was held in [Sociedad Sportiva Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociedad_Sportiva_Argentina).[^cite_note-44] Since then, the *albiceleste* shirt has remained as the definitive kit for Argentina.[^cite_note-KitArchive-37] The team's away kits have been in dark blue shades, with the colours of shorts and socks varying from time to time.[^cite_note-Argkit-45]

Argentina has also sported other kits; on 3 June 1919 in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), playing against [Brazil](149286.md), Argentina wore a light blue kit, similar to that of Uruguay, out of respect for Roberto Chery, a substitute goalkeeper for Uruguay, who had collapsed and died during a match against [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) at the [1919 South American Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919_South_American_Championship);[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-celeste-47] the match between Argentina and Brazil was organised by the [Brazilian Football Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Football_Confederation) for the benefit of Chery's relatives. At the [1958 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup), Argentina wore the yellow jersey of Swedish club [IFK Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFK_Malm%C3%B6) against West Germany, as the team had arrived in Sweden without an away kit.



[Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona), wearing Argentina's custom blue kit, celebrates his "[Goal of the Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_of_the_Century)" against England in 1986

At the [1986 World Cup](157231.md) in Mexico, the then-manager, [Carlos Bilardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Bilardo), asked the team's kit supplier, [Le Coq Sportif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Coq_Sportif), for lighter blue shirts for their quarter-final against [England](9904.md) in three days, but they could not be provided. Then, a member of the coaching staff scoured the shops of [Mexico City](18987.md) for 38 plain shirts, which were transformed with an improvised version of the AFA emblem embroidered on the shirts[^cite_note-viejos-48] and silvery [American football](18951490.md) numbers ironed to the backs.[^cite_note-shirts-49]

Sporting the makeshift jerseys, [Argentina beat England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_v_England_\(1986_FIFA_World_Cup\)) on 22 June, with Diego Maradona scoring his famous "[Hand of God goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_hand_of_God)".[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Afterwards, the shirt became a symbol of the occasion and an important collector's item.[^cite_note-52]

At the [2018 World Cup](13327177.md) in Russia, Argentina debuted a black away kit;[^cite_note-53] and at the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md) in Qatar, they wore a purple away kit in a competitive match for the first time.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

In July 2026, the [Argentina national rugby team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_national_rugby_union_team) played their [2026 Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Nations_Championship_Southern_Hemisphere_Series) match vs [England](691024.md) wearing a kit that was an exact replica of the model worn in 1986, including details like the silver numbers on back. The kit –manufactured by *Pumas* kit supplier Le Coq Sportif, the same than Argentina in 1986– was a limited edition to commemorate the 40th. anniversary of the historic football match.[^cite_note-espn1-56][^cite_note-57]

### Crest



The AFA emblem that was added to playing kits in 1976

Argentina has used the logo of the Argentine Football Association as its emblem since it was first worn at the [1958 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup) in Sweden; the logo was added to the team's jackets, but not the shirts.[^cite_note-viejos-48] The emblem was not used on jerseys until 16 November 1976, when Argentina played the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_football_team) at [Estadio Monumental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Monumental_\(Buenos_Aires\)). At the beginning, the crest used did not include a [laurel wreath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_wreath),[^cite_note-escudo-68] which was first added for the [1982 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-viejos-48]

As a respected and common practice,[^cite_note-fifa-equipment-regs-69] two stars were added above the crest in 2004, symbolising Argentina's World Cup titles in 1978 and 1986.[^cite_note-escudo-68] In 2022, a third star was added after Argentina were crowned world champions for the third time.[^cite_note-70]

The 1976 crest has been credited to Norberto "Toto" Rud, a Buenos Aires businessman, graphic designer, football supporter, and member of the Jewish sports club Club Náutico Hacoaj. Rud submitted approximately 20 designs to the Argentine Football Association, and the selected crest debuted in a friendly against the Soviet Union on 28 November 1976.[^cite_note-71]

## Rivalries

### Brazil

Main article: [Argentina–Brazil football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Brazil_football_rivalry)

Argentina and Brazil have a fierce rivalry which is one of the oldest in South America.[^cite_note-bleacherreport-86][^cite_note-87] Matches between the two teams, even those that are only [friendly matches](977090.md), are often marked by notable and controversial incidents. The rivalry has also been referred to as the "Superclassic of the Americas." [FIFA](11049.md) has described it as the "essence of football rivalry".[^cite_note-88]

The rivalry has extended to comparisons between [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9) and [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona).[^cite_note-pag12fifa-89][^cite_note-peleormarad-90] Some of their countrymen also feature regularly in such debates. The next most notable pair are perhaps [Garrincha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrincha) (Brazil)[^cite_note-91] and [Alfredo Di Stéfano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Di_St%C3%A9fano) (Argentina).[^cite_note-92] The most dominant figures from the two countries in the modern game are [Neymar](15326144.md) (Brazil) and [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) (Argentina). Both Pelé and Maradona have declared Neymar and Messi their respective "successors".[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

### England

Main article: [Argentina–England football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93England_football_rivalry)

With a rivalry stemming from the [1966 World Cup](61629.md) and intensified by the [Falklands War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands_War) of 1982, Argentina and [England](9904.md) have had numerous confrontations in World Cup tournaments. Among them was the [quarter-final match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_v_England_\(1986_FIFA_World_Cup\)) in [1986](157231.md), where Diego Maradona scored two goals against England. The first was [a handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_hand_of_God), but was ruled legal by the referee. The second, scored minutes later, saw Maradona passing five England outfield players before scoring, and is often described as one of, if not the greatest goal in football history.[^cite_note-95]

The nations were paired together in the round of 16 at the [1998 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), won by Argentina on penalties,[^cite_note-96] and again at the group stage in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), with England winning 1–0 through a penalty by [David Beckham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Beckham) who had been sent off in the tie four years earlier.[^cite_note-97]

Argentina and England played in the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) [semi-final](81667005.md) in [Atlanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_Stadium). This marked the first time in twenty-one years the teams have met at all, the first in twenty-four years in a major tournament and the first in twenty-eight years that both teams met in the knockout rounds.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] England took the lead in the 55th minute with a goal from [Anthony Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Gordon_\(footballer\)) before Argentina came back to win the match with two late goals, as [Enzo Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Fern%C3%A1ndez) equalised in the 85th minute before [Lautaro Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lautaro_Mart%C3%ADnez) scored a dramatic stoppage-time winner to eliminate England and secure Argentina's place in the final.[^cite_note-100]

### Germany



[Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) and [Karl-Heinz Rummenigge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl-Heinz_Rummenigge) prior to the 1986 World Cup final between Argentina and West Germany

Argentina has played [Germany](250204.md) in seven [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) matches including three World Cup finals. In [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) Argentina won 3–2, but in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) and [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) it was the Germans who were the victors by a 1–0 scoreline both times.[^cite_note-101]

In [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_FIFA_World_Cup) they met for the first time in the group stage, where Argentina suffered a 1–3 loss to [defending champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_FIFA_World_Cup_Final) West Germany.[^cite_note-102] In [1966](61629.md) both again faced each other in the group stage which ended in a scoreless draw.[^cite_note-103] In 2006, they met in the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Germany_vs_Argentina); Argentina lost on penalties after a 1–1 draw, which was followed by a brawl on the pitch involving several players.[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] They met again at the [same stage in 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_knockout_stage#Argentina_vs_Germany), this time ending with a 4–0 victory for Germany.[^cite_note-106] They played each other for the third consecutive World Cup in [Brazil 2014](656933.md)'s final, where Argentina was defeated [in extra time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_final) by a score of 1–0.[^cite_note-107]

### Uruguay

Main article: [Argentina–Uruguay football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Uruguay_football_rivalry)

Argentina has a long-standing rivalry with its neighbour, that came into existence from the early [South American Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Am%C3%A9rica), the [1928 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1928_Summer_Olympics) and the [first World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930_FIFA_World_Cup_Final), held in 1930.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] Argentina and Uruguay hold the record for most international matches played between two countries.[^cite_note-First_match-7] The two teams have faced each other 197 times since 1902. The [first match between Argentina and Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1902_Uruguay_v_Argentina_football_match) was also the first official international match to be played outside the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-111]

### Netherlands

Main article: [Argentina–Netherlands football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina%E2%80%93Netherlands_football_rivalry)

Considered by sports media to be two historically great teams,[^cite_note-112] the Argentines and Dutch have developed an intense rivalry. They have met ten times in total, including six times during the World Cup.[^cite_note-FIFA-NedvArg-113][^cite_note-Lupo2022-114][^cite_note-McLaughlin2022-115]

The two teams first met on 26 May 1974 in an international friendly, won by the Netherlands 4–1. Their most high-profile matchup occurred in the [1978 World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup_final) which was won by Argentina.[^cite_note-Independent-116] One of the most recent and intense meetings happened in the [2022 World Cup](17742072.md), a 2–2 draw where Argentina advanced on penalties, in what is known as the [Battle of Lusail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lusail).[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]
