The **Miguel de Cervantes Prize** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Premio de Literatura en Lengua Castellana Miguel de Cervantes*) is awarded annually to honour the lifetime achievement of an outstanding writer in the [Spanish language](26825.md). The *[Encyclopædia Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica)* calls it the "most prestigious and remunerative award given for Spanish-language literature".[^cite_note-brit-1]

## History

The prize was established in 1975 by the [Ministry of Culture of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_of_Spain) and first awarded the following year.[^cite_note-brit-1] The winner receives a monetary award of 125,000 [euros](9472.md), which makes it one of the [richest literary prizes in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_world's_richest_literary_prizes).[^cite_note-wolfe-2] The prize rewards authors from any [Spanish-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanophone) nation and recognizes the recipient's overall body of work.[^cite_note-brit-1] Of the forty-seven prizes awarded in the history of the Cervantes Prize, only six have ever been awarded to women. In 1988, the Spanish writer [María Zambrano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar%C3%ADa_Zambrano) (1904–1991) was the first female writer to be honored. The award is named after [Miguel de Cervantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_de_Cervantes), author of *[Don Quixote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Quixote)*.[^cite_note-wolfe-2] The candidates are proposed by the [Association of Spanish Language Academies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Spanish_Language_Academies) (i.e., the [Royal Spanish Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Academy)).[^cite_note-3]

As of the presentation of the 2024 award to [Álvaro Pombo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Pombo), the recipients have been recognized for their writing of novels, poetry, short stories, essays, translations, philosophy or dramas – or for combinations thereof. With two winners in 1979, there have been 50 recipients of the Miguel de Cervantes Prize.

### The Cervantes Prize and the Nobel Prize in Literature

Three of the 50 winners of the Miguel de Cervantes Prize have also won the [Nobel Prize in Literature](23385442.md). [Octavio Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavio_Paz) (Cervantes 1981, Nobel 1990) and [Mario Vargas Llosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Vargas_Llosa) (Cervantes 1994, Nobel 2010), were awarded the Nobel Prize in subsequent years, while [Camilo José Cela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camilo_Jos%C3%A9_Cela) received the Nobel Prize in 1989 and was awarded the Cervantes Prize in 1995.
