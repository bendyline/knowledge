The **United States District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia** (**EDVA**; in [case citations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_citation), **E.D. Va.**) is one of two [United States district courts](70235.md) serving the [Commonwealth of Virginia](32432.md). Its jurisdiction includes over 85 percent of the state's population, including the metropolitan areas of [Northern Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Virginia), [Hampton Roads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampton_Roads), and [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond-Petersburg). Courthouses are located in [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria,_VA), [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk,_VA), [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_VA), and [Newport News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newport_News,_VA).

Known widely as "[the rocket docket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_docket)", the Eastern District of Virginia has been the fastest federal trial court in the country for over 50 years.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] Adhering to the maxim "[justice delayed is justice denied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_delayed_is_justice_denied)", the court aims to complete cases within one year, regardless of complexity. It schedules trials on weekends and holidays and maintains a virtual ban on [continuance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuance).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-5] The court utilizes a unique "master docket" to expedite cases: rather than assign individual caseloads, when a [motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_\(legal\)) is up for consideration or a trial is scheduled to start, the matter will be handled by whichever judge is available. Judges are assigned only for [patent cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_patent_law) and in rare exceptions.[^cite_note-6]

With a jurisdiction which includes the [Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon), much of the [Intelligence Community](501244.md), and [Naval Station Norfolk](896196.md), the world's largest naval base, the EDVA is a fixture in [U.S. national security law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Law_of_the_United_States), handling a large portion of the nation's espionage and terrorism cases. More than 1 in 5 terrorism charges filed in the United States since 1995 were filed in the EDVA.[^cite_note-\:1-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] One of the district's [senior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_status) judges also [presides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presiding_judge) over the [U.S. Foreign Intelligence Surveillance Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Foreign_Intelligence_Surveillance_Court).

The U.S. Attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia wields one of the largest federal prosecutor offices in the country, staffed by over 300 attorneys and support staff.[^cite_note-\:1-7]

Appeals from the Eastern District of Virginia are taken to the [United States Court of Appeals for the Fourth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Fourth_Circuit), except for [patent claims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patent_infringement_under_United_States_law#Remedies) and claims against the U.S. government under the [Tucker Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tucker_Act), which are appealed to the [Federal Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Federal_Circuit).

## History

The [United States District Court for the District of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_District_of_Virginia) was one of the original 13 courts established by the [Judiciary Act of 1789](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_Act_of_1789), 1 [Stat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Statutes_at_Large) [73](https://legislink.org/us/stat-1-73), on September 24, 1789.[^cite_note-S388-10][^cite_note-fjcva-11]

On February 13, 1801, the [Judiciary Act of 1801](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_Act_of_1801), 2 [Stat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Statutes_at_Large) [89](https://legislink.org/us/stat-2-89), divided Virginia into three judicial districts: the District of Virginia, which included the counties west of the [Tidewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidewater_\(region\)) and south of the [Rappahannock River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rappahannock_River); the [District of Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_District_of_Norfolk), which included the Tidewater counties south of the Rappahannock; and the [District of Potomac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_District_of_Potomac), which included the counties north and east of the Rappahannock as well as [Maryland](18858.md) counties along the [Potomac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potomac_River).[^cite_note-fjcva-11] Just over a year later, on March 8, 1802, the Judiciary Act of 1801 was repealed and Virginia became a single district again, 2 [Stat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Statutes_at_Large) [132](https://legislink.org/us/stat-2-132), effective July 1, 1802.[^cite_note-fjcva-11]

The District of Virginia was subdivided into Eastern and [Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Western_District_of_Virginia) Districts on February 4, 1819, by 3 [Stat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Statutes_at_Large) [478](https://legislink.org/us/stat-3-478).[^cite_note-S388-10][^cite_note-fjcva-11] At that time, [West Virginia](32905.md) was still part of Virginia, and was encompassed in Virginia's Western District, while the Eastern District essentially covered what is now the entire state of Virginia. With the division of West Virginia from Virginia during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), the Western District of Virginia became the District of West Virginia, and those parts of the Western District that were not part of West Virginia were combined with the Eastern District to again form a single District of Virginia on June 11, 1864, by 13 [Stat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Statutes_at_Large) [124](https://legislink.org/us/stat-13-124).[^cite_note-fjcva-11] Congress again divided Virginia into the Eastern and Western Districts on February 3, 1871, by 16 [Stat.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Statutes_at_Large) [403](https://legislink.org/us/stat-16-403).[^cite_note-fjcva-11]

Courts at Richmond are located in the [Spottswood W. Robinson III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spottswood_William_Robinson_III) and [Robert R. Merhige Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_R._Merhige_Jr.) Federal Courthouse,[^cite_note-12] having previously been held in the historic [Lewis F. Powell Jr. United States Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_F._Powell_Jr._United_States_Courthouse).

Most recent acting U.S. Attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia [Lindsey Halligan](74616180.md) was disqualified on November 24, 2025 after being deemed to have been unlawfully appointed in order to prosecute former FBI director [James Comey](2156690.md) and Attorney General of New York [Letitia James](1598829.md), two people who previously led legal cases against U.S. President Trump.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

### The rocket docket

The Eastern District of Virginia is the originator of the term "[rocket docket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_docket)", which has since been applied to various other courts at times. The culture of speed was embedded in the EDVA by two judges who served on the court in the 1960's: [Albert V. Bryan Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_V._Bryan_Jr.), who often ruled on cases on the spot after motions were argued, and [Walter E. Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Edward_Hoffman), who was known for scheduling trials for weekends, holidays and reportedly once on Christmas Day.[^cite_note-\:0-4] Current chief judge Mark S. Davis has said that the current bench is driven in part by a shared belief in the maxim that "[justice delayed is justice denied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_delayed_is_justice_denied)", but that the "expectations of the \[EDVA] bar" nevertheless ensure that the court continues to maintain its famed pace.[^cite_note-\:0-4]

## Jurisdiction



Map of the United States District Courts in Virginia, showing the boundaries of the Eastern and Western Districts, and their divisions.

The Eastern District of Virginia court's jurisdiction covers slightly over six million people, comprising approximately 85% of the state's population. Its jurisdiction is grouped into four geographic divisions:

### Alexandria Division



View of the US District Court for the Eastern District of Virginia at Alexandria, Virginia.

## United States Attorney

The **U.S. attorney for the Eastern District of Virginia** serves as prosecution for criminal cases brought by the federal government, and representing the United States in civil cases in the court. The U.S. Attorney's office also manages the [Project Safe Neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Safe_Neighborhoods) program within the district to reduce [gun violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_violence), and is involved with federal initiatives on [drug trafficking](218449.md), [terrorism](30636.md), [cybercrime](341598.md), and the prevention of [elder care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elder_care) abuse.[^cite_note-16]

## Chief judges

Chief judges have administrative responsibilities with respect to their district court. Unlike the Supreme Court, where one justice is specifically nominated to be chief, the office of chief judge rotates among the district court judges. To be chief, a judge must have been in active service on the court for at least one year, be under the age of 65, and have not previously served as chief judge.&#x20;

A vacancy is filled by the judge highest in seniority among the group of qualified judges. The chief judge serves for a term of seven years, or until age 70, whichever occurs first. The age restrictions are waived if no members of the court would otherwise be qualified for the position.

When the office was created in 1948, the chief judge was the longest-serving judge who had not elected to retire, on what has since 1958 been known as [senior status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_status), or declined to serve as chief judge. After August 6, 1959, judges could not become or remain chief after turning 70 years old. The current rules have been in operation since October 1, 1982.
