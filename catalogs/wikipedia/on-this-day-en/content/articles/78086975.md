The **Tesla Cybercab** is a two-passenger [battery-electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_electric_vehicle) [robotaxi](46530282.md) manufactured by [Tesla, Inc.](5533631.md) for its [Robotaxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Robotaxi) ride-hailing service. Designed exclusively for autonomous operation, it has no steering wheel, pedals, side mirrors, or rear window and uses a camera-only autonomous-driving system rather than the [lidar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidar) and [radar](25676.md) used by some competing robotaxis.

Tesla unveiled the Cybercab in October 2024, began production in February 2026, and started offering paid public rides in [Austin, Texas](1998.md), in September 2026. The vehicle is intended to become the principal vehicle in Tesla's Robotaxi fleet and is central to the company's plans for autonomous [ride-hailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ride-hailing). Tesla stated the Cybercab design priorities were low-cost production, ease of manufacturing and maximum efficiency.[^cite_note-\:6-2][^cite_note-\:18-3] Tesla has targeted an operating cost of less than US\$0.30 per mile for the Cybercab.[^cite_note-auto-4]

In September 2026, the [National Highway Traffic Safety Administration](989530.md) opened an investigation into Tesla's self-certification that the Cybercab complies with applicable federal motor-vehicle safety standards, which were written for human-driven vehicles.&#x20;

## History

### Background

In 2019, Tesla CEO [Elon Musk](909036.md) stated that he believed that Tesla would have one million autonomous robotaxis operating on public roads by the end of 2020; observers speculated that he meant converting already sold Tesla vehicles to be autonomous.[^cite_note-fc20240410-5]

Since approximately 2020, Tesla had made public statements about a mass market electric car product that would follow the [Model Y](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_Y)[^cite_note-forbes20230304-6] and would be considerably cheaper than the [Model 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_3). In 2022, Musk was advocating inside the company that the Robotaxi would be Tesla's next vehicle, but by September 2022, he had reluctantly accepted the recommendation of Tesla executives [Franz von Holzhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_von_Holzhausen) and Lars Moravy that [the next-generation vehicle platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_next-generation_vehicle_platform) should support both a small, inexpensive, mass-market car and a Robotaxi that would be built with no steering wheel at all, and that both could be manufactured on the platform and use the same next-generation vehicle [assembly line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_line).[^cite_note-Isaacson2023-7] In October 2022, the company stated publicly that the Tesla engineering team had turned its focus to the [new platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_next-generation_vehicle_platform), and that the company expected the platform would enable cars to be half of the price of the [Tesla Model 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_3) or [Y](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_Y).[^cite_note-electrek20221020-8]

In April 2024, Musk announced that the Robotaxi reveal would take place in August, subsequently delayed to October.[^cite_note-9]

### Announcement



Rear view of the Tesla Cybercab at unveiling

Musk unveiled the Cybercab at the Tesla *We, Robot* event held October 10, 2024, at [Warner Bros. Studios Burbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner_Bros._Studios_Burbank) in California, where 20 [concept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concept_car) Cybercabs [autonomously](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_robot) drove around the studio outlot at night and gave rides to event attendees.[^cite_note-yahoo20241011-10][^cite_note-wired20241010-11] Musk stated that Tesla intended to produce the Cybercab before 2027.[^cite_note-bb20241011-12] Tesla used both "Robotaxi" and "Cybercab" throughout the event to refer to the cars.[^cite_note-yahoo20241011-10] Tesla also demonstrated its humanoid robot, [Optimus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimus_\(robot\)), at the event and showed off a single concept prototype of a [Robovan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Robovan) that could reportedly hold as many as 20 passengers.[^cite_note-verge20241010-13]

The concept Cybercab shown was a [two-passenger car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-passenger_car).[^cite_note-14] It had two [butterfly doors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterfly_door) but no door handles as the doors opened automatically, and no rear window or side view mirrors.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The car had a [hatchback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hatchback) opening for cargo, with no external charge port showing.[^cite_note-yahoo20241011-10][^cite_note-17]

Investor reaction to the announcement was muted, particularly given the long time frame between the announcement and expected start of production.[^cite_note-bb20241011-12]

### Production



A Cybercab drives on a public street in San Francisco in June 2026.

On Tesla's earnings call on October 23, 2024, Elon Musk said the Cybercab would reach volume production in 2026 and that Tesla was aiming for at least 2 million Cybercabs a year across more than one factory.[^cite_note-Fool-Q3-2024-18][^cite_note-Investopedia-Q3-19][^cite_note-CNBC-Q3-2024-20]

In November 2025 at the Shareholder meeting, Elon Musk announced that the Cybercab production would begin in the second quarter of 2026.[^cite_note-21]

In February 2026, Tesla announced the first Cybercab production vehicle had been produced at the [Gigafactory Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigafactory_Texas) near Austin.[^cite_note-\:0-22] By mid-March, Cybercab test units were spotted on public roads in Austin, Texas, and in [Silicon Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicon_Valley), as well at Gigafactory Texas, many of them operating in test cycles on area roads.[^cite_note-23] Formal production began in April 2026.[^cite_note-24] By July 2026, hundreds of Cybercab vehicles had been spotted near Gigafactory Texas.[^cite_note-25]

### Commercial introduction

On September 3, 2026, Tesla held an invitation-only Cybercab launch event in Austin, Texas at [ACL Live](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACL_Live_at_The_Moody_Theater).[^cite_note-\:1-26][^cite_note-reuters-20260903-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] Tesla executives presented for about 15 minutes;[^cite_note-reuters-20260903-27][^cite_note-\:6-2] Elon Musk was not present.[^cite_note-reuters-20260903-27] An expected live stream of the event did not occur.[^cite_note-30]

The next day, Tesla started offering paid Cybercab rides in Austin through its existing Robotaxi service.[^cite_note-\:2-31] The service has operated since June 2025[^cite_note-\:3-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] using autonomous Model Y vehicles.[^cite_note-\:3-32]

As of August 2026, Texas motor vehicle records reported about 45 Cybercabs registered to Tesla’s Robotaxi fleet.[^cite_note-\:1-26]

On September 9, 2026, Tesla started an Asia Cybercab road show in Hong Kong, and plans to show it simultaneously in Japan (Tokyo, Osaka and Nagoya) and China (Beijing and Shanghai).[^cite_note-\:14-35] The road show does not involve sales, test drives or commercial robotaxi operations.[^cite_note-\:14-35][^cite_note-36]

Tesla has taken steps to investigate the possibility of selling the Cybercab to third-party fleet operators.[^cite_note-37] Autoevolution reported in September 2026 that the Cybercab was not designed for individual buyers, despite Musk's 2024 statements to the contrary.[^cite_note-38]

### Regulatory investigations

The vehicle lacks steering wheel, pedal, and side mirror equipment typically required under federal motor vehicle safety standards.[^cite_note-\:1-26] Tesla said it self-certified that the Cybercabs were in compliance with federal regulations, which were written for human-driven vehicles.[^cite_note-\:12-39][^cite_note-FedInvestigate-40] The [National Highway Traffic Safety Administration](989530.md) (NHTSA) will conduct an audit regarding the process and technical data that the company used in the certification.[^cite_note-41] At the same time, the NHTSA is pushing to change the regulations and is separately drafting new rules to accommodate autonomous vehicles.[^cite_note-\:12-39]

As of September 2026, there are also several ongoing federal investigations into the safety of Tesla's self-driving software and whether the company violated regulatory requirements to report crashes promptly.[^cite_note-FedInvestigate-40][^cite_note-42]

## Design



Interior front of the concept Cybercab



Side view of the concept Cybercab

Cybercab is designed for [autonomous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_robot) operation and only uses cameras, unlike autonomous competitors such as [Waymo](29156200.md) and [Zoox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoox) which use [lidar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidar), [radar](25676.md), and other sensors.[^cite_note-\:6-2] It does not have a steering wheel, pedals, side mirrors, or a back window.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-ievs20241018-44] The butterfly doors have no door handles because they open and close automatically.[^cite_note-\:6-2]

Tesla chose it to be a two-passenger car because it says over 90% of vehicle miles traveled are with two or fewer passengers.[^cite_note-\:11-45] For rides with more than two passengers, the [Model Y](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_Y) robotaxi is available now, and Tesla says other Tesla vehicles will be available in the future.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-\:11-45]

Tesla stated the Cybercab design priorities were low-cost production, ease of manufacturing and maximum efficiency.[^cite_note-\:6-2][^cite_note-\:18-3] Tesla has said it has about half of the parts of a [Tesla Model 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_3).[^cite_note-47] It is Tesla's first vehicle produced using the "[unboxed process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_next-generation_vehicle_platform#Assembly_process)", which Tesla says makes the production line half as long, unlocking much greater factory capacity, and significantly cutting costs.[^cite_note-\:6-2][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] Musk says the process was "redesigned ... to make its production speed more than 5 times faster than traditional automotive manufacturing!"[^cite_note-\:17-50] The exterior body panels use [reaction injection molding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reaction_injection_molding) instead of paint to provide the gold color, which Tesla says reduces time and cost.[^cite_note-51] The gold color was chosen to represent the "golden era of transport".[^cite_note-\:5-52] Plastic parts are welded ultrasonically.[^cite_note-53] The Cybercab is the narrowest of all of Tesla vehicles (including the [original Roadster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Roadster_\(first_generation\))), and the first with only front wheel drive, both of which save on cost and reduce complexity.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

Tesla says the Cybercab has a [drag coefficient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_coefficient) under 0.20, making it one of the most aerodynamically efficient vehicles ever produced.[^cite_note-\:6-2] Tesla said its motor is "18 percent smaller, 25 percent lighter, and more efficient than other top-performing electric vehicle drive units."[^cite_note-\:6-2] At 165 watt-hours per mile (103 Wh/km), it is the most efficient electric vehicle by a wide margin.[^cite_note-\:7-56] Tesla says the designed life of the vehicle is 500,000 miles (800,000 km).[^cite_note-57] It uses an electric [brake-by-wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brake-by-wire) system.[^cite_note-58]

The range is \~293 miles (472 km).[^cite_note-Electrek-1] Battery capacity is 48 [kWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt-hour) and uses [4680](5533631.md#4680) cells in an 88S6P configuration.[^cite_note-59]

In August 2026, the vehicle's roof was displayed with a flush-mounted integrated [Starlink](45111627.md) version 5 antenna.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-\:13-61] Musk said Starlink capabilities would be offered on the vehicle,[^cite_note-\:5-52] such as passengers watching [4K live video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4K_video), as well as vehicle operations such [over-the-air updates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-the-air_update), [navigation data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navigational_database), [remote diagnostics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_diagnostics), [dispatch coordination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridesharing_company), and [fleet management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_management).[^cite_note-\:13-61] The Starlink connection is not required for autonomous driving.[^cite_note-\:13-61]

### Accessibility

The Cybercab was designed for accessibility.[^cite_note-\:15-62][^cite_note-\:16-63] Tesla has stated its goal is "an autonomous future that is accessible to all people."[^cite_note-\:15-62] With its braille on the physical buttons, and voice control of other capabilities, it is designed to be operated by a blind person.[^cite_note-\:15-62][^cite_note-\:16-63] It allows an easier approach by a wheelchair due to its butterfly doors and an easier transfer because its bench seats are at the same height as standard wheelchairs.[^cite_note-\:15-62][^cite_note-\:16-63]

## Full self-driving (unsupervised)

As of September 2026, Cybercabs are using [Full-Self Driving (FSD) version 15 (unsupervised)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Autopilot#Full_Self_Driving_\(Robotaxi\)), which is not yet available for consumer cars.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] Musk described V15 as "a complete overhaul of the software architecture",[^cite_note-70] with ten times the parameters of its predecessor,[^cite_note-71] and including seven "core technologies" with 40% of them already running in the robotaxi fleet.[^cite_note-72] The Cybercabs are running this software on a modified version of [Hardware 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Autopilot#Hardware_4_plus_\(AI4.1_or_AI4+\)), which is also not available to consumer cars.[^cite_note-73]

## Charging

The production Cybercab uses a concealed [North American Charging Standard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Charging_Standard) (NACS) port for direct-current (DC) fast charging at [Tesla Superchargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Supercharger) and other [public DC chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EV_charger).[^cite_note-NATA-AC-74] The vehicles do not currently support alternating-current (AC) charging[^cite_note-\:4-75] and therefore could not use a Tesla Wall Connector, Mobile Connector, or other residential Level 2 equipment.[^cite_note-NATA-AC-74][^cite_note-IThome-AC-76] At the October 2024 unveiling, Tesla said the Cybercab would recharge by inductive wireless charging and would not need a plug;[^cite_note-Reuters-unveil-77][^cite_note-CNBC-plug-78] Tesla has described wireless charging as a planned fleet feature and has filed plans for robotaxi hubs that include wireless pads.[^cite_note-NATA-AC-74][^cite_note-TipRanks-hub-79] Tesla has said its inductive system is expected to be more than 90 percent efficient.[^cite_note-Reuters-unveil-77]

## Manufacturing cost and operational efficiency

The [International Energy Agency](285292.md) (IEA) reported in 2026 that Waymo's [Jaguar I-Pace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar_I-Pace) robotaxis initially cost more than \$100,000 per vehicle and that newer Waymo vehicles had reduced the cost to around \$70,000.[^cite_note-\:9-80] The IEA said that Tesla was targeting much lower robotaxi costs by relying solely on cameras;[^cite_note-\:9-80] *[TechCrunch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TechCrunch)* also attributed Waymo's higher vehicle costs to its use of multiple types of sensors and vehicles manufactured by other companies, while Tesla uses a camera-based autonomous-driving system and manufactures its own vehicles.[^cite_note-81]

Tesla has targeted an operating cost of less than \$0.30 per mile for the Cybercab.[^cite_note-auto-4] In December 2025, *[The Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Information)* reported that [Morgan Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Stanley) analysts estimated Tesla's robotaxi operating cost at \$0.81 per mile, compared with approximately \$1.43 per mile for Waymo.[^cite_note-\:10-82] The analysts projected that newer Waymo vehicles planned for deployment in 2026 would reduce Waymo's operating cost to \$0.99–\$1.08 per mile.[^cite_note-\:10-82]

[ARK Invest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARK_Invest) projected that, at scale in 2030, the Cybercab would have an incremental operating cost of approximately \$0.16 per mile, compared with approximately \$0.32 per mile for Waymo's sixth-generation vehicles.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-\:8-84] They estimate that by 2030, global robotaxi companies could represent an [enterprise value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enterprise_value) of \$34 trillion, and believe Tesla may capture a significant share of it.[^cite_note-\:17-50]
