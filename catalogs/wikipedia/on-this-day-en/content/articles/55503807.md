# Phase-out of fossil fuel vehicles



[VinBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VinBus) [electric bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_bus) charging at [VF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VinFast) station. [Solar cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_cell) are on top of the [roof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roof).

A **phase-out of fossil fuel vehicles** refers to bans or discouragement (for example via taxes) on the sale of new fossil-fuel powered vehicles or use of existing fossil-fuel powered vehicles, as well the encouragement of using [other forms of transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_share). Vehicles that are powered by [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel), such as [gasoline (petrol)](23639.md), [diesel](81761.md), [kerosene](16992.md), and [fuel oil](392547.md) have been phased out in some countries and are scheduled to be phased out by a number of other countries. It is one of the three most important parts of the general [fossil fuel phase-out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_phase-out) process, the others being the [phase-out of fossil fuel power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_power_station#Phase_out_of_fossil_fuel_power_plants) for [electricity generation](9540.md) and [decarbonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decarbonisation) of [industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy).[^cite_note-1]

Many countries and cities around the world have stated they will ban the sale of passenger vehicles (primarily cars and buses) powered by [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) such as [petrol](23639.md), [liquefied petroleum gas](361384.md), and [diesel](81761.md) at some specific time in the future.[^cite_note-EVOutlook2020-2][^cite_note-climateprotection.org-3] A few have actually implemented this change.[^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-\:1-5] Synonyms for the bans include phrases like "banning gas cars",[^cite_note-6] "banning petrol cars",[^cite_note-7] "the petrol and diesel car ban",[^cite_note-BBC_News-2020-8] or simply "the diesel ban".[^cite_note-Leipzig_ruling-9] Another method of phase-out is the use of [zero-emission zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-emission_zone) in cities.

Further information: [Environmental impact of the petroleum industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_the_petroleum_industry) and [Environmental aspects of the electric car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_aspects_of_the_electric_car)

Reasons for banning the further sale of fossil fuel vehicles include: reducing health risks from pollution [particulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate), notably [diesel PM10s](1202358.md#Health_concerns), and other emissions, notably [nitrogen oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_oxides);[^cite_note-10] meeting national [greenhouse gas](21350772.md), such as CO<sub>2</sub>, targets under international agreements such as the [Kyoto Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyoto_Protocol) and the [Paris Agreement](30242372.md); or [energy independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_independence). The intent to ban vehicles powered by [fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuels) is attractive to governments as it offers a simpler compliance target,[^cite_note-11] compared with a [carbon tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_tax) or [phase-out of fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_phase-out).[^cite_note-12]

The automotive industry is working to introduce [electric vehicles](279350.md) to adapt to bans[^cite_note-climateprotection.org-3] with varying success and it is seen by some in the industry as a possible source of money in a declining market. A 2020 study from the [Eindhoven University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven_University_of_Technology) showed that the manufacturing emissions of batteries of new electric cars are much smaller than what was assumed in the 2017 IVL study[^cite_note-15] (around 75 kg CO<sub>2</sub>/kWh) and that the lifespan of lithium batteries is also much longer than previously thought (at least 12 years with a mileage of 15,000 km annually): they are cleaner than internal combustion cars powered by diesel or petrol.[^cite_note-Ekker-16]

There is some opposition to simply moving from fossil-fuel-powered cars to electric cars, as they would still require a large proportion of urban land.[^cite_note-17] On the other hand, there are many [types of (electric) vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_shift) that take up little space, such as ([cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo_bike)) bicycles and [electric motorcycles and scooters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_motorcycles_and_scooters).[^cite_note-Casson-18] Making cycling and [walking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walking) over short distances, especially in urban areas, more attractive and feasible with measures such as removing roads and [parking spaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parking_spaces) and improving [cycling infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cycling_infrastructure) and [footpaths](312678.md) (including [pavements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidewalk)), provides a partial alternative to replacing all fossil-fuelled vehicles with electric vehicles.[^cite_note-Casson-18][^cite_note-19] Although there are as yet very few completely [carfree cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carfree_city) (such as [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice)), several are banning all cars in parts of the city, such as city centers.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

## Methods

The banning of fossil-fuelled vehicles of a defined scope requires authorities to enact legislation that restricts them in a certain way. Proposed methods include:

- A prohibition on further sales or registration of new vehicles powered with specific fuels from a certain date in a certain area.

  [^cite_note-Jacobs-22]

  &#x20;At the date of implementation, existing vehicles would remain legal to drive on public highways.

  [^cite_note-BBC_News-2020-8]

- A prohibition on the importation of new vehicles powered with specific fuels from a certain date into a certain area. This is planned in countries such as Denmark and Israel;

  [^cite_note-Ekblom-23]

  [^cite_note-Solomon-24]

  &#x20;however, some countries, such as Israel, have no legislation on the subject.

  [^cite_note-haaretz1-25]

- A prohibition on any use of certain vehicles powered with specific fuels from a certain date within a certain area. Restrictions such as these are already in place in many European cities, usually in the context of their [low-emission zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-emission_zone) (LEZs).

  [^cite_note-diesel_restrictions_analysis-26]

[Fuel cell (electric) vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_cell_vehicle) (FCVs or FCEVs) also allow running on (some) non-fossil fuels (i.e., hydrogen, ethanol,[^cite_note-27] methanol,[^cite_note-28] ).

Cities generally use the introduction of [low-emission zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-emission_zone) (LEZs) or zero-emission zones (ZEZs), sometimes with an accompanying air quality certificate [sticker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vignette_\(road_tax\)) such as [Crit'air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crit'air) (France), to restrict the use of fossil-fuelled cars in some or all of its territory.[^cite_note-Jacobs-22] These zones are growing in number, size, and strictness.[^cite_note-Jacobs-22][^cite_note-29] Some city bans in countries such as Italy, Germany, and Switzerland are only temporarily activated during particular times of the day, during winter, or when there is a smog alert (for example, in Italy in January 2020); these do not directly contribute to the phase-out of fossil fuel vehicles, but they make owning and using such vehicles less attractive as their utility is restricted and the cost of driving them increases.[^cite_note-ADAC-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

Some countries have given consumers various incentives such as subsidies or tax breaks to stimulate the purchase of electric vehicles, while fossil-fuelled vehicles are taxed increasingly heavily.[^cite_note-Jacobs-22]

## Places with existing phase-out

Helped by government incentives, Norway became the first country to have the majority of new vehicles sold be electric. In 2017, Norway set a target of ending sales of new Internal Combustion Engined cars by 2025. Measures included tax incentives and access to bus lanes and reduced tolls. In January 2022, 88 per cent of new vehicles sold in the country were electric.[^cite_note-33] In January 2026, only seven new petrol cars were sold in Norway, with 29 hybrid, 98 diesel and more than 2000 battery electric cars.[^cite_note-34] However, contrary to many false reports on the matter, this was a collection of incentives rather than an outright ban on new fossil fuel vehicles sales.[^cite_note-35] EV sales reached 50% of new car sales by September 2018[^cite_note-36] and 98% of new sales in December 2025.[^cite_note-\:1-5] By 2024, the majority of cars on the road were electric.[^cite_note-37]

In 2024, Ethiopia announced a ban on the import of Internal Combustion Engined vehicles, leading to a boom in electric vehicle sales.[^cite_note-\:0-4][^cite_note-38]

### International

At the [2021 United Nations Climate Change Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference) held in Glasgow multiple governments and companies signed a non-legally-binding declaration to accelerate the transition to 100% zero emission cars and vans (the Glasgow Declaration). They wanted all new cars and vans to not emit any greenhouse gas at the tailpipe by 2035 in leading markets and by 2040 globally.[^cite_note-Glasgow-39][^cite_note-Accelerating_to_Zero_Coalition-40][^cite_note-Green_Car_Congress-2021-41] The United States and China (the biggest car markets) did not sign and neither did Germany (the biggest car market in the EU). Also absent from the list of signatories were major car manufacturers Volkswagen, Toyota, Renault-Nissan and Hyundai-Kia.[^cite_note-42]

### European Union

In 2018, Denmark proposed an EU-wide prohibition on petrol and diesel cars, but that turned out to be contrary to EU regulations. In October 2019, Denmark made a proposal for phasing out fossil fuel vehicles on the member state level by 2030 which was supported by 10 other EU member states.[^cite_note-Ekblom-23] In July 2021, France opposed a ban on combustion-powered cars and in particular on hybrid vehicles.[^cite_note-43] In July 2021, the [European Commission](9974.md) proposed a 100% reduction of emissions for new sales of cars and vans as of 2035.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] On 8 June 2022, the [European Parliament](9581.md) voted in favour of the proposal of the European Commission, but agreement with the European Union member states was necessary before a final law could be passed.[^cite_note-46]

On 22 June 2022, German Finance Minister [Christian Lindner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Lindner) stated that his government would refuse to agree on the ban.[^cite_note-47] But on 29 June 2022, after 16 hours of negotiations, all climate ministers of the 27 EU member states agreed to the commission's proposal (part of the 'Fit for 55' package) to effectively ban the sale of new internal combustion vehicles by 2035 (through '\[introducing] a 100% CO<sub>2</sub> emissions reduction target by 2035 for new cars and vans').[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-Guardian_29June22-49][^cite_note-50] Germany backed the 2035 target, asking the Commission whether hybrid vehicles or CO<sub>2</sub>-neutral fuels could also comply with the proposal; [Frans Timmermans](9524458.md) responded that the Commission kept an "open mind", but at the time 'hybrids did not deliver sufficient emissions cuts and alternative fuels were prohibitively expensive.'[^cite_note-Guardian_29June22-49] The law for "zero CO<sub>2</sub> emissions for new cars and vans in 2035" was approved by the European Parliament on 14 February 2023.[^cite_note-51]

In September 2024, Italy's industry minister called on the EU to reassess its 2035 ban on petrol and diesel cars, suggesting an earlier review for clarity. The Italian government pushed for greater flexibility in achieving decarbonization goals and a more gradual transition from combustion engines.[^cite_note-52]

### Countries

Countries with proposed bans or implementing 100% sales of [zero-emissions vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-emissions_vehicle) include China (including Hong Kong and Macau), Japan, Singapore, the UK, South Korea, Iceland, Denmark, Sweden, Slovenia, Germany, Italy, France, Belgium, the Netherlands, Portugal, Canada, the 12 US states that adhered to [California's Zero-Emission Vehicle (ZEV) Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZEV_mandate), Sri Lanka, Cabo Verde, and Costa Rica.[^cite_note-EVOutlook2020-2]

Countries that have already reached near 100% zero-emissions vehicles are Norway and Ethiopia.

Some cities or territories have planned or taken measures to partially or entirely phase out fossil fuel vehicles earlier than their national governments. In some cases, this is achieved through local or regional government initiatives, in other cases through legal challenges brought on by citizens or civil organisations enforcing partial phase-outs based on the right to clean air.[^cite_note-Gropp-94]

Some cities listed have signed the Fossil Fuel Free Streets Declaration, committing to banning emitting vehicles by 2030,[^cite_note-95] but this does not necessarily have the force of law in those jurisdictions. The bans typically apply to a select number of streets in the urban centre of the city where most people live, not to its entire territory. Some cities take a gradual approach to prohibit the most polluting categories of vehicles first, then the next-most polluting, all the way up to a complete ban on all fossil-fuel vehicles; some cities have not yet set a deadline for a complete ban, and/or are waiting for the national government to set such a date.[^cite_note-Utrecht_2020-96][^cite_note-Seijdel-97][^cite_note-Willems-98]

In California, emissions requirements for automakers to be permitted to sell any vehicles in the state were expected to force 15% of new vehicles offered for sale between 2018 and 2025 to be zero emission. Much cleaner emissions and increased efficiency in petrol engines mean this will be met with just 8% of ZEV vehicles.[^cite_note-99] The "Ditching Dirt Diesel" law SB 44 sponsored by [Nancy Skinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nancy_Skinner_\(California_politician\)) and adopted on 20 September 2019 requires the [California Air Resources Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Air_Resources_Board) (CARB) to "create a comprehensive strategy for deploying medium- and heavy-duty vehicles" to make California meet federal ambient air quality standards, and 'establish goals and spur technology advancements for reducing GHG emissions from the medium- and heavy-duty vehicle sectors by 2030 and 2050'. It stops short of directly requiring a phase-out of all diesel vehicles by 2050 (as the original bill did), but it would be the most obvious means of achieving the reduction goals.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] In August 2022, California Governor Gavin Newsom signed off on a new EV mandate. The plan's targets are 35% ZEV market share by 2026, 68% by 2030, and 100% by 2035.[^cite_note-102] This plan is accompanied by supporting funding for infrastructure and ZEV rebates totaling \$10 billion. Newsom has stated his commitment to keep California at the forefront of zero-emission transportation.

In the [European Union](9317.md), Council Directive 96/62/EC on ambient air quality assessment and management and [Directive 2008/50/EC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directive_2008/50/EC) on ambient air quality form the legal basis for EU citizens' right to clean air.[^cite_note-DUH_2019-103] On 25 July 2008 in the case Dieter Janecek v Freistaat Bayern CURIA, the [European Court of Justice](87187.md) ruled that under Directive 96/62/EC[^cite_note-Europa1996-104] citizens have the right to require national authorities to implement a short-term action plan that aims to maintain or achieve compliance to air quality limit values.[^cite_note-CURIA_2008-105] The ruling of the [German Federal Administrative Court in Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Administrative_Court_\(Germany\)) on 5 September 2013 significantly strengthened the right of environmental associations and consumer protection organisations to sue local authorities to enforce compliance with air quality limits throughout an entire city.[^cite_note-DUH_2019-103] The Administrative Court of Wiesbaden declared on 30 June 2015 that financial or economic aspects were not a valid excuse to refrain from taking measures to ensure that the limit values were observed, the Administrative Court of Düsseldorf ruled on 13 September 2016 that driving bans on certain diesel vehicles were legally possible to comply with the limit values as quickly as possible, and on 26 July 2017, the Administrative Court of Stuttgart ordered the state of [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md) to consider a year-round ban on diesel-powered vehicles.[^cite_note-DUH_2019-103] By mid-February 2018, citizens in the [EU member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU_member_state) the [Czech Republic](5321.md), France, Germany, [Hungary](13275.md), Italy, [Romania](25445.md), [Slovakia](26830.md), Spain, and the United Kingdom were suing their governments for violating the limit of 40 micrograms per cubic meter of breathable air as stipulated in the Ambient Air Quality Directive.[^cite_note-Gropp-94]

A landmark ruling by the [German Federal Administrative Court in Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Administrative_Court_\(Germany\)) on 27 February 2018 declared that the cities of [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart) and [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf) were allowed to legally prohibit older, more polluting diesel vehicles from driving in zones worst affected by pollution, rejecting appeals made by German states against the bans imposed by the two cities' local courts. The case was strongly influenced by the ongoing [Volkswagen emissions scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_emissions_scandal) (also known as Dieselgate), which in 2015 revealed that many [Volkswagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen) diesel engines were deceptively tested and marketed as much cleaner than they were. The decision was predicted to set a precedent for other places in the country and in Europe.[^cite_note-Leipzig_ruling-9] Indeed, the ruling triggered a wave of dozens of local diesel restrictions, brought about by [Environmental Action Germany](49107156.md) (DUH) suing city authorities and winning legal challenges across Germany.[^cite_note-106] While some groups and parties such as the [AfD](38708229.md) again tried to overturn them, others such as the [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_90/The_Greens) advocated for a national phaseout of diesel cars by 2030.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-Reuters_Factbox-108] On 13 December 2018, the [European Court of Justice](87187.md) overturned a 2016 [European Commission](9974.md) relaxation of car NO<sub>*x*</sub> emission limits to 168 mg/km, which the Court declared illegal. This allowed the cities of Brussels, Madrid, and Paris, who had filed the complaint, to proceed with their plans to also reject Euro 6 diesel vehicles from their urban centres, based on the original 80 mg/km limit set by EU law.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-Krajinska-110][^cite_note-113]

Sales of electric vehicles (EVs) indicate a trend away from gas-powered vehicles that generate greenhouse gases.

In 2022, [Maserati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maserati) announced its plans to offer full-electric variants of all its models by 2025 and its intention to halt production of combustion engine vehicles by 2030.[^cite_note-181]

## Shipping

See also: [Mobility transition § Measures in freight transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobility_transition#Measures_in_freight_transport), [MARPOL 73/78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MARPOL_73/78), [Environmental effects of shipping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_effects_of_shipping), [Regulation of ship pollution in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_of_ship_pollution_in_the_United_States), [Cruise ship pollution in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_ship_pollution_in_the_United_States), and [Cruise ship pollution in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_ship_pollution_in_Europe)

Emissions will be banned from Norway's World Heritage Sites [Geirangerfjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geirangerfjord) and [Nærøyfjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A6r%C3%B8yfjord) from 2026.[^cite_note-205]

Besides boats driven by batteries or indeed [trolley boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolley_boat), there have been several attempts to adapt [nuclear marine propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_marine_propulsion) which has been a part of the military naval forces of many countries for decades in the form of [nuclear submarines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_submarine), nuclear [aircraft carriers](2219.md) and [nuclear icebreakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_icebreaker) to civilian uses. While prototypes like [Otto Hahn (ship)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Hahn_\(ship\)) (German) [NS Savannah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NS_Savannah) (American) and [RV Mirai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RV_Mirai) (Japan) were built, the only non-icebreaker nuclear powered ship to remain in civilian service is the Russian [Sevmorput](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevmorput) built in the late 1980s by the Soviet Union. The Soviet Union and its successor state Russia also maintains a fleet of [nuclear icebreakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_icebreaker) to keep the [Northern Sea Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Sea_Route) open.

[Sail ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sail_ships) and [oars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oars) rely on renewable resources rather than fossil fuels (wind and human muscle-power respectively) but have disadvantages in terms of speed and labour-costs and have thus been phased out of virtually all commercial uses. There are some attempts to use wind-powered ships for commercial purposes, but as of 2022 they have remained marginal.[^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207][^cite_note-208]

[Alternative fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_fuel) are being explored more and more as part of the [decarbonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decarbonization) of the maritime freight sector. For example, [methanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methanol) has become known as a feasible transition fuel: it can be used in [dual-fuel engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_fuel_engine) and, when manufactured from [renewable electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_electricity), [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) and captured [CO2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CO2) as "e-methanol", can offer considerable [greenhouse gas reductions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_reduction) when compared to traditional [heavy fuel oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_fuel_oil).[^cite_note-Hapag-Lloyd-2024-209][^cite_note-Early-2025-210][^cite_note-Njovu-2024-211] Meanwhile, [ammonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia) is under active development as a [zero-carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-carbon) [fuel alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_alternative) in its "e-ammonia" form, which contains no [carbon atoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_atoms) and thus emits no [CO2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CO2) at the time of [combustion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combustion).[^cite_note-Hapag-Lloyd-2024-209][^cite_note-Njovu-2024-211] Although numerous infrastructure, safety, and cost-challenges remain, the commercial shipping industry anticipates [ammonia-fueled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ammonia_fuel) vessels to enter service in the coming years.[^cite_note-Early-2025-210][^cite_note-Njovu-2024-211]

[Energy conservation and efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conservation) measures are also key to reducing shipping [emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emission_of_greenhouse_gases). A widely adopted practice is [slow steaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slow_steaming): reducing a ship's cruising speed can yield major [fuel savings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_savings) since [fuel consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_consumption) is roughly proportional to the [cube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cube_\(algebra\)) of the speed. For instance, reducing a ship's cruising speed from 27 to 18 [knots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knot_\(unit\)) can cut consumption by \~50% or more.[^cite_note-212] Another rising technology is [wind-assisted propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind-assisted_propulsion), notably via [Flettner rotors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flettner_rotors), which are vertical spinning cylinders that harness the [Magnus effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnus_effect) to generate thrust. Trials and retrofits show [fuel savings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_savings) of at least 10% when Flettner rotors are applied, and that the technology is gaining wider uptake.[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214]

## Aviation

Further information: [Electric aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_aircraft) and [Aviation taxation and subsidies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_taxation_and_subsidies)

Norway, and possibly some other [Scandinavian countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), are aiming for all [domestic flights](30208174.md) to be emission-free by 2040.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216] A major obstacle to decarbonising air travel is the low [energy density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_density) of current and foreseeable battery technology.[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218] Thus alternatives to [electric planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_plane) such as so called [sustainable aviation fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_aviation_fuel)[^cite_note-219] or [e-fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-fuel) (fuels derived from [electrochemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrochemical) conversion of substances like water and carbon dioxide into hydrocarbons) are also proposed as a future replacement of current jet fuels.[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222] In 2021 the first production scale plant for e-fuels to be used in aviation opened in northern Germany. Production capacity is planned to reach 8 [barrels](594043.md) a day by 2022.[^cite_note-223] [Lufthansa](97733.md) will be among the chief users of the synthetic fuel produced in the new facility.[^cite_note-224] Germany's plan to transform aviation to [net zero carbon emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_neutrality) relies heavily on e-fuels.[^cite_note-225]

Besides the need to rapidly scale up currently minuscule production capacity, the main obstacles to wider deployment of sustainable aviation fuels and e-Fuels are their much higher cost in the absence of meaningful [carbon pricing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_pricing) in aviation.[^cite_note-226] Furthermore, with current [CORSIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CORSIA) regulations for sustainable aviation fuels allowing up to 90% of emissions compared to conventional fuels, even those options are currently far from [carbon neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_neutral).[^cite_note-227] However, short haul, low demand routes can be easily flown using electric aircraft, and manufacturers such as [Heart Aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_Aerospace) are planning to introduce them with [United Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Airlines) in 2026.

There were attempts at building [nuclear-powered aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear-powered_aircraft) during the [Cold War](325329.md), which unlike [nuclear marine propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_marine_propulsion) never got very far and were always only proposed for military uses. As of 2022 no country or private enterprise is seriously pursuing nuclear propulsion for passenger aircraft.

## Unintended side-effects

### Second-hand vehicle dumping

Main article: [Used car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Used_car)

Further information: [Effects of the car on societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Effects_of_the_car_on_societies) and [Road ecology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_ecology)

From the European Union, there is already an export market which includes millions of used cars which are sent to Eastern Europe and the Caucasus, Central Asia and Africa.[^cite_note-euobserver.com-228][^cite_note-229] According to UNECE, the global on-road vehicle fleet is to double by 2050 (from 1.2 billion to 2.5 billion,[^cite_note-230] see introduction), with most future car purchases taking place in developing countries. Some experts predict that the number of vehicles in developing countries will increase four- or five-fold by 2050 (compared to [current car use levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_share)), and that the majority of these will be second-hand.[^cite_note-Edwards-2017-231][^cite_note-232] There are currently no global or even regional agreements that rationalise and govern the flow of second-hand vehicles.[^cite_note-Edwards-2017-231] Others say that new electric two-wheelers may sell widely in developing countries as they are affordable.[^cite_note-233]

Internal combustion engine cars that may no longer comply to local environmental standards are exported to developing countries, where legislation on vehicle emissions is often less strict. In addition, in some developing countries, such as Uganda, the average age of a car imported is already 16.5 years and it will likely be driven for another 20 years. In such cases, [fuel efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_efficiency) levels of these vehicles become worse as they age.[^cite_note-Edwards-2017-231][^cite_note-234] In addition, [national vehicle inspection requirements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_inspection) vary widely depending on the country.

On the other hand the [embodied carbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embodied_carbon) in a car represents a significant proportion of its lifetime carbon, so extending the life of a vehicle may be carbon efficient.

### Potential solutions

- **Export prohibitions**: Some propose that the European Union could implement a rule that does not allow the most polluting cars to leave the EU.

  [^cite_note-euobserver.com-228]

  &#x20;The European Union itself is of the opinion that it "should stop exporting its waste outside of the EU" and it will therefore "revisit the rules on waste shipments and illegal exports".

  [^cite_note-235]

- **Import prohibitions**: This includes used vehicle bans, used vehicle import age limits, taxation and inspection tests as a precondition to vehicle registration.

  [^cite_note-236]

- **Convert fossil fuel vehicles to electric**: As of 2021, this is expensive, so it tends to only be done for classic cars.

  [^cite_note-237]

- **Conversion to green hydrogen or biofuels**

- **Mandatory recycling**: The European Commission is considering plans to introduce rules on mandatory recycled content in specific product groups for packaging, vehicles, construction materials and batteries, for instance.

  [^cite_note-238]

  &#x20;The EU announced a new Circular Economy Action Plan in March 2020,

  [^cite_note-239]

  &#x20;and it mentioned that the Commission will also propose to revise [the rules on end-of-life vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_of_Life_Vehicles_Directive) with a view to promoting more circular business models.

  [^cite_note-240]

- **[Scrappage programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrappage_program)**: Governments can offer a premium to owners to have their fossil fuel vehicles voluntarily scrapped and to buy a cleaner vehicle from that money (if they so choose). For example, the city of [Ghent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghent) offers a scrapping premium of €1,000 for diesel vehicles and €750 for petrol vehicles; as of December 2019, the city had allocated €1.2 million for this purpose to the scrapping fund.

## Mobility transition

Main article: [Mobility transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobility_transition)

In Germany, activists have coined the term *Verkehrswende* ([mobility transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobility_transition), analogous to "[Energiewende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energiewende)", energy transition) for a project of not only changing the motive power of cars (from fossil fuels to renewable power sources) but the entire mobility system to one of [walkability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkability), [complete streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_streets), [public transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transit), [electrified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_electrification) railways and [bicycle infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_infrastructure).

There is similar research being done in the United States around the term mobility justice.[^cite_note-241] Geologist Dr. Jason Henderson of [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco) argues that supporting electric vehicles while neglecting compact city design and public transportation will lead to car-oriented city design.[^cite_note-242] This comes with numerous sustainability issues that disproportionately affect disadvantaged communities such as [environmental gentrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_gentrification), less [low-income housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-income_housing), and unequal access to the benefits of electric vehicle adoption. In addition, the production of electric vehicles can come at the price of laborers in other countries, and the environmental costs there are seldom taken into account when calculating the environmental benefits of electric vehicles. According to mobility justice critiques, relying primarily on electric vehicles for the phase out of fossil fuels comes at an opportunity cost of investing in other types of sustainable transportation such as bike lanes, safe walking spaces, electric trains, and electric buses.
