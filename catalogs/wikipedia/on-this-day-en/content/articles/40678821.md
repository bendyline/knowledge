**Monique Barbut** (French pronunciation: [\[mɔnik baʁby\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); born 22 August 1956)[^cite_note-ahpne-1] is a French civil servant and politician who has served as [Minister of Ecological Transition](3366829.md) in the [second government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Lecornu_government) of Prime Minister [Sébastien Lecornu](54364868.md) since 12 October 2025.

From 2013 until 2019, Barbut served as the Executive Secretary of the [United Nations Convention to Combat Desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Convention_to_Combat_Desertification) (UNCCD).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] She was appointed to this position by the [United Nations Secretary-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary-General) [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) on 16 September 2013.[^cite_note-4]

## Early life and education

Barbut was born in [Safi, Morocco](1560340.md), a few months after the end of the [French protectorate in Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_in_Morocco).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-ahpne-1] Barbut obtained her Master of Philosophy in Economics and Bachelor of Arts in English from the [University of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris).

## Career

Due to her role in the French government in the [1992 Earth Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth_Summit) and the establishment of the French [Global Environment Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Environment_Facility), Barbut became the first chief executive officer of the French GEF.[^cite_note-6] She subsequently held a number of high-ranking positions, including chief executive officer and chairperson of GEF (2006–2012) and director of the Division of Technology, Industry and Economics at the [United Nations Environment Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Environment_Programme) (2003–2006).

Later in her career, Barbut served as the special adviser to the chief executive officer of the [French Development Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Development_Agency) (AFD) from 2012 to 2013.[^cite_note-7] In 2019, she was a member of the "High-Level Group of Wise Persons" appointed by the [Council of the European Union](5866.md) and charged with drafting recommendations on how to reform existing financial instruments for [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development) managed by the [European Commission](9974.md), the [European Investment Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Investment_Bank) (EIB) and the [European Bank for Reconstruction and Development](37963.md) (EBRD).[^cite_note-8]

On 12 October 2025 she was appointed [Minister of Ecological Transition](3366829.md) in the [second government](81029757.md) of Prime Minister [Sébastien Lecornu](54364868.md).[^cite_note-9] She offered her resignation on 22 July 2026 to President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) in protest of a bill passed by the French legislature that would allow the [ French Agency for Food, Environmental and Occupational Health & Safety (ANSES)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agence_nationale_de_s%C3%A9curit%C3%A9_sanitaire_de_l'alimentation,_de_l'environnement_et_du_travail) to authorise for the use of [acetamiprid](23876423.md) and [flupyradifurone](69195377.md), [pesticides](48340.md) accused of harming [bees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bee).[^cite_note-10] After meeting with President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md), her resignation was ultimately rejected, and she remained in office.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]
