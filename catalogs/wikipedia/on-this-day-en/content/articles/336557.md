# Blood test

For the novel by Jonathan Kellerman, see [Blood Test (novel)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_Test_\(novel\)).



Modern hospital hematology laboratory

A **blood test** is a [laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_laboratory) analysis performed on a [blood](3997.md) sample that is usually extracted from a [vein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vein) in the arm using a [hypodermic needle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypodermic_needle), or via [fingerprick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingerprick). Multiple tests for specific blood components, such as a [glucose test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose_test) or a [cholesterol test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholesterol_test), are often grouped together into one [test panel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_panel) called a **blood panel** or **blood work**. Blood tests are often used in [health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care) to determine [physiological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physiological) and [biochemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biochemical) states, such as [disease](8072.md), [mineral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) content, [pharmaceutical drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmaceutical_drug) effectiveness, and organ function. Typical [clinical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicine#Clinical_practice) blood panels include a [basic metabolic panel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_metabolic_panel) or a [complete blood count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_blood_count). Blood tests are also used in [drug tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_test) to detect drug abuse.

## Extraction



A [venipuncture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venipuncture) performed using a [vacutainer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacutainer)

A [venipuncture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venipuncture) is useful as it is a [minimally invasive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasiveness_of_surgical_procedures) way to obtain [cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_\(biology\)) and [extracellular fluid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extracellular_fluid) ([plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_plasma)) from the body for analysis. Blood flows throughout the body, acting as a medium that provides [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) and nutrients to tissues and carries waste products back to the excretory systems for disposal. Consequently, the state of the bloodstream affects or is affected by, many medical conditions. For these reasons, blood tests are the most commonly performed [medical tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_test).[^cite_note-1]

If only a few drops of blood are needed, a [fingerstick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fingerstick) is performed instead of a [venipuncture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venipuncture).[^cite_note-pmid17630562-2]

Indwelling arterial, central venous and peripheral venous lines can also be used to draw blood.[^cite_note-3]

[Phlebotomists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phlebotomist), [laboratory practitioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_laboratory) and [nurses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurse) are those in charge of extracting blood from a patient. This can be done in-clinic, in-office, or through mobile (home) phlebotomists. However, in special circumstances, and/or emergency situations, [paramedics](75006.md) and [physicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physician) extract the blood. Also, [respiratory therapists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respiratory_therapist) are trained to extract arterial blood to examine [arterial blood gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arterial_blood_gas).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

## Types of tests



[Vacutainer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacutainer) tubes used in the collection of blood. During venipuncture, pressure differences between the vein and the vacuum in the Vacutainer forces blood into the tube.

### Biochemical analysis

A [basic metabolic panel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_metabolic_panel) measures [sodium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium), [potassium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potassium), [chloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloride), [bicarbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicarbonate), [blood urea nitrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_urea_nitrogen) (BUN), [magnesium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesium), [creatinine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creatinine), [glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose), and sometimes [calcium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium). Tests that focus on cholesterol levels can determine [LDL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LDL_cholesterol) and [HDL cholesterol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HDL_cholesterol) levels, as well as [triglyceride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triglyceride) levels.[^cite_note-6]

Some tests, such as those that measure glucose or a [lipid profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid_profile), require fasting (or no food consumption) eight to twelve hours prior to the drawing of the blood sample.[^cite_note-7]

For the majority of tests, blood is usually obtained from the patient's vein. Other specialized tests, such as the [arterial blood gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arterial_blood_gas) test, require blood extracted from an [artery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artery). Blood gas analysis of arterial blood is primarily used to monitor [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) and [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) levels related to [pulmonary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary) function, but is also used to measure blood [pH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PH) and [bicarbonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicarbonate) levels for certain metabolic conditions.[^cite_note-8]

While the regular [glucose test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose_test) is taken at a certain point in time, the [glucose tolerance test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose_tolerance_test) involves repeated testing to determine the rate at which [glucose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose) is processed by the body.[^cite_note-9]

#### Specimen storage and stability

Blood specimens for [complete blood count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complete_blood_count) (CBC) analysis should ideally be analysed as soon as possible after collection to minimise pre-analytical changes. When delays are unavoidable, [EDTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDTA)-anticoagulated blood may be stored under refrigerated conditions (2–8 °C), although the stability of individual CBC parameters varies. [Haemoglobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haemoglobin-based_oxygen_carriers) concentration and [red blood cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_blood_cell) and [white blood cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_blood_cell) counts are generally more stable during refrigerated storage than [platelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platelet)-related parameters and some [red blood cell indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_blood_cell_indices). Laboratories should follow validated storage recommendations and consider potential analytical changes when interpreting delayed specimens. [^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

Blood tests are also used to identify autoimmune diseases and [Immunoglobulin E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunoglobulin_E)-mediated food allergies (see also [Radioallergosorbent test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioallergosorbent_test)).[^cite_note-13]<sup>: 1118 </sup>

## Future alternatives

### Saliva tests

In 2008, scientists announced that the more cost effective [saliva testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saliva_testing) could eventually replace some blood tests, as saliva contains 20% of the proteins found in blood.[^cite_note-18] Saliva testing may not be appropriate or available for all markers. For example, lipid levels cannot be measured with saliva testing.

### Microemulsion

In February 2011, Canadian researchers at the University of Calgary's Schulich School of Engineering announced a microchip for blood tests. Dubbed a microemulsion, a droplet of blood captured inside a layer of another substance. It can control the exact size and spacing of the droplets. The new test could improve the efficiency, accuracy, and speed of laboratory tests while also doing it cheaply.[^cite_note-19]

### SIMBAS

In March 2011, a team of researchers from [UC Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_Berkeley), [DCU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_City_University) and [University of Valparaíso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Valpara%C3%ADso) have developed [lab-on-a-chip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lab-on-a-chip) that can diagnose diseases within 10 minutes without the use of external tubing and extra components. It is called Self-powered Integrated Microfluidic Blood Analysis System (SIMBAS). It uses tiny trenches to separate blood cells from plasma (99 percent of blood cells were captured during experiments). Researchers used plastic components, to reduce manufacturing costs.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]
