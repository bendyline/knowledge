**Cap Ferret** (French pronunciation: [\[kap fɛʁɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French); English: Cape Ferret) is a [headland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headland) situated at the south end of the [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_France) of [Lège-Cap-Ferret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A8ge-Cap-Ferret) in the [Gironde](64654.md) [department](38523.md) in the French southwestern [Nouvelle-Aquitaine](45092904.md) [region](58846.md). The headland takes the form of a [spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spit_\(landform\)), which separates the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) from [Arcachon Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcachon_Bay). At the same time, the entrance to Arcachon Bay separates Cap Ferret from the resort town of [Arcachon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcachon).

Cap Ferret is notable for its [lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lighthouse) and as an up-market resort that has retained its natural feel at the heart of the [Landes of Gascony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landes_of_Gascony) and the [Pays de Buch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pays_de_Buch). It is also famous for its ostréicole ([oyster farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyster_farming)) activity and numerous tasting sites.

Cap Ferret is accessible by road from the north, and by passenger [ferry](50771.md) from Arcachon. The [Tramway du Cap-Ferret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tramway_du_Cap-Ferret), a diesel operated narrow-gauge railway, links the ferry landing at Bélisaire on the shores of Arcachon Bay with the beaches on the Atlantic coast.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

In July 2026, authorities ordered a complete evacuation of around 40,000 people from the Cap Ferret peninsula due to [wildfires](83729774.md) which had started to the north in [Saumos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saumos) and had consumed 8,700 hectares (c. 21,500 acres).[^cite_note-4]
