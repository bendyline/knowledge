# Shootout

An armed suspect flees the scene while engaged in a shootout with police

A still from [police body camera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_body_camera) footage of a shootout between a suspect and [Volusia County, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volusia_County,_Florida) [sheriff's deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volusia_Sheriff%27s_Office) in 2019

A **shootout**, also called a **firefight**, **gunfight**, or **gun battle**, is a confrontation in which parties armed with [firearms](11966.md) exchange gunfire. The term can be used to describe any such fight, though it is typically used in a non-[military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) context or to describe combat situations primarily using firearms (generally excluding [crew-served weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crew-served_weapon), [combat vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_vehicle), [armed aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aircraft), or [explosives](10192.md)).&#x20;

Shootouts often pit [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement) against [criminals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime), though they can also involve parties outside of law enforcement, such as rival [gangs](80408.md), [militias](20622.md), or individuals. Military combat situations are rarely titled "shootouts", and are almost always considered [battles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle), [engagements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engagement_\(military\)), [skirmishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skirmisher), exchanges, or firefights.&#x20;

Shootouts are often depicted in [action films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_film), [Westerns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_\(genre\)), and [video games](5363.md).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]&#x20;
