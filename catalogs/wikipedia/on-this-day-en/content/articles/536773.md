A **patrol boat** (also referred to as a **patrol craft**, **patrol ship**, or **patrol vessel**) is a relatively small [naval vessel](53159.md) generally designed for [coastal defence](25242026.md), [border security](247927.md), or [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement). However, large size patrol vessels are also operated by multiple navies and coastguards. There are many designs for patrol boats, and they generally range in size. They may be operated by a nation's [navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navy), [coast guard](146790.md), [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police), or [customs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs), and may be intended for marine ("[blue water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_geography#Blue_water)"), [estuarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary) ("[green water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_geography#Green_water)"), or riverine ("[brown water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_geography#Brown_water)") environments.

Per their name, patrol boats are primarily used to patrol a country's [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_economic_zone) (EEZ), but they may also be used in other roles, such as anti-[smuggling](28645.md), anti-[piracy](50715.md), [fishery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishery) patrols, [immigration law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_law) enforcement, or [search and rescue](172599.md). Depending on the size, organization, and capabilities of a nation's armed forces, the importance of patrol boats may range from minor support vessels that are part of a coast guard, to flagships that make up a majority of a navy's fleet. Their small size and relatively low cost make them one of the most common naval vessels in the world.

Patrol boats are of varying types, including: **offshore patrol vessels** (**OPV**), **patrol boat fast** (**PBF**), **patrol boat with surface-to-surface missile** (**PBG**), **patrol boat inshore** (**PBI**), **patrol boat with SAM** (**PBM**), **patrol boat riverine** (**PBR**), **patrol boat with torpedo** (**PBT**).[^cite_note-1]

## Classification



*Kawachi*, a patrol boat in service with the [Osaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osaka_Prefecture) [Prefectural Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectural_police)



A [Vietnam People's Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_People's_Navy) [TT-400TP midsize patrol boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TT-400TP_gunboat). It is equipped with a [76mm naval gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-176), hence being designated as a [gunboat](274471.md) in Vietnamese service.

The classification of a patrol boat is often subjective, but they are generally small naval vessels that are used to patrol national waters or a certain [jurisdiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction). They may be as large as a [corvette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette) or even a [frigate](11464.md), though the term may also be used for vessels as small as a [yacht](34291.md) or [rigid inflatable boat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigid_inflatable_boat). They can include [gunboat](274471.md), [fast attack craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_attack_craft), [torpedo boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torpedo_boat), and [missile boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_boat). They may be broadly classified as inshore patrol vessels (IPVs) or offshore patrol vessels (OPVs). OPVs and medium size cutters are usually the smallest ship in a navy's fleet that are large and seaworthy enough to patrol off-shore in the open [ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean), while IPVs are typically too small to do so and are instead kept in [lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake) or [rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River), or close to [coasts](5236.md); IPVs specifically used in rivers can also be called "riverine patrol vessels". Large patrol vessels, offshore patrol vessels and Large cutters are the largest type of patrol ships and are typically more than 100 m (330 ft) in length. Some OPVs may possess significant armament and may approach [corvettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corvette) in terms of armed capability.

Seagoing patrol boats are typically around 30 m (100 ft) in length and usually carry a single medium caliber [artillery gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_artillery) as main armament, and a variety of lighter secondary armament such as [machine guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_gun), while others include the sophisticated [close-in weapon system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close-in_weapon_system). Depending on their role, vessels in this class may also have more sophisticated sensors and [fire control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-control_system) systems that would enable them to carry [torpedoes](31424.md), [anti-ship missiles](140725.md), and [surface-to-air missiles](182664.md).[^cite_note-2]

## History



*PCE-872*, a [World War II](32927.md) patrol craft escort of the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md)

During both World Wars, in order to rapidly build up numbers, all sides created auxiliary patrol boats by arming [motorboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorboat) and seagoing [fishing trawlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_trawler) with machine guns and obsolete naval weapons. Some modern patrol vessels are still based on fishing and leisure boats.

The [United States Navy](20518076.md) operated the [*Pegasus* class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pegasus-class_hydrofoil) of armed hydrofoils for years in a patrol boat role. During the [Vietnam War](32611.md), the U.S. Navy ordered 193 aluminum hulled [Patrol Craft, Fast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol_Craft_Fast) (PCFs),[^cite_note-3] also known as *Swiftboats*, for brown water naval operations. The [Patrol Boat, River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol_Boat,_River) (PBR, sometimes called "Riverine" and "Pibber") was a fiberglass hulled vessel also designed and used for inland river operations during the Vietnam War, and became an icon of water operations during the war due to its use in the 1979 film *[Apocalypse Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apocalypse_Now)*.

Most modern designs are powered by [gas turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_turbine) arrangements such as [CODAG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_diesel_and_gas), and speeds are generally in the 25–30 knots (46–56 km/h; 29–35 mph) range. The largest OPVs might also have a [flight deck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_deck) and [helicopter](8286923.md) embarked. In times of crisis or war, these vessels are expected to support the larger vessels in a navy, though some smaller navies are mostly composed of just patrol boats.
