**De Nederlandsche Bank** (Dutch pronunciation: [\[də ˈneːdərlɑntsə bɑŋk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch), lit. 'The Dutch Bank', abbr. **DNB**) is the [national central bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_central_bank_\(Eurosystem\)) for the [Netherlands](21148.md) within the [Eurosystem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosystem). It was the Dutch [central bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_bank) from 1814 to 1998, issuing the [guilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_guilder). It was originally founded by [King William I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I_of_the_Netherlands), and has been since transformed into a state-owned [public limited company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_limited_company) ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *[naamloze vennootschap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naamloze_vennootschap)*, abbreviated NV). In English, it generally goes by its name in [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language), but is also occasionally referred to as the **Netherlands Bank**.[^cite_note-2]

In addition to its monetary role, De Nederlandsche Bank is also a [financial supervisory authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_supervisory_authority). In that capacity, it increasingly implements policies set at the [European Union](9317.md) level. It is the national competent authority for the Netherlands within [European Banking Supervision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Banking_Supervision).[^cite_note-3] It is a voting member of the respective Boards of Supervisors of the [European Banking Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Banking_Authority) (EBA)[^cite_note-4] and [European Insurance and Occupational Pensions Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Insurance_and_Occupational_Pensions_Authority) (EIOPA).[^cite_note-5] It is the designated Dutch [National Resolution Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Resolution_Authority) and plenary session member of the [Single Resolution Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_Resolution_Board) (SRB).[^cite_note-6] It provides the permanent single common representative for the Netherlands in the Supervisory composition of the General Board of the [Anti-Money Laundering Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Money_Laundering_Authority) (AMLA).[^cite_note-7] It is also a member of the [European Systemic Risk Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Systemic_Risk_Board) (ESRB).[^cite_note-8]

## History

On 2 May 1998, the European heads of state or government decided that the Economic and Monetary Union (EMU) would begin on 1 January 1999 with eleven member states of the European Union (EU), the Netherlands included. As from 1 June 1998, the Dutch central bank, De Nederlandsche Bank, forms part of the [European System of Central Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_System_of_Central_Banks) (ESCB). On the same day, the new Bank Act (of 1998) came into force. Nearly 185 years into its existence, the Nederlandsche Bank entered a new phase.

In 2026, DNB shifted 86 tonnes of gold from US and Canada to London, stating that the reserves held in London would be easier to trade while also citing "geopolitical unrest." The share of gold held in London increased from 18.1 per cent to 32.1 per cent while the share held in New York and Ottawa were reduced to 18.5 per cent each.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## Tasks

Under the 1998 Bank Act – replacing that of 1948 – the bank has the following tasks:

- Within the framework of the ESCB, the bank shall contribute to the definition and implementation of monetary policy within the European Community (EC). The bank has the objective to maintain price stability. Without prejudice to this objective, the bank shall support the general economic policy in the EC.

- The bank shall hold and manage the official foreign reserves, and shall conduct foreign-exchange operations.

- The bank shall collect statistical data and produce statistics.

- The bank shall promote the smooth operation of payment systems; it shall take care of the banknote circulation.

- The bank shall supervise banks, investment institutions and exchange offices.

- The bank may, subject to permission by royal decree, perform other tasks in the public interest. The European Central Bank (ECB) may also ask the bank to perform extra tasks.

The first two tasks – also known as the ESCB tasks – ensue entirely from the Maastricht Treaty. Decisions in these areas are taken at the European level by the ECB Governing Council, on which the president of the Nederlandsche Bank has a seat. Promoting the smooth operation of payment systems has both a European and a national dimension. The statistical task is also partly ESCB-related and partly a national concern. The DNB is responsible for international macro-economic statistical analysis for countries outside the EU.[^cite_note-11] These two tasks will not be transferred to ESCB level at the start of EMU. Here the Nederlandsche Bank remains fully in control. However, in a Europe where economies are becoming increasingly interlocked, many banking supervisory rules are drawn up at the international level. DNB serves as the banker's bank to general Dutch banks.

One of the government appointed members of the [Social-Economic Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social-Economic_Council) is always a representative of DNB.

## Head office



Then DNB governor [Klaas Knot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaas_Knot) meeting with fellow bank governors [Octavian Armașu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Octavian_Arma%C8%99u) ([NBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Moldova)) and [Pierre Wunsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Wunsch?action=edit\&redlink=1) ([NBB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bank_of_Belgium)), April 2022

The bank was established from the start on [Oude Turfmarkt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oude_Turfmarkt?action=edit\&redlink=1), where it occupied 17th-century houses. In the 1860s, a purpose-built head office, designed by architect [Willem Anthonie Froger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Anthonie_Froger?action=edit\&redlink=1), was erected on the same location and inaugurated in 1869. Nearly a century later in 1968, as the bank has left the building for its new seat, it was taken over by the [University of Amsterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Amsterdam) which had long been established just nearby, and subsequently repurposed as the location of the [Allard Pierson Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allard_Pierson_Museum), which opened there in October 1976.[^cite_note-12]

In the 1960s, a new head office complex was constructed on [Frederiksplein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederiksplein_\(Amsterdam\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and inaugurated by [Queen Juliana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliana_of_the_Netherlands) in May 1968. Architect [Marius Duintjer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marius_Duintjer?action=edit\&redlink=1)'s stark design of 1961 consisted on a low square base and a rectangular office tower. In the late 1980s, as the bank was running out of space, it was complemented with a second, round tower designed in 1984 by architects [Jelle Abma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelle_Abma?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Marc a Campo, the construction of which was finished in 1989.[^cite_note-13] In 2008, Marc a Campo designed a further extension by adding an extra floor to the square base. In a comprehensive renovation during the early 2020s, the round tower was demolished and the base remodeled to return the building to an exterior appearance more similar to the original one of the 1960s.[^cite_note-14]
