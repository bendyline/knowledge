**Davao Occidental** ([Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Kasadpang Dabaw*; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Kanluraning Davao*), officially the **Province of Davao Occidental** ([Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language): *Lalawigan sa Kasadpang Dabaw, Lalawigan sa Davao Occidental*; [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_language): *Lalawigan ng Kanluraning Davao, Lalawigan ng Davao Occidental*), is a [province](165100.md) in the [Philippines](23440.md) located in the [Davao Region](296162.md) in [Mindanao](20546.md).[^cite_note-GovPH-RA10360-5][^cite_note-Cayon-6][^cite_note-YahooNewsPH-DavaoOcc-7] Its capital is the municipality of [Malita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malita,_Davao_Occidental), the most populous town in the province. To the east lies [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf). It also shares a maritime border with the Indonesian province of [North Sulawesi](453688.md) to the south.

### Sultanate era

Davao Occidental was part of a [Sultanate of Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Maguindanao) but for the [Sarangani](2154628.md), it was later became part of [Buayan Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajah_Buayan).

### American colonization

What is now Davao Occidental was once a part of the now-defunct [Davao Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_\(former_province\)) which encompasses the entirety of present-day [Davao Region](296162.md). Section 1 of [Philippine Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commission) Act No. 164 dated December 10, 1904 indicated that much of its area as far as what is now [Malita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malita,_Davao_Occidental) once belonged to the municipality of [Santa Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Cruz,_Davao_del_Sur). The original chief inhabitants of the area were the indigenous [Lumad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad) tribes including the Matigsalugs and Tagakaulos. Around the early 1900s onward, migrants from [Luzon](69375.md) and the [Visayas](72418.md) settled in the area, many of whom intermarried with the indigenous people; as decades progressed, the descendants of the migrants became the majority of the population.

The municipality of Malita was founded on November 13, 1936 per Proclamation No. 64 signed by President Manuel Quezon.[^cite_note-8] It was the first town to be established in the area of what is now Davao Occidental and would later serve as its provincial capital. More towns in the area are established later: [Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Abad_Santos,_Davao_Occidental) (now Jose Abad Santos) in 1948, [Sta Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria,_Davao_Occidental) in 1968, [Don Marcelino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Marcelino,_Davao_Occidental) in 1979, and [Sarangani](2154628.md) in 1980.

### Philippine independence

On May 8, 1967, Davao Province was split into three provinces, one of them being [Davao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Sur) which included the municipalities of what would later comprise Davao Occidental.

#### Foundation

Main article: [2013 Davao Occidental creation plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Davao_Occidental_creation_plebiscite)

Davao Occidental was created through [Republic Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Act) No. 10360 enacted on July 23, 2012, comprising five of the eight municipalities that constitute the [2nd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Sur's_2nd_congressional_district) of [Davao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Sur). The law was passed by the [House of Representatives](293605.md) and [Senate](299521.md) on November 28, 2012, and December 5, 2012, respectively, and signed by [President](271126.md) [Benigno Aquino III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benigno_Aquino_III) on January 14, 2013.[^cite_note-GovPH-RA10360-5][^cite_note-9] A [plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite) was held on October 28, 2013, along with the barangay elections and the majority of votes cast were "Yes", ratifying the province.[^cite_note-Cayon-6]

The motive of creating the province was to boost the economic condition and social progress of the municipalities. Senator [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md), who sponsored the creation of Davao Occidental, said that the great distance of [Digos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digos), Davao del Sur's provincial capital, to other municipalities in the 2nd congressional district, were impairing the effective delivery of basic services, as well as access to provincial government offices.[^cite_note-10] However, Davao del Sur Representative Marc Douglas Cagas considered the creation of the province as nothing more than [gerrymandering](12987.md) and political convenience.[^cite_note-11]

Government officials of Davao del Sur, with assistance from the [Department of the Interior and Local Government](4718691.md), exercised jurisdiction over the Davao Occidental until the elected local officials of the [2016 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Philippine_general_election) assumed office on June 30, 2016.[^cite_note-bw-12][^cite_note-pdi-13]

### Patroness

Our Lady of the Rosary of [Malita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malita) is the patroness and protectress of the province since October 7, 2024, through the Resolution No. 82-2024 released by the Provincial Government Office of Davao Occidental on September 11, 2024. It was also recognized canonically when Bishop [Guillermo Afable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Afable) of the [Diocese of Digos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Digos) issued a decree on 24 April 2025.[^cite_note-14] The devotion started during the 19th century which further propagated through its episcopal coronation that took place on October 7, 2022.[^cite_note-15]

## Geography

Davao Occidental covers a total area of 2,163.45 square kilometers (835.31 sq mi)[^cite_note-nscb-16] occupying the southwestern tip of the [Davao Region](296162.md) in [Mindanao](20546.md). The province is bordered on the northwest by Davao del Sur; west by [Sarangani](297388.md) and northeast by [Davao Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_Gulf).

The topography of Davao Occidental is hilly, rugged and sloping, with nearly the whole province consisting of mountains. Its eastern shoreline consists of cliffs and beaches with hills immediately on their backs. Coconut trees and hardwood trees mostly dominate the provincial mainland.

The municipality of [Sarangani](2154628.md), which include [Balut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balut_Island) and Sarangani Island, are a group of islands that constitute the insular portion of Davao Occidental.

## Government

The province of Davao Occidental is governed by a governor and a vice governor. The whole province is a lone [congressional legislative district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_districts_of_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-nscb-16] The municipality of [Malita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malita,_Davao_Occidental), the largest and most populous in the province, serves as the provincial capital and the place where the provincial officials of Davao Occidental convene.

The population mostly consists of people whose ancestors originate from the migrants from [Visayan Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visayan_Islands) and [Luzon](69375.md). The [Lumad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumad) natives only form a small part of the population and live in the more mountainous and forested areas of the province. Indonesian settlers of [Sangirese descent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangirese_people) even live in the province.

## Economy



The shores of [Malita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malita,_Davao_Occidental) as seen from the hilltops.

The main industries in Davao Occidental are [aquaculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquaculture) and agriculture. Economic produce in the province include fish, [bananas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bananas) and [coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconuts) which are then exported to several major cities within southern Mindanao, including [Davao City](489401.md). Rice farms are only limited to the few flat lands in the province due to its mountainous and thickly forested nature.

The capital town of [Malita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malita,_Davao_Occidental) is the province's main commercial hub.

Tourism has started to gain traction in the province, with potential tourist destinations mostly concentrated in the province's coastal beaches and the [Sarangani](2154628.md) and [Balut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balut_Island) islands in the south.[^cite_note-22]

## Transportation

Davao Occidental is served by only one partially-coastal highway that traverses the whole length of the provincial mainland from north to south, and could only be accessed by going through the road crossings in [Sulop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulop,_Davao_del_Sur) in [Davao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davao_del_Sur) province and, farther ahead, in the city of [General Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Santos). Buses, [jeepneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeepneys) and [passenger vans](40666464.md) that originate from and serve the cities of Davao and [Digos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digos) are the main primary modes of transportation in the province. Boats serve as the primary maritime mode of transportation for coastal areas not yet accessible by roads and the island municipality of [Sarangani](2154628.md).
