**Gandaki Province** ([Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language): गण्डकी प्रदेश, romanized: *Gaṇḍakī pradēśa* ([\[ɡʌɳɖʌki\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Nepali)) [Listen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a5/Ne-Gandaki_pradesh.oga/Ne-Gandaki_pradesh.oga.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is one of the seven [federal provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Nepal) established by the [current constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Nepal) of [Nepal](171166.md) which was promulgated on 20 September 2015.[^cite_note-statoids-1] [Pokhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhara) is the province's capital city. It borders the [Tibet Autonomous Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) in [Southwestern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_China) to the north, [Bagmati Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagmati_Province) to the east, [Karnali Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnali_Province) to the west, and [Lumbini Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumbini_Province) and [Bihar](442482.md) in [India](14533.md) to the south. The total area of the province is 21,733 km<sup>2</sup> (8,391 sq mi), constituting 14.66% of Nepal's total area. According to the [2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nepal_census), the population of the province was 2,479,745.[^cite_note-2] The newly elected Provincial Assembly adopted Gandaki Province as the permanent name, replacing its initial name **Province No. 4** on 27 April 2023. Surendra Raj Pandey is the present chief minister of Gandaki Province.

## Etymology

Further information: [Gandaki River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandaki_River)

Gandaki Province was named after the river [Gandaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandaki_River), which is a major river in the province. This river has important links with historical Hindu civilization. The Gandaki river is mentioned in the ancient [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) epic [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata). It has been said that [Valmiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmiki) wrote the great epic *[Ramayana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramayana)* here. It is also believed to be the birthplace of Luv & Kush. Its evolution is described in Shiva Purana, Kumarakhand, in the chapter of the killing of Shankhachuda.

## History

Further information: [Chaubisi Rajya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaubisi_Rajya) and [Western Development Region, Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Development_Region,_Nepal)

Gandaki province was once home to the [Chaubisi Rajya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaubisi_Rajya), the 24 kings, of which [Gorkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_Kingdom) was one. From Gorkha, [Prithvi Narayan Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prithvi_Narayan_Shah) arose to unify all the kingdoms and expand to include all of present-day Nepal. Gandaki Province was known as "Gandak Kshetra" which was established in 1956. Gandak Kshetra was composed by grouping the then 4 districts. Those 4 districts are now divided into many districts. Gandak Kshetra had total area of 5,400 square miles (14,000 km<sup>2</sup>) and total population was 12 Lakhs.[^cite_note-3] The four districts were:

1. Lumbini District ([Rupandehi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupandehi_District), [Parasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasi_District), [Palpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palpa_District))

2. Syangja District ([Syangja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syangja_District), [Nawalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawalpur_District))

3. Pokhara District ([Tanahun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanahun_District), [Kaski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaski_District))

4. Gorkha District ([Gorkha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorkha_District), [Lamjung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamjung_District), [Manang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manang_District))

In 1962, the administrative system was restructured again and the "Kshetras" system was cancelled. The country was restructured into 75 development districts and those districts were grouped into zones.[^cite_note-4] In 1972 area of the Gandaki Province named [Western Development Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Development_Region,_Nepal). It had 3 zones and 16 districts.

## Geography

The province has an area of 21,773 km<sup>2</sup> which is about 14.66% of the total area of Nepal. The state extends between 27°20′N–29°20′N latitude and 82°52′E–85°12′E longitude. In terms of terrain, the province is spread over the Himalayan, Hilly and Terai region of Nepal; 5,919 km<sup>2</sup> (26.8%) of the area falls under the [Himalayan region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayan_Region), 14,604 km<sup>2</sup> (67.2%) of the area falls under the [Hilly region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_Region) and 1,310 km<sup>2</sup> (6%) of the area falls under the [Terai region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terai).[^cite_note-\:0-5]

## Demographics

As of the 2021 census, Gandaki Province has a population of 2,466,427. 6.85% of the population was under 5 years of age. Gandaki Province has a literacy rate of 81.65% and a sex ratio of 1107 females per 1000 males.[^cite_note-provincialreport-7]

Khas people are the largest ethnicity in the province, making up 53% of the population. Bahun are the largest group, making up 20% of the population. Of these Khas Dalits are 18% of the population. Hill Janjatis are the second-largest group in the province, making up 38% of the population. Of these Magars are the largest group, making up 19% of the population. Other large groups include the Gurung (11%) and Tamang (2%). Newars are 4% of the population, and Tharus are nearly 2% of the population.[^cite_note-caste-8]

Languages in Gandaki Province (2021)

[Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language) is the most common mother tongue in the province with 73.39% of the population speaking [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language) as their mother tongues. The second-largest language is [Magar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magar_language) (10%). 8.44% speak [Gurung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurung_language), 1.74% [Tharu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tharu_languages), 1.53% [Nepal Bhasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newar_language) and 1.36% [Tamang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamang_language).[^cite_note-mothertongue-9] Magar is mostly spoken in Nawalpur, Syangja and Tanahun districts, while Gurung and other Tibetic languages are spoken in Gorkha, Lamjung, Kaski, Manang and Mustang districts. A small population of Tharus live in Nawalpur district.

The [Language Commission of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_Commission_\(Nepal\)) has recommended [Magar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magar_language), [Gurung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurung_language) and [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language) as official language in the province. The commission has also recommended [Tharu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tharu_language), [Nepal Bhasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newar_language) and [Tamang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamang_language) to be additional official languages, for specific regions and purposes in the province.[^cite_note-langcomreport-10]

## Government and administration

Main articles: [Provincial governments of Nepal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_governments_of_Nepal), [Gandaki Provincial Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandaki_Provincial_Assembly), and [Cabinet of Gandaki Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Surendra_Raj_Pandey_cabinet)

### History and overview

The Provincial Assembly of Gandaki Province is formed under Article 175 of the [Constitution of Nepal 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Nepal) which guarantees a provincial legislative for each province in the country. The first provincial elections were conducted for all seven provinces in Nepal and the elections in Gandaki Province was conducted for 60 seats to the assembly. The first meeting of the provincial assembly was held on 4 February 2018. Netra Nath Adhikari from [Maoist Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPN_\(Maoist_Centre\)) was elected as the first speaker of the provincial assembly, and Srijana Sharma from [CPN (UML)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CPN_\(UML\)) as the first deputy speaker of the provincial assembly.

The Governor acts as the head of the province while the Chief Minister is the head of the provincial government. The Chief Judge of the Pokhara High Court is the head of the judiciary.[^cite_note-15] The present Governor, Chief Minister and Chief Judge are [Sita Kumari Poudel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sita_Kumari_Poudel) (governor), [Surendra Raj Pandey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surendra_Raj_Pandey) (chief minister) and Purushottam Bhandari.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] The province has 60 [provincial assembly constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pradesh_Sabha) and 18 federal [House of Representative constituencies](2707214.md).[^cite_note-18]

Gandaki Province has a [unicameral](204304.md) legislature, like that of the other provinces in Nepal. The tenure of the provincial assembly is of five years. The [Provincial Assembly of Gandaki Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_Gandaki_Province) is temporarily housed at the Urban Development Training Centre in [Pokhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhara).[^cite_note-19]

## Economy

The economy of the province is largely dependent on tourism.

## Infrastructure

### Roadways

9 out of 11 districts of Gandaki Province are connected via surfaced roads. Road connections to Mustang and Manang are unsurfaced and can be impassable during the rainy season. [Siddhartha Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddhartha_Highway) connects Lumbini to Gandaki. The highway starts at the Nepal-India Border in Siddharthanagar and terminates at Prithivi chowk, Pokhara. The major settlements on the highway are Siddharthanagar, Butwal, Tansen, Waling, Putalibazar, Syangja, and Pokhara.
