The **Israeli–Lebanese conflict**, or the **South Lebanon conflict**,[^cite_note-Rongxing-1] is a long-running conflict involving [Israel](9282173.md), [Lebanon](17771.md)-based paramilitary groups, and sometimes [Syria](7515849.md). The conflict peaked during the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War). In response to [Palestinian attacks from Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_insurgency_in_South_Lebanon), Israel invaded the country [in 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_South_Lebanon_conflict) and again [in 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War). After this it [occupied southern Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation_of_Southern_Lebanon_\(1982%E2%80%932000\)) until 2000, while fighting [a guerrilla conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_conflict_\(1985%E2%80%932000\)) against Shia paramilitaries. After Israel's withdrawal, [Hezbollah](13919.md) attacks sparked the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War). A [new period of conflict](75005425.md) began in 2023, leading to the [2024 Israeli invasion of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Israeli_invasion_of_Lebanon).

The [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO) recruited militants in Lebanon from among the [Palestinian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_refugee) who had been expelled or fled after the creation of Israel in 1948.[^cite_note-lonelyplanet-8][^cite_note-meria.idc.ac.il-9] After the PLO leadership and its [Fatah](11104.md) brigade were [expelled from Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_in_Jordan) in 1970–71 for fomenting a revolt against the king, they entered southern Lebanon, resulting in an increase of internal and cross-border violence. Meanwhile, [demographic tensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Lebanon) over the Lebanese [National Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Pact) led to the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) (1975–1990).[^cite_note-Mor_2002_192-10] PLO actions were one of the key factors in the eruption of the Lebanese Civil War and its bitter battles with Lebanese factions caused foreign intervention. Israel's [1978 invasion of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_South_Lebanon_conflict) pushed the PLO north of the [Litani River](1920925.md), but the PLO continued their campaign against Israel. This invasion led to the deployment of [United Nations peacekeepers](787766.md) in southern Lebanon. [Israel invaded Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War) again in 1982 and, in alliance with the Christian [Lebanese Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Forces_\(militia\)), forcibly expelled the PLO. In 1983, Israel and Lebanon signed the [May 17 Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_17_Agreement) providing a framework for the establishment of normal bilateral relations between the two countries, but relations were disrupted with [takeover of Shia and Druze militias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_6_Intifada) in early 1984. Israel withdrew from most of Lebanon in 1985, but kept control of a 19-kilometre (12-mile)[^cite_note-israel.lebanon.timeline-11] [security buffer zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation_of_Southern_Lebanon_\(1982%E2%80%932000\)), held with the aid of proxy militants in the [South Lebanon Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_Army) (SLA).

In 1985, [Hezbollah](13919.md), a Lebanese [Shia Islamist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islamist) movement sponsored by [Iran](14653.md),[^cite_note-bbc-hi-me-1908671-12] called for armed struggle to end the Israeli occupation of Lebanese territory.[^cite_note-Hezbollah-13] It [fought a guerrilla war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_conflict_\(1985%E2%80%932000\)) against the IDF and SLA in south Lebanon. Israel launched two major operations in southern Lebanon during the 1990s: [Operation Accountability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Accountability) in 1993 and [Operation Grapes of Wrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Grapes_of_Wrath) in 1996. Fighting with Hezbollah weakened Israeli resolve and led to a collapse of the SLA and an Israeli withdrawal in 2000 to their side of the [UN designated border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_\(Lebanon\)).[^cite_note-news.bbc.co.uk-14]

Citing Israeli control of the [Shebaa farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shebaa_farms), Hezbollah continued cross-border attacks intermittently over the next six years. Hezbollah now sought the release of [Lebanese citizens in Israeli prisons](6403729.md) and successfully used the tactic of capturing Israeli soldiers as leverage for a [prisoner exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_MIA_prisoner_exchanges) in 2004.[^cite_note-Factfile:_Hezbollah-15][^cite_note-Israel,_Hezbollah_swap_prisoners-16] The capturing of two Israeli soldiers by Hezbollah ignited the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War), which saw cross-border attacks and another Israeli invasion of the south.[^cite_note-UNFIL-17] Its [ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1701) called for the disarmament of Hezbollah and the respecting of the territorial integrity and sovereignty of Lebanon by Israel. Hostilities were suspended on 8 September.

After the 2006 war the situation became relatively calm, despite both sides violating the ceasefire agreements; Israel by making near-daily flights over Lebanese territory, and Hezbollah by not disarming. There was an increase in violence during the [April 2023 Israel–Lebanon shellings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Israel%E2%80%93Lebanon_shellings).

The [Gaza war](74998519.md) sparked a [renewed Israel–Hezbollah conflict](75005425.md), beginning one day after the [October 7 Hamas-led attack on Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Hamas-led_attack_on_Israel). The conflict initially consisted of tit-for-tat airstrikes and shelling.[^cite_note-18] The conflict escalated in September 2024, beginning with the Israeli [explosion of Lebanese pagers and walkie talkies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Lebanon_pager_explosions). Israel then began an [aerial bombing campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_2024_Lebanon_strikes) throughout Lebanon, killing at least 569 people on 23 September; the largest conflict-related loss of life in a single day in Lebanon since the Civil War.[^cite_note-19]

## Background

Main article: [Israel–Lebanon relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93Lebanon_relations)

See also: [Partitioning of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitioning_of_the_Ottoman_Empire), [Mandatory Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), and [French Mandate of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Mandate_of_Syria)



Zones of French and British influence and control imagined by the 1916 [Sykes–Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes%E2%80%93Picot_Agreement).

The territories of what would become the states of Israel and Lebanon were once part of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) which lasted from 1299 until its defeat in [World War I](4764461.md) and subsequent [dissolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Ottoman_Empire) in 1922. As a result of the [Sinai and Palestine Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_and_Palestine_Campaign) in 1917, the British occupied [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)) and parts of what would become [Syria](7515849.md). French troops took [Damascus](8914.md) in 1918. The [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) officially gave the French the [Mandate of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Mandate_of_Syria) and the British the [Mandate of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine) after the 1920 [San Remo conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Remo_conference), in accordance with the 1916 [Sykes–Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes%E2%80%93Picot_Agreement).

The largely [Christian](18337522.md) enclave of the French Mandate became the [French-controlled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon) [Lebanese Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Lebanon) in 1926. Lebanon became independent in 1943 as France was under German occupation, though French troops did not completely withdraw until 1946.

The rise of [antisemitism](1078.md) in [Europe](9239.md), culminating in the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust) during [World War II](32927.md), had meant an increase of [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jew) immigrants to a minority Jewish, majority [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab) Mandate.[^cite_note-20] During the [1936–1939 Arab revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%931939_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine) and thereafter the British increasingly came to rely on [Jewish police forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notrim) to help maintain order.[^cite_note-21] Eventually, the resultant rise in ethnic tensions and violence between the Arabs and Jews due to Jewish immigration and [collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration) would force the British to withdraw in 1947. (The area of their [mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate) east of the [Jordan river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_river) had already become the independent state of [Jordan](7515964.md) in 1946.) The [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) developed a [gerrymandered](12987.md) [1947 UN Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_UN_Partition_Plan),[^cite_note-22] to attempt to give both Arabs and Jews their own states from the remains of the British Mandate; however, this was rejected by the Arabs, and the situation quickly devolved into a full-fledged civil war.

## History

### 1948 Arab–Israeli War

Main articles: [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War) and [1948 Palestinian expulsion and flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_expulsion_and_flight)

In 1948, the Lebanese army had by far the smallest regional army, consisting of only 3,500 soldiers.[^cite_note-23] At the prompting of Arab leaders in the region, Lebanon agreed to join the other armies that were being assembled around the perimeter of the British Mandate territory of Palestine for the purpose of invading Palestine. Lebanon committed 1,000 of these soldiers to the cause. The Arab armies waited for the end of the Mandate and the withdrawal of British forces, which was set for 15 May 1948.

Israel [declared its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_the_Establishment_of_the_State_of_Israel) on 14 May 1948. The next day, the British Mandate officially expired and, in [an official cablegram](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Cablegram%20from%20the%20Secretary-General%20of%20the%20League%20of%20Arab%20States%20to%20the%20Secretary-General%20of%20the%20United%20Nations), the seven-member [Arab League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Arab_States), including Lebanon, publicly proclaimed their aim of creating a democratic "United State of Palestine" in place of the [United Nations Partition Plan for Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan_for_Palestine). The League soon entered the conflict on the side of the Palestinian Arabs, thus beginning the international phase of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War). [Egypt](8087628.md), [Lebanon](17771.md), [Syria](7515849.md), [Transjordan](7515964.md), and [Iraq](7515928.md) declared war on the new state of Israel. They expected an easy and quick victory in what came to be called the 1948 Arab–Israeli War. The Lebanese army joined the other Arab armies in the invasion. It crossed into the northern [Galilee](12639.md). By the end of the conflict, however, it had been repulsed by Israeli forces, which occupied [South Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon). Israel signed [armistice agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements) with each of its invading neighbors. The armistice with Lebanon was signed on 23 March 1949.[^cite_note-24] As part of the agreement with Lebanon, Israeli forces withdrew to the international border.

By the conclusion of that war, Israel had signed [ceasefire agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements) with all of the neighbouring Arab countries.[^cite_note-Encarta:_Israel-25] The territory it now controlled went well beyond what had been allocated to it under the [United Nations Partition Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Partition_Plan), incorporating much of what had been promised to the Palestinian Arabs under the Plan. However, it was understood by all the state parties at the time that the armistice agreements were not peace treaties with Israel, nor the final resolution of the conflict between them, including the borders.

After the war, the United Nations estimated 711,000[^cite_note-26] [Palestinian Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Arab), out an estimated 1.8 million dwelling in the [Mandate of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine),[^cite_note-27] [fled, emigrated or were forced out of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestinian_exodus) and entered neighboring countries. By 1949, there were 110,000 Palestinian Arabs in Lebanon,[^cite_note-28] moved into camps established by and administered by the [United Nations Relief and Works Agency for Palestine Refugees in the Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Relief_and_Works_Agency_for_Palestine_Refugees_in_the_Near_East).[^cite_note-LeMonde-29]

With the exception of two camps in the [Beirut](37428.md) area, the camps were mostly [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim).[^cite_note-LeMonde-29] [Lebanese Christians](66391078.md) feared that the Muslim influx would affect their political dominance and their [assumed demographic majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Lebanon).[^cite_note-LeMonde-29] Accordingly, they imposed restrictions on the status of the Palestinian refugees. The refugees could not work, travel, or engage in political activities. Initially the refugees were too impoverished to develop a leadership capable of representing their concerns.[^cite_note-LeMonde-29] Less democratic regimes also feared the threat the refugees posed to their own rule, but Lebanon would prove too weak to maintain a crackdown.[^cite_note-lonelyplanet-8]

The [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO) recruited militants in Lebanon from among the families of [Palestinian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_refugee) who had left Israel in 1948.[^cite_note-lonelyplanet-8][^cite_note-meria.idc.ac.il-9]

### War over water and the Six-Day War (1964–1967)

Main articles: [War over Water (Jordan river)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_over_Water_\(Jordan_river\)) and [Six-Day War](29328.md)

Despite sharing in the ongoing border tensions over water,[^cite_note-30] Lebanon rejected calls by other Arab governments to participate in the 1967 [Six-Day War](29328.md).[^cite_note-winslow-31] Militarily weak in the south, Lebanon could not afford conflict with Israel.[^cite_note-winslow-31]

Nevertheless, the loss of additional territory radicalized the Palestinians languishing in refugee camps hoping to return home.[^cite_note-lonelyplanet-8] The additional influx of refugees turned Palestinian camps throughout the Middle East into centers of [guerrilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guerrilla) activity.[^cite_note-lonelyplanet-8]

### Rise of the PLO militants (1968–1975)

Main articles: [Black September in Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_in_Jordan) and [Palestinian insurgency in South Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_insurgency_in_South_Lebanon)

The PLO, from its inception in 1964 by [Ahmed Shukeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Shukeri), began executing numerous terror attacks on Israeli civilians in attempt to fulfill its mission charter's vow to pursue in "the path of holy war (al-[jihad](16203.md))" until the establishment of a [Palestinian State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_State) in place of the State of Israel. The series of attacks (such as the 1966 bombings in [Romema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romema), Jerusalem) drove the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF) to strike in return, instigating the long and still unresolved struggle between the PLO and the IDF.

From 1968 onwards, the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) (PLO) began conducting [raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_insurgency_in_South_Lebanon) from Lebanon into Israel, and Israel began making retaliatory raids against Lebanese villages to encourage the Lebanese people to themselves deal with the fedayeen.[^cite_note-fisk_p74-32] After an Israeli airline [was machine-gunned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Al_Flight_253_attack) at [Athens Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athens_International_Airport), Israel [raided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Israeli_raid_on_Lebanon) the [Beirut International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beirut_International_Airport) in retaliation, destroying 13 civilian aircraft.[^cite_note-lonelyplanet-8]

The unarmed citizenry could not expel the armed foreigners, while the Lebanese army was too weak militarily and politically.[^cite_note-fisk_p74-32] The Palestinian camps came under Palestinian control after a series of clashes in 1968 and 1969 between the Lebanese military and the emerging Palestinian guerrilla forces.[^cite_note-LeMonde-29] In 1969 the [Cairo Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo_Agreement_\(1969\)) guaranteed refugees the right to work, to form self-governing committees, and to engage in armed struggle.[^cite_note-LeMonde-29] "The Palestinian resistance movement assumed daily management of the refugee camps, providing security as well as a wide variety of health, educational, and social services."[^cite_note-LeMonde-29]

On 8 May 1970, a PLO faction, called the [Democratic Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine), (DFLP) crossed into Israel and carried out the [Avivim school bus massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avivim_school_bus_massacre).

In 1970, the PLO attempted to overthrow a reigning monarch, King [Hussein of Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_of_Jordan), and following his quashing of the rebellion in what Arab historians call [Black September](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_September_in_Jordan), the PLO leadership and their troops fled from [Jordan](7515964.md)[^cite_note-33] to [Syria](7515849.md) and finally Lebanon, where cross-border violence increased.

With headquarters now in [Beirut](37428.md), PLO factions recruited new members from the Palestinian refugee camps.[^cite_note-meria.idc.ac.il-9] South Lebanon was nicknamed "Fatahland" due to the predominance there of [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat)'s [Fatah](11104.md) organization. With its own army operating freely in Lebanon, the PLO had created a state within a state.[^cite_note-34] By 1975, more than 300,000 Palestinian [displaced persons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displaced_persons) lived in Lebanon.[^cite_note-35]

In reaction to the 1972 [Munich massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_massacre), Israel carried out [Operation Spring of Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Spring_of_Youth). Members of [Israel's elite Special Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayeret_Matkal) landed by boat in Lebanon on 9 April 1973, and with the aid of [Israeli intelligence](27619007.md) agents, infiltrated the PLO headquarters in Beirut and assassinated several members of its leadership.

In 1974 the PLO altered its focus to include political elements, necessary for a dialogue with Israel. Those who insisted on a military solution left to form the [Rejectionist Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rejectionist_Front), and [Yassir Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yassir_Arafat) took over the PLO leadership role.[^cite_note-36]

The [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine – General Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine_%E2%80%93_General_Command), which split from the PLO in 1974, carried out the [Kiryat Shmona massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiryat_Shmona_massacre) in April of that year. In May 1974, the DFLP crossed again into Israel and carried out the [Ma'alot massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'alot_massacre). In retaliation for this attack, Israel bombed and destroyed the [Nabatieh refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabatieh_refugee_camp).

### Lebanese Civil War (1975–1990)

Main article: [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War)

The [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) (1975–1990) was a complex conflict in the form of various factions and shifting alliances between and among Lebanese [Maronite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronite) Catholics, Lebanese Muslims, Palestinian Muslims, Lebanese Druze, and other non-sectarian groups. Governmental power had been allotted among the different religious groups by the [National Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Pact) based partially on the results of the [1932 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Lebanon). [Changes in demographics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Lebanon) and increased feelings of deprivation by certain ethnic groups, as well as Israeli–Palestinian clashes in the south of the county all contributed to the outbreak of the Lebanese Civil War.[^cite_note-Mor_2002_192-10]

#### Israeli support to Lebanese Forces

Beginning in May 1976, Israel supplied the Maronite militias, including the [Lebanese Forces](2113218.md), led by [Bachir Gemayel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachir_Gemayel), with arms, tanks, and military advisers.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-LexicOrient-38] The border between Israel and Lebanon was at this time was nicknamed the [Good Fence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Fence).

Fearing loss of commercial access to the port of [Beirut](37428.md), in June 1976 [Syria](7515849.md) intervened in the civil war to support the Maronite dominated government,[^cite_note-39] and by October had 40,000 troops stationed within Lebanon.

#### First Israeli invasion of Lebanon

Main articles: [Operation Litani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Litani) and [South Lebanon Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_Army)

On 11 March 1978, eleven PLO militants made a beach landing 30 km. south of [Haifa, Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa,_Israel), where they seized a bus,[^cite_note-FRD-40] full of people, killing those on board in what is known as the [Coastal Road massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Road_massacre). By the end of the incident, nine hijackers[^cite_note-41] and 38 Israeli civilians (including 13 children) were dead.[^cite_note-FRD-40]

In response, on 14 March 1978, Israel launched [Operation Litani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Litani) occupying southern Lebanon, except for the city of [Tyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyre_\(Lebanon\)),[^cite_note-42] with 25,000 troops. The objective was to push the PLO away from the border and bolster a Lebanese Christian militia allied with Israel, the [South Lebanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanese_Army) (SLA).[^cite_note-FRD-40] However, the PLO concluded from the name of the operation that the invasion would halt at the [Litani River](1920925.md) and moved their forces north, leaving behind a token force of a few hundred men.[^cite_note-Fisk_p126-43] As a result, the casualties were almost all civilians.[^cite_note-Fisk_p126-43]

On 19 March 1978, the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) passed [Resolution 425](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_425), which called for Israel's immediate withdrawal and the establishment of a [United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon](787766.md).[^cite_note-44] When Israel forces withdrew later in 1978, they turned over its positions in Lebanon to the South Lebanon Army which would continue fighting as a proxy for Israel against the PLO until Israel drove the PLO out of Lebanon in 1982.

On 22 April 1979, [Samir Kuntar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samir_Kuntar) and three other members of the [Palestinian Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Liberation_Front), a faction of the PLO, landed in [Nahariya](323332.md), Israel from [Tyre, Lebanon](30796.md) by boat. After killing a police officer who had discovered their presence, they took a father and his daughter hostage in an apartment building. After fleeing with the hostages from police back to the beach, a shootout killed one policeman and two of the militants. Kuntar then executed the hostages before he and the remaining invader were captured.

In April 1981, the United States brokered a cease-fire in southern Lebanon among Israel, Syria and the PLO.

#### Second Israeli invasion of Lebanon

See also: [1982 Lebanon war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_war)



Israeli troops in the Lebanese port city of [Sidon](28366.md), August 1982

The [1982 Lebanon war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_war) began on 6 June 1982,[^cite_note-45] when Israel invaded again for the purpose of attacking the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization). The Israeli army [laid siege to Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Beirut). During the conflict, according to Lebanese sources, between 15,000 and 20,000 people were killed, mostly civilians.[^cite_note-Fisk-6][^cite_note-Race-7] According to American military analyst Richard Gabriel, between 5,000 and 8,000 civilians were killed.[^cite_note-Gabriel-5] Fighting also occurred between Israel and [Syria](7515849.md). The United States, fearing a widening conflict and the prestige the siege was giving PLO leader [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat), got all sides to agree to a cease-fire and terms for the PLO's withdrawal on 12 August. The [Multinational Force in Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multinational_Force_in_Lebanon) arrived to keep the peace and ensure PLO withdrawal. The PLO leadership retreated from Beirut on 30 August 1982 and moved to [Tunisia](30188.md).

The [National Assembly of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Lebanon) narrowly chose [Bachir Gemayel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachir_Gemayel) as [president-elect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President-elect), but when he was assassinated on 14 September 1982, Israel reoccupied West Beirut. In parallel, Maronite militia [Kataeb Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kataeb_Party) carried out the [Sabra and Shatila massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabra_and_Shatila_massacre).

### 1983 Israeli-Lebanese accords and their collapse



Map showing power balance in Lebanon, 1983: Green – controlled by [Syria](7515849.md), purple – controlled by Christian groups, yellow – controlled by [Israel](9282173.md), blue – controlled by the UN

In 1983, the United States brokered the [May 17 Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_17_Agreement), a peace treaty between Israel and Lebanon in all but name. The agreement called for a staged Israeli withdrawal over the next eight to twelve weeks and the establishment of a "security zone" to be patrolled by the Lebanese army in southern Lebanon,[^cite_note-46] but was conditional on Syrian withdrawal as well. In August 1983, as Israel withdrew from the areas southeast of Beirut to the [Awali River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awali_River),[^cite_note-47] Lebanese factions clashed for control of the freed territory.[^cite_note-USSTATE-48]

In February 1984, the Lebanese Army collapsed, with many units forming their own militias. [Shia and Druze militias took over much of Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_6_Intifada) in early 1984 and consolidated power. The National Assembly of Lebanon, under pressure from Syria and Muslim militias, cancelled the 17 May Agreement on 5 March 1984.[^cite_note-USSTATE-48]

On 15 January 1985, Israel adopted a phased withdrawal plan, finally retreating to the [Litani River](1920925.md) to form the 4–12 kilometers (2.5–7.5 miles) deep[^cite_note-LexicOrient-38] [Israeli Security Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupation_of_Southern_Lebanon_\(1982%E2%80%932000\)) (map at[^cite_note-49]) while using the native [South Lebanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanese_Army) militia to help control it.

### South Lebanon conflict (February 1985 – May 2000)

Main article: [South Lebanon conflict (1985–2000)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_conflict_\(1985%E2%80%932000\))

#### Consolidation of Hezbollah



Map of southern Lebanon, featuring the [Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_\(Lebanon\)), [UNIFIL](787766.md) zone, and [Litani River](1920925.md) (2006).

See also: [Hezbollah](13919.md), [Operation Accountability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Accountability), [Operation Grapes of Wrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Grapes_of_Wrath), and [Hezbollah–Israel conflict](62111540.md)

On 16 February 1985, [Shia](26961.md) Sheik Ibrahim al-Amin declared a [manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifesto)[^cite_note-Hezbollah-13] in Lebanon, announcing a [resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_movement) called [Hezbollah](13919.md), whose goals included combating the Israeli occupation. During the [South Lebanon conflict (1985–2000)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_conflict_\(1985%E2%80%932000\)) the Hezbollah militia waged a guerrilla campaign against Israeli forces occupying Southern Lebanon and their [South Lebanon Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_Army) proxies. "Throughout the period of 1985–92, there were very few limited exchanges between Israeli and Hezbollah or Amal forces in southern Lebanon", and "with the exception of 1988, during which twenty-one Israeli soldiers were killed, the number of Israeli fatalities per year over this period was in the single-digit figure".[^cite_note-50]

By the end of 1990, the [Lebanese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Civil_War) was effectively over. In March 1991, the [National Assembly of Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Lebanon) passed an amnesty law that pardoned all political crimes prior to its enactment, and in May 1991, the militias—with the important exceptions of Hezbollah and the SLA—were dissolved, and the [Lebanese Armed Forces](17779.md) began to slowly rebuild themselves as Lebanon's only major non-sectarian institution.

#### Security belt conflict

From 1985 through 2000, Israel continued to fund the South Lebanon Army. In 1992, Hezbollah won ten out of 128 seats in the Lebanese [National Assembly](1618242.md).

On 25 July 1993, Israel launched [Operation Accountability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Accountability), known in Lebanon as the Seven-Day War. The given reason was to retaliate for the death of IDF soldiers in the ["security zone"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_security_belt), which Israel had created in 1985 in southern Lebanon to protect its northern borders from both Hezbollah and the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine – General Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine_%E2%80%93_General_Command). On 10 July Hezbollah undertook an operation in which 5 Israeli soldiers were killed; a further attack on 19 July caused several further casualties to the IDF, and on the 23rd. another Israeli soldier was killed. Cross-border raids were frequent from both sides, and Operation Accountability arose from the escalation in hostilities.[^cite_note-Brecher-51] Thousands of buildings were bombed, resulting in 120 dead and 500,000 displaced civilians. Israeli forces also destroyed infrastructure such as power stations and bridges. According to Michael Brecher, the aim of Operation Accountability was to precipitate a large flight of Lebanese refugees from the south towards Beirut and thereby put the Lebanese government under pressure to rein in Hezbollah.[^cite_note-Brecher-51] Hezbollah retaliated with rocket attacks on Israeli villages, though inflicting significantly fewer casualties. After Lebanon complained to the UN, the [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council) called on Israel to withdraw its occupying forces from Lebanese territory. A truce agreement brokered by the US secured an Israeli undertaking to stop attacks north of its security zone in Lebanon, and a Hezbollah agreement to desist from firing rockets into Israel.[^cite_note-Brecher-51]

On 11 April 1996, Israel initiated [Operation Grapes of Wrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Grapes_of_Wrath), known in Lebanon as the April War, which repeated the pattern of Operation Accountability.,[^cite_note-Brecher-51] which was triggered by Hezbollah Katyusha rockets fired into Israel in response to the killing of two Lebanese by an IDF missile, and the killing of Lebanese boy by a road-side bomb. Israel conducted massive air raids and extensive shelling in southern Lebanon. 106 Lebanese died in the [shelling of Qana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_shelling_of_Qana), when a UN compound was hit in an Israeli shelling. The conflict ended on 26 April 1996 with the [Israeli-Lebanese Ceasefire Understanding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli-Lebanese_Ceasefire_Understanding)[^cite_note-52] in which both Hezbollah and Israel agreed to, respect the "rules of the game" and forgo attacks on civilians.[^cite_note-Brecher-51]

In January 2000, Hezbollah assassinated the man responsible for day to day SLA operations, Colonel Akel Hashem.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] The Israeli Air Force, in apparent response, on 7 February struck Lebanon's civilian infrastructure, including power stations at [Baalbek](58207.md), Deir Nbouh and Jambour. Eighteen people were reported to have been injured.[^cite_note-55]

Following its declaration of intent to implement [UNSC Resolution 425](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_425) on 1 April 1998, and after the collapse of the South Lebanon Army in the face of a Hezbollah onslaught, Israel declared 24 May 2000 that they would withdraw to their side of the UN designated border,[^cite_note-news.bbc.co.uk-14] the [Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_\(Lebanon\)), 22 years after the resolution had been approved. The South Lebanon Army's equipment and positions largely fell into the hands of Hezbollah. Lebanon celebrates 25 May, [Liberation Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_Day_\(Lebanon\)), as a national holiday.

### 2006 Israel–Hezbollah War

Main article: [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War)



Destroyed buildings in [Beirut](37428.md) in 2006

On 12 July 2006, in an incident known as [Zar'it-Shtula incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zar'it-Shtula_incident), the Hezbollah initiated diversionary rocket attacks on Israeli military positions near the coast and near the Israeli border village of [Zar'it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zar'it),[^cite_note-UNFIL-17] while another Hezbollah group crossed from Lebanon into Israel and ambushed two Israeli Army vehicles, killing three Israeli soldiers and seizing two.[^cite_note-HRTZ_Harel-66][^cite_note-wsh-67]

Hezbollah promptly demanded the release of Lebanese prisoners held by Israel, including [Samir Kuntar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samir_Kuntar) and an [alleged surviving perpetrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Skaf) of the [Coastal Road massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Road_massacre), in exchange for the release of the captured soldiers.[^cite_note-nasrallah-68]

Heavy fire between the sides was exchanged across the length of the [Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_\(Lebanon\)), with Hezbollah targeting IDF positions near Israeli towns.[^cite_note-UNFIL-17]

Thus began the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War). Israel responded with massive airstrikes and artillery fire on targets throughout Lebanon, an air and naval blockade, and a ground invasion of southern Lebanon. In Lebanon the conflict killed over 1,100 people, including combatants,[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-UNHRC-73][^cite_note-LUS-74] severely damaged infrastructure, and displaced about one million people. Israel suffered 42 civilian deaths as a result of prolonged rocket attacks being launched into northern Israel causing the displacement of half a million Israelis.[^cite_note-75] Normal life across much of Lebanon and northern Israel was disrupted, in addition to the deaths in combat.

A [United Nations](31769.md)-brokered ceasefire went into effect on 14 August 2006. The blockade was lifted on 8 September.[^cite_note-76]

On 8 October 2023, Hezbollah launched guided rockets and artillery shells at Israeli-occupied positions in Shebaa Farms during the 2023 [Gaza war](74998519.md). Israel retaliated with drone strikes and artillery fire on Hezbollah positions near the Golan Heights–Lebanon border, since then a [conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Israel%E2%80%93Lebanon_border_clashes) has broken out between [militants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militants),[^cite_note-131] and [Israel](9282173.md) on the [border](987817.md).[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-auto3-134]

On 2 January, Israel conducted an airstrike in the [Dahieh](6082788.md) neighborhood of [Beirut](37428.md), resulting in [the assassination of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Saleh_al_Arouri) [Saleh al-Arouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saleh_al-Arouri), the deputy chairman of the [Hamas](13913.md) political bureau.[^cite_note-2jan-135] on 30 September 2024 Israel launched an [invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Israeli_invasion_of_Lebanon) of Lebanon against Hezbollah.[^cite_note-136]

In April 2025, Hezbollah signaled willingness to discuss disarmament with President Joseph Aoun, on the condition that Israel withdraws from five southern hilltop positions and halts its strikes. Aoun, under rising pressure, seeks to bring all weapons under state control. The group, weakened by the 2024 conflict, insists Israel must act first before any transfer of arms.[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138]

On 5 July 2025, Israel carried out four drone attacks on cities in southern Lebanon resulting in one death and several injuries.[^cite_note-\:0-139] Lebanon's [Ministry of Public Health](77943319.md) said that an “Israeli enemy drone attack on a vehicle” in the Saf al-Hawa area in the city of [Bint Jbeil](6124393.md) “killed one person and wounded two others.”; a second attack on the same area was conducted following this.[^cite_note-\:0-139] The Ministry also reported that another Israeli drone attack wounded one person in [Shebaa](42907053.md) earlier that morning.[^cite_note-\:0-139] Another drone attack was made on the town of [Chaqra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaqra-Doubay), in the [Bint Jbeil](6124393.md) District, wounding two people.[^cite_note-\:0-139]

On 30 October, Israeli troops stormed a municipal hall in [Blida](42906975.md), [Marjayoun District](8968039.md), in southern Lebanon and killed an employee who was asleep. In response, Lebanese president [Joseph Aoun](53467128.md) ordered the [Armed Forces](17779.md) to repel any Israeli incursions, marking a possible new front to the conflict.[^cite_note-140]

## Issues during the conflict

### Israeli incursions into Lebanon

Since the civil war, Israel has routinely breached Lebanese airspace, waters, and borders, which is illegal since it violates Lebanon's territory and [United Nations Security Council Resolution 425](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_425) and [1701](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1701).[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143]

The most frequent breaches are overflights by Israeli war planes and drones; such violations have occurred since the inception of the Israeli–Lebanese conflict, and have happened continuously and almost daily since the [2006 Lebanon war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_war), being the source of much conflict between Lebanon and Israel.[^cite_note-144] Reporting estimates over 22,000 Israeli incursions into Lebanese airspace have occurred since 2007.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] Israeli warplanes sometimes stage mock attacks on Lebanese cities, and emit [sonic booms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonic_booms) that frighten civilians.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149]

In 2007 the Lebanese government complained that Israeli planes had flown into Lebanese airspace 290 times within four months, and that Israeli troops had crossed the border 52 times.[^cite_note-150]

In 2006 French Defense Minister [Michele Alliot-Marie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michele_Alliot-Marie) stated: "I remind that the violations of the airspace are extremely dangerous, they are dangerous first because they may be felt as hostile by forces of the coalition that could be brought to retaliate in cases of self defense and it would be a very serious incident."[^cite_note-151] US officials on visit in Israel also demanded that Israel stop the overflights since they undermined the standing of Lebanese Prime Minister [Fouad Siniora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fouad_Siniora).[^cite_note-152]

On 19 August 2010, the Lebanese military reported that 12 aircraft belonging to the IDF entered into Lebanese territory, which they claim is a violation of [Resolution 1701](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolution_1701). In the three incidents, the IDF planes made circle maneuvers, fired no shots and left Lebanese airspace soon after.[^cite_note-153]

The [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN) has continuously protested the repeated Israeli overflights.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] Lebanese officials fear the escalation in overflights heighten tensions and could lead to war.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157]

Israel rejects such criticism, and claim the overflights are necessary.[^cite_note-158][^cite_note-159] In spite of this, a leaked US cable shows that Israel offered to stop such violations.[^cite_note-160]

On land, the [Blue Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_\(Lebanon\)) is often crossed,[^cite_note-161] as well as incursions into the [Shebaa Farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shebaa_Farms) (which Israel considers Israeli territory as part of [Golan Heights](7515865.md), which it captured from Syria in 1967, but which Lebanon claims is Lebanese territory).[^cite_note-162] The [2010 Israel–Lebanon border clash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Israel%E2%80%93Lebanon_border_clash) was also performed on the basis of claims of such violations.[^cite_note-163]

At sea, Israeli gunboats have shot into Lebanese territorial waters, and there have been Lebanese claims that Israel is breaching the [law of the sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_the_sea) and might lay claim on Lebanese natural resources through the [Tamar gas field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamar_gas_field).[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167]

Hezbollah has cited such violations as justification for the legitimacy of its continued armed resistance against Israel.[^cite_note-168]
