# Subdivisions of Kyiv

&#x20;**Subdivisions of [Kyiv](585629.md)**, the capital city of [Ukraine](31750.md), include formal administrative subdivisions known as [urban districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_districts_of_Ukraine) (raions) and also more specific, informal subdivisions referred to as historical neighborhoods. The city is divided in half by the river [Dnipro](380138.md), and therefore creates two important portions of the capital city. The so-called "Left-bank of Kyiv", as in reference to the river [Dnipro](380138.md), and which is the newer half of the city, plus the "Right-bank of Kyiv", which includes the original or historic [City of Kyiv](585629.md).

## History of subdivision

The first known formal subdivisions of [Kyiv](585629.md) date back to the year 1810, when the city was subdivided into four sections: [Pechersk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pechersk,_Kyiv), Starokyiv ([Old Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kyiv)), and the first and second sections of [Podil](3535288.md). In 1833–1834 according to [Tsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsar#Russia) [Nicholas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_I_of_Russia)'s decree, Kyiv was subdivided into six police districts; later being increased to ten. As of 1917, there were eight district councils (called a Duma or Dumas), and which were later reorganized by [Pavlo Skoropadskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Skoropadskyi) into 17 raions. In 1924, the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) reorganized them into six larger, party-administrated districts, with various sub-districts under the administration of [Hryhoriy Hrynko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hryhoriy_Hrynko). Districts of the city that start with the letter "D" are located on the left bank of the river [Dnipro](380138.md), and until 1927 were part of the [Chernigov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernigov_Governorate) with [Darnytsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darnytsia) being the first to be incorporated into the city limits in that same year.

During the Soviet era, as the city was expanding, the number of districts had also gradually increased. Districts were commonly named for Soviet party leaders, and as political situations changed and some of the leaders were overturned by the other, district names therefore also changed.

The last district reform took place in 2001, when the number of districts was decreased from 14 to 10.

Under [Oleksandr Omelchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksandr_Omelchenko) ([mayor](7187856.md) from 1999 to 2006), there were further plans for the merger of some districts and revision of their boundaries, and the total number of districts had been planned to be decreased from 10 to 7. With the election of the new mayor-elect ([Leonid Chernovetskyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Chernovetskyi)) in 2006, these plans were conducted.

## Informal subdivision

### The Right Bank and the Left Bank

The natural first level of subdivision of the city is into the Right Bank and the Left Bank of the [Dnieper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnieper_River) (a few large islands belong to the left-bank *raions*).

The Right Bank ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Правий Берег, Pravyi Bereh), located on the western side of the river, contains the older portions of the city, as well as the majority of Kyiv's business and governmental institutions.

The eastern Left Bank ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Лівий Берег, Livyi Bereh), incorporated into the city only in the twentieth century, is predominantly [residential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Residential_area). There are large industrial and green areas in both the Right Bank and the Left Bank.

The terms "Right Bank" and, especially, "Left Bank" are recognized in the names of Kyiv's infrastructure, e.g. [Livoberezhna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livoberezhna_\(Kyiv_Metro\)) metro station.

### Historical neighborhoods

Residents widely recognize a system of the non-formal historical neighborhoods. Such neighborhoods count in dozens, however, constituting a kind of [hierarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchy), since most of them have lost their distinctive [topographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography) limits.

The names of the oldest neighborhoods go back to the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), and sometimes pose a great [linguistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistics) interest. The newest whole-built developments bear numeric designations or residential [marketing](59252.md) names.

Lypky and Zvirynets of the [Pecherskyi Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecherskyi_Raion) are the most expensive areas to live. Koncha-Zaspa is arguably the most interesting neighborhood name dating back to the times of [Kyivan Rus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyivan_Rus). A local legend explaining the name of a locality states the Rus warriors who felt asleep (*zaspaly*) during their watch at the [outpost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outpost_\(military\)) were killed (*koncheni*) by [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde) invaders. Koncha-Zaspa is now a prestigious area too.

## Practical orientation patterns

Another useful pattern of city division is the [Kyiv Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Metro) system. However, metro lines do not cover significant parts of Kyiv, making such orientation very approximate (but easy for newcomers). Sometimes, the system of [*elektrychka* train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elektrichka) stops are used for the same purpose.

Names of well-known shopping malls, restaurants, night clubs are used for orientation purposes as well.

The full informal set of addresses in Kyiv (used, for example, in [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate) advertising) would include:

- Formal district

- Historical neighborhood

- Nearest metro station or train stop

- Formal address
