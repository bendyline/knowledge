The **federal subjects of Russia**, also referred to as the **subjects of the Russian Federation** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): субъекты Российской Федерации, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *subyekty Rossiyskoy Federatsii*) or simply as the **subjects of the federation** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): субъекты федерации, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *subyekty federatsii*), are the [constituent entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division) of [Russia](25391.md), its top-level political divisions.[^cite_note-gov_constitution-1] According to the [Constitution of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Russia), the federation consists of [republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_Russia), [krais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krais_of_Russia), [oblasts](646268.md), [cities of federal importance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia), an [autonomous oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Autonomous_Oblast), and [autonomous okrugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_okrugs_of_Russia), all of which are equal subjects of the federation.[^cite_note-gov_constitution-1]

Every federal subject has its own [head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_\(Russia\)), a [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_parliaments_of_Russia), and a constitutional court. Each subject has its own constitution or charter and legislation, although the authority of these organs differ. Subjects have equal rights in relations with federal government bodies.[^cite_note-gov_constitution-1] The subjects have equal representation – two delegates each – in the [Federation Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_Council_of_Russia), the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house) of the [Federal Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Assembly_of_Russia). They differ in the degree of [autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_area) they enjoy; republics are offered more autonomy.

Post-Soviet Russia formed during the history of the [Russian Soviet Federative Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic) within the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and did not change at the time of the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1991. In 1992, during the so-called "[parade of sovereignties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parade_of_sovereignties)", [separatist sentiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalisms_in_Russia) and the [War of Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Laws) within Russia, the Russian regions signed the [Federation Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_Treaty) ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Федеративный договор, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Federativnyy dogovor*),[^cite_note-2] establishing and regulating the current inner composition of Russia, based on the division of authorities and powers among Russian government bodies and government bodies of constituent entities. The Federation Treaty was included in the text of the [1978 Constitution of the Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Constitution_of_1978). The current Constitution of Russia, adopted by federal referendum on 12 December 1993, came into force on 25 December 1993 and abolished the model of the [Soviet system of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_republic_\(system_of_government\)) introduced in 1918 by [Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin) and based on the right to secede from the country and on unlimited sovereignty of federal subjects (in practice secession was never allowed), which conflicts with the country's integrity and federal laws. The new constitution eliminated a number of legal conflicts, reserved the rights of the regions, introduced local self-government and did not grant the Soviet-era right to secede from the country. In the late 1990s and early 2000s the political system became *de jure* closer to other modern [federal states with a republican form of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_republic). In the 2000s, following the policies of [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) and of the ruling [United Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Russia) party, the Russian parliament changed the distribution of tax revenues, reduced the number of elections in the regions and gave more power to the federal authorities.

The Russian Federation was composed of 89 federal subjects in 1993. Mergers reduced the number to 83 by 2008. [Russia annexed Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) from [Ukraine](31750.md) in 2014, with the Russian government claiming Sevastopol and the Republic of Crimea to be the 84th and 85th federal subjects of Russia, a move that is not recognized internationally.[^cite_note-Hea23-3][^cite_note-Reuters-4] During the 2022 [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), [Russia claimed that it had annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_annexation_of_Donetsk,_Kherson,_Luhansk_and_Zaporizhzhia_oblasts) four Ukrainian oblasts, though they remain internationally recognized as part of Ukraine and are only partially occupied by Russia.[^cite_note-5]

## Terminology

An official government translation of the [Constitution of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Russia) from Russian to English uses the term "constituent entities of the Russian Federation". For example, Article 5 reads: "The Russian Federation shall consist of [republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_Russia), [krais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krais_of_Russia), [oblasts](646268.md), [cities of federal significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia), an [autonomous oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Autonomous_Oblast), and [autonomous okrugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_okrugs_of_Russia), which shall have equal rights as constituent entities of the Russian Federation."[^cite_note-gov_constitution-1] A translation provided by Garant-Internet instead uses the term "subjects of the Russian Federation".[^cite_note-constitution.ru-6]

Tom Fennell, a translator, told the 2008 [American Translators Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Translators_Association) conference that "constituent entity of the Russian Federation" is a better translation than "subject".[^cite_note-7] This was supported by Tamara Nekrasova, Head of Translation Department at Goltsblat [BLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berwin_Leighton_Paisner), who said in a 2011 presentation at a translators conference that "*constituent entity of the Russian Federation* is more appropriate than *subject of the Russian Federation* (*subject* would be OK for a monarchy)".[^cite_note-8]

## Mergers, splits and internal territorial changes&#x20;



Map of the federal subjects of Russia highlighting those that merged in the first decade of the 21st century (in yellow), and those whose merger has been discussed in the same decade (in orange)

Starting in 2005, some of the federal subjects were merged into larger territories. In this process, six very sparsely populated subjects (comprising in total 0.3% of the population of Russia) were integrated into more populated subjects, with the hope that the economic development of those territories would benefit from the much larger means of their neighbours. The merging process was finished on 1 March 2008. No new mergers have been planned since March 2008. The six territories became "administrative-territorial regions with special status". They have large proportions of minorities, with Russians being a majority only in three of them. Four of those territories have a second official language in addition to Russian: [Buryat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buryat_language) (in two of the merged territories), [Komi-Permian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komi-Perm_language), [Koryak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryak_language). This is an exception: all the other official languages of Russia (other than Russian) are set by the Constitutions of its constituent Republics ([Mordovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordovia), [Chechnya](6095.md), [Dagestan](751099.md) etc.). The status of the "administrative-territorial regions with special status" has been a subject of criticism because it does not appear in the Constitution of the Russian Federation.

In addition to those six territories that entirely ceased to be subjects of the Russian Federation and were downgraded to territories with special status, another three subjects have a status of subject but are simultaneously part of a more populated subject:

- [Nenets Autonomous Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nenets_Autonomous_Okrug) (2010 population of 42,090) has been a subject since 1993, but is also, according to its Constitution, part of [Arkhangelsk Oblast](472779.md)

- [Khanty-Mansi Autonomous Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanty-Mansi_Autonomous_Okrug) obtained autonomy in 1977, but is also part of [Tyumen Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyumen_Oblast)

- [Yamalo-Nenets Autonomous Okrug](482044.md) obtained the status of subject in 1992 (after obtaining autonomy in 1977), but is also part of Tyumen Oblast.

With an estimated population of 49,348 as of 2018, [Chukotka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chukotka_Autonomous_Okrug) is currently the least populated subject of Russia that is not part of a more populated subject. It was separated from [Magadan Oblast](480997.md) in 1993. Chukotka is one of the richest subjects of Russia (with a gross regional product \[GRP] per capita equivalent to that of Australia) and therefore does not fit in the pattern of merging a subject to benefit from the economic dynamism of the neighbour.

In 1992, [Ingushetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingushetia) separated from [Chechnya](6095.md) to stay away from the growing violence in Chechnya. Those two Muslim republics, populated in vast majority (95%+) by closely related [Vainakh people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakh_peoples), speaking [Vainakhish languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vainakhish_language), remain the two poorest subjects of Russia, with the GRP per capita of Ingushetia being equivalent to that of Iraq. According to 2016 statistics, however, they are also the safest regions of Russia, and also have the lowest alcohol consumption, with alcohol poisoning at least 40 times lower than the federal average.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

Until 1994, [Sokolsky District, Nizhny Novgorod Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokolsky_District,_Nizhny_Novgorod_Oblast) was part of [Ivanovo Oblast](463943.md).

In 2011–2012, the territory of Moscow increased by 140% (to 2,511 km<sup>2</sup> (970 sq mi)) by acquiring part of [Moscow Oblast](372315.md).

On 13 May 2020, the governors of Arkhangelsk Oblast and Nenets Autonomous Okrug announced their plan to merge following the collapse of oil prices stemming from the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] The process was scrapped on 2 July due to its unpopularity among the population.[^cite_note-25]
