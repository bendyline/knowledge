The **Iraqi–Syrian border** is the border between [Syria](7515849.md) and [Iraq](7515928.md) and runs for a total length of 599 km (372 mi) across [Upper Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Mesopotamia) and the [Syrian desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_desert), from the [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) with [Jordan](7515964.md) in the south-west to the tripoint with [Turkey](11125639.md) in the north-east.[^cite_note-1]

## Description

The border starts in the west at the tripoint with Jordan at [33°22′29″N 38°47′37″E  /  33.3747°N 38.7936°E  / 33.3747; 38.7936](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Iraq%E2%80%93Syria_border\&params=33.3747_N_38.7936_E_), with the initial section being a continuation of the long straight line that forms the eastern section of the [Jordan–Syria border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan%E2%80%93Syria_border). The boundary then shifts in the vicinity of the [Euphrates river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphrates_river) and the [Al-Qa'im border crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qa'im_border_crossing), proceeding northwards via a series of short straight lines, and then north-eastwards to the [Tigris river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris_river). The Tigris then forms a short 3-4 mile section of the border up to the Turkish tripoint at the confluence with the [Khabur river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khabur_\(Tigris\)) at [37°06′22″N 42°21′26″E  /  37.1060°N 42.3572°E  / 37.1060; 42.3572](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Iraq%E2%80%93Syria_border\&params=37.1060_N_42.3572_E_).

## History

At the start of the 20th century, the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) controlled what is now Syria and Iraq.[^cite_note-IBS100-2] During the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), an [Arab Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Revolt) - supported by Britain - succeeded in removing the Ottomans from most of the Middle East. As a result of the Anglo-French [Sykes-Picot Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sykes-Picot_Agreement), Britain gained control of the Ottoman [vilayets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilayet) of [Mosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosul_Vilayet), [Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad_Vilayet) and [Basra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basra_Vilayet), which it organised into the [mandate of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Iraq) in 1920, with France organising a [mandate over Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Syria_and_the_Lebanon).



[Islamic State](9087364.md)'s territory, in grey, at the time of its greatest territorial extent in May 2015

In the period 1920-23 France and Britain signed a series of agreements, collectively known as the [Paulet–Newcombe Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulet%E2%80%93Newcombe_Agreement), which created the modern Jordan-Syria and Iraq–Syria borders, as an amendment to what had been designated the *A zone* in the Sykes–Picot Agreement.[^cite_note-IBS100-2] In 1932, it was finalised following a [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) commission review.[^cite_note-IBS100-2][^cite_note-3]

Since the beginning of the 2003 [Iraq War](5043324.md), the border region has become unstable, exacerbated since 2011 by the ongoing [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md).[^cite_note-4] Most of the border region on both sides was part of the [Islamic State](9087364.md) (IS) for some years, although its northernmost portion remained under [Kurdish](17068.md) control, forming the border between [Rojava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Administration_of_North_and_East_Syria) and [Iraqi Kurdistan](679693.md).[^cite_note-5] The Islamic State has since lost control of the border, which is controlled by Rojava and the [Syrian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_government) on the Syrian side, and the [Kurdistan Regional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Regional_Government) and [Iraqi government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_government) on the Iraqi side.

## Border crossings

There are three official border crossings between [Syria](7515849.md) and [Iraq](7515928.md),[^cite_note-6] and one makeshift crossing:

- The [Rabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabia,_Iraq) border crossing, on the [Al-Shaddadah](39348357.md)–[Mosul](200475.md) road, is the most northerly official crossing.

- The [Al-Qa'im border crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qa'im_border_crossing) between [Abu Kamal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Kamal) in Syria and [Al Qa'im](1831738.md) in Iraq. The crossing was reopened on 30 September 2019, after eight years of closure due to [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md) and [Iraqi Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Civil_War_\(2014%E2%80%932017\)).

  [^cite_note-7]

  &#x20;Since the reopening of the border crossing, the Iranian-backed militia [Kata'ib Hezbollah](23626646.md), a group under the [Popular Mobilization Units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Mobilization_Units), has played an important military and security role on the Iraqi side of the border.

  [^cite_note-8]

  [^cite_note-9]

- The [Al Waleed border crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Waleed_border_crossing), known in Syria as al-Tanf, is located in the [Ar-Rutba District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ar-Rutba_District) of the [Al Anbar Governorate](467036.md), close to the westernmost point of Iraq and the northeasternmost point of [Jordan](7515964.md), in the desert [Badia region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badia_\(region\)). It serves as the main border checkpoint on the highway between [Damascus](8914.md) and [Baghdad](4492.md). The al-Tanf checkpoint is on the Syrian side of the border, in [Homs province](7445154.md). There are [Palestinian](23267.md) refugee camps on both sides: the [Al-Waleed camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Waleed_\(camp\)) on the Iraqi side and the [Al Tanf camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinians_in_Iraq#Al_Tanf_Camp) on the Syrian side. The crossing was captured by ISIL in May 2015 and [repelled in March 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Tanf_offensive_\(2016\)).

  [^cite_note-10]

- The [Semalka Border Crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semalka_Border_Crossing) is a pontoon bridge across the Tigris established by the [Kurdistan Regional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Regional_Government) during the Syrian Civil War about 1 km (0.62 mi) downstream from the Iraqi–Syrian–Turkish tripoint, and just north of [Faysh Khabur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faysh_Khabur) in Iraq.
