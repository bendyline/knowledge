The **Brotherly Leader and Guide of the Revolution of the Great Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriya** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الأخ القائد ومرشد الثورة الجماهرية العربية الليبية الشعبية الإشتراكية العظمى) was a title held by former [Libyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya) leader [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md), who claimed to be merely a symbolic figurehead of the country's official [governance structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_People's_Committee).[^cite_note-telegraph_gaddafi-1][^cite_note-jazeera_gaddafi-2] However, critics long described him as a [dictator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictator),[^cite_note-nytimes_green-3] referring to his position as the *[de facto](43594.md)* former political office, despite the Libyan state's denial of him holding any power.[^cite_note-telegraph_gaddafi-1][^cite_note-jazeera_gaddafi-2]

## History

Main article: [History of Libya under Muammar Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Libya_under_Muammar_Gaddafi)

After the [coup d'état on 1 September 1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Libyan_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), in which [King Idris I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idris_of_Libya) was deposed, Libya was governed by the [Revolutionary Command Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Revolutionary_Command_Council) (RCC) headed by Gaddafi. On 2 March 1977, after the adoption of the *[Declaration on the Establishment of the Authority of the People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_on_the_Establishment_of_the_Authority_of_the_People)*, the RCC was abolished and the supreme power passed into the hands of the [General People's Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_People's_Congress_\(Libya\)).[^cite_note-4] Gaddafi then became Secretary-General of the General People's Congress.

On 2 March 1979, Gaddafi renounced all public functions and was designated the "Leader" (*_Qāʾid_*) of the Libyan state and was accorded the honorifics "Guide of the First of September Great Revolution of the Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriya" or "Brotherly Leader and Guide of the Revolution" in government statements and the official press.[^cite_note-5]

On 9 March 1990, the General People's Congress adopted a resolution, the Declaration of Revolutionary Legitimacy, which declared that Gaddafi had absolute legitimacy and legal authority over Libya's people and national institutions because of his role in leading the coup in 1969.[^cite_note-6] This came after he asked the [Basic People's Congresses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_People's_Congress_\(administrative_division\)) to give him institutional legitimacy.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-\:0-8] The resolution made any orders issued by Gaddafi, such as directives and resolutions, [legally binding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legally_binding) and required them to be followed without question.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-\:0-8] It also designated Gaddafi as the "supreme leader".[^cite_note-10] In effect, this resolution confirmed Gaddafi's role as the *de facto* head of state and the ultimate authority in the country.[^cite_note-11] As well as being the *de facto* head of state, Gaddafi became the *de facto* [head of government](37570.md), leaving him with absolute power in Libya.[^cite_note-12] Despite these changes, the Libyan state continued to claim that Gaddafi had no power as he still had no official government position.

Although Gaddafi's title as "Brotherly Leader and Guide of the Revolution" was officially used only from 1979, it is often applied for the [whole period of Gaddafi's rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Libya_under_Muammar_Gaddafi). Although Gaddafi had no official government function from 1979, it was understood that he exercised near-absolute control over the government and the country. His 42 years in power prior to the [First Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Libyan_Civil_War) in 2011 have made him the fifth longest-ruling non-royal national leader since 1900, as well as the longest-ruling Arab leader.[^cite_note-13]

Although [ousted from Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tripoli_\(2011\)) at the end of August 2011, Gaddafi retained this *de facto* position as leader of the [Pro-Gaddafi forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya), until his defeat and [killing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Muammar_Gaddafi) by the [Anti-Gaddafi forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberation_Army_\(Libya\)) in the [Battle of Sirte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sirte_\(2011\)) on 20 October 2011.[^cite_note-14]

## Political system of Libya under Gaddafi

Main article: [Politics of Libya under Muammar Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Libya_under_Muammar_Gaddafi)

The Great Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriya was formally managed on the basis of the political ideology set out in *[The Green Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Green_Book_\(Muammar_Gaddafi\))*. The ideology was based on the idea of [direct democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_democracy), and direct decision-making of all citizens on all political matters. Across the country there were the basic people's congresses, which were considered the original holders of sovereignty. These included the composition of its adult citizens both men and women. Of their managerial and executive bodies formed the municipal people's congresses and committees, and then the General People's Congress (legislative body) and the [General People's Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_People's_Committee) (executive body).

Gaddafi rejected the parliamentary representation of the people or by elected representatives, as the General People's Congress was seen as a coordinating body that brought together all the popular congresses and committees, and not elected members who made decisions on behalf of the people. All laws were approved by the basic people's congresses, and then finally adopted by the General People's Congress.

## Succession proposals

One of Gaddafi's plans was passing of the country’s leadership on to his son, [Saif al-Islam](1196902.md), and the plan’s potential ramifications; the second is the political line adopted by the latter, and the internal and external roles he has played. Saif al-Islam was portrayed as the leader of the reform movement in contrast with the "conservatives" represented by the regime’s old guard, in particular the Revolutionary Committees.[^cite_note-15]
