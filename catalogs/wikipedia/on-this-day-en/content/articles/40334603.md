**Zurich** ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Zürich*; [Alemannic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannic_German_language): *Züri*; see [below](#Name)) is the [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Switzerland) in [Switzerland](26748.md) and the capital of the [canton of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Zurich). It is in north-central Switzerland,[^cite_note-4] at the northwestern tip of [Lake Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Zurich). As of the end of 2025, the municipality had a population of 440,935,[^cite_note-population-2] while the [urban area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_agglomeration) was home to 1.45 million people (2020), and the [Zurich metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Metropolitan_Area) had a total population of 2.1 million (2020).[^cite_note-Agglomeration-5] Zurich is a hub for railways, roads, and air traffic. Both [Zurich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Airport) and [Zurich's main railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich_Hauptbahnhof) are the largest and busiest in the country.

Zurich is a cosmopolitan city. While German is the official language and [Zurich German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_German), the most widely spoken variety, the use of both German and Swiss German at home is steadily declining, whereas the use of English has increased by 60% over ten years, reaching 14% of the population in 2024,[^cite_note-6] and 40% of Zurich residents speak English at work.[^cite_note-7]

Evidence of early, sparse settlements in the area dates back more than 6,400 years, indicating human presence prior to the establishment of the town.[^cite_note-8] Permanently settled for over 2,000 years, Zurich was eventually founded by the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire), who called it *_[Turicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turicum)_*. During the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), Zurich gained the independent and privileged status of [imperial immediacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_immediacy) and, in 1519, became a primary centre of the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation) in Europe under the leadership of [Huldrych Zwingli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huldrych_Zwingli).[^cite_note-9]

The city has a large presence of operations from 7 of the 10 globally leading AI companies. This, along with the presence of [ETH Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich)[^cite_note-10], makes the city a significant location in developing technologies. It also leads continental Europe as a financial and insurance center, according to the [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index) in 2026.&#x20;

Many museums and art galleries can be found in the city, including the [Swiss National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_National_Museum), [Natural History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_History_Museum_of_the_University_of_Zurich) and [Kunsthaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunsthaus_Z%C3%BCrich). [Schauspielhaus Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schauspielhaus_Z%C3%BCrich) is generally considered to be one of the most important theatres in the German-speaking world.[^cite_note-11]

## Name

The name is traditionally written in English as *Zurich*, without the [umlaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umlaut_\(diacritic\)). It is pronounced [/ˈzjʊərɪk/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/2f/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Zurich.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Zurich.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/2f/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Zurich.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Zurich.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*ZURE-ik*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

In German, the city name is written *Zürich* and pronounced [\[ˈtsyːrɪç\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/5b/De-Z%C3%BCrich2.ogg/De-Z%C3%BCrich2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/5b/De-Z%C3%BCrich2.ogg/De-Z%C3%BCrich2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>. In [the local dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_German), the name is pronounced without the final consonant and with two short vowels, as *Züri* [\[ˈtsyri\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Alemannic_German), although the demonym remains *Zürcher(in)*. The city is called *Zurich* [\[zyʁik\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French) in French, *Zurigo* [\[dzuˈriːɡo\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Italian) in Italian, and *Turitg* [\[tuˈritɕ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Romansh) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/66/Rm-sursilv-Turitg.flac/Rm-sursilv-Turitg.flac.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/66/Rm-sursilv-Turitg.flac/Rm-sursilv-Turitg.flac.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> in [Romansh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language).

The earliest known form of the city's name is *[Turicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turicum)*, attested on a [tombstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tombstone) of the late 2nd century AD in the form *STA(tio) TURICEN(sis)* ("Turicum customs post"). The name is interpreted as a derivation from a given name, possibly the [Gaulish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaulish) [personal name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_onomastics) *Tūros*. The toponym \**Turīcon* would then mean "belonging to Tūros", "place of Tūros".[^cite_note-15] The Latin stress on the long vowel of the Gaulish name, [\[tʊˈriːkõː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Latin), was lost in German [\[ˈtsyːrɪç\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) but is preserved in Italian [\[dzuˈriːɡo\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Italian) and in Romansh [\[tuˈritɕ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Romansh).

The first development towards its later [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages) form is attested as early as c. 680–700 with the form *Ziurichi*.[^cite_note-16] From the 9th century onward, the name is established in an [Old High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_High_German) form *Zuri(c)h* (857 *in villa Zurih*, 924 *in Zurich curtem*, 1416 *Zürich Stadt*).[^cite_note-17]

In [Neo-Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Latin) texts dating from c. 1500–1800, Zurich is often referred to as *Tigurum (see [Tigurini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigurini#Tigurum:_Zurich's_Neo-Latin_name))*.

## History

Main article: [History of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Zurich)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Zurich).

### Early history



[Johann Balthasar Bullinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Balthasar_Bullinger)'s imagining of Zurich in Roman times (engraving 1773), with [Wellenberg Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellenberg_Tower) on the river and another tower on [Lindenhof hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindenhof_hill) (to the right)

Settlements of the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) and [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) were found around [Lake Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Zurich). Traces of pre-Roman Celtic [La Tène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_T%C3%A8ne_culture) settlements were discovered near the [Lindenhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindenhof_hill), a [morainic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraine) hill dominating the SE – NW waterway constituted by Lake Zurich and the river [Limmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limmat).[^cite_note-dr571-18] In [Roman times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Gaul), during the conquest of the alpine region in 15 BC, the Romans built a *[castellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castellum)* on the Lindenhof.[^cite_note-dr571-18] Later here was erected *[Turicum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turicum)* (a [toponym](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/toponym) of clear [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) origin), a tax-collecting point for goods trafficked on the Limmat, which constituted part of the border between [Gallia Belgica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gallia_Belgica) (from AD 90 [Germania Superior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germania_Superior)) and [Raetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raetia): this customs point developed later into a *[vicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicus)*.[^cite_note-dr571-18] After Emperor [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine_the_Great)'s reforms in AD 318, the border between [Gaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaul) and Italy (two of the four [praetorian prefectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Praetorian_prefectures) of the Roman Empire) was located east of Turicum, crossing the river [Linth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linth) between [Lake Walen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Walen) and Lake Zurich where a castle and garrison looked over Turicum's safety. The earliest written record of the town dates from the 2nd century, with a tombstone referring to it as the *_Statio Turicensis [Quadragesima Galliarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadragesima_Galliarum)_* ("Zurich post for collecting the 2.5% value tax of the Galliae"), discovered at the [Lindenhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindenhof).[^cite_note-dr571-18]

In the 5th century, the Germanic [Alemanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemanni) tribe settled in the [Swiss Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Plateau). The Roman castle remained standing until the 7th century. A [Carolingian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian) castle, built on the site of the Roman castle by the grandson of [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne), [Louis the German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_the_German), is mentioned in 835 (*in Castro Turicino iuxta fluvium Lindemaci*). Louis also founded the [Fraumünster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraum%C3%BCnster) abbey in 853 for his daughter Hildegard. He endowed the [Benedictine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine) convent with the lands of Zurich, [Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri), and the [Albis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albis) forest, and granted the convent immunity, placing it under his direct authority. In 1045, King [Henry III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_III,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) granted the convent the right to hold markets, collect tolls, and mint coins, and thus effectively made the abbess the ruler of the city.[^cite_note-19]

Zurich gained [Imperial immediacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_immediacy) (*_Reichsunmittelbarkeit_*, becoming an [Imperial free city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_free_city)) in 1218 with the extinction of the main line of the [Zähringer family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Z%C3%A4hringen) and attained a status comparable to statehood. During the 1230s, a [city wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defensive_wall) was built, enclosing 38 hectares, when the earliest stone houses on the Rennweg were built as well. The Carolingian castle was used as a quarry, as it had started to fall into ruin.[^cite_note-20]

Emperor [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) promoted the abbess of the *Fraumünster* to the rank of a [duchess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchess) in 1234. The abbess nominated the mayor, and she frequently delegated the minting of coins to citizens of the city. The political power of the convent slowly waned in the 14th century, beginning with the establishment of the *_Zunftordnung_* ([guild](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCnfte_of_Zurich) laws) in 1336 by [Rudolf Brun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Brun), who also became the first independent mayor, i.e. not nominated by the abbess.



Manesse Codex Folio 371r

An important event in the early 14th century was the completion of the [Manesse Codex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manesse_Codex), a key source of medieval German poetry. The famous [illuminated manuscript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illuminated_manuscript) has been described as "the most beautifully illumined German manuscript in centuries."[^cite_note-21] It was commissioned by the Manesse family of Zurich, and copied and illustrated in the city at some time between 1304 and 1340. Producing such a work was a highly expensive prestige project, requiring several years of work by highly skilled scribes[^cite_note-22] and miniature painters, and it testifies to the increasing wealth and pride of Zurich citizens in this period. The work contains 6 songs by [Süsskind von Trimberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BC%C3%9Fkind_von_Trimberg). Von Trimberg may have been Jewish, since the work itself contains reflections on medieval Jewish life, though little is known about him.[^cite_note-23]

The first mention of [Jews in Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Zurich) was in 1273. Sources show that there was a synagogue in Zurich in the 13th century, implying the existence of a Jewish community.[^cite_note-24] With the rise of the Black Death in 1349, Zurich, like most other Swiss cities, responded by [persecuting and burning the local Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Massacre), marking the end of the first Jewish community there. The second Jewish community of Zurich formed towards the end of the 14th century, was short-lived, and Jews were expelled and banned from the city from 1423 until the 19th century.[^cite_note-25]

#### Archaeological findings

A woman who died in about 200 BC was found buried in a carved tree trunk during a construction project at the Kern school complex in March 2017 in Aussersihl. Archaeologists revealed that she was approximately 40 years old when she died and likely carried out little physical labor when she was alive. A sheepskin coat, a belt chain, a fancy wool dress, a scarf, and a pendant made of glass and amber beads were also discovered with the woman.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

### Old Swiss Confederacy



A scene depicting the [Old Zurich War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Zurich_War) in 1443 (1514, illustration in Federal Chronicle by Werner Schodoler)

On 1 May 1351, the citizens of Zurich had to swear allegiance before representatives of the cantons of [Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Lucerne), [Schwyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Schwyz), Uri and [Unterwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unterwalden), the other members of the [Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy). Thus, Zurich became the fifth member of the Confederacy, which was at that time a loose confederation of *[de facto](43594.md)* [independent states](180752.md). Zurich was the presiding canton of the Diet from 1468 to 1519. This authority was the executive council and lawmaking body of the confederacy, from the Middle Ages until the establishment of the [Swiss federal state](26748.md) in 1848. Zurich was temporarily expelled from the confederacy in 1440 due to a war with the other member states over the territory of [Toggenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toggenburg) (the [Old Zurich War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Zurich_War)). Neither side had attained significant victory when peace was agreed upon in 1446, and Zurich was readmitted to the confederation in 1450.[^cite_note-History_of_Zurich-30]



The *_[Murerplan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murerplan)_* of 1576

[Zwingli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huldrych_Zwingli) started the [Swiss Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Reformation) at the time when he was the main preacher at the *[Grossmünster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grossm%C3%BCnster)* in 1519. The [Zurich Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Bible_of_1531) was printed by [Christoph Froschauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Froschauer) in 1531. The Reformation resulted in major changes in state matters and civil life in Zurich, spreading also to several other cantons. Several cantons remained Catholic and became the basis of serious conflicts that eventually led to the outbreak of the [Wars of Kappel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wars_of_Kappel).

During the 16th and 17th centuries, the Council of Zurich adopted an isolationist attitude, resulting in a second ring of imposing fortifications built in 1624. The [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War) which raged across Europe motivated the city to build these walls. The fortifications required a lot of resources, which were taken from subject territories without reaching any agreement. The following revolts were crushed brutally. In 1648, Zurich proclaimed itself a republic, shedding its former status of a free imperial city.[^cite_note-History_of_Zurich-30] In this time the political system of Zurich was an [oligarchy](22315.md) (*_Patriziat_*): the dominant families of the city were the following ones: Bonstetten, Brun, Bürkli, Escher vom Glas, Escher vom Luchs, Hirzel, Jori (or von Jori), Kilchsperger, Landenberg, Manesse, Meiss, Meyer von Knonau, Mülner, von Orelli.



Fighting on the *[Paradeplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradeplatz)* during the *[Züriputsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCriputsch)*

The [Helvetic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_Republic) of 1798 saw the fall of the *[Ancien Régime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancien_R%C3%A9gime)*. Zurich lost control of the land and its economic privileges, and the city and the canton separated their possessions between 1803 and 1805. In 1839, the city had to yield to the demands of its urban subjects, following the *[Züriputsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCriputsch)* of 6 September. Most of the ramparts built in the 17th century were torn down, without ever having been besieged, to allay rural concerns over the city's [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony). The [Treaty of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Zurich) between Austria, France, and Sardinia was signed in 1859.[^cite_note-31]

### Modern history



[*Bahnhofplatz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahnhofplatz,_Z%C3%BCrich) in 1902

Zurich was the Federal capital for 1839–40, and consequently, the victory of the Conservative party there in 1839 caused a great stir throughout Switzerland. But when in 1845 the Radicals regained power at Zurich, which was again the Federal capital for 1845–46, Zurich took the lead in opposing the [Sonderbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonderbund) cantons. Following the Sonderbund War and the formation of the Swiss Federal State, Zurich voted in favor of the Federal constitutions of 1848 and 1874. The enormous [immigration](4599312.md) from the country districts into the town from the 1830s onwards created an industrial class which, though "settled" in the town, did not possess the privileges of burghership, and consequently had no share in the municipal government. First of all in 1860 the town schools, hitherto open to "settlers" only on paying high fees, were made accessible to all, next in 1875 ten years' residence *[ipso facto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipso_facto)* conferred the right of burghership, and in 1893 the eleven outlying districts were incorporated within the town proper.

When Jews began to settle in Zurich following their equality in 1862, the [Israelitische Cultusgemeinde Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelitische_Cultusgemeinde_Z%C3%BCrich) was founded.[^cite_note-auto-32]



Aerial view (1961)

Extensive developments took place during the 19th century. From 1847, the *_Spanisch-Brötli-Bahn_*, the first railway on Swiss territory, connected Zurich with [Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden,_Switzerland), putting the *[Zürich Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich_Hauptbahnhof)* at the origin of the [Swiss rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Railways) network. The present building of the *_Hauptbahnhof_* (the main railway station) dates to 1871. Zurich's *[Bahnhofstrasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahnhofstrasse)* (Station Street) was laid out in 1867, and the [Zurich Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIX_Swiss_Exchange) was founded in 1877. [Industrialisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialisation) led to migration into the cities and to rapid population growth, particularly in the suburbs of Zurich.

The *[Quaianlagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaianlagen_\(Zurich\))* are an important milestone in the development of the modern city of Zurich, as the construction of the new lakefront transformed Zurich from a small medieval town on the rivers [Limmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limmat) and [Sihl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sihl) to a modern city on the *Zürichsee* shore, under the guidance of the city engineer [Arnold Bürkli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_B%C3%BCrkli).[^cite_note-33]

In 1893, the twelve outlying districts were incorporated into Zurich, including Aussersihl, the workman's quarter on the left bank of the Sihl, and additional land was reclaimed from Lake Zurich.[^cite_note-34]

In 1934, eight additional districts in the north and west of Zurich were incorporated.

Zurich was [accidentally bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombings_of_Switzerland_in_World_War_II) during World War II. As persecuted Jews sought refuge in Switzerland, the [SIG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federation_of_Jewish_Communities) (*Schweizerischer Israelitischer Gemeindebund*, Israelite Community of Switzerland) raised financial resources. The Central Committee for Refugee Aid, created in 1933, was located in Zurich.

In the 1980s, Zurich experienced a severe drug crisis that culminated in an open drug scene at the [Platzspitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platzspitz) park, which became colloquially known as "Needle Park."[^cite_note-35] In the 1990s, Zurich pioneered a harm reduction strategy that introduced the distribution of clean syringes, supervised safe injection rooms, and low-threshold [methadone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methadone) dispensaries. This significantly reduced drug-related mortality and permanently dismantled the city's open drug scenes.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

The canton of Zurich did not recognize the Jewish religious communities as legal entities (and therefore as equal to national churches) until 2005.[^cite_note-auto-32]

### Heraldic achievement

The [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) of Zurich, used by both the city and the canton, consists of a divided field featuring white (argent) and blue (azure). Its origins date back to the 14th century, with the earliest documentation found on a seal of the Imperial Court of Justice from 1384. The shield appeared in colour on a banner in 1437 and a coin around 1417/18.[^cite_note-38]

When the canton of Zurich was established in 1803, it adopted the [heraldic achievement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraldic_achievement) that had been the city's for centuries, and a new version was created for the city by adding a [mural crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mural_crown) as a [crest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crest_\(heraldry\)). There are slight differences between the [supporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supporter) of the city and the canton, too: Both have their coats of arms supported by two lions, but the lions of the canton hold a sword and a palm leaf (which belonged to the city before the canton came into existence; see pictures below).[^cite_note-39]

## Geography



The [Limmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limmat) in Zurich



The city stretches on both sides of the [Limmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limmat), which flows out of [Lake Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Zurich). The Alps can be seen from the city centre, background to the lake.

Zurich is situated at 408 m (1,339 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level) on the lower (northern) end of [Lake Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Zurich) (*_Zürichsee_*) about 30 km (19 mi) north of the [Alps](981.md), nestling between the wooded hills on the west and east side. The Old Town stretches on both sides of the [Limmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limmat), which flows from the lake, running northwards at first and then gradually turning into a curve to the west. The geographic (and historic) centre of the city is the [Lindenhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindenhof_hill), a small natural hill on the west bank of the Limmat, about 700 m (2,300 ft) north of where the river issues from Lake Zurich. Today the incorporated city stretches somewhat beyond the natural confines of the hills and includes some districts to the northeast in the [Glatt Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glatt_Valley) (*_Glattal_*) and to the north in the [Limmat Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limmat_Valley) (*_Limmattal_*). The boundaries of the older city are easy to recognize by the Schanzengraben canal. This artificial watercourse has been used for the construction of the [third fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Zurich) in the 17th and 18th centuries.

### Topography

The municipality of Zurich has an area of 91.88 km<sup>2</sup> (35.48 sq mi), of which 4.1 km<sup>2</sup> (1.6 sq mi) is made up of Lake Zurich. The area includes a section of the northern [Swiss Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Plateau). The banks of the Limmat constitute the densest part of the city. The river is oriented in the southeast–northwest direction, with the flat valley floor having a width of two to three km (1.2 to 1.9 mi). The partially channeled and straightened Limmat does not flow in the central part of the valley, but always along its right (northeastern) side. The [Sihl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sihl) meets with the Limmat at the end of Platzspitz, which borders the [Swiss National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_National_Museum). The Limmat reaches the lowest point of the municipality in [Oberengstringen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberengstringen) at 392 m (1,286 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level).



Topographic map of Zurich and surroundings



Felsenegg from Lake Zurich



Zurich from Waidberg

On its west side, the Limmat valley is flanked by the wooded heights of the [Albis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albis) chain, which runs along the western border. The [Uetliberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uetliberg) is, with 869 m (2,851 ft) above sea level, the highest elevation of the surrounding area. Its summit can be reached easily by the [Uetlibergbahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uetlibergbahn). From the platform of the observation tower on the summit, an impressive panorama of the city, the lake, and the Alps can be seen.

The northeast side of the Limmat valley includes a range of hills, which marks the watershed between the Limmat and the Glatt. From the northwest to the southeast, the height of the mostly wooded knolls generally increases: the Gubrist (615 m or 2,018 ft), the Hönggerberg (541 m or 1,775 ft), the [Käferberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A4ferberg) (571 m or 1,873 ft), the [Zürichberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrichberg) (676 m or 2,218 ft), the [Adlisberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adlisberg) (701 m or 2,300 ft) and the [Öschbrig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96schbrig) (696 m or 2,283 ft). Between the Käferberg and the Zürichberg is located the saddle of the Milchbuck (about 470 m or 1,540 ft), an important passage from the Limmat valley to the Glatt valley.

The northernmost part of the municipality extends to the plain of the Glatt valley and to the saddle which makes the connection between the Glattal and Furttal. Also, a part of the [Katzensee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katzensee) (nature reserve) and the Büsisee, both of which are drained by the Katzenbach to Glatt, belong to the city.

### Climate

Zurich features a [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperateness) [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfb*, [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dobk*), with warm summers, cool winters and four distinct seasons.[^cite_note-40] Decisive for the climate of Zurich are both the winds from westerly directions, which often result in precipitation and, on the other hand, the [Bise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bise) (east or north-east wind), which is usually associated with high-pressure situations, but cooler weather phases with temperatures lower than the average. The [Foehn wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foehn_wind), which plays an important role in the northern alpine valleys, also has some impact on Zurich.[^cite_note-41]

Climate normals (reference period 1991–2020): The annual mean temperature at the measuring station of the [Federal Office of Meteorology and Climatology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MeteoSwiss) in Zurich-Fluntern (556 m\[1,824 ft] [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level) on the slope of the Zürichberg, 150 m\[490 ft] above the level of the city centre) is 9.8 °C (49.6 °F). The lowest monthly mean of daily minimum temperature is measured in January with −1.4 °C (29.5 °F) and the highest monthly mean of daily maximum temperature is measured in July with 24.3 °C (75.7 °F).[^cite_note-MeteoSwissSMA-42] On average there are 71.9 days in which the minimum temperature is below 0 °C (32 °F) (so-called *frost days*), and 19.9 days in which the maximum temperature is below 0 °C (32 °F) (so-called *ice days*). There are on average 44.8 so-called *summer days* (maximum temperature equal to or above 25 °C \[77 °F]) throughout the year, while so-called *heat days* (with maximum temperature equal to or above 30 °C \[86 °F]) are 8.5 days.[^cite_note-MeteoSwissSMA-42]

The average high temperature in July is 24.0 °C (75.2 °F) and average low temperature is 14 °C (57.2 °F). The highest recorded temperature in Zurich was 37.7 °C (100 °F), recorded in July 1947, and typically the warmest day reaches an average of 32.2 °C (90.0 °F).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

Spring and autumn are generally cool to mild, but sometimes with large differences between warm and cold days even during the same year. The highest temperature of the month March in 2014 was on the 20th at 20.6 °C (69.1 °F) during a sunny afternoon and the lowest temperature was on the 25th at −0.4 °C (31.3 °F) during the night/early morning.[^cite_note-45] Record low of average daily temperatures in March since 1864 is −12 °C (10 °F) and record high of average daily temperatures in March is 16 °C (61 °F). Record low of average daily temperatures in October is −16 °C (3 °F) and record high of average daily temperatures in October is 20 °C (68 °F).[^cite_note-46]

Zurich has an average of 1,694 hours of sunshine per year; that is 42% of its potential time throughout the year. From April to September, the sun shines between 164 and 229 hours per month. The 1,108 mm (43.6 in) rainfall spread on 130.4 days with at least 1 mm of precipitation throughout the year. Roughly about every third day you will encounter at least some precipitation, which is very much a Swiss average. During the warmer half of the year and especially from May to August, the strength of rainfall is higher than in winter, but the number of days with precipitation stays about the same throughout the year (in average 9.0–12.4 days per month). February has the lowest number (9.0) of days with some precipitation. There is an average of 68.3 so-called *bright days* (number of days with sunshine duration greater than 80%) through the year, the most in July and August (8.1, 8.8 days), and the least in January and December (3.0, 2.2 days). The average number of days with sunshine duration less than 20%, so-called *cloudy days*, is 150.1 days, while the most cloudy days are in November (17.7 days), December (21.2 days), and January (18.7 days).[^cite_note-MeteoSwissSMA-42]

#### Climate change

The climate in Zurich is becoming not only warmer but also wetter, with reduced sunlight. A comparison between the periods 1940–1950 and 2013–2024 reveals significant changes. For example, in July, temperatures have increased by 1.77 °C, while December has seen a rise of 3.41 °C. Precipitation in October has increased by 1.45 mm. In contrast, snowfall in February has decreased by 0.16 mm, and in March, there has been a reduction in sunshine duration by 0.56 hours.[^cite_note-50] – In the whole of Switzerland, the mean annual temperature ("climate mean") has risen by 2.9 °C since the pre-industrial period of 1871–1900 (as of 2025).[^cite_note-51]

#### Climate protection

In November 2008[^cite_note-52] the people of Zurich voted in a public [referendum](51582.md) to write into law the quantifiable and fixed deadline of [one tonne of CO<sub>2</sub> per person per annum by 2050](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000-watt_society). This forces any decision of the executive to support this goal, even if the costs are higher in all dimensions. Some examples are the new disinfection section of the public city hospital in Triemli ([Minergie-P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minergie) quality – [passive house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_house)), the continued optimisation and creation of public transportation, enlargement of the bicycle-only network, and projects for [renewable energy](25784.md).

### Urban area

The areas surrounding the Limmat are almost completely developed with residential, industrial, and commercial zones. The sunny and desirable residential areas in the hills overlooking Zurich, Waidberg and Zürichberg, and the bottom part of the slope on the western side of the valley on the Uetliberg, are also densely built.

The "green lungs" of the city include the vast forest areas of Adlisberg, Zürichberg, Käferberg, Hönggerberg and Uetliberg. Major parks are also located along the lakeshore (Zürichhorn and Enge), while smaller parks dot the city. Larger contiguous agricultural lands are located near [Affoltern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affoltern_am_Albis) and [Seebach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seebach_\(Z%C3%BCrich\)). Of the total area of the municipality of Zurich (in 1996, without the lake), 45.4% is residential, industrial and commercial, 15.5% is transportation infrastructure, 26.5% is forest, 11% is agriculture and 1.2% is water.

### Languages

The official formal language used by governmental institutions, print, news, schools and universities, courts, theatres and in any kind of written form is the Swiss variety of Standard [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Standard_German), while the spoken language is [Zurich German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_German) (*Züritüütsch*), one of the several more or less distinguishable, but mutually intelligible [Swiss German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_German) dialects of Switzerland with roots in the medieval [Alemannic German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemannic_German) dialect groups. However, because of Zurich's national importance, and therefore its existing high fluctuation, its inhabitants and commuters speak all kinds of Swiss German dialects. As of the December 2010 census, 69.3% of the population speaks [diglossic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diglossia) [Swiss German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_German)/[Swiss Standard German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Standard_German) as their mother-tongue at home. Some 22.7% of inhabitants speak [Standard German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_German) in their family environment ("at home"). Dramatically increasing, according to the last census in 2000, 8.8% now speak English. Italian follows behind at 7.1% of the population, then French at 4.5%. Other languages spoken here include: Bosnian (4.1%), Spanish (3.9%), Portuguese (3.1%), and Albanian (2.3%). (Multiple choices were possible.) Thus, 20% of the population speak two or more languages at home.[^cite_note-56]

Before the [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation) reached Zurich, it was *[de jure](43594.md)* and *de facto* [Roman Catholic](606848.md).

The [Protestant Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformation), led by [Huldrych Zwingli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huldrych_Zwingli), made Zurich both a theological centre and a stronghold of [Protestantism in Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Switzerland). Another Swiss city with a comparable status was [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), the so-called *Protestant Rome*, where [John Calvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Calvin) and his [Protestant Reformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformers) operated, as well as [Basel](4911.md). Zurich attracted other influential [Protestant Reformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformers) like [Heinrich Bullinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Bullinger). Zwingli translated the Bible ([Zurich Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Bible)) into the local variety of German, and introduced the Reformation by winning support of the magistrates, the princess abbess [Katharina von Zimmern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katharina_von_Zimmern), and the largely peasant population of the [Canton of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Zurich). The canton unanimously adopted the [Reformed tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism), as represented by Zwingli. Religious wars between Catholics and Protestants tormented the [Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Confederacy). Zwingli died for political and religious reasons by defending the Canton of Zurich in the [Battle of Kappel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kappel). Bullinger took over his role as the city's spiritual leader.

In 1970, about 53% of the population were [Swiss Reformed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Reformed_Church), while almost 40% were Roman Catholic. Since then, both large Swiss churches, the Roman Catholic Church and Swiss Reformed Church, have been constantly losing members, though for the Catholic Church, the decrease started 20 years later, in around 1990. Nevertheless, for the last twenty years, both confessions have been reduced by 10%, to the current figures (census 2010): 30% Roman Catholic, and 26% Swiss Reformed (organized in [Evangelical Reformed Church of the Canton of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Reformed_Church_of_the_Canton_of_Zurich)). In 1970, only 2% of Zurich's inhabitants claimed to be not affiliated with any religious confession. In accordance with the loss by the large Swiss churches, the number of people declaring themselves as non-affiliated rose to 17% in the year 2000. In the last ten years, this figure rose to more than 25%. For the group of people, being between 24 and 44 years old, this is as high as one in every third person.[^cite_note-Etablierte_Kirchen-58]

5% of Zurich's inhabitants are Muslims, a slight decrease of 1%, compared to the year 2000. The [Mahmood Mosque Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmood_Mosque_\(Zurich\)), situated in Forchstrasse, is the first mosque built in Switzerland.[^cite_note-Etablierte_Kirchen-58][^cite_note-59]

The population of Jewish ethnicity and religion has been more or less constant since 1970, at about 1%. The [Synagoge Zürich Löwenstrasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synagoge_Z%C3%BCrich_L%C3%B6wenstrasse) is the oldest and largest synagogue of Zurich.[^cite_note-Etablierte_Kirchen-58][^cite_note-icz-synagogue-60]

The social assistance rate[^cite_note-63] in 2024 was 3.9%, down from 5.1% in 2014.[^cite_note-64]

## Politics

### City districts

Main article: [Districts of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Zurich)



Zurich's twelve municipal districts

The previous boundaries of the city of Zurich (before 1893) were more or less synonymous with the location of the old town. Two large expansions of the city limits occurred in 1893 and in 1934 when the city of Zürich merged with many surrounding municipalities, that had been growing increasingly together since the 19th century. Today, the city is divided into twelve districts (known as *_Kreis_* in German), numbered 1 to 12, each one of which contains between one and four neighborhoods:

- *[Kreis 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstadt_\(Zurich\))*, known as **_Altstadt_**, contains the old town, both to the east and west of the start of the [Limmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limmat). District 1 contains the neighbourhoods of *_Hochschulen_*, *_Rathaus_*, *_Lindenhof_*, and *City*.

- *[Kreis 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_2_\(Zurich\))* lies along the west side of [Lake Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Zurich), and contains the neighbourhoods of *[Enge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enge_\(Zurich\))*, *Wollishofen* and *Leimbach*.

- *[Kreis 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiedikon)*, known as **_Wiedikon_** is between the [Sihl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sihl) and the [Uetliberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uetliberg), and contains the neighbourhoods of *_Alt-Wiedikon_*, *_Sihlfeld_* and *_Friesenberg_*.

- *[Kreis 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industriequartier_\(Zurich\))*, known as **_Aussersihl_** lies between the Sihl and the train tracks leaving *[Zürich Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich_Hauptbahnhof)*, and contains the neighbourhoods of *_Werd_*, *_Langstrasse_*, and *_Hard_*.

- *[Kreis 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_5_\(Zurich\))*, known as **_Industriequartier_**, is between the Limmat and the train tracks leaving *[Zürich Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich_Hauptbahnhof)*, it contains the former industrial area of Zürich which has undergone large-scale rezoning to create upscale modern housing, retail, commercial real estate, and a few big vocational schools. It contains the neighborhoods of *_Gewerbeschule_* and *_Escher-Wyss_*.

- *[Kreis 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_6_\(Zurich\))* is on the edge of the *[Zürichberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrichberg)*, a hill overlooking the eastern part of the city. District 6 contains the neighbourhoods of *_[Oberstrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberstrass)_* and *_[Unterstrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unterstrass)_*.

- *[Kreis 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_7_\(Zurich\))* is on the edge of the *[Adlisberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adlisberg)* hill as well as the Zürichberg, on the eastern side of the city. District 7 contains the neighbourhoods of *_Fluntern_*, *_[Hottingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hottingen_\(Zurich\))_*, and *_[Hirslanden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirslanden)_*. These neighbourhoods are home to Zurich's wealthiest and more prominent residents. The *_[Witikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witikon)_* neighbourhood also belongs to district 7.

- *[Kreis 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riesbach)*, officially called **_Riesbach_**, but colloquially known as **Seefeld**, lies on the eastern side of [Lake Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Zurich). District 8 consists of the neighbourhoods of *_Seefeld_*, *_Mühlebach_*, and *_Weinegg_*.

- *[Kreis 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_9_\(Zurich\))* is between the Limmat to the north and the *Uetliberg* to the south. It contains the neighbourhoods *_[Altstetten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altstetten_\(Zurich\))_* and *_[Albisrieden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albisrieden)_*.

- *[Kreis 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_10_\(Zurich\))* is to the east of the [Limmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limmat) and to the south of the *Hönggerberg* and *[Käferberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A4ferberg)* hills. District 10 contains the neighbourhoods of *_[Höngg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H%C3%B6ngg)_* and *_[Wipkingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wipkingen)_*.

- *[Kreis 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_11_\(Zurich\))* is in the area north of the *Hönggerberg* and *[Käferberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A4ferberg)* and between the [Glatt Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glatt_Valley) and the *[Katzensee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katzensee)* (Cats Lake). It contains the neighbourhoods of *_[Affoltern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affoltern_\(Zurich\))_*, *_[Oerlikon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oerlikon_\(Zurich\))_* and *_[Seebach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seebach_\(Zurich\))_*.

- *[Kreis 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwamendingen)*, known as **_Schwamendingen_**, is in the *[Glattal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glatt_Valley)* (Glatt valley) on the northern side of the [Zürichberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrichberg). District 12 contains the neighbourhoods of *_Saatlen_*, *_Schwamendigen Mitte_*, and *_Hirzenbach_*.

Most of the district boundaries are fairly similar to the original boundaries of the previously existing municipalities before they were incorporated into the city of Zurich.

### Government

See also: [List of mayors of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Zurich)

The City Council (**_Stadtrat_**) constitutes the executive government of the City of Zurich and operates as a [collegiate authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_authority?action=edit\&redlink=1). It is composed of nine councilors, each presiding over a department. Departmental tasks, coordination measures and implementation of laws decreed by the Municipal Council are carried out by the City Council. The regular election of the City Council by any inhabitant valid to vote is held every four years. The mayor ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Stadtpräsident(in)*) is elected as such by a public election by a system of [Majorz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorz?action=edit\&redlink=1) while the heads of the other departments are assigned by the collegiate. Any resident of Zurich allowed to vote can be elected as a member of the City Council. In the mandate period 2026–2030 (*Legislatur*) the City Council is presided by mayor [Raphael Golta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Golta?action=edit\&redlink=1). The executive body holds its meetings in the City Hall ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Stadthaus*), on the left bank of the Limmat. The building was built in 1883 in Renaissance style.

As of May 2026, the Zurich City Council is made up of four representatives of the SP ([Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland), one of whom is the mayor), three members of the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Switzerland), and one member each of the FDP ([Free Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland)) and of GLP ([Green Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Liberal_Party_of_Switzerland)), giving the left parties a combined seven out of nine seats.[^cite_note-SR-69] The last regular election was held on 8 March 2026.[^cite_note-W26-70]

The Municipal Council (**_Gemeinderat_**) holds the [legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power). It is made up of 125 members (*Gemeinderat / Gemeinderätin*), with elections held every four years. The Municipal Council decrees regulations and by-laws that are executed by the City Council and the administration. The sessions of the Municipal Council are held in public. Unlike those of the City Council, the members of the Municipal Council are not politicians by profession but are paid a fee based on their attendance. Any resident of Zürich allowed to vote can be elected as a member of the Municipal Council. The legislative body holds its meetings in the [town hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Town_Hall) (*Rathaus*, on the right bank of the Limmat opposite to the City Hall (*Stadthaus*)).[^cite_note-75]

### International relations

Zurich engages in a variety of international collaborations through city partnerships and participates in international networks and dialogues.[^cite_note-80]

#### Twin towns

Zurich is partnered with two [sister cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_towns_and_sister_cities): [Kunming](197181.md) and [San Francisco](49728.md).[^cite_note-81]

The formal partnership agreement with the city of [Kunming](197181.md) was established in 1982, making it Zurich's longest-standing twin town relationship. Cooperation has centred on water‑supply engineering, urban planning and cultural projects. A notable outcome of this relationship is the [Chinese Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Garden,_Zurich), inaugurated in 1994 near [Blatterwiese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blatterwiese) on Lake Zurich's right bank. Kunming donated the garden to express gratitude for Zurich's technical assistance with drinking water and drainage systems.[^cite_note-82] The garden is considered one of the most significant [temple gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_garden) outside of China.[^cite_note-83]

The partnership with San Francisco was established in 2003, covering exchanges in entrepreneurship, digital innovation, urban sustainability, and public health.[^cite_note-84]

#### International initiatives

Zurich participates in international city networks such as [Eurocities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocities) and the [Mayors Migration Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayors_Migration_Council?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-85]

In 2016 Zurich launched an outreach series that presents its economy, culture and research abroad. Editions have been held in London (2016), Hong Kong (2017), San Francisco (2018), Seoul (2019), Berlin (2022) and Copenhagen (2023).[^cite_note-86]

From 2017 to 2024, the municipality of Zurich partnered with [Tyre, Lebanon](30796.md), in a project facilitated by [UN-Habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Human_Settlements_Programme).[^cite_note-\:0-87] This collaboration aimed to support Tyre in addressing challenges related to the [Syrian refugee crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refugees_of_the_Syrian_civil_war), with a focus on improving urban mobility and fostering social cohesion between host and refugee communities.[^cite_note-\:0-87] The city of Zurich provided funding for this initiative.[^cite_note-\:0-87]

#### Diplomatic missions

Zurich hosts a substantial diplomatic community, with 24 consulates‑general, 40 consulates and 1 Consular Agency in operation as of March 2025.[^cite_note-88]

### Churches

- [Grossmünster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grossm%C3%BCnster) (Great Minster) According to legend, [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne) discovered the graves of the city's martyrs Felix and Regula and had built the first church as a monastery; start of current building around 1100; in the first half of the 16th century, the Great Minster was the starting point of the Swiss-German Reformation led by [Huldrych Zwingli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zwingli) and [Heinrich Bullinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_Bullinger); declared by Charlemagne imperial church; romanesque crypt, romanesque capitals in the church and cloister; choir windows by [Augusto Giacometti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Giacometti) (1932) and [Sigmar Polke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmar_Polke) (2009), bronze doors by [Otto Münch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_M%C3%BCnch) (1935 and 1950).

  [^cite_note-89]

- [Fraumünster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraum%C3%BCnster) (Women's Minster) Church of a former abbey for aristocratical women from southern Germany which was founded in 853 by Louis the German for his daughter Hildegard; first church built before 874; the romanesque choir dates from 1250 to 1270; the church enjoyed the patronage of kings and had the right of coinage from Zurich to the 13th century; after the Reformation, church and convent passed into the possession of the city; the most important jewelry – in addition to the largest organ in the canton with its 5,793 pipes and 92 stops – are colour windows: the window in the north transept of Augusto Giacometti (1945), the five-part cycle in the choir (1970) and the rosette in the southern transept (1978) are by [Marc Chagall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Chagall); also the church of Zurich's largest choir with 100 and more singers.

  [^cite_note-90]

### Architecture



The 88-metre (289 ft)

[^cite_note-101]

&#x20;high [Sunrise Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunrise_Tower_\(Zurich\)) (2005) was the first approved high-rise building in twenty years.

Compared to other cities, there are few tall buildings in Zurich. The municipal building regulations (Article 9)[^cite_note-102] limit the construction of high-rise buildings to areas in the west and north of the city. In the industrial district, Altstetten and Oerlikon, buildings up to 80 m (260 ft) in height are allowed (high-rise area I). In the adjacent high-rise areas II and III the height is limited to 40 m (130 ft). Around the year 2000, regulations became more flexible and high-rise buildings were again planned and built. The people's initiative "40 m (130 ft) is enough," which would have reduced both the maximum height and the high-rise buildings area, was clearly rejected on 29 November 2009.[^cite_note-103] At this time in Zurich about a dozen high-rise buildings were under construction or in planning, including the [Prime Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Tower_\(Zurich\)) as the tallest skyscraper in Switzerland at the time of its construction. There are numerous examples of brutalist buildings throughout the city, including the [Swissmill Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swissmill_Tower) which, at 118 m (387 ft), is the world's tallest grain silo.

## Economy

See also: [Economy of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Switzerland)

Paradeplatz, heart of Zurich's financial centre

[Paradeplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradeplatz), the heart of Zurich's financial centre

Zurich contributes approximately 10% to Switzerland's GDP.[^cite_note-105] Approximately 90% of workers are employed in the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector).[^cite_note-106]

In a 2009 survey by [CityMayors.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CityMayors.com), Zurich was ranked 9th among the "World's 10 Most Powerful Cities".[^cite_note-World's_10_Most_Powerful_Cities-107] In the 2017 [Global Financial Centres Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index), Zurich was ranked as having the 11th most competitive financial centre in the world, and second most competitive in Europe after London.[^cite_note-108] The [Greater Zurich Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_metropolitan_area) is Switzerland's economic centre and home to many international companies.

Some of Switzerland's largest financial institutions have their headquarters in Zurich, most notably: [UBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBS), [Zurich Cantonal Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Cantonal_Bank), [Migros Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migros_Bank), [Julius Baer](1965173.md), [Zurich Insurance Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Insurance_Group), [Swiss Re](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Re) and [Swiss Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Life).

For a list of the largest companies in Zurich, see: [List of Swiss companies by revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Swiss_companies_by_revenue).

### Contributory factors to economic strength

The high quality of life has been cited as a reason for economic growth in Zurich. The [consulting firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_consulting) [Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercer_\(consulting_firm\)) has for many years ranked Zurich as a city with the highest quality of life in the world.[^cite_note-Mercer07-109][^cite_note-Mercer08-110] In particular, Zurich received high scores for work, housing, leisure, education and safety. Local planning authorities ensure clear separation between urban and recreational areas and there are many protected nature reserves.[^cite_note-usatoday1-111] Zurich is also ranked the third [most expensive city in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_cities_for_expatriate_employees), behind Hong Kong and Tokyo and ahead of Singapore.[^cite_note-Mercer-CoL-18-112]

Zurich benefits from the high level of investment in education which is typical of Switzerland in general and provides skilled labour at all levels. The city is home to two major universities, thus enabling access to graduates and high technology research. Professional training incorporates a mix of practical work experience and academic study while, in general, emphasis is placed on obtaining a good level of general education and language ability. As a result, the city is home to many multilingual people and employees generally demonstrate a high degree of motivation and a low level of absenteeism. In addition, employment laws are less restrictive than in nearby Germany or France.[^cite_note-usatoday1-111]

[Google](1092923.md) Zurich is a main hub for its operations in Europe with about 5000 employees. In March 2023, workers at the centre staged a solidarity movement in coordination with the IT workers' union [Syndicom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndicom) to reduce lay-offs and to oppose salary cuts. According to a spokesperson for the company, Google Zurich would remain a major employer despite the measures.[^cite_note-113]

### Swiss stock exchange

The Swiss stock exchange is called [SIX Swiss Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIX_Swiss_Exchange), formerly known as SWX. The SIX Swiss Exchange is the head group of several different worldwide operative financial systems: [Eurex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurex), [Eurex US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Us_futures_exchange), EXFEED, [STOXX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STOXX), and [virt-x](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virt-x). The exchange turnover generated at the SWX was in 2007 of 1,780,499.5 million CHF; the number of transactions arrived in the same period at 35,339,296 and the [Swiss Performance Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Performance_Index) (SPI) arrived at a total [market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization) of 1,359,976.2 million CHF.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

## Education and research



Main building of the University of Zurich

About 70,000 people study at the 20 universities, colleges and institutions of higher education in Zurich in 2019.[^cite_note-116] Two of Switzerland's most distinguished universities are located in the city: the [Swiss Federal Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETH_Zurich) (ETH Zurich), which is controlled by the federal government, and the [University of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Zurich), under direction of the canton of Zurich. Both universities were listed in the top 50 world universities rated in 2007, while the ETH has consistently remained in the top 10 universities worldwide since 2016.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]



Main building of the Swiss Institute of Technology in Zurich (ETH)

ETH was founded in 1854 by the Swiss Confederation and opened its doors in 1855 as a polytechnic institute. ETH achieved its reputation particularly in the fields of chemistry, mathematics and physics and there are 21 Nobel Laureates who are associated with the institution. ETH is usually ranked the top university in continental Europe.[^cite_note-119] The institution consists of two campuses, the main building in the heart of the city and the new campus on the outskirts of the city.

The University of Zurich was founded in 1833, although its beginnings date back to 1525 when the Swiss reformer [Ulrich Zwingli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulrich_Zwingli) founded a college of theology. Nowadays with its 24,000 students and 1,900 graduations each year, the University of Zurich is the largest in Switzerland and offers the widest range of subjects and courses at any Swiss higher education institution.

The Pedagogical College, the [Zurich University of Applied Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_University_of_Applied_Sciences) (ZHAW) and the [Zurich University of the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_University_of_the_Arts) (ZHdK) are another three top-class technical colleges which contribute to Zurich's reputation as a knowledge and research pole by providing applied research and development. Zurich is also one of the co-location centres of the Knowledge and Innovation Community (Climate Change Mitigation and Adaptation) of the [European Institute of Innovation and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Institute_of_Innovation_and_Technology).[^cite_note-120]

In addition to the university libraries, the city is also served by the [Zentralbibliothek Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zentralbibliothek_Z%C3%BCrich), a research and public library, and the [Pestalozzi-Bibliothek Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pestalozzi-Bibliothek_Z%C3%BCrich), a public library with 14 locations.

## Transport

### Public transport

Main article: [Public transport in Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_in_Zurich)

See also: [Zurich model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_model)



One of the city's two [paddle steamers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddle_steamer) on [Lake Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Zurich)



[Zurich's main railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Hauptbahnhof), Switzerland's busiest railway station

[Public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) is extremely popular in Zurich, and its inhabitants use public transport in large numbers. About 70% of the visitors to the city use the [tram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trams_in_Zurich) or bus (motor or [trolley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Zurich)), and about half of the journeys within the municipality take place on public transport.[^cite_note-122] The city's [network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrcher_Verkehrsverbund) of public transport includes four main modes of transit: trains, in particular the local [S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_S-Bahn), trams, buses, and [boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZSG). Tickets purchased for a trip are valid on each mode of transport. The public-transport network also includes three [funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular) railways within the city's [fare zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fare_zones_of_Zurich).

Zurich is a mixed hub for railways, roads, and air traffic. Zurich's main railway station, [Zürich Hauptbahnhof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich_Hauptbahnhof) (*Zürich HB*), is the largest and busiest station in Switzerland and an important railway hub in Europe. As of 2019, it served around 470,000 passengers and nearly 3,000 trains every day.[^cite_note-123] In total, there are [28 operational railway stations in Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_railway_stations_in_Zurich).

### Zurich Airport

Main article: [Zurich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Airport)



Zurich Airport in Kloten

Zurich Airport is about 8 km (5.0 mi) north of central Zurich, partly lying in the municipality of [Kloten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kloten). Zurich Airport has its own railway station, which is located underground. It is directly connected to Zurich and most of the major Swiss cities. Zurich Airport is served by more than 60 passenger airlines from around the world. It is also served by one cargo airline and is a hub for [Swiss International Air Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_International_Air_Lines). There is also an [airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCbendorf_Air_Base) in [Dübendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCbendorf).

The [A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_\(Switzerland\)), [A3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A3_motorway_\(Switzerland\)), and [A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A4_\(Switzerland\)) motorways pass close to Zurich. ### Bicycle transport

See also: [Swiss National Bike Routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_National_Bike_Routes)

In 2012, the city council launched a program to improve the city's attractiveness for bicycle traffic. The so-called "Masterplan Velo"[^cite_note-124] is part of the superordinate framework *Stadtverkehr 2025* which shapes the future of the different means of transport. Research revealed that infrastructure and the social environment are essential factors in improving a city's appeal to bicycle traffic.[^cite_note-125] Three main goals are specified: First, the [modal share](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modal_share) of bicycle traffic should be enhanced to twice the value of 2011 by 2015. Second, cyclists' safety should be improved to lower the overall accident risk. Third, cycling should be established as an everyday means of transport with a special focus on children and young people.

In terms of infrastructure, the city aims to build up a network of distinctive bicycle routes in order to achieve these objectives. At a final stage, the network will consist of main routes (*Hauptrouten*) for everyday use and comfort routes (*Komfortrouten*), with the latter focusing on leisure cycling. Additional measures such as special *Velostationen* providing bike-related services are expected to help to further improve the quality. One of the key projects of the system is a tunnel beneath the tracks of the [main railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich_Hauptbahnhof) planned to combine a main connection with staffed possibilities where commuters can leave their bikes throughout the day.[^cite_note-126] Apart from infrastructural measures, further approaches are planned in the fields of communication, education and administration.

However, these efforts cause critique, mainly due to postponing. The institution of the bike tunnel at the main railway station, originally planned for 2016, was delayed to at least 2019.[^cite_note-127] Pro Velo, a nationwide interest group, has publicly questioned whether the masterplan already failed.[^cite_note-128] The critique aims at badly governed traffic management at construction sites, missing possibilities to park bikes in the city as well as rather diffident ambitions. In response, the responsible city department points to the big investments made every year and mentions ongoing discussions that would finally lead to even better results.[^cite_note-129]

Opening of the [Zurich Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Film_Festival) (2008)

In addition to high-quality museums and galleries, Zurich has high-calibre chamber and symphony orchestras and several important theatres.[^cite_note-130]

The [Zurich Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Film_Festival) is an international film festival, lasting 11 days and featuring popular international productions.[^cite_note-131]



Zurich during the [Street Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Parade) (2008)

One of the largest and most popular annual events in Zurich is the [Street Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Parade), which is also one of the largest techno and dance music festivals in the world. Proceeding along the side of Lake Zurich, it is normally held on the second Saturday in August. The first edition was held in 1992 with about 1,000 participants. By 2001 the event attracted one million participants.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-markstravelnotes1-133] The Zürifäscht, on the other hand, is a triennial public festival. It features music, fireworks set to music,[^cite_note-markstravelnotes1-133] and other attractions throughout the old town. It is the largest public festival in Switzerland and attracts up to 2 million visitors.[^cite_note-134]

The Kunst Zürich is an international [contemporary art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_art) fair with an annual guest city; it combines most recent arts with the works of well-established artists.[^cite_note-135] Another annual public art exhibit is the city campaign, sponsored by the City Vereinigung (the local equivalent of a [chamber of commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_commerce)) with the cooperation of the [city government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government). It consists of decorated sculptures distributed over the city centre, in public places. Past themes have included lions (1986), cows (1998), benches (2003), teddy bears (2005), and huge flower pots (2009). From this originated the concept of the [CowParade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CowParade) that has been featured in other major world cities.

Zurich has been the home to several art movements. The [Dada movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dada) was founded in 1916 at the [Cabaret Voltaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabaret_Voltaire_\(Zurich\)). Artists like [Max Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Bill), [Marcel Breuer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Breuer), [Camille Graeser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camille_Graeser) or [Richard Paul Lohse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Paul_Lohse) had their ateliers in Zurich, which became even more important after the takeover of power by the [Nazi regime](21212.md) in Germany and World War II.

The best known traditional holiday in the city is the [Sechseläuten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sechsel%C3%A4uten) (*Sächsilüüte*), including a parade of the [guilds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCnfte_of_Zurich) and the burning of "winter" in effigy at the [Sechseläutenplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sechsel%C3%A4utenplatz). During this festival the popular [march](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_\(music\)) known as the [Sechseläutenmarsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alter_J%C3%A4germarsch) is played. It has no known composer but likely originated in Russia.[^cite_note-136] Another is the [Knabenschiessen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knabenschiessen) [target shooting competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sch%C3%BCtzenfest) for teenagers (originally boys, open to female participants since 1991).

### Opera, ballet, and theaters

Further information on the a cappella choir: [Zürcher Vokalisten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrcher_Vokalisten)



Opernhaus

The [Zurich Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Opera_House) (German: *Zürcher Opernhaus*), built in 1834, was the first permanent theatre in the heart of Zurich and was at the time, the main seat of [Richard Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Wagner)'s activities. Later in 1890, the theatre was re-built as an ornate building with a neo-classical architecture. The portico is made of white and grey stone ornamented with the busts of Wagner, Weber and Mozart. Later, busts of Schiller, Shakespeare and Goethe were also added. The auditorium is designed in the rococo style. Once a year, it hosts the *Zürcher Opernball* with the [President of the Swiss Confederation](33730924.md) and the economic and cultural elite of Switzerland.[^cite_note-137] The [Ballett Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballett_Z%C3%BCrich) performs at the opera house. The [Zurich Opera Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Opera_Ball), a major social event, is held annually at the Opera House as a fundraiser for the opera and ballet companies.

The [Schauspielhaus Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schauspielhaus_Z%C3%BCrich) is the main theatre complex of the city. It has two dépendances: *Pfauen* in the Central City District and *Schiffbauhalle*, an old industrial hall, in Zurich West. The Schauspielhaus was home to emigrants such as [Bertolt Brecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertolt_Brecht) or [Thomas Mann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Mann), and saw premieres of works of [Max Frisch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Frisch), [Friedrich Dürrenmatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_D%C3%BCrrenmatt), [Botho Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botho_Strau%C3%9F) and [Elfriede Jelinek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elfriede_Jelinek). The Schauspielhaus is one of the most prominent and important theatres in Switzerland.[^cite_note-138]

The [Theater am Neumarkt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_am_Neumarkt) is one of the oldest theatres of the city. Established by the old guilds in the Old City District, it is in a baroque palace near Niederdorf Street. It has two stages staging mostly avantgarde works by European directors.

The [Zürcher Theater Spektakel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrcher_Theater_Spektakel) is an international theatre festival, featuring contemporary [performing arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performing_arts).[^cite_note-139]

### Food

The traditional cuisine of Zurich reflects the centuries of rule by patrician burghers as well as the lasting imprint of [Huldrych Zwingli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huldrych_Zwingli)'s [puritanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puritan). Traditional dishes include *[Zürcher Geschnetzeltes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrcher_Geschnetzeltes)* and *[Tirggel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirggel)*. [Sternen Grill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sternen_Grill) is a popular restaurant that is known for its [St. Galler Bratwurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Galler_Bratwurst). Other notable restaurants in Zurich include the [Blindekuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blindekuh_\(restaurant\)) (dark dining), [Hiltl vegetarian restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiltl_Restaurant), [Kronenhalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kronenhalle) and [Ristorante Cooperativo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ristorante_Cooperativo). [Café & Conditorei 1842](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caf%C3%A9_&_Conditorei_1842) is the oldest café in the city.

### Nightlife and clubbing



Zurich at night

Zurich is host city of the [Street Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_Parade), which takes place in August every year (see above).

The most famous districts for Nightlife are the [Niederdorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niederdorf_district) in the old town with bars, restaurants, lounges, hotels, clubs, many fashion shops and the [Langstrasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langstrasse) in the districts 4 and 5 of the city. There are authentic amusements: bars, punk clubs, [hip hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop) stages, Caribbean restaurants, arthouse cinemas, Turkish kebabs and Italian espresso-bars, but also [sex shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_shop) and the [red-light district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red-light_district) of Zurich.

In recent years new parts of the city have risen into the spotlight. Notably, the area known as [Zurich West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_West) in district 5, near the Escher-Wyss square and the S-Bahn Station of [Zürich Hardbrücke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrich_Hardbr%C3%BCcke_railway_station).

## Sports



[FIFA Headquarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Headquarters)

Zurich is home to several international sport federations. The [Fédération Internationale de Football Association (FIFA)](11049.md) is headquartered in the city. In 2007 were inaugurated the new FIFA headquarters building, designed by architect Tilla Theus.

[Association football](10568.md) is an essential aspect of sports in Zurich. The city is home to two major rivaling Swiss [football teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_team); [Grasshopper Club Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grasshopper_Club_Zurich) founded in 1886 and [FC Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Z%C3%BCrich) founded in 1896, both competing in [Switzerland's highest league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Super_League).

Another popular sport in Switzerland is [ice hockey](14790.md). Zurich is represented by the [ZSC Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZSC_Lions). The [International Ice Hockey Federation (IIHF)](16760449.md) officiating as head organisation for ice hockey leagues worldwide is based in Zurich as well.

Cycling is a popular sport as well as a means of transport in Zurich. Cycling routes are generally marked with red and white signs and the yellow lanes are exclusively meant for cyclists. Also hiking trails are well marked with yellow signs, most of which give the hiker the probable time it will take them to reach their destination. There are specific maps available for hiking and walking trails throughout Switzerland. Some of the most accessible walks in the Zurich area are the Uetliberg and the Zürichberg. The Offene Rennbahn otherwise known as the [Oerlikon Velodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oerlikon_Velodrome) takes place on Tuesday evenings in the summer.

As many as 30 clubs and seven indoor curling facilities can be found in the Greater Zurich Area. The curling season starts in early September and continues until the end of April.[^cite_note-140]

Zurich is Switzerland's hub for [lacrosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacrosse). The Zurich Lions Lacrosse Academics have been the country's dominant team and a major competitor at international events.[^cite_note-141]

### Events



2007 Zurich Weltklasse

[Weltklasse Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weltklasse_Z%C3%BCrich), sometimes referred to as the one-day Olympics,[^cite_note-142] is a one-day athletics meet held annually at the [Letzigrund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Letzigrund) Stadium. Since it started on 12 August 1928, the sporting event has witnessed new world records and national records. To date as many as 24 world records were set in Weltklasse.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145]

Zurich Marathon is a popular sport event held in the city. The course starts in Zurich and passes through Bahnhofstrasse, [Bellevueplatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevueplatz), [Mythenquai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mythenquai), [Quaibrücke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaibr%C3%BCcke,_Zurich), Talstrasse and [Utoquai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utoquai), and along Lake Zurich. [New Year's Eve run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neujahrsmarathon_Z%C3%BCrich) is another notable running event held on 1 January and starts at midnight.

Zurich was one of six venues of the [1954 FIFA World Cup](60523.md) and one of eight venues of the [UEFA Euro 2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Euro_2008). The Euro 2008 games were held in the Letzigrund Stadium. Work on the new Letzigrund was completed in exceptionally quick time and the stadium opened in August 2007 just one year after the demolition of the old arena.[^cite_note-Swissinfo-Matthew-146]

Zurich has hosted the [UCI Track Cycling World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCI_Track_Cycling_World_Championships) six times at the Oerlikon Velodrome. The first event was in 1929 and most recently in 1983.

Since 2013, the international [Literaturfestival Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literaturfestival_Z%C3%BCrich) takes place annually in Zurich, presented by Literaturhaus Zürich and Kaufleuten.

Zurich hosted the [1998 Men's Ice Hockey World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Men's_Ice_Hockey_World_Championships). The city previously co-hosted the [1953](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953_Ice_Hockey_World_Championships) and [1939](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1939_Ice_Hockey_World_Championships) championships.

Zurich was also host to the [2012 Men's World Floorball Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Men's_World_Floorball_Championships). This was the first time the event had been held in the city.
