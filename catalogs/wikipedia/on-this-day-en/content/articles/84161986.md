**21 Air Flight 7598** was a scheduled domestic cargo flight operated by [21 Air](69248824.md) on behalf of [Amazon Air](56149507.md) from [Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Mu%C3%B1oz_Mar%C3%ADn_International_Airport) in [Carolina, Puerto Rico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina,_Puerto_Rico) to [Miami International Airport](128968.md) in [Miami-Dade County, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami-Dade_County,_Florida), United States. On September 6, 2026, the [Boeing 767](4165.md) freighter overran the runway while landing in Miami, striking multiple vehicles in the process. Five people were killed, while five others, including both pilots, sustained injuries.

The crash was the third and deadliest major accident involving a Boeing 767 aircraft flying for [Amazon](90451.md). It subsequently raised questions about the safety standards at 21 Air.[^cite_note-NPRconcerns-3]

## Background

The aircraft involved was a 32-year-old [Boeing 767-33AER(BDSF)](4165.md#Converted_freighters),[^cite_note-type-1] registered N1997A, operated by [21 Air](69248824.md) on behalf of [Amazon Air](56149507.md), and powered by two [General Electric CF6-80C2B6F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric_CF6#CF6-80C2) engines. The aircraft was originally ordered by aircraft lessor [AWAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AWAS_\(company\)) and was first delivered to Belgian carrier [Sobelair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sobelair) in 1994. It was converted to a freighter in 2016 by [Israel Aerospace Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Aerospace_Industries) under its "Bedek Special Freighter" (BDSF) program.[^cite_note-BloombergInfo2-4] The airframe was [written off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_loss) following the accident.[^cite_note-5]

The 55-year-old [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilot_in_command), Joseph Carroll, received his type rating in the 767 in May 2026 and previously flew as a US military helicopter pilot. [First officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_officer_\(aviation\)) Jaime Felipe Silva Molina received his type rating in April 2025.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

## Accident

On September 6, 2026, 21 Air Flight 7598 departed from [Luis Muñoz Marín International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Mu%C3%B1oz_Mar%C3%ADn_International_Airport) in Carolina, Puerto Rico, and arrived at [Miami International Airport](128968.md).[^cite_note-7] The aircraft overran runway 30 while landing around 1\:53 p.m. The plane first struck a [Ford Econoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_E-Series#Fourth_generation_\(1992%E2%80%93present\)) van carrying seven passengers working for an airline cleaning company. It then crashed through a fence and was crossing Northwest 67th Avenue when it struck a [Toyota Corolla Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_Corolla_Cross) SUV.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-NYTimesInfo-9][^cite_note-CNNStruck-10] A minor fire broke out as a result of the crash, which was soon extinguished. Part of the plane’s right wing was burned off and its fuselage was damaged by the fire. The last moments of the overrun and the plane coming to rest were captured on video. Five people were killed, and five others were injured, three of them critically.[^cite_note-BBC_2026-11][^cite_note-APIncident-12] All five fatalities were occupants of the Ford Econoline and employees of Professional Ocean Service Corporation, an airline cleaning contractor.[^cite_note-CNNStruck-10][^cite_note-13]

## Investigation

A [National Transportation Safety Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transportation_Safety_Board) (NTSB) investigation was opened on September 6, with media briefings released on September 7 and 8.[^cite_note-14] A preliminary report was released on October 1.[^cite_note-15]

Based on flight data recovered by the NTSB, a timeline of the accident sequence has been released. At 30 seconds before the end of the recording, the nosewheel and right main landing gear touched down at a [ground speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_speed) of 158 knots (182 mph; 293 km/h). At T-23 seconds, brakes were applied at a speed of 146 knots (168 mph; 270 km/h). At T-19, left main landing gear touched down at 134 knots (154 mph; 248 km/h). At T-15, brakes were released and engine throttles were increased to [go-around](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go-around) thrust at a speed of 120 knots (138 mph; 222 km/h). At T-11, throttles were reduced to idle and brakes were reapplied at a speed of 117 knots (135 mph; 217 km/h). At T-0, the data recording ended at a speed of 65 knots (75 mph; 120 km/h). The NTSB notes that neither [speed brakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_brake_\(aeronautics\)) or [thrust reversers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust_reversal) were deployed.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

A summary of the [cockpit voice recorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cockpit_voice_recorder) released by the NTSB indicated that one of the pilots cautioned that they were too fast. While the pilot made additional comments regarding the aircraft's excessive speed throughout the recording, there were no consistent acknowledgements from the other pilot. A pilot also called for a go around shortly before the end of the recording.[^cite_note-NTSB-NR20260909-16]

Weather conditions at the time were described as "light thunderstorms and gusting winds".[^cite_note-BBC-c0e3p1j7n3ro-17]

The NTSB later confirmed that the aircraft came to a stop approximately 1,300 feet (400 m) past the paved surface of the runway in a fenced parking lot.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Investigators described the scene as "utter devastation".[^cite_note-USATodayNTSB2026-20]

## Aftermath



NTSB investigators at the site of the 21 Air Flight 7598 accident at the Miami airport

The [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) issued a [ground stop](19108133.md) at the airport in response to the crash.[^cite_note-21] It was reported that some people were trapped in their vehicles.[^cite_note-22] The ground stop was rescinded at 5\:00 p.m. as the airport partially reopened, with a [ground delay program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_delay_program) remaining in effect.[^cite_note-23] According to website [FlightAware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FlightAware), more than 160 flights were canceled and nearly 325 others delayed as a result of the crash. Some flights were subsequently diverted to [Orlando International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orlando_International_Airport), more than 200 miles (320 km) north of Miami.[^cite_note-APIncident-12]

### Lawsuit

On September 9, 2026, [Morgan & Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_&_Morgan) filed a lawsuit in [Miami-Dade court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami-Dade_County_Courthouse) against [Amazon.com Inc](90451.md)., 21 Air LLC, and others, alleging criminal negligence that resulted in the death of Yoel Rodriguez Naranjo, one of those killed aboard the van. The legal action alleges that the use of a 32-year-old aircraft, pilot error, and insufficient training contributed to the accident.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Responses

On September 14, an Amazon spokesperson said that the company had decided to pause its operations with 21 Air.[^cite_note-NPRconcerns-3][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-APSuspending-28] Amazon, 21 Air, and Boeing expressed condolences and their readiness to cooperate with investigations.[^cite_note-BBC_2026-11]
