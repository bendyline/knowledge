**Instructure Holdings, Inc.** is an [educational technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Educational_technology) company based in [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City), Utah, United States. It is the developer and publisher of **Canvas**, a web-based [learning management system](1729908.md).

## History

The company was founded in 2008 by two [BYU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigham_Young_University) graduate students, Brian Whitmer and Devlin Daley.[^cite_note-5] Its initial funding came from [Mozy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mozy) founder Josh Coates, who served as Instructure's CEO from 2010 to 2018 and chairman of the board through 2020.[^cite_note-6]

In December 2010, the [Utah Education Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utah_Education_Network) (UEN), a representative of some Utah colleges and universities, announced that Instructure would be replacing [Blackboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackboard_Inc.).[^cite_note-7] By 2013, the company's customer base had increased to at least 9 million users.[^cite_note-tc-8]

In 2011, Instructure launched Canvas, a learning management system.[^cite_note-9] The company announced that Canvas would be made freely available under an [Affero General Public License](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affero_General_Public_License) (AGPL) license as [open-source software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_software). However, the company continued to sell Canvas management [as a service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_as_a_service).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Canvas became available on [iOS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOS) in 2011, and on [Android](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(operating_system\)) in 2013.[^cite_note-13]

In 2015, Instructure launched Bridge, a cloud-based corporate learning management system.[^cite_note-14] It was acquired by Learning Technologies Group (LTG) in 2021.[^cite_note-15]

As of 2015, the company had raised \$90 million in funding from investors.[^cite_note-Locke,_Charley-16] On November 13, 2015, the firm began trading as a publicly held company on the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md).[^cite_note-Schaffler,_Rhonda-17]

In 2017, the company acquired Philadelphia-based video learning startup [Practice XYZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practice_XYZ), formerly known as [ApprenNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Practice_XYZ), and merged the offerings into its own products.[^cite_note-Tech1-18][^cite_note-19]

In 2020, [Thoma Bravo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thoma_Bravo) acquired the company for \$2 billion.[^cite_note-20] As of 2020, Canvas was used in approximately 4,000 institutions worldwide.[^cite_note-21] In June 2021, Instructure again filed for an IPO,[^cite_note-22] and began trading under the symbol INST.[^cite_note-23]

In 2024, it was announced that [KKR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KKR_&_Co.) and [Dragoneer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragoneer) had completed their \$4.8 billion purchase of the company.[^cite_note-kkr-acquisition-2]

Later in 2024, Instructure announced the acquisition of Parchment, a credential management platform.[^cite_note-24]

### 2026 Canvas security breach

Main article: [2026 Canvas data breach](83130365.md)

In late April 2026, Canvas LMS experienced a security breach, with an outage following in early May.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-Instructure52026-26]The hack is considered the largest educational security breach on record due to its unprecedented global scale, affecting 8,809 universities, educational ministries, and other institutions worldwide.[^cite_note-27] Instructure disclosed that it was investigating a cybersecurity attack involving certain user data, including names, email addresses, student ID numbers, and messages amongst users.[^cite_note-Instructure52026-26] The company claimed it had found no evidence that passwords, birth dates, government IDs, or financial information were involved in the hacking.[^cite_note-ABC-News-2026-28] However, other sources indicate that this cybersecurity attack has put 275 million[^cite_note-29] students' and teachers' data at risk.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

Despite Instructure's claim that the situation had been resolved, on May 7th, Canvas was hacked again; its login page was replaced with a [ransomware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ransomware) message by [ShinyHunters](66491058.md), the criminal hacking group that claimed responsibility. ShinyHunters threatened to release Canvas's sensitive data unless its ransom was paid by the end of May 12.[^cite_note-Hollingsworth-2026-33][^cite_note-Halbleib-2026-34]

*[404 Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/404_Media)* stated this was the largest educational security breach ever on record.[^cite_note-m404-35] ShinyHunters claimed to have stolen 3.65 [terabytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terabytes) of data (approximately 275 million records).[^cite_note-36]

By May 8, seven lawsuits in federal courts had been filed against Instructure, including one that also named KKR as a defendant.[^cite_note-37] On May 13, a California-specific proposed class action lawsuit was filed by a San Diego resident against Instructure in the [Southern District of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Southern_District_of_California), alleging the breach of [personally identifiable information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personally_Identifiable_Information).[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]
