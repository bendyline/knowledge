**Arequipa** ([Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_language): *Ariqipa*) is a [department of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Peru). Located to the country's southwest, it is the sixth largest department in Peru, after [Puno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Puno), [Cuzco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Cuzco), [Madre de Dios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Madre_de_Dios), [Ucayali](715608.md), and [Loreto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Loreto), its sixth most populous department, and its eleventh least densely populated department. It is bordered by the departments of [Ica](713288.md), [Ayacucho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayacucho_Region), [Apurímac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apur%C3%ADmac_Region) and Cuzco to the north, [Puno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Puno) to the east, the [Department of Moquegua](714958.md) to the south, and the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) to the west. It is administered by a [regional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Government_of_Arequipa). Its capital, also called [Arequipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arequipa), is Peru's second-largest city.

## History

The department was created by the *Reglamento Provisional de Elecciones*, published on April 26, 1822, which established the department of Arequipa alongside those of [Cuzco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Cusco), [Huamanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Ayacucho), [Huancavelica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Huancavelica) and [Puno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Puno).[^cite_note-1] Prior to this establishment, it had been part of the [Intendancy of Arequipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intendancy_of_Arequipa), an administrative division of the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru).

Following the [civil war of 1856–1858](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peruvian_Civil_War_of_1856%E2%80%931858), due to the department having served as a starting point for the rebellion against the government at the time, then president [Ramón Castilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Castilla) downgraded Arequipa to a province through two decrees expedited on March 12 and 14, 1858. On May 13 of the same year, however, he made a request to the [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Peru) that the department, its prefecture, superior court and administrative offices, be reestablished.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBasadre2005Volume289-2]

In 1980, the Peruvian Air Force responded to a [UFO incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_reported_UFO_sightings#1975%E2%80%931999) over Arequipa.[^cite_note-3]

This department has a rough topography, which is characterised by heavy layers of [volcanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano) [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) covering large areas of its inter-[Andean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean) sector. It has deep canyons such as the ones formed by the [Ocoña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oco%C3%B1a_River) and Majes rivers. [Plateaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plateaus) range in height from medium, such as La Joya, and high-altitude ones such the Arrieros Pampa and those located in the zones of Chivay, Huambo and Pichucolla. Volcanic cones, such as [Misti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Misti), [Chachani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chachani), [Ampato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampato), [Mismi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevado_Mismi), [Solimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solimana_\(volcano\)) and [Coropuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coropuna), emerge above the plateaus and attract snowfall. In contrast with these heights, there are deep [canyons](78478.md), including Majes, [Colca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colca_Canyon), Sihuas and Ocoña, where important aspects of the [ecological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecology) evolution of this zone can be clearly observed.

Along the coast, small plateaus and [dunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dune) represent characteristics of the desert of Arequipa, such as the ones located in the Majes, Sihuas and La Joya plains. These are particularly beautiful and developed.

From a [hydrographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrography) point of view, the rivers draining its territory belong mainly to the Pacific watershed. Some rivers belong to the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River) hydrographic system. Some of the region's main rivers are: Ocoña, Yauca, Camaná and Quilca. The starting point of the [Amazon River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River) is located in the Arequipa region.

### Languages

According to the [2007 Peru Census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Peru_Census), the first language learned by most of the residents was [Spanish](26825.md) (83.17%), followed by the native language of [Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechua_language) (14.78%). The Quechua variety spoken in Arequipa is [Cusco–Collao Quechua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cusco%E2%80%93Collao_Quechua).

There are numerous points of interest in the Arequipa region. The three coastal provinces, Caraveli, Camana and Islay all have popular beaches. Various ports can also be found along the coastline, the two most important being [Mollendo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mollendo) and [Matarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matarani), both in the province of Islay.

The [Colca Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colca_Canyon), twice as deep as the [Grand Canyon](46989.md) in the United States, is situated in the province of Caylloma. The [Cotahuasi Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotahuasi_Canyon) is in the La Union Province. Colca Valley provides incredible close-up views of majestic [Andean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) [condors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condor) soaring in their natural [habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_\(ecology\)). Cotahuasi, at 3,535 metres (11,598 ft), is thought to be the deepest canyon in the world. Both canyons offer spectacular scenery and villages as yet unaffected by the modern world.

In the Castilla Province near Corire, [Toro Muerto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toro_Muerto) (meaning "dead bull" in Spanish) is an area with more than 3,000 ancient [petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph) dating to the ancient [Wari culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wari_culture) of 500–1000 CE, which dominated much of this region. Further to the north near Andaguas lies the Valley of Volcanoes. Almost 100 cones of various sizes dominate the lava-hardened landscape.
