The **Toronto Blue Jays** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Blue Jays de Toronto*) are a Canadian professional [baseball](3850.md) team based in [Toronto](64646.md). The Blue Jays compete in [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB) as a member club of the [American League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League) (AL) [East Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League_East). Since 1989, the team has played its home games primarily at [Rogers Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Centre) in [downtown Toronto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Toronto).

The name "Blue Jays" originates from the [blue jay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_jay) bird, and blue is also the [traditional colour of Toronto's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Toronto#Toronto's_association_with_the_colour_blue) [collegiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U_of_T_Blue) and [professional sports teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_teams_of_the_United_States_and_Canada) including the [Maple Leafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs) ([ice hockey](14790.md)) and the [Argonauts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Argonauts) ([Canadian football](6963.md)). In 1976, out of the over 4,000 suggestions, 154 people selected the name "Blue Jays."[^cite_note-7] In addition, the team was originally owned by the [Labatt Brewing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labatt_Brewing_Company), makers of the popular beer Labatt Blue. Colloquially nicknamed the "**Jays**," the team's official colours are royal blue, white, red, and navy blue.[^cite_note-NewBlueJaysLogo2011-1][^cite_note-BlueJaysLogos-2]

An [expansion franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansion_team), the club was founded in Toronto in 1977. Originally based at [Exhibition Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_Stadium), the team began playing its home games at SkyDome upon its opening in 1989. They are the second MLB franchise to be based outside the United States, and the only one remaining since the first Canadian franchise, the [Montreal Expos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Expos), became the [Washington Nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Nationals) in 2005. Since 2000, the Blue Jays have been owned by [Rogers Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Communications) and in 2004, SkyDome was purchased by that company, which renamed it Rogers Centre. The Blue Jays and the [Atlanta Braves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Braves)[^cite_note-8] are the only two MLB teams wholly under corporate ownership; the Blue Jays are the only American League team to be under such ownership.&#x20;

Due to border restrictions brought about by the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), the Blue Jays played home games at [TD Ballpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TD_Ballpark) in [Dunedin, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin,_Florida), for April and May of the [2021 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Major_League_Baseball_season), and [Sahlen Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahlen_Field) in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York), for the [2020 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Major_League_Baseball_season) as well as June and July 2021, returning home to Toronto on July 30 of that year.

In the late 1970s and early 1980s, the Blue Jays went through struggles typical of an expansion team, frequently finishing last in their division. In 1983, they had their [first winning season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season) and two years later, became [division champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season). From 1985 to 1993, the Blue Jays were an AL East powerhouse, winning five division championships in nine seasons, including three consecutive from [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season) to [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season). During that run, the team also became back-to-back [World Series](23489161.md) champions in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_World_Series) and [1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_World_Series), led by a core group of [award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Major_League_Baseball_awards)-winning [All-Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_All-Star_Game) players, including [Hall of Famer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Baseball_Hall_of_Fame_and_Museum) [Roberto Alomar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Alomar), [Joe Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Carter), [John Olerud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Olerud), and [Devon White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devon_White_\(baseball\)). The Blue Jays became the first (and, to date, only) team based outside the U.S. to appear in and win a World Series and the fastest AL expansion team to do so, winning in their 16th year.

Following their championship seasons, the Blue Jays failed to qualify for the playoffs for 21 consecutive seasons until clinching a playoff berth and division championship in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season). The team clinched a second consecutive playoff berth in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season), after securing an [AL wild card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_American_League_Wild_Card_winners) position. In both years, the Blue Jays beat the [Texas Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Rangers_\(baseball\)) in the [AL Division Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League_Division_Series), but lost the [AL Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League_Championship_Series). The Blue Jays qualified for the playoffs four times in six seasons, in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season), [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season), [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season), and [2025](79597307.md). In 2025 they made it to the World Series for the third time, which they lost to the defending champion [Los Angeles Dodgers](18213.md).

From 1977 to 2025, the Blue Jays' overall win-loss record is 3,855–3,856–3 (.500).[^cite_note-9]

## History

The Toronto Blue Jays came into existence in 1976[^cite_note-10] as one of two teams slated to join the [American League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League) for the following season via the [1977 Major League Baseball expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Major_League_Baseball_expansion). [Toronto](64646.md) had been mentioned as a potential major league city as early as the 1880s and been home to the [Toronto Maple Leafs baseball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs_\(International_League\)) of the [International League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_League) from 1896 to 1967. In January 1976, the [San Francisco Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Giants) nearly relocated to Toronto after owner [Horace Stoneham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horace_Stoneham) agreed to sell the team to a Canadian consortium. The group, which included [Labatt Breweries of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labatt_Breweries_of_Canada), *[The Globe and Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Globe_and_Mail)*'s Howard Webster, and the [Canadian Imperial Bank of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Imperial_Bank_of_Commerce) (CIBC), planned to rebrand the team as the Toronto Giants and play at [Exhibition Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_Stadium). However, a court ruling halted the move, and the Giants remained in San Francisco. Despite this setback, Toronto's ambition for an MLB team persisted, leading to their successful bid in the 1976 American League expansion, driven by a need to balance the league after Seattle was granted a team as a result of a lawsuit over their loss of the Pilots.

The new Toronto franchise, purchased for \$7 million, was named the Toronto Blue Jays following a contest that attracted over 4,000 suggestions. The name reflected Toronto's tradition of using blue in team colours and was influenced by majority owner Labatt Breweries' flagship beer, Labatt Blue. The franchise's first employee, [Paul Beeston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Beeston), began as vice president of business operations, and before the inaugural 1977 season, [Peter Bavasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Bavasi) and [Pat Gillick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Gillick) were appointed as president and assistant general manager, respectively. The Blue Jays debuted on April 7, 1977, with a win against the [Chicago White Sox](5945.md) amid a snowstorm, marking the beginning of a journey from early struggles to eventual success. Throughout the late 1970s and early 1980s, the Blue Jays showed gradual improvement, highlighted by their first winning season in 1983. The team's fortunes rose significantly under manager [Bobby Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Cox) in 1985 when they won their first American League East title. The late 1980s and early 1990s, under manager [Cito Gaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cito_Gaston), were particularly successful, with the Blue Jays winning multiple division titles and back-to-back [World Series](23489161.md) championships in 1992 and 1993, making them the first team outside the US to achieve this feat. Key players during this golden era included [Roberto Alomar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Alomar), [Joe Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Carter), and [Dave Stieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Stieb).

After the mid-1990s strike and subsequent downturn, the Blue Jays faced challenges but also saw the rise of talents like [Roy Halladay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Halladay) and [Carlos Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Delgado). The late 1990s brought brief revitalization with the acquisition of [Roger Clemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Clemens). In the early 2000s, general manager [J. P. Ricciardi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._P._Ricciardi) led a rebuilding phase, culminating in a competitive roster by the mid-2000s. The team's resurgence in the 2010s featured playoff appearances in 2015 and 2016, driven by stars like [José Bautista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Bautista) and [Josh Donaldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Donaldson). The Blue Jays continue to build for future success, with young talents like [Vladimir Guerrero Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Guerrero_Jr.), [Bo Bichette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bo_Bichette), and [Cavan Biggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavan_Biggio) leading the charge, though Biggio was later traded in 2024, and Bichette would leave through [free agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_agent).

### 2025 season

Main article: [2025 Toronto Blue Jays season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season)

In 1977, after just 50 home games, the Blue Jays set an MLB record for a first-year expansion team, with an overall attendance of 1,219,551 during those games.[^cite_note-16] By the end of the season, 1,701,152 fans had attended. After setting an attendance record in 1990, with 3,885,284 fans, in 1991, the Blue Jays became the first MLB team to attract over [four million fans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_Baseball_attendance_records), with an attendance of 4,001,526, followed by 4,028,318 in 1992.[^cite_note-history-17] Each of those records were broken in 1993 by the expansion [Colorado Rockies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Colorado_Rockies_season), although the Blue Jays' 1993 attendance of 4,057,947 stood as an AL record for 12 years until it was broken by the [2005 New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_New_York_Yankees_season).[^cite_note-18]

Several Blue Jays became very popular in Toronto and throughout the major leagues, starting with [Dave Stieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Stieb), whose seven All-Star selections is a franchise record. He is closely followed by [Roy Halladay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Halladay) and [José Bautista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Bautista), who were selected six times each, and by [Roberto Alomar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Alomar) and [Joe Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Carter), who were selected five times each. Bautista set a major league record in 2011 (which only stood for a year), with 7,454,753 All-Star votes.[^cite_note-19] In his first season with the Blue Jays in 2015, [Josh Donaldson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Donaldson) set a new major league record by receiving 14,090,188 All-Star votes.[^cite_note-20]

The team is popular throughout Canada, as the only MLB team based in the country following the relocation of the Montreal Expos to Washington. ## Culture



Ace and Junior exchange greetings before the game. Ace was the Blue Jays' second mascot, introduced in 2000. Junior is a mascot occasionally seen for Junior Jays day promotions.

### "OK Blue Jays"

Main article: [OK Blue Jays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OK_Blue_Jays)

During the [seventh-inning stretch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh-inning_stretch) of home games, before singing "[Take Me Out to the Ball Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Take_Me_Out_to_the_Ball_Game)," Blue Jay fans sing and clap to the pop song "[OK Blue Jays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OK_Blue_Jays)" by [Keith Hampshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Hampshire) and The Bat Boys, which was released in 1983. The song was remixed in 2003, and since then, the new, shortened version has been played at home games as a [fight song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fight_song).

### "Let's Go Blue Jays"

At home games, the "Let's Go Blue Jays" chant is played four times followed by "[Charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charge_\(fanfare\))" being played three times.

### Mascots

Main article: [Toronto Blue Jays mascots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Blue_Jays_mascots)

From 1979 to 1999, BJ Birdy served as the Blue Jays' sole mascot, played by Kevin Shanahan. In 2000, he was replaced by a duo named Ace and Diamond. After the 2003 season, Diamond was removed by the team, leaving Ace as the team's sole mascot. Since the 2010s, Ace has been accompanied by his younger brother, Junior. This usually happens on the Jr. Jay Saturday promotions until the end of the [2017 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season). The promotions were moved to select Sundays since the [2018 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season) since the Blue Jays can no longer hold early Saturday afternoon games to accommodate [American national broadcasts on Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_on_Fox), though Fox did occasionally broadcast Blue Jays games at the Rogers Centre.

### Sunday Salute

Since 2012, every Sunday home game, the Blue Jays pay tribute to a member of the [Canadian Armed Forces](182792.md). During the third inning, the team presents the honoured member with a personalized jersey.[^cite_note-canada-45]

### National anthems

Since 2005, "[The Star-Spangled Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Star-Spangled_Banner)" has been sung before "[O Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Canada)" at every home game as all of the Blue Jays' opponents are based in the United States. In some home games (including Canada Day home games), "O Canada" is sung in English and French to reflect Canada's bilingual and multicultural heritage and to represent the diverse population of the country. When "O Canada" was sung during the Home Opener, Canada Day, playoff games, and World Series, a giant Canadian flag was presented by members of the [Canadian Armed Forces](182792.md). Due to time constraints and broadcast scheduling, Sportsnet only airs the national anthems during the home opener, Canada Day, playoff games, and World Series because a commercial break occurs during the anthems. On June 29, 2019, "O Canada" was sung in [Cree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cree_language) and English.[^cite_note-46] On September 30, 2021, the [National Day for Truth and Reconciliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Day_for_Truth_and_Reconciliation), "O Canada" was sung in English, French, and [Anishinaabemowin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anishinaabemowin). On the following National Day for Truth and Reconciliation, September 30, 2022, "O Canada" was sung in French, English, and [Blackfoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackfoot_language). For Blue Jays road games, "O Canada" is sung before the "Star-Spangled Banner" as all road games (since the Expos moved to Washington, DC) for the Blue Jays are in the United States.

### Canada Day

The Blue Jays traditionally host an afternoon home game during [Canada Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Day). During the game, the team wears red jerseys instead of blue jerseys. During the pre-game ceremony, a giant Canadian flag is presented by members of the [Canadian Armed Forces](182792.md) while "O Canada" is sung in English and French by a member of the [Royal Canadian Air Force Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Canadian_Air_Force_Band) or military personnel. The game was cancelled in 2020 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md). In 2021, the game was held in [Buffalo, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York) due to Canada–U.S. travel restrictions.

### Fan Appreciation Weekend

Fan Appreciation Weekend traditionally falls on the last weekend of September marking the team's final home games.

## Uniforms

### 1977–1988: Pullovers and powder blue

The Blue Jays wore pullover uniforms during their first decade of existence. The front of the home white uniforms contained the team name in a unique blue/white/blue split-letter style, with the team logo centred below. The road uniforms were powder blue, with the city name in front and the team logo centred below. Initially, the city name was rendered in bold blue letters before gaining a white outline the following season. By 1979, it was replaced by the team name in a split-lettered white/blue/white style. Player numerals also used the split-letter style, except on the road uniform during its first two seasons. Player names in blue were added to both uniforms for the 1980 season but were dropped from the road uniform in 1981. The caps were blue with the Blue Jays logo on a white panel in front.[^cite_note-originaljays-47]

In 2008, the Blue Jays' powder blue road uniform from this era was restored as an alternate home uniform worn every Friday until 2010.[^cite_note-48]

### 1989–1996: Championship blues

The Blue Jays adopted buttoned uniforms upon moving to [Rogers Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Centre) (then SkyDome) in 1989. Aside from the additions of buttons and belts, the only change affecting the home uniforms was the relocation of the team logo to the left chest. The road uniforms changed from powder blue to grey, while the city name and numerals in blue/white/blue split letters were emblazoned, and the logo moved to the left chest. Player names were also added to the road uniform. All-blue caps were worn with their road uniform while keeping the white-panelled blue caps at home. By 1993, the all-blue caps were worn universally, supplanting and eventually retiring the original cap design.[^cite_note-originaljays-47] The full-time switch to all-blue caps came out of superstition. After the Blue Jays lost five consecutive games of a road trip midway through the 1993 season, they decided to abandon the white-panelled caps for home games in favour of the road all-blue caps, and promptly won their first home game back, en route to winning their second consecutive World Series championship.[^cite_note-TORWhitePanels-49]

In 1994 the Blue Jays began wearing blue alternate uniforms with the team name and numerals in white/blue/white split letters.[^cite_note-originaljays-47]

### 1997–2003: Red, teal and blue

The Blue Jays updated their logo prior to the 1997 season with a new bird design and an enlarged red maple leaf at the back. The usage of red was greatly increased on the team's new uniforms. On the home uniforms, the letters and numerals were changed to blue/teal/blue split letters, while road uniform letters and numerals were changed to blue/red/blue split letters. On the alternate blue uniforms, split letters and numerals became red/blue/red. Red also appeared on the pant and sleeve stripes, while the new logo occupied the left sleeve. Player names also took on the new block split-letter style. An updated all-blue cap was paired with the home and road uniforms, while a red-brimmed blue cap (with a modified logo without a baseball) was used with the blue alternates.[^cite_note-redjays-50]

In 1999 the Blue Jays unveiled an alternate sleeveless white uniform, featuring the same lettering style as the regular home uniform. However, the chest numerals were replaced with the primary logo. Blue undershirts were worn with this uniform.[^cite_note-redjays-50]

Before the 2001 season, slight modifications were made to the uniforms, eliminating the tricolour stripes and adding a single colour piping along the chest and neck. While the home uniforms remained mostly intact, the road uniforms gained blue sleeves in a faux-vest design. On the alternate white uniform (now a faux vest instead of a straight sleeveless design), the new "T-bird" logo replaced the primary "jay leaf" logo, which moved to the left sleeve. In 2003, the "T-bird" logo became the primary, taking over the previous logo's placement on the caps and sleeves, while the alternate white uniforms brought back chest numerals.[^cite_note-redjays-50] The "T-bird" logo depicts a blue jay flexing its biceps and is nicknamed "Muscle Jay."

### 2004–2011: The "Black-and-Graphite Jays"

Before the 2004 season, the Blue Jays adopted a new visual identity, going with a black, silver and graphite motif. The home and black alternates simply read "Jays" in front and in a 3D-oriented diagonal arrangement, with the bird connected to the letter "J." Letters and numerals were in graphite with light blue and silver trim. The road uniforms featured the city name in a similar letter style as the logo, with graphite letters and numerals trimmed in light blue and silver. In 2008, however, amid complaints of illegibility, the Blue Jays tweaked their road uniforms to include 3D-style block letters and numerals in light blue trimmed in black and white, along with chest numerals. The "J-bird" alternate was added to the left sleeve. A red maple leaf would be added on the right sleeve starting in 2009. The Blue Jays wore all-black caps with the "J-bird" logo for much of the uniforms' existence, save for the 2004 and 2005 seasons when they wore all-graphite caps at home, and in 2007 when an alternate all-black cap with the "T" from the previous road uniform was used.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

### 2012–present: Return to traditional look

Prior to the 2012 season, the Blue Jays unveiled new uniforms and a new logo. The logo is a modernized version of the original logo used from 1977 to 1996. The bird's head was also made sleeker than its 1977–1996 predecessor. The uniforms are similar to the ones used from 1989 to 1996, the team's most successful era. New serifed split letters were also released.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] In 2015, the Blue Jays began wearing a modernized version of the white-panelled blue caps they originally wore from 1977 to 1993 as an alternate.[^cite_note-55] Initially, the modern white-panelled caps were worn occasionally from 2015 to 2022, but after a two-year hiatus, it was brought back for three of the Blue Jays' final four home games in 2025.[^cite_note-56] The return of the white-panelled caps was suggested by Blue Jays pitcher [Jeff Hoffman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Hoffman) after the team lost six of their last seven games amid a heated division race with the [New York Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_New_York_Yankees_season). Consequently, the Blue Jays finished the season winning the final four games, earning the division title.[^cite_note-TORWhitePanels-49]

Before the 2020 season, the Blue Jays unveiled a modernized version of the powder blue uniforms, featuring navy/white/navy serifed split letters and numerals. These uniforms are paired with a powder-brimmed navy cap and an all-navy helmet.[^cite_note-57]

### Canada Day uniforms

Since 1996, the Blue Jays wore predominantly red or red-accented uniforms every July 1, [Canada Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Day). The uniforms were based on the team's alternate uniforms they wore at the time but with red as the primary colour. On a few occasions, the Blue Jays added red trim to an existing white uniform (or, in the case of the 2006 uniforms, their black alternates) and sometimes added the [flag of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Canada) or a red maple leaf on the uniform.[^cite_note-58]

### City Connect uniform

In 2024, the Blue Jays unveiled a [City Connect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Connect) uniform inspired by Toronto's nightlife. The jersey is nicknamed "Night Mode" and is a "pitch blue" (dark blue) uniform that features an illustration of the Toronto skyline in royal blue, centred by the "Toronto" wordmark in red. The "pitch blue" colour is a reference to [Lake Ontario](17947.md)'s reflection at night. The traditional Blue Jays logo patch on the sleeve is recoloured to match the uniform. The cap is also "pitch blue" and features a red [maple leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_leaf) at the centre of a stylized red and blue "T" in split letters.[^cite_note-59]

## Rivalries

### Detroit Tigers

The [Detroit Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Tigers) are the Blue Jays' geographic and traditional rival, dating back to the 1980s, when the teams were AL East contenders. The Tigers moved to the [AL Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League_Central) in 1998, and the rivalry has died down as a result, with the teams facing each other only six to seven times per year since 2011.[^cite_note-60] Depending on traffic and border delays, Detroit is about a four-hour drive from Toronto via [Highway 401](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Highway_401). According to *[The Detroit News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Detroit_News)*, a July 2017 three-game series at [Comerica Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comerica_Park) against the Blue Jays drew a season-best-to-date total attendance of 115,088.[^cite_note-61]

### Seattle Mariners

Although the [Seattle Mariners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Mariners) are not a divisional rival, many Blue Jays fans from Western Canada travel to Seattle when the Blue Jays play there as Seattle is geographically closer to Western Canada than Toronto is. Depending on traffic and border delays, Seattle is about a three-hour drive from [Vancouver](32706.md). *[The Seattle Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Seattle_Times)* estimated that Blue Jays fans represented around 70 percent of the crowd in [Safeco Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safeco_Field) for a June 2017 weekend series.[^cite_note-62] Additionally, both teams joined the league at the same time in the [1977 Major League Baseball expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Major_League_Baseball_expansion). In 2025, the teams met in a dramatic [American League Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_American_League_Championship_Series) (ALCS), with the Blue Jays defeating the Mariners in seven games.[^cite_note-63]

### Montreal Expos (historic)

Main article: [Pearson Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_Cup)

The [Montreal Expos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Expos) were the Blue Jays' geographic [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_\(baseball\)) rival, being the other Canadian MLB team before it was relocated. From 1978 to 1986, the teams played an annual mid-season exhibition game known as the [Pearson Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearson_Cup), named after former [Prime Minister](24135.md) [Lester B. Pearson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_B._Pearson). The teams began facing each other in the regular season in 1997 with the advent of [interleague play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interleague_play). During the 2003 and 2004 seasons, the Expos' last two seasons before relocating to Washington, D.C., as the [Nationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Nationals), the Pearson Cup was awarded after a pair of three-game sets.

### Radio

See also: [Toronto Blue Jays Radio Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Blue_Jays_Radio_Network)

The Blue Jays' former radio play-by-play announcer, [Tom Cheek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cheek), called every Toronto Blue Jays game from the team's inaugural contest on April 7, 1977, until June 3, 2004, when he took two games off following the death of his father—a streak of 4,306 consecutive regular-season games and 41 postseason games. Cheek later died on October 9, 2005, and the team commemorated him during their 2006 season by wearing a circular patch on the left sleeve of their home and road game jerseys. The patch was adorned with the letters 'TC', Cheek's initials, as well as a stylized [microphone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microphone). Cheek is also honoured with a place in the Blue Jays' "Level of Excellence" in the upper level of the Rogers Centre; the number 4,306 is depicted beside his name. In 2008, Cheek received the third most votes from fans to be nominated for the [Ford C. Frick Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_C._Frick_Award) for broadcasting excellence. Cheek finally received the Frick Award posthumously in 2013 after nine years on the ballot.[^cite_note-64]

Radio broadcasts of Blue Jays games originate from [Sportsnet 590 CJCL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJCL) in Toronto, which, like the Blue Jays, is owned by Rogers Communications. After Cheek's retirement in 2005, [Jerry Howarth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Howarth), who had been Cheek's broadcasting partner since 1982, took over as lead play-by-play announcer, with [Mike Wilner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Wilner) as the secondary play-by-play announcer. During the 2007 to 2012 seasons, former Blue Jays catcher [Alan Ashby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Ashby) was the colour commentator. Former Blue Jays pitcher [Jack Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Morris) served as the [colour commentator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_commentator) during the 2013 season, after which he was replaced by former Montreal Expos catcher [Joe Siddall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Siddall) since the 2014 season.

Former Blue Jays pitcher [Dirk Hayhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirk_Hayhurst) filled in for Morris for some games during the 2013 season.

Another former catcher for the Blue Jays, [Gregg Zaun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Zaun), has served as the occasional colour commentator from the [2011 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season) until the end of the [2017 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Toronto_Blue_Jays_season) when he was terminated amid accusations of improper conduct from several female employees.[^cite_note-65]

Following Howarth's retirement in the 2017 season, [Ben Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Wagner) was hired as the primary radio play-by-play announcer, splitting said duties with [Dan Shulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shulman) and Mike Wilner. After Dan moved exclusively to television in 2023, his son [Ben Shulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Shulman) was eventually brought in to do select games before becoming the primary radio announcer as of 2024. Ben is paired with [Chris Leroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Leroux) on the Blue Jays radio broadcasts.

In November 2020, Mike Wilner was laid off by the team.[^cite_note-66] In February 2021, it was announced that "in an effort to minimize travel and closely adhere to team, league, and government protocols related to the [pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Canada)", all radio broadcasts for the [2021 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Major_League_Baseball_season) will be a simulcast of the television broadcast. Wagner will assume an alternative role.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] However, once the Blue Jays returned to Rogers Centre in late July 2021, dedicated radio broadcasts resumed.

The Blue Jays have the largest geographical home market in all of baseball, encompassing all of Canada. Despite this, the number of radio stations that broadcast games is actually quite small. Only 18 radio stations across the country aired at least some Blue Jays games during the 2021 season,[^cite_note-69] which is fewer affiliates than most MLB teams, which have more stations covering smaller geographic areas.

### Television

All Blue Jays games are carried nationally on [Sportsnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsnet) (which, like the Blue Jays, is owned by Rogers Communications). As of 2026, [Dan Shulman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Shulman) serves as the lead play-by-play announcer, with [Joe Siddall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Siddall) as the primary colour commentator; on select series, [Caleb Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caleb_Joseph) and [Madison Shipman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Shipman) serve as alternate colour commentators.[^cite_note-TBJannouncers-70] From 1987 to 2000 and from 2010 to 2025, [Buck Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_Martinez) had been part of the broadcast team, both as a primary play-by-play announcer and colour commentator.[^cite_note-71] During his tenure as a play-by-play announcer, Martinez was paired alongside colour commentator [Pat Tabler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Tabler), with Shulman only calling games sporadically from 2016 until 2022 due to his [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN_Major_League_Baseball) assignments in the United States. In previous years, the colour analyst role rotated between Pat Tabler, [Rance Mulliniks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rance_Mulliniks), [Darrin Fletcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darrin_Fletcher), and from 2011 to 2017, [Gregg Zaun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Zaun). Sportsnet became the team's primary carrier soon after it launched in the late 1990s and became the team's exclusive broadcaster in 2010.[^cite_note-72] As of August 2010, [Sportsnet One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsnet_One) also broadcasts Blue Jays games (often in case of scheduling conflicts with the main Sportsnet channels). Rogers was, however, criticized by fans and critics due to Sportsnet One only being carried by [Rogers Cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Cable) systems on launch.[^cite_note-73]

Sportsnet's broadcasts of the [2015 American League Division Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_American_League_Division_Series) involving the Blue Jays were among the highest-rated telecasts in network history, with Game 4 drawing an audience of 4.38 million viewers.[^cite_note-yahoo-jaysrecord-74]



[Jamie Campbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_Campbell_\(sportscaster\)) and [Gregg Zaun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregg_Zaun) providing [Sportsnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportsnet) coverage of a Toronto Blue Jays game in 2011

In September 2012, [AMI-tv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMI-tv) simulcast three Blue Jays games with [described video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Descriptive_Video_Service) provided by CJCL correspondent [Sam Cosentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Cosentino), which included explanations of on-screen graphics. Paul Beeston praised AMI's involvement, stating that "to our knowledge, we are the first sports organization to have our games provided through this revolutionary approach to accommodating the needs of the blind and low-vision community."[^cite_note-jays-amitv-75]

On June 27, 2013, Rogers' over-the-air Toronto multicultural [Omni Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omni_Television) station [CJMT-DT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CJMT-DT) simulcast a Blue Jays game, scheduled to be started by Taiwanese player [Chien-Ming Wang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chien-Ming_Wang), with commentary in [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese), marking the first ever Canadian MLB broadcast in the language.[^cite_note-omni-bluejays-76][^cite_note-bc-omnijays-77] In June 2018, Omni announced that it would air Sunday afternoon games in [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language), the most spoken [language of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Philippines), through the remainder of the season.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] Sportsnet and Omni announced a regular season of Sunday broadcasts in Tagalog for the 2019 season.[^cite_note-80]

[TVA Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TVA_Sports) has aired games in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) since 2011, with Denis Casavant and François Paquet on play-by-play and [Rodger Brulotte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodger_Brulotte) on colour. The channel currently has rights to 81 Blue Jay games per season in a three-year deal signed in 2023.[^cite_note-81] [Jacques Doucet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Doucet_\(sportscaster\)), former Montreal Expos radio announcer, broadcast the Blue Jays on TVA Sports from 2011 until his retirement in 2022.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

[The Sports Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sports_Network) (TSN), which (like the Jays) was owned by Labatt from 1984 to 1995, served as the primary cable television outlet for the Blue Jays prior to the launch of Sportsnet. TSN (and later, its sister channel [TSN2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSN2)) continued to carry approximately ten Jays games through the 2009 season until May 2010; most recently, [Rod Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Black) handled play-by-play while Tabler served as colour commentator on these telecasts. [CBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_Television) has carried Blue Jays games intermittently throughout the team's history, most recently in 2007 and 2008; those broadcasts featured [Jim Hughson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Hughson) as the play-by-play announcer, and former Blue Jays Rance Mulliniks and [Jesse Barfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Barfield) on colour commentary.[^cite_note-84] Games also aired on [CTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CTV_Television_Network) (except in Montreal) from the team's inception until the late 1990s; the channel also served as the primary outlet of Blue Jays postseason games simulcast from the United States, at the time when the Blue Jays were not permitted to produce their own postseason broadcasts.[^cite_note-85]

In 2008, Rogers Communications, owner of the Jays, was granted a license by the [Canadian Radio-Television Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Radio-television_and_Telecommunications_Commission) (CRTC) for a "Baseball TV" [specialty channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialty_channel).[^cite_note-86] The channel would have been dedicated to coverage of baseball, combining content from the United States–based [MLB Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_Network) with original Canadian content.[^cite_note-87] However, the channel was never launched, and Rogers sponsored an application to allow distribution of the U.S. MLB Network on Canadian providers instead.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89]

Until 2022, Sportsnet was not permitted to use its domestic production for Blue Jays games if the team is in postseason play (as it was technically still considered a regional broadcaster) and instead carries the U.S. broadcast (such as [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_on_Fox) in 2015, and [TBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_on_TBS) in 2016). Buck Martinez has served as a colour commentator for post-season coverage ultimately simulcast by Sportsnet, however, having formerly worked Division Series games for TBS and on the [MLB International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB_International) broadcast of the [2016 World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_World_Series).[^cite_note-metro-4mill-90] Beginning in 2022, MLB allowed Sportsnet to carry its own production of Blue Jays postseason games as the network's classification was changed from a regional to a national broadcaster.[^cite_note-dh-jays-playoffs-broadcasters-91] During the Blue Jays' [2025 World Series](79597307.md) run, Sportsnet's sister over-the-air channel [Citytv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citytv) carried Fox's broadcast for [simultaneous substitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simultaneous_substitution) purposes.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

### No-hitters

Only one Blue Jays pitcher has thrown a [no-hitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-hitter) in franchise history. It was accomplished by [Dave Stieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Stieb) on September 2, 1990, against the [Cleveland Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Indians), after losing three no-hit bids with two outs in the ninth inning.[^cite_note-97]

No [perfect games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_game_\(baseball\)), a special subcategory of no-hitter, has been thrown in Blue Jays history. The franchise came closest on August 4, 1989, when Stieb gave up a double to Yankees' batter [Roberto Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Kelly) with two outs in the ninth, who then scored by the next batter.[^cite_note-history_2-98]

Soon after becoming the first person to be inducted in the Hall of Fame as a Blue Jay, on July 31, 2011, second baseman Roberto Alomar was the first person to have his number, #12, retired by the Blue Jays.[^cite_note-104] However, #12 was unretired due to controversies surrounding Alomar.[^cite_note-105] [Jordan Hicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Hicks_\(baseball\)) became the first Blue Jay since 2011 to wear this number in 2023.

In 1996, the Blue Jays instituted a "Level of Excellence" on the 500 level of the [Rogers Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Centre), honouring "tremendous individual achievement."

- [Tony Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Fern%C3%A1ndez) (SS, 3B: 1983–1990, 1993, 1998–1999, 2001)

- [George Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Bell_\(outfielder\)) (LF: 1981–1990)

- [Carlos Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Delgado) (1B: 1993–2004)

- [Joe Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Carter) (RF, 1B: 1991–1997)

- [Dave Stieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Stieb) (P: 1979–1992, 1998)

- [Cito Gaston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cito_Gaston) (manager: 1989–1997, 2008–2010)

- [Tom Cheek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cheek) (broadcaster: 1977–2005)

- [Paul Beeston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Beeston) (VP: 1976–1989; president: 1989–1997, 2008–2015)

- [Roy Halladay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roy_Halladay) (P: 1998–2009)

- [Pat Gillick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Gillick) (GM: 1978–1994)

- [José Bautista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Bautista) (RF, 3B: 2008–2017)

- [Buck Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_Martinez) (C: 1981–1986; broadcaster: 1987–2000, 2010-2025; manager: 2001–2002)

Players' uniform numbers are listed, and in Tom Cheek's case, the number of consecutive games he called for the Blue Jays;[^cite_note-Level_of_Excellence-107] most these numbers have not been retired. During the 2013 All-Star Break, the Level of Excellence was redesigned for the addition of Carlos Delgado's name. The redesign removed all uniform numbers from the Level of Excellence aside from Roberto Alomar's then-retired #12, Roy Halladay's retired #32 and Tom Cheek's 4306 consecutive called games streak.[^cite_note-McNeil_retired_numbers-108] On April 30, 2021, the Blue Jays announced that they would remove Alomar from the Level of Excellence and take down his banner at Rogers Centre after he was banned from baseball for sexual harassment.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

On March 19, 2026, the Blue Jays organization announced that the Level of Excellence would be transformed into the new Hall of Excellence, with all existing members being moved into the new Hall, which would open on August 29. It was also announced that the team's first inductee into the new Hall would be former player, manager, and broadcaster [Buck Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buck_Martinez), who retired from calling games during the 2025-26 offseason.[^cite_note-111]

## Charity and partnerships

The Jays Care Foundation is the charitable arm of the Toronto Blue Jays baseball organization and conducts events to support local organizations and community members.[^cite_note-112] They also provide baseball education and life skill workshops to the youth of communities across Canada.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] The Jays Care Foundation is mainly funded by the Blue Jays 50/50 tickets.
