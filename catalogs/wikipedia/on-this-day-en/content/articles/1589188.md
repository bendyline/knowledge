The **Chamber of Senators** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Cámara de Senadores*), formerly known as the **National Senate** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Senado Nacional*), is the [upper chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_chamber) of the [Plurinational Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurinational_Legislative_Assembly), the [bicameral legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral_legislature) of Bolivia. Like the [lower chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_chamber), the [Chamber of Deputies](1589189.md), it meets in [La Paz](42821.md), the seat of government. It is an elected body consisting of 36 members and their [substitutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitute_\(elections\)).

The composition and powers of the Senate are established in the [Political Constitution of the State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Bolivia) and others determined by [Bolivian laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_Bolivia). Each of the country's nine [departments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Bolivia) returns four [senators](1589188.md) elected by [proportional representation](23298.md) (using the [D'Hondt method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method)).[^cite_note-RegimenElectoral-4] (From 1985 to 2009, the Senate had 27 seats: three seats per department: two from the party or formula that receives the most votes, with the third senator representing the second-placed party.) Senators are elected from party lists to serve five-year terms, and the minimum age to hold a Senate seat is 35 years.

The Senate was established in the 1831, disestablished later, and re-established in 1878.[^cite_note-5]
