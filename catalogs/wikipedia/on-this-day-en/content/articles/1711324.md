The **Indonesian Army** ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Tentara Nasional Indonesia Angkatan Darat* (TNI-AD), lit. 'Indonesian National Military Land Force') is the [land branch](1376.md) of the [Indonesian National Armed Forces](14650.md). It has an estimated strength of 1.632.000[^cite_note-3] active personnel.[^cite_note-IISS1-4] The history of the Indonesian Army has its roots in 1945 when the *Tentara Keamanan Rakyat* (TKR) "[People's Security Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Security_Army)" first emerged as a [paramilitary](146730.md) and police corps.[^cite_note-DAVESJ-5]

Since the nation's independence movement, the Indonesian Army has been involved in multifaceted operations ranging from the [incorporation of Western New Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Trikora), the [Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konfrontasi), to the [annexation of East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Seroja), as well as internal [counter-insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-insurgency) operations in [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh), [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_Islands), and [Papua](15199.md). The army's operations have not been without controversy; it has been periodically associated with [human rights](13831.md) violations, particularly in [West Papua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Papua_\(region\)), [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Timor), and Aceh.[^cite_note-SCHWARZ15-6][^cite_note-HILL-SMITH-7]

The Indonesia Army is composed of a headquarters, 15 military regional command ([Kodam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Regional_Command)), a strategic reserve command ([Kostrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostrad)), a special forces command ([Kopassus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopassus)), and various adjunct units. It is headed by the [Chief of Staff of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_Indonesian_Army) (Kepala Staf Angkatan Darat – KSAD or KASAD).

## History

### Formation

In the week following the Japanese surrender of 1945, the *Giyūgun* ([PETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenders_of_the_Homeland)) and *[Heiho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heiho)* groups were disbanded by the Japanese. Most PETA and *Heiho* members did not yet know about the declaration of independence. Command structures and membership vital for a national army were consequently dismantled. Thus, rather than being formed from a trained, armed, and organised army, the Republican armed forces began to grow in early October from usually younger, less trained groups under the national [People's Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Security_Agency) built around charismatic leaders in the regions.[^cite_note-RICKLEFSp214-215-8] Creating a rational military structure that was obedient to central authority from such disorganisation, was one of the major problems of the revolution, a problem that remains through to contemporary times.[^cite_note-DESTINIESp35-9] In a meeting between former KNIL and former PETA Division Commanders, organised by chief of staff (KSO) of [People's Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Security_Agency), [Oerip Soemohardjo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oerip_Soemohardjo), a thirty-year-old former school teacher and PETA member, [Sudirman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudirman), was elected 'commander-in-chief' in Yogyakarta on 12 November 1945.[^cite_note-REID-78-10][^cite_note-Historia_2016-11] Under his leadership, the young Army began to fight as a unified force during the National Revolution.

Aware of the limitations of their military in the face of the Dutch aggression, the people and government of Indonesia decided to fight foreign threats to the nation's independence. Thus, in 1947, the People's War Doctrine in which all the power of the national armed forces and the community and resources were deployed to confront the Dutch aggression, was officially implemented within the army and the wider armed forces as the national military strategy. Thus, the integrity and existence of the Unitary Republic of Indonesia has been able to be maintained by military forces with the people. By 1947, the young Army (then named *Tentara Republik Indonesia Angkatan Darat*) was organized into 10 infantry divisions, 7 Javanese and 3 Sumatran.

In accordance with the decision of the [Round Table Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch%E2%80%93Indonesian_Round_Table_Conference) (RTC), at the end of 1949 the [United States of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Indonesia) (RIS) came into being. Correspondingly, the TNI's ground forces thus formed part of the Angkatan Perang Republik Indonesia Serikat (APRIS) (later the Angkatan Perang Republik Indonesia or APRI when the republic became unitary in 1950). It would be the formal merger of the TNI and the former [KNIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KNIL) and all military personnel of the two forces, plus the independent paramilitary groups (*laskar*) which fought the war on the side of the independence movement.

### Action against rebellions



Indonesian army [M3 Stuart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M3_Stuart) tank patrolling in Ambon during [Republic of South Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_South_Maluku) rebellion, 1950

The period is also called the period of liberal democracy is characterized by various rebellions in the country. In 1950 most of the former members of the Colonial Army launched an uprising in [Bandung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandung) which is known as the [Legion of the Just Ruler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_the_Just_Ruler) / APRA uprising and was led by former KNIL officer [Raymond Westerling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Westerling). The army also needed to confront the uprising in [Makassar](389464.md) led by Andi Azis and the [Republic of South Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_South_Maluku) (RMS) in [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_\(province\)). Meanwhile, [DaruI Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DaruI_Islam?action=edit\&redlink=1) in West Java widened its influence to [South Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kalimantan), [South Sulawesi](389458.md) and [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh). In 1958 the Revolutionary Government of the Republic of Indonesia / People's Struggle ([PRRI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Government_of_the_Republic_of_Indonesia) / [Permesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permesta)) started a rebellion in large parts of Sumatra and [North Sulawesi](453688.md) endangering the national integrity. As part of the National Armed Forces the Army helped defeat all these uprisings, increasing its prestige in the eyes of the government and the people. Future Chief of Staff of the Army [Ahmad Yani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Yani) was instrumental in one of these first victories against rebels in Central Java.

On 17 November 1952, [General Nasution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Nasution) was suspended as army chief of staff following army indiscipline over command and support that threatens the government. From the 1950s, the military articulated the doctrines of *[dwifungsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwifungsi)* and *hankamrata*, the military roles in the country's socio-political development as well as security; and a requirement that the resources of the people be at the call of the armed forces and police if the State warrants it. On 5 July 1959, Sukarno, with armed forces support and the advice of Nasution, issued a [decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Sukarno's_1959_Decree) dissolving the [Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Assembly_of_Indonesia) and reintroducing the [Constitution of 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Indonesia) with strong presidential powers. By 1963, he also assumed the additional role of Prime Minister, which completed the structure of 'Guided Democracy', and was named "President for Life",[^cite_note-12] also with army assistance, the year after.



Indonesian soldiers in [Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula), 1957. They were part of the [Garuda Contingent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garuda_Contingent) under the [UNEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Emergency_Force).

At the same time, the Indonesian government started sending their troops on [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN) [peacekeeping missions](15652027.md). The first batch of soldiers were sent to [Sinai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Peninsula), [Egypt](8087628.md), and were known as Garuda Contingent I. Garuda Contingent I began its first deployment 8 January 1957 to Egypt. Garuda Contingent I consisted of the combined personnel of the 15th Infantry Regiment Territorial Command (TT) IV/Diponegoro, as well as one company of the 18th Infantry Regiment TC V/Brawijaya in [Malang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malang). This contingent was led by Lt. Col. of Infantry Hartoyo which was later replaced by Lieutenant Colonel of Infantry Suadi Suromihardjo, while his deputy was Major of Infantry Soediono Suryantoro. The contingent departed on 8 January 1957, on board the [Douglas C-124 Globemaster II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-124_Globemaster_II) transport aircraft of the [United States Air Force](32090.md) for [Beirut](37428.md), the Lebanese capital. From Beirut the contingent was divided by two, the majority heading to Abu Suweir and partly to Al Sandhira. Furthermore, the El Sandhira troops moved into [Gaza](12047.md), the border area of Egypt and [Israel](9282173.md), while the command is in [Rafah](584373.md). This contingent returned to Indonesia on 29 September 1957. Garuda Contingent I had a total number of 559 army personnel of all ranks.

### 1965 to 1998



Members of the Sarawak People's Guerilla Force (SPGF), North Kalimantan National Army (NKNA) and the Indonesian Army (TNI-AD) during the [Indonesia–Malaysia confrontation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konfrontasi)

The army was heavily involved in the [Indonesian killings of 1965–1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_killings_of_1965%E2%80%931966). The killings were an [anti-communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-communist) [purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purge) following a failed coup of the [30 September Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_September_Movement). The most widely accepted estimates are that more than 500,000 people were killed. The purge was a pivotal event in the [transition to the "New Order"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_to_the_New_Order); the [Indonesian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Communist_Party) (PKI) was eliminated as a political force. The failed coup released pent-up communal hatreds which were fanned by the Indonesian Army, which quickly blamed the PKI. Communists were purged from political, social, and military life, and the PKI itself was banned. The [massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massacre) began in October 1965, in the weeks following the coup attempt, and reached their peak over the remainder of the year before subsiding in the early months of 1966. They started in the capital, [Jakarta](16275.md), and spread to [Central](453584.md) and [East Java](320443.md) and, later, [Bali](4147.md). Thousands of local [vigilantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigilante) and army units killed actual and alleged PKI members. Although killings occurred across Indonesia, the worst were in the PKI strongholds of Central Java, East Java, Bali, and northern [Sumatra](28679.md). It is possible that over one million people were imprisoned at one time or another.

Sukarno's balancing act of "[Nasakom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasakom)" (nationalism, religion and communism) had been unravelled. His most significant pillar of support, the PKI, had been effectively eliminated by the other two pillars—the army and political [Islam](6037917.md); and the army was on the way to unchallenged power. In March 1968, Suharto was formally elected president.

The killings are skipped over in most Indonesian history books and have received little introspection by Indonesians and comparatively little international attention. Satisfactory explanations for the scale and frenzy of the violence have challenged scholars from all ideological perspectives. The possibility of a return to similar upheavals is cited as a factor in the "New Order" administration's political conservatism and tight control of the political system. Vigilance against a perceived communist threat remained a hallmark of Suharto's thirty-year presidency. The CIA described the massacre as "one of the worst [mass murders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_murders) of the 20th century, along with the Soviet purges of the 1930s, the Nazi mass murders during the Second World War, and the Maoist bloodbath of the early 1950s."[^cite_note-Blumenthal-13] Later army operations have not been without [controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_mass_killings_of_1965%E2%80%9366) however.[^cite_note-SCHWARZ15-6]

The size of the Army has expanded over the years; in July 1976 the Army was estimated to consist of solely 180,000 personnel, one armoured cavalry brigade, part of Kostrad (one tank battalion, plus support units), 14 infantry brigades (90 infantry, 1 para, 9 artillery, 11 anti-aircraft, and 9 engineer battalions) of which three of the brigades were in [Kostrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostrad), two airborne brigades totalling six battalions, also part of Kostrad, one independent tank battalion, 7 independent armoured cavalry battalions, and four independent para-commando battalions.[^cite_note-IISS_p.55-14]

### 1998–present

Involvement in UN Peacekeeping operations continued after the fall of Suharto, but in 2010, the [United Nations Interim Force in Lebanon](787766.md) was strongly criticized after two soldiers from Indonesia were filmed fleeing a clash on the Israeli-Lebanon border in a taxi.[^cite_note-15]

Starting in 2025, the Army embarked on an expansion program, with the [Ministry of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Indonesia\)) aiming at the formation of 150 new battalions per year with the stated aim of one territorial development battalion per [regency/city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regencies_and_cities_in_Indonesia) and one Kodam for each province.[^cite_note-16]

## Organisation



Indonesian Army Infantry soldiers from [Kostrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostrad) and [Raider Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Army_infantry_battalions#Raider_infantry_battalions) during a ceremony

Territorially, the Indonesian Army is currently organized into 21 military regions which are spread throughout the Indonesian archipelago. They are placed under the jurisdiction of the army headquarters. Six are based in [Sumatra](28679.md), four are based in [Java](69336.md), three are based in [Kalimantan](165470.md), one based in [Lesser Sunda Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Sunda_Islands), three based in [Sulawesi](28740.md), one based in [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_\(province\)) and three based in [Papua](15199.md). The [Komando Cadangan Strategis Angkatan Darat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostrad) (Kostrad, strategic reserve forces) and [Komando Pasukan Khusus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komando_Pasukan_Khusus) (Kopassus, the Army special force) are independent formations and directly subordinate to the chief of staff. The army headquarters is under coordination with the armed force Headquarters. The highest-ranking officer within the army is the Chief of Staff of the Army which has the rank of a four-star [General](148537.md) and is responsible to the Commander of the Armed Forces.

The Indonesian Army and its relation to the Armed Forces General Headquarters and the other military branches are structured into the following in accordance with the provisions of Presidential Regulation No. 84/2025 on the Organization of the Indonesian National Armed Forces:[^cite_note-17]

### Leadership elements (*Unsur pimpinan*)

   The current Chief of Staff of the Army, [General](148537.md) [Maruli Simanjuntak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maruli_Simanjuntak)

Exceptions are made for the Army Territorial Center, Army Military Police Center, and Army Central Hospital as they are all headed by a three-star Lieutenant General.

### Principal Commands under the Army Headquarters

#### Army Strategic Reserve Command



Soldiers from [Kostrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostrad)

The **[Army Strategic Reserve Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostrad)** (*Komando Cadangan Strategis Angkatan Darat*), better known by its abbreviation **Kostrad** is the Indonesian Army's strategic [operational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_operations) command. It is a corps-level command which has around 40,000 troops, organized into three divisions.[^cite_note-18] It also supervises operational readiness among all commands and conducts defence and security operations at the strategic level in accordance with policies of the TNI commander. **Green berets** are worn by its personnel, and it is the main deployable combat force of the army.

While [Kopassus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopassus) is the elite special forces of the Indonesian Army, Kostrad is the premier conventional combat force.[^cite_note-19] It is the main deployable combat force, and includes airborne infantry units. #### Army Special Force Command

The **[Army Special Force Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopassus)** (*Komando Pasukan Khusus*) or **Kopassus** for short, composed of an estimated 5,530 personnel organized into five brigade-level groups:

#### Army territorial commands

Further information: [Military Regional Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Regional_Command)



The Military Area Commands (*Komando Daerah Militer*, or KODAM) as of 2021

The territorial Military Regional Command or Military Area Command (*Komando Daerah Militer*) and its units below hierarchically serve as the main operational organization of the Indonesian Army. These [military territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_district) were established by General [Sudirman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudirman) (the then-[Commander of the Indonesian National Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander_of_the_Indonesian_National_Armed_Forces)), following the model of the [Nazi German *Wehrkreis* system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_district_\(Germany\)). The system was later codified in *Surat Perintah Siasat No. 1*, signed into doctrine in November 1948.

The hierarchy of Indonesian Army territorial command is as follows:

- **Kodam** (*Komando Daerah Militer*) Military Regional Command or officially known as Military Area Command ([Provincial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia) or multiple province level) is commanded by a two-star Major General;

- **Korem** (*Komando Resor Militer*) Military Resort Command or officially known as Military Subarea Command (Covering a province or multiple cities and regencies) is further divided into 2 type, A and B, commanded by a one-star Brigadier General and Colonel respectively;

- **Kodim** (*Komando Distrik Militer*) Military District Command ([City or Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_regencies_and_cities_of_Indonesia) level) is further divided, into 3 type, independent, A and B, commanded by a Colonel, Lieutenant Colonel, and Major respectively; and

- **Koramil** (*Komando Rayon Militer*) Military Rayon Command or officially known as Military Subdistrict Command (*[Kecamatan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kecamatan)* or district level) is further divided into 2 type, A and B, commanded by a Major and Captain respectively.

Jayakarta Military Area Command headquarters

The Army's structure underwent various reorganizations throughout its early years. From 1946 to 1952 the Army was organized into a number of set combined arms [divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_\(military\)), dominated by infantry brigades. These were further consolidated in 1951, and then dispersed in 1952. From 1952 to 1958–59, the Army was organized into seven Territorial Armies (*Tentara & Teritorium*) composed of regiments and independent formations in the battalion level and below. In August 1958, the Indonesian Army reconsolidated its territorial organization. There were then established sixteen regional commands, which retained earlier divisional titles; the [Siliwangi Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siliwangi_Division), for example, became Kodam VI/Siliwangi.[^cite_note-21] The RCs, then as in today, were subdivided administratively into Areas (the former territorial regiments), Districts (the former regimental battalions) and District Sectors, and operationally composed of a number of specialty battalions and in some regional commands, an infantry brigade.

A reorganisation in 1985 made significant changes in the army chain of command. The four multiservice Defence Region Commands (*Kowilhan*s) and the National Strategy Command (*Kostranas*) were eliminated from the defence structure, re-establishing the Military Regional Command (*Kodam*) as the key organisation for strategic, tactical, and territorial operations for all services.[^cite_note-22] The chain of command flowed directly from the ABRI commander in chief via the Chief of Staff of the Army to the ten territorial commands' commanders, and then to subordinate army territorial commands.

The territorial commands incorporate [provincial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia) and district commands each with a number of [infantry battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indonesian_infantry_battalions), sometimes a [cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalry) battalion, [artillery](2508.md), or [engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer), and there are an increasing number of infantry brigades being activated.[^cite_note-23] Some have Raider battalions attached either under divisional control, under brigades, or as territorial infantry.

4. **[Mechanized Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanized_Infantry) Battalion** (abbreviated *Yonif Mekanis*) are mobilized infantry battalions, equipped with [APCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_personnel_carriers) and [IFVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_fighting_vehicle).

5. **Infantry Battalion** (abbreviated *Yonif*) are [light Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_Infantry) battalions.

6. **Territorial Development Infantry Battalion** (abbreviated *Yonif TP*) are battalions established starting in 2025, tasked with regional economic development in addition to combat roles. - **[Cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalry)** (**KAV**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Kavaleri*) is the armored forces unit of the army. Its main function is as a combat support element. Cavalry units do not just rely on [Tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank), [APCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_personnel_carrier) and [IFVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry_fighting_vehicle) as combat assets, but also use horses specially trained for combat and combat support operations in any terrain. Troopers wear **black berets**. The cavalry formations of the Army are under the supervision of the Cavalry Branch Center (*_Pusat Kesenjataan Kavaleri_*).

- **[Field Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Artillery)** (**ARM**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Artileri Medan*) is the field artillery unit of the army. It also acts as a combat support similar to the cavalry unit. Its main function is to support ground combat mission for the Infantry Branch. Units of the Field Artillery use either towed or self propelled artillery guns and multiple rocket launchers. **Brown berets** are worn by its gunners. The Field Artillery units report under the Field Artillery Branch Center (*_Pusat Kesenjataan Artileri Medan_*).

- **[Air Defense Artillery](146640.md)** (**ARH**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Artileri Pertahanan Udara*) are the anti-aircraft defense units of the army. Its main function to defend other ground units from an air attack and help to protect installations from destruction. They are equipped with both anti-air defense guns and short range air defense missile systems, either MANPADs or vehicle-mounted systems. Like the Field Artillery, **Brown berets** are worn by its gunners and missile crews. The Air Defense Artillery units report to the Air Defense Artillery Branch Center (*_Pusat Kesenjataan Artileri Pertahanan Udara_*). Four detachments of missile artillery units called Den Rudal (*_Detasemen Rudal_*) are part of the Air Defense Artillery.

- **[Engineering Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Army_Corps_of_Engineers)** (**CZI**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Korps Zeni*) - The Military Engineering Corps is specialty branch of the army whose primary function as a combat support, such as the construction of [military bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_bridge) for vehicles to pass by or converting highways into temporary [runways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway). Another function of this unit is to expand troop movements and narrowing enemy movements while assisting friendly units. The Military Engineers are also involved in relief operations in the aftermath of calamities and in building civil projects in the local communities. Engineers, regardless of rank, wear **Grey berets** or **construction helmets** in their uniforms. The unit is under the Directorate of Army Military Engineering.

- **[Ordnance Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_logistics)** (**CPL**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Korps Peralatan*) is a unit whose main function is the maintenance and testing of military ordnance. The unit is under the Directorate of Army Ordnance .

- **[Signal Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signal_Corps)** (**CKE**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Korps Komunikasi dan Elektronika*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Electronics and Communications Corps') is a unit whose main function to deliver and maintain the best possible information to combat units. The unit is under the Directorate of Army Signals. Previously known as the Signal Corps (CHB; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Korps Perhubungan*), the nomenclature was changed in January 2024.

- **[Logistics and Transportation / Quartermaster Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Army_Logistics_and_Transportation_Corps)** (**CBA**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Korps Pembekalan dan Angkutan*) is tasked to provide services and transport logistic cargo within the Indonesian Army. Dark blue berets are worn by its personnel. The Logistic Transportation Corps is under the Directorate of Army Logistics and Transportation.

- **[Topography / Survey Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography)** (**CTP**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Korps Topografi*) – This unit's main function is to make topographic research and maps about the battlefield for the purposes of the Indonesian Army during combat operations. This unit is under the Directorate of the Army Topography Service.

- **[Army Women's Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Women's_Army_Corps)** (**K**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Korps Wanita Angkatan Darat*) Operationally dependent on Army commands and services the Women's Army Corps serves as the official administrative branch of women who actively spend their active military service in the ranks of the Army.

- **Medical Corps** (**CKM**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Korps Kesehatan*) main function is to maintain the health and medical wellness of all Army officers, warrant officers, NCOs and enlisted personnel. and their families. The health unit is under the Army Medical Department.

- **Finance Corps** (**CKU**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Korps Keuangan*) main function is to foster the financial administration of the army. The finance unit is under the Directorate of Army Finance.

- **Legal Corps** (**CHK**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Korps Hukum*) main function is to maintain law and justice within the army. The law unit is under the command of Directorate of the Army Justice Service. This unit is also responsible for military courts, and military attorneys and judges report under this unit.&#x20;

  - **[Bandsmen Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_band)** (**CMU**; [Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Korps Musik*) – The Army Corps of Bandsmen, which is an administrative organization operationally dependent on Army commands and services, is responsible for the organization of the military bands, [corps of drums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps_of_drums) and [drum and bugle corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drum_and_bugle_corps_\(classic\)) within the entire Army, alongside dedicated ensembles (big bands, rock and pop bands, native ensembles, etc.). Unlike the other support units, these are under the direct control of their respective unit commanders as HQ units and its overall supervision is under the Adjutant General of the Army. Bandsmen and field musicians wear the service dress berets or helmets of their reporting arm or branch of service.

## Rank structure

Main article: [Indonesian military ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_military_ranks)

Command and Staff rank





An example of a Second lieutenant rank (left) with red band indicating a command rank. While (right) without red band indicating a staff rank.

In the Army, as well as in other armed forces branches in Indonesia, the rank consists of three groups of ranks: *Perwira* for officers, *Bintara for* NCOs, and *Tamtama* for [enlisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlisted_rank). The proper title to address of rank are as follows and applicable to all branch of TNI, all flag officers (generals, admirals, and air marshals) use their rank followed by "(TNI)", while senior and junior officers use their rank followed by respective branch/corps abbreviation. For example, an Army colonel with Infantry branch use the title "Kolonel INF" (read as *_Kolonel Infanteri_*), while an Army Major General from Infantry branch use the title "Mayor Jenderal (TNI)". Enlisted personnel are not required to put their respective branch/corps specialty.[^cite_note-pangkat-28]

**Note**: Indonesia is not a member of [NATO](21133.md), so there is not an official equivalence between the Indonesian military ranks and those defined by NATO. The displayed parallel is approximate and for illustration purposes only.

**Note**: The red banding on the rank insignia denotes the personnel holding a command position which is agnostic of rank.
