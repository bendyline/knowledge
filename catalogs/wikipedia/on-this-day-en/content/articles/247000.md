**Civil service examinations** are examinations implemented in various countries for recruitment and admission to the [civil service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service). They are intended as a method to achieve an effective, rational [public administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_administration) on a [merit system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merit_system) for recruiting prospective politicians and public sector employees.

The most ancient example of such exams were the [imperial examinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_examination) of [ancient China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China#Ancient_China).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Competitive exam

Competitive examinations are tests where candidates are ranked according to their grades and/or percentile and then top rankers are selected.[^cite_note-4] If the examination is open for *n* positions, then the first *n* candidates in ranks pass, the others are rejected. They are used as [entrance examinations](1795037.md) for [university and college admissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_and_college_admissions) such as the [Joint Entrance Examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Entrance_Examination) or to [secondary schools](554992.md). Types are civil service examinations, required for [positions in the public sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_service); the U.S. [Foreign Service Exam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_Service_Exam), and the [United Nations](31769.md) Competitive Examination. Competitive examinations are considered an [egalitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egalitarian) way to select worthy applicants without risking [influence peddling](630570.md), [bias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bias) or other concerns.[^cite_note-5] However, implicit biases within society may adversely impact the purported fairness of the exams.[^cite_note-6]
