# Laos–Thailand border



Map of the Laos–Thailand border

The **Laos–Thailand border** is the international border between the territory of [Laos](17752.md) and [Thailand](30128.md). The border is 1,845 km (1,146 mi) in length, over half of which follows the [Mekong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_River), and runs from the [tripoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoint) with [Myanmar](19457.md) in the north to tripoint with [Cambodia](334751.md) in the south.[^cite_note-1]

## Description



Tonpheung border checkpoint (2007)

The border starts in the north at the tripoint with Myanmar at the confluence of the [Kok River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kok_River) and [Mekong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_River), following the latter towards the south-east.[^cite_note-IBS-2] It then leaves the river and proceeds overland in a broadly southwards direction over various hill ridges, down to the [Hueang River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hueang_River). It then follows this river to the north-east up to the confluence with the Mekong, from where it follows the Mekong for the majority of the border's length. Just north-west of [Pakse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakse) the border leaves the Mekong and then follows the ridge of the [Dângrêk Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A2ngr%C3%AAk_Mountains) south the tripoint with Cambodia.[^cite_note-IBS-2]

## History



A pillar marking the border (2002)

For centuries, the [Mekong River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekong_River) did not act as a rigid international border, but rather as a lifeline connecting communities sharing deep cultural, linguistic, and historical ties. In the mid-19th century, the [Lao kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Luang_Phrabang) and [parts of Cambodia](493472.md) existed as tributary states under the suzerainty of the [Kingdom of Siam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rattanakosin_Kingdom_\(1782%E2%80%931932\)) (modern-day [Thailand](30128.md)). This traditional Asian system of overlapping loyalties was shattered by the arrival of French gunboat diplomacy. Beginning in the 1860s, [France](5843419.md) established a firm foothold in [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_of_Cambodia), officially consolidating these territories into the colony of [French Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina) in 1887, seeking to expand its empire to the natural boundary of the Mekong River.[^cite_note-IBS-2] France provoked the [Franco-Siamese Crisis of 1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_Franco-Siamese_crisis), as the French warships sailed up the [Chao Phraya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Phraya_River), threatening [Bangkok](56615.md) and forcing Siam to cede all territorial claims east of the Mekong including [Laos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_protectorate_of_Laos) to the French due to colonial pressures continued over the next decade. Through a series of unequal treaties enforced in [1904](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Siamese_Treaty_of_1904) and [1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Siamese_Treaty_of_1907), a vulnerable Siam was compelled to cede strategic territories west of the Mekong as well, including modern-day [Sainyabuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sainyabuli), [Champasak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champasak_province), and portions of northern Cambodia. A minor treaty of 1926 Franco-Siamese Convention have finally settled the micro-sovereignty of the shifting islets within the Mekong River itself.[^cite_note-IBS-2][^cite_note-Stuart-Fox_1997-3][^cite_note-RBSJ-4] The geopolitical equilibrium fractured during [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War). Following the [Fall of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_military_administration_in_occupied_France_during_World_War_II) in 1940, [Vichy France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France) maintained nominal control over [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Indochina_in_World_War_II) but was heavily compromised. Sensing imperial weakness, Thailand under the militaristic leadership of [Plaek Phibunsongkhram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaek_Phibunsongkhram) launched the [Franco-Thai War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Thai_War), supported by [Imperial Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan), Thailand successfully reclaimed the ceded territories of Cambodia and Laos.[^cite_note-IBS-2][^cite_note-RBSJ-4] This triumph was short-lived. Following [Japan's total defeat in 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan), the international community annulled wartime territorial transfers of the pre-war colonial borders were strictly reinstated, forcing Thailand to return the lands to French administration.[^cite_note-IBS-2][^cite_note-RBSJ-4] The post-war era witnessed the irreversible decline of European colonialism. Laos achieved partial autonomy within the [French Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Union) in 1949 and finally secured complete independence on 22 October 1953, followed by the [Kingdom of Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Cambodia_\(1953%E2%80%931970\)) on 9 November that same year. The former French colonial lines were instantly transformed into international borders between fully sovereign states.[^cite_note-IBS-2]

As the [Cold War](325329.md) raged on [between the two superpowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union%E2%80%93United_States_relations) like the [United States](3434750.md) and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) (until the [Soviet collapse in 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union)), the fall of French colonial rule and the American imperialism had shattered the geopolitical landscape of [Southeast Asia](28741.md) which led the rise of [Marxist–Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism) regimes through Indochina such as the [Fall of Phnom Penh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Phnom_Penh) to the [Khmer Rouge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmer_Rouge) (Cambodian Reds) on 17 April, led to the establishment of [Democratic Kampuchea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_in_Cambodia) on 5 January 1976, the [Fall of Saigon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Saigon) to the [Viet Cong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Cong) on 30 April, had led to the [unification of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reunification_Day) as the [Socialist Republic of Vietnam](202354.md) by renaming [Saigon](66956.md) into "Ho Chi Minh City" in honor of the [late revolutionary leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ho_Chi_Minh) on 2 July 1976 and the victory of [Pathet Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathet_Lao) in [Vientiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vientiane) on 2 December 1975 by renaming the country of "[Lao People's Democratic Republic](17752.md)" had transformed Thailand into the ultimate frontline state of the [Western world](21208200.md)'s "[Asian Domino Theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domino_theory)". Thailand's borders are surrounded by newly declared communist regimes backed by the Soviet Union and [China](5405.md) had doubled down on its constitutional monarchy and military alliance with the United States and the Western allies. While the 1976 friendship treaties attempted to codify peace, they could not erase decades of deep-seated territorial trauma.[^cite_note-RBSJ-4][^cite_note-RBSJ-4][^cite_note-cs-5]However fighting broke out in 1984 over disputed villages adjacent to the frontier in Sainyabuli/Uttaradit Province, and [again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai%E2%80%93Laotian_Border_War) in 1987–88 over a nearby area.[^cite_note-RBSJ-4] A joint committee was established in 1991 which aimed to resolve the dispute peacefully, however discussions dragged on throughout the decade. The [First Thai–Lao Friendship Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Thai%E2%80%93Lao_Friendship_Bridge) was opened on 4 April 1994.[^cite_note-RBSJ-4] A joint boundary commission was established in 1997, however its work was suspended in 1998 following the [Asian financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_financial_crisis).[^cite_note-RBSJ-4] As of 2018 border demarcation is still ongoing.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

### Checkpoints for border trade



The [First Thai–Lao Friendship bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Thai%E2%80%93Lao_Friendship_Bridge)

These border crossings are open for cross-border local trade only. There are 29 checkpoints for border trade officially recognized by the Ministry of Interior, located in provinces. Entering the opposite country beyond these checkpoints and their associated markets is illegal.
