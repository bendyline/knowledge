The **Sociedade Esportiva Palmeiras** (Brazilian Portuguese: [\[sosjeˈdadʒi ispoʁˈtʃivɐ pawˈmejɾɐs\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/bd/Sociedade-esportiva-palmeiras-pronunciation-ptbr.ogg/Sociedade-esportiva-palmeiras-pronunciation-ptbr.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/bd/Sociedade-esportiva-palmeiras-pronunciation-ptbr.ogg/Sociedade-esportiva-palmeiras-pronunciation-ptbr.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), commonly known as **Palmeiras**, is a Brazilian professional [football](10568.md) club based in the city of [São Paulo](390875.md), in the district of [Perdizes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perdizes_\(district_of_S%C3%A3o_Paulo\)). Palmeiras is one of the most popular clubs in [Brazil](3383.md), with around 15 million fans.[^cite_note-2] The football team plays in the [Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Paulista), the [state of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Brazil) [São Paulo](229422.md)'s premier [state league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_football_leagues_in_Brazil), as well as in the [Brasileirão Série A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A), the top tier of the [Brazilian football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_football_league_system).

The Sociedade Esportiva Palmeiras was founded by Italian immigrants on 26 August 1914, as "[Palestra Itália](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestra%20Italia)" (pronounced [\[paˌlɛstɾiˈtaljɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese)). However, the club changed its name on 14 September 1942, as a result of Brazil joining the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) in the [Second World War](32927.md) against [Italy](14532.md) ("Itália" in Portuguese) and the [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) powers. Since then, Palmeiras has won 18 top-tier national competitions, including a record 12 [Campeonato Brasileiro Série A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A), 4 [Copas do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_do_Brasil), 1 [Copa dos Campeões](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_dos_Campe%C3%B5es), and 1 [Supercopa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercopa_do_Brasil), the 1999, 2020 and 2021 [Copa Libertadores](299730.md), the [2022 Recopa Sudamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Recopa_Sudamericana), and the 1998 [Copa Mercosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Mercosul). The club has also been successful at a regional level as they have won 5 Interstate titles ([Torneio Rio – São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torneio_Rio_%E2%80%93_S%C3%A3o_Paulo)), and 26 State Championship titles ([Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Paulista)).[^cite_note-3]

Palmeiras currently occupies the second position on [CBF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBF_ranking) and the first position [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) rankings, and was the first Brazilian club to win the [IFFHS Men's Club World Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IFFHS_World's_Best_Club) in 2021.

The squads for all five [FIFA World Cups](11370.md) and two Olympic gold medals won by [Brazil](149286.md) have had at least one Palmeiras player in them, an honour shared with cross-city rivals [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_FC).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

The name Palmeiras usually refers to the football club, yet its athletic department competes in more than a dozen different sports. These include [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sociedade_Esportiva_Palmeiras_\(basketball\))[^cite_note-deRose2014-6] and roller hockey (rink hockey), American football, archery, and karate among others.

### Foundation

At the beginning of the 20th century, several young Italians decided to start a club whose main goal was to form a football team that would be representative of the Italian community, and face the big names of São Paulo's football elite. Just over three decades earlier, Italy had been unified – a fact that was not known to some Italian-Brazilians and to some non-Italian Brazilians.

There were numerous Italian clubs, but each one represented an Italian province or was geared to activities other than [football](10568.md). At the time, the game was starting to take hold and drew many players and fans.

The founders of the club sought out the *Fanfulla* newspaper, which was the media outlet that defended the interests of Italians in Brazil, and entrusted young Vincenzo Ragognetti – another supporter of the idea – to draft an invitation to those interested in forming a sports club.

After several meetings, 46 interested individuals (led by Luigi Marzo and Luigi Cervo) gathered at the Alhambra Room on what is now Rua do Riachuelo, and founded a sports club for all Italian-Brazilians named "Palestra Italia" on 26 August 1914. Ezequiel Simone was named club president. The Italian Consulate in São Paulo became interested in the new club because it would help spread the word among Italians that their country now had one flag and one anthem.

After some initial difficulties, Palestra Italia played its first game in the town of Votorantim (São Paulo State) – beating Savoia 2–0 with goals from Bianco and Alegretti to win the Taça Savoia, the club's first title.[^cite_note-palmeiras.com.br-7]

### 1920–1945: First state title and new stadium



Oscar Francisco Nascimento in 1930

In 1916, the team joined the city's main sports league and played its first official championship match. The following year it would be runner-up in the São Paulo State Championship, facing [Corinthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Club_Corinthians_Paulista) for the first time. Palestra won that initial game 3–0 with three goals from Caetano; it also won the rematch 3–1 of what would become the team's chief rivalry. In 1920, Palestra Italia captured the São Paulo State championship with a victory over the rugged Paulistano squad in the deciding match.

Palestra continued to grow as a sports club and also began acquiring more assets. [Estádio Palestra Itália](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Palestra_It%C3%A1lia), purchased in 1920, was remodeled and expanded in 1933, when it became the first Brazilian stadium with concrete grandstands and barbed-wire fences. Starting in 1964, the playing field would be suspended, which gave fans a complete, broad view and also created space in the lower levels.

The club continued to grow and win more championships, and at the outset of the 1930s became the three-time São Paulo State football and basketball champion – a feat that prompted Palestra fans to chant in celebration: "With the feet or with the hands, Palestra is the best in the land."[^cite_note-palmeiras.com.br-7]

### A Leader Dies, A Champion is Born



[Oberdan Cattani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberdan_Cattani)



Photo of Palestra Italia State Champion in 1940



Palmeiras in 1942

In 1942 during World War II, the government of President Getúlio Vargas issued a decree banning any organization from using names related to the Axis Powers (Germany, Italy, and Japan). Palestra Italia was forced to change its name and became Palestra São Paulo ("palestra" is a Greek word, loosely translated as "gymnasium," which therefore did not violate this rule). However, the change still did not soothe political and sporting pressures to alter the name completely. At risk of forfeiting all its assets to other clubs and being ejected from the championship that it currently led, Palestra was forced to change its name a second time. The night before the last game of the [state championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Paulista), scheduled for 20 September 1942, the Palestra board of directors held a heated meeting and changed the club's name. When the debate reached its peak, Dr. Mario Minervino took the floor and asked club secretary, Dr. Pascoal W. Byron Giuliano, to note in the minutes:

– "They don't want us to be Palestra, so then we shall be Palmeiras – born to be champions."

Tensions flared during the final league match, where Palmeiras's opponent was São Paulo Futebol Clube (SPFC), which was laying claim to the assets of the former Palestra Italia.

Palmeiras took the field carrying the Brazilian flag under the leadership of army Captain Adalberto Mendes. Palmeiras was leading the match by 3–1 when a penalty was given in its favor. At that moment, the SPFC ordered its players to consider the Palmeiras squad an enemy of the homeland and pulled its side off the field amid jeers from even the club's own fans. The celebrations began on the spot. The next day, newspapers contained a photograph of Palmeiras entering the field with the headline: "A Leader Dies, A Champion is Born."[^cite_note-palmeiras.com.br-7]

### Copa Rio: 1951



Copa Rio 1951 Trophy



Palmeiras team profiled before the final against Juventus in 1951 at Maracanã Stadium

On 27 October 2017, the FIFA Council changed its position: while not promoting the statistical unification of the Intercontinental Cup and the Club World Cup, in respect to the history of the two tournaments[^cite_note-kit20172-8] (which merged in 2005),[^cite_note-FIFA_Activity_Report_20053-9] it made the Intercontinental Cup an official world title, recognizing all its winners as club world champions with the same title as [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup) winners: "FIFA Club World Champions."[^cite_note-\:F154233224-10][^cite_note-\:F15423-11] In April 2019, [FIFA](11049.md) president [Gianni Infantino](9286117.md), in an interview with Brazilian media, reiterated FIFA's perspective that only the winners of the Intercontinental Cup and the Club World Cup were officially world champions. Also in April 2019, former [FIFA](11049.md) president [Joseph Blatter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Blatter) (who held office until December 2015) again stated to the Brazilian press that Palmeiras had been recognized as world club champions by FIFA in 2014.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

&#x20;\


&#x20;In February 2021, the [FIFA](11049.md) website praised Palmeiras's victory in the Copa Rio thus:&#x20;

> > "A world championship had been dreamed of and discussed for years by some of football's foremost shot-callers – Jules Rimet, Ottorino Barassi and Stanley Rous among them – and was finally scheduled for 1951 in Brazil, which had recently hosted the FIFA World Cup. The eight-team competition involved some of Europe's top teams, Uruguayan behemoths Nacional and Brazilian duo Vasco da Gama and Palmeiras, who qualified as Rio-Sao Paulo Tournament winners".
> >
> > [^cite_note-Superheroes_in_green-14]

In any case, the Copa Rio was the first intercontinental football tournament to bring together the most prestigious clubs from the most important leagues around the world. The matches were played at the same locations in which the [1950 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_FIFA_World_Cup) games had been played the year before. The tournament triggered discussions within continental football federations about exploring club football internationally, which eventually led to the launch of international championships such as the European Cup (now known as the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md)) and the [Copa Libertadores](299730.md).

In the end, Palmeiras won the first intercontinental championship of the pre-international era of football, making the 'Copa Rio' one of the most important starting points of globalization for football. The 'Copa Rio' was important at the time but Palmeiras is still not recognized as an intercontinental champion by FIFA.

### The "Academy" becomes a Brazilian giant



Ademir da Guia

In the 1960s, the standard of quality of Palmeiras played – led by the one who would come to symbolize this period of football excellence, [Ademir da Guia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ademir_da_Guia) – led the Palestra Italia team to be called the "Academy" of Brazilian football.



The First Academy in 1960

The first academy had [Djalma Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djalma_Santos), Djalma Dias, [Dudu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudu_\(footballer,_born_1939\)), Ademir da Guia, [Julinho Botelho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julinho_Botelho), [Vavá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vav%C3%A1), Liminha and Chinesinho as some of the outstanding players.



The Second Academy in 1969



Luís Pereira (right) at the [1974 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_FIFA_World_Cup)

Managed by Filpo Núñez, Palmeiras players won the most important national competition in 1965, the Rio – São Paulo championship, with stand-out performances. Blow-outs against top rivals included seven goals scored against [Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_FC), five against [Botafogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botafogo_de_Futebol_e_Regatas) in their home stadium of the [Maracanã](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_Stadium), five against [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_FC), and another four scored against [Vasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CR_Vasco_da_Gama). The title came to Palmeiras in another lopsided victory against Botafogo at Pacaembú Stadium in São Paulo.

That same year, the Brazilian Sports Federation (CBD) used the entire Palmeiras roster to inaugurate the [Mineirão Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineir%C3%A3o) and represent Brazil in an official national team match against Uruguay for the Inconfidência Cup. The day that it donned the green and white, Palmeiras as Brazil was victorious 3–0 over the Uruguayan blue.

In the previous year, Palmeiras had won the Rio de Janeiro Quadricentennial Cup ([Torneio do IV Centenário do Rio de Janeiro de 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torneio_do_IV_Centen%C3%A1rio_do_Rio_de_Janeiro_de_1965?action=edit\&redlink=1)) by beating the Paraguay national team 5–2 and besting [Peñarol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe%C3%B1arol) of Uruguay in the final.

By the end of the 1960s, Palmeiras won the Copa do Brasil and the Roberto Gomes Pedrosa tournament – the Brazilian Championship equivalent at the time.

These victories laid the groundwork for the second Palmeiras Academy, with players like [Luís Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_Pereira), [Leivinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leivinha), [Emerson Leão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerson_Le%C3%A3o), Dudu, [Ademir da Guia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ademir_da_Guia) and [César](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Augusto_da_Silva_Lemos).

Led by [Osvaldo Brandão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osvaldo_Brand%C3%A3o), the team captured several titles in the 1970s. It was a three-time São Paulo state champion – emerging undefeated in one of those tournaments – a two-time Brazilian champion, a three-time winner of Spain's Ramón de Carranza Trophy, and the winner of Argentina's Mar del Plata Trophy – considered the South American Club Championship.[^cite_note-palmeiras.com.br-7]

### 1980s: The lost decade



Palmeiras's supporters

Already used to victories at the "Academy" in the 1960s and 1970s, Palmeiras fans saw the 1980s come and go without championships or titles. In 1986, Palmeiras fielded a good team – routing Corinthians 5–1 and playing a historic game in the semifinals of the São Paulo State Championship against that same rival, prevailing 3–0. Ten years after winning its last state title, Palmeiras finally arrived at the final of the state championship, but lost to [Inter de Limeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associa%C3%A7%C3%A3o_Atl%C3%A9tica_Internacional_\(Limeira\)).

On 29 October 1986, Palmeiras fans adopted the pig as their mascot. At a game against [Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_FC), the rival fans were chanting "pig"; the Palmeiras crowd responded with "Come On Pig!! Come On Pig!! Olé Olé Olé..." and "Go Piiiig...." A few days later, Placar sports magazine popularized the new nickname when it published an issue with Jorginho Putinatti – the symbol of that generation – holding a pig in his lap.

There were two noteworthy events during this decade. In the 1983 State Championship against Santos, referee José de Assis Aragão scored a goal for Palmeiras in the 47th minute of the second half. Striker Jorginho kicked the ball inside the penalty area, the ball was on its way out, but hit Aragão – who was on the goal line about a meter from the goal and went into the Santos net. The game ended in a 2–2 tie – much to the chagrin of Santos.

The second unexpected event occurred on 11 November 1988, when striker Gaúcho saved two penalties against [Flamengo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clube_de_Regatas_do_Flamengo) in a game for the Brazilian Championship at Maracanã. Gaúcho was put in goal after keeper Zetti broke his leg in the final minutes of the match. The game ended in a tie, and advanced to the penalty shootout phase. During the shootout, Gaúcho stopped two shots, from Aldair and Zinho. To cap off the evening, he scored a penalty himself while wearing the goalkeeper's jersey.



Edmundo

In 1989, Palmeiras had another chance to win a title. Undefeated until the penultimate match, the team was eliminated when it lost to Bragantino in the semifinals of the São Paulo State Championship. The 1980s ended without significant victories, but the 1990s would make up for that.[^cite_note-palmeiras.com.br-7]

### The end of the 20th century – The Greatest in Brazil

Palmeiras found itself in the midst of a sixteen-year drought, without any significant trophies, until 1992, when the club signed a sponsorship deal with Italian dairy giant [Parmalat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parmalat). The deal lasted for eight years and quickly turned Palmeiras into Brazil's richest club.[^cite_note-15]

In the 1990s, Palmeiras enjoyed countless achievements, winning numerous important titles. In the first full year of the relationship with Parmalat, the team won the [Campeonato Paulista in 1993](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Paulista), beating its biggest rival [Corinthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Club_Corinthians_Paulista) in the final, under the command of coach [Vanderlei Luxemburgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderlei_Luxemburgo) and with a squad featuring [Evair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evair), [Zinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinho), [Edmundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmundo_\(footballer\)), [César Sampaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Sampaio), [Mazinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazinho), [Antônio Carlos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B4nio_Carlos_Zago), Roberto Carlos and [Edílson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed%C3%ADlson) .



Vanderlei Luxemburgo

That same year, Palmeiras also captured the Rio-São Paulo Championship, once again against [Corinthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Club_Corinthians_Paulista), and the [1993 Campeonato Brasileiro Série A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A). In 1994, it achieved the unprecedented feat of winning consecutive state championships and Brazilian championships, the latter victory coming against [Corinthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Club_Corinthians_Paulista) once again.

In [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Sociedade_Esportiva_Palmeiras_season) Palmeiras won the friendly [Euro-America Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Euro-Am%C3%A9rica), defeating the [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) champions and future [1996-97 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996-97_UEFA_Champions_League) and [1997 Intercontinental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Intercontinental_Cup) champions [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund) by a score of 6–1. Later, the team handily won the [1996 Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Campeonato_Paulista), scoring more than 100 goals and accumulating historic victories, such as a 6–0 thrashing of [Santos FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_FC). [Rivaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivaldo), [Muller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muller), [Djalminha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djalminha) and [Luizão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiz%C3%A3o_\(footballer,_born_1968\)) were the standouts of this historic team.

Palmeiras also had a remarkable sequence of games in the [1996 Copa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Copa_do_Brasil), beating [Clube Atletico Mineiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clube_Atletico_Mineiro) 5-0 (7–1 on aggregate) and defeating the [1995 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Copa_Libertadores) winners [Gremio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gremio) in the semifinals, but the team eventually suffered a surprise loss to [Cruzeiro EC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruzeiro_EC), who would eventually win the [1997 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Copa_Libertadores).

In 1998, Palmeiras won the [1998 Copa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Copa_do_Brasil) and the [1998 Copa Mercosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Copa_Mercosur), both against [Cruzeiro EC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruzeiro_EC). The latter was the first official international cup won by Palmeiras since 1951.

### 1999 – The first Copa Libertadores trophy

See also: [Copa Libertadores](299730.md)



Zinho

Already famous for winning the [1991 Copa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Copa_do_Brasil), [1994 Copa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Copa_do_Brasil), [1995 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Copa_Libertadores), and [1996 Campeonato Brasileiro Série A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A), [Luiz Felipe Scolari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiz_Felipe_Scolari) was hired as manager in 1997 and led the team to a number of important trophies, including their first [Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Copa_Libertadores).

Palmeiras faced a challenging road to the finals, defeating the [defending champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Copa_Libertadores) [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clube_de_Regatas_Vasco_da_Gama) in the round of 16 by 5–3 on aggregate (including coming back to secure a historic 4–2 away victory), rival [Corinthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Club_Corinthians_Paulista) in the quarterfinals on penalties, and [1996 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Copa_Libertadores) winners [River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Atletico_River_Plate) in the semifinals by 3–1 on aggregate.

The final matches were against [Deportivo Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportivo_Cali) from Colombia, the [1978 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Copa_Libertadores) runners up. In the first leg in [Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali), Deportivo beat Palmeiras 1–0. In the second leg, at [Estádio Palestra Itália](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Palestra_It%C3%A1lia), Palmeiras beat Deportivo 2–1 and won the competition in a penalty shootout.

Important players from that team were [World Cup](11370.md) winners [Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_Roberto_Silveira_Reis), [Zinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinho) and [Roque Júnior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_J%C3%BAnior), as well as [Alex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexsandro_de_Souza), [Evair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evair_Aparecido_Paulino), [Paulo Nunes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Nunes), and [César Sampaio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Sampaio).

That same year, Palmeiras disputed the [1999 Intercontinental Cup in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Intercontinental_Cup) [Tokyo](30057.md), but were defeated by [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) of England. Despite enjoying the better of the game, Palmeiras had a goal from [Alex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_\(footballer,_born_1977\)) ruled out, and conceded a goal after an uncharacteristic mistake by [Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_\(footballer,_born_1973\)), one of the team's stars and future [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) champion.[^cite_note-16]



Luiz Felipe Scolari

### 2000 – Four finals disputed

In 2000, Palmeiras disputed four finals. First, the team won the [Rio-São Paulo Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torneio_Rio-S%C3%A3o_Paulo) after beating [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CR_Vasco_da_Gama) by 6–1 on aggregate in the final. In the first leg, in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md), Palmeiras beat Vasco 2–1. In the second leg, at [Morumbi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_do_Morumbi), Palmeiras prevailed by a score of 4–0.

The club again reached the [2000 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Copa_Libertadores) finals, this time against [Boca Juniors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Juniors) from [Argentina](18951905.md). In the first leg in [Buenos Aires](255919.md), the game ended 2–2. In the second leg, at the [Morumbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_do_Morumbi) in São Paulo, the game ended 0–0, with Boca winning the competition in a penalty shootout and preventing a consecutive championship for Palmeiras.

After the Copa Libertadores final, [Luiz Felipe Scolari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiz_Felipe_Scolari) left Palmeiras, along with a number of players. Given the circumstances, Palmeiras invested in younger players and won the first ever [Brazilian Champions' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_dos_Campe%C3%B5es) after beating [Sport Recife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Club_do_Recife) in the final. The result qualified the club to play in their 3rd consecutive [Copa Libertadores](299730.md) in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Copa_Libertadores).

At the end of the year, the club also reached the quarterfinals of the [2000 Campeonato Brasileiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Jo%C3%A3o_Havelange) playoffs and played their 3rd consecutive [Copa Mercosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Copa_Mercosur) final, which was also their 5th consecutive [CONMEBOL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) championship final since the second half of 1998 ([1998 Copa Mercosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Copa_Mercosur), [1999 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Copa_Libertadores), [1999 Copa Mercosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Copa_Mercosur), [2000 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Copa_Libertadores), and the [2000 Copa Mercosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Copa_Mercosur)).

The young Palmeiras team eventually lost the [2000 Copa Mercosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Copa_Mercosur) final to a [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clube_de_Regatas_Vasco_da_Gama) full of stars such as [Romario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romario), [Juninho Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juninho_Paulista), and [Euller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euller_\(footballer,_born_1971\)). The team was called “*bom e barato*” (good and cheap) and their success triggered a new management philosophy in the club, which was eventually proven to not be as effective in subsequent years.

Having won key national and international competitions, Palmeiras was proclaimed [Brazilian football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_football)'s *Best Team of the 20th Century of Brazil* by the [São Paulo State Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federa%C3%A7%C3%A3o_Paulista_de_Futebol) (FPF), the *[Folha de](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folha_de_S.Paulo) [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Estado_de_S._Paulo)* and *[Estado de São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Estado_de_S._Paulo)* newspapers, and the *Placar* magazine.[^cite_note-palmeiras.com.br-7]

### 2000s – Difficult years



Palmeiras against [Liga Deportiva Universitaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Deportiva_Universitaria_de_Quito) in 2009

Parmalat's sponsorship ended in 2000, leaving Palmeiras in dire straits. After reaching the [2001 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Copa_Libertadores) semi-finals (where they lost to the [Boca Juniors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Juniors)), the club had a bad 2002 season that ended in relegation to the [Série B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_B), but immediately returned in 2004.

The 2004 and 2005 seasons were rather successful, with the team finishing in 4th both years and qualifying for the [Libertadores](299730.md) in 2005 and 2006. Palmeiras was eliminated by rivals [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_FC) in the Round of 16 in both years.

In 2007, [Edmundo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmundo_\(footballer\)) played his last season for the club, just missing out on the top 4 in [Série A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A) in the final game of the season.

In 2008, Palmeiras arrived at a sponsorship agreement with Traffic, a sports marketing agency. The club made big investments on new players and [Vanderlei Luxemburgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanderlei_Luxemburgo). This new strategy paid dividends as Palmeiras won their [22nd Paulista Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Campeonato_Paulista), capped off with a 5–0 victory over [Ponte Preta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associa%C3%A7%C3%A3o_Atl%C3%A9tica_Ponte_Preta) in the second leg of the final. Palmeiras finished 4th in the [Campeonato Brasileiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A), which qualified them for the [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Copa_Libertadores) edition of the [Copa Libertadores](299730.md).

### Nubank Parque



Nubank Parque

The year of 2008 also marked the beginning of the planning phase for a new stadium for the club, as well as remodeling the social club, to prepare Palmeiras for the club's centenary year in 2014. The planned arena is now known as [Nubank Parque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubank_Parque).

In 2009, the club reached the quarterfinals of Copa Libertadores, eventually losing to Uruguayan side [Nacional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Nacional_de_Football) on away goals. In the same season, Palmeiras came close to winning the [Brazilian League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A), but political problems inside the club caused internal turmoil and affected on-field performances, and Palmeiras finished the season in fifth place.

From 2010 to 2014, Palmeiras played its home matches in the municipal [Pacaembu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_do_Pacaembu), as the [Estádio Palestra Itália](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Palestra_It%C3%A1lia) was demolished.

### 2012: Copa do Brasil and relegation



Marcos Assunção

In 2012, Palmeiras won the [Copa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Copa_do_Brasil) for the second time, beating [Coritiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coritiba_Foot_Ball_Club) in the final.[^cite_note-17] Led by manager [Luiz Felipe Scolari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luiz_Felipe_Scolari) and captained by midfielder [Marcos Assunção](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcos_Assun%C3%A7%C3%A3o), Palmeiras did not suffer a defeat in the entire tournament.

Less than three months after winning the Copa do Brasil, Scolari would leave the club due to poor performances in the league. He was replaced by [Gilson Kleina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilson_Kleina),[^cite_note-18] the manager of [Ponte Preta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associa%C3%A7%C3%A3o_Atl%C3%A9tica_Ponte_Preta), but the team failed to improve its performances and was relegated to the [Campeonato Brasileiro Série B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_B) for the second time in its history after a draw against [Flamengo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clube_de_Regatas_Flamengo) on November 18, [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A).[^cite_note-19]

### 2013–2015: Beginning of a resurgence



Palmeiras' supporters at Nubank Parque

In 2013, now under the administration of newly elected President [Paulo Nobre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulo_Nobre),[^cite_note-20] Palmeiras was easily promoted back to the first division with six games to spare, ensuring their participation in the [2014 Série A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A) for the club's centennial season.

Palmeiras suffered a setback early on in its 2014 [centennial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A) season as head coach Kleina was sacked, swiftly followed by the departure of striker [Alan Kardec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Kardec) and defender [Henrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrique_Adriano_Buss).[^cite_note-21] Argentinian [Ricardo Gareca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Gareca) was signed to coach the team after the break for the [World Cup in Brazil](656933.md), but failed to meet expectations and was sacked after a short spell. [Dorival Júnior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorival_J%C3%BAnior) replaced Gareca until the end of the season, as the club was fighting against relegation. With first-choice goalkeeper [Fernando Prass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Prass) and midfielder [Jorge Valdivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Valdivia) both returning from injury, the players led Palmeiras throughout the second half of the season as the club managed to avoid relegation and finished the season in 16th place.[^cite_note-22]

#### Another Copa do Brasil



Zé Roberto

In 2015, Palmeiras underwent an extensive rebuilding project, hiring a new coach and new football director. The club signed 25 players over the year and promoted several new talents from the club's youth teams, while almost every player from the 2014 squad was moved on. Palmeiras also improved its official paid supporters program, *Avanti*, eventually reaching 114,000 paid supporters.[^cite_note-23]

This was also Palmeiras' first season playing in their newly built stadium, the [Nubank Parque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubank_Parque), which seated 43,713 fans and included fully covered spectator seating; it was inaugurated on 19 November 2014.[^cite_note-24]

Palmeiras reached the [2015 Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Campeonato_Paulista) finals, which they lost on penalties to rivals [Santos FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_FC).[^cite_note-25]

On 9 June 2015, manager [Oswaldo de Oliveira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswaldo_de_Oliveira) was sacked by Palmeiras due to a slow start to the [Campeonato Brasileiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A).[^cite_note-26] On 10 June 2015, Palmeiras reached an agreement with [Marcelo Oliveira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Oliveira), recently sacked by [Cruzeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruzeiro_Esporte_Clube) despite having won the Brazilian league with them in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A) and [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A).

Palmeiras won the [2015 Copa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Copa_do_Brasil) on 2 December 2015. After a 1–0 loss to Santos in the first leg, the players were received at the stadium by more than 40,000 supporters both inside and outside Nubank Parque. Palmeiras won the second leg 2–1, with both goals scored by [Dudu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudu_\(footballer,_born_1992\)), before winning the trophy on penalties (with Fernando Prass saving a penalty and converting the winning penalty). [Gabriel Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Jesus) was the great revelation of that team, while the experience of midfielder [Zé Roberto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%A9_Roberto) was also fundamental for Palmeiras to win the competition.

With this title, Palmeiras increased its supremacy as Brazil's greatest champion, with 12 national titles (8 league titles, 1 Brazilian Champions Cup and 3 Copa do Brasil titles).[^cite_note-27] As champions of the 2015 Copa do Brasil, Palmeiras also secured a place in the [2016 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Copa_Libertadores) group stage.

### 2016–2018: Back on top of Brazilian football



Yerry Mina

On 12 March 2016, Palmeiras reached a verbal agreement with [Cuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuca_\(footballer,_born_1963\)) to become its new manager.[^cite_note-28] Palmeiras re-signed [Zé Roberto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%A9_Roberto), while also signing other key players for the 2016 season, including [Dudu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dudu_\(footballer,_born_1992\)), [Edu Dracena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edu_Dracena), [Moisés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mois%C3%A9s_Lima_Magalh%C3%A3es), [Róger Guedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%B3ger_Guedes), [Jean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_\(footballer,_born_1986\)), [Yerry Mina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerry_Mina), and [Tchê Tchê](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tch%C3%AA_Tch%C3%AA).

#### 2016: 9th national title



[Gabriel Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Jesus)

After a dominant year, leading the league for 29 weeks, Cuca led Palmeiras to their ninth league title, and first in 22 years, making it the club with the most league titles in Brazil. [Gabriel Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Jesus) was the team's leading scorer with 12 goals. On 27 November 2016, Palmeiras was guaranteed the title before the 38th week, beating Chapecoense at home 1–0 with a goal from [Fabiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabiano_Leismann) in the 26th minute.[^cite_note-29] With an attendance of 40,986 supporters, this broke the old record attendance of 40,035 from 12 July 2016 against Santos.

#### 2018: 10th national title



Dudu in 2018

On 25 November 2018, Palmeiras clinched its 10th [Campeonato Brasileiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A) title after defeating [Vasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CR_Vasco_da_Gama) 1–0 in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md). On 2 December 2018, Palmeiras played their last game of the season in front of a record-breaking crowd of 41,216. With a 3–2 win over [Vitória](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esporte_Clube_Vit%C3%B3ria), Palmeiras set a new Campeonato Brasileiro record for the longest undefeated streak (23 matches).[^cite_note-30]

### 2020: Treble



[Breno Lopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breno_Lopes_\(footballer,_born_1996\))

In 2020, during the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Brazil), Palmeiras beat archrival Corinthians in a historic final of the Campeonato Paulista. In the first final in the history of the competition without any spectators, Palmeiras won after beating Corinthians in a penalty shootout.[^cite_note-31]

On 30 January 2021, Palmeiras, led by Portuguese coach Abel Ferreira, won the [2020 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Copa_Libertadores) against [Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_Futebol_Clube) by a score of [1–0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Copa_Libertadores_Final) at the [Maracanã](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maracan%C3%A3_Stadium), in [Rio de Janeiro](25936.md). It was Palmeiras' second title in the competition.[^cite_note-32] [Breno Lopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breno_Lopes_\(footballer,_born_1996\)) scored the only goal of the match nine minutes into second-half stoppage time.

As champions, Palmeiras qualified for the [2020 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) in [Qatar](8238258.md) and earned the right to play against the winners of the [2020 Copa Sudamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Copa_Sudamericana) in the [2021 Recopa Sudamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Recopa_Sudamericana).[^cite_note-33]

In March 2021, Palmeiras won the [2020 Copa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Copa_do_Brasil), defeating [Grêmio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gr%C3%AAmio) with an overall score of 3–0. It was the 4th Copa do Brasil title for Palmeiras.[^cite_note-34]



[Abel Ferreira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Ferreira)

### 2021: Defending the Libertadores



Palmeiras' supporters at Estadio Centenario

On 27 November 2021, Palmeiras won the [2021 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Copa_Libertadores) against [Flamengo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clube_de_Regatas_do_Flamengo), with a score of [2–1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Copa_Libertadores_Final), in a single match in a decisive confrontation held at [Estadio Centenario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Centenario), in [Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montevideo), [Uruguay](31843.md). Once again led by the Portuguese coach Abel Ferreira, Palmeiras managed to win the second consecutive title in less than a year – the first time they ever achieved such feat in their history.[^cite_note-35]

[Raphael Veiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Veiga) and [Deyverson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deyverson) scored for the *Verdão* five minutes into the first half and into extra time respectively. [Gabriel Barbosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Barbosa) scored the only goal for Flamengo at 72 minutes.

As champions, Palmeiras qualified for the [2021 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) in the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), and earned the right to play against the winners of the [2021 Copa Sudamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Copa_Sudamericana) in the [2022 Recopa Sudamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Recopa_Sudamericana).[^cite_note-36]

On 20 January 2022 the [International Federation of Football History & Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_of_Football_History_&_Statistics) announced Palmeiras as the winner of the Men's World Best Club Ranking of 2021. It was the first time a Brazilian club finished a year on top of this ranking.

### 2022: Setting new records



The Palmeiras squad with the Campeonato Paulista trophy

In January 2022, Palmeiras' under-20s won the [Copa São Paulo de Futebol Júnior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_S%C3%A3o_Paulo_de_Futebol_J%C3%BAnior) for the first time, defeating rival [Santos FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_FC) 4–0 in the final. This victory brought home the only major youth trophy that had yet to be won by Palmeiras, and served as the culmination of a sustained period of investment in the youth teams, beginning in 2013.

In February 2022, Palmeiras played the [2021 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIFA_Club_World_Cup) and finished in the second place after beating [Al-Ahly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Ahly_SC) in the semifinals, and losing the final match 2–1 in extra time against [Chelsea](7473.md) after a penalty confirmed by the [video assistance referee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_assistant_referee).

In March 2022, Palmeiras defeated [Athletico Paranaense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletico_Paranaense) by 4–2 on aggregate and won the [2022 Recopa Sudamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Recopa_Sudamericana).



The Palmeiras squad with the Recopa trophy

In April 2022, Palmeiras won their 24th [Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Campeonato_Paulista) after beating [Sao Paulo FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sao_Paulo_FC) in the Finals by 5–3 on aggregate, coming back from a 3–1 loss in the first leg.

In November 2022, Palmeiras won their 11th [Campeonato Brasileiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A) after a solid season, having lost only three games. The trophy was the remaining possible trophy to be won by [Abel Ferreira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Ferreira) in South America.

### 2023: Another victorious season

In January 2023, Palmeiras won the Copa São Paulo de Futebol Júnior for the second time in a row, defeating [América Mineiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%A9rica_Mineiro) 2–1 in the final. The *Verdão* then added the [2023 Supercopa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Supercopa_do_Brasil) to their trophy cabinet on 28 January 2023 after scoring 4–3 against [Clube de Regatas Flamengo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clube_de_Regatas_Flamengo). The match was played at [Mane Garrincha Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Nacional_Mane_Garrincha) in [Brasília](4752.md).

As they did the season before they reached the Campeonato Paulista finals without a single loss, however, Palmeiras lost the first leg of the final by a 2–1 scoreline against [Esporte Clube Água Santa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esporte_Clube_%C3%81gua_Santa), but proceeded to win the second leg 4–0 as they had done the year before to win their 25th, and second consecutive Campeonato Paulista title.

In the [Copa Libertadores](299730.md), Palmeiras entered directly into the group stage and was drawn into Group C alongside Barcelona from Guayaquil, Ecuador, Bolívar from Bolivia, and Cerro Porteño from Paraguay, having the best campaign in the group stage. In the round of 16, eliminated [Atlético-MG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clube_Atl%C3%A9tico_Mineiro) after winning the first leg away at Mineirão 1-0 and drawing 0–0 at home at Nubank Parque. In the quarterfinals, they faced [Deportivo Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportivo_Pereira) from Colombia. Palmeiras won the first leg at [Estádio Hernán Ramírez Villegas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Hern%C3%A1n_Ram%C3%ADrez_Villegas) with a 4–0 victory and drew 0–0 in the second leg at home. In the semifinals, they were defeated by Argentine side [Boca Juniors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Juniors) after drawing both matches and losing in the penalty shootout.

In the [Copa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_do_Brasil), Palmeiras entered in the third round, where they faced [Tombense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tombense_Futebol_Clube) by draw and won 5–3 on aggregate. They defeated [Fortaleza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortaleza_Esporte_Clube) 3–1 on aggregate in the round of 16 but were eliminated in the quarterfinals by their rival [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_FC) with a 1-3 aggregate score.

In the Campeonato Brasileiro, with young [Endrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endrick_\(footballer,_born_2006\)) standing out as the main highlight Palmeiras debuted at home against Cuiabá. They frequently held the second place in the tournament standings but reached the top of the table in the 34th round and maintained their position until the end of the competition. They clinched their 12th Brazilian title by drawing with [Cruzeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruzeiro_Esporte_Clube) in the final round, further solidifying their status as the most successful national champion and repeating their achievement from 1994 when they last secured consecutive championships.

### Current season (2024)

Palmeiras' season started on 4 February 2024, with the [Supercopa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercopa_do_Brasil), where they faced their local rivals São Paulo. The match ended in a 0–0 draw, and São Paulo won 4–2 on penalties to claim their first title in the tournament.

In the [Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Paulista), Palmeiras was placed in Group B. Palmeiras remained unbeaten until the first leg of the finals, where they lost 1–0 to [Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_FC). In the second leg at their home stadium, Nubank Parque, Palmeiras defeated Santos 2–0, securing their 26th title.

Palmeiras got knocked out of the Copa Libertadores in the Round Of 16 competition phase. They faced Botafogo as their opponents, they lost 2–1 in the first leg at the Estadio Olimpico Nilton Santos. In the second leg, they conceded two more goals but scored 3, the last one being a last-minute goal that was disallowed because of the video assistant referee. The score ended 4–3 on aggregate.

## Stadium

### Estádio Palestra Itália (1917–2010)

The [Estádio Palestra Itália](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Palestra_It%C3%A1lia) was home of Palmeiras from 1917 to 2010. The venue was also known as *Parque Antártica* because the area was a park built by the Antarctica Paulista Brewing Company in the beginning of the last century, before being acquired by Palmeiras in 1920. In the past its capacity was listed as 35,000 spectators. However, even though its grandstands were extended in the late 1990s, it held only seats 27,640 people[^cite_note-37] due to regulations which enforce safety and comfort.



Estádio Palestra Itália in 2010

It was one of the most important Brazilian grounds, considering the amount of decisive and important matches played there. Examples of matches played in Palestra Itália include [1999 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Copa_Libertadores) final, the [Copa Mercosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Mercosur) finals of 1998, 1999 and 2000, [1996 Copa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Copa_do_Brasil) final and several [Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campeonato_Paulista) finals.

The last official match played in the stadium was against Grêmio for the Série A on 22 May 2010, and the last match played was a friendly against [Boca Juniors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Juniors) on 9 July 2010.

### Allianz Parque (2014–present)



The new [Allianz Parque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz_Parque)



External view



External view

Opened in November 2014, the Allianz Parque has 43,713 covered seats, being 25,395 lower seats, 14,888 upper seats and 3,430 in the cabins.[^cite_note-WTorre_Engenharia-1] The stadium was built for multipurpose events. Many other facilities are in place, including an enhanced parking area, a VIP area, a media center for up to 1,000 media members, 3 restaurants and bars and a heliport. The first official game at Allianz Parque was held on 19 November 2014, between Palmeiras and Sport in the Brazilian Série A, when hosts Palmeiras lost to Sport Recife 0–2. The first official goal of the stadium was scored by Ananias.

On 14 June 2015, Palmeiras won their first Brasileirão match in Allianz Parque with a 2–1 win over [Fluminense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluminense_FC).

## Kit

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [Sociedade Esportiva Palmeiras kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Sociedade%20Esportiva%20Palmeiras%20kits).

Palmeiras' first kit consisted of green jerseys, white shorts and green socks. Palmeiras' first jersey was blue jersey tribute to Italian National team. After that, a green with a horizontal white band, and a white with a red Savoy cross as the crest.[^cite_note-38] Palmeiras have played in blue shirts many times as a tribute to the Italian National Team. Their supporters are also well known for creating the *mancha verde* (green stain) of fog and smoke when Palmeiras is entering the pitch.

From 2007 to 2009, Palmeiras used a third jersey: a light yellow shirt with dark green shorts and socks, produced by Adidas.&#x20;

In 2010 Palmeiras the light yellow jersey became the second jersey, and started using a blue and white shirt, with white shorts, for their third jersey.

In 2016, Palmeiras announced the extension of the sponsorship agreement with Crefisa and FAM (Faculdade das Américas), which have exclusivity in the uniform of Palmeiras. The two companies, which are part of the same group controlled by the couple José Roberto Lamacchia and Leila Pereira, paid Palmeiras around R\$78 million (\$20 million) a year, the highest amount ever deposited by a partner in the history of Palmeiras. Palmeiras' uniform is among the most valuable uniforms in [South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONMEBOL) since 2016.[^cite_note-39]

## Supporters



Palmeiras' supporters in the [Estádio Palestra Itália](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Est%C3%A1dio_Palestra_It%C3%A1lia)

Originally, Palmeiras was a club heavily supported by Brazilians of Italian descent in São Paulo State. Over time, that distinction has reduced, and today the fan base is very diverse.

Palmeiras' largest [supporters group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torcida_organizada) are the Mancha Alvi-Verde (*White and Green Stain*, a green version of [Phantom Blot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phantom_Blot)), TUP (the oldest group), Acadêmicos da Savóia (the newest group), among others. There are big concentrations of Palmeiras fans across Brazil and in some places across the world known as “consulados” (consulates).

According to the famous Brazilian journalist and Palmeiras fan [Joelmir Beting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joelmir_Beting), who was a huge fan of the club, “it is unnecessary to explain the emotion of supporting Palmeiras to its fans, and impossible to do it to the non-fans”. This became one of the club's mottos and reflected in the walls of [Allianz Parque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz_Parque)'s home dressing room.

## Rivalries



Palmeiras against [Corinthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Club_Corinthians_Paulista) in 2010

### Corinthians

Main article: [Paulista Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paulista_Derby)

Palmeiras' biggest rival is [Corinthians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_Club_Corinthians_Paulista). The rivalry between the two clubs is considered Brazil's greatest, and the most intense in country, entering the conversation of being one of the biggest rivalries in the world, and considerably one of the oldest as well. Their matches are known as the Paulista Derby.

The most important matches between the two clubs were the [1999 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Libertadores_1999) quarterfinals and [2000 Copa Libertadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Libertadores_2000) semifinals, both won by Palmeiras on penalties.

Palmeiras and Corinthians played the [1994 Campeonato Brasileiro Série A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Campeonato_Brasileiro_S%C3%A9rie_A) final, also won by Palmeiras 4–2 on aggregate.

Other relevant Palmeiras and Corinthians matches:

- **Palmeiras 4-0 Corinthians**, [1993 Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Campeonato_Paulista) Final Match – Palmeiras ended a series of 16 years without winning a championship.

- **Palmeiras 1-0 Corinthians**, [1974 Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Campeonato_Paulista) Final Match – Palmeiras won the Campeonato Paulista and increased Corinthians's negative series without winning any championships to 21 years.

- **Palmeiras 1-1 Corinthians**, [1954 Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Campeonato_Paulista) Final Match – Corinthians won the Campeonato Paulista dedicated to São Paulo City's quadricentennial.

- **Palmeiras 8-0 Corinthians**, [1933 Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_Campeonato_Paulista) Season Match – Largest score of the Derby.



Palmeiras against São Paulo in 2007

The Derby is often featured in Brazilian popular culture. The game is the central plot of [Mazzaropi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%A1cio_Mazzaropi)'s film *O Corintiano* (1967), about a barber who is also a Corinthians fan that does not charge services from other Corinthians fans and does not like to provide services to Palmeiras fans.

In [O Casamento de Romeu e Julieta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O_Casamento_de_Romeu_e_Julieta), the rivalry between Palmeiras and Corinthians played a major role in the plot.

### Santos

Main article: [Clássico da Saudade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cl%C3%A1ssico_da_Saudade)

[Santos FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos_FC) is another major rival; the games between the two clubs are called the Clássico da Saudade (*The Good Times Classic*). Located in the [city of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santos,_S%C3%A3o_Paulo), only 76 km (47,5 mi) from São Paulo, Santos is also one of the 4 big clubs of the [state](229422.md).

The most important matches between the two clubs were the [2020 Copa Libertadores Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Copa_Libertadores_final) and [2015 Copa do Brasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Copa_do_Brasil) Final, both won by Palmeiras.

### São Paulo

Main article: [Choque-Rei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choque-Rei)

[São Paulo FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_FC) is another local rival; the games between the two clubs are called the Choque-Rei (*Clash of Kings*).

The most important matches between the two clubs were played in [Copa Libertadores](299730.md). Palmeiras won the [Copa Libertadores 2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Libertadores_2021) quarterfinals by 4–1 on aggregate. [Sao Paulo FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sao_Paulo_FC) won the second round matches of [Copa Libertadores 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Libertadores_1994), [Copa Libertadores 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Libertadores_2005) and [Copa Libertadores 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Libertadores_2006).

The most recent final match featuring both teams was the [2022 Campeonato Paulista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Campeonato_Paulista) won by Palmeiras. The first leg was held on 30 March 2022, and São Paulo FC defeated Palmeiras 3–1. The second leg was held on 3 April 2022 and Palmeiras made a comeback to defeat their rivals 4–0.

## Club culture

### Official mascot

The club's official mascots are a green [parakeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parakeet), named Periquito, and a [pig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig), named Gobatto.[^cite_note-51]

In 1986, at the Campeonato Paulista playoffs, supporters adopted the pig as their mascot.[^cite_note-pig-52] Although the parakeet is the official mascot, fans will refer to and yell: "PORCO!" (Pig) enthusiastically during matches, as the pig became their preferred mascot.

On 6 November 2016, Palmeiras incorporated the pig as one of the official mascots of the club.

### Media

Palmeiras official YouTube channel, [TV Palmeiras](https://youtube.com/palmeiras), has more than 1.8 million subscribers. The channel often exhibits highlights of the training sessions, pre-game preparations, post-game interviews, history facts, etc.

Palmeiras official Instagram account, [Palmeiras](https://instagram.com/palmeiras?igshid=Zjc2ZTc4Nzk=), has more than 4.4 million subscribers.

Some other channels are dedicated to Palmeiras, for example the [PodPorco](https://youtube.com/c/PodPorco), which is a podcast managed by Palmeiras fans with the purpose of interviewing people identified with the club, including players, former managers, journalists, famous supporters, etc.

### Anthem

Palmeiras' anthem was composed in 1949 by conductor Antonio Sergi. Sergi also wrote the lyrics for the anthem, but did that under the pseudonym Gennaro Rodrigues.[^cite_note-53]
