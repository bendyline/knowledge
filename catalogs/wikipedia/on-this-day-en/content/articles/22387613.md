Many [gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas) have [toxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxicity) properties, which are often assessed using the [LC<sub>50</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LC50) (median lethal concentration) measure. In the United States, many of these gases have been assigned an [NFPA 704](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFPA_704) health rating of 4 (may be fatal) or 3 (may cause serious or permanent injury), and/or [exposure limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_exposure_limit) ([TLV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threshold_limit_value), [TWA/PEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permissible_exposure_limit), [STEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-term_exposure_limit), or [REL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recommended_exposure_limit)) determined by the [ACGIH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ACGIH) professional association. Some, but by no means all, toxic gases are detectable by [odor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odor), which can serve as a warning. Among the best known toxic gases are [carbon monoxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_monoxide), [chlorine](5667.md), [nitrogen dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_dioxide) and [phosgene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosgene).

## Definitions

- Toxic: a chemical that has a median lethal concentration (LC

  50

  ) in air of more than 200 [parts per million](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parts_per_million) (ppm) but not more than 2,000 parts per million by volume of gas or vapor, or more than 2 milligrams per liter but not more than 20 milligrams per liter of mist, fume or dust, when administered by continuous inhalation for 1 hour (or less if death occurs within 1 hour) to albino rats weighing between 200 and 300 grams each.

  [^cite_note-1]

  [^cite_note-upenn-2]

- Highly Toxic: a gas that has a LC

  50

  &#x20;in air of 200 ppm or less.

  [^cite_note-upenn-2]

- [NFPA 704](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFPA_704): Materials that, under emergency conditions, can cause serious or permanent injury are given a Health Hazard rating of 3. Their acute inhalation toxicity corresponds to those vapors or gases having LC

  50

  &#x20;values greater than 1,000 ppm but less than or equal to 3,000 ppm. Materials that, under emergency conditions, can be lethal are given a Health Hazard rating of 4. Their acute inhalation toxicity corresponds to those vapors or gases having LC

  50

  &#x20;values less than or equal to 1,000 ppm.
