The **chairman of the Hamas Political Bureau** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): رئيس المكتب السياسي لحركة حماس, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Ra’īs al-Maktab as-Siasi li-Ḥarakat Ḥamās*), also known as the **chairman of the Hamas Shura Council** (Arabic: رئيس مجلس شورى لحركة حماس, romanized: *Ra’īs Majlis Shūra li-Ḥarakat Ḥamās*) from 1987 until 2004, is the overall and *de facto* leader of [Hamas](13913.md), a [Palestinian](23267.md) [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) [Islamist](15012.md) political and military organisation that has been [governing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas_government_in_the_Gaza_Strip) the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) since [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gaza_\(2007\)). The position is currently held by [Khalil al-Hayya](43349601.md), who was elected in July 2026.[^cite_note-\:0-17]

The chairman of the Hamas Political Bureau is expected to oversee the organization and its various components, while [military operations](1703333.md) are managed separately by military commanders. The chairman serves as a figurehead for Hamas during [Palestinian elections](1381784.md) and becomes the central leader in the operations against [Israeli occupation](46216.md). Additionally, he plays a crucial role in [foreign relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Hamas), leading negotiations with Israeli officials regarding [peace processes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_peace_process), [fostering reconciliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah%E2%80%93Hamas_reconciliation_process) with [Fatah](11104.md), and enhancing ties with other [Middle Eastern](19323.md) countries.

## History

Main article: [History of Hamas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Hamas)

[Ahmed Yassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Yassin), the founder of [Hamas](13913.md), became the first chairman of the Hamas Shura Council and *de facto* leader of Hamas from December 1987 until March 2004. Following his [assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Ahmed_Yassin), his deputy, [Abdel Aziz al-Rantisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel_Aziz_al-Rantisi) took over for only 26 days before he was [assassinated by Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Abdel_Aziz_al-Rantisi).

The chairman of the Hamas Political Bureau, [Khaled Mashal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Mashal), took over Hamas leadership; he was declared the overall and *[de facto](43594.md)* leader of Hamas from April 2004. Although he had held this position from 1996, he was not the overall leader of Hamas: the chairman of the Hamas Shura Council at that time was considered to be the *de facto* leader. Hamas elected [Mousa Abu Marzook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mousa_Abu_Marzook), the previous political bureau's chairman, as the deputy chairman of Hamas Political Bureau in January 1997.

In May 2017, [Ismail Haniyeh](41207463.md), the deputy chairman of Hamas Political Bureau, was elected by the Hamas Shura Council as the chairman of Hamas Political Bureau. Hamas also elected [Saleh al-Arouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saleh_al-Arouri) as the deputy chairman of Hamas Political Bureau. However, Al-Arouri was [assassinated by an Israeli strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Saleh_al-Arouri) in January 2024. Six months later, Haniyeh was assassinated in Iran while attending for the inauguration ceremony of the [President of Iran](233913.md), [Masoud Pezeshkian](2650383.md).[^cite_note-18]

On 31 July 2024, [Khaled Mashal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Mashal) was selected as the acting chairman of Hamas Political Bureau until the new leader was elected.[^cite_note-auto4-19][^cite_note-auto6-20] Mashal, the then-chairman of the Hamas Political Bureau, was expected to lead Hamas again.[^cite_note-21]

On 5 August 2024, [Muhammad Ismail Darwish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ismail_Darwish) was expected to become the next chairman of Hamas Political Bureau.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Before this, he serving as the chairman of the Hamas Shura Council from October 2023, succeeded [Osama Mazini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osama_Mazini), after his killing on 16 October 2023 by Israeli strike.

However, on 6 August 2024, [Yahya Sinwar](53195047.md) was officially appointed as the next chairman of Hamas Political Bureau and *de facto* leader of Hamas, six days after the [assassination](77478235.md) of his predecessor, [Ismail Haniyeh](41207463.md). The announcement came after the Shura Council, the body that elects Hamas' politburo, voted unanimously to choose Sinwar as the new leader, in what was described by a Hamas official as a "message of defiance to Israel".[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] [Khalil al-Hayya](43349601.md) was selected as the deputy chairman of Hamas Political Bureau on the same day. Previously, Al-Hayya was the deputy leader of Hamas in the Gaza Strip.



[Khalil al-Hayya](43349601.md), previous deputy chairman of the Hamas Political Bureau who served from 6 August 2024 until 16 October 2024, when Yahya Sinwar was [killed by the IDF](78140031.md). He is also serving as the deputy leader of Hamas in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) since 2017.

On 16 October 2024, Sinwar was [killed](78140031.md) after leading Hamas for only two and a half months.[^cite_note-28]

In the aftermath, [Khaled Mashal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Mashal) was performing duties again as acting *de facto* leader of Hamas, for the second time, until the new leader was elected.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

Sinwar recommended that, in case of his death, Hamas appoint a council of leaders to govern and manage the transition following his death. The Sinwar's recommended council include:[^cite_note-auto3-33][^cite_note-auto2-34]

- [Khaled Mashal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Mashal), second [chairman of the Hamas Political Bureau](#List_of_current_members_of_main_political_bureau) (1996–2017) and current [chairman of Hamas Political Bureau in the Diaspora / Palestinian Abroad](#List_of_current_members_of_political_bureau_in_the_Diaspora_/_Palestinian_Abroad) (2017–present)

- [Khalil al-Hayya](43349601.md), current deputy chairman of the Hamas Political Bureau (2024) and current [chairman of the Hamas Political Bureau in the Gaza Strip](#List_of_current_members_of_political_bureau_in_the_Gaza_Strip) (2017–present)

- [Zaher Jabarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaher_Jabarin), current [chairman of the Hamas Political Bureau in the West Bank](#List_of_current_members_of_political_bureau_in_the_West_Bank) (2024–present)

- [Muhammad Ismail Darwish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Ismail_Darwish), current chairman of Hamas Shura Council (2023–present)

- *Unknown*, known to be as the current secretary of the Hamas Political Bureau

The Hamas official indicated that [Khalil al-Hayya](43349601.md) has assumed responsibility for most political and [foreign affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Hamas) in addition to his direct oversight of Gaza-related matters and is effectively the acting *de facto* leader of Hamas.[^cite_note-auto5-40]

There were a number of potential successors, including [Khaled Mashal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Mashal), second chairman of Hamas Political Bureau from 1996 until 2017, [Mohammed Sinwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Sinwar), brother of [Yahya Sinwar](53195047.md) and one of the leaders of the [Ezzedeen Al-Qassam Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezzedeen_Al-Qassam_Brigades), [Zaher Jabarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaher_Jabarin), current Hamas leader in the [West Bank](33209.md), and [Khalil al-Hayya](43349601.md), current deputy chairman of Hamas Political Bureau since 2024 and longtime deputy of Yahya Sinwar in Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

In May 2025, Mohammed Sinwar, the new military leader of Hamas in Gaza, was [killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Gaza_European_Hospital_strikes) by Israeli military.[^cite_note-44]

In July 2026, [Khalil al-Hayya](43349601.md) was elected as the new chairman of the Hamas Political Bureau and *de facto* leader of Hamas, after defeating [Khaled Mashal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Mashal) in a narrow margin vote of 35 to 34.

## Structure of organisation and selection

Hamas inherited a tripartite organization of social services, religious instruction, and [military operations](1703333.md) overseen by a Shura Council. It used to have four different roles:

- a security service ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic): *Jehaz Aman*);

- a military division for acquiring weapons and conducting operations (Arabic: *Al-Mujahideen al Filastinun*);

- a charitable social welfare division (Arabic: *Dawah*); and

- a media branch (Arabic: *A'alam*).

Hamas is led both internally, in the [West Bank](33209.md) and the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), and externally, by two groups: Kuwaiti organization (*Kuwaidi*), led by [Khaled Mashal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_Mashal), and a Gaza group led by [Mousa Abu Marzook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mousa_Abu_Marzook), who was exiled first to [Damascus](8914.md) and subsequently to [Egypt](8087628.md). Following its leader Mashal's decision to demand that [Iraq withdraw from Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_invasion_of_Kuwait) and defy [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat)'s decision to support [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) in the invasion, the Kuwaiti group of Palestinian exiles started to receive substantial money from the Gulf States. [Ismail Haniyeh](41207463.md) was selected by the Hamas Shura Council in May 2017 to succeed Mashal as the leader of [Hamas](13913.md).

The organization's operational actions are concealed by a veil of secrecy, making its actual structure unclear. Although this has been called into question, Hamas formally claims that the wings are separate and independent. Its wings, it has been suggested, are both distinct and united for political purposes, both foreign and internal. The wide network of informants and the depth of Israeli intelligence surveillance pose challenges to communication between Hamas' military and political wings. Field commanders were granted more discretionary authority over operations and the political direction of the militant wing was weakened following [the assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Abdel_Aziz_al-Rantisi) of [Abdel Aziz al-Rantisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdel_Aziz_al-Rantisi).

The Shura Council (Arabic: *Majlis al-Shura*), with nearly 60 members, most in Gaza, is Hamas' highest consultative body, overseeing the election of the 15-member Political Bureau (Arabic: *Al-Maktab al-Siyasi*).[^cite_note-DarwishProfileAJ-45][^cite_note-46] It is modeled after the [Quranic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) idea of [shura](28840.md), or [popular assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_assembly), which Hamas officials claim allows for [democracy within an Islamic framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_and_democracy). The General Consultative Council, whose members are chosen from local council groupings, replaced the Shura Council as the organization became increasingly intricate and Israeli pressure mounted.

## Powers and duties

The chairman of Hamas Political Bureau was expected to rule over Hamas and all its components. However, there's some exception on its [military operations](1703333.md), who have their own military command.

The chairman also led the negotiations for [Hamas foreign relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Hamas) such as negotiations with [Israeli government officials](9282173.md) for [peace process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli%E2%80%93Palestinian_peace_process), [reconciliation process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatah%E2%80%93Hamas_reconciliation_process) with [Fatah](11104.md) and strengthen relations with [other countries](19323.md#Countries_and_territory) in the [Middle East](19323.md).

## Location

At Hamas's inception, Mashal was in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), [Palestine](241405.md) before relocating to [Amman](45007.md), [Jordan](7515964.md) from 1992 to 1997 after the [assassination of the group's second chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Abdel_Aziz_al-Rantisi). After that, they moved to [Damascus](8914.md), [Syria](7515849.md) due to conflict with King [Hussein of Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein_of_Jordan). He resided there from 1997 until 2012.

Hamas closed its office in Damascus in 2012 after supporting the [revolution](39750126.md) against Syrian President [Bashar al-Assad](364813.md). Assad repeatedly denounced Hamas for betrayal and hypocrisy. Hamas announced in August 2023 that it intended to reopen its office in Syria.[^cite_note-47]

Hamas's political leadership has resided in [Doha](26214389.md), Qatar, since 2012, in an arrangement supported by the United States. In 2024, Hamas explored moving its political headquarters to another country, such as Oman, amidst pressure from the United States and Israel over Qatar's failure to use its leverage with Hamas to facilitate a ceasefire deal to the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-WSJrelocate-48]

The previous chairman, [Yahya Sinwar](53195047.md), led Hamas from [Gaza Strip](12047.md), [Palestine](241405.md), since he also led the [Hamas government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamas_government_in_the_Gaza_Strip) in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md). Sinwar was the first chairman of Hamas Political Bureau to reside in [Gaza Strip](12047.md), doing so while the [Gaza war](74998519.md) was ongoing. The chairman of Hamas Political Bureau usually lived outside the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), due to the security reasons.

## List of current members of political bureau of Hamas

The Politburo comprises 15 members elected by the Hamas Shura Council every four years. Until his death on 16 October 2024 it was headed by Yahya Sinwar, who replaced Ismail Haniyeh in August 2024 following the assassination of Haniyeh. In addition to the main Politburo, Hamas has regional political bureau elected by four regional shura council, representing the West Bank, Gaza, the diaspora / Palestinian abroad, and Israeli prisoners.
