**Marjayoun** or **Jdeidet Marjayoun** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مرجعيون, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Marjʿayūn*:  [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Arabic) pronunciation [\[ˈmaɾʒ.ʕajuːn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic)), also **Marj 'Ayoun**, **Marjuyun** or **Marjeyoun** (lit. "meadow of springs") and **Jdeideh / Jdeida / Jdeidet Marjeyoun**, is a municipality in the [Marjeyoun District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjeyoun_District), [Nabatieh Governorate](4981449.md) in [Southern Lebanon](4205968.md).

## History

### Crusader period

On 10 June 1179, during the [Battle of Marj Ayyun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marj_Ayyun), the [Ayyubids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid) defeated the [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades). The Crusader king narrowly escaped capture.[^cite_note-1]

### Ottoman period

In 1596, Jadida appears in the [daftar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daftar) of [Ottoman](22278.md) *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* (subdistrict) of [Tibnin](6787186.md) in the *[liwa'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_\(Arabic\))* (district) of [Safad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safad), as a Muslim village of 28 households and 12 bachelors. The villagers paid a fixed tax-rate of 25% on agricultural products, such as [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), olive trees, vineyards, goats and beehives, in addition to "occasional revenues" and a press for olive oil or grape syrup; a total of 9,606 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e).[^cite_note-Hutteroth-2][^cite_note-3]

In 1875 [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) visited Marjayoun (which he called *Djedeideh*), and found 2,000 inhabitants, mostly "[Schismatic Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Church)" (i.e. [Melkite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite) [Uniats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniat)), but also some [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church_of_Antioch) and Muslims.[^cite_note-4]

[Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter)'s Cathedral, built in 1892, was restored in 1968 after a fire. It was also later restored in 2009.[^cite_note-5]

### 20th–21st centuries

During the [Syria-Lebanon Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria-Lebanon_Campaign) of [World War II](32927.md), British and Australian forces advancing from [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) entered the town on 11 June 1941, but were forced to withdraw on 15 June following a [Vichy French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_French) counterattack.[^cite_note-6] The [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) recaptured the town on 24 June in the [Battle of Merdjayoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Merdjayoun).[^cite_note-7]

Marjayoun was the headquarters of the [South Lebanon Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lebanon_Army), the militia that controlled southern Lebanon during Israel's occupation of the region after the [1982 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War) until its withdrawal from the region in 2000.[^cite_note-8] Following the pullout, many residents of Marjayoun fled to Israel, fearing accusations of collaboration.[^cite_note-9]

On 10 August 2006, after the breakdown of ceasefire negotiations in the [2006 Lebanon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Lebanon_War), Israeli forces took control of Marjayoun.[^cite_note-10] The next day, a convoy of 3,000 people fled from the town. The convoy was attacked by the Israeli Air Force (IAF) northeast of [Hasbaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasbaya) en route to [Kefraya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kefraya), in the south of the [Bekaa valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bekaa_valley). The bombing killed at least seven people, and is known as the [Marjayoun convoy incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjayoun_convoy_incident).[^cite_note-11]

On 29 May 2026, rockets fired by [Hezbollah](13919.md) struck Saint George's Orthodox Church in the village.[^cite_note-12]

## Geography

Marjayoun is 860 metres (2,822 ft) above sea level, standing on the west side of the [Jordan Rift Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Rift_Valley) just across from the ancient regional capital, [Caesarea Philippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarea_Philippi), which was located at the foot of [Mount Hermon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Hermon) on the east side of the Rift Valley.&#x20;

Marjeyoun stands on a hill facing Mt Hermon to the east, the [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem) castle of [Beaufort](6853436.md), set above the [Litani River](1920925.md) and overlooking [Mount Amel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Amil) (Jabal Amel), to the west, the [Mount Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon) range with the Rihan and Niha peaks to the north, with the fertile Marjeyoun plains extending southward into the [Galilee](12639.md) plains and the [Golan Heights](7515865.md).

## Demographics

In 2014 [Christians](4796145.md) made up 74.36% and [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Lebanon) made up 25.36% of registered voters in Marjayoun. 39.05% of the voters were [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Greek_Orthodox_Christians), 18.98% were [Greek Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Melkite_Christians) and 19.69% were [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Sunni_Muslims).[^cite_note-13]

The town of Marjayoun has a Christian majority population of about 5,000 people. [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Christianity_in_Lebanon) Christians compose the vast majority of the town's population, however, there are also [Maronite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronite_Christianity_in_Lebanon) and [Greek Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Christianity_in_Lebanon) Christians living in Marjayoun.[^cite_note-14] Outside the town, most villages in the surrounding valleys and mountains are predominantly [Shia](26961.md) Muslim.[^cite_note-15]

The Melkite [Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Peter)'s Cathedral was built in 1892 and restored in 1968 after a fire and in 2009.[^cite_note-16] Marjayoun is the seat of the [Melkite (Greek Catholic) Archeparchy of Baniyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Archeparchy_of_Baniyas), which includes the southeastern part of Lebanon.[^cite_note-17]

### Parliamentary representation

The [district of Marjayoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjeyoun_District), which includes the town, is largely [Shia Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Islam_in_Lebanon). It holds three seats in the Lebanese government, two belonging to Shia Muslims and one belonging to [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Christianity_in_Lebanon) Christians.

## Hospital

Marjayoun is home to a regional government hospital founded in 1960,[^cite_note-18] and a [Lebanese Red Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Red_Cross) First Aid Center.[^cite_note-19] This government hospital was closed due to [Israeli attacks to Lebanon in October 2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Israeli_invasion_of_Lebanon) and killed and injured number of hospital staff.[^cite_note-20]

## Marjayoun Airfield

An abandoned airfield is located 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) south of Metula. Ruins of buildings and outline of the runways and taxiway are all that remains. In a strategic triangle linking Lebanon with Israel and Syria are located the ruins of "Marjayoun Airport" or what is known as "Al-Marj Airport" or "English Airport". The green color of the Marjayoun Plain is only disturbed by forgotten walls from the days of World War II, their hard stones separating the fertile agricultural lands of the Marjayoun Plain. During the Second World War, the region of the Marjayoun Plain formed an arena of confrontation between the allies on one side and the German army on the other, so the allies had to fortify themselves, specifically in the Marjayoun Plain, which was a defensive area or a back line of confrontations if Egypt fell into the hands of the German army, or if the German Army managed to advance into Palestine, Lebanon and Syria.[^cite_note-21]
