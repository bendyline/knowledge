The **Western Cape** ([Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans_language): *Wes-Kaap* [\[ˈvɛskɑːp\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Afrikaans); [Xhosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xhosa_language): *iNtshona-Kolini*;[^cite_note-xhname-1] [Khoekhoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoekhoe_language): *Huri!hub*) is a [province](53215.md) of [South Africa](17416221.md), situated on the south-western coast of the country. It is geographically the [fourth largest of the country's nine provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_African_provinces_by_area), with an area of 129,449 square kilometres (49,981 sq mi), and [the third most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_African_provinces_by_population), with an estimated 7.43 million inhabitants in 2022.[^cite_note-cens2022-3]

About two-thirds of the province's residents live in the metropolitan area of [Cape Town](6653.md), which is also the provincial capital, and South Africa's second-largest city. The Western Cape was created in 1994 from part of the former [Cape Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Province). The two largest cities are [Cape Town](6653.md) and [George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George,_Western_Cape).

The Western Cape is generally regarded as the [best-run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_governance) of South Africa's provinces, with a robust system of governance, proactive administration, high quality [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure), and strong political and civil accountability.[^cite_note-The_worst-run_municipality_in_the_Western_Cape_for_3_years_in_a_row-8][^cite_note-Western_Cape_is_the_top_performing_Province_on_service_delivery,_General_Household_Survey-9] The province also has South Africa's lowest unemployment rate, by a significant margin.[^cite_note-The_only_province_in_South_Africa_where_unemployment_is_lower_than_20%-10]

As of the third quarter of 2023, the Western Cape's total real GDP was R656.27 billion, translating to 14.2% of South Africa's total GDP.[^cite_note-i003-11] The province has had the strongest-performing provincial economy in South Africa for a decade, with average annual growth rates well above the national average. Purchasing power is also strong in the Western Cape, with GDP per capita in the province significantly higher than the national average.[^cite_note-Western_Cape_tops_provincial_growth,_Free_State_at_the_bottom-12]

Furthermore, the Western Cape has South Africa's highest [secondary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education) [graduation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graduation) rate.[^cite_note-DA-run_Western_Cape_is_better_than_ANC_provinces:_Alan_Winde-13] In the 2024 Governance Performance Index (GPI), the Western Cape achieved the highest scores across all categories, by a large degree.[^cite_note-The_best_and_worst-run_municipalities_in_South_Africa-14]

[Cape Town](6653.md), the capital of the Western Cape, has the country's highest household incomes,[^cite_note-South_Africans_flock_to_Cape_Town-15] lowest rate of unemployment,[^cite_note-Cape_Town_Deviates_from_National_Labour_Trends-16] highest level of infrastructure investment,[^cite_note-2025_to_2026_Budget_-_Annexure_A-17] strongest service delivery performance,[^cite_note-Cape_Town_ranked_first_in_latest_Good_Governance_Africa_index-18] largest tourism appeal,[^cite_note-Cape_Town_crowned_world’s_top_city_for_travel_–_again-19] and most robust real estate market.[^cite_note-Cape_Town's_property_market_continues_to_outpace_other_cities_as_the_wealth_divide_grows-20]

## Geography



Topography of the Western Cape. The Roggeveld and Nuweveld mountains are part of the [Great Escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Escarpment,_Southern_Africa) (see diagrams below). The other mountain ranges belong to the [Cape Fold Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fold_Belt), also shown in the diagrams below. The Western Cape's inland boundary lies for the most part at the foot of the Great Escarpment.

The Western Cape is roughly L-shaped, extending north and east from the [Cape of Good Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_of_Good_Hope), in the southwestern corner of South Africa. It stretches about 400 kilometres (250 mi) northwards along the Atlantic coast and about 500 kilometres (300 mi) eastwards along the South African south coast ([Southern Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Indian_Ocean)). It is bordered on the north by the [Northern Cape](447008.md) and on the east by the [Eastern Cape](313645.md).

The total land area of the province is 129,462 square kilometres (49,986 sq mi),[^cite_note-cib2011-2]<sup>: 9 </sup> about 10.6% of the country's total. It is roughly the size of [England](9316.md) or the [State of Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Louisiana). Its capital city and largest city is [Cape Town](6653.md), and some other major cities include [Stellenbosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellenbosch), [Worcester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worcester,_Western_Cape), [Paarl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paarl), and [George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George,_South_Africa). The [Garden Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_Route) and the [Overberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overberg) are popular coastal tourism areas.

The Western Cape is the southernmost region of the African continent with [Cape Agulhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Agulhas) as its southernmost point, only 3,800 kilometres (2,400 mi) from the Antarctic coastline. The coastline varies from sandy between capes, to rocky to steep and mountainous in places. The only natural harbour is [Saldanha Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saldanha_Bay) on the west coast, about 140 kilometres (90 mi) north of Cape Town. However a lack of fresh water in the region meant that it has only recently been used as a harbour.

The province's main harbour was built in [Table Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Bay), which in its natural state was fully exposed to the northwesterly storms that bring rain to the province in winter, as well as the almost uninterrupted dry southeasterly winds in summer. But fresh water coming off [Table Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Mountain) and [Devil's Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil's_Peak_\(Cape_Town\)) allowed the early European settlers to build Cape Town on the shores of this less than satisfactory anchorage.

### Topography

The province is topographically exceptionally diverse. Most of the province falls within the [Cape Fold Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fold_Belt), a set of nearly parallel ranges of [sandstone folded mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Mountain_Sandstone_\(Geological_Formation\)) of [Cambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian)-[Ordovician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordovician) age (the age of the rocks is from 510 to about 330 million years ago; their folding into mountains occurred about 350 to about 270 million years ago).[^cite_note-compton-21][^cite_note-McCarthy-22][^cite_note-Truswell-23]

The height of the mountain peaks in the different ranges varies from 1,000 metres (3,300 ft) to 2,300 metres (7,500 ft). The valleys between ranges are generally very fertile, as they contain the weathered loamy soils of the [Bokkeveld mudstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fold_Belt#Bokkeveld_Group) (see the diagrams below).[^cite_note-McCarthy-22]

The far interior forms part of the [Karoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karoo). This region of the province is generally arid and hilly, with a [prominent escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Escarpment,_Southern_Africa) that runs close to the Province's most inland boundary.

#### Escarpment



View of [Table Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Mountain) at night.

The [escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment) marks the southwestern edge of [South Africa's central plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_South_Africa#Topographical_Divisions) (see the middle and bottom diagrams on the left).[^cite_note-McCarthy-22][^cite_note-Altas-24] It runs parallel to the entire South African coastline, except in the very far northeast, where it is interrupted by the [Limpopo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limpopo_River) valley, and in the far northwest, where it is interrupted by the [Orange River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_River) valley. The 1,000-kilometre-long (620 mi) northeastern stretch of the escarpment is called the [Drakensberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drakensberg), which is geographically and geologically quite distinct from the [Cape Fold Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fold_Belt#Cape_Supergroup), which originated much earlier and totally independently of the origin of the escarpment.[^cite_note-McCarthy-22][^cite_note-Truswell-23][^cite_note-McCarthy1-25][^cite_note-norman-26]

#### Rivers

The principal rivers of the province are the [Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berg_River) and [Olifants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olifants_River_\(Western_Cape\)) which drain into the Atlantic Ocean, and the [Breede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breede_River) and [Gourits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gourits_River) which drain into the Indian Ocean.

### Flora



The Red Disa ([disa uniflora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disa_uniflora)) is an orchid endemic to the Western Cape. It is the province's official flower.



The [Cape Floral Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_floristic_region) is one of the world's most diverse, and is found exclusively in the Cape.

The vegetation of the region is also extremely diverse, with one of the world's six [floral kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floral_kingdom) almost exclusively endemic to the province, namely the [Cape Floral Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_floristic_region), most of which is covered by [Fynbos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fynbos) (from the Afrikaans meaning "Fine Bush" (Dutch: Fijnbos), though precisely how it came to be referred to as such, is uncertain.).[^cite_note-manning-27][^cite_note-maytham-28]

These evergreen heathlands are extremely rich in species diversity,[^cite_note-manning-27][^cite_note-maytham-28] with at least as many plant species occurring on [Table Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Mountain) as in the entire United Kingdom.[^cite_note-maytham-28] It is characterised by various types of shrubs, thousands of herbaceous flowering plant species and some grasses.[^cite_note-manning-27] With the exception of the silver tree (*[Leucadendron argenteum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leucadendron_argenteum))*, which only grows on the granite and clay soils of the [Cape Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Peninsula),[^cite_note-manning2-29] open fynbos is generally treeless aside from the wetter mountain ravines where patches of [Afrotemperate forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Afrotemperate_Forest) persist.[^cite_note-manning-27][^cite_note-maytham-28]

The arid interior is dominated by [Karoo drought-resistant shrubbery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karoo#Karoo_flora). The [West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_South_Africa#Namaqualand) and [Little Karoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karoo#Divisions) are [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid) regions and are typified by many species of succulents and drought-resistant shrubs and [acacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia) trees.

The [Garden Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_Route) on the south coast (between the [Outeniqua Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outeniqua_Mountains) and the [Southern Indian Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Indian_Ocean)) is extremely lush, with temperate rainforest (or [Afromontane Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knysna-Amatole_montane_forests)) covering many areas adjacent to the coast, in the deep river valleys and along the southern slopes of the Outeniqua mountain range. Typical species are hardwoods of exceptional height, such as [Yellowwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podocarpus), [Stinkwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stinkwood) and [Ironwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ironwood) trees.

### Climate



View of the [Cape Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Peninsula) and [Boland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boland,_Western_Cape) region, seen from space

The Western Cape is climatologically diverse, with many distinct micro- and macroclimates created by the varied topography and the influence of the surrounding ocean currents. These are the warm [Agulhas Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agulhas_Current) which flows southwards along South Africa's east coast, and the cold [Benguela Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguela_Current) which is an upwelling current from the depths of the [South Atlantic Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_Ocean) along South Africa's west coast.[^cite_note-Branch-30][^cite_note-tyson-31]

Thus, climatic statistics can vary greatly over short distances. Most of the province is considered to have a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) with cool, wet winters and warm, dry summers.

Both the [Great Karoo and Little Karoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karoo), in the interior, have an [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid) to [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid) climate with cold, frosty winters and hot summers with occasional [thunderstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderstorms). The [Garden Route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_Route) and the [Overberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overberg) on the south coast have a maritime climate with cool, moist winters and mild, moist summers. [Mossel Bay](370621.md) in the Garden Route is considered to have the second mildest climate worldwide after Hawaii. The La Niña phase of the El Niño-Southern Oscillation cycle tends to increase rainfall in this region in the dry season (November to April).[^cite_note-Carlowicz_2021-32]

The effects of El Niño on rainfall in southern Africa differ between the summer and winter rainfall areas. Winter rainfall areas tend to get higher rainfall than normal and summer rainfall areas tend to get less rain. The effect on the summer rainfall areas is stronger and has led to severe drought in strong El Niño events.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

Sea surface temperatures off the west and south coasts of South Africa are affected by ENSO via changes in surface wind strength.[^cite_note-Nhesvure_2020-35] During El Niño the south-easterly winds driving upwelling are weaker which results in warmer coastal waters than normal, while during La Niña the same winds are stronger and cause colder coastal waters.

These effects on the winds are part of large scale influences on the tropical Atlantic and the [South Atlantic High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_High)-pressure system, and changes to the pattern of westerly winds further south. There are other influences not known to be related to ENSO of similar importance. Some ENSO events do not lead to the expected changes.[^cite_note-Nhesvure_2020-35]

Thunderstorms are generally rare in the province (except in the [Karoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karoo)) with most precipitation being of a [frontal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_front) or [orographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orographic) nature. Extremes of heat and cold are common inland, but rare near the coast. Snow is a common winter occurrence on the [Western Cape Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Fold_Belt) occasionally reaching down into the more inland valleys. Otherwise, frost is relatively rare in coastal areas and many of the heavily cultivated valleys.

### Cape Liberal Tradition

The Cape has had a long tradition of holding liberal values.[^cite_note-39] For example, the Cape Qualified Franchise before the Union of South Africa.

#### Cape Qualified Franchise

Main article: [Cape Qualified Franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Qualified_Franchise)

The Cape Qualified Franchise was the system of non-racial [franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffrage) that was adhered to in the [Cape Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Cape_Colony), and in the [Cape Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Province) in the early years of the [Union of South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_South_Africa). Qualifications for the right to vote at parliamentary elections were applied equally to all men, regardless of race.

This local system of multi-racial suffrage was later gradually restricted, and eventually abolished, under various [National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_\(South_Africa\)) and [United Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Party_\(South_Africa\)) governments. In 1930, [white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_South_African) [women were enfranchised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Enfranchisement_Act,_1930), and in 1931 [property qualifications for white voters were removed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franchise_Laws_Amendment_Act,_1931).

In 1936, [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_peoples_in_South_Africa) voters were then removed from the common voters' rolls and [allowed only to elect separate members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_Natives_Act,_1936) in 1936, and subsequently [denied all representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promotion_of_Bantu_Self-government_Act,_1959) in the [House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Assembly_\(South_Africa\)) in 1960. [Coloured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coloured) voters [similarly followed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coloured_vote_constitutional_crisis) in [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separate_Representation_of_Voters_Act,_1951) and [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separate_Representation_of_Voters_Amendment_Act,_1968), respectively.

#### Contribution of the Western Cape in the National Youth Uprisings

The [Black Consciousness Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Consciousness_Movement) (BCM) was a grassroots anti-Apartheid activist movement that emerged in South Africa in the mid-1960s out of the political vacuum created by the jailing and banning of the African National Congress and Pan Africanist Congress leadership after the Sharpeville Massacre in 1960. The BCM represented a social movement for political consciousness.[^cite_note-40]

In December 1968, the [South African Student Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Student_Organization) (SASO) was formed at a conference held in Marianhill, Natal. The conference was exclusively attended by Black students. After its launch, SASO became the medium through which black consciousness ideology spread to schools and other university campuses across the country.[^cite_note-41]

In 1974, South African Minister of Bantu Education and Development MC Botha, constituted the imposition of using [Afrikaans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrikaans) as a medium of instruction in black schools, effective with students in Grade 7 (Standard 5) upwards.[^cite_note-42]

Student leaders at the [University of the Western Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Western_Cape) (UWC) and the [University of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town) (UCT) organised marches. Poster parades by UWC and Black Power Salute marches by UCT was broken by the police, resulting in 73 students getting arrested and detained at [Victor Verster Prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Verster_Prison), near Paarl.

On 1 September 1976, the unrest spread to the city of Cape Town itself. Approximately 2000 black students from Western Cape townships, namely Langa, Nyanga and Gugulethu, matched the Cape Town central business district (CBD). Coloured students also contributed to the protests by peacefully marching to the city, but were blockaded by the police in the CBD. The protests turned violent when coloured students started burning schools, libraries and a magistrate's court in support of the student revolt. Thereafter, 200,000 coloured workers partook in a two-day strike staying away from work in the Cape Town area.

According to a report by the [Truth and Reconciliation Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_and_Reconciliation_Commission_\(South_Africa\)) (TRC), the Western Cape experienced the second highest number of deaths and casualties associated with the 1976 uprising protests.[^cite_note-43]

#### 1994 and the Western Cape post-apartheid

In 1994, at the introduction of the [Interim Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Constitution_of_South_Africa) and the [first non-racial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_general_election,_1994), South Africa's original provinces and [bantustans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantustan) were abolished and [nine new provinces](53215.md) were established. The former [Cape Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Province) was divided into the Western Cape, [Northern Cape](447008.md), Eastern Cape and part of [North West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_\(South_African_province\)).

In the 1994 election, the Western Cape was one of two provinces that did not elect an [African National Congress](2503.md) (ANC) provincial government (the other being [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md)). The [National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Party_\(South_Africa\)) (NP) won 53% of the votes and 23 seats in the 42-seat provincial legislature, and [Hernus Kriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernus_Kriel), a former Minister of Law and Order, was elected [Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_the_Western_Cape). He resigned in 1998 and was replaced by [Gerald Morkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Morkel).

The [1999 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_general_election,_1999) marked the beginning of a period of great turbulence in Western Cape politics. No party achieved an absolute majority in the provincial parliament, as the ANC won 18 seats while the [New National Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_National_Party_\(South_Africa\)) (NNP), successor to the NP, won 17. The NNP went into coalition with the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(South_Africa\)) (DP), which won 5 seats, to form a government, and Morkel remained Premier. In 2000 the DP and the NNP formalised their coalition by forming the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)) (DA).

In 2001, however, the NNP broke with the DA over the removal of [Peter Marais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Marais) from office as [Mayor of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Cape_Town) by DA leader [Tony Leon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Leon). The NNP instead went into coalition with the ANC; Gerald Morkel, who was opposed to the split, resigned as Premier and was replaced by Peter Marais. In 2002 Marais resigned as Premier due to a sexual harassment scandal, and was replaced by NNP leader [Marthinus van Schalkwyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marthinus_van_Schalkwyk).

During the [2003 floor-crossing period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_South_African_floor-crossing_window_period) four members of the provincial parliament crossed to the ANC, giving it an absolute majority of 22 seats in the 42-seat house. However, the ANC remained in coalition with the NNP and van Schalkwyk remained as Premier.

In the [2004 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_general_election,_2004), there was again no absolute winner in the provincial parliament; this time the ANC won 19 seats, the DA won 12, and the NNP won 5. The ANC-NNP coalition continued in power, but van Schalkwyk took up a ministerial post in the national cabinet and was replaced as Premier by the ANC's [Ebrahim Rasool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebrahim_Rasool).

The NNP was finally dissolved after the [2005 floor-crossing period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_South_African_floor-crossing_window_period) and its members joined the ANC, again giving that party an absolute majority of 24 seats. In the [2007 floor-crossing period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_South_African_floor-crossing_window_period) the ANC gained a further three members of the provincial parliament. In 2008 Rasool resigned as Premier due to internal party politics, and was replaced by [Lynne Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynne_Brown).

The [2009 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_general_election,_2009) marked a significant change in Western Cape politics, as the Democratic Alliance won 51% of the votes and an absolute majority of 22 seats in the provincial parliament, while the ANC won 14 seats with 31% of the vote. The DA leader [Helen Zille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Zille) was elected Premier. In 2010 the [Independent Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Democrats), which had won 3 seats with 5% of the vote, merged with the DA.

In the [2014 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_general_election,_2014) the DA won 59% of the votes and an absolute majority of 26 seats in the provincial parliament, while the ANC won 14 seats with 32% of the vote. In 2018 King Khoebaha Cornelius III Declared the independence of the "Sovereign State of Good Hope".[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

In the [2019 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_South_African_general_election), the DA retained their majority in the province, but with a reduction in support. It had won 24 seats with 55%. Helen Zille was term-limited and the DA premier candidate [Alan Winde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Winde) succeeded her. The ANC also lost support. It had received 12 seats with 28% support, its lowest showing since 1994. Veteran politician [Peter Marais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Marais) returned to the provincial parliament as the sole representative of the [Freedom Front Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Front_Plus). Patricia de Lille formed another party, [Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_\(political_party\)), and it achieved a seat.[^cite_note-46]

The DA continued to win a majority of the votes in the [2021 municipal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_South_African_municipal_elections), receiving 54% of the vote province-wide, with support in Cape Town at 58%.[^cite_note-47]

### Cape Independence Movement

Main article: [Cape independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_independence)

Since the late 2000s there has been growing support for Western Cape, or Greater Cape, independence from South Africa. Political parties such as the [Referendum Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Referendum_Party_\(South_Africa\)), [Freedom Front Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Front_Plus) and organisations such as the Cape Independence Advocacy Group[^cite_note-48] and CapeXit,[^cite_note-49] wish to bring forth the constitutional and peaceful secession of the Western Cape.[^cite_note-\:1-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

Proponents claim substantial support for the idea, with CapeXit having over 800,000 signed mandates in May 2021. Additionally, a poll conducted in 2023 by Victory Research on behalf of the Cape Independence Advocacy Group claimed that 58% of the Western Cape's registered voters would support independence, while 68% would support a referendum on the issue.[^cite_note-53]

## Law and government

Main articles: [Government of the Western Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Western_Cape) and [Politics of the Western Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_the_Western_Cape)



Provincial government headquarters in Cape Town

The provincial government is established under the [Constitution of the Western Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Western_Cape), which was adopted in 1998. The people of the province elect the 42-member [Western Cape Provincial Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Cape_Provincial_Parliament) every five years by a system of [party-list proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-list_proportional_representation). The sixth provincial parliament was elected in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Western_Cape_provincial_election); 24 seats are held by the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)), 8 by the [African National Congress](2503.md), 3 by the [Patriotic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotic_Alliance), 2 by the [Economic Freedom Fighters](40005536.md), and 1 each by the [African Christian Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Christian_Democratic_Party), [Al Jama-ah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_\(political_party\)), [Good](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_\(political_party\)), [Freedom Front Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Front_Plus), and [National Coloured Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coloured_Congress).

The provincial parliament is responsible for legislating within its responsibilities as set out by the [national constitution](441610.md); these responsibilities include agriculture, education, environment, health services, housing, language policies, tourism, trade, and welfare.

The provincial parliament also elects the [Premier of the Western Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_the_Western_Cape) to lead the provincial executive. [Alan Winde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Winde), a member of the [DA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)) and former Provincial Minister of Community Safety, has served as Premier since the [2019 provincial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Western_Cape_provincial_election).

The Premier appoints ten members of the provincial legislature to serve as a [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Council_of_the_Western_Cape) of ministers, overseeing the departments of the [provincial government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Western_Cape). These departments are Agriculture, [Community Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Cape_Department_of_Community_Safety), Cultural Affairs and Sport, Economic Development and Tourism, [Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Cape_Education_Department), Environmental Affairs and Development Planning, [Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Cape_Department_of_Health), Human Settlements, Local Government, Social Development, [Transport and Public Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Cape_Department_of_Transport_and_Public_Works), and the Provincial Treasury.

Politically, the Western Cape is a stronghold for the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(South_Africa\)) (DA). The DA has won an absolute majority of the vote in the province in every national, provincial, and municipal election since [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_South_African_general_election).

## Housing

See also: [Housing in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_in_South_Africa)

The Western Cape is one of South Africa's most desirable provinces in which to live, with many high net worth individuals moving to the province from elsewhere in the country in recent years (often referred to as "semigration"). The province is also popular amongst those immigrating to South Africa.

[Cape Town](6653.md), the province's capital, has South Africa's most-desirable and most robust [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate) market.[^cite_note-Cape_Town's_property_market_continues_to_outpace_other_cities_as_the_wealth_divide_grows-20] In 2021, the city's property market was valued at [R](298686.md)1.22 trillion, constituting 72% of the entire Western Cape residential property market value, and 21% of the national property market value.[^cite_note-Cape_Town_Housing_Market_Report_-_2021-59]

The Western Cape's average house price in 2024 was R2.33 million, which was well above the national average. Property market strength is also high, with property value growth at 8.7% year-on-year in January 2025, which was also significantly above the national average.[^cite_note-Message_to_South_Africans_who_want_to_buy_property_in_Cape_Town-60]

### Affordable housing

See also: [Affordable housing in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing_in_South_Africa)

Numerous [affordable housing](3531662.md) projects have been established in the [City of Cape Town](4043753.md), with the development of more underway. As of 2025, a total of 10 social housing estates have been built across Cape Town, with 3 of them located within 10 kilometers of [Cape Town CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town_CBD).[^cite_note-Explainer:_affordable_housing_in_Cape_Town-61]

In July 2025, [Western Cape MEC for Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Western_Cape), [Tertuis Simmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertuis_Simmers), and [Western Cape Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_the_Western_Cape), [Alan Winde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Winde), unveiled [Cape Town CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town_CBD)'s largest ever government housing development project, called Founders Garden. Located next to the [Artscape Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artscape_Theatre), the project will feature a high-rise, mixed-use development, and has a projected value of more than [R](298686.md)2 billion.[^cite_note-Western_Cape_launches_R2_billion_project_for_2,600_new_homes_in_Cape_Town-62]

In September 2025, the Western Cape Infrastructure Department announced that it had identified over 1,800 hectares of land for affordable housing development in the province. This includes 303 hectares of provincial government-owned land, and over 1,500 hectares of national government-owned land. The department stated that it was committed to addressing the housing backlog, but that doing so required the national government to release land.[^cite_note-More_than_1,800_hectares_of_land_identified_for_affordable_housing_projects_in_WC_-_dept-63]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of the Western Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Western_Cape)



As of 2023, the Western Cape has the highest [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) (HDI) in South Africa

In 2024, the Western Cape's total real GDP increased to [R](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Rand)666.75 billion. That equated to 14.2% of South Africa's total GDP, and a real GDP per capita of R87,859.[^cite_note-2024GDP-5][^cite_note-2024GDPpc-64] The Western Cape has the third-largest economy of South Africa's nine provinces, behind [Gauteng](309661.md) and [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md).

The Western Cape has had the strongest-performing provincial economy in South Africa for a decade, with an average annual growth rate of around 0.87% - well above the national average. Purchasing power is also strong in the Western Cape, GDP per capita in the province is significantly higher than the national average.[^cite_note-Western_Cape_tops_provincial_growth,_Free_State_at_the_bottom-12]

As of 2023, the Western Cape's [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) (HDI) is the highest in South Africa at 0.778, and has had SA's highest HDI for almost three decades. This is as compared to the 2023 [South African average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_African_provinces_by_Human_Development_Index) of 0.741.[^cite_note-Sub-national_HDI_-_Area_Database_-_Global_Data_Lab-65]

As of 2023, the biggest sector in the Western Cape's economy is the financial, business services and real estate sector, which constitutes 33.55% of gross value added, followed by manufacturing at 14.26% and wholesale and retail trade, hotels, and restaurants at 13.67%.[^cite_note-i003-11]

[Cape Town](6653.md), the province's capital, also has a thriving tech sector (especially [fintech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fintech)). The city is home to numerous tech headquarters, including those of [Yoco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoco), Admyt, Xneelo, [Jumo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumo), Luno, [Rain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rain_\(telecommunications\)), and Aerobotics.

The city also has a major [retail](183515.md) sector, with the headquarters of 9 of South Africa's major retail companies, including most of its largest [supermarket](51862.md) chains, such as [Woolworths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woolworths_\(South_Africa\)), [Pick n Pay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pick_n_Pay), [Clicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clicks_\(South_Africa\)), [Shoprite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoprite_\(retailer\)), [Foschini Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foschini_Group), and [Pepkor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepkor). The headquarters of South Africa's largest e-commerce retailer, [Takealot Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takealot_Group), is also located in Cape Town.

Customer service, fashion design, and advertising are industries rapidly gaining in importance in the Western Cape.[^cite_note-SAIRR-66]

Cape Town is home to a large [film industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_industry), with numerous international productions choosing to film on location in the city each year. A significant portion of the industry is based out of the [Cape Town Film Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Town_Film_Studios).[^cite_note-67]

As of 2023, Cape Town accounts for a large majority (roughly 73%) of the Western Cape's GDP.[^cite_note-i003-11]

95% of [wine produced in South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_wine) is produced in the Western Cape. South Africa is the 7th largest wine producing region in the world.[^cite_note-68]

The province's unemployment rate in Q2 2026 was 19.5% – the lowest in South Africa by a significant margin, and considerably below the national unemployment rate of 33.6%.[^cite_note-Quarterly_Labour_Force_Survey,_Quarter_2:_2026-69]

The Western Cape Government, via its Growth for Jobs strategy, aims to grow the Western Cape into a R1 trillion economy by 2035, and to increase private sector investment to a total of 20% of the province's GDP.[^cite_note-Western_Cape_announces_summit_to_catalyse_R1_trillion_economy-70]

## Transport



The N2 as it enters the [City Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Bowl) of [Cape Town](6653.md).



Railway network in the Western Cape

The Western Cape has an excellent network of highways comparable with any first-world country. The primary highways are the [N1](1211545.md) (from Cape Town to [Three Sisters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Sisters,_Northern_Cape), continuing outside the province towards [Bloemfontein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloemfontein) and [Johannesburg](58893.md)), [N2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N2_\(South_Africa\)) (from Cape Town to Bloukrans River, towards [Port Elizabeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Elizabeth)), [N7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N7_\(South_Africa\)) (from Cape Town to Bitterfontein, continuing towards [Springbok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springbok,_Northern_Cape) and [Namibia](21292.md)), [N9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N9_\(South_Africa\)) (from George to Uniondale, continuing towards [Graaff-Reinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graaff-Reinet) and [Colesberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colesberg,_Northern_Cape)) and [N12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N12_\(South_Africa\)) (from George to Three Sisters, continuing towards [Kimberley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberley,_Northern_Cape) and Johannesburg).[^cite_note-\:12-71]

Other routes are the "R" roads which connect the smaller towns. All major roads are tarred with major rural gravel roads well maintained. Limited access motorways are limited to the Cape Metropolitan Area, Winelands and Garden Route, however due to the low population density of the remainder of the province, the highways remain efficient and high-speed, except during peak holiday travel seasons, when travel can be slow-going in places due to heavy traffic.&#x20;

There were 260,952 people in the province who had been born outside of South Africa, comprising 4% of the population. In 2011, 894,289 residents of the Western Cape had been born in the [Eastern Cape](313645.md) (16% of the population), 167,524 in [Gauteng](309661.md) (3%) and 61,945 (1%) in [KwaZulu-Natal](193596.md).[^cite_note-CBiol-76]

Between 2001 and 2007 the Western Cape received the second-most internal migration within South Africa after Gauteng, with a large majority of these new Western Cape residents coming from the former [Transkei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transkei) region of the Eastern Cape, which served as the historic native reserve of the Cape Colony and the political banishment site for native "troublemakers".[^cite_note-77]

### Economic status

The average annual household income was [R](298686.md)143,460, the second-highest in the country after [Gauteng](309661.md).[^cite_note-censusrep2011-74]<sup>: 37 </sup> As of September 2012, 69% of the population aged 15–64 are economically active, and of these 25% are unemployed. Overall, 52% of the working-age population are employed.[^cite_note-78]

Around 2 million people in the Western Cape labour market (those aged 16 to 64) are employed, 1.3 million are not economically active, 552,733 are unemployed with an additional 122,753 who are discouraged work seekers who want to work but have given up looking for it.[^cite_note-CBiol-76]

90% of households in the province have a [flush toilet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flush_toilet)[^cite_note-cib2011-2]<sup>: 84 </sup> and 90% have refuse removed by the local council at least once a week.[^cite_note-cib2011-2]<sup>: 96 </sup> 75% of households have piped tap water inside the dwelling, while a further 13% have piped water on their property; 11% receive piped water at a community tap, while 1% have no access to piped water.[^cite_note-cib2011-2]<sup>: 77 </sup> One in seven people live in an informal dwelling.[^cite_note-CBiol-76]

86.9% of households use electricity for cooking,[^cite_note-cib2011-2]<sup>: 84 </sup> and 93% use it for lighting.[^cite_note-cib2011-2]<sup>: 93 </sup> 89% of households have a cellphone and 31% have a landline telephone, while 86% own a television, 81% own a refrigerator, and 34% own a computer.[^cite_note-cib2011-2]<sup>: 99 </sup> 44% of households have access to the Internet.[^cite_note-cib2011-2]<sup>: 101 </sup>

The [University of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town)

The Western Cape province has the most highly educated residents with a very skilled workforce in comparison to any other African region.[^cite_note-79] The high school graduation rate is consistently around 80%, higher than any other province. The proportion of adults with a degree or higher was 4.8% (2005),[^cite_note-SAIRR-66] the highest in the country.

The province also boasts four universities:

- [Cape Peninsula University of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Peninsula_University_of_Technology)

- [Stellenbosch University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellenbosch_University)

- [University of Cape Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Cape_Town)

- [University of the Western Cape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Western_Cape)

The province is also home to the [South African Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Military_Academy).

## Culture

### Cuisine

See also: [South African cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_cuisine)

Types of cuisine originating from the Western Cape include [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_cuisine) and [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_cuisine) cuisines. Other types of [South African cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_cuisine) are also found and commonly enjoyed in the province. Over 50% of all cheese in South Africa is produced in the Western Cape.[^cite_note-80] Four of the top ten entries in Trip Advisor's Best [Fine Dining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_dining) Restaurants – Africa list for 2021 are in the Western Cape.[^cite_note-81]

### Winelands

See also: [Western Cape wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Cape_wine)

The Western Cape is known for its wine production and vineyards.[^cite_note-82] The winelands are divided into [six main regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine_regions_of_South_Africa): Boberg, [Breede River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breede_River_Valley), Cape South Coast, Coastal Region, [Klein Karoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klein_Karoo) and [Olifants River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olifants_River_\(Southern_Cape\)). Each has unique climate, topography and fertile soil. Distilled wine or [brandy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandy) is produced in the Cape Winelands, Overberg, and Garden Route districts of the province.[^cite_note-83] Brandy from these regions is regarded as amongst the best in the world due to the high, legally-enforced distilling standards in the region, technically making it equivalent to [Cognac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognac).[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-Oxford-85]
