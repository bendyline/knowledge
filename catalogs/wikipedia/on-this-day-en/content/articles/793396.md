In law, **_motu proprio_** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) for 'on his own impulse') describes an official act taken without a formal request from another [party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_\(law\)). Some jurisdictions use the term *[sua sponte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sua_sponte)* for the same concept.

In [Catholic canon law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_canon_law), it refers to a document issued by the [pope](23056.md) on his own initiative and personally signed by him.[^cite_note-ODCC-1] Such a document may be addressed to the whole church, to part of it, or to some individuals.[^cite_note-ODCC-1] The first papal *motu proprio* was [promulgated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promulgation_\(Catholic_canon_law\)) by [Pope Innocent VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Innocent_VIII) in 1484.[^cite_note-EnCath-2] It continues to be a common form of [papal rescript](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_rescript), especially when establishing institutions, making minor changes to law or procedure, and when granting favours to persons or institutions.

## Catholic Church

### Effect

An important effect of issuing a document in this way is that a rescript containing the clause *motu proprio* is valid and produces its effect even in cases where fraud would ordinarily have vitiated the document, since the pope does not rely on the reasons alleged when he grants a favour.[^cite_note-EnCath-2] Withholding of the truth in what, according to canonical law, style and practice, must for validity be expressed, normally renders a rescript invalid, but not if the rescript is issued *motu proprio*.[^cite_note-3] Consequently, canonists traditionally called the clause the "mother of repose".[^cite_note-EnCath-2] The designation *motu proprio* indicates that the validity of the document is independent of the validity of whatever reasons may have been adduced in a request for its issuance.[^cite_note-EnCath-2]

However, a *motu proprio* has no effect in so far as it harms the acquired right of another or is contrary to a law[^cite_note-4] or approved custom, unless it expressly states that it is derogating from these matters.[^cite_note-5]

### Form

A *motu proprio* rescript begins by giving the reasons for issuing it, and then indicates the law or regulation made or the favour granted. It is less formal than a constitution and carries no papal seal. Its content may be instructional (e.g., on the use of [chant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregorian_chant)), administrative (e.g., concerning a church law or the establishment of a commission), or merely to confer a special favour.[^cite_note-6]

More generically, this phrase (or *proprio motu*; Latin allows [free word order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_word_order)) is used to indicate an act taken by a court without a [motion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motion_\(legal\)) from a party to the case. The term is used very rarely in legal opinions in the United States, where *[sua sponte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sua_sponte)* is preferred, but *proprio motu* is used in Canada.[^cite_note-7] *Proprio motu* is used to refer to a decision by the prosecutor of the [International Criminal Court](14880.md) to initiate an investigation into a situation without a referral from the [Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_Council) or a state party; this power is granted by article 15(1) of the [Rome Statute](68762.md). [European Court of Justice](87187.md) judgments use the phrase "of its own motion" and its equivalent in other community [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_European_Union).[^cite_note-8]

As it relates to a monarch, the term *motu proprio* describes the condition of a royal decree being made expressly on the sovereign's initiative, a practice more usual in some nations than in others.

Relating to [orders of chivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orders_of_chivalry), like the [Order of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Malta), the [grand master](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_master_\(order\)) according to the statutes can confer the order *motu proprio*, instead of in response to a nomination by a national delegation.
