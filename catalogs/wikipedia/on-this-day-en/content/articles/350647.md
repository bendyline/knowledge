The **prime minister of Singapore**[^cite_note-2] is the [head of government](37570.md) of [Singapore](27318.md). The [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Singapore) appoints the prime minister on the [advice and consent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent) of the [Cabinet of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Singapore). The incumbent prime minister is [Lawrence Wong](32336575.md), an MP for [Marsiling–Yew Tee Group Representation Constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marsiling%E2%80%93Yew_Tee_Group_Representation_Constituency) from the governing [People's Action Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Action_Party), who took office on 15 May 2024.[^cite_note-3]

Singapore is modelled after the [Westminster system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_system). The prime minister only governs with the [confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confidence_and_supply) of the majority in [Parliament](1890794.md); as such, the prime minister typically sits as a [member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament) (MP) and leads the largest party or a [coalition](6036.md) of parties. In practice, the prime minister is the leader of the majority party in Parliament.

## History

The office of prime minister succeeded the office of [chief minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Singapore) in 1959 after Singapore had attained [self-governance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-governance_of_Singapore) from the United Kingdom, as the State of Singapore,[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] with [Lee Kuan Yew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Kuan_Yew) being sworn in as the first prime minister on 5 June 1959.[^cite_note-6]

Concerning Singapore's autonomous status in the Malaysia Agreement, the title of prime minister remained unchanged after Singapore's [merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_in_Malaysia) with [Malaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Malaya), [Sarawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_Sarawak) and [North Borneo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Colony_of_North_Borneo) to form [Malaysia](3607937.md) in 1963, despite the existence of the [prime minister of Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister_of_Malaysia) for the entire federation of which Singapore was briefly a part.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Following the independence of Singapore in 1965, the office of Prime Minister was retained, with the [president of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Singapore) becoming a ceremonial head of state. In 1991, amendments to the [Constitution of Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Singapore) vested executive powers in the presidency, along with discretionary veto powers over the [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Singapore).[^cite_note-9] The Constitution also vests "general direction and control of the government" in the [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Singapore), with the president almost always bound to act on the advice of the Cabinet or any minister acting under the Cabinet authority. Thus, in practice, most of the actual work of governing is done by the prime minister and Cabinet.[^cite_note-10]

Under Article 26(4)(a) of the Constitution of Singapore, the prime minister may appoint a [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Singapore) minister, subject to [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Singapore) approval, as acting prime minister if the prime minister is on medical leave, away from Singapore or is granted a leave of absence under Article 32 of the Constitution.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] The deputy prime minister or [senior minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_Minister_of_Singapore) will usually become the acting prime minister. Still, any Cabinet minister may be appointed to the role.

## Authority

Under the Constitution, executive power is nominally vested in the president. However, the president can only exercise that authority on the advice of the Cabinet or a minister acting under general Cabinet authority. Hence, in practice, the prime minister, as the Cabinet's leader, does most of the actual work of governing.

The prime minister is responsible for overseeing the government's day-to-day affairs and executing government policy. As leader of the majority party in Parliament, the prime minister is also responsible for passing legislation through Parliament.&#x20;

The prime minister also nominates the [speaker of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Parliament_of_Singapore) and [leader of the house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_House_\(Singapore\)), who are responsible for arranging government business and organising legislative programmes, usually under the directive of the prime minister and the Cabinet.[^cite_note-13]

The prime minister chooses the other members of the Cabinet by advising the president; the president must exercise their powers in accordance with the advice of the prime minister. The prime minister may change, retain, or revoke any sitting minister's appointment under their prerogative.[^cite_note-14] The prime minister also advises the president on appointments, such as [Attorney-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney-General_of_Singapore), and [Permanent Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_secretary) of a ministry.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

The prime minister can advise the president to make a Proclamation of Emergency; the president issues the Proclamation if satisfied.[^cite_note-17]

The prime minister can declare a defence or security measure,[^cite_note-18] and has executive authority over the [Singapore Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Armed_Forces) (SAF) through the Armed Forces Council, which consists of Minister for Defence, Permanent Secretaries of the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Singapore\)) (MINDEF), [Chief of Defence Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Defence_Force_\(Singapore\)), [Chief of Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Army#List_of_chiefs_of_Army), [Chief of Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Singapore_Air_Force#List_of_chiefs_of_Air_Force) and [Chief of Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Navy_\(Singapore\)); all of whom are appointed by the president under the prime minister's advice.[^cite_note-19]

## Privileges

See also: [Cabinet of Singapore § Remuneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Singapore#Remuneration)

[Sri Temasek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sri_Temasek) is the prime minister's official residence, though none of the prime ministers have ever lived there. [The Istana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Istana) is the working office of the prime minister.[^cite_note-20] Since 2024 however, Sri Temasek has been the working office of the Prime Minister due to the ongoing renovations at The Istana, as confirmed by Lawrence Wong in his National Day Address.[^cite_note-21] The prime minister is protected by the Specialised Security Command of the [Singapore Police Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Police_Force) (SPF), who also ensure the protection of the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Singapore), [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_Singapore) ministers and visiting foreign dignitaries.[^cite_note-22]

The annual salary package the prime minister would receive, including the 13th-month bonus, Annual Variable Component, and National Bonus, which is twice the MR4 benchmark, stands at S\$2.2 million. As there is no one to decide on the annual performance bonus for the prime minister, the prime minister's bonus will be based only on the National Bonus.[^cite_note-23] The prime minister is also on the Medisave-cum-Subsidised Outpatient (MSO) scheme—where an additional 2% of their gross monthly salary will be credited into their Medisave account.[^cite_note-24]

The prime minister's annual salary was S\$3.07 million before a salary review in 2011.[^cite_note-PSD_Salary_2012-25] On 21 May 2011, a committee was set by Prime Minister [Lee Hsien Loong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Hsien_Loong), to review the salaries of the prime minister as well as the president, ministers, members of Parliament and other political officeholders.[^cite_note-26] After the recommended wage reductions by the committee were accepted in Parliament, the prime minister's salary was reduced by 36%—including the removal of his pension to S\$2.2 million or approximately US\$1.7 million at that time.[^cite_note-PSD_Salary_2012-25] A further revision of the salary on 8 September 2026 further increased their annual salary to S\$3.6 million (approximately US\$2.8 million), which becomes effective starting 15 October.[^cite_note-27] Nonetheless, the Prime Minister's salary remains the most expensive salary for a politician worldwide to date.[^cite_note-28]

Unlike many heads of government, Singapore's prime minister does not have a dedicated government aircraft and instead travels on commercial flights. When representing the government abroad, they typically fly with [Singapore Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Singapore_Airlines) or, for less prominent destinations, with its budget subsidiary [Scoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoot).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]
