**Nigerians** are the citizens and nationals of [Nigeria](21383.md), as well as people with ancestry from there. The name Nigeria was derived from the [Niger River](21437.md) running through the country. This name was allegedly coined in the late 19th century by British journalist [Flora Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_Shaw,_Lady_Lugard), who later married Baron [Frederick Lugard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Lugard,_1st_Baron_Lugard), a British colonial administrator.[^cite_note-25] Nigeria is composed of various [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups) and [cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture) and the term *Nigerian* refers to a [citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship)-based civic [nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationality).[^cite_note-Gordon_(2003),_233-26] Though there are multiple ethnic groups in Nigeria, economic factors result in significant mobility of Nigerians of multiple ethnic and religious backgrounds to reside in territories in Nigeria that are outside their ethnic or religious background, resulting in the mixing of the various ethnic and religious groups, especially in Nigeria's cities.[^cite_note-Toyin_Fall_2001._pp._8-27] The [English language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language_in_England) is the *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca)* of Nigerians.[^cite_note-April_A._Gordon_2003._pp._233-28] Nigeria is divided roughly in half between [Muslims](19541.md), who live mostly in the north, and [Christians](18337522.md), who live mostly in the south; [indigenous religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions), such as those native to the [Ibibio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibibio_people), [Efik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efik), [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odinani) and [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_religion) ethnicities, are in the minority.[^cite_note-USEN-29]

Nigerians come from multiple ethnic, cultural and religious backgrounds as the founding of [Nigeria](21383.md) was the outcome of a [colonial creation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Nigeria) by the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire).[^cite_note-April_A._Gordon_2003._pp._233-28]

## History

Further information: [History of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nigeria)

There have been several major historical kingdoms and states in Nigeria that have influenced Nigerian society through their kings and their legal and taxation systems, and the use of religion to legitimize the power of the king and to unite the people.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._15-16-30] [Northern Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Nigeria) has been culturally influenced by Islam, including several major historic Islamic states in the region.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._15-16-30] The [Songhai Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songhai_Empire), [Kanem-Bornu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem-Bornu_Empire) and the [Sokoto Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokoto_Caliphate) were major historical Islamic states in northern Nigeria.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._15-16-30] Southern [Nigeria](21383.md) historically held several powerful states, including the [Benin Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_Empire), [Ibibio Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibibio_Kingdom), [Efik Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efik_Kingdom), [Oyo Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyo_Empire), and [Aro Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aro_Confederacy).[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._15-16-30]

## Culture

[Nigerian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_culture) was profoundly affected by the [British colonial rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Nigeria).[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._18-31] Such as British colonial authority's denouncement and attacks upon [polygamy](24475.md), trial by ordeal, and certain types of sacrifices.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._18-31] At the same time, British colonial authorities maintained and promoted traditional Nigerian culture that strengthened colonial administration.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._18-31] The [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) spread [Christianity](5211.md) throughout southern Nigeria and Christian missionaries assisted British authorities in establishing a [Western](21208200.md)-style education system in [Nigeria](21383.md) that resulted in the teaching of [English language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) in Nigeria and its subsequent adoption as Nigeria's main language.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._18-31] The British replaced unpaid household labor with [wage labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wage_labour).[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._18-31] Prior to colonisation in the twentieth century, Nigeria's tribes usually [possessed the land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_tenure) as a community, such that land could not be bought or sold.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._4-32] [Colonisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonization) brought the notion of individuals owning land and the commercialisation of land began.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._4-32]

In Nigeria, more than fifty percent of Nigerians live in [villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village) of two different types: the first type used by the [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_people), [Ibibio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibibio_people) and [Tiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiv_people) involves a collection of dispersed compounds while the second type used amongst the [Hausa-Fulani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa%E2%80%93Fulani), [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people), and Kanuri involves nuclei of compounds.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._6-33] These villages compose members of the ethnicity-related through ancestry as well as strangers who have been assimilated into the ethnicity.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._6-33] Since the time prior to colonisation to the present it has been a common practice of Nigeria's tribes to adopt strangers into the tribes.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._4-32] A male elder in the community commonly serves as a village chief or head.[^cite_note-Toyin_Falola_2001._pp._6-33]

In the large cities of Nigeria, there is a substantial [intermingling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermingling) of Nigerians with foreigners, especially Europeans, [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_people), and [Indians](7564733.md).[^cite_note-Toyin_Fall_2001._pp._8-27] The economic importance of Nigeria's cities has resulted in migrations of people from their traditional ethnic or cultural homeland to cities outside those territories.[^cite_note-Toyin_Fall_2001._pp._8-27] Igbo, Hausa-Fulani and Ibibio people have commonly migrated to [Lagos](85232.md) and many southerners migrate to the north to trade or work while a number of northern seasonal workers and small-scale entrepreneurs go to the south.[^cite_note-Toyin_Fall_2001._pp._8-27]

## Sectarianism

Ethnic, religious, and regional disputes and tensions have commonly divided Nigerians on political issues.[^cite_note-April_A._Gordon_2003._pp._111-36] In particular, cultural and political divisions between the [Muslim](6037917.md) north and the [Christian](5211.md) south has politicised religion and caused significant political disputes in Nigeria.[^cite_note-April_A._Gordon_2003._pp._111-36] Ethnic-motivated and religious-motivated violence by extremists has increased these tensions as well.[^cite_note-April_A._Gordon_2003._pp._233-28]

However, despite instances of extremism, most Nigerians continue to peacefully coexist, and a common Nigerian identity has been fostered amongst the more educated and affluent Nigerians as well as with the many Nigerians who leave small homogeneous ethnic communities to seek economic opportunities in the cities where the population is ethnically mixed.[^cite_note-April_A._Gordon_2003._pp._233-28] Although there are cultural divisions amongst Nigerians, the English language is commonly used as their primary language.[^cite_note-April_A._Gordon_2003._pp._233-28] Also, most Nigerians share a strong commitment to individual liberties and democracy.[^cite_note-April_A._Gordon_2003._pp._233-28] Even during periods of military rule, such military governments were pressured to maintain democratic stances by the Nigerian people.[^cite_note-April_A._Gordon_2003._pp._233-28] Nigeria's political figures are commonly known as multiple indigenous languages outside their own indigenous language.[^cite_note-April_A._Gordon_2003._pp._233-28]
