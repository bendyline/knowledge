**Tulkarm** or **Tulkarem** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): طولكرم, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Ṭūlkaram*) is a city in the [West Bank](33209.md), [Palestine](241405.md) and the capital of the [Tulkarm Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulkarm_Governorate). [Netanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netanya) is to the west in [Israel](9282173.md), while [Nablus](233369.md) and [Jenin](50997.md) are to the east. According to the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics), in 2017 Tulkarm had a population of 64,532.[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1] Tulkarm is under the administration of the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority).

## Etymology

The [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) name translates as 'length of vineyard'[^cite_note-3] but is a distortion of the [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic_language) name *Tur Karma*[^cite_note-4] ('mount of the vineyard'), which was used for Tulkarm by the [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusaders) and by the mediaeval [Samaritan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritan) inhabitants.[^cite_note-Palmer194-2][^cite_note-5]

## History

[Benjamin Mazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Mazar) identified Tulkarm with the toponym *Birat Seriqa* (בירת סריקא, lit. '[Saracen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saracen) tower' or 'vineyard tower'), mentioned in the [Talmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talmud) (b. [AZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avodah_Zarah) 31a; y. AZ 5\:4) as located near the Samaritan town of Burgata (בורגתא/ברקתא, perhaps from *[burgus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgus)*, 'tower'[^cite_note-6]), which may be modern Burj al-Atut.[^cite_note-7] However, [Félix-Marie Abel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9lix-Marie_Abel) put Birat Seriqa closer to [Kafr Qallil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_Qallil),[^cite_note-8] and others put it near [Qalqilya](851829.md).[^cite_note-9] [Isaiah Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isaiah_Press) and Dov Zudkevitz suggest the true site may be somewhat west of Tulkarm, at [Tel Zureiqiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Zureiqiya?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the [Poleg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poleg) basin.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

### Ayyubid and Mamluk periods

During the [Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty) era, after the Muslim reconquest of Palestine under Sultan [Saladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladin) in 1187, the first families to settle in Tulkarm were from the [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people) clan of Zaydan.[^cite_note-JQ-12] A military group, the Zaydan were dispatched to the Wadi al-Sha'ir area, which includes Tulkarm, by Saladin to buttress the defense of the western approaches of Muslim-held Palestine from the Crusaders who dominated the coastal area.[^cite_note-JQ-12]

The Zaydan politically dominated Tulkarm and the vicinity until the early 17th century. Around 1230, during the late Ayyubid period, a group of [Arabs](2185.md) from southern Palestine immigrated to Tulkarm. They had originally migrated to Palestine from [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia) many generations prior and had become semi-nomadic farmers and grazers.[^cite_note-JQ-12] Among the Arab families were the Fuqaha clan, who were considered *[ashraf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashraf)* (related to the [Islamic](6037917.md) prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad)) and served as the *[ulama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulama)* (religious scholars) of the village.[^cite_note-JQ-12]

During the Ayyubid, and later the [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate_\(Cairo\)) era (1260–1517), the majority of Tulkarm's lands were made part of a *[waqf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqf)* (religious trust) to support the [al-Farisiyya Madrasa](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/category\:Al-Farisiyya%20Madrasa), an [Islamic](6037917.md) religious school in [Jerusalem](16043.md), located north of the [Masjid Al-Aqsa](65719.md) compound. Two-thirds of the village's farmlands were confirmed as part of this trust in 1354 by the deputy-governor of Damascus, Faris al-Din al-Baki. During Mamluk rule another wave of Arab immigrants arrived in Tulkarm from [North Africa](21714.md) and nearby [Nablus](233369.md). They largely engaged in agriculture and animal husbandry, supplying hides to leather merchants in the coastal villages, retaken from the Crusaders in the second half of the 13th century.[^cite_note-JQ-12]

### Ottoman era

Tulkarm was incorporated into the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in 1517. Afterward, Sultan [Suleiman the Magnificent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suleiman_the_Magnificent) (r. 1520–66) transferred Tulkarm's *waqf* to the al-Jawhariyya Madrasa ([Commons](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category\:Al-Jawhariyya)), located in the [Muslim Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_Quarter_\(Jerusalem\)), northwest of the al-Aqsa Mosque. Under this arrangement, Tulkarm's inhabitants paid a third of their harvest as a tax towards the *waqf*, called *qasm*. At the time of the *waqf'*&#x73; reassignment, the population of the village was estimated at 522 (95 households) and the *qasm* consisted of eight [carats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carat_\(mass\)) of wheat and three carats of barley. The town's elite families administered the trust, which enabled them to reach higher social and economic status. The population increased through intermarriage with families fleeing violent [feuds](292136.md) between the various clans of Jabal Nablus. By 1548, the population had grown to 189 households or roughly 1,040 persons.[^cite_note-JQ-12]

Tulkarm appeared in Ottoman tax registers in 1596 as being in the *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* (subdistrict) of [Qaqun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaqun), which was a part of the *[sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak)* (district) of [Nablus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nablus_Sanjak). The largest village in the *nahiya*,[^cite_note-JQ-12] Tulkarm had a population of 176 Muslim households (roughly 968 persons) and paid taxes on wheat, barley, summer crops, olives, goats, beehives and a press for olives or grapes.[^cite_note-13] During this early period of Ottoman rule, there were five neighborhoods (pl. *harat*) centered around the Shaykh Ali al-Jazri al-Mughrabi Mosque, today referred to simply as the "Old Mosque". The population was overwhelmingly [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim), and most residents were *[fellahin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallah)* (peasants who worked the land.) The elite families during that time were the Zaydan and the [Lajjun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lajjun)-based [Tarabay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turabay_dynasty), the latter belonging to the [Bani Harith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bani_Harith) tribe. Because of the decentralized nature of the Ottoman state, these families and their successors in later centuries ruled the area with a high degree of autonomy. The Zaydan had particular authority over Tulkarm, being appointed as the *mutassalim* (tax collectors or enforcers) on behalf of the central authorities.[^cite_note-JQ-12]

In the mid-17th-century most members of the Zaydan family, with the exception of the children and the elderly, were killed in a massacre by Tulkarm's inhabitants during [Friday prayers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_prayer). This was in reaction to the Zaydan having forced Tulkarm's residents to harvest and process the village's grains for taxation purposes. Consequently, political power in Tulkarm passed to the Badran clan, while the Fuqaha family took control of administering the *"waqf"* lands, firmly establishing them as the village's religious leaders. The Fuqaha had derived much of their authority from their classification as *ashraf* and their association with the Sufi [Rifa'iyya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rifa'i) *[zawiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zawiya_\(institution\))* of the village. The western neighborhood was mostly emptied of Zaydan members and would serve as the main area of settlement for newcomers.[^cite_note-JQ-12]

Tulkarm appears on sheet 45 of [Pierre Jacotin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Jacotin)'s map drawn up during [Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon)'s [invasion in 1799](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_campaign_in_Egypt_and_Syria), named *Toun Karin.*[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]



Tulkarm Municipality building

Following the adoption of the Ottoman Land Code in 1858, the *musha* (collective landownership) system was gradually abrogated and residents were required to register their property with the central authorities. The [fellahin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellah) were wary of registering their names for fear of military conscription by the Ottoman state and instead entrusted various elite clans with the role of landlords, who were in effect absentee owners. This altered the area's social structure, with the Samara, al-Hajj Ibrahim and Hanun clans legally obtaining vast swathes of Tulkarm's lands. Leadership of the town's two main religious establishments were generally supplied by the [Kur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kur,_Tulkarm)-based [Jayyusi clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayyusi_clan) and the al-Barqawi clan of [Shufa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shufa,_Tulkarm).[^cite_note-JQ-12] The Barqawi clan controlled the area around the town in the 19th century.[^cite_note-16]

The 1860s French explorer [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) visited Tulkarm, which he described as being of "considerable" size, with about 1,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-Guerin353-17] During this time, the Ottoman authorities granted the village an agricultural plot of land called Ghabat Tulkarm in the former confines of the Forest of Arsur (Ar. Al-Ghaba) in the coastal plain, west of the village.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]



Old City of Tulkarm

The *Survey of Western Palestine* in 1882 described Tulkarm as a "long straggling village, on high ground", surrounded by arable land and rock. There were several "good-sized" houses, mainly of stone in the village.[^cite_note-20]

Tulkarm became the administrative center of a new subdistrict (*[qada'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qada')*) Bani Saʿb-Tulkarm in 1876,[^cite_note-21] later becoming a municipality in 1892.[^cite_note-Thawab31-22] Tulkarm was also appointed a governor, bringing the residents who numbered only a few thousand and who were mostly *[fellahin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellahin)*, closer to the central government. This elevated status gave Tulkarm precedence over the nearby villages, which at that time also included [Qalqilya](851829.md). Tulkarm's center shifted from the Old Mosque to an empty space in the northwest as the town expanded northward with the construction of government buildings, a post office, a school and a hospital in that area.[^cite_note-23]

Around the turn of the 20th century, Tulkarm was one of the villages in which the Hannun family owned extensive estates. The Hannuns fostered close ties with clans in the village.[^cite_note-24]

Tulkarm became a major rail junction in 1908 on the [Hejaz Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz_Railway) line running up from [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Egypt_under_the_British) and southern Palestine to [Haifa](55606.md) and [Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre,_Israel) in the northwest, [Jerusalem](16043.md), [Nablus](233369.md) and [Ramallah](44132.md) to the south, [Lebanon](17771.md) to the north, and [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Syria) and [Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transjordan_\(region\)) to the east. The [Ottoman Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Army) used Tulkarm as one of its principal bases during the [Sinai and Palestine campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_and_Palestine_campaign) in [World War I](4764461.md). It was bombed by British planes carried by [HMS Anne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Anne_\(1915\)). It was captured by [British forces](4887.md) in 1918.[^cite_note-SandB-25]

### British Mandate era



The region of Tulkarm in the 1940s.

The [British Mandatory administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) (1942–1947) in [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) designated Tulkarm as the center of the [Tulkarm Subdistrict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulkarm_Subdistrict).[^cite_note-Fischbach494-26]

A road was constructed in 1920 to connect the town with [Netanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netanya) on the coast. In order to cope with a significant increase in population and unorganized infrastructural development, a civil planning scheme was designed for Tulkarm and its satellite villages in 1945. At the time Tulkarm was divided into four main sections, with the bulk of commercial activity concentrated along the north–south and east–west roads. Meanwhile, the town continued to expand past its northern fringes, which had previously been characterized by green spaces.[^cite_note-27]

Tulkarm became a haven for Palestinian Arab rebel activity during the [1936–1939 Arab revolt in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%931939_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine) against British rule. General Commander of the Revolt [Abd al-Rahim al-Hajj Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Rahim_al-Hajj_Muhammad) hailed from Dhinnaba, today part of Tulkarm municipality, and led many operations in the region.[^cite_note-28]

In the [1945 statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) the population of Tulkarm consisted of 8,090; of whom 7,790 were Muslims, 280 Christian and 20 "other",[^cite_note-1945p22-29] with a land area of 1,672 [dunams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunam) (urban) and 32,610 dunams (rural), according to an official land and population survey.[^cite_note-Hadawi45-30] Of this, 2,399 dunams were designated for citrus and bananas, 276 plantations and irrigable land, 28,256 for [cereals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cereal),[^cite_note-31] while 1,492 dunams were built-up areas.[^cite_note-32]

### Jordanian rule

During the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), Tulkarm was occupied by the [Iraqi Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Army) and later [annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) as part of the [Jordanian](7515964.md)-held [West Bank](33209.md). The [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements) between Israel and Jordan left roughly 30,000 dunams of Tulkarm's 32,610 dunams of land, mostly agricultural, in Israeli territory. In consequence, many residents moved to [Transjordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transjordan_\(region\)) or went abroad in search of employment.[^cite_note-33] Straddling the armistice line, Tulkarm was cut off from nearby Arab towns. Its principal economic and social connection was with Nablus.[^cite_note-34]

In 1950, the [Tulkarm Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulkarm_Camp) was established by [UNRWA](32270.md) in the city, comprising an area of 0.18 square kilometres (0.07 sq mi). Most of the refugees who resided in the camp came from [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa), [Caesarea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarea_\(modern_town\)) and [Haifa](55606.md). Today it is the second largest [Palestinian refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_refugee_camp) in the [West Bank](33209.md).[^cite_note-35] A period of significant municipal expansion began in Tulkarm after a new civil development scheme was authorized in 1961. As part of this plan, in 1963, the hamlet of Jarrad in the southeast and other lands in the northeast (total of 1.8 square km) were annexed to the city, while the eastern village of [Dhinnaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhinnaba) was incorporated into the municipality in 1964, adding another 0.75 square kilometres (0.29 sq mi) of territory. The village of [Shuweika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuweika) to the north and the smaller village of [Irtah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irtah) to the south were annexed in 1967.[^cite_note-Thawab37-36]

### Contemporary period



2018 [United Nations](1602284.md) map of the area, showing the [Israeli occupation](59076253.md) arrangements.

Since the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, Tulkarm has been under [Israeli occupation](59076253.md).[^cite_note-Fischbach494-26] A [military government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Military_Governorate) governed Tulkarm until transfer in 1982 to the [Israeli Civil Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Civil_Administration).

During the early months of the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada), 16 May 1989, Muhammad As'ad Fokhah, 50 years old, from Shuweika, died in [Megiddo prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megiddo_prison) after a three-day hunger strike. [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin) reported to a Member of [Knesset](74656.md) that Fokhah died of a [heart attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_attack) caused by [dehydration](155056.md) and that the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Defence_Force) investigation found that prison staff had acted in accordance with orders.[^cite_note-37]

In the wake of the 1993 [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords) between Israel and the Palestine Liberation Organization (PLO), control of Tulkarm was transferred to the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) (PNA) on 10 December 1995, becoming the third Palestinian city from which Israeli forces withdrew.[^cite_note-Fischbach494-26] During the early years of the [Second Intifada](71717.md), Israel temporarily reoccupied Tulkarm. Israeli military administration over Tulkarm ended in 2005, when control of the city was handed back to the PNA.[^cite_note-38] Upon assuming control of the city, the PNA instituted new weapons restrictions limiting militants to a single registered weapon that may not be loaded or carried in public.[^cite_note-39]

#### Gaza War

Main article: [Israeli incursions in Tulkarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_incursions_in_Tulkarm)

On 19 October 2023, the IDF [entered the city](75337172.md).[^cite_note-40] In August 2024, Israeli bulldozers destroyed miles of the city, including homes, businesses and infrastructure; Israeli soldiers blocked emergency responders from assisting residents. News reports include videos of this attack, as well as assertions by the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) that it is rooting out terrorism; and that it "undertakes all feasible precautions to avoid damaging essential infrastructure," while acknowledging that these "operations in the area have caused unavoidable harm to certain civilian structures."[^cite_note-41]

## Geography

The city is situated on the western edge of northern West Bank, about 15 kilometers (9.3 mi) west of [Nablus](233369.md) further southwest of [Jenin](50997.md) and 15 kilometers (9.3 mi) east of the Israeli coastal city of [Netanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netanya). It is bordered by the [1948 ceasefire line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Israel\)), with Israel's [Central](844282.md) and [Haifa Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifa_District) to the west, and Palestine's [Qalqilya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalqilya_Governorate) and [Ramallah and al-Bireh Governorates](12007076.md) to the south. Its central location between a plain and a mountain has made it commercially and strategically significant and has had a great impact on its growth. In the past, Tulkarm was a caravan station and a trading center for products from the city's surrounding villages and farms, as well as a point from which armies crossed to [Egypt](8087628.md) and the [Levant (al-Sham)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levant).

Tulkarm is at the crossroads of three historically important arteries: A road which runs north from the [Latrun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latrun) area along the edge of the coastal plain to [Mount Carmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Carmel,_Israel), [Mount Tabor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor), [Mount Gilboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Gilboa), [Nazareth](21702.md) and the [Galilee](12639.md) and the [Golan Heights](7515865.md), a road which winds northward along the outer tier of hills from the [Ajalon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajalon) valley to the [Jezreel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley), and a road that rises from the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) at modern-day Netanya east to Nablus. In the past it was a junction of the coastal railroad from north of [Haifa](55606.md) to [Cairo](6293.md) and a branch of the narrow gauge [Hejaz railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz_railway) to [Damascus](8914.md).

According to the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted by the [British Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine) authorities, Tulkarm had a population of 3,350 (3,109 Muslims, 208 Christians, 23 Jews, eight [Samaritans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritans), one [Baha'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith), and one [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze)).[^cite_note-Census1922A-42] At the time of the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine), Tulkarm had a population of 4,827 (4,540 Muslims, 255 Christians, 18 Jews, six Samaritans, and one Druze) with 541 in nearby suburbs (516 Muslims, 15 Jews, and 10 Christians).[^cite_note-Census1931-43]

The village statistics of 1938 list Tulkarm's population as 5,700 with 629 in nearby suburbs (including 17 Jews).[^cite_note-vs1938-48] The [village statistics of 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) list the population as 8,090 (7,790 Muslims, 280 Christians, and 20 "other").[^cite_note-49]

The populations of Tulkarm, Dhinnaba, Shuweika and Irtah steadily increased by an average of 2% annually between 1931 and 1961, with a drastic increase after the 1948 War as the area experienced an influx of Palestinian refugees. The Jews presumably left/fled during the war. Following the 1967 War, the population saw a temporary decrease as some residents fled to Jordan. In the 1967 census by the [Israel Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) the population of Tulkarm city was recorded as 10,255, Tulkarm Camp as 5,020, Dhinnaba as 1,342, Irtah as 925, Shuweika as 2,332 and Khirbet Jarrad as 128, a total of 20,002.[^cite_note-1967Census-45] Most of the inhabitants were Muslims, although there was a community of 103 [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Christians) according to the census.[^cite_note-50]

In the first census by the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (PCBS) in 1997, Tulkarm had a population of 33,921 and the [Tulkarm Refugee Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulkarm_Camp) had a population of 5,884. [Palestinian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_refugee) made up 31.4% of the city's residents and 94% of the camp's inhabitants.[^cite_note-PCBSRefugees-46] The sex ratio for the city was 50.7% male and 49.3% female. Over half (52.2%) of the city's population was under the age of 20, 44.5% were between the ages of 20 and 64 and 4.1% were over the age of 64.[^cite_note-51] In the 2007 PCBS census Tulkarm's population grew to 51,300 while the camp's increased to 10,641. The sex ratio for the city was 50.3% male and 49.7% female.[^cite_note-PCBS2007-47]



[Church of Saint George, Tulkarm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_George_\(Tulkarm\)).

Today the population is almost entirely Muslim. During the British Mandate period, a Christian community of c. 250 members developed in Tulkarm, as a result of trade and work related migrations.[^cite_note-52] By Israel's occupation of the city in 1967, an estimated 1,000 Christians were living in Tulkarm, but roughly half of the community emigrated in the aftermath of the war, while most of the remaining Christians gradually emigrated afterward.[^cite_note-CMC-53] There are two Christian families who continue to live in Tulkarm,[^cite_note-CMC-53] who are part of the same extended family.[^cite_note-FOX-54] There is a [Greek Orthodox church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_George_\(Tulkarm\)) in the city dedicated to [St. George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_George_\(Tulkarm\)),[^cite_note-CMC-53] built in the early 19th century.[^cite_note-FOX-54][^cite_note-55] The church is active and opens for visitors.[^cite_note-CMC-53]

## Climate

The climate of Tulkarm is [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean) and [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical) as the area surrounding it, with rainfall limited to the winter. The average temperature in the winter ranges from 8 to 16 °C (46 to 61 °F), while the average temperature in the summer ranges from 17 to 30 °C (63 to 86 °F). Tulkarm is distinguished by the moderating effect the [sea breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_breeze) has on its climate because of its location in the mountains. The average temperature does not exceed 27 °C (81 °F) in August, while February's average temperature does not fall below 13.5 °C (56 °F). Humidity is moderate in summer, about 40-70%, though it rises in winter to between 70 and 85%. Tulkarm receives in excess of 550 millimeters (22 in) of rain yearly, which is dispersed and intermittent, characteristic of the [Mediterranean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Basin).

The rainy season starts in October and continues through May. Between December and February, almost 70% of annual rainfall occurs, while 20% of annual rainfall occurs in October and November. Rain in June and September is rare and comes to negligible amounts. July and August have no rain at all, except for one rainfall of 1.5 millimeters (0.059 in) on July 10, 1995, in Tulkarm city (Tulkarm Agricultural Department). The mean annual rainfall in the city of Tulkarm is 642 millimeters (25.3 in) for the period from 1952 to 1995 (Tulkarm Agricultural Department).

## Culture

The [traditional costumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_costumes) of women from Tulkarm were plain, dark-colored gowns with or without embroidery, as most rural women were from the north of Palestine.[^cite_note-61] Today, embroidery is the main source of income for the women of the city.[^cite_note-SandB-25] The most popular embroidered images are maps of historic Palestine. The Palestinian dish [musakhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musakhan) is popular in the city. Tulkarm shares many of its cultural features with neighboring [Haifa](55606.md), [Jenin](50997.md), [Nablus](233369.md), [Qalqilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalqilia), and [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa).[^cite_note-SandB-25]

A Tulkarm amusement park called Mega Land attracts tens of thousands of visitors on Muslim holidays.[^cite_note-62]
