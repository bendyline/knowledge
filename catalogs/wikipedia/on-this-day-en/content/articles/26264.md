**Robert Reiner** ([/ˈraɪnər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); March 6, 1947 – December 14, 2025) was an American filmmaker, actor, and political activist. He directed a series of acclaimed studio films in a career that spanned [comedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_film), [drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drama_film), [romance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_\(film\)), and [documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Documentary). Reiner received [numerous accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Rob_Reiner), including winning three [Primetime Emmy Awards](6579025.md) and a [Hugo Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Award), as well as nominations for an [Academy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award), a [BAFTA Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award), and nine [Golden Globe Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award).[^cite_note-1] He was honored with a star on the [Hollywood Walk of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Walk_of_Fame) in 1999 and received the Chaplin Gala Tribute at the [Film at Lincoln Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Film_at_Lincoln_Center) in 2014. Three of his films have been inducted into the [National Film Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Film_Registry).[^cite_note-2]

Reiner was born in New York City to [Estelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estelle_Reiner) and [Carl Reiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Reiner), who were themselves actors. Reiner began his career as an actor before transitioning to filmmaking. He rose to prominence with his portrayal of [Michael "Meathead" Stivic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Stivic) in the 1970s American sitcom *[All in the Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_in_the_Family)* (1971–1979), a role that earned him two [Primetime Emmy Awards for Outstanding Supporting Actor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Supporting_Actor_in_a_Comedy_Series).

He directed a string of critically acclaimed films, starting with the heavy metal [mockumentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mockumentary) *[This Is Spinal Tap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_Spinal_Tap)* (1984), followed by the romantic road comedy *[The Sure Thing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sure_Thing)* (1985), the coming-of age drama *[Stand by Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_by_Me_\(film\))* (1986), the adventure romance *[The Princess Bride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princess_Bride)* (1987), the romantic comedy *[When Harry Met Sally...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Harry_Met_Sally...)* (1989), the psychological thriller *[Misery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misery_\(film\))* (1990), the legal drama *[A Few Good Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Few_Good_Men)* (1992), which was nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Picture](61702.md), and the political romance *[The American President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_President)* (1995). He acted in films such as *[Sleepless in Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleepless_in_Seattle)* (1993), *[Bullets Over Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullets_Over_Broadway)* (1994), *[The First Wives Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_First_Wives_Club)* (1996), *[Primary Colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_Colors_\(film\))* (1998), *[EDtv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDtv)* (1999), and *[The Wolf of Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wolf_of_Wall_Street_\(2013_film\))* (2013). He also co-founded the production company [Castle Rock Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Rock_Entertainment) in 1987.

Reiner was also a progressive political activist who advocated for causes such as [LGBTQ rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_rights), [early childhood education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_childhood_education), and [environmental protection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_protection), and who campaigned for a variety of [Democratic](5043544.md) candidates. He chaired the 1998 campaign to pass California's [First 5 childhood education initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_California_Proposition_10), and in 2008 he and his wife, [Michele](81849725.md), co-founded the [American Foundation for Equal Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Foundation_for_Equal_Rights), which initiated the court challenge against California's [same-sex marriage ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_California_Proposition_8).

On December 14, 2025, Reiner and his wife were found [stabbed to death](81621986.md) in their Los Angeles home. Their son Nick Reiner has been indicted with two counts of [first-degree murder](20171.md), and the case is ongoing.

## Early life



Reiner as a child, from the credits of *More Than Friends*, a 1978 TV movie co-starring his then-wife [Penny Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Marshall)

Robert Reiner was born into a Jewish family in [the Bronx](3338.md), New York City, on March 6, 1947.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-guard-15dec2025-2-4] His parents were the actors [Estelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estelle_Reiner) and [Carl Reiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Reiner).[^cite_note-5] His siblings are poet, playwright, and author [Annie Reiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Reiner) and painter, actor, and director [Lucas Reiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Reiner). The family lived on [the Grand Concourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Concourse_\(Bronx\)) in the Bronx.[^cite_note-6] Later in his childhood, they moved to [New Rochelle, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Rochelle,_New_York).[^cite_note-7] Reiner had no middle name. "My mother didn't have a middle name, my father didn't have one, so they didn't give me one."[^cite_note-8] Carl Reiner loved baseball and took Rob to his first [Major League Baseball](38776.md) game in 1951, a [doubleheader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doubleheader_\(baseball\)) at [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium): "It was [Mickey Mantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Mantle)'s first year in the Majors and [Joe DiMaggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_DiMaggio)'s last...I was four years old, and I stayed through both games. I was fascinated. I was hooked at that point."[^cite_note-Dye-9]

The family eventually moved to Los Angeles,[^cite_note-NBCobit-10] where Reiner made his television acting debut at age 14 on the series *[Manhunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhunt_\(1959_TV_series\))*.[^cite_note-guard-15dec2025-2-4] In the early 1960s, he served as a trainee and apprentice at the [Bucks County Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucks_County_Playhouse) in [New Hope, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hope,_Pennsylvania).[^cite_note-11] He attended [Beverly Hills High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beverly_Hills_High_School) and studied at the [UCLA Film School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCLA_Film_School).[^cite_note-NBCobit-10][^cite_note-12] He was at UCLA for two years, from 1964 to 1966, but did not graduate with a degree.[^cite_note-13]

## Career

### 1966–1969: Early work and acting roles



Reiner (left) guest starred opposite [Andy Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Griffith) in *[Headmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headmaster_\(TV_series\))*, a series for which he also wrote.

In the late 1960s, Reiner acted in bit roles on several television shows including *[Batman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batman_\(TV_series\))*, *[That Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That_Girl)*, *[The Andy Griffith Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Andy_Griffith_Show)*, *[Room 222](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_222)*, *[Gomer Pyle – USMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomer_Pyle_%E2%80%93_USMC)*, and *[The Beverly Hillbillies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Beverly_Hillbillies)*.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-peopleobit-15] At age 19, Reiner and actor [Larry Bishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Bishop) were part of an [improv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Improvisation) group, and performed as the opening act for [Carmen McRae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen_McRae) at the [Hungry I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungry_I) club in San Francisco.[^cite_note-guard-15dec2025-2-4] He began his career in television writing for *[The Smothers Brothers Comedy Hour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Smothers_Brothers_Comedy_Hour)* in 1968 and 1969, working alongside [Steve Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Martin) and [Carl Gottlieb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Gottlieb) as his writing partners.[^cite_note-vogue-16] In 1969 he directed a one-act play at the [Oxford Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastwood_Performing_Arts_Center) in Los Angeles, and starred in another, both written by his friend [Philip Mishkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Mishkin).[^cite_note-17] During this period, he also appeared in several films, including some directed by his father such as *[Where's Poppa?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where's_Poppa%3F)* (1970).[^cite_note-tcmpoppa-18] Reiner and [Jeff Bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bridges) both had roles in a school desegregation picture from [United Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Artists) called *[Halls of Anger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halls_of_Anger)* (1970).[^cite_note-19] Also in 1970, Reiner starred in and wrote for *[Headmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headmaster_\(TV_series\))*, providing an opportunity to work again with [Andy Griffith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Griffith), after portraying a printer's assistant in a 1967 episode of *The Andy Griffith Show*. *Headmaster* was the first of five TV comedies on which Reiner and frequent writing partner Mishkin collaborated. *Headmaster*, *[The Super](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Super_\(TV_series\))* (1972), *[Free Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Country_\(TV_series\))* (1978), and *[Morton & Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morton_&_Hayes)* (1991) were all canceled after one season.[^cite_note-Doherty-20]

### 1970–1979: *All in the Family* and stardom



Reiner (top-right, with [Sally Struthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Struthers), [Carroll O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carroll_O'Connor), and [Jean Stapleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Stapleton)) as Michael Stivic on *All in the Family*, 1976

In the 1970s, Reiner became widely known for playing [Michael Stivic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Stivic), [Archie Bunker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archie_Bunker)'s [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_liberal) son-in-law, on [Norman Lear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Lear)'s situation comedy *[All in the Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_in_the_Family)*,[^cite_note-21] which was adapted from the British sitcom *[Till Death Us Do Part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Till_Death_Us_Do_Part)*. The series was the most-watched television program in the United States for five consecutive seasons (1971–1976). The character's nickname "Meathead" (given to him by his cantankerous father-in-law Archie) became closely associated with Reiner, even after he left the role and established himself as a director. Reflecting on the enduring association, Reiner remarked, "I could win the Nobel Prize and they'd write 'Meathead wins the Nobel Prize.'"[^cite_note-22] For his performance, Reiner won two [Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Awards), received three additional nominations, and earned five [Golden Globe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe) nominations.[^cite_note-guard-14dec2025-23]

In October 1971, Reiner appeared as a guest in an episode of *[The Partridge Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Partridge_Family)*.[^cite_note-24] The following year, Reiner, Mishkin, and Gerry Isenberg created the situation comedy *[The Super](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Super_\(TV_series\))* for [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company), starring [Richard S. Castellano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_S._Castellano), about the life of a harried Italian-American building [superintendent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_superintendent) in New York City; the show ran for one season during the summer of 1972.[^cite_note-25] In 1978 Reiner co-wrote an ABC-TV movie called *More Than Friends*, in which he starred opposite his then-wife [Penny Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Marshall).[^cite_note-26] Early in the film, Reiner's character lamented, "*Whaaat?* I have no sex appeal? That hurts my feelings. And I'm going to be bald on top of it."[^cite_note-27]

### 1980–1996: Transition to directing and acclaim

Beginning in the 1980s, Reiner established himself as a director of several successful Hollywood films spanning multiple genres. His early works include [cult classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cult_classics) such as the rock-band [mockumentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mockumentary) *[This Is Spinal Tap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_Spinal_Tap)* (1984), the [coming-of-age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coming-of-age) drama *[Stand by Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_by_Me_\(film\))* (1986), and the comedic fantasy *[The Princess Bride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princess_Bride)* (1987).[^cite_note-THRLegacy-28] He also appeared as faux-documentarian Marty Di Bergi in *This Is Spinal Tap*. He frequently collaborated with film editor [Robert Leighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Leighton_\(film_editor\)),[^cite_note-afi-4jul2014-29] who also worked on several films with fellow director-actor [Christopher Guest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Guest).[^cite_note-cm-18aug2021-30]



Reiner at the 1988 Emmy Awards

Reiner directed and produced a number of critically and commercially successful films through his company, [Castle Rock Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Rock_Entertainment). These include the romantic comedy *[When Harry Met Sally...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Harry_Met_Sally...)* (1989), which has been ranked among the all-time best of its genre;[^cite_note-31] the tense thriller adaptation of [Stephen King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_King)'s novel *[Misery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misery_\(film\))* (1990), for which [Kathy Bates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathy_Bates) won the [Academy Award for Best Actress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Actress); and his most commercially successful work, the military [courtroom drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtroom_drama) *[A Few Good Men](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Few_Good_Men)* (1992), which was nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Picture](61702.md).[^cite_note-THRLegacy-28][^cite_note-32] The next film Reiner released through Castle Rock was the family comedy *[North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_\(1994_film\))*, which was a [box-office bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Box-office_bomb)[^cite_note-33] and was panned by critics. [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert), whose extremely negative review of the film became well-known, regarded it as "some sort of lapse" from Reiner.[^cite_note-34] It has since been included on many prominent lists of [the worst films ever made](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_20th-century_films_considered_the_worst).

Subsequent films directed by Reiner include the political romance *[The American President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_President)* (1995) and the courtroom drama *[Ghosts of Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghosts_of_Mississippi)* (1996). Reiner continued to act in supporting roles in a number of films and television shows, including *[Throw Momma from the Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throw_Momma_from_the_Train)* (1987), *[Sleepless in Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleepless_in_Seattle)* (1993), *[Bullets Over Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullets_Over_Broadway)* (1994), and *[The First Wives Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_First_Wives_Club)* (1996).

### 1997–2025: Continued work as a director, later acting roles, and final films

Reiner went on to direct films such as *[The Story of Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Story_of_Us_\(1999_film\))* (1999) and *[Alex & Emma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_&_Emma)* (2003), in which he also appeared, and the uplifting comedy *[The Bucket List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bucket_List)* (2007).[^cite_note-35] He appeared in the films *[Primary Colors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_Colors_\(film\))* (1998) and *[EDtv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDtv)* (1999), and parodied himself with cameos in works such as *[Dickie Roberts: Former Child Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickie_Roberts:_Former_Child_Star)* (2003) and *[30 Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_Rock)* (2010).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-30rock-37] In 2013, Reiner played [Jordan Belfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Belfort)'s father in the [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese) film *[The Wolf of Wall Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wolf_of_Wall_Street_\(2013_film\))*.[^cite_note-guard-14dec2025-23]

Decades after *All in the Family*, Reiner returned to television with a recurring role on *[New Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Girl)* (2012–2018) playing Jess's dad [Bob Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Day_\(character\)).[^cite_note-vogue-16] His late-career TV guest star work also included "politically infused roles" on *[The Good Fight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Fight)*, *[When We Rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_We_Rise)*, and the miniseries *[Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_\(miniseries\))*. He played business consultant [Albert Schnur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Schnur) on *[The Bear](71135592.md)* (2025), for which he won a posthumous [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Guest Actor in a Comedy Series](3626722.md).[^cite_note-Feinberg-2025-38]

In 2015, he directed the semi-autobiographical film *[Being Charlie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Being_Charlie)*, co-written by his son Nick.[^cite_note-Zeitchik-39] He directed and played a major supporting role in the journalism drama *[Shock and Awe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_and_Awe_\(film\))* (2017). In November 2023, Reiner and journalist [Soledad O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soledad_O'Brien) launched the ten-part podcast series *Who Killed JFK?*.[^cite_note-40] In December 2023, Reiner opened the primetime CBS special *Dick Van Dyke: 98 Years of Magic* with a tribute to, and conversation with, [Dick Van Dyke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Van_Dyke).[^cite_note-41]

His final completed film, *[Spinal Tap II: The End Continues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_Tap_II:_The_End_Continues)* (2025), was released three months before his death.[^cite_note-Wilker-42] In August 2025, Reiner filmed Spinal Tap performing at [Stonehenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stonehenge) for a concert film, *Stonehenge: The Final Finale*,[^cite_note-43] with musical guests [Eric Clapton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Clapton), [Shania Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shania_Twain) and [Josh Groban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Groban).[^cite_note-44] *Final Finale* was planned for a 2026 theatrical release.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] The film's release was put on hold following Reiner's death.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

Reiner was a [progressive activist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressivism_in_the_United_States) on a wide range of issues,[^cite_note-49] and an outspoken critic of [Donald Trump](4848272.md).

In 1998, Reiner chaired the campaign to pass [California Proposition 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_California_Proposition_10), the California Children and Families Initiative, which created [First 5 California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_California_Proposition_10#First_5_California), a program of [early childhood development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_childhood_development) services funded by a tax on [tobacco products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_products). He served as the organization's first chairman from 1999 to 2006. His lobbying, particularly as an anti-smoking advocate, led to his likeness being used satirically in the *[South Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Park)* episode "[Butt Out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butt_Out)", where he was depicted as a morbidly obese, hypocritical tyrant.[^cite_note-50]

In 2006, Reiner came under criticism for campaigning for California [Proposition 82](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposition_82), a ballot measure to fund public [preschools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preschool), while serving as the chair of the California First 5 Commission. In response to the criticism, he resigned from his position as chair on March 29, 2006, two months before the measure was voted on.[^cite_note-51] An audit later concluded that the commission had not violated state law and had clear legal authority to conduct public advertising campaigns related to preschool. However, the auditor reported that the commission awarded more than \$77 million in media contracts without reviewing their costs.[^cite_note-52] Proposition 82 ultimately failed to win approval.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

Reiner was a member of the Social Responsibility Task Force, an organization advocating moderation where social issues such as violence and tobacco use intersect with the entertainment industry.[^cite_note-55] He was also active in environmental causes, successfully leading the effort to preserve California's [Ahmanson Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmanson_Ranch) as a state park and [wildlife refuge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_refuge) rather than as a commercial real estate development.[^cite_note-vogue-16] In July 2007, he introduced the reunited [Spinal Tap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinal_Tap_\(band\)) at the London [Live Earth concert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Earth_concert,_London).[^cite_note-NME2007-56]

In 2009, Reiner co-founded the [American Foundation for Equal Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Foundation_for_Equal_Rights), which initiated the court challenge against [California Proposition 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Proposition_8) that banned [same-sex marriage in the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_California).[^cite_note-57]

### Democratic Party activism and criticism of Donald Trump



Reiner at the [LBJ Presidential Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LBJ_Presidential_Library) in 2016

Reiner campaigned extensively for [Democratic](5043544.md) presidential nominee [Al Gore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Gore) in the [2000 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_United_States_presidential_election)[^cite_note-58] He campaigned in [Iowa](26810748.md) for Democratic candidate [Howard Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Dean) ahead of the [2004 Iowa caucuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Iowa_Democratic_caucuses).[^cite_note-59]

In 2006, Reiner was mentioned as a possible candidate to run against California Governor [Arnold Schwarzenegger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Schwarzenegger) in that years election, but declined for personal reasons.[^cite_note-60] He was quoted as saying "I don't want to be an elected official, I want to get things done."[^cite_note-guard-15dec2025-2-4]

Reiner endorsed [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton) in the [2008 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Democratic_Party_presidential_primaries), and in 2015 donated \$10,000 to [Correct the Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correct_the_Record), a [political action committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_action_committee) supporting her [2016 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton_2016_presidential_campaign).[^cite_note-correcttherecordcenter2016-61]

After the 2016 election, Reiner continued to campaign against [Donald Trump](4848272.md), calling him [racist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racist), [sexist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexist), [homophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophobic), and [antisemitic](1078.md). He remarked that [Harvey Weinstein](1624086.md) is a "bad guy" but that Trump was "also an abuser".[^cite_note-62] Reiner served on the advisory board of the committee to Investigate Russia.[^cite_note-InvestigateRussia-63][^cite_note-Variety19092017-64] He and [David Frum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Frum) launched the site in September 2017, accompanied by a video narrated by [Morgan Freeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Freeman) warning that "We are at war" and that [Russia had attacked the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_interference_in_the_2016_United_States_elections).[^cite_note-Variety19092017-64] Other advisory board members at the time of launch included [James Clapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Clapper), [Max Boot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Boot), [Charles Sykes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Sykes), and [Norman Ornstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Ornstein).[^cite_note-Variety19092017-64] The board also included [Evelyn Farkas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evelyn_Farkas), [Michael Hayden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Hayden_\(general\)), [Michael Morrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Morrell), [Leon Panetta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Panetta), and [Clint Watts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clint_Watts).[^cite_note-InvestigateRussia-63]

Reiner endorsed [Joe Biden](145422.md) for president in the [2020 United States presidential election](48410011.md).[^cite_note-65] In June 2021, Reiner announced he was developing a television project, *The Spy and the Asset*, about the [relationship between Trump and Vladimir Putin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Links_between_Trump_associates_and_Russian_officials). He said the series would explore the leaders' childhoods and trace their lives up to the point where they intersect. Reiner said he was collaborating with writer Ward Parry on the project.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

In September 2025, Reiner gave an interview with CNN, where he spoke out against Trump and the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission). He said it "may be the last time you ever see me", in reference to the [suspension of *Jimmy Kimmel Live!*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspension_of_Jimmy_Kimmel_Live!)[^cite_note-68]

## Personal life



Michele and Rob Reiner at the LBJ Presidential Library for a screening of his film *LBJ* in 2016

Reiner married actress and director [Penny Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Marshall) in 1971.[^cite_note-69] He adopted Marshall's daughter, actress [Tracy Reiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Reiner), from her previous marriage to Michael Henry. Reiner and Marshall divorced in 1981.[^cite_note-70]

While directing *When Harry Met Sally...*, Reiner was introduced to photographer [Michele Singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michele_Singer). The meeting not only influenced his decision to change the film's ending,[^cite_note-71] but also led to their marriage in 1989.[^cite_note-peopledeath-72] They had three children: sons Jake and Nick, and daughter Romy.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] Rob and Michele's eldest son, Jake, has been involved in filmmaking and was a news reporter in [Houston](13774.md), Texas.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-robmichelechildren-77] Rob Reiner had [season tickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Season_ticket) for [Dodger Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodger_Stadium) beginning in the 1960s, and he would go to baseball games all around the country with Jake, who had a [Dodgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodgers) podcast circa 2023.[^cite_note-Dye-9] His second son Nick entered the first of a number of stays in [drug rehab](427922.md) at the age of 14,[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-moredetailondeaths-79] cycling "in and out of rehab" subsequently for years.[^cite_note-moredetailondeaths-79] Their youngest child, daughter Romy, is a filmmaker and had a very close relationship with both of her parents.[^cite_note-robmichelechildren-77]

In 1997, Reiner and Singer founded the I Am Your Child Foundation and, in 2004, they established Parents' Action for Children, a nonprofit organization with a dual purpose of raising awareness of the importance of a child's early years by producing and distributing celebrity-hosted educational videos for parents, and advancing public policy through parental education and advocacy.[^cite_note-80]

Reiner said his childhood home was not observantly Jewish, although he did have a [bar mitzvah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_mitzvah) ceremony.[^cite_note-thejc-81] His father, Carl, had become an [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheist) in response to [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler) and [the Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holocaust).[^cite_note-82] Reiner identified as an atheist on the January 13, 2012, episode of *[Real Time with Bill Maher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Time_with_Bill_Maher)*.[^cite_note-reinerbelief-83][^cite_note-84] He later said that, while he rejected organized religion, he was sympathetic to the ideas of [Buddhism](3267529.md).[^cite_note-reinerbelief-83]

## Death, funeral, and memorial service

Main article: [Killing of Rob and Michele Reiner](81621986.md)

On December 14, 2025, Reiner, 78, and his wife Michele, 70,[^cite_note-85] were found in their [Brentwood, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brentwood,_Los_Angeles), home at 255 South Chadbourne Avenue, dead from [sharp force injuries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penetrating_trauma).[^cite_note-peopledeath-72][^cite_note-86] The couple's youngest child, Romy, had gone to the house with her roommate after the Reiners' [massage therapist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massage_therapist) informed her that the couple had missed their appointment; she discovered her father's body, but was not aware that her mother was at home, and was later told of her mother's death.[^cite_note-87] The [Los Angeles Fire Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Fire_Department) responded to a medical aid call at the residence at 3\:38 p.m. [PST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Time_Zone).[^cite_note-moredetailondeaths-79][^cite_note-88]

The same day, [Los Angeles police](218985.md) arrested the Reiners' son Nick, 32, near the [University of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Southern_California) on suspicion of murder.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] Nick had been living with his parents at the time.[^cite_note-moredetailondeaths-79] Rob and Nick had attended a Christmas party hosted by [Conan O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conan_O'Brien) on the evening of December 13 and had reportedly had an argument.[^cite_note-91] Nick had been disruptive at the party and had made guests feel uneasy.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] Sources who attended the party but wished to remain anonymous told *[The New York Times](30680.md)* that the loud argument between Nick and Rob started after Rob told him his behavior at the party was not appropriate.[^cite_note-94] On December 16, Nick was charged with two counts of [first-degree murder](20171.md) with a special circumstance of multiple murders.[^cite_note-95]

In September 2026, Los Angeles County prosecutors announced that they would not seek the death penalty against Nick Reiner, who was charged with murdering his parents. If convicted, he faces a maximum sentence of life imprisonment without the possibility of parole.[^cite_note-96]

Rob and Michele's other two children, Jake and Romy, released a joint statement on December 17 describing their parents as their "best friends".[^cite_note-97] Close friends and collaborators [Albert Brooks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Brooks), [Billy Crystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Crystal), [Larry David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_David), [Martin Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Short), [Barry Levinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Levinson), [Marc Shaiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Shaiman), and [Alan Zweibel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Zweibel) released a joint statement that read in part, "There is no other director who has his range...he was always at the top of his game. He charmed audiences. They trusted him." They also wrote, "Michele and Rob Reiner devoted a great deal of their lives for the betterment of our fellow citizens... They were a special force together—dynamic, unselfish and inspiring. We were their friends, and we will miss them forever".[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] Former U.S. presidents [Bill Clinton](3356.md) and [Joe Biden](145422.md) also released statements memorializing Reiner and offering condolences to his loved ones.[^cite_note-100] Crystal later appeared at the [98th Academy Awards](79369474.md) to pay tribute to Reiner.[^cite_note-101]

The Reiners were [cremated](175142.md) at [Mount Sinai Memorial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sinai_Memorial_Park). Their remains were given to their son Jake for private disposition.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] Funeral plans for the family were not made public.[^cite_note-104] On December 22, Jake and Romy Reiner issued a statement confirming that they were making plans for a public memorial service and would release details at a future date.[^cite_note-105]

## Legacy and reception



Reiner in 2013

Reiner was a notable figure in the development of mainstream American cinema and television. *[This Is Spinal Tap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Is_Spinal_Tap)* popularized the [mockumentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mockumentary) genre, and its use of improvisation was revolutionary for a Hollywood film. The movie established conventions that later filmmakers adopted in both cinema and television, particularly in comedy built around realism, parody, and unscripted performance. Its success also contributed to the wider acceptance of mock-documentary formats in mainstream media.[^cite_note-GuardianOBIT-106] It is also considered one of the greatest comedies of all time.[^cite_note-THRLegacy-28]

Across the 1980s and 1990s, Reiner directed a series of culturally influential films in multiple genres, including coming-of-age drama *[Stand by Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand_by_Me_\(film\))*, fantasy romance *[The Princess Bride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Princess_Bride)*, and romantic comedy *[When Harry Met Sally...](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/When_Harry_Met_Sally...)*.[^cite_note-GuardianOBIT-106] *The Princess Bride* is credited with having numerous phrases enter the regular lexicon.[^cite_note-THRLegacy-28]

*When Harry Met Sally...*, in particular, helped redefine the modern romantic comedy through its balanced focus on male and female perspectives[^cite_note-GuardianOBIT-106] and is considered one of the greatest romantic comedies of all time.[^cite_note-THRLegacy-28] Reiner has regularly been cited by critics as having one of the greatest directing streaks in Hollywood with his first seven films.[^cite_note-THRLegacy-28] *[The Bucket List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bucket_List)* popularized the eponymous term.[^cite_note-THRLegacy-28] Three of Reiner's films – *When Harry Met Sally...*, *The Princess Bride* and *This Is Spinal Tap* – have been selected for preservation by the [National Film Registry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Film_Registry) for cultural, historical, and aesthetic contributions.[^cite_note-107]

Following his death, [CNN](62028.md) published an article describing how Reiner "changed movies forever by challenging himself as an artist".[^cite_note-108] In its obituary, the [Associated Press](18935732.md) noted that Reiner was "the son of a [comedy giant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Reiner) who became one himself as one of the preeminent filmmakers of his generation".[^cite_note-109]

Beyond his work as a director, Reiner had a significant impact as a producer and studio executive through his production company [Castle Rock Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Rock_Entertainment). The company was instrumental in bringing influential film and television projects to a wide audience, most notably *[Seinfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seinfeld)*, which reshaped expectations of what network television comedy could achieve. Castle Rock also produced several highly regarded films, including *[The Shawshank Redemption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Shawshank_Redemption)* (1994) and *[The Green Mile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Green_Mile_\(film\))* (1999), both of which were directed by [Frank Darabont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Darabont) and achieved long-term critical and popular recognition.[^cite_note-GuardianOBIT-106]
