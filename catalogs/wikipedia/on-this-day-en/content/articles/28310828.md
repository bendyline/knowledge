**Universal basic income** (**UBI**)[^cite_note-2] is a [social welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_welfare) proposal in which all members of a given population regularly receive a minimum [income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income) in the form of an unconditional [transfer payment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_payment), i.e., without a [means test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Means_test) or need to perform [work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Work_\(human_activity\)).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] In contrast, a [guaranteed minimum income](457460.md) (GMI) is paid only to those who do not already receive an income that is enough to live on. A UBI would be received independently of any other income. If the level is sufficient to meet a person's basic needs (i.e., at or above the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line)), it is considered a *full basic income*; if it is less than that amount, it is called a *partial basic income*.[^cite_note-USBIG-2011-6] As of 2026, no country has implemented a full UBI system, but two countries—[Mongolia](19271.md) and [Iran](14653.md)—have or had a partial UBI, Mongolia from 2010 to 2012, and Iran still to this day.[^cite_note-7] There have been [numerous pilot projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_basic_income_pilots),[^cite_note-8] and the idea [is discussed in many countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_basic_income_around_the_world). Some have labelled UBI as [utopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopian) due to its historical origin.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

There are several welfare arrangements that can be considered similar to basic income, although they are not unconditional. Many countries have a system of [child benefit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_benefit), which is essentially a basic income for guardians of children. A [pension](43613.md) may be a basic income for retired persons. There are also quasi-basic income programs that are limited to certain population groups or time periods, like [Bolsa Familia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsa_Familia) in [Brazil](3383.md), which is concentrated on the poor, or the Thamarat Program in [Sudan](27421.md), which was introduced by the transitional government to ease the effects of the economic crisis inherited from the [Bashir](393625.md) regime.[^cite_note-12] Likewise, the [economic impact of the COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic) prompted some countries to send direct payments to its citizens. The [Alaska Permanent Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Permanent_Fund) is a fund for all residents of the U.S. state of [Alaska](624.md) which averages \$1,600 annually (in 2019 currency), and is sometimes described as the only example of a real basic income in practice. A [negative income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_income_tax) (NIT) can be viewed as a basic income for certain income groups in which citizens receive less and less money until this effect is reversed the more a person earns.[^cite_note-13]

Critics claim that a basic income at an appropriate level for all citizens is not financially feasible, fear that the introduction of a basic income would lead to fewer people working, and consider it socially unjust that everyone should receive the same amount of money regardless of their individual needs. Proponents say it is indeed financeable, arguing that such a system, instead of many individual means-tested social benefits, would eliminate more expensive social administration and bureaucratic efforts, and expect that unattractive jobs would have to be better paid and their working conditions improved because there would have to be an incentive to do them when already receiving an income, which would increase the willingness to work. Advocates also argue that a basic income is fair because it ensures that everyone has a sufficient financial basis to build on and less financial pressure, thus allowing people to find work that suits their interests and strengths.[^cite_note-14]

Early examples of unconditional payments to citizens date back to antiquity, and the first proposals to introduce a regular unconditionally paid income for all citizens were developed and disseminated between the 16th and 18th centuries. After the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), public awareness and support for the concept increased. At least since the mid-20th century, basic income has repeatedly been the subject of political debates. In the 21st century, several discussions are related to the debate about basic income, including those concerning the [automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation) of large parts of the human [workforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workforce) through [artificial intelligence](1164.md) (AI), and associated questions regarding the [future of the necessity of work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-work_society). A key issue in these debates is whether automation and AI will significantly [reduce the number of available jobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_unemployment) and whether a basic income could help prevent or alleviate such problems by allowing everyone to benefit from a society's wealth, as well as whether a UBI could be a stepping stone to a [resource-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource-based_economy) or [post-scarcity economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-scarcity_economy).

## History

### Antiquity

See also: [Cura Annonae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cura_Annonae)

[Ancient Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Egypt) had a strong, unified [theocratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theocratic) state that owned key parts of the Egyptian economy, including [granaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granary) that dispensed grain to the public during hard times.[^cite_note-MooreLewis2009-15]

In a 46 BC [triumph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_triumph), [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) general and dictator [Julius Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Caesar) gave each [common Roman citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebeians) 100 [denarii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denarii). Following [his assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Julius_Caesar) in 44 BC, Caesar's will left 300 [sestertii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sestertii) (or 75 denarii) to each citizen.[^cite_note-16] [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan), emperor of [Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) from 98 to 117 AD, personally gave 650 denarii (equivalent to perhaps US\$430 in 2023) to all common Roman citizens who applied.[^cite_note-17]

### 16th century

In *[Utopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopia_\(More_book\))* (1516), English statesman and philosopher [Thomas More](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_More) depicts a society in which every person receives a guaranteed income.[^cite_note-18] In this book, basic income is proposed as an answer to the statement "No penalty on earth will stop people from stealing, if it's their only way of getting food", stating:[^cite_note-19]

> instead of inflicting these horrible punishments, it would be far more to the point to provide everyone with some means of livelihood, so that nobody's under the frightful necessity of becoming first a thief, and then a corpse.

Spanish scholar [Johannes Ludovicus Vives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Ludovicus_Vives) (1492–1540) proposed that the municipal government should be responsible for securing a subsistence minimum to all its residents "not on the grounds of justice but for the sake of a more effective exercise of morally required charity." Vives also argued that to qualify for poor relief, the recipient must "deserve the help he or she gets by proving his or her willingness to work."[^cite_note-20]

### 18th century

English-born American philosopher [Thomas Paine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Paine) authored *Common Sense* (1776) and *The American Crisis* (1776–1783), the two most influential pamphlets at the start of the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution). His essay *[Agrarian Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrarian_Justice)*[^cite_note-21] was published in 1797. In it, he proposed concrete reforms to abolish poverty. In particular, he proposed a universal social insurance system comprising old-age pensions and disability support, and universal stakeholder grants for young adults, funded by a 10% inheritance tax focused on land, it is also considered one of the earliest proposals for a [social security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_security) system. Thomas Paine summarized his view by stating that "Men did not make the earth. It is the value of the improvements only, and not the earth itself, that is individual property. Every proprietor owes to the community a ground rent for the land which he holds." Paine saw inheritance as being partly a common fund and wanted to supplement the citizen's dividend in a tax on inheritance transfers.

In 1797, English [Radical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radicals_\(UK\)) [Thomas Spence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Spence) published *The Rights of Infants*[^cite_note-22] as a response to Thomas Paine's *Agrarian Justice*. In this essay Spence proposes the introduction of an unconditional basic income to all members of the community. Such allowance would be financed through the [socialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_ownership) of land and the benefits of the rents received by each municipality. A part of everyone's earnings would be seized by the State, and given to others.

### 19th century

[Henry George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_George) proposed to create a pension and disability system, and a broad social support system from a single tax on land and natural resource value. Social support would be distributed to residents "as a right" instead of as charity. George mentioned, but did not stress, the possibility of direct cash distribution of land rent. His ideas gave rise to the economic philosophy now known as [Georgism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgism) or the "single tax movement", which is an [economic ideology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_ideology) holding that, although people should own the value they produce themselves, the [economic rent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_rent) derived from [land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_\(economics\))—including from all [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resources), the [commons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commons), and urban locations—should belong equally to all members of society.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-Heavey-24][^cite_note-25] Some Georgists refer to unconditional basic income funded by the single tax as a [citizen's dividend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizen's_dividend) in reference to Thomas Paine's proposal from the 19th century.

### Early 20th century

Around 1920, support for basic income started growing, primarily in England.

[Bertrand Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_Russell) (1872–1970) argued for a new social model that combined the advantages of socialism and anarchism, and that basic income should be a vital component in that new society. In his 1918 book *Roads to Freedom*, Russell wrote "... the plan we are advocating amounts essentially to this: that a certain small income, sufficient for necessaries, should be secured to all, whether they work or not, and that a larger income – as much larger as might be warranted by the total amount of commodities produced – should be given to those who are willing to engage in some work which the community recognizes as useful..."[^cite_note-26]

In the United Kingdom at the end of World War I, Dennis and Mabel Milner, a Quaker married couple of the Labour Party, published a short pamphlet entitled "Scheme for a State Bonus" (1918) that argued for the "introduction of an income paid unconditionally on a weekly basis to all citizens of the United Kingdom." They considered it a moral right for everyone to have the means to subsistence, and thus it should not be conditional on work or willingness to work.[^cite_note-Szreter_517–523-27][^cite_note-28]

[C. H. Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._H._Douglas) was an engineer who became concerned that most British citizens could not afford to buy the goods that were produced, despite the rising productivity in British industry. His solution to this paradox was a new social system he called [social credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_credit), a combination of [monetary reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monetary_reform) and basic income.

In 1944 and 1945, the [Beveridge Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beveridge_Committee), led by the British economist [William Beveridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Beveridge), developed a proposal for a comprehensive new welfare system of social insurance, means-tested benefits, and unconditional allowances for children. Committee member [Lady Rhys-Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliet_Rhys-Williams) argued that the incomes for adults should be more like a basic income. She was also the first to develop the negative income tax model.[^cite_note-sloman-2015-29][^cite_note-30] Her son Sir [Brandon Rhys-Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandon_Rhys-Williams) proposed a basic income to a parliamentary committee in 1982, and soon after that in 1984, the Basic Income Research Group, now the Citizen's Basic Income Trust, began to conduct and disseminate research on basic income.[^cite_note-31]

### Late 20th century

[Milton Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milton_Friedman) proposed a [negative income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_income_tax) (NIT), which effectively sanctioned a basic income for all, in his 1962 book *[Capitalism and Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism_and_Freedom)*.[^cite_note-Szreter_517–523-27] In his 1964 [State of the Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Union) address, U.S. President [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) declared an "unconditional [war on poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_Poverty)," implemented in coming years with sweeping legislation.[^cite_note-32] Johnson broadened the agenda to the [Great Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Society), including education, civil rights, health care, and support for the arts.[^cite_note-33] In this political climate, the idea of a guaranteed income for every American also took root.[^cite_note-34] Notably, a 1968 document, signed by 1200 economists, called for a guaranteed income for every American.[^cite_note-35] Four ambitious basic income experiments started on the related concept of negative income tax.[^cite_note-36] President [Richard Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Nixon) explained the [Family Assistance Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_Assistance_Plan)'s purpose as to provide both a safety net for the poor and a financial incentive for welfare recipients to work.[^cite_note-Nixon_UBI-37] Congress eventually approved a guaranteed minimum income for the elderly and the disabled.[^cite_note-Nixon_UBI-37]

In the mid-1970s, the main competitor to basic income and negative income tax, the [Earned income tax credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earned_income_tax_credit) (EITC) and its advocates won over enough legislators for the US Congress to pass laws on that policy.[^cite_note-38] In 1986, the Basic Income European Network (later renamed the [Basic Income Earth Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Income_Earth_Network) (BIEN)) was founded, with academic conferences every second year.[^cite_note-basicincome.org-39] Other advocates included the [green political movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_politics), other activists, and some groups of unemployed people.[^cite_note-archiv-grundeinkommen.de-40]

In the late 20th century, discussions were held around automatization and jobless growth,[^cite_note-41] the possibility of combining economic growth with ecologically sustainable development, and how to reform the welfare state bureaucracy.[^cite_note-42] Basic income was interwoven in these and many other debates.[^cite_note-43] During the BIEN academic conferences, scholars published papers about basic income from a wide variety of perspectives.[^cite_note-44]

### 21st century

Further information: [List of advocates of basic income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_advocates_of_basic_income)

In recent years, the idea has come to the forefront more than before. The [Swiss referendum about basic income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Swiss_referendums) in 2016 was covered in media worldwide, despite its rejection.[^cite_note-45] Famous business people like [Elon Musk](909036.md),[^cite_note-Clifford-2018-46] [Pierre Omidyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Omidyar),[^cite_note-47] and [Andrew Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Yang) have lent their support, as have high-profile politicians like [Jeremy Corbyn](415036.md)[^cite_note-48] and [Tulsi Gabbard](26328774.md).[^cite_note-49] The Institute for Public Policy Research predicted that 59% of tasks currently done by humans could be affected by AI in the next three to five years. Universal basic Income could help fill the gap left by this "jobs apocalypse."[^cite_note-50]

In 2019-2021, in [Stockton, California](99302.md), then-Mayor [Michael Tubbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Tubbs#Mayor_of_Stockton,_CA) initiated a 24-month pilot program of guaranteed income for 125 residents as part of the privately funded S.E.E.D. project there.[^cite_note-auto-51][^cite_note-52]

In the 2020 Democratic Party primaries, political newcomer Andrew Yang touted basic income as his core policy. His policy, referred to as a "[Freedom Dividend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Dividend)", would have provided adult American citizens US\$1,000 a month independent of employment status.[^cite_note-53]

On 21 January 2021, in California, the two-year donor-funded Compton Pledge[^cite_note-auto-51] began distributing monthly guaranteed income payments to a "pre-verified" pool of low-income residents,[^cite_note-auto-51] in a program gauged for a maximum of 800 recipients, at which point it would be one of the larger among 25 U.S. cities exploring this approach to community economics.

Beginning in December 2021, [Tacoma, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacoma,_Washington), piloted "Growing Resilience in Tacoma" (GRIT), a guaranteed income initiative that provided \$500 a month to 110 families. GRIT is part of the [University of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pennsylvania)'s Center for Guaranteed Income Research larger study. A report on the results of the GRIT experiment was published in 2024.[^cite_note-54]

#### Response to COVID-19

As a response to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and [its economic impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic), universal basic income and similar proposals such as [helicopter money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicopter_money) and [cash transfers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_transfer) were increasingly discussed across the world.[^cite_note-55] Most countries implemented forms of partial unemployment schemes, which effectively subsidized workers' incomes without a work requirement. Around ninety countries and regions including the United States, Spain, Hong Kong, and Japan introduced temporary direct cash transfer programs to their citizens.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

In Europe, a petition calling for an "emergency basic income" gathered more than 200,000 signatures,[^cite_note-58] and polls suggested widespread support in public opinion for it.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] Unlike the various stimulus packages of the US administration, the EU's stimulus plans did not include any form of income-support policies.[^cite_note-61]

## Basic income vs negative income tax

Two ways of looking at basic income when combined with a flat income tax, both of which result in the same net income (orange line): 1. (red) stipend with conventional tax for income above the stipend. 2. (blue) negative tax for low-income people and conventional tax for high-income people.

The associated diagram shows a basic income/negative tax system combined with [flat income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flat_income_tax) (the same percentage in tax for every income level). Axis *y* is here the pre-tax salary given by the employer and *y'* is the net income.

*Negative income tax*

For low earnings, there is no income tax in the negative income tax system. They receive money, in the form of a negative income tax, but they do not pay any tax. Then, as their labour income increases, this benefit, this money from the state, gradually decreases. That decrease is to be seen as a mechanism for the poor, instead of the poor paying tax.

*Basic income*

That is, however, not the case in the corresponding basic income system in the diagram at right. There, everyone typically pays income taxes. But on the other hand, everyone also gets the same amount of basic income.

*But the net income is the same*

But, as the orange line in the diagram shows, the net income is anyway the same. No matter how much or how little one earns, the amount of money received is the same, regardless of which of these two systems are used.

## Perspectives and arguments

### Economist opinions

60% of top U.S. economist panel opposed and 2% favored universal basic income (UBI) financed by ending all transfer programs (including Social Security and corporate subsidies). [Daron Acemoglu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daron_Acemoglu) argued that a better safety net is needed but "UBI is expensive and not generous enough", [Markus Brunnermeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_Brunnermeier) that it could shift the norm of whether to work, [Barry Eichengreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Eichengreen) that one should wait for further experimental data, [Oliver Hart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Hart_\(economist\)) that redistribution should be targeted to the most needy, [Caroline Hoxby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Hoxby) that UBI ignores research on social insurance and optimal taxation. Some oppose ending other transfers.[^cite_note-65]

### Automation

> There is a prevailing opinion that we are in an era of technological unemployment – that technology is increasingly making skilled workers obsolete.

— Prof. Mark MacCarthy (2014)

[^cite_note-MacCarthy-66]

One central rationale for basic income is the belief that [automation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation) and robotisation could result in [technological unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_unemployment), leading to [a world with fewer paid jobs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-work_society). A key question in this context is whether a basic income could help prevent or alleviate such problems by allowing everyone to benefit from a society's wealth, as well as whether a UBI could be a stepping stone to a [resource-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource-based_economy) or [post-scarcity economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-scarcity_economy).[^cite_note-Clifford-2018-46][^cite_note-Diam-2016-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

U.S. presidential candidate and nonprofit founder [Andrew Yang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Yang) has stated that automation caused the loss of 4 million manufacturing jobs and advocated for a UBI (which he calls a [Freedom Dividend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Dividend)) of \$1,000/month rather than worker retraining programs.[^cite_note-70] Yang has stated that he is influenced by [Martin Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Ford_\(author\)). Ford believes that the emerging technologies will fail to deliver much employment; on the contrary, because the new industries will "rarely, if ever, be highly labor-intensive".[^cite_note-nytimes-71] Similar ideas have been debated before in history—that "the machines will take the jobs". What is new is the existence of several academic studies that forecast a future with substantially less employment, in the decades to come.[^cite_note-replicants-72][^cite_note-Martin-73][^cite_note-74] Additionally, US President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) stated that he believes that the growth of artificial intelligence will lead to an increased discussion around the idea of "unconditional free money for everyone".[^cite_note-75]

### Economics and costs

See also: [Cost of living](522934.md)

Some proponents of UBI have argued that basic income could increase economic growth because it would sustain people while they invest in education to get higher-skilled and well-paid jobs.[^cite_note-Tanner,_Michael_2015-76][^cite_note-Sheahen,_Allan_2012-77] However, there is also a discussion of basic income within the [degrowth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Degrowth) movement, which argues against economic growth.[^cite_note-78]

Advocates contend that the guaranteed financial security of a UBI will increase the population's willingness to take risks,[^cite_note-79] which would create a culture of inventiveness and strengthen entrepreneurial spirit.[^cite_note-80]

The cost of a basic income is one of the biggest questions in the public debate as well as in the research and depends on many things. It first and foremost depends on the level of the basic income as such, and it also depends on many technical points regarding exactly how it is constructed.

While opponents claim that a basic income at an adequate level for all citizens cannot be financed, their supporters propose that it could indeed be financed, with some advocating a strong [redistribution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redistribution_of_income_and_wealth) and restructuring of bureaucracy and administration for this purpose.[^cite_note-81]

According to statements of [American Enterprise Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Enterprise_Institute)-affiliated Libertarian/conservative scholar [Charles Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Murray_\(political_scientist\)), recalled and sanctioned in 2016 by the George Gibbs Chair in Political Economy and Senior Research Fellow at the [Mercatus Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercatus_Center) at George Mason University and nationally syndicated columnist[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] Veronique de Rugy, as of 2014, the annual cost of a UBI in the US would have been about \$200 billion less expensive than the US social safety-net system put in place at that date.[^cite_note-84] By 2020, it would have been nearly a trillion dollars less expensive.[^cite_note-85]

American economist [Karl Widerquist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Widerquist) argues that simply multiplying the amount of the grant by the population would be a naive calculation, as this is the gross costs of UBI and does not take into account that UBI is a system where people pay taxes on a regular basis and receive the grant at the same time.[^cite_note-86]

According to Swiss economist [Thomas Straubhaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Straubhaar), the concept of UBI is basically financeable without any problems. He describes it as "at its core, nothing more than a fundamental tax reform" that "bundles all social policy measures into a single instrument, the basic income paid out unconditionally."[^cite_note-Straubhaar-2017-87] He also considers a universal basic income to be socially just, arguing that although all citizens would receive the same amount in the form of the basic income at the beginning of the month, the rich would have lost significantly more money through taxes at the end of the month than they would have received through the basic income, while the opposite is the case for poorer people, similar to the concept of a [negative income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_income_tax).[^cite_note-Straubhaar-2017-87]

Recent theoretical work has introduced alternative mathematical approaches to the allocation of basic income. One such example is the [Boltzmann fair division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boltzmann_fair_division) model, which applies the Boltzmann distribution from statistical mechanics to resource or income allocation.[^cite_note-88] In this framework, each individual's share is assigned probabilistically according to an exponential function of a specified attribute (such as need or contribution), providing a flexible mechanism to balance fairness and efficiency in basic income distribution.

### Inflation of labor and rental costs

One of the most common arguments against UBI stems from the upward pressure on prices, in particular for labor and housing rents, which would likely cause [inflation](38286.md).[^cite_note-miller-89] Public policy choices such as rent controls or land value taxation would likely affect the inflationary potential of universal basic income.[^cite_note-miller-89]

### Work

Many critics of basic income argue that people, in general, will work less, which in turn means less tax revenue and less money for the state and local governments.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-cangov94-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] Studies include:

- In negative income tax experiments in the United States in 1970, there was a five percent decline in the hours worked. The work reduction was largest for second earners in two-earner households and weakest for primary earners. The reduction in hours was higher when the benefit was higher.

  [^cite_note-cangov94-91]

- In the [Mincome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mincome) experiment in rural Dauphin, Manitoba, also in the 1970s, there were slight reductions in hours worked during the experiment. However, the only two groups who worked significantly less were new mothers, and teenagers working to support their families. New mothers spent this time with their infant children, and working teenagers put significant additional time into their schooling.

  [^cite_note-94]

- A 2024 study investigated the impact of \$1,000/month UBI over a period of 3 years for 1,000 randomized low-income participants in two U.S. states, which represented an around 40% increase in household income. The study found an [income effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_choice#Income_effect) with a decrease of non-UBI income by \$1,500/year, a decrease in non-UBI household income by 21% of the UBI transfer, a 2% decrease in labor market participation, no significant change in time spent on childcare, no self-reported decrease in barriers to employment, a [null result](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Null_result) for changes in job quality, an increase in entrepreneurial orientation but no significant change in entrepreneurial activity, while enrollment in [tertiary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education) showed a slight increase for participants below 30 years of age.

  [^cite_note-o097-95]

Although it is difficult to know for sure what will happen if a whole country introduces basic income, there are nevertheless some studies that have attempted to look at this question:

- A study from 2017 showed no evidence that people worked less because of the [Iranian subsidy reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_subsidy_reform_plan) (a basic income reform).

  [^cite_note-96]

Regarding the question of basic income vs jobs, there is also the aspect of so-called [welfare traps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_trap). Proponents of basic income often argue that with a basic income, unattractive jobs would necessarily have to be better paid and their working conditions improved, so that people still do them without need, reducing these traps.[^cite_note-97]

A 2026 meta-analysis conducted by the American Enterprise Institute of 122 UBI programs found that among the thirty randomized pilots with published results, the net employment effect was +0.8%. However, in the four programs with 500 or more participants, the net employment effect was -3.2%. The median study [N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampling_\(statistics\)) was 151 people. Studies that tracked program attrition found an average dropout rate of 37%.[^cite_note-98]

### Philosophy and morality

See also: [Community cohesion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_cohesion) and [Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity)

By definition, universal basic income does not make a distinction between "deserving" and "undeserving" individuals when making payments. Opponents argue that this lack of discrimination is unfair: "Those who genuinely choose idleness or unproductive activities cannot expect those who have committed to doing productive work to subsidize their livelihood. Responsibility is central to fairness."[^cite_note-Bidadanure-2019-99]

Proponents usually view UBI as a fundamental human right that enables an [adequate standard of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_an_adequate_standard_of_living) which every citizen should have access to in modern society.[^cite_note-100] It would be a kind of foundation guaranteed for everyone, on which one could build and never fall below that subsistence level.

It is also argued that this lack of discrimination between those who supposedly deserve it and those who do not is a way to reduce [social stigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stigma).[^cite_note-Bidadanure-2019-99]

In addition, proponents of UBI may argue that the "deserving" and "undeserving" categories are a superficial classification, as people who are not in regular gainful employment also contribute to society, e.g. by raising children, caring for people, or doing other value-creating activities which are not institutionalized. UBI would provide a balance here and thus overcomes a concept of work that is reduced to pure gainful employment and disregards sideline activities too much.[^cite_note-101]

### Health and poverty

See also: [Social determinants of mental health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_determinants_of_mental_health), [Social determinants of health in poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_determinants_of_health_in_poverty), and [Social determinants of health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_determinants_of_health)

The first comprehensive systematic review of the health impact of basic income (or rather unconditional cash transfers in general) in low- and middle-income countries, a study that included 21 studies of which 16 were randomized controlled trials, found a clinically meaningful reduction in the likelihood of being sick by an estimated 27%. Unconditional cash transfers, according to the study, may also improve food security and dietary diversity. Children in recipient families are also more likely to attend school and the cash transfers may increase money spent on health care.[^cite_note-doi10.1002/14651858.CD011135.pub2-102] A 2022 update of this review confirmed these findings based on a body of evidence (35 studies, the majority being large [randomized controlled trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_controlled_trials)) and additionally found that unconditional cash transfers also reduce the likelihood of living in [extreme poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_poverty).[^cite_note-doi10.1002/14651858.CD011135.pub3-103]

The [Canadian Medical Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Medical_Association) passed a motion in 2015 in support of basic income and for basic income trials in Canada.[^cite_note-104]

## Pilot programs and experiments

Main article: [Universal basic income pilots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_basic_income_pilots)

See also: [Universal basic income by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_basic_income_by_country)



[Omitara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omitara), one of the two poor villages in [Namibia](21292.md) where a local basic income was tested in 2008–2009

Since the 1960s, but in particular since the late 2000s, several pilot programs and experiments on basic income have been conducted around the world. Some examples include:

### 1960s−1970s

- Experiments with [negative income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negative_income_tax) in the United States and Canada in the 1960s and 1970s.

- The province of [Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manitoba,_Canada), Canada experimented with [Mincome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mincome), a basic guaranteed income, in the 1970s. In the town of [Dauphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dauphin,_Manitoba), Manitoba, labor decreased by 13%, less than expected. This program was ended after issues with the cost becoming unsustainable started to arise.

  [^cite_note-105]

  [^cite_note-106]

### 2000−2009

- The basic income grant in [Namibia](21292.md) launched in 2008 and ended in 2009.

  [^cite_note-107]

- An independent pilot implemented in [São Paulo](390875.md), Brazil launched in 2009.

  [^cite_note-108]

### 2010−2019

- Basic income trials ran in 2011–2012 in several villages in [India](14533.md),

  [^cite_note-109]

  &#x20;whose government has proposed a guaranteed basic income for all citizens.

  [^cite_note-WorldCrunch-110]

  &#x20;It was found that basic income in the region raised the education rate of young people by 25%.

  [^cite_note-111]

- [Iran](14653.md) became the first country to introduce a system of UBI in December 2010.

  [^cite_note-112]

  [^cite_note-113]

  &#x20;It was paid to all citizens and replaced the gasoline subsidies, electricity, and some food,

  [^cite_note-114]

  &#x20;that the country applied for years to reduce inequalities and poverty. The sum corresponded in 2012 to approximately US\$40 per person per month, US\$480 per year for a single person, and US\$2,300 for a family of five people.

  [^cite_note-115]

  [^cite_note-116]

- In Spain, the *ingreso mínimo vital*, the income guarantee system, is an economic benefit guaranteed by the [social security in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_security_in_Spain), but in 2016 was considered in need of reform.

  [^cite_note-117]

- In [South Korea](27019.md) the Youth Allowance Program was started in 2016 in the City of [Seongnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seongnam), which would give every 24-year-old citizen 250,000 [won](298884.md) (\~US\$215) every quarter in the form of a "local currency" that could only be used in local businesses. This program was later expanded to the entire province of [Gyeonggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gyeonggi) in 2018.

  [^cite_note-118]

  [^cite_note-119]

- The [GiveDirectly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GiveDirectly) experiment in a disadvantaged village of [Nairobi](21482.md), [Kenya](188171.md), benefitting over 20,000 people living in rural Kenya, was the longest-running basic income pilot as of November 2017, which is set to run for 12 years.

  [^cite_note-120]

  [^cite_note-121]

  [^cite_note-122]

- A project called Eight in a village in [Fort Portal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Portal), [Uganda](31816.md), was launched by a nonprofit organization in January 2017, providing income for 56 adults and 88 children through mobile money.

  [^cite_note-123]

- A two-year pilot the [Finnish government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_government) began in January 2017 involved 2,000 subjects.

  [^cite_note-124]

  [^cite_note-125]

  &#x20;In April 2018, the Finnish government rejected a request for funds to extend and expand the program from [Kela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kela_\(Finnish_institution\)) ([Finland](10577.md)'s [social security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_security) agency).

  [^cite_note-126]

- An experiment in the city of [Utrecht](50333.md), [Netherlands](21148.md) launched in early 2017, that is testing different rates of aid.

  [^cite_note-WorldCrunch-110]

- A three-year [basic income pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_Basic_Income_Pilot_Project) that the [Ontario provincial government](2670174.md), Canada, launched in the cities of [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_Ontario), [Thunder Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder_Bay) and [Lindsay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsay,_Ontario) in July 2017.

  [^cite_note-127]

  &#x20;Although called basic income, it was only made available to those with a low income and funding would be removed if they obtained employment,

  [^cite_note-128]

  &#x20;making it more related to the current welfare system than true basic income. The pilot project was canceled on 31 July 2018 by the newly elected [Progressive Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Conservative_Party_of_Ontario) government under [Ontario Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_Ontario) [Doug Ford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Ford).

- In Israel, in 2018 a non-profit initiative GoodDollar started with an objective to build a global economic framework for providing universal, sustainable, and scalable basic income through the new digital asset technology of [blockchain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockchain). The non-profit aims to launch a peer-to-peer money transfer network in which money can be distributed to those most in need, regardless of their location, based on the principles of UBI. The project raised US\$1 million from a financial company.

Each farm owner receives 4,000 INR per acre twice a year for [rabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabi_crop) and [kharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharif_crop) harvests. To finance the program a budget allocation of 120 billion INR (US\$1.55 Billion as of May 2022) was made in the 2018–2019 state budget.

- Swiss non-profit [Social Income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Income) started paying out basic incomes in the form of mobile money in 2020 to people in need in [Sierra Leone](27308.md). Contributions finance the international initiative from people worldwide, who donate 1% of their monthly paychecks.

  [^cite_note-132]

- In May 2020, Spain introduced a minimum basic income, reaching about 2% of the population, in response to COVID-19 in order to "fight a spike in poverty due to the coronavirus pandemic". It was expected to cost state coffers three billion euros (\$3.5 billion) a year."

  [^cite_note-133]

- In August 2020, [a project in Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mein_Grundeinkommen) started that gives a €1,200 monthly basic income in a lottery system to citizens who applied online.   [^cite_note-134]

  &#x20;When the project was finished in August 2023, Mein Grundeinkommen calculated that a tax-financed universal basic income of €1,200 per month could be financed for every adult in Germany that would make 80% of adults better off.

  &#x20;to give \$500 monthly basic income to 25 residents. It will last five years and be compared against 50 people who are not receiving basic income.

  [^cite_note-BBC-139]

  &#x20;From July 2022 over 500 people leaving care in Wales were offered £1600 per month in a 3-year £20 million pilot scheme, to evaluate the effect on the lives of those involved in the hope of providing independence and security to people.

  [^cite_note-140]

- In July 2022, [Chicago](6886.md) began a year-long guaranteed income program by sending \$500 a month to 5,000 households for one year in a lottery system to citizens who applied online.

  [^cite_note-141]

  &#x20;A similar program was launched in late 2022 by [Cook County, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cook_County,_Illinois) (which encompasses the entirety of Chicago as well as several suburbs) which sent monthly \$500 payments to 3,250 residents with a household income at or below 250% of the federal poverty level for two years.

  [^cite_note-142]

- In June 2023, *The Guardian* reported that a universal basic income of £1,600 a month is to be trialled in two places in England – [Jarrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarrow) and [East Finchley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Finchley).

  [^cite_note-143]

- In February 2025, South Korea announced a "farmers' opportunity income" plan to be integrated into their basic income for farmers, and deployed in 24 cities and counties of the [Gyeonggi Province](323103.md). An estimated 210,000 selected farmers and fishermen will receive either 1.8 million won annually or 50,000 won monthly.

  [^cite_note-newsweek_2025_UBI-144]

## Payments with similarities

### Alaska Permanent Fund

Main article: [Alaska Permanent Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaska_Permanent_Fund)

The Permanent Fund of Alaska in the United States provides a kind of yearly basic income based on the oil and gas revenues of the state to nearly all state residents. More precisely the fund resembles a [sovereign wealth fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_wealth_fund), investing resource revenues into [bonds](60737.md), [stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_capital), and other conservative investment options with the intent to generate renewable revenue for future generations. The fund has had a noticeable yet diminishing effect on reducing poverty among rural Alaska Indigenous people, notably in the elderly population.[^cite_note-148] However, the payment is not high enough to cover basic expenses, averaging \$1,600 annually per resident in 2019 currency[^cite_note-ADN_1-149] (as of 2019 it has never exceeded \$2,100), and is not a fixed, guaranteed amount. For these reasons, it is not always considered a basic income.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-151]

### Wealth Partaking Scheme

Main article: [Wealth Partaking Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wealth_Partaking_Scheme)

[Macau](19068.md)'s Wealth Partaking Scheme provides some annual basic income to permanent residents, funded by revenues from the city's casinos. However, the amount disbursed is not sufficient to cover basic living expenses, so it is not considered a basic income.[^cite_note-152]

### Bolsa Família

Main article: [Bolsa Família](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsa_Fam%C3%ADlia)

Bolsa Família is a large social welfare program in Brazil that provides money to many low-income families in the country. The system is related to basic income, but has more conditions, like asking the recipients to keep their children in school until graduation. As of March 2020, the program covers 13.8 million families, and pays an average of \$34 per month, in a country where the [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage) is \$190 per month.[^cite_note-NPR_1-153]

### Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians

The [Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Band_of_Cherokee_Indians) (ECBI) opened [Harrah's Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrah's_Cherokee) Casino in 1997 and it has generated jobs and revenue for the tribe, providing money that the EBCI applies to its people's education, welfare and culture. Each member of the tribe is paid an annual income that started at \$500 and has increased to \$10,000 as of 2015.[^cite_note-154]

### Other welfare programs

- Pension: A payment that in some countries is guaranteed to all citizens above a certain age. The difference from true basic income is that it is restricted to people over a certain age.

- [Child benefit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_benefit): A program similar to pensions but restricted to parents of children, usually allocated based on the number of children.

- [Conditional cash transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conditional_cash_transfer): A regular payment given to families, but only to the poor. It is usually dependent on basic conditions such as sending their children to school or having them vaccinated. Programs include [Bolsa Família](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolsa_Fam%C3%ADlia) in Brazil and [Programa Prospera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oportunidades) in Mexico.

- [Guaranteed minimum income](457460.md) differs from a basic income in that it is restricted to those in search of work and possibly other restrictions, such as savings being below a certain level. Example programs are [unemployment benefits](378430.md) in the UK, the [revenu de solidarité active](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenu_de_solidarit%C3%A9_active) in France, and [citizens' income in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizens'_income_\(Italy\)).
