A **spree killer** is someone who commits a criminal act that involves two or more [murders](20171.md) in a short time, often in multiple locations. There are differing opinions about what durations of time a killing spree may take place in. The [United States Bureau of Justice Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bureau_of_Justice_Statistics) has spoken of "almost no time break between murders", but some academics consider that a killing spree may last weeks or months, e.g. the case of [Andrew Cunanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Cunanan), who murdered five people over three months, including fashion designer [Gianni Versace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianni_Versace).

## Definition

The general definition of spree murder is two or more murders committed by a person (or people) with no cooling-off period, in contrast to [serial murder](42626.md), where there is a period of time between killings.[^cite_note-Morton-1] The U.S. [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) (FBI) has found the category to be of no real value to law enforcement because quantifying a "cooling-off period" is arbitrary. Serial killers commit murders in separate events, at different times. [Mass murderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_murder) are defined by one incident, with no distinctive period between the murders.[^cite_note-Morton-1]

The differences between a spree killer, a [mass murderer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_murderer), or a [serial killer](42626.md) is subject to considerable debate, and the terms are not consistently applied within the academic literature.

The [United States Bureau of Justice Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Bureau_of_Justice_Statistics) has defined a spree killing as "killings at two or more locations with almost no time break between murders".[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

The *Encyclopedia of Crime and Punishment* lists five different categories of spree killers and cites [Mark O. Barton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Atlanta_day_trading_firm_shootings) as an example of the second one.[^cite_note-4] He is also noted alongside mass murderers, such as [Patrick Sherrill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmond_post_office_shooting), in the respective entry about mass murder.[^cite_note-5] In *The Anatomy of Motive*, [John E. Douglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_E._Douglas) cites [Charles Starkweather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Starkweather) and [Andrew Cunanan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Cunanan) (who murdered five people over three months) as examples of spree killers,[^cite_note-6] while [Jack Levin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Levin_\(sociologist\)) calls Starkweather a mass murderer and Cunanan a serial killer.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

In *Controversial Issues in Criminology*, Fuller and Hickey write that "\[t]he element of time involved between murderous acts is primary in the differentiation of serial, mass, and spree murderers", later elaborating those spree killers "will engage in the killing acts for days or weeks" while the "methods of murder and types of victims vary". Andrew Cunanan is given as an example of spree killing, while [Charles Whitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Whitman) is mentioned in connection with mass murder and [Jeffrey Dahmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Dahmer) with serial killing.[^cite_note-9]

In *Serial Murder,* Ronald M. Holmes and Stephen T. Holmes defines spree murder as "the killing of three or more people within a 30-day period" and add that killing sprees are "usually accompanied by the commission of another felony."[^cite_note-10] They cite Charles Starkweather and the [Beltway Snipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beltway_sniper_attacks) as examples of spree killers.[^cite_note-11] They define *serial* murder as "the killing of three or more people over a period of more than 30 days, with a significant cooling-off period between the killings."[^cite_note-12] Under this definition, Andrew Cunanan would be categorized as a serial killer and not a spree killer.

In *Sexual Homicide*, Ressler, Burgess and Douglas gave more emphasis to killings being at more than one location, and less precision about the time span, saying that a single event can last a short or long time, citing [Christopher Wilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Wilder)'s seven-week "murder event" or "killing spree".[^cite_note-13]

Douglas wrote that the identity of serial killers is generally unknown until they are caught, and a mass murderer's identity is learned only after they have committed the crime. The identity of the spree killer, on the other hand, usually becomes known by police while the spree is still in progress.[^cite_note-14]

The term ***rampage killer*** has also been used sometimes to describe spree killers,[^cite_note-15] especially when a single individual perpetrator:

> A rampage involves the (attempted) killing of multiple persons at least partly in public space by a single physically present perpetrator using (potentially) deadly weapons in a single event without any cooling-off period.[^cite_note-16]
