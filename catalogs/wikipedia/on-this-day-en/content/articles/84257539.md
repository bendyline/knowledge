The **tilcayo** (***Leopardus tilcayo***; [/tɪlˈkaɪoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d9/En-us-Tilcayo.ogg/En-us-Tilcayo.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/d/d9/En-us-Tilcayo.ogg/En-us-Tilcayo.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*til-KYE-oh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); Spanish pronunciation: [\[tilˈkaʝo\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/68/Tilcayo_pronunciation.ogg/Tilcayo_pronunciation.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/68/Tilcayo_pronunciation.ogg/Tilcayo_pronunciation.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), or **tilcayo tiger cat**,[^cite_note-Bush-2026-1] is a species of small wild cat in the genus *[Leopardus](2680144.md)* native to [Bolivia](3462.md). Identified through a genetic study of tiger cats published in September 2026, the tilcayo is the first extant cat species [formally described](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_description) since 1923. The tilcayo was already known to local communities under that name.[^cite_note-Bittel-2026-2] Its appearance is similar to that of other tiger cat species such as the [oncilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncilla), and its status as a genetically distinct population was not documented by [zoologists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoology) until the 2026 study.[^cite_note-3]

## Discovery

The tilcayo is the first living [felid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felidae) species formally identified since the identification of the Pampas cat *[(L. fasciatus)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pampas_cat)* in 1923.[^cite_note-Bittel-2026-2] The tilcayo was already known to local communities but had not been scientifically identified as genetically distinct from other tiger cat species.[^cite_note-Serafinko-2026-4]

[Biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biologist) and [National Geographic Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geographic_Society) explorer [Paola Nogales-Ascarrunz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paola_Nogales-Ascarrunz) first encountered the species in 2017, when the [Senda Verde Wildlife Sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Senda_Verde) in Bolivia contacted her about a cat that had been brought in. For around a year prior, the cat had been living with a man in [La Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz_Department_\(Bolivia\)) who found it as a kitten and initially believed it to be a [domestic cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_cat) before realizing it required the care of a [wildlife sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_reserve). During that time, he had attempted to feed it "noodles, rice, and eggs".[^cite_note-Bush-2026-1] Nogales-Ascarrunz was intrigued by the cat and photographed it, believing at the time that it was a unique-looking member of the related species *[Leopardus tigrinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopardus_tigrinus)* (Oncilla).[^cite_note-Bittel-2026-2]

In 2019, while preparing a booklet of Bolivian cat species, Nogales-Ascarrunz realized that the individual from the [wildlife sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_sanctuary) appeared distinct from *L. tigrinus*.[^cite_note-Bittel-2026-2] In 2026, the tilcayo was identified as a distinct species using genomic analysis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELescroart_et_al.20268-5]

## Etymology

The name "tilcayo" was already used for the species by local communities in the region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELescroart_et_al.20266-6] Publications on Bolivian fauna predating 2026 associate the term *tilcayo* with *[Leopardus tigrinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopardus_tigrinus).*[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAguirre_et_al.2009436-7] It was originally reported that the exact etymology of the term is unknown, as it did not appear to be of [Spanish](26825.md), [Quechuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quechuan_languages), or [Aymara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymara_language) origin. The term has been passed down in the local communities through generations, with its meaning reportedly lost in transfer.[^cite_note-Bittel-2026-2][^cite_note-8] However, in an interview following the announcement of the species identification, Nogales-Ascarrunz stated that native Aymara speakers had reached out through social media suggesting that the term likely comes from *t'ili kayu* (lit. 'tiny foot'[^cite_note-9]).[^cite_note-10]

## Distribution and habitat

The tilcayo was found near the town of [Arapata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arapata) in [Nor Yungas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nor_Yungas_Province) province, in the Bolivian department of [La Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz_Department_\(Bolivia\)), at an elevation of 1,570 m (5,150 ft).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELescroart_et_al.20266-6] This locality is positioned in the mountainous [Yungas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yungas) forest region, a [cloud forest](58903.md), on the eastern slope of the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) mountain range.[^cite_note-Bittel-2026-2]

## Taxonomy

A [genetic analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_analysis) of 38 cats from South and Central America, including 26 individuals in the *[Leopardus tigrinus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopardus_tigrinus)* species complex, revealed that the *tigrinus* complex consists of five distinct species, including *L. tilcayo*, which is believed to have split off from [other *Leopardus* species](2680144.md) more than 1.4 million years ago.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELescroart_et_al.20266-6] The study incorporated genetic material from previously unsampled tiger cats in the [Guianas region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guianas), near where *L. tigrinus* was originally described in 1775. [Jonas Lescroart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Lescroart?action=edit\&redlink=1), an evolutionary [ecologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecologist) at the [University of Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Antwerp) and co-lead author of the study, called it "the most complete genetic study yet" of tiger cats.[^cite_note-Cara-2026-11]

## Characteristics

The fur of the tilcayo is light brown with irregularly shaped [rosettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosette_\(zoology\)).[^cite_note-Serafinko-2026-4] Its rosettes are large and dark brown to black in color, and are either open or partially closed –they do not coalesce.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELescroart_et_al.20266-6] It also has short, round ears, long whiskers, and a scrunched-up face. Like many species in the *Leopardus* genus, it is smaller than a domestic cat.[^cite_note-Bittel-2026-2] It has a head–body length of 42.5–50 cm (16.7–19.7 in), with a tail length of 25–26.5 cm (9.8–10.4 in). It weighs 1.5–2.0 kg (3.3–4.4 lb).[^cite_note-Ferrari-2026-13] The tilcayo so closely resemble other tiger cat species they are considered a [cryptic species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptic_species), difficult to distinguish from others by morphology alone.[^cite_note-14]

## Conservation

As of September 2026, *L. tilcayo* has not been formally assessed by the [IUCN Red List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IUCN_Red_List). It currently recognizes two tiger cat species, *L. tigrinus* and *[L. guttulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopardus_guttulus)*, both listed as [vulnerable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerable_species).[^cite_note-Cara-2026-11] According to study co-lead author Jonas Lescroart, the research team shared their findings with the IUCN ahead of a planned reassessment of tiger cats.[^cite_note-Cara-2026-11] Study co-author Tadeu de Oliveira, an ecologist at the [State University of Maranhão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_University_of_Maranh%C3%A3o) in Brazil, said the tiger cat species identified in the study are found in habitats that are "vanishing fast".[^cite_note-Bush-2026-1]

The individual which first came to the attention of Nogales-Ascarrunz remains in protective captivity at the Senda Verde animal refuge in Bolivia. Some ten years old, he has been nicknamed "Tigrino".[^cite_note-15]

Habitat loss, deforestation and mining have been identified as threats to the species. UMSA researchers have also identified hunting resulting from conflicts with local inhabitants when the cats prey on domestic poultry, as well as keeping of the animals as pets, as potential threats to the tilcayo.[^cite_note-UMSA18sep-16]

The species has not yet been formally assessed by the [International Union for Conservation of Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_for_Conservation_of_Nature) (IUCN). Researchers have indicated that further studies of its distribution, population, reproduction and habitat requirements are needed before its conservation status can be established.[^cite_note-UMSA18sep-16]

## Research

Some of the initial genetic research on *Leopardus tilcayo* was conducted at the Molecular Genetics Laboratory of the [Higher University of San Andrés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_University_of_San_Andr%C3%A9s) (UMSA) in Bolivia. According to the university, the samples examined included tissue, hair and teeth, from which DNA was extracted and subjected to initial genetic analyses. These results were subsequently compared with samples obtained by researchers from other countries as part of the international study that led to the formal description of the species.[^cite_note-UMSA21sep-17]

Researcher Patricia Mollinedo stated that this initial stage involved at least two years of work, including DNA extraction and processing of the samples and other stages of the analysis. The infrastructure and equipment used belonged to UMSA, while laboratory supplies were financed through external resources obtained for the research project.[^cite_note-UMSA21sep-17]

Bolivian researchers [Paola Nogales-Ascarrunz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paola_Nogales-Ascarrunz) and Enzo Aliaga-Rossel participated in the international research that established the genetic identity of the species. UMSA subsequently reported that both researchers were recognized by [Department of La Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz_Department_\(Bolivia\)) for their participation in the discovery.[^cite_note-UMSA18sep-16]
