**Missouri** (*see [pronunciation](#Etymology_and_pronunciation)*) is a landlocked [state](18618239.md) in the [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States) region of the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-6] Ranking [21st in land area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area), it borders [Iowa](26810748.md) to the north, [Illinois](14849.md), [Kentucky](16846.md) and [Tennessee](30395.md) to the east, [Arkansas](1930.md) to the south and [Oklahoma](22489.md), [Kansas](16716.md), and [Nebraska](21647.md) to the west. With over six million residents, it is the [19th-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population) state of the country. The largest urban areas are [St. Louis](27687.md), [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri), [Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Missouri), and [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_Missouri). The [capital](181337.md) is [Jefferson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_City,_Missouri).

Humans have inhabited present-day Missouri for at least 12,000 years. The [Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture), which emerged in the ninth century, built cities with pyramidal and other ceremonial [mounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mound_Builders) before declining in the 14th century. The Indigenous [Osage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osage_Nation) and [Missouria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouria) nations inhabited the area when European people arrived in the 17th century. The French incorporated the territory into [Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)), founding [Ste. Genevieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ste._Genevieve,_Missouri) in 1735 and [St. Louis](27687.md) in 1764. After a brief period of [Spanish rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain), the United States acquired Missouri as part of the [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase) in 1803. Americans from the [Upland South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_South) rushed into the new [Missouri Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Territory), taking advantage of its productive agricultural plains; Missouri played a central role in the westward expansion of the United States.[^cite_note-7] Missouri was admitted as a slave state as part of the [Missouri Compromise of 1820](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Compromise). As a [border state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_states_\(American_Civil_War\)), [Missouri's role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_in_the_American_Civil_War) in the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) was complex, and it was subject to rival governments, raids, and guerilla warfare. After the war, both [Greater St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_St._Louis) and the [Kansas City metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_metropolitan_area) became large centers of industrialization and business.

Today the state is divided into [114 counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Missouri) and the [independent city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_city_\(United_States\)) of St. Louis. Missouri has been called the "Gateway to the West",[^cite_note-8] the "Mother of the West", the "Cave State", and the "Show Me State".[^cite_note-9] Its culture blends elements of the Midwestern and [Southern United States](179553.md). It is the birthplace of the musical genres [ragtime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragtime), [Kansas City jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_jazz) and [St. Louis blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_blues_\(music\)). The well-known [Kansas City-style barbecue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City-style_barbecue), and the lesser-known [St. Louis-style barbecue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis-style_barbecue), can be found across the state and beyond.

Missouri is a major center of beer brewing and has some of the most permissive [alcohol laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri's_alcohol_laws) in the U.S.[^cite_note-10] It is home to [Anheuser-Busch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anheuser-Busch), the world's largest beer producer, and produces [Missouri wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_wine), especially in the [Missouri Rhineland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Rhineland). Outside the state's major cities, popular tourist destinations include the [Lake of the Ozarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_of_the_Ozarks), [Table Rock Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Rock_Lake) and [Branson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branson,_Missouri). Some of the largest companies based in the state include [Cerner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerner), [Express Scripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_Scripts), [Monsanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsanto), [Emerson Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerson_Electric), [Edward Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Jones_Investments), [H\&R Block](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H\&R_Block), [Wells Fargo Advisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo_Advisors), [Centene Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centene_Corporation), and [O'Reilly Auto Parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Reilly_Auto_Parts). Well-known universities in Missouri include the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri), [Saint Louis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Louis_University_\(United_States\)), and [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis).[^cite_note-11]

## Etymology and pronunciation

The state is named for the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River), which was named after the indigenous [Missouria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouria), a [Siouan-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siouan_languages) tribe. French colonists adapted a form of the [Illinois language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_language)-name for the people: *Wimihsoorita*. Their name means 'one who has dugout canoes'.[^cite_note-12]

The name *Missouri* has several different pronunciations even among its present-day inhabitants,[^cite_note-wheaton20121013-13] the two most common being [/mɪˈzɜːri/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f0/En-us-Missouri.ogg/En-us-Missouri.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f0/En-us-Missouri.ogg/En-us-Missouri.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*mih-ZUR-ee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key) and [/mɪˈzɜːrə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b2/En-us-Missouri-2.ogg/En-us-Missouri-2.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b2/En-us-Missouri-2.ogg/En-us-Missouri-2.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*mih-ZUR-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-lance2003-15] Further pronunciations also exist in Missouri or elsewhere in the United States, involving the realization of the medial consonant as either [/z/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) or [/s/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English); the vowel in the second syllable as either [/ɜːr/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) or [/ʊər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-16] and the third syllable as [/i/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) or [/ə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English).[^cite_note-lance2003-15] Any combination of these phonetic realizations may be observed coming from speakers of [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English). In British [Received Pronunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Received_Pronunciation), the preferred variant is [/mɪˈzʊəri/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), with [/mɪˈsʊəri/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) being a possible alternative.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

Donald M. Lance, a professor of English at the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri), stated that no pronunciation could be declared correct, nor could any be clearly defined as native or outsider, rural or urban, southern or northern, educated or otherwise.[^cite_note-19] Politicians often employ multiple pronunciations, even during a single speech, to appeal to a greater number of listeners.[^cite_note-wheaton20121013-13] In informal contexts respellings of the state's name, such as "Missour-*ee*" or "Missour-*uh*", are occasionally used to distinguish pronunciations phonetically.

### Nicknames

There is no official state nickname.[^cite_note-Slogan-20] The unofficial nickname, appearing on Missourian [license plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Missouri), is "the Show Me State". This derives from the saying "I'm from Missouri, you'll have to show me", meaning "I'm skeptical of what you're saying", with allusion to Missourians' reputation for skepticism and incredulity.[^cite_note-21] The saying first appeared in the 1890s, and was popularized by Congressman [Willard Vandiver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard_Vandiver), who declared in a 1900 speech, "I come from a state that raises corn and cotton, [cockleburs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cocklebur) and Democrats, and frothy eloquence neither convinces nor satisfies me. I'm from Missouri, and you have got to show me."[^cite_note-22]

Other nicknames for Missouri include "the Lead State", "the Bullion State", "the Ozark State", "the Mother of the West", "the Iron Mountain State", and "Pennsylvania of the West".[^cite_note-23] It is also known as the "Cave State"[^cite_note-24]<sup>: 53 </sup> because there are more than 7,300 recorded caves in the state (second to [Tennessee](30395.md)). [Perry County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry_County,_Missouri) is the county with the most caves and the single longest cave.[^cite_note-MSS-25][^cite_note-26]

The official state motto is *Salus Populi Suprema Lex Esto*, [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) for "Let the welfare of the people be the supreme law."[^cite_note-27]

### Early history

[Archaeological](18951655.md) excavations along river valleys have shown continuous habitation since about 9000 BCE.[^cite_note-28] Beginning before 1000 [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era), the people of the [Mississippian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture) created regional political centers at present-day [St. Louis](27687.md) and across the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) at [Cahokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahokia), near present-day [Collinsville, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collinsville,_Illinois). Their large cities included thousands of individual residences. Still, they are known for their surviving massive [earthwork mounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthwork_\(archaeology\)), built for religious, political and social reasons, in [platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platform_mound), [ridgetop](714908.md) and [conical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cone_\(geometry\)) shapes. Cahokia was the center of a regional trading network that reached from the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) to the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md). The civilization declined by 1400 CE, and most descendants left the area long before the arrival of Europeans. St. Louis was at one time known as Mound City by the European Americans because of the numerous surviving prehistoric mounds since lost to urban development. The Mississippian culture left mounds throughout the middle Mississippi and Ohio river valleys, extending into the southeast and the upper river.

The land that became the state of Missouri was part of numerous different territories, possessed changing and often indeterminate borders, and had many different [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) and European names between the 1600s and statehood. For much of the first half of the 1700s, the west bank of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) that would become Missouri was mostly uninhabited, something of a no man's land that kept peace between the [Illinois](14849.md) on the east bank of the Mississippi River and to the North, and the Osage and Missouri Indians of the lower Missouri Valley. In the early 1700s, French traders and missionaries explored the whole of the Mississippi Valley, and named the region "Louisiana". Around the same time, a different group of French Canadians established five villages on the east bank of the Mississippi River and identified their settlements as being in le pays des Illinois, "the country of the Illinois". When settlers of [French Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canadians) descent began crossing the Mississippi River to establish settlements such as Ste. Genevieve, they continued to identify their settlements as being in the Illinois Country. At the same time, the French settlements on both sides of the Mississippi River were part of the French province of [Louisiana](18130.md). To distinguish the settlements in the Middle Mississippi Valley from French settlements in the lower Mississippi Valley around New Orleans, French officials and inhabitants referred to the Middle Mississippi Valley as La Haute Louisiane, "The High Louisiana", or "Upper Louisiana".



*[Fur Traders Descending the Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_Traders_Descending_the_Missouri)* by Missouri painter [George Caleb Bingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Caleb_Bingham)

The first European settlers were mostly ethnic [French Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Canadian), who created their first settlement in Missouri at present-day [Ste. Genevieve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ste._Genevieve,_Missouri), about 45 miles (72 km) south of St. Louis. They had migrated in about 1750 from the [Illinois](14849.md) Country. They came from colonial villages on the east side of the Mississippi River, where soils were becoming exhausted and there was insufficient river bottom land for the growing population. The early Missouri [settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_settlement) included many enslaved Africans and Native Americans, and slave labor was central to both commercial agriculture and the fur trade. Sainte-Geneviève became a thriving agricultural center, producing enough surplus wheat, [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and tobacco to ship tons of grain annually downriver to Lower Louisiana for trade. Grain production in the Illinois Country was critical to the survival of Lower Louisiana and especially the city of New Orleans.

St. Louis was founded on February 14, 1764, by French fur traders [Gilbert Antoine de St. Maxent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Antoine_de_St._Maxent), [Pierre Laclède](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Lacl%C3%A8de), and [Auguste Chouteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_Chouteau).[^cite_note-Cazorla_et_al-29] From 1764 to 1803, European control of the area west of the Mississippi to the northernmost part of the Missouri River basin, called Louisiana, was assumed by the Spanish as part of the Viceroyalty of [New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain), due to [Treaty of Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fontainebleau_\(1762\))[^cite_note-foley_1989_26-30] (in order to have Spain join with France in the war against England). The arrival of the Spanish in St. Louis was in September 1767.

St. Louis became the center of a regional [fur trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) with Native American tribes that extended up the Missouri and Mississippi rivers, dominating the regional economy for decades. Trading partners of major firms shipped their furs from St. Louis by river down to New Orleans for export to Europe. They provided a variety of goods to traders for sale and trade with their Native American clients. The fur trade and associated businesses made St. Louis an early financial center and provided the wealth for some to build fine houses and import luxury items. Its location near the confluence of the Illinois River meant it also handled produce from the agricultural areas. River traffic and trade along the Mississippi were integral to the state's economy. As the area's first major city, St. Louis expanded greatly after the invention of the [steamboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboat) and the increased river trade.

### 19th century



Areas of [Spanish Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Louisiana) around 1803 overlaid over the current American states that it encompassed.

Part of the 1803 [Louisiana Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase) by the United States, Missouri earned the nickname *Gateway to the West* because it served as a significant departure point for expeditions and settlers heading to the West during the 19th century. [St. Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Charles,_Missouri), just west of St. Louis, was the starting point and the return destination of the [Lewis and Clark Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition), which ascended the Missouri River in 1804, to explore the western lands to the Pacific Ocean. For decades, [St. Louis](27687.md) was a major supply point for parties of settlers heading west.

Missouri was historically a Southern state. As many of the early settlers in western and southeastern Missouri migrated from the [Upper South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_South) including [Kentucky](16846.md), [Tennessee](30395.md), and [Virginia](32432.md), they brought enslaved [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) as agricultural laborers, and they desired to continue their culture and the institution of [slavery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States). They settled predominantly in 17 counties along the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River), in an area of flatlands that enabled [plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantations_in_the_American_South) agriculture and became known as "[Little Dixie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Dixie_\(Missouri\))".

The state was rocked by the [1811–12 New Madrid earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1811%E2%80%9312_New_Madrid_earthquakes). Casualties were few due to the sparse population.

### Admission as a state in 1821

See also: [History of slavery in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery_in_Missouri)



The states and territories of the United States as a result of Missouri's admission as a state on August 10, 1821. The remainder of the former [Missouri Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Territory) became unorganized territory.

In 1821, the former Missouri Territory was admitted as a [slave state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_and_free_states), under the [Missouri Compromise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Compromise), and with a temporary state capital in St. Charles. In 1826, the [capital](181337.md) was shifted to its permanent location of [Jefferson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_City,_Missouri), also on the Missouri River.

Originally the state's western border was a straight line, defined as the meridian passing through the Kawsmouth,[^cite_note-31] the point where the [Kansas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_River) enters the Missouri River. The river has moved since this designation. This line is known as the Osage Boundary.[^cite_note-32] In 1836 the [Platte Purchase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platte_Purchase) was added to the northwest corner of the state after purchase of the land from the native tribes, making the Missouri River the border north of the Kansas River. This addition increased the land area of what was already the largest state in the Union at the time (about 66,500 square miles (172,000 km<sup>2</sup>) to Virginia's 65,000 square miles, which then included West Virginia).[^cite_note-33]

In the early 1830s, [Mormon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon) migrants from northern states and Canada began settling near [Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence,_Missouri) and areas just north of there. Conflicts over religion and slavery arose between the 'old settlers' (mainly from the South) and the Mormons (mainly from the North). The [Mormon War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormon_War_\(1838\)) erupted in 1838. Following Governor [Lilburn Boggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lilburn_Boggs)'s issuance of the "Extermination Order" in late 1838, the Mormons surrendered and were violently driven out of Missouri by local citizens and militia over the course of the following year.

Conflicts over slavery exacerbated border tensions among the states and territories. From 1838 to 1839, a border dispute with [Iowa](26810748.md) over the so-called [Honey Lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey_Lands) resulted in both states' calling-up of [militias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_\(United_States\)) along the border.

With increasing migration, from the 1830s to the 1860s, Missouri's population almost doubled with every decade. Most newcomers were American-born and Southern, but many later arrivals were Northern migrants as well as Irish and German immigrants who arrived in the late 1840s and 1850s. A growing [Catholic](606848.md) majority established its own institutional infrastructure, altering the religious landscape of the historically [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) state. Many settled in cities, creating a regional and then state network of Catholic churches and schools. 19th-century German immigrants created the wine industry along the Missouri River and the beer industry in St. Louis.

While many German immigrants were strongly anti-slavery,[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-\:1-35] many Irish immigrants living in cities were pro-slavery, fearing that liberating African-American slaves would create a glut of unskilled labor, driving wages down.[^cite_note-\:1-35]

Most Missouri farmers practiced [subsistence farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsistence_farming) before the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War). The majority of those who held slaves had fewer than five each. [Planters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planter_\(plantation_owner\)), defined by some historians as those holding 20 slaves or more, were concentrated in the counties known as "[Little Dixie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Dixie_\(Missouri\))", in the central part of the state along the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River) as well as southeastern Missouri. The tensions over slavery chiefly had to do with the future of the state and nation. In 1860, enslaved [African Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American) made up less than 10% of the state's population of 1,182,012.[^cite_note-36] In order to control the flooding of farmland and low-lying villages along the Mississippi, the state had completed construction of 140 miles (230 km) of [levees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levee) along the river by 1860.[^cite_note-37]

### American Civil War

Main articles: [Ordinance of Secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinance_of_Secession), [Missouri in the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_in_the_American_Civil_War), and [Confederate government of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_government_of_Missouri)



[Price's Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price's_Raid) in the [Trans-Mississippi Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Mississippi_Theater_of_the_American_Civil_War), 1864

After the secession of Southern states began in 1861, the Missouri legislature called for the election of a special convention on secession. This convention voted against secession, but also qualified their support of the Union. In the aftermath of [Battle of Fort Sumter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fort_Sumter) Pro-Southern Governor [Claiborne F. Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claiborne_F._Jackson) ordered the mobilization of several hundred members of the state militia who had gathered in a camp in [St. Louis](27687.md) for training. In secret, he also requested Confederate arms and artillery to help take the [St. Louis Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Arsenal). Alarmed at this action, and discovering the Confederate aid, General [Nathaniel Lyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Lyon) struck first, encircling the camp and forcing the state troops to surrender. Lyon directed his soldiers, largely non-English-speaking German [immigrants](4599312.md), to march the prisoners through the streets, and this led to riot by pro-secession citizens. While it is disputed how it started, this riot led to violence and Union soldiers killed by St. Louis civilians. The event as a whole, is called the [Camp Jackson Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Jackson_Affair).

These events sharpened the divisions within the state. Governor Jackson appointed [Sterling Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_Price), president of the convention on secession, as head of the new [Missouri State Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_State_Guard). In the face of Union General Lyon's rapid advance through the state, Jackson and Price were forced to flee the capital of [Jefferson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_City) on June 14, 1861. In [Neosho, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neosho,_Missouri), Jackson called the state legislature into session to call for secession. However, the elected legislative body was split between pro-Union and pro-Confederate. As such, few of the pro-unionist attended the session called in Neosho, and the ordinance of secession was quickly adopted. The Confederacy recognized Missouri secession on October 30, 1861.

With the elected governor absent from the capital and the legislators largely dispersed, the state convention was reassembled with most of its members present, save twenty who fled south with Jackson's forces. The convention declared all offices vacant and installed [Hamilton Gamble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_Gamble) as the new governor of Missouri. President Lincoln's administration immediately recognized Gamble's government as the legal Missouri government. The federal government's decision enabled raising pro-Union militia forces for service within the state and volunteer regiments for the Union Army.

Fighting ensued between Union forces and a combined army of General Price's Missouri State Guard and Confederate troops from [Arkansas](1930.md) and Texas under General [Ben McCulloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_McCulloch). After winning victories at the [battle of Wilson's Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Wilson's_Creek) and the siege of [Lexington, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexington,_Missouri) and suffering losses elsewhere, the Confederate forces retreated to Arkansas and later [Marshall, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall,_Texas), in the face of a largely reinforced Union Army.

Though regular Confederate troops staged some large-scale raids into Missouri, the fighting in the state for the next three years consisted chiefly of [guerrilla warfare](12720.md). "Citizen soldiers" or insurgents such as Captain [William Quantrill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Quantrill), [Frank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_James) and [Jesse James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_James), the [Younger brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James-Younger_gang), and [William T. Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_T._Anderson) made use of quick, small-unit tactics. Pioneered by the Missouri Partisan Rangers, such insurgencies also arose in portions of the Confederacy occupied by the Union during the Civil War. Historians have portrayed stories of the James brothers' outlaw years as an American "Robin Hood" myth.[^cite_note-38] The vigilante activities of the [Bald Knobbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bald_Knobbers) of the Ozarks in the 1880s were an unofficial continuation of insurgent mentality long after the official end of the war, and they are a favorite theme in [Branson's self-image](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branson,_Missouri).[^cite_note-39]

[Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(St._Louis\)) in St. Louis was the world's largest and busiest train station when it opened in 1894.

Missouri remained electorally competitive during the [Jim Crow era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws), and did not disenfranchise African Americans, who comprised less than 10% of the state's population from 1870 to 1960. In particular, Missouri never implemented a [poll tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poll_taxes_in_the_United_States) as a requirement to vote.[^cite_note-Williams_1952-40]

However, Missouri did enact racial segregation. Democratic President [Harry S. Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman) grew up in Missouri, where segregation was practiced and largely accepted. Truman would later issue [Executive Order 9981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_9981) in July 1948, prohibiting racial segregation in the armed forces.[^cite_note-41]

### 20th century



Child shoe workers in [Kirksville, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirksville,_Missouri), 1910

The [Progressive Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Era) (1890s to 1920s) saw numerous prominent leaders from Missouri trying to end corruption and modernize politics, government, and society. [Joseph "Holy Joe" Folk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Folk) was a key leader who made a strong appeal to the middle class and rural evangelical Protestants. Folk was elected governor as a progressive reformer and [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Democratic_Party_\(United_States\)) in the [1904 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_gubernatorial_election,_1904). He promoted what he called "the Missouri Idea", the concept of Missouri as a leader in public morality through popular control of law and strict enforcement. He successfully conducted antitrust prosecutions, ended free railroad passes for state officials, extended bribery statutes, improved election laws, required formal registration for lobbyists, made racetrack gambling illegal and enforced the Sunday-closing law. He helped enact Progressive legislation, including an initiative and referendum provision, regulation of elections, education, employment and child labor, railroads, food, business, and public utilities. Several efficiency-oriented examiner boards and commissions were established during Folk's administration, including many agricultural boards and the Missouri library commission.[^cite_note-42]



World War I General [John J. Pershing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_J._Pershing) was raised in [Laclede, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laclede,_Missouri).

Between the Civil War and the end of World War II, Missouri transitioned from a rural southern state to a hybrid industrial-service-agricultural midwestern state as the Midwest rapidly industrialized and expanded into Missouri. The expansion of railroads to the West transformed Kansas City into a major transportation hub within the nation, and led to major Midwestern migration after the war overtaking the state's original Southern population. The growth of the Texas cattle industry along with this increased rail infrastructure and the invention of the [refrigerated boxcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrigerated_boxcar) also made Kansas City a major [meatpacking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meatpacking) center, as large [cattle drives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_drives) from Texas brought herds of cattle to [Dodge City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_City) and other Kansas towns. There, the cattle were loaded onto trains destined for Kansas City, where they were butchered and distributed to the eastern markets. The first half of the 20th century was the height of Kansas City's prominence, and its downtown became a showcase for stylish [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco) [skyscrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyscrapers) as construction boomed.

In 1930, there was a [diphtheria](58937.md) epidemic in the area around Springfield, which killed approximately 100 people. Serum was rushed to the area, and medical personnel stopped the epidemic.



African American boy in a [sharecropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharecropping#United_States) shack, [New Madrid County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Madrid_County), 1938

During the mid-1950s and 1960s, St. Louis and Kansas City suffered deindustrialization and loss of jobs in railroads and manufacturing, as did other [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern) industrial cities. [St. Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Charles,_Missouri) claims to be the site of the first [interstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System) highway project in 1956.[^cite_note-43] Such highway construction made it easy for middle-class residents to leave the city for newer housing developed in the suburbs, often former farmland where land was available at lower prices. These major cities have gone through decades of readjustment to develop different economies and adjust to demographic changes. Suburban areas have developed separate job markets, both in knowledge industries and services, such as major retail malls.

### 21st century

In 2014, Missouri received national attention for the [protests and riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferguson_unrest) that followed the [shooting of Michael Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooting_of_Michael_Brown) by a police officer of [Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferguson,_Missouri),[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] which led Governor [Jay Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Nixon) to call out the [Missouri National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_National_Guard).[^cite_note-NYTaug19-47][^cite_note-48] A [grand jury](34323893.md) declined to [indict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indict) the officer, and the [U.S. Department of Justice](52563.md) concluded, after careful investigation, that the police officer legitimately feared for his safety.[^cite_note-49] However, in a separate investigation, the Department of Justice also found that the Ferguson Police Department and the City of Ferguson relied on unconstitutional practices in order to balance the city's budget through racially motivated excessive fines and punishments,[^cite_note-50] that the Ferguson police "had used excessive and dangerous force and had disproportionately targeted blacks,"[^cite_note-51] and that the municipal court "emphasized revenue over public safety, leading to routine breaches of citizens' constitutional guarantees of due process and equal protection under the law."[^cite_note-52]

[A series of student protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_University_of_Missouri_protests) at the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri) against what the protesters viewed as poor response by the administration to racist incidents on campus began in September 2015.[^cite_note-colu_Stud-53][^cite_note-them_Seco-54]

On June 7, 2017, the [National Association for the Advancement of Colored People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_for_the_Advancement_of_Colored_People) issued a warning to prospective African-American travelers to Missouri. This is the first NAACP warning ever covering an entire state.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] According to a 2018 report by the [Missouri Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Attorney_General)'s office, for the past 18 years, "African Americans, Hispanics and other people of color are disproportionately affected by stops, searches and arrests."[^cite_note-57] The same report found that the biggest discrepancy was in 2017, when "black motorists were 85% more likely to be pulled over in traffic stops".[^cite_note-58]

In 2018, the USDA announced its plans to relocate Economic Research Service (ERS) and National Institute of Food & Agriculture (NIFA) to Kansas City. They have since decided on a specific location in downtown Kansas City, Missouri.[^cite_note-59] With the addition of the KC Streetcar project and construction of the Sprint Center Arena, the downtown area in KC has attracted investment in new offices, hotels, and residential complexes. Both Kansas City and St. Louis are undergoing a rebirth in their downtown areas with the addition of the new Power & Light (KC) and Ballpark Village (STL) districts and the renovation of existing historical buildings in each downtown area.[^cite_note-60] The 2019 announcement of an MLS expansion team in St. Louis is driving even more development in the downtown west area of St. Louis.[^cite_note-61] Kansas City has experienced a boom in population, with new developments such as Three Light apartments being centered in [Downtown Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Kansas_City),[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] as well as suburban development in the [Northland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northland,_Kansas_City).[^cite_note-64]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Missouri)

Missouri borders eight different states, a figure equaled only by its neighbor, [Tennessee](30395.md). Missouri is bounded by [Iowa](26810748.md) on the north; by [Illinois](14849.md), [Kentucky](16846.md), and Tennessee across the Mississippi River on the east; on the south by [Arkansas](1930.md); and by [Oklahoma](22489.md), [Kansas](16716.md), and [Nebraska](21647.md) (the last across the Missouri River) on the west. Whereas the northern and southern boundaries are straight lines, the [Missouri Bootheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Bootheel) extends south between the [St. Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Francis_River) and the [Mississippi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) rivers. The two largest rivers are the Mississippi (which defines the eastern boundary of the state) and the Missouri River (which flows from west to east through the state), essentially connecting the two largest metros of Kansas City and St. Louis.

Although today it is usually considered part of the [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States),[^cite_note-65] Missouri was historically seen by many as a [border state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border_states_\(American_Civil_War\)), chiefly because of the settlement of migrants from the South and its status as a slave state before the Civil War, balanced by the influence of St. Louis. The counties that made up "[Little Dixie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Dixie_\(Missouri\))" were those along the Missouri River in the center of the state, settled by Southern migrants who held the greatest concentration of slaves.

In 2005, Missouri received 16,695,000 visitors to its national parks and other recreational areas totaling 101,000 acres (410 km<sup>2</sup>), giving it \$7.41 million in annual revenues, 26.6% of its operating expenditures.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-66]

### Topography



A physiographic map of Missouri

North of, and in some cases just south of, the Missouri River lie the Northern Plains that stretch into Iowa, Nebraska, and Kansas. Here, rolling hills remain from the [glaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaciation) that once extended from the Canadian Shield to the Missouri River. Missouri has many large river bluffs along the Mississippi, Missouri, and [Meramec Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meramec_River). Southern Missouri rises to the [Ozark Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozark_Mountains), a [dissected plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissected_plateau) surrounding the [Precambrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precambrian) [igneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous) [St. Francois Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Francois_Mountains). This region also hosts [karst topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst_topography) characterized by high limestone content with the formation of sinkholes and caves.[^cite_note-67]

The southeastern part of the state is known as the [Missouri Bootheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Bootheel) region, which is part of the Mississippi Alluvial Plain or [Mississippi embayment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_embayment). This region is the lowest, flattest, warmest, and wettest part of the state. It is also among the poorest, as the economy there is mostly agricultural.[^cite_note-68] It is also the most fertile, with cotton and rice crops predominant. The Bootheel was the epicenter of the four [New Madrid Earthquakes of 1811 and 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1811%E2%80%9312_New_Madrid_earthquakes).

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Missouri)



Köppen climate types of Missouri

Missouri generally has a [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) with cool, sometimes cold, winters and hot, humid, and wet summers. In the southern part of the state, particularly in the [Bootheel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Bootheel), the climate becomes [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate). Located in the interior United States, Missouri often experiences extreme temperatures. Without high mountains or oceans nearby to moderate temperature, its climate is alternately influenced by air from the cold Arctic and the hot and humid Gulf of Mexico. Missouri's highest recorded temperature is 118 °F (48 °C) at [Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw,_Missouri) and [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union,_Missouri) on July 14, 1954, while the lowest recorded temperature is −40 °F (−40 °C) also at Warsaw on February 13, 1905.

Located in [Tornado Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tornado_Alley), Missouri also receives extreme weather in the form of severe thunderstorms and tornadoes. On May 22, 2011, a [massive EF-5 tornado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Joplin_tornado) killed 158 people and destroyed roughly one-third of the city of [Joplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joplin,_Missouri). The tornado caused an estimated \$1–3 billion in damages, killed 159 people and injured more than a thousand. It was the first EF5 to hit the state since 1957 and the deadliest in the U.S. since 1947, making it the seventh deadliest tornado in American history and 27th deadliest in the world. [St. Louis](27687.md) and its suburbs also have a history of experiencing particularly severe tornadoes, the most recent one of note being an EF4 that damaged [Lambert-St. Louis International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambert-St._Louis_International_Airport) on April 22, 2011. [One of the worst tornadoes in American history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1896_St._Louis%E2%80%93East_St._Louis_tornado) struck St. Louis on May 27, 1896, killing at least 255 people and causing \$10 million in damage (equivalent to \$3.9 billion in 2009 or \$5.85 billion in today's dollars).

### Flora and fauna

Main article: [Wildlife of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Missouri)



The [Lake of the Ozarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_of_the_Ozarks) is one of several man-made lakes in Missouri, created by the damming of several rivers and tributaries.



Missouri River near [Rocheport, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocheport,_Missouri)

Missouri is home to diverse [flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora) and [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna), including several [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) species.[^cite_note-70] There is a large amount of [fresh water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_water) present due to the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River), [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River), [Table Rock Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Rock_Lake) and [Lake of the Ozarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_of_the_Ozarks), with numerous smaller tributary rivers, streams, and lakes. North of the Missouri River, the state is primarily rolling hills of the [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains), whereas south of the Missouri River, the state is dominated by the Oak-Hickory [Central U.S. hardwood forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_U.S._hardwood_forest).

Recreational and commercial uses of public forests, including grazing, logging, and mining, increased after World War II. Fishermen, hikers, campers, and others started lobbying to protect forest areas with a "wilderness character". During the 1930s and 1940s, [Aldo Leopold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_Leopold), [Arthur Carhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Carhart) and [Bob Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Marshall_\(wilderness_activist\)) developed a "wilderness" policy for the Forest Service. Their efforts bore fruit with the [Wilderness Act of 1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilderness_Act_of_1964), which designated wilderness areas "where the earth and its community of life are untrammeled by men, where man himself is a visitor and does not remain." This included [second growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_forest) public forests like the [Mark Twain National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain_National_Forest).[^cite_note-71]

### Counties

See also: [List of counties in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_Missouri)

Missouri has 114 counties and one [independent city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_city_\(United_States\)), [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis,_Missouri). The three largest counties by population are [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_County,_Missouri) (1,004,125), [Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_County,_Missouri) (717,204), and [St. Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Charles_County,_Missouri) (406,262); [Worth County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worth_County,_Missouri) has the smallest population (1,973). The largest counties by area are [Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_County,_Missouri) at 1,179 square miles (3,050 km<sup>2</sup>) and [Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_County,_Missouri) at 1,004 square miles (2,600 km<sup>2</sup>), while Worth County is the smallest at 266 square miles (690 km<sup>2</sup>). The city of St. Louis, with 5,140 people per square mile, is the most densely populated [administrative unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_unit) in the state, St. Louis County is the most densely populated county with 1,943 people per square mile, and Worth County is the most sparsely populated with approximately 7 people per square mile.

### Cities and towns

See also: [List of cities in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Missouri) and [List of towns and villages in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_towns_and_villages_in_Missouri)

Jefferson City is the capital city of Missouri, while the state's five largest cities are Kansas City, St. Louis, Springfield, [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_Missouri), and Independence.[^cite_note-largest-72]

St. Louis is the principal city of the largest metropolitan area in Missouri, composed of 17 counties and the independent city of St. Louis; eight of its counties are in Illinois. As of 2022, St. Louis was the [21st-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_metropolitan_area#Top) in the nation with 2.80 million people. If ranked using [Combined Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Statistical_Area), it is also the [21st-largest with 2.91 million people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_of_United_States_Combined_Statistical_Areas) in 2022. Some of the major cities making up the St. Louis metro area in Missouri are [O'Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O'Fallon,_Missouri), [St. Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Charles,_Missouri), [St. Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Peters,_Missouri), [Florissant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florissant,_Missouri), [Chesterfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesterfield,_Missouri), [Wentzville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wentzville,_Missouri), [Wildwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildwood,_Missouri), [University City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_City,_Missouri), [Ballwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballwin,_Missouri), and [Kirkwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkwood,_Missouri).

Kansas City is Missouri's largest city and the principal city of the fourteen-county [Kansas City Metropolitan Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Metropolitan_Area), including five counties in the state of Kansas. As of 2022, it was the 31st-largest metropolitan area in the U.S., with 2.21 million people. In the [Combined Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Statistical_Area) in 2022, it ranked 29th with 2.55 million. Some of the other major cities comprising the Kansas City metro area in Missouri include [Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence,_Missouri), [Lee's Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee's_Summit,_Missouri), [Blue Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Springs,_Missouri), [Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty,_Missouri), [Raytown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raytown,_Missouri), [Gladstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladstone,_Missouri), [Grandview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandview,_Missouri), and [Belton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belton,_Missouri).

Springfield is Missouri's third-largest city and the principal city of the [Springfield-Branson Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield-Branson,_Missouri_metropolitan_area), which has a population of 549,423 and includes seven counties in southwestern Missouri. [Branson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branson,_Missouri) is a major tourist attraction in the [Ozarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ozarks) in southwest Missouri. Some of the other major cities comprising the Springfield-Branson metro area include [Nixa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nixa,_Missouri), [Ozark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozark,_Missouri), and [Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic,_Missouri).

The [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) estimates that the population of Missouri was 6,137,428 on July 1, 2019, a 2.48% increase since the [2010 United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_Census).[^cite_note-PopEstUS-75]

Missouri had a population of 5,988,927, according to the 2010 census; an increase of 137,525 (2.3 percent) since the year 2010. From 2010 to 2018, this includes a natural increase of 137,564 people since the last census (480,763 births less 343,199 deaths) and an increase of 88,088 people due to net [migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_migration) into the state. [Immigration](15051.md) from outside the United States resulted in a net increase of 50,450 people, and migration within the country produced a net increase of 37,638 people. More than half of Missourians (3,294,936 people, or 55.0%) live within the state's two largest metropolitan areas—[St. Louis and Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_census_statistical_areas). The state's population density of 86.9 people per square mile in 2009, was also [closer to the national average (86.8 in 2009) than any other state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_population_density). The top countries of origin for Missouri's immigrants in 2018 were [Mexico](3966054.md), [China](5405.md), [India](14533.md), [Vietnam](202354.md) and [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md).[^cite_note-76]

According to [HUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development)'s 2022 [Annual Homeless Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Homeless_Assessment_Report_to_Congress), there were an estimated 5,992 [homeless](19394651.md) people in Missouri.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

African Americans are a substantial part of the population in St. Louis (56.6% of African Americans in the state lived in [St. Louis](27687.md) or [St. Louis County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_County,_Missouri) as of the 2010 census), Kansas City, Boone County and in the southeastern Bootheel and some parts of the Missouri River Valley, where plantation agriculture was once important. Missouri [Creoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_peoples) of French ancestry are concentrated in the [Mississippi River Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River_Valley) south of St. Louis (see [Missouri French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_French)). Kansas City is home to large and growing immigrant communities from Latin America esp. [Mexico](3966054.md) and [Colombia](5222.md), Africa (i.e. [Sudan](27421.md), Somalia and [Nigeria](21383.md)), and Southeast Asia including China and the [Philippines](23440.md); and Europe like the former [Yugoslavia](34244.md) (see [Bosnian American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_American)). A notable [Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee) Indian population exists in Missouri, and 30,518 identified as being Native American alone in 2020, while 152,917 did in combination with one or more other races.[^cite_note-90]

In 2004, 6.6 percent of the state's population was reported as younger than 5, 25.5 percent younger than 18, and 13.5 percent 65 or older. Females were approximately 51.4 percent of the population. 81.3 percent of Missouri residents were high school graduates (more than the national average), and 21.6 percent had a bachelor's degree or higher. 3.4 percent of Missourians were foreign-born, and 5.1 percent reported speaking a language other than English at home.

In 2010, there were 2,349,955 households in Missouri, with 2.45 people per household. The homeownership rate was 70.0 percent, and the median value of an owner-occupied housing unit was \$137,700. The median household income for 2010 was \$46,262, or \$24,724 per capita. There was 14.0 percent (1,018,118) of Missourians living below the poverty line in 2010.

The mean commute time to work was 23.8 minutes.

The population center for the United States has been in Missouri since 1980. As of 2020, it is near [Interstate 44 in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_44_in_Missouri) as it approaches [Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Missouri).

The vast majority of people in Missouri speak English. Approximately 5.1% of the population reported speaking a language other than English at home. The Spanish language is spoken in small Latino communities in the St. Louis and Kansas City Metro areas.[^cite_note-103]

Missouri is home to an endangered dialect of the French language known as [Missouri French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_French). Speakers of the dialect, who call themselves *[Créoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creole_peoples)*, are descendants of the French pioneers who settled the area then known as the [Illinois Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illinois_Country) beginning in the late 17th century. It developed in isolation from French speakers in Canada and [Louisiana](18130.md), becoming quite distinct from the varieties of [Canadian French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_French) and [Louisiana French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_French). Once widely spoken throughout the area, Missouri French is now nearly extinct, with only a few elderly speakers able to use it.[^cite_note-Ammon-104][^cite_note-105]

The largest denominations by number of adherents in 2010 were the [Southern Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention) with 749,685; the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) with 724,315; and the [United Methodist Church](32304.md) with 226,409.\[^cite_note-[www.thearda.com-107](http://www.thearda.com-107/)]

Among the other denominations there are approximately 93,000 Mormons in 253 congregations, 25,000 Jewish adherents in 21 [synagogues](48559.md), 12,000 Muslims in 39 [masjids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masjid), 7,000 Buddhists in 34 temples, 20,000 Hindus in 17 temples, 2,500 [Unitarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitarian_Universalism) in nine congregations, 2,000 of the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith) in 17 temples, five [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh) temples, a [Zoroastrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrian) temple, a [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jain) temple and an uncounted number of [neopagans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neopagans).[^cite_note-108]

Several religious organizations have headquarters in Missouri, including the [Lutheran Church–Missouri Synod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutheran_Church%E2%80%93Missouri_Synod), which has its headquarters in [Kirkwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkwood,_Missouri), as well as the [United Pentecostal Church International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Pentecostal_Church_International) in [Hazelwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazelwood,_Missouri), both outside St. Louis.

[Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence,_Missouri), near Kansas City, is the headquarters for the [Community of Christ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_of_Christ) (formerly the Reorganized Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints), the [Church of Christ (Temple Lot)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Christ_\(Temple_Lot\)) and the group [Remnant Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remnant_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter_Day_Saints). This area and other parts of Missouri are also of significant religious and historical importance to [the Church of Jesus Christ of Latter-day Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) (LDS Church), which maintains several sites and visitor centers.

[Springfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield,_Missouri) is the headquarters of the [Assemblies of God USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God_USA) and the [Baptist Bible Fellowship International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist_Bible_Fellowship_International). The [General Association of General Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Association_of_General_Baptists) has its headquarters in [Poplar Bluff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poplar_Bluff,_Missouri). The [Unity Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_Church) is headquartered in [Unity Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_Village,_Missouri). Springfield is particularly known as a Christian center in the state[^cite_note-109] and is considered by some to be a "buckle" of the [Bible Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_Belt).[^cite_note-110]

The [Hindu Temple of St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Temple_of_St._Louis) is the largest Hindu Temple in Missouri, serving more than 14,000 Hindus.

## Economy

See also: [Missouri locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_locations_by_per_capita_income)



Missouri State quarter featuring the [Lewis and Clark Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition)

[^cite_note-111]

- Total employment in 2016: 2,494,720

- Total Number of employer establishments in 2016: 160,912

  [^cite_note-112]

In 2025, the [Bureau of Economic Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Economic_Analysis) estimated Missouri's [gross state product](12594.md) was \$468.4 billion and the state's [per capita personal income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_personal_income_in_the_United_States) was \$67,587.[^cite_note-113]

Major industries include [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace), [transportation equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicles), [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), [chemicals](58721.md), printing/publishing, [electrical equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_equipment), [light manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_manufacturing), and financial services.

The agriculture products of the state are beef, [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybeans), pork, [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_products), [hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hay), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), poultry, [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum), [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), and [eggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egg_\(food\)). Missouri is ranked sixth in the nation for the production of hogs and seventh for cattle. Missouri is ranked in the top five states in the nation for production of soy beans, and it is ranked fourth in the nation for the production of rice. In 2001, there were 108,000 farms, the second-largest number in any state after Texas. Missouri actively promotes its rapidly growing [wine industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_wine). According to the Missouri Partnership, Missouri's agriculture industry contributes \$33 billion in GDP to Missouri's economy, and generates \$88 billion in sales and more than 378,000 jobs.[^cite_note-114] In 2025, [small businesses](276495.md) made up 99.4% of Missouri businesses, and employed 1.1 million people, or 44.4% of the state's workforce.[^cite_note-115]



The [Gateway Arch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_Arch) in St. Louis

Missouri has vast quantities of [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone). Other resources mined are lead, coal, and crushed [stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_\(geology\)). Missouri produces the most lead of all the states. Most of the lead mines are in the [central eastern portion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lead_Belt) of the state. Missouri also ranks first or near first in the production of [lime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lime_\(mineral\)), a key ingredient in [Portland cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portland_cement).

Missouri also has a growing science, agricultural technology, and biotechnology field. [Monsanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsanto), formerly one of the largest biotech companies in America, was based in [St. Louis](27687.md) until it was acquired by [Bayer AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_AG) in 2018. It is now part of the Crop Science Division of [Bayer Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_Corporation), Bayer's U.S. subsidiary.

Tourism, services, and wholesale/retail trade follow manufacturing in importance; tourism benefits from the many rivers, lakes, caves, and parks throughout the state. In addition to a network of state parks, Missouri is home to [Gateway Arch National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_Arch_National_Park) in St. Louis and the [Ozark National Scenic Riverways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozark_National_Scenic_Riverways). A much-visited show cave is [Meramec Caverns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meramec_Caverns), near [Stanton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanton,_Missouri).



Missouri is the only state to have two main [Federal Reserve Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank) ([Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_Kansas_City) bank pictured).

Missouri is the only state in the Union to have two [Federal Reserve Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank): one in [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_Kansas_City) (serving western Missouri, Kansas, Nebraska, Oklahoma, Colorado, northern New Mexico, and Wyoming) and one in [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_St._Louis) (serving eastern Missouri, southern Illinois, southern Indiana, western Kentucky, western Tennessee, northern Mississippi, and all of Arkansas).[^cite_note-116]

The state's unemployment rate as of May 2025 was 4.0 percent.[^cite_note-117] In 2017, Missouri became a right-to-work state,[^cite_note-118] but in August 2018, Missouri voters rejected a [right-to-work law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-to-work_law) with 67% to 33%.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]

### Taxation

Main article: [Taxation in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Missouri)

Personal [income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_tax) is taxed in ten different earning brackets, ranging from 1.5% to 6.0%. Missouri's state [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) rate for most items is 4.225%, with some additional local levies. More than 2,500 Missouri local governments rely on [property taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) levied on real property (real estate) and [personal property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_property).

Most personal property is exempt, except for motorized vehicles. Exempt real estate includes property owned by governments and property used as nonprofit cemeteries, exclusively for religious worship, for schools and colleges, and purely charitable purposes. There is no [inheritance tax](5824627.md) and limited Missouri [estate tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_tax) related to [federal estate tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estate_tax_\(United_States\)) collection.

In 2017, the Tax Foundation rated Missouri as having the fifth-best corporate tax index,[^cite_note-\:0-122] and the 15th-best overall tax climate.[^cite_note-\:0-122] Missouri's corporate income tax rate is 6.25%; however, 50% of federal income tax payments may be deducted before computing taxable income, leading to an effective rate of 5.2%.[^cite_note-123]

### Energy

In 2012, Missouri had roughly 22,000 MW of installed electricity generation capacity.[^cite_note-EIAMissouri-124] In 2011, 82% of Missouri's electricity was generated by [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil-fuel_power_station).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-125] Ten percent was generated from the state's only [nuclear power plant](207347.md),[^cite_note-autogenerated1-125] the [Callaway Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callaway_Nuclear_Generating_Station) in Callaway County, northeast of [Jefferson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_City). Five percent was generated by [natural gas](22131.md).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-125] One percent was generated by [hydroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric) sources,[^cite_note-autogenerated1-125] such as the dams for [Truman Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_Reservoir) and [Lake of the Ozarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_of_the_Ozarks). Missouri has a small but growing amount of wind and solar power—wind capacity increased from 309 MW in 2009 to 459 MW in 2011, while photovoltaics have increased from 0.2 MW to 1.3 MW over the same period.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-Sherwood,_Larry-127] As of 2016, Missouri's solar installations had reached 141 MW.[^cite_note-128]

[Oil wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_well) in Missouri produced 120,000 barrels of [crude oil](23195.md) in fiscal 2012.[^cite_note-129] There are no oil refineries in Missouri.[^cite_note-Sherwood,_Larry-127][^cite_note-130]

## Culture

### Music

Main article: [Music of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Missouri)



The historic Gem Theatre, located in Kansas City's renowned [18th and Vine Jazz District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_and_Vine_Jazz_District)

Many well-known musicians were born or have lived in Missouri. These include guitarist and rock pioneer [Chuck Berry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Berry), singer and actress [Josephine Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephine_Baker), "Queen of Rock" [Tina Turner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_Turner), pop singer-songwriter [Sheryl Crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheryl_Crow), [Michael McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_McDonald_\(singer\)) of the [Doobie Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doobie_Brothers), rap producer [Metro Boomin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Boomin), and rappers [Nelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelly), [Chingy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chingy), and [Akon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akon), all of whom are either current or former residents of St. Louis.

Country singers from Missouri include Perryville native [Chris Janson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Janson), [New Franklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Franklin,_Missouri) native [Sara Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Evans), [Cantwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Francois_County,_Missouri) native [Ferlin Husky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferlin_Husky), [West Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Plains,_Missouri) native [Porter Wagoner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter_Wagoner), [Tyler Farr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyler_Farr) of [Garden City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_City,_Missouri), and [Mora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mora,_Missouri) native [Leroy Van Dyke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leroy_Van_Dyke), along with bluegrass musician [Rhonda Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhonda_Vincent), a native of [Greentop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greentop,_Missouri). Rapper [Eminem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eminem) was born in St. Joseph and also lived in Savannah and Kansas City. Ragtime composer [Scott Joplin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Joplin) lived in St. Louis and Sedalia. Jazz saxophonist [Charlie Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Parker) lived in Kansas City. Rock and Roll singer [Steve Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Walsh_\(musician\)) of the group [Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_\(band\)) was born in St. Louis and grew up in St. Joseph. Pop singer [Chappell Roan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chappell_Roan) was born in [Willard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willard,_Missouri), and used to perform in the [Springfield metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springfield_metropolitan_area,_Missouri) from 2012 to 2015, before signing up with her first label.[^cite_note-131]

The [Kansas City Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Symphony) and the [St. Louis Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Symphony_Orchestra) are the state's major orchestras. The latter is the nation's second-oldest symphony orchestra and achieved prominence in recent years under conductor [Leonard Slatkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Slatkin). [Branson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branson,_Missouri) is well known for its music theaters, most of which bear the name of a star performer or musical group.

### Literature



[Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain)'s boyhood home in [Hannibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal,_Missouri)

Missouri is the native state of [Mark Twain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Twain). His novels *[The Adventures of Tom Sawyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Tom_Sawyer)* and *[The Adventures of Huckleberry Finn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Adventures_of_Huckleberry_Finn)* are set in his boyhood hometown of [Hannibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal,_Missouri). Authors [Kate Chopin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate_Chopin), [T. S. Eliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._S._Eliot) and [Tennessee Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennessee_Williams) were from St. Louis. Kansas City-born writer [William Least Heat-Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Least_Heat-Moon) resides in [Rocheport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocheport,_Missouri). He is best known for *[Blue Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Highways)*, a chronicle of his travels to small towns across America, which was on The New York Times Bestseller list for 42 weeks in 1982–1983. Novelist [Daniel Woodrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Woodrell), known for depicting life in the Missouri Ozarks, was born in Springfield and lives in West Plains.

The [Missouri Governor's Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Governor's_Mansion) is included in the [Missouri State Capitol Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_State_Capitol_Historic_District).

The Constitution of Missouri, the fourth constitution for the state, was adopted in 1945. It provides for three branches of government: the legislative, judicial, and executive branches. The legislative branch consists of two bodies: the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_House_of_Representatives) and the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Senate). These bodies comprise the [Missouri General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_General_Assembly).

The House of Representatives has 163 members apportioned based on the last [decennial census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census). The Senate consists of 34 members from districts of approximately equal populations. The judicial department comprises the [Supreme Court of Missouri](1771393.md), which has seven judges, the [Missouri Court of Appeals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Court_of_Appeals) (an intermediate [appellate court](643.md) divided into three districts), sitting in Kansas City, St. Louis, and Springfield, and 45 Circuit Courts which function as local trial courts. The executive branch is headed by the [Governor of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Governors_of_Missouri) and includes five other statewide elected offices. Following the departure from office of State Auditor [Nicole Galloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicole_Galloway) on January 9, 2023, there are no Democrats holding statewide elected positions in Missouri.[^cite_note-135]

[Harry S Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S_Truman) (1884–1972), the 33rd President of the United States (Democrat, 1945–1953), was born in [Lamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamar,_Missouri). He was a judge in [Jackson County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackson_County,_Missouri) and then [represented the state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_senators_from_Missouri) in the [United States Senate](24909346.md) for ten years, before being elected vice-president in [1944](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_United_States_presidential_election). He lived in Independence after retiring as president in 1953.

In a 2020 study, Missouri was ranked as 48th on the [Cost of Voting Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cost_of_Voting_Index) with only Texas and Georgia ranking higher.[^cite_note-136]

Missouri retains the [death penalty](5902.md). Authorized methods of execution include the [gas chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_chamber).[^cite_note-137] [Abortion in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_Missouri) is legal as a result of [2024 Missouri Amendment 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Missouri_Amendment_3).[^cite_note-138]

### Former status as a political bellwether

Main article: [Missouri bellwether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_bellwether)

Further information: [Political party strength in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party_strength_in_Missouri)



2024 United States presidential election results by county&#x20;

&#x20; Democratic

&#x20; Republican

Prior to 2008, Missouri had been widely regarded as a bellwether in American politics, often making it a [swing state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swing_state). The state had a longer stretch of supporting the winning presidential candidate than any other state, having voted for the winning candidate in every election from 1904 to 2004 with a single exception: [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_United_States_presidential_election) when Democratic candidate [Adlai Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adlai_Stevenson_II) of neighboring Illinois lost the election despite carrying Missouri. However, since 2000, Missouri has always voted for the Republican presidential candidate, with the last Democrat winning the state being [Bill Clinton in 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_United_States_presidential_election_in_Missouri). Missouri voted for [John McCain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCain) and [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney) over Democrat [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) of neighboring Illinois, despite Obama being elected to the Presidency in both 2008 and 2012. Missouri voted for [Mitt Romney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitt_Romney) by nearly 10% in 2012 and voted for [Donald Trump](4848272.md) by over 18% in 2016 and 2024, and 15% in 2020.

On October 24, 2012, there were 4,190,936 registered voters.[^cite_note-registered-139] At the state level, both Democratic Senator [Claire McCaskill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claire_McCaskill) and Democratic Governor [Jay Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Nixon) were re-elected.

On November 3, 2020, there were 4,318,758 registered voters, with 3,026,028 voting (70.1%).[^cite_note-140] By this time, the state had favored more Republican candidates for federal offices. The offices held by Democratic party officials a decade before were subsequently held by Republican Senator [Josh Hawley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Hawley) and Republican Governor [Mike Parson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Parson).

Missouri's accuracy rate for the last 29 presidential elections is now 89.66%. This percentage is on par with that of Ohio, which has voted for the winner of every presidential election since 1896, except in [1944](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1944_United_States_presidential_election), [1960](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_United_States_presidential_election) and [2020](48410011.md).

### Alcohol and tobacco laws

Main articles: [Alcohol laws of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_Missouri) and [List of smoking bans in the United States § Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_smoking_bans_in_the_United_States#Missouri)

Missouri has been known for its population's generally "stalwart, conservative, noncredulous" attitude toward regulatory regimes, which is one of the origins of the state's unofficial nickname, the "Show-Me State".[^cite_note-Slogan-20] As a result, and combined with the fact that Missouri is one of America's leading alcohol states, regulation of alcohol and tobacco in Missouri is among the most [laissez-faire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laissez-faire) in America. In 2013, the [Mercatus Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercatus_Center) ranked Missouri third for alcohol freedom and first for tobacco freedom.[^cite_note-gmufreedom-141] The state's alcohol laws are notably lax, with no blue laws, low taxes, and broad access to alcohol in locations like drugstores and gas stations. Additionally, Missouri's tobacco laws are equally permissive, including the lowest cigarette excise tax in the nation.[^cite_note-gmufreedom-141] Missouri law makes it "an improper employment practice" for an employer to refuse to hire, to fire, or otherwise to disadvantage any person because that person lawfully uses alcohol or tobacco products outside of work.[^cite_note-142]

With a large German immigrant population and the development of a brewing industry, Missouri always has had among the most permissive [alcohol laws in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_laws_of_the_United_States_by_state). It has never enacted statewide [prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_the_United_States). Missouri has no statewide [open container law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_open_container_laws) or prohibition on [drinking in public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_in_public), no alcohol-related [blue laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_law), no [local option](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_option), no precise locations for selling liquor by the package (allowing even [drug stores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_store) and [filling stations](61260.md) to sell any kind of liquor), and no differentiation of laws based on alcohol percentage. State law protects persons from arrest or criminal penalty for [public intoxication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_intoxication).[^cite_note-143]

Missouri law expressly prohibits any jurisdiction from going [dry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_county).[^cite_note-144] Missouri law also expressly allows parents and guardians to serve alcohol to their children.[^cite_note-145] The [Power & Light District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_&_Light_District) in Kansas City is one of the few places in the United States where a state law explicitly allows persons over 21 to possess and consume open containers of alcohol in the street (as long as the beverage is in a plastic cup).[^cite_note-146]

Missouri had the lowest cigarette excise taxes in the United States in 2016, at 17 cents per pack,[^cite_note-147] and the state electorate voted in 2002, 2006, 2012, and twice in 2016 to keep it that way.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] According to the [Centers for Disease Control and Prevention](6811.md), in 2008 Missouri had the fourth highest percentage of adult smokers among U.S. states, at 24.5%.[^cite_note-150] Although federal law prohibits the sale of tobacco to persons under 21, tobacco products can be distributed to persons under 21 by family members on private property.[^cite_note-151]

No statewide [smoking ban](386818.md) ever has been seriously entertained before the [Missouri General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_General_Assembly), and in October 2008, a statewide survey by the Missouri Department of Health and Senior Services found that only 27.5% of Missourians support a statewide ban on smoking in all bars and restaurants.[^cite_note-152] Missouri state law permits restaurants seating less than 50 people, bars, bowling alleys, and billiard parlors to decide their own smoking policies, without limitation.[^cite_note-153]

### Cannabis laws

See also: [Cannabis in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_in_Missouri)

In 2014, a Republican-led legislature and Democratic governor [Jay Nixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Nixon) enacted a series of laws to partially decriminalize possession of cannabis by making first-time possession of up to 10 grams no longer punishable with jail time and legalizing [CBD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabidiol) oil. In November 2018, [66% of voters approved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_in_Missouri) a constitutional amendment that established a right to medical marijuana and a system for licensing, regulating, and taxing medical marijuana.

The [Missouri State Board of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_State_Board_of_Education) has general authority over all public education in the state of Missouri. It is made up of eight citizens appointed by the governor and confirmed by the Missouri Senate.

### Primary and secondary schools

See also: [List of school districts in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_school_districts_in_Missouri) and [List of high schools in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_schools_in_Missouri)

Education is compulsory from ages seven to seventeen. It is required that any parent, guardian, or another person with custody of a child between the ages of seven and seventeen, the compulsory attendance age for the district, must ensure the child is enrolled in and regularly attends public, private, parochial school, home school or a combination of schools for the full term of the school year. Compulsory attendance also ends when children complete sixteen credits in high school.

Children in Missouri between the ages of five and seven are not required to be enrolled in school. However, if they are enrolled in a public school, their parent, guardian, or custodian must ensure they regularly attend.

Missouri schools are commonly but not exclusively divided into three tiers of primary and secondary education: elementary school, [middle school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school) or [junior high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_high_school) and high school. The public school system includes kindergarten to 12th grade. District territories are often complex in structure. In some cases, elementary, middle, and junior high schools of a single district feed into high schools in another district. As another example, special education and related services for students in the twenty-two school districts of St. Louis County are provided by staff employed by a special school district, a local education agency that serves students county-wide. High school athletics and competitions are governed by the [Missouri State High School Activities Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_State_High_School_Activities_Association) (MSHSAA).

[Homeschooling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeschooling) is legal in Missouri and is an option to meet the compulsory education requirement. It is neither monitored nor regulated by the state's Department of Elementary and Secondary Education.[^cite_note-154]

Another gifted school is the [Missouri Academy of Science, Mathematics and Computing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Academy_of_Science,_Mathematics_and_Computing), which is at the [Northwest Missouri State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Missouri_State_University).

### Colleges and universities

See also: [List of colleges and universities in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_Missouri) and [List of defunct colleges and universities in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_defunct_colleges_and_universities_in_Missouri)



[Jesse Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Hall) on the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri) campus



Brookings Hall at [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis)

The [University of Missouri System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri_System) is Missouri's statewide public university system. The flagship institution and largest university in the state is the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri) in [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_Missouri). The others in the system are [University of Missouri–Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri%E2%80%93Kansas_City), [University of Missouri–St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri%E2%80%93St._Louis), and [Missouri University of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_University_of_Science_and_Technology) in [Rolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolla,_Missouri).

During the late nineteenth and early twentieth century, the state established a series of [normal schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normal_school) in each region of the state, originally named after the geographic districts: Northeast Missouri State University (now [Truman State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truman_State_University)) (1867), Central Missouri State University (now the [University of Central Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Central_Missouri)) (1871), [Southeast Missouri State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Missouri_State_University) (1873), Southwest Missouri State University (now [Missouri State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_State_University)) (1905), [Northwest Missouri State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Missouri_State_University) (1905), [Missouri Western State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Western_State_University) (1915), [Maryville University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maryville_University) (1872) and [Missouri Southern State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Southern_State_University) (1937). [Lincoln University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_University_\(Missouri\)) and [Harris–Stowe State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harris%E2%80%93Stowe_State_University) were established in the mid-nineteenth century and are [historically black colleges and universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historically_black_colleges_and_universities).

Among private institutions [Washington University in St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis) and [Saint Louis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Louis_University_\(United_States\)) are two top ranked schools in the US.[^cite_note-155] There are numerous junior colleges, trade schools, church universities, and other private universities in the state. Founded in 1858, [Hannibal–LaGrange University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal%E2%80%93LaGrange_University) in Hannibal, Missouri, was one of the first colleges west of the Mississippi.[^cite_note-156] [A.T. Still University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.T._Still_University) was the first [osteopathic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteopathic) medical school in the world.

The state funds a \$3000, renewable merit-based scholarship, [Bright Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bright_Flight_\(Missouri_scholarship\)), given to the top three percent of Missouri high school graduates who attend a university in-state.

The 19th-century border wars between Missouri and Kansas have continued as a sports rivalry between the [University of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Missouri) and [University of Kansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Kansas). The rivalry was chiefly expressed through football and basketball games between the two universities, but since Missouri left the [Big 12 Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_12_Conference) in 2012, the teams no longer regularly play one another. It was the oldest college rivalry west of the [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) and the second-oldest in the nation. Each year when the universities met to play, the game was coined the "Border War". Following the game, an exchange occurred where the winner took a historic Indian War Drum, which had been passed back and forth for decades.[^cite_note-157] Though Missouri and Kansas no longer have an annual game after the University of Missouri moved to the [Southeastern Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_Conference), rivalry still exists between them.

Two of the nation's three busiest rail centers are in Missouri. [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri) is a major railroad hub for [BNSF Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNSF_Railway), [Norfolk Southern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Southern_Railway), [Kansas City Southern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Southern_Railway), and [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad), and every class 1 railroad serves Missouri. Kansas City is the second-largest freight rail center in the U.S. (but is first in the amount of tonnage handled). Like Kansas City, St. Louis is a major destination for train freight. Springfield remains an operational hub for BNSF Railway.

[Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) passenger trains serve [Kansas City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Kansas_City,_Missouri\)), [La Plata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Plata_\(Amtrak_station\)), [Jefferson City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jefferson_City_station), [St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_Multimodal_Transportation_Center), [Lee's Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee's_Summit,_Missouri), [Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_\(Amtrak_station\)), [Warrensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrensburg,_Missouri), [Hermann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann,_Missouri), [Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington,_Missouri), Kirkwood, [Sedalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedalia,_Missouri), and [Poplar Bluff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poplar_Bluff,_Missouri). A proposed [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) route in Missouri as part of the [Chicago Hub Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Hub_Network) has received \$31 million in funding.[^cite_note-160]

The only urban light rail/subway system operating in Missouri is [MetroLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetroLink_\(St._Louis\)), which connects the city of St. Louis with suburbs in Illinois and St. Louis County. It is one of the largest systems (by track mileage) in the United States. The [KC Streetcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC_Streetcar) in downtown Kansas City opened in May 2016.[^cite_note-161]

The [Gateway Multimodal Transportation Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_Multimodal_Transportation_Center) in St. Louis is the largest active multi-use transportation center in the state. It is in downtown St. Louis, next to the historic [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(St._Louis\)) complex. It serves as a hub center/station for MetroLink, the [MetroBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_MetroBus) regional bus system, [Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines), Amtrak, and taxi services.

In 2018, a [Missouri Hyperloop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Hyperloop) was proposed to connect St. Louis, Kansas City, and Columbia, reducing travel time across the entire state to around a half hour.[^cite_note-162] The project stalled in December 2023, with the shutdown of the corporate partner [Hyperloop One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperloop_One).

### Bus

Many cities have regular fixed-route systems, and many rural counties have rural public transit services. [Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines) and [Trailways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailways) provide inter-city bus service in Missouri. [Megabus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megabus_\(North_America\)) serves St. Louis, but discontinued service to Columbia and Kansas City in 2015.[^cite_note-163]

### Rivers



The [Mississippi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippi_River) at [Hannibal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannibal,_Missouri)

The Mississippi River and Missouri River are commercially navigable over their entire lengths in Missouri. The Missouri was channelized through dredging and jetties, and the Mississippi was given a series of [locks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock_\(water_transport\)) and dams to avoid rocks and deepen the river. St. Louis is a major destination for barge traffic on the Mississippi.

### Roads

Main articles: [Missouri State Highway System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_State_Highway_System), [List of Interstate Highways in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Interstate_Highways_in_Missouri), [List of U.S. Routes in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._Routes_in_Missouri), [List of state highways in Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_highways_in_Missouri), and [Missouri supplemental route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_supplemental_route)

Following the passage of Amendment 3 in late 2004, the [Missouri Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_Department_of_Transportation) (MoDOT) began its Smoother, Safer, Sooner road-building program with a goal of bringing 2,200 miles (3,500 km) of highways up to good condition by December 2007. From 2006 to 2011 traffic deaths have decreased annually from 1,257 in 2005, to 1,096 in 2006, to 992 in 2007, to 960 in 2008, to 878 in 2009, to 821 in 2010, to 786 in 2011.[^cite_note-164]
