An **AI boom**[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] is a period of rapid growth in the field of [artificial intelligence](1164.md). The most recent boom happened in the 2020s[^cite_note-3] before seeing increased acceleration and media coverage. [Generative AI](73291755.md) technologies include [large language models](73248112.md) (LLM), which generate text and [code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI-assisted_software_development), [image models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text-to-image_model), and [video models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text-to-video_model), and [world models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_model_\(artificial_intelligence\)). This period is sometimes referred to as an **AI spring**, a term used to contrast it with previous [AI winters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_winter).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Key advances preceding the boom include the [transformer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformer_\(deep_learning\)) (2017), [generative pre-trained transformer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_pre-trained_transformer) (2018) and [reinforcement learning from human feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinforcement_learning_from_human_feedback). During the boom, LLMs have evolved via the [reasoning model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reasoning_model), [mixture of experts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixture_of_experts) technique, and [multimodal learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimodal_learning).&#x20;

[Controversies of the AI boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_controversies) include [its environmental impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_AI), dominance by US [Big Tech](57174306.md) companies, [effect on copyright](73455792.md), proliferation of [deepfakes](56641018.md), threats to [cybersecurity](7398.md) and [biosecurity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosecurity), [its use in warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_applications_of_artificial_intelligence), [a potential economic bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_bubble), and [existential risks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_catastrophic_risk) such as [AI takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_takeover) resulting from [artificial general intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_general_intelligence) and [superintelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_superintelligence). Since 2018, observers including [Ian Bremmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Bremmer), [Nicholas Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Thompson_\(editor\)), and [Kai-Fu Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai-Fu_Lee) have argued an [AI arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_arms_race) or [AI Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_Intelligence_Cold_War) exists between the US and China.[^cite_note-14] The boom has impacted the [semiconductor industry](612813.md), causing [a global memory supply shortage](81854017.md). As of 2025, [ChatGPT](72417803.md) has emerged as the [4th-most-visited website](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most-visited_websites) globally, surpassed only by Google, YouTube, and Facebook.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

## History

For broader coverage of this topic, see [History of artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_artificial_intelligence).



The number of Google searches for the term "AI" accelerated in 2022.

In 1950, [Alan Turing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Turing) proposed the idea of "Thinking Machines". These were computers that would be able to reason at the same level as humans.[^cite_note-Brookings-17] He began his well-known "[Turing test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turing_test)", where an interrogator is provided with two materials and they must determine which one was done by artificial intelligence and which one was done by a human being.[^cite_note-Brookings-17][^cite_note-18]



Computer scientist John McCarthy was one of the founders of the discipline of artificial intelligence

In 1956, [John McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_McCarthy_\(computer_scientist\)) used the term "[artificial intelligence](1164.md)" for the first time.[^cite_note-Brookings-17][^cite_note-19] That year, McCarthy, [Nathaniel Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Rochester_\(computer_scientist\)), [Marvin Minsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvin_Minsky), and [Claude Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Shannon) organized the [Dartmouth conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmouth_workshop), which formalized artificial intelligence as an academic field.[^cite_note-www-formal.stanford.edu-20][^cite_note-21] In 1958, McCarthy created the programming language [LISP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_\(programming_language\)),[^cite_note-22] LISP stands for "[List Processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisp_\(programming_language\))" and was the main [programming language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programming_language) for artificial intelligence.[^cite_note-23] which remained the most common programming language for artificial intelligence in the United States for decades.[^cite_note-projects.csail.mit.edu-24] In 1962, McCarthy founded [Stanford Artificial Intelligence Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University_centers_and_institutes) (SAIL).[^cite_note-engineering.stanford.edu-2023-25] McCarthy was also a [cofounder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizational_founder) of MIT's first [Artificial Intelligence Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Computer_Science_and_Artificial_Intelligence_Laboratory), now known as [MIT Computer Science and Artificial Intelligence Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MIT_Computer_Science_and_Artificial_Intelligence_Laboratory).[^cite_note-26]

In 1966, [Joseph Weizenbaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Weizenbaum) created [ELIZA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELIZA), the first [chatbot](148349.md), as an experimental emotional tool.\[^cite_note-[www.csail.mit.edu-27](http://www.csail.mit.edu-27/)]

The [text-to-image models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text-to-image_model) DALL-E 2 and Midjourney were released in 2022.[^cite_note-28]

[ChatGPT](72417803.md), an AI chatbot created by [OpenAI](48795986.md), was launched at the end of 2022. It grew to over 100 million users in 2 months,[^cite_note-29] becoming the fastest-growing software application.[^cite_note-30] [Large language models](73248112.md) are designed to respond to human language by accessing a large amount of training data.[^cite_note-IBM-2021-31]

## Advances

See also: [Progress in artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progress_in_artificial_intelligence)

### Biomedical

In 2020, DeepMind's [AlphaFold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AlphaFold) program, which is designed to predict [protein folding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_folding), scored more than 90 in [CASP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASP)'s [Global distance test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_distance_test) (GDT).[^cite_note-science20201130-32][^cite_note-Callaway-2020-33] The [structural biologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_biology) and [Nobel Prize](25416893.md) winner [Venki Ramakrishnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venki_Ramakrishnan) called the result "a stunning advance on the protein folding problem".[^cite_note-science20201130-32] The ability to predict [protein structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_structure) accurately based on the constituent [amino acid sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amino_acid_sequence) may accelerate [drug discovery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_discovery) and enable a better understanding of diseases.[^cite_note-Callaway-2020-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-mittr20201130-35]

### Images and videos



An image generated by Stable Diffusion based on the text prompt "a photograph of an astronaut riding a horse"

As time passed, the power of [generative AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generative_artificial_intelligence) grew stronger. In 2015, initial popularity began to grow with the release of [Google](1092923.md)'s [DeepDream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeepDream). DeepDream is a generative AI that takes inputs from a previous image and morphs them to produce [hallucinogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucinogen) images.[^cite_note-36]

In January 2021, [OpenAI](48795986.md) released [DALL-E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DALL-E), allowing for [image generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text-to-image_model) through text prompts.[^cite_note-37] This allows users to generate any image with a simple prompt. Soon after, other powerful models followed DALL-E, such as [Google](1092923.md)'s [Gemini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemini_\(language_model\)).[^cite_note-38]

The popularity of [text-to-video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text-to-video_model) generative AI tools grew exponentially. With the release of models such as [OpenAI](48795986.md)'s [Sora](76106601.md) in 2024, the use of [text-to-video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Text-to-video) tools became normalized, as people used them for [advertisements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising), which saves on production costs and increases production speed.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

Generative AI is growing at a rapid rate, outpacing modern-day [detection tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_content_detection).[^cite_note-41] With the common public having access to these tools, it raises concerns about the ethical use of generative AI. There have been multiple occasions where misinformation has been spread over the internet about politics due to a generated or [deep-faked](56641018.md) video, posing as a [security threat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Threat_\(computer_security\)).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

### Language

As of August 2026, large AI models have been released by OpenAI, Anthropic, [Google DeepMind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_DeepMind), [Meta Superintelligence Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_Superintelligence_Labs), [SpaceXAI](74330685.md), and [Nvidia](39120.md) in the United States, and [in China](57024219.md) by [Alibaba Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alibaba_Cloud), [DeepSeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeepSeek), [Moonshot AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moonshot_AI), [Z.ai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z.ai), and [ByteDance](54450737.md). [Mistral AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_AI), based in France, is the largest European AI company.[^cite_note-44] [Open-source AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open-source_artificial_intelligence) includes [small language models](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_language_model), which are more suited to being run on [personal computers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_computer).[^cite_note-u191-45] These include many models under Alibaba's [Qwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qwen), Google's [Gemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemma_\(language_model\)), Nvidia's [Nemotron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemotron), and Meta's [Muse Glimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meta_Superintelligence_Labs#Models).

[GPT-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-3) is a [large language model](73248112.md) that was released in 2020 by [OpenAI](48795986.md) and is capable of generating human-like text.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] A new version called [GPT-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPT-4) was released on 14 March 2023, and was used in the [Microsoft Bing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft_Bing) search engine.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

### Music and voice

In 2016, [Google](1092923.md)'s [DeepMind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_DeepMind) produced [WaveNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WaveNet). WaveNet allowed the generation of raw audio of speech and piano.[^cite_note-research.google-wavenet-53] WaveNet is able to generate different voices by identifying the speakers.[^cite_note-research.google-wavenet-53] This acted as a fundamental building block for future models, allowing audio to be formed from scratch. This wouldn't only help with the production of music, but voice generation as well.[^cite_note-54]

[OpenAI](48795986.md) released Jukebox, the first large-scale model to generate songs, in 2020. Jukebox allowed for [raw audio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raw_audio_format) in different genres and styles.[^cite_note-55]

In 2024, AI models capable of producing high-fidelity music became available to the public.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] In June 2024, AI-generated music services [Udio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udio) and [Suno AI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suno_AI) were sued by a group of major record labels over copyright infringement concerns.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

In March 2020, [15.ai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15.ai) was founded. 15.ai allowed for [audio deepfakes](65679106.md).[^cite_note-60] Artificially generated vocals can be generated with tools such as [ElevenLabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ElevenLabs), which allows for the creation of vocals from any audio.[^cite_note-61] This allows for any celebrity or politician who has voice clips on the internet to be subject to audio deepfakes for both speech and singing. The voices of politicians, such as [Joe Biden](145422.md), have been used for fake [robocalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robocall) to voters to attempt to manipulate elections.[^cite_note-62]

## Impact

### Energy

See also: [AI datacenter § Energy sourcing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_datacenter#Energy_sourcing)

Electricity consumed by hardware used for AI has increased demands on power grids, which has led to prolonged use of fossil fuel power plants which would otherwise have been deactivated, since renewable energy sources often lack the continuous baseload power required for 24/7 AI model training and inference.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

Microsoft, Google, and [Amazon](90451.md) have all invested in existing or proposed nuclear power plants to meet these demands.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] In September 2024, Microsoft signed a deal with [Constellation Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation_Energy) to purchase power from a reactor at [Three Mile Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Mile_Island) which had been shut down in 2019. The reactor is set to reopen in 2028 to provide power to Microsoft's data centers. The reactor is next to the unit which caused the [worst nuclear power accident in US history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Mile_Island_accident) in 1979.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

### Cultural

According to a report from [Pew Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research), opinions on AI are divided, with most Americans expressing concerns over a lack of control over AI and potential negative effect on [human creativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creativity).[^cite_note-Fuentes-2025-71] Nikolova & Angrisani (2025) found that people are specifically distrustful of the use AI in personal relationships, while being more accepting of its use in medicine, such as for creating new antibiotics.[^cite_note-72]

### Business and economy

See also: [2025-present global memory supply shortage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025-present_global_memory_supply_shortage)



In 2024, AI patents in China and the U.S. numbered more than three-fourths of AI patents worldwide.

[^cite_note-RandDworld_20241103-73]

&#x20;Though China had more AI patents, the U.S. had 35% more patents per AI patent-applicant company than China.

[^cite_note-RandDworld_20241103-73]

Some [economists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economist) have been optimistic about the potential of the current wave of AI to boost productivity and economic growth. Notably, [Stanford University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanford_University) economist [Erik Brynjolfsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Brynjolfsson), in a series of articles has argued for an "AI-powered Productivity Boom"[^cite_note-74] and a "Coming Productivity Boom".[^cite_note-75] At the same time, others like [Northwestern University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_University) economist [Robert Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_J._Gordon) remain more pessimistic.[^cite_note-76] Brynjolfsson and Gordon have made a formal bet, registered at [long bets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_bets), about the rate of productivity growth in the 2020s, to be resolved at the end of the decade.[^cite_note-77]

[Big Tech](57174306.md) companies view the AI boom as both opportunity and threat; Alphabet's Google, for example, realized that ChatGPT could be an [innovator's dilemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovator's_dilemma)-like replacement for [Google Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Search). The company merged DeepMind and [Google Brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Brain), a rival internal unit, to accelerate its AI research.[^cite_note-kantrowitz20240329-78]

The [market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_capitalization) of [Nvidia](39120.md), whose [GPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPUs) are in high demand to train and use generative AI models, rose to over US\$3.3 trillion, making it the world's [largest company by market capitalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_public_corporations_by_market_capitalization) as of 19 June 2024[^cite_note-79] and became the first company to reach US\$4 trillion on 9 July 2025[^cite_note-80] and subsequently US\$5 [trillion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trillion) on 29 October later that same year.[^cite_note-81]

In 2023, San Francisco's population increased for the first time in years, with the boom cited as a contributing factor.[^cite_note-pandemicsf-82]

Machine learning resources, hardware or software can be bought and licensed off-the-shelf or as [cloud](19541494.md) platform services.[^cite_note-BrynMcAf-83] This enables wide and publicly available uses, spreading AI skills.[^cite_note-BrynMcAf-83] Over half of businesses consider AI to be a top organizational priority and to be the most crucial technological advancement in many decades.[^cite_note-84]

Across industries, generative AI tools are becoming widely available through the AI boom and are increasingly used in businesses across regions.[^cite_note-McKi23-85] A main area of use is [data analytics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_analytics). Seen as an incremental change, machine learning improves industry performance.[^cite_note-86] Businesses report AI to be most useful in increased process efficiency, improved decision-making and strengthening of existing services and products.[^cite_note-87] Through adoption, AI has already positively influenced revenue generation in multiple business functions. Businesses have experienced revenue increases of up to 16%, mainly in manufacturing, [risk management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_management) and [research and development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_and_development).[^cite_note-McKi23-85]

AI and generative AI investments have been increasing with the boom, increasing from \$18 billion in 2014 to \$119 billion in 2021. Most notably, the share of generative AI investments was around 30% in 2023.[^cite_note-88] Further, generative AI businesses have seen considerable [venture capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital) investments even though regulatory and economic outlooks remain in question.[^cite_note-89]

Tech giants capture the bulk of the monetary gains from AI and act as major suppliers to or customers of private users and other businesses.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

With the introduction of AI, there has been an exponential rise in production for businesses. It's expected that workers could use resources provided by artificial intelligence to boost their [productivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Productivity).[^cite_note-92] As many small businesses don't use AI, it's believed that if it's adopted by more businesses, the whole work structure could be changed, as many tasks will be [automated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automation) by AI.[^cite_note-93]

The [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau)'s Business Trends and Outlook Survey measured AI use at 3–9% of businesses in March 2025, using language that asked whether firms used AI "to produce goods and services."[^cite_note-94] After revising the question to cover "any business function," the Bureau's measured adoption rate increased to 18% in March 2026.[^cite_note-95]

According to Bell & [Korinek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Korinek) (2023), the economic effect of AI could worsen [economic inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality), which could potentially threaten democracy in ways which are separate from threats caused by AI's use in misinformation and propaganda.[^cite_note-96]

## Concerns

See also: [Artificial intelligence controversies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_controversies) and [AI build-out financing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_build-out_financing)

Inaccuracy, [cybersecurity](7398.md) and intellectual property infringement are considered to be the main risks associated with the boom, although not many actively attempt to mitigate the risk.[^cite_note-McKi23-85] Large language models have been criticized for reproducing [biases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algorithmic_bias) inherited from their training data, including discriminatory biases related to ethnicity or gender.[^cite_note-97] As a [dual-use technology](1003982.md), AI carries risks of misuse by malicious actors.[^cite_note-98] As AI becomes more sophisticated, it may eventually become cheaper and more efficient than human workers, which could cause [technological unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_unemployment) and a transition period of economic turmoil.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-AItransform-100] Reaction to the AI boom has been mixed, with some hailing the new possibilities that AI creates, its sophistication and potential for benefiting humanity;[^cite_note-101] while others denounced it for [threatening job security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_unemployment)[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] and for giving '[uncanny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncanny_valley)' or flawed responses.[^cite_note-104] Some observers argue an [AI arms race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_intelligence_arms_race) or [AI Cold War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_Intelligence_Cold_War) exists between the US and China.[^cite_note-105] The term "AI Cold War" was coined in 2018 by [Ian Bremmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Bremmer) and [Nicholas Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Thompson_\(editor\)) in a [*Wired*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_\(magazine\)) magazine article.[^cite_note-x592-12] [Kai-Fu Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai-Fu_Lee), the president of [Google China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_China) from 2005 to 2009, made a similar claim in his 2018 book *[AI Superpowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_Superpowers)*, further arguing "If data is the new oil, then China is the new [Saudi Arabia](349303.md)."[^cite_note-\:5-13]

### Dominance by tech giants

Commercial AI is dominated by American [Big Tech](57174306.md) companies such as [Alphabet Inc.](47489893.md), [Amazon](90451.md), [Apple Inc.](856.md), [Meta Platforms](62420226.md), and [Microsoft](19001.md), whose investments in this area have surpassed those from U.S.-based [venture capitalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital).[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] These companies own the majority of [cloud infrastructure](19541494.md), AI chips, and [computing](5213.md) power from [data centers](579730.md).[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

### Intellectual property

Main article: [Artificial intelligence and copyright](73455792.md)

Tech companies such as Meta, OpenAI and [Nvidia](39120.md) have been sued by artists, writers, journalists, and software developers for using their work to train AI models.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] Early generative AI chatbots, such as the GPT-1, used the [BookCorpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BookCorpus), and books are still the best source of training data for producing high-quality language models. ChatGPT aroused suspicion that its sources included libraries of pirated content after the chatbot produced detailed summaries of every part of [Sarah Silverman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Silverman)'s *[The Bedwetter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bedwetter)* and verbatim excerpts of [paywalled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paywall) content from *[The New York Times](30680.md)*.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] In protest of the [UK government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_government) holding consultations on how copyrighted music can legally be used to train AI models,[^cite_note-115] more than a thousand British musicians released an album with no sounds, entitled *[Is This What We Want?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Is_This_What_We_Want%3F)*[^cite_note-116]

### Likeness and impersonation

Main article: [Deepfake](56641018.md)

A [Voice of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_America) video covering potential dangers of AI-generated impersonation, and laws passed in California to combat it

The ability to generate convincing, personalized messages as well as realistic images may facilitate large-scale [misinformation](2203174.md), manipulation, and propaganda.[^cite_note-117]

On 19 April 2024, as part of [an ongoing feud with fellow rapper Kendrick Lamar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake%E2%80%93Kendrick_Lamar_feud), the artist [Drake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drake_\(musician\)) released the [diss track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diss_track) "[Taylor Made Freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Made_Freestyle)", which featured AI-generated vocals imitating the voices of [Tupac Shakur](31341.md) and [Snoop Dogg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snoop_Dogg).[^cite_note-118] Shakur's estate threatened to sue over the use of Shakur's likeness,[^cite_note-119] saying that it constituted a violation of Shakur's [personality rights](225178.md).

On 20 May 2024, following the release of a demo of updates to OpenAI's ChatGPT Voice Mode feature a week earlier,[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121] actor [Scarlett Johansson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlett_Johansson) issued a statement[^cite_note-122] in relation to the "Sky" voice shown in the demo, accusing OpenAI of producing it to be very similar to her own, and her portrayal of the artificial intelligence voice assistant Samantha in the film *[Her](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_\(2013_film\))* (2013), despite Johansson refusing an earlier offer from the company to provide her voice for the system. The agent of the unnamed voice actress who voiced Sky stated that she had recorded her lines in her natural speaking voice and that OpenAI had not mentioned the movie *Her* nor Johansson.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

Several incidents involving the sharing of non-consensual [deepfake pornography](62590682.md) have occurred. In late January 2024, [deepfake images of American musician Taylor Swift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Swift_deepfake_pornography_controversy) proliferated. Several experts have warned that deepfake pornography is more quickly created and disseminated, due to the relative ease of using the technology.[^cite_note-125] Canada introduced federal legislation targeting sharing of non-consensual sexually explicit AI-generated photos; most provinces already had such laws.[^cite_note-126] In the United States, the [DEFIANCE Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DEFIANCE_Act) was introduced in March 2024.[^cite_note-127]

### Environment

Main article: [Environmental impact of artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_artificial_intelligence)

A large amount of electricity is [needed to power](67895571.md) generative AI products,[^cite_note-128] making it more difficult for companies to achieve [net zero emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_zero_emissions). From 2019 to 2024, Google's [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md) increased by nearly 50%, partly as a result of increased energy consumption by AI data centres.[^cite_note-129]

### Biosecurity and cybersecurity

AI is expected by researchers of the [Center for AI Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_AI_Safety) to improve the "accessibility, success rate, scale, speed, stealth and potency of [cyberattacks](37925700.md)", potentially causing "significant geopolitical turbulence" if it reinforces attack more than defense.[^cite_note-Hendrycks-2023-130][^cite_note-131] Concerns have been raised about the potential capability of future AI systems to engineer particularly lethal and contagious [pathogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathogen).[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133]

The AI boom is said to have started an arms race in which large companies are competing against each other to have the most powerful AI model on the market, with speed and profit prioritized over safety and user protection.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

### Human extinction

Further information: [AI takeover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_takeover)

Industry leaders and others have signed the [Statement on AI Risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_on_AI_Risk), arguing that humanity might irreversibly [lose control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_takeover) over a sufficiently advanced [artificial general intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_general_intelligence) (AGI).[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-Vincent-2023-138]

### Digital sentience

Coverage of advances in machine learning and artificial intelligence has coincided with discussions of [digital sentience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentience#Digital_sentience) and morality,[^cite_note-139] such as whether AI programs should be granted rights.[^cite_note-140]

### Financial concerns and potential bubble

Main article: [AI bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_bubble)

Much of the AI boom has been funded by loans and venture capital, but many commercial AI services remain of questionable practical utility or quality for business.[^cite_note-Karma-2025-141] Despite more than \$60 [billion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billion) in corporate investment in AI in 2025,[^cite_note-142] 95% of business AI projects are unprofitable, according to research from MIT.[^cite_note-Quartz-2025-143] Producers of generative AI, such as OpenAI, also themselves currently have costs greatly exceeding their revenue.[^cite_note-144] As other major tech companies such as [Nvidia](39120.md) are both heavily invested into AI and dependent on the AI ecosystem and its hardware demands for their own ongoing growth,[^cite_note-Karma-2025-141][^cite_note-Quartz-2025-143] this has raised speculation of a wider [economic bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_bubble) in the tech industry, particularly if future demand falls short of the current levels of AI investment.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]
