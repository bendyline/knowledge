**South Carolina** ([/ˌkærəˈlaɪnə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/5a/En-us-South_Carolina.ogg/En-us-South_Carolina.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/5a/En-us-South_Carolina.ogg/En-us-South_Carolina.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*KARR-ə-LY-nə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [state](18618239.md) in the [Southeastern](816925.md), [South Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Atlantic_states) and [Deep South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_South) regions of the United States. It borders [North Carolina](21650.md) to the north and northeast, the Atlantic Ocean to the southeast, and [Georgia](48830.md) to the west and south across the [Savannah River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah_River). Along with North Carolina, it makes up the [Carolinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolinas) region of the [East Coast](89126.md). South Carolina is the [11th-smallest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_area) and [23rd-most populous U.S. state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_and_territories_by_population), with an estimated population of 5.5 million as of 2025, South Carolina is composed of [46 counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_counties_in_South_Carolina). Its capital is [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_South_Carolina),[^cite_note-6] while its [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_South_Carolina) is [Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_South_Carolina).[^cite_note-7] Other urban areas include [the Upstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstate_South_Carolina), the state's largest metropolitan area which includes [Greenville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenville,_South_Carolina) and [Spartanburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartanburg,_South_Carolina), as well as [Myrtle Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrtle_Beach,_South_Carolina).[^cite_note-PopEstCBSA-8]

South Carolina was named in honor of King [Charles I of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_England), who first formed the English colony, with *Carolus* being Latin for "Charles".[^cite_note-9] In 1712 the [Province of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_South_Carolina) was formed. One of the original [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies), South Carolina became a [royal colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_colony) in 1719. During the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), South Carolina was the site of major activity among the American colonies, with more than 200 battles and skirmishes fought within the state.[^cite_note-10] South Carolina became the eighth state to ratify the [U.S. Constitution](31644.md) on May 23, 1788.

As a [slave state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slave_states_and_free_states), South Carolina was the first state to vote in favor of secession from the United States in 1860; after being part of [the Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America) during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), the state was readmitted to the Union in 1868. During the early to mid-20th century, the state started to see economic progress as many [textile mills](2162735.md) and [factories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_town) were built across the state. The [civil rights movement](49001.md) of the mid-20th century helped end segregation and legal discrimination policies within the state. [Economic diversification](63252200.md) in South Carolina continued to pick up speed during and in the ensuing decades after [World War II](32927.md).

In the early 21st century, [South Carolina's economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_South_Carolina) is based on industries such as [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace), [agribusiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agribusiness), [automotive manufacturing](163778.md), and tourism.[^cite_note-11] In 2019, its GDP was \$213.45 billion. Within South Carolina from east to west are three main geographic regions, the [Atlantic coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_coastal_plain), the [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(plateau\)), and the [Blue Ridge Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Mountains) in the northwestern corner of [Upstate South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstate_South_Carolina). South Carolina has primarily a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate), with hot, humid summers and mild winters. Areas in the Upstate have a [subtropical highland climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate). Along South Carolina's eastern coastal plain are many [salt marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_marsh) and [estuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary). South Carolina's southeastern [Lowcountry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_Lowcountry) contains portions of the [Sea Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Islands), a chain of [barrier islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_island) along the Atlantic Ocean.

## History

Main article: [History of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_South_Carolina)

### Precolonial period



Top left, the shores of Florida and the future Carolina explored in 1500 and showed in 1502 on the [Cantino planisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantino_planisphere)

There is evidence of human activities in the area dating to about 50,000 years ago.[^cite_note-12] At the time Europeans arrived, marking the end [Pre-Columbian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era) around 1500, there were many separate [Native American polities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_culture) including the powerful [Cofitachequi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofitachequi) populated by a variety of nations including the largest the [Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee) and the [Catawba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catawba_people), with a total population being up to 20,000 around 1600.[^cite_note-leifermann-13]

Up the rivers of the eastern coastal plain lived about a dozen tribes of [Siouan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siouan_languages) background. Along the [Savannah River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savannah_River) were the [Apalachee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apalachee), [Yuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuchi), and the [Yamasee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamasee). Further west were the Cherokee, and along the [Catawba River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catawba_River), the Catawba. These tribes were village-dwellers, relying on agriculture as their primary food source.[^cite_note-leifermann-13] The Cherokee lived in [wattle and daub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wattle_and_daub) houses made with wood and clay, roofed with wood or [thatched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thatching) grass.[^cite_note-14]

About a dozen or more separate small tribes summered on the coast harvesting oysters and fish, and cultivating corn, peas and beans. Travelling inland as much as 50 miles (80 km) mostly by canoe, they wintered on the coastal plain, hunting deer and gathering nuts and fruit. The names of these tribes survive in place names like [Edisto Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edisto_Island,_South_Carolina), [Kiawah Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiawah_Island,_South_Carolina), and the [Ashepoo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashepoo_River).[^cite_note-leifermann-13]

### Exploration

Main articles: [Spanish Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Florida) and [French Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Florida)



Map of [French Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Florida), which included modern-day South Carolina

The Spanish were the first Europeans in the area. From June 24 to July 14, 1521, they explored the land around [Winyah Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winyah_Bay). On October 8, 1526, they founded [San Miguel de Gualdape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_de_Gualdape), near present-day [Georgetown, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown,_South_Carolina). It was the first European settlement in what is now the [contiguous United States](83759.md). Established with five hundred settlers, it was abandoned eight months later by one hundred and fifty survivors. In 1540, [Hernando de Soto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hernando_de_Soto) explored the region and the main town of [Cofitachequi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cofitachequi), where he captured the queen of the [Maskoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscogee) (Muscogee) and the [Chelaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee) (Cherokee) who had welcomed him.

In 1562 French [Huguenots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenot) established a settlement at what is now the [Charlesfort-Santa Elena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlesfort-Santa_Elena_Site) archaeological site on [Parris Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parris_Island,_South_Carolina). Many of these settlers preferred a natural life far from civilization and the atrocities of the [Wars of Religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Wars_of_Religion). The garrison lacked supplies, however, and the soldiers (as in the [France Antarctique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_Antarctique)) soon ran away. The French returned two years later but settled in present-day Florida rather than South Carolina.[^cite_note-leifermann-13]

### Colonization

Main articles: [Carolana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolana), [Province of Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Carolina), and [Province of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_South_Carolina)

See also: [Mississippian shatter zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mississippian_shatter_zone)



The Carolina Colony grants of 1663 and 1665

In 1629, King [Charles I of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_I_of_England) established the province of [Carolana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolana), an area covering what is now South and [North Carolina](21650.md), [Georgia](48830.md) and [Tennessee](30395.md). Carolana was granted to [Sir Robert Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Robert_Heath), who intended to allow French [Huguenots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huguenots) to settle there; however, King Charles refused to grant permission to settle to anyone who was not a member of the [Anglican Church](5955.md), leading to the failure of the colony. In 1663, King [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England) created the [Province of Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Carolina) by granting the same land to eight [Lords Proprietors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lords_Proprietors) in return for their financial and political assistance in [restoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Restoration_\(1660\)) him to the throne in 1660.[^cite_note-15] [Anthony Ashley Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Ashley_Cooper,_1st_Earl_of_Shaftesbury), one of the Lord Proprietors, planned the [Grand Model for the Province of Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Model_for_the_Province_of_Carolina) and wrote the [Fundamental Constitutions of Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundamental_Constitutions_of_Carolina), which laid the basis for the future colony.[^cite_note-16] His [utopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopia) was inspired by [John Locke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Locke), an English philosopher and physician, widely regarded as one of the most influential of Enlightenment thinkers and commonly known as the "Father of Liberalism".

The Carolina slave trade, which included both trading and direct raids by colonists,[^cite_note-Ethridge2010-17]<sup>: 109 </sup> was the largest among the British colonies in North America.[^cite_note-Ethridge2010-17]<sup>: 65 </sup> Between 1670 and 1715, between 24,000 and 51,000 captive Native Americans were exported from South Carolina – more than the number of Africans imported to the colonies of the future United States during the same period.[^cite_note-gallay-18][^cite_note-Ethridge2010-17]<sup>: 237 </sup> Additional enslaved Native Americans were exported from South Carolina to other U.S. colonies.[^cite_note-gallay-18] The historian [Alan Gallay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Gallay) says, "the trade in Indian slaves was at the center of the English empire's development in the American South. The trade in Indian slaves was the most important factor affecting the South in the period 1670 to 1715".[^cite_note-gallay-18]

In the 1670s, English planters from [Barbados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_slave_trade_between_Barbados_and_South_Carolina) established themselves near present-day Charleston. Settlers from all over Europe built rice plantations in the [South Carolina Lowcountry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_Lowcountry), east of the [Atlantic Seaboard fall line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Seaboard_fall_line). Plantation labor was done by enslaved Africans who formed the majority of the population by 1720.[^cite_note-sc-lib-19] Another cash crop was the [indigo plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigofera), a plant source of blue dye, developed by [Eliza Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliza_Lucas).

Meanwhile, [Upstate South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstate_South_Carolina), west of the Fall Line, was settled by small farmers and traders, who due to resource competition fought a number of wars with confederated Native American tribes westward. Colonists overthrew the proprietors' rule, seeking more direct representation. In 1712, the former [Province of Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Carolina) split into North and South Carolina. In 1719, South Carolina was officially made a [royal colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_colony).

South Carolina prospered from the fertility of the lowcountry and the harbors, such as at [Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_South_Carolina). It allowed religious toleration, encouraging settlement, and trade in deerskin, lumber, and beef thrived. Rice cultivation was developed on a large scale on the back of slave labor.

By the second half of the 1700s, South Carolina was one of the richest of the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies).[^cite_note-sc-lib-19]

### The American Revolution

Main articles: [South Carolina in the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_in_the_American_Revolution) and [Southern theater of the American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_theater_of_the_American_Revolutionary_War)

This Bill intitles the Bearer to Twenty Dollars or Thirty two Pounds ten shillings Current Money of this State pursuant to an Ordinance of the General Assembly passed the 14th Day of Feb. 1777." ; Denominations stated as: "TWENTY DOLLARS" and "L32.10". ; Within emblem: "UBI LIBERTAS IBI PATRIA" ; Verso: "XX Dollars. DEATH TO COUNTERFEIT. L. 32\:10\:0".

A twenty-dollar banknote issued by South Carolina in 1777

On March 26, 1776, the colony adopted the [Constitution of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_South_Carolina),[^cite_note-20] electing [John Rutledge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Rutledge) as the state's first president. In February 1778, South Carolina became the first state to ratify the [Articles of Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation),[^cite_note-21] the initial governing document of the United States, and in May 1788, South Carolina ratified the [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution), becoming the eighth state to enter the union.

This flag flew during the Battle of Sullivan's Island (June 28, 1776), where patriot forces defended a fort made of palmetto logs against a British naval attack. The spongy palmetto wood absorbed cannon fire, helping the Americans repel the British and save Charleston for years. Sergeant William Jasper's heroic act of retrieving the shot-down flag became legendary.[^cite_note-22]

During the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War) (1775–1783), about a third of combat action took place in South Carolina,[^cite_note-gordon-23] more than any other state.[^cite_note-sc-lib-19] Inhabitants of the state endured being invaded by British forces and an ongoing civil war between loyalists and partisans that devastated the backcountry.[^cite_note-gordon-23] It is estimated 25,000 slaves (30% of those in South Carolina) fled, migrated or died during the war.[^cite_note-24]

### Antebellum

Main article: [Antebellum South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antebellum_South_Carolina)



[Millford Plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millford_Plantation) built 1839–41, is an example of [Greek Revival architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Revival_architecture)

America's first census in 1790 put the state's population at nearly 250,000. By the 1800 census, the population had increased 38 per cent to nearly 340,000 of which 146,000 were slaves. At that time South Carolina had the largest population of Jews in the sixteen states of the United States, mostly based in Savannah and Charleston,[^cite_note-har-25] the latter being the country's fifth largest city.[^cite_note-26]

In the Antebellum period (before the Civil War) the state's economy and population grew. Cotton became an important crop after the invention of the [cotton gin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton_gin). While nominally democratic, from 1790 until 1865, wealthy male landowners were in control of South Carolina. For example, a man was not eligible to sit in the State House of Representatives unless he possessed an estate of 500 acres of land and 10 Negroes, or at least 150 pounds sterling.[^cite_note-27]

[Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_South_Carolina), the new state capital was founded in the center of the state, and the State Legislature first met there in 1790. The town grew after it was connected to Charleston by the [Santee Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santee_Canal) in 1800, one of the first canals in the United States.

As dissatisfaction of the planters ruling class with the federal government grew, in the 1820s [John C. Calhoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_C._Calhoun) became a leading proponent of [states' rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States'_rights), [limited government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_government), [nullification of the U.S. Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullification_\(U.S._Constitution\)), and [free trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_trade). In 1832, the [Ordinance of Nullification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinance_of_Nullification) declared federal tariff laws unconstitutional and not to be enforced in the state, leading to the [Nullification Crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nullification_Crisis). The federal [Force Bill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_Bill) was enacted to use whatever military force necessary to enforce federal law in the state, bringing South Carolina back into line.

&#x20;An 1831 House Report from the Committee on Military Affairs noted that&#x20;

> Before the commencement of the war with Great Britain, and for a long time afterwards, the State of South Carolina was almost destitute of any of the means of military protection, excepting as such could be furnished by her own resources. In the harbor of Charleston alone were there any forts, and these were in so feeble a condition, that at a period, when a British squadron was engaged in sounding the depth of water off the bar, and its commander apparently meditating an attack upon the forts, the quantity of gunpowder in the harbor, belonging to the United States, was not more than sufficient to have enabled the garrison to fire a single round.[^cite_note-28]

In the [United States presidential election of 1860](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1860_United_States_presidential_election), voting was sharply divided, with the South voting for the [Southern Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Democrats) and the North for [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln)'s [Republican Party](32070.md). Lincoln was anti-slavery, did not acknowledge the right to [secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession_in_the_United_States), and would not yield federal property in Southern states. Southern secessionists believed Lincoln's election meant long-term doom for their slavery-based agrarian economy and social system.[^cite_note-29]

Lincoln was elected president on November 6, 1860. The state House of Representatives three days later passed the "Resolution to Call the Election of Abraham Lincoln as U.S. President a Hostile Act",[^cite_note-30] and within weeks South Carolina became the first state to [secede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secession_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-sc-lib-19]

### American Civil War 1861–1865

Main articles: [Ordinance of Secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinance_of_Secession), [Confederate States of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_America), and [South Carolina in the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_in_the_American_Civil_War)



[Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_South_Carolina) after the end of the American Civil War, 1865

On April 12, 1861, Confederate batteries began shelling the Union [Fort Sumter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Sumter) in Charleston Harbor, and the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) began. In November of that year, the Union attacked [Port Royal Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Royal_Sound) and soon occupied [Beaufort County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaufort_County,_South_Carolina) and the neighboring [Sea Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Islands). For the rest of the war, this area served as a Union base and staging point for other operations. Whites abandoned their plantations,[^cite_note-palm-hist-31] leaving behind about ten thousand enslaved people. Several Northern charities partnered with the federal government to help these people run the cotton farms themselves under the [Port Royal Experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Royal_Experiment). Workers were paid by the pound harvested and thus became the first enslaved people freed by the Union forces to earn wages.[^cite_note-vcu-32]

Although the state was not a major battleground, the war ruined the state's economy. More than 60,000 soldiers from South Carolina served in the war,[^cite_note-palm-hist-31] with the state losing an estimated 18,000 troops.[^cite_note-33] Though no regiments of [Southern Unionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Unionists) were formed in South Carolina due to a smaller unionist presence, the [Upstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstate_South_Carolina) region of the state would be a haven for Confederate Army deserters and resisters, as they used the Upstate topography and traditional community relations to resist service in the Confederate ranks.[^cite_note-34] At the end of the war in early 1865, the troops of General [William Tecumseh Sherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Tecumseh_Sherman) marched across the state devastating plantations and most of Columbia. South Carolina would be readmitted to the Union on July 9, 1868.

### Reconstruction 1865–1877

Main article: [Reconstruction era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconstruction_era)



[Joseph Rainey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Rainey) was the first African American person to serve in the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md). He represented [SC's 1st congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina's_1st_congressional_district).

In *[Texas vs. White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_vs._White)* (1869), the Supreme Court ruled the ordinances of secession (including that of South Carolina) were invalid, and thus those states had never left the Union. However, South Carolina did not regain representation in Congress until that date.

Until the [1868 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1868_United_States_presidential_election), South Carolina's legislature, not the voters, chose the state's electors for the presidential election. South Carolina was the last state to choose its electors in this manner. During Reconstruction, South Carolina maintained a majority-black government, which lasted until approximately 1876 when Democrats and former Confederates committed voter fraud to regain power.[^cite_note-Richardson2018-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] On October 19, 1871, President [Ulysses S. Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulysses_S._Grant) suspended [habeas corpus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habeas_corpus) in nine South Carolina counties under the authority of the [Ku Klux Klan Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ku_Klux_Klan_Act).[^cite_note-McFeely_pp._367-374-38] Led by Grant's Attorney General [Amos T. Akerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_T._Akerman), hundreds of Klansmen were arrested while 2,000 Klansmen fled the state.[^cite_note-McFeely_pp._367-374-38] This was done to suppress Klan violence against African-American and white voters in the South.[^cite_note-McFeely_pp._367-374-38] In the mid-to-late 1870s, white Democrats used paramilitary groups such as the [Red Shirts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Shirts_\(Southern_United_States\)) to intimidate and terrorize black voters. They regained political control of the state under conservative white "[Redeemers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redeemers)" and pro-business [Bourbon Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_Democrats). In 1877, the federal government withdrew its troops as part of the [Compromise of 1877](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compromise_of_1877) that ended Reconstruction.

### Populist and agrarian movements

The state became a hotbed of racial and economic tensions during the Populist and Agrarian movements of the 1890s. A [Republican-Populist biracial coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusionism_in_North_Carolina) took power away from White Democrats temporarily in North Carolina. To prevent that from happening, Democrats gained passage of a new constitution in 1895 which effectively [disenfranchised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disfranchisement#United_States) almost all blacks and many poor whites by new requirements for [poll taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poll_tax_\(United_States\)), residency, and [literacy tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_test) that dramatically reduced the voter rolls. By 1896, only 5,500 black voters remained on the voter registration rolls, although they constituted a majority of the state's population.[^cite_note-39] The 1900 census demonstrated the extent of disenfranchisement: the 782,509 African American citizens comprised more than 58% of the state's population, but they were essentially without any political representation in the [Jim Crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow) society.[^cite_note-40]

The 1895 constitution overturned local representative government, reducing the role of the counties to agents of state government, effectively ruled by the General Assembly, through the legislative delegations for each county. As each county had one state senator, that person had considerable power. The counties lacked representative government until home rule was passed in 1975.[^cite_note-governance-41]

Governor ["Pitchfork Ben" Tillman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Tillman), a Populist, led the effort to disenfranchise the blacks and poor whites, although he controlled Democratic state politics from the 1890s to 1910 with a base among poor white farmers. During the constitutional convention in 1895, he supported another man's proposal that the state adopt a [one-drop rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-drop_rule), as well as prohibit marriage between whites and anyone with any known African ancestry.

Some members of the convention realized prominent white families with some African ancestry could be affected by such legislation. In terms similar to a debate in Virginia in 1853 on a similar proposal (which was dropped), George Dionysius Tillman said in opposition:

> If the law is made as it now stands respectable families in [Aiken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aiken_County,_South_Carolina), [Barnwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnwell_County,_South_Carolina), [Colleton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleton_County,_South_Carolina), and [Orangeburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orangeburg_County,_South_Carolina) will be denied the right to intermarry among people with whom they are now associated and identified. At least one hundred families would be affected to my knowledge. They have sent good soldiers to the Confederate Army, and are now landowners and taxpayers. Those men served creditably, and it would be unjust and disgraceful to embarrass them in this way. It is a scientific fact that there is not one full-blooded Caucasian on the floor of this convention. Every member has in him a certain mixture of ... colored blood. The pure-blooded white has needed and received a certain infusion of darker blood to give him readiness and purpose. It would be a cruel injustice and the source of endless litigation, of scandal, horror, feud, and bloodshed to undertake to annul or forbid marriage for a remote, perhaps obsolete trace of Negro blood. The doors would be open to scandal, malice and greed; to statements on the witness stand that the father or grandfather or grandmother had said that A or B had Negro blood in their veins. Any man who is half a man would be ready to blow up half the world with dynamite to prevent or avenge attacks upon the honor of his mother in the legitimacy or purity of the blood of his father.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

The state postponed such a one-drop law for years. Virginian legislators adopted a one-drop law in 1924, forgetting that their state had many people of mixed ancestry among those who identified as white.

### 20th century



Children workers c. 1912. Some children who worked in South Carolina textile mills went to school half a day and worked before and after school—and eight or nine hours on Saturday.

Early in the 20th century, South Carolina developed a thriving [textile industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile_industry). The state also converted its main agricultural base from cotton, to more profitable crops. It would attract large [military bases](843566.md) during [World War I](4764461.md), through its majority Democratic congressional delegation, part of the one-party [Solid South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_South) following disenfranchisement of blacks.

In the late 19th century, South Carolina would implement [Jim Crow laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Jim_Crow_law_examples_by_state) which enforced [racial segregation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_segregation_in_the_United_States) policies until the 1960s. During the early-to-mid part of the 20th century, millions of African Americans left South Carolina and other southern states for jobs, opportunities, and relative freedom in U.S. cities outside the former Confederate states. In total from 1910 to 1970, 6.5 million blacks left the South in the [Great Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_\(African_American\)). By 1930, South Carolina had a white majority population for the first time since 1708.[^cite_note-info-46] South Carolina was one of several states that initially rejected the [Nineteenth Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nineteenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) (1920) giving women the right to vote. The South Carolina legislature later ratified the amendment on July 1, 1969.

See also: [South Carolina in the civil rights movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_in_the_civil_rights_movement)

The struggle of the [civil rights movement](49001.md) took place in South Carolina, as they did in other Southern states and elsewhere within the country. South Carolina would experience a much less violent movement than other Deep South states.[^cite_note-47] This tranquil transition from a [Jim Crow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws) society occurred because the state's white and black leaders were willing to accept slow change, rather than being utterly unwilling to accept change at all.[^cite_note-48] Other South Carolina political figures, like Sen. [Strom Thurmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strom_Thurmond), on the other hand, were among the nation's most radical and effective opponents of social equality and integration.

During the mid-to-late 20th century, South Carolina started to see economic progress first in the textile industry and then in manufacturing. Tourism also started to form into a major industry within the state during the 20th century, especially in areas such as [Myrtle Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrtle_Beach,_South_Carolina) and [Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_South_Carolina).[^cite_note-49]

### 21st century



North Ocean Boulevard in [Myrtle Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrtle_Beach,_South_Carolina), 2010

As the 21st century progresses, South Carolina has attracted new business by having a 5% [corporate income tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_income_tax) rate, no state [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax), no local income tax, no inventory tax, no [sales tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sales_tax) on manufacturing equipment, industrial power or materials for finished products; no wholesale tax, and no unitary tax on worldwide profits.[^cite_note-50]

South Carolina was one of the first states to stop paying for "early elective" deliveries of [babies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant), under either [Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid) and private insurance.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] The term early elective is defined as a [labor induction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_induction) or [Cesarean section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesarean_section) between 37 and 39 weeks. The change was intended to result in healthier babies and fewer costs for the state of South Carolina.[^cite_note-urlSC_DHHS-53]

On November 20, 2014, South Carolina became the 35th state to legalize [same-sex marriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_South_Carolina), when a federal court ordered the change.[^cite_note-54]

As of 2022, South Carolina had one of the lowest percentages among all states of women in state legislature, at 17.6% (only five states had a lower percentage; the national average is 30.7%; with the highest percentage being in [Nevada](21216.md) at 61.9%).[^cite_note-55]

### Regions

Main article: [Geographical regions of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geographical_regions_of_South_Carolina)

The state can be divided into three natural geographic areas which then can be subdivided into five distinct cultural regions. The natural environment is divided from east to west by the [Atlantic coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_coastal_plain), the [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont_\(plateau\)), and the [Blue Ridge Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Mountains). Culturally, the coastal plain is split into the [Lowcountry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_Lowcountry) and the [Pee Dee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pee_Dee) region. While, the upper [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont,_South_Carolina) region is referred to as the Piedmont and the lower Piedmont region is referred to as the Midlands. The area surrounding the Blue Ridge Mountains is known as the [Upstate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upstate_South_Carolina).[^cite_note-netstate-56] The Atlantic Coastal Plain makes up two-thirds of the state. Its eastern border is the [Sea Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Islands), a chain of tidal and [barrier islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_islands). The border between the lowcountry and the upcountry is defined by the [Atlantic Seaboard fall line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Seaboard_fall_line), which marks the limit of navigable rivers.

Altogether, the state has a total area of 32,020.49 square miles (82,932.7 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 30,060.70 square miles (77,856.9 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 1,959.79 square miles (5,075.8 km<sup>2</sup>) (6.12%) is water.[^cite_note-2010TotalArea-57]

### Atlantic Coastal Plain

Main article: [South Carolina Lowcountry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_Lowcountry)

The Atlantic [Coastal plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_plain) consists of sediments and sedimentary rocks that range in age from Cretaceous to Present. The terrain is relatively flat and the soil is composed predominantly of sand, silt, and clay. Areas with better drainage make excellent farmland, though some land is swampy. An unusual feature of the coastal plain is a large number of low-relief topographic depressions named [Carolina bays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_bay). The bays tend to be oval, lining up in a northwest to southeast orientation. The eastern portion of the coastal plain contains many [salt marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_marsh) and [estuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary), as well as natural ports such as [Georgetown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgetown,_South_Carolina) and [Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_South_Carolina). The [natural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature) areas of the coastal plain are part of the [Middle Atlantic coastal forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Atlantic_coastal_forests) [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion).[^cite_note-ecoregions-58]

The [Sandhills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandhills_\(Carolina\)) or Carolina Sandhills is a 10–35 mi (16–56 km) wide region within the Atlantic Coastal Plain province, along the [inland](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/inland) margin of this province. The Carolina Sandhills are interpreted as eolian (wind-blown) sand sheets and dunes that were mobilized episodically from approximately 75,000 to 6,000 years ago. Most of the published luminescence ages from the sand are coincident with the last glaciation, a time when the southeastern United States was characterized by colder air temperatures and stronger winds.[^cite_note-59]

### Piedmont

Much of Piedmont consists of Paleozoic metamorphic and igneous rocks, and the landscape has relatively low relief. Due to the changing economics of farming, much of the land is now reforested in [loblolly pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loblolly_pine) for the lumber industry. These forests are part of the [Southeastern mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_mixed_forests) ecoregion.[^cite_note-ecoregions-58] At the southeastern edge of Piedmont is the [fall line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Seaboard_fall_line), where rivers drop to the coastal plain. The fall line was an important early source of water power. Mills built to this resource encouraged the growth of several cities, including the capital, [Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_South_Carolina). The larger rivers are navigable up to the fall line, providing a trade route for mill towns.

The northwestern part of Piedmont is also known as the Foothills. The [Cherokee Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_Highway_11) is a scenic driving route through this area. This is where [Table Rock State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_Rock_State_Park_\(South_Carolina\)) is located.

### Blue Ridge



[Pinnacle Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinnacle_Mountain_\(South_Carolina\)) viewed from [Caesars Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesars_Head)

The Blue Ridge consists primarily of Precambrian metamorphic rocks, and the landscape has relatively high relief. The Blue Ridge Region contains an [escarpment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escarpment) of the [Blue Ridge Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Mountains) that continues into [North Carolina](21650.md) and [Georgia](48830.md) as part of the southern [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains). [Sassafras Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassafras_Mountain), South Carolina's highest point at 3,560 feet (1,090 m), is in this area.[^cite_note-usgs-60] Also in this area is [Caesars Head State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesars_Head_State_Park). The environment here is that of the [Appalachian-Blue Ridge forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian-Blue_Ridge_forests) ecoregion.[^cite_note-ecoregions-58] The [Chattooga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chattooga_River), on the border between South Carolina and Georgia, is a favorite [whitewater rafting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitewater_rafting) destination.

### Earthquakes

Main article: [List of earthquakes in South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_South_Carolina)

South Carolina is the most seismically active state on the [East Coast](89126.md).[^cite_note-63] Between July 1, 2021, and July 1, 2022, there were 74 recorded earthquakes in South Carolina,[^cite_note-auto1-64] six of which exceeded a 3 magnitude.[^cite_note-65] In 2021 and 2022, most of which were concentrated in [Kershaw County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kershaw_County,_South_Carolina) and the coastal area of [Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston,_South_Carolina).[^cite_note-auto1-64] The Charleston area demonstrates the greatest frequency of earthquakes in South Carolina. South Carolina averages 10–15 earthquakes a year below magnitude 3 (FEMA). The [Charleston earthquake of 1886](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1886_Charleston_earthquake) was the largest quake ever to hit the eastern United States. The 7.0–7.3 magnitude earthquake killed 60 people and destroyed much of the city.[^cite_note-66] Faults in this region are difficult to study at the surface due to thick sedimentation on top of them. Many of the ancient faults are within plates rather than along plate boundaries.

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_South_Carolina)



A map of the average annual [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) in South Carolina

&#x20;South Carolina has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa*), although high-elevation areas in the Upstate area have fewer subtropical characteristics than areas on the Atlantic coastline. In the summer, South Carolina is hot and humid, with daytime temperatures averaging between 86–93 °F (30–34 °C) in most of the state and overnight lows averaging 70–75 °F (21–24 °C) on the coast and from 66–73 °F (19–23 °C) inland. Winter temperatures are much less uniform in South Carolina. Coastal areas of the state have very mild winters, with high temperatures approaching an average of 60 °F (16 °C) and overnight lows around 40 °F (5–8 °C).&#x20;



A snow plow in South Carolina. The upstate and mountainous region of the state receives the most measurable snowfall.

Inland, the average January overnight low is around 32 °F (0 °C) in Columbia and temperatures well below freezing in the Upstate. While precipitation is abundant the entire year in almost the entire state, the coast tends to have a slightly wetter summer, while inland, the spring and autumn transitions tend to be the wettest periods and winter the driest season, with November being the driest month. The highest recorded temperature is 113 °F (45 °C) in [Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnston,_South_Carolina) and Columbia on June 29, 2012, and the lowest recorded temperature is −19 °F (−28 °C) at [Caesars Head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesars_Head) on January 21, 1985.

Snowfall in South Carolina is minimal in the lower elevation areas south and east of Columbia. It is not uncommon for areas along the southernmost coast to not receive measurable snowfall for several years. In the Piedmont and Foothills, especially along and north of Interstate 85, measurable snowfall occurs one to three times in most years. Annual average total amounts range from 2 to 6 inches. The Blue Ridge Escarpment receives the most average total measurable snowfall; amounts range from 7 to 12 inches.

South Carolina averages around 50 days of [thunderstorm](70807.md) activity a year. This is less than some of the states further south, and it is slightly less vulnerable to [tornadoes](37530.md) than the states which border on the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md). Some notable tornadoes have struck South Carolina, and the state averages around 14 tornadoes annually. [Hail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hail) is common with many of the thunderstorms in the state, as there is often a marked contrast in temperature of warmer ground conditions compared to the cold air aloft.[^cite_note-Annual_average_number_of_tornadoes-67]

#### Hurricanes and tropical cyclones

Main article: [List of South Carolina hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_South_Carolina_hurricanes)



Category 4 Hurricane Hugo in 1989

The state is occasionally affected by [tropical cyclones](8282374.md). This is an annual concern during hurricane season, which lasts from June 1 to November 30. The peak time of vulnerability for the southeast Atlantic coast is from early August to early October, during the [Cape Verde hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Verde_hurricane) season. Memorable [hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane) to hit South Carolina include [Hazel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Hazel) (1954), [Hugo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Hugo) (1989), [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Florence) (2018), and [Helene](77945659.md) (2024).

#### Climate change

This section is an excerpt from [Climate change in South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_South_Carolina).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Climate_change_in_South_Carolina\&action=edit)]



Köppen climate types in South Carolina, showing a large majority of the state being humid subtropical, with smaller, outlier pockets of an oceanic climate in the Blue Ridge Mountains.

[Climate change in South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_South_Carolina) encompasses the effects of [climate change](5042951.md), attributed to man-made increases in atmospheric [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide), in the U.S. state of South Carolina.

Studies show that South Carolina is among a string of "[Deep South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_South)" states that will experience the worst effects of climate change.[^cite_note-68] According to the [United States Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md):

> South Carolina's climate is changing. Most of the state has warmed by one-half to one degree Fahrenheit (300-600 m°C) in the last century, and the sea is rising about one to one-and-a-half inches (2.5-3.8 cm) every decade. Higher water levels are eroding beaches, submerging low lands, and exacerbating [coastal flooding](31546082.md). Like other southeastern states, South Carolina has warmed less than most of the nation. But in the coming decades, the region's changing climate is likely to reduce crop yields, harm livestock, increase the number of unpleasantly hot days, and increase the risk of heat stroke and other heat-related illnesses.[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_South_Carolina_EPA-69]

As of January 2020, "South Carolina's failure to develop a comprehensive climate plan means the state has no overall effort to cut [greenhouse gas pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_the_United_States), limit [sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl) or educate the public on how to [adapt to the changing climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_adaptation)."

[^cite_note-Climate_change_in_South_Carolina_\:0-70]

South Carolina released its Climate, Energy, and Commerce Committee Final Report in 2008. The report recommends a voluntary economy-wide goal of reducing emissions to 5% below 1990 levels by 2020. Key policy recommendations in the report include developing renewable portfolio standards, increasing use of local agricultural products, and increasing advanced recycling and composting.

### Flora and fauna

Further information: [List of mammals of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mammals_of_South_Carolina) and [List of snakes of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_snakes_of_South_Carolina)

South Carolina is home to two dominant ecosystems, the bottomlands, which consist of floodplains and creeks, and the toplands. The floodplains contain large tracts of old and mature second growth cypress and tupelo forest. The uplands are home to longleaf pine, shortleaf pine, and mixed hardwood forests.[^cite_note-\:0-71] The Longleaf Pine are an important part of South Carolina's coastal ecosystem. They improve soil, water, and air quality while providing a habitat for deer and songbirds.[^cite_note-72] These forests are endangered by logging for agriculture and development.[^cite_note-\:0-71][^cite_note-\:1-73]

Oysters are a critical part of South Carolina's coastal ecology. They serve a dual function, filtering the water and forming reefs that provide a habitat for small fish and crabs. Oysters are imperiled by overharvesting because young oysters need older oysters to latch on to as they age.[^cite_note-\:1-73] South Carolina is home to many shorebirds including various [sandpipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandpiper) and [ibises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibis).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-\:2-75] The state serves as a stopover site for birds migrating farther south and a wintering ground for birds that do not fly as far south.[^cite_note-\:2-75]

Population density of South Carolina

The 2020 census determined the state had a population of 5,118,425, a 10.7% percentage increase since the 2010 census.[^cite_note-PopEstUS-91]

According to [HUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Housing_and_Urban_Development)'s 2022 [Annual Homeless Assessment Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annual_Homeless_Assessment_Report_to_Congress), there were an estimated 3,608 [homeless](19394651.md) people in South Carolina.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

At the 2020 census, the racial make up of the state was 63.4% White (62.1% [non-Hispanic white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_white)), 25.0% Black or African American, 0.5% American Indian and Alaska Native, 1.8% Asian, 0.1% Native Hawaiian and other Pacific Islander, 3.5% from some other race, and 5.8% from two or more races. 6.9% of the total population was of Hispanic or Latino origin of any race.[^cite_note-2020DP1-86]

At the 2019 [census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_census) estimate, South Carolina had an estimated population of 5,148,714, which is an increase of 64,587 from the prior year and an increase of 523,350, or 11.31%, since the year 2010. [Immigration](15051.md) from outside the United States resulted in a net increase of 36,401 people, and [migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_migration) within the country produced a net increase of 115,084 people. According to the [University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina)'s Arnold School of Public Health, Consortium for Latino Immigration Studies, South Carolina's foreign-born population grew faster than any other state between 2000 and 2005.[^cite_note-USC_Aug2007-94][^cite_note-95] South Carolina has banned [sanctuary cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary_city).[^cite_note-96]

The top countries of origin for South Carolina's immigrants were [Mexico](3966054.md), [India](14533.md), [Germany](11867.md), [Honduras](13394.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md), as of 2018.[^cite_note-97]

Enslaved Africans were brought to the state during the [slave trade](49005.md).[^cite_note-98] There is also a [Gullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gullah) community in South Carolina.[^cite_note-99] Additionally, there is one federally recognized tribe in South Carolina, the Catawba Indian Nation, and 24,303 identified as being Native American alone, and 83,808 did in combination with one or more other races in 2020.[^cite_note-\:4-81]

### Languages

Many indigenous languages such as Muskogean languages have disappeared. Cherokee and Catawba lasted the longest. European settlers in South Carolina spoke Spanish, French, German, Irish, English, Welsh, and Scots. Gullah is spoken in the state by African Americans. Newer residents in South Carolina speak Tagalog, Greek, Indic, Italian, Chinese, Korean or Japanese.[^cite_note-101]

According to the [Association of Religion Data Archives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Religion_Data_Archives) (ARDA), in 2010, the largest religion is Christianity, of which the largest denominations were the [Southern Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention) with 913,763 adherents, the [United Methodist Church](32304.md) with 274,111 adherents, and the [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) with 181,743 adherents. Fourth-largest is the [African Methodist Episcopal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Methodist_Episcopal_Church) with 564 congregations and 121,000 members and fifth-largest is the [Presbyterian Church (USA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_\(USA\)) with 320 congregations and almost 100,000 members.\[^cite_note-[www.thearda.com-103](http://www.thearda.com-103/)] As of 2010, South Carolina was the American state with the highest per capita proportion of citizens who follow the [Baháʼí Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith), with 17,559 adherents,[^cite_note-104] making Baháʼí the second-largest religion in the state at the time.[^cite_note-105]

According to the [Public Religion Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Religion_Research_Institute) in 2020, Christianity remained the largest religion at approximately 74% of the population.[^cite_note-106] Among the Christian population, [evangelical Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) remained the majority; the irreligious community was 18% of the total population. Per ARDA's 2020 religion census, Southern Baptists remained the majority with 816,405 adherents, and Roman Catholics had 407,840 adherents, followed by United Methodists at 242,467. As other Baptist denominations had from 10 to 40,000+ members individually, [nondenominational/interdenominational Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nondenominational_Christianity) increased to 454,063 adherents.[^cite_note-\:3-107]

Outside of Christianity, ARDA's 2020 study reported 6,677 [Muslims](19541.md) in the state, and 830 [Orthodox Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Judaism); [Reform Judaism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_Judaism) consisted of 3,430 adherents. Altogether, [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) had 8,383 adherents.[^cite_note-\:3-107] With respect to Judaism in the state, there is a substantial [history of the Jews in Charleston, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Charleston,_South_Carolina).

In 2022, the Public Religion Research Institute estimated that Christians increased to 76% of the population (64% Protestant, 11% Catholic, and 1% Jehovah's Witness). The unaffiliated also increased, forming 20% of the state's population, although [New Agers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Age) constituted 3% of the state. Judaism was 1% of the total population.

## Economy

See also: [Economy of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_South_Carolina) and [South Carolina locations by per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_locations_by_per_capita_income)



A roller coaster in the South Carolina portion of [Carowinds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carowinds)

- Total employment (2021): 1,936,015

  [^cite_note-2020CensusQuickFacts-79]

- Total employer establishments (2021): 116,896

  [^cite_note-2020CensusQuickFacts-79]

- [Unemployment rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unemployment) (May 2025): 4.1%

According to the U.S. [Bureau of Economic Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Economic_Analysis), in 2025, South Carolina's [gross state product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product) (GSP) was \$378.830 billion and its per capita personal income was \$63,179.[^cite_note-108] The state debt in 2012 was calculated by one source to be \$22.9bn, or \$7,800 per taxpayer.[^cite_note-109]

Industrial outputs include textile goods, chemical products, paper products, machinery, automobiles, automotive products and tourism. Major agricultural outputs of the state are tobacco, poultry, cotton, cattle, dairy products, soybeans, hay, rice, and swine.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111] According to the Bureau of Labor Statistics, as of March 2012, South Carolina had 1,852,700 nonfarm jobs of which 12% are in manufacturing, 11.5% are in leisure and hospitality, 19% are in trade, transportation, and utilities, and 11.8% are in education and health services. The service sector accounts for 83.7% of the South Carolina economy.[^cite_note-112] In 2025, 99.4% of South Carolina's businesses were [small businesses](276495.md) and employed 42.9% of the state's work force.[^cite_note-113]

Many large corporations have facilities in South Carolina. [Boeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_South_Carolina) opened an aircraft manufacturing facility at [Charleston International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston_International_Airport) in 2011, which serves as the final assembly site for the [787 Dreamliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/787_Dreamliner). South Carolina is a right-to-work state[^cite_note-114] and many businesses use staffing agencies to temporarily fill positions.[^cite_note-115] [Domtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domtar), in Rock Hill, used to be the only [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) company headquartered in South Carolina, but it was later moved into the [Fortune 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_1000) list.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-\:5-117] The three Fortune 1000 companies headquartered in the state are Domtar, [Sonoco Products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoco_Products), and ScanSource.[^cite_note-\:5-117]

South Carolina also benefits from foreign investment. There are 1,950 foreign-owned firms operating in South Carolina employing almost 135,000 people.[^cite_note-118] Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) brought 1.06 billion dollars to the state economy in 2010.[^cite_note-119] Since 1994, [BMW](3772.md) has had a production facility in Spartanburg County near Greer and since 1996 the [Zapp Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapp_Group) operates in Summerville near Charleston.

## Education

Main article: [Education in South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_South_Carolina)

South Carolina has 1,167 K–12 schools in 79 school districts with an enrollment of 751,660 as of 2022.[^cite_note-123] In 2022, South Carolina spent \$11,747 per public school student.[^cite_note-124]

In 2023, the average [SAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAT) score in South Carolina was 1028, in line with the national average.[^cite_note-125]

South Carolina does not currently implement [Common Core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Core) in schools, having repealed the standards in 2014.[^cite_note-126]

In 2014, the [South Carolina Supreme Court](6985896.md) ruled the state had failed to provide a "minimally adequate" education to children in all parts of the state as required by the state's constitution.[^cite_note-127]

South Carolina is the only state which owns and operates a statewide school bus system. As of December 2016, the state maintains a 5,582-bus fleet with the average vehicle in service being fifteen years old (the national average is six) having logged 236,000 miles.[^cite_note-128] Half of the state's school buses are more than 15 years old and some are reportedly up to 30 years old. In 2017 in the budget proposal, Superintendent of Education Molly Spearman requested the state lease to purchase 1,000 buses to replace the most decrepit vehicles. An additional 175 buses could be purchased immediately through the State Treasurer's master lease program.[^cite_note-129] On January 5, 2017, the [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Environmental_Protection_Agency) awarded South Carolina more than \$1.1 million to replace 57 school buses with new cleaner models through its [Diesel Emissions Reduction Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_Emissions_Reduction_Act) program.[^cite_note-130]

### Higher education

Further information: [List of colleges and universities in South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_South_Carolina) and [University of South Carolina System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina_System)

South Carolina has diverse institutions from large state-funded research universities to small colleges that cultivate a liberal arts, religious, or military tradition. (List below sorted by year established.)

- The [College of Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Charleston), founded in 1770 and chartered in 1785, is the oldest institution of higher learning in South Carolina, the 13th oldest in the United States, and the first [municipal college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_college) in the country. The college is in company with the [Colonial Colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Colleges) as one of the original and foundational institutions of higher education in the United States. Its founders include three signers of the [United States Declaration of Independence](31874.md) and three signers of the [United States Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution). The college's historic campus, listed on the [U.S. Department of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_the_Interior)'s [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places), forms an integral part of Charleston's colonial-era urban center. The Graduate School of the College of Charleston offers a number of degree programs and coordinates support for its nationally recognized faculty research efforts.

- The [University of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_South_Carolina), in Columbia, is a [flagship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flagship_campus), [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_university_\(U.S.\)), co-educational, [research university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university) with seven satellite campuses. It was founded in 1801 as [South Carolina College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_College), and its original campus, [The Horseshoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Campus_District,_University_of_South_Carolina), is listed on the National Register of Historic Places. The university's main campus covers over 359 acres (1.5 km

- [Furman University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furman_University) is a private, coeducational, non-sectarian, liberal arts university in [Greenville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenville,_South_Carolina). Founded in 1826, Furman enrolls approximately 2,900 undergraduate and 500 graduate students. Furman is the largest private institution in South Carolina. The university is primarily focused on undergraduate education (only two departments, education and chemistry, offer graduate degrees).

- [Erskine College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erskine_College) is a private, coeducational liberal arts college in [Due West, South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Due_West,_South_Carolina). The college was founded in 1839 and is affiliated with the [Associate Reformed Presbyterian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associate_Reformed_Presbyterian_Church), which maintains a theological seminary on the campus.

- [The Citadel, The Military College of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Citadel) is a state-supported, comprehensive college in Charleston. Founded in 1842, it is best known for its undergraduate Corps of Cadets military program for men and women, which combines academics, physical challenges and military discipline. In addition to the cadet program, the Citadel Graduate College offers evening certificate, undergraduate and graduate programs to civilians. The Citadel has 2,200 undergraduate cadets in its residential military program and 1,200 civilian students in the evening programs.

- [Wofford College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wofford_College) is a small liberal arts college in [Spartanburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartanburg,_South_Carolina). Wofford was founded in 1854 with a bequest of \$100,000 from the Rev. Benjamin Wofford (1780–1850), a Methodist minister and Spartanburg native who sought to create a college for "literary, classical, and scientific education in my native district of Spartanburg". It is one of the few four-year institutions in the southeastern United States founded before the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) that operates on its original campus.

- [Newberry College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newberry_College) is a small liberal arts college in [Newberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newberry,_South_Carolina). Founded in 1856, Newberry is a co-educational, private liberal-arts college of the Evangelical Lutheran Church in America (ELCA) on a historic 90-acre (36 ha) campus in Newberry, South Carolina. It has roughly 1,110 students and a 14\:1 student-teacher ratio. According to U.S. News & World Report's America's Best Colleges, Newberry College ranks among the nation's top colleges in the southern region.

- [Claflin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claflin_University), founded in 1869 by the [American Missionary Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Missionary_Association), is the oldest [historically black college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historically_black_college) in the state. After the Democratic-dominated legislature closed the university in 1877, before passing a law to restrict admission to whites, it designated Claflin as the only state college for blacks.

- [Lander University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lander_University) is a public liberal arts university in [Greenwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenwood,_South_Carolina). Lander was founded in 1872 as Willamston Female College.

## Health care

For overall health care, South Carolina is ranked 37th out of the 50 states in 2022, according to [The Commonwealth Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Fund), a private health foundation working to improve the health care system.[^cite_note-135] The state's teen birth rate was 53 births per 1,000 teens, compared to the national average of 41.9 births, according to the [Kaiser Family Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Family_Foundation).[^cite_note-136] The state's [infant mortality rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality_rate) was 9.4 deaths per 1,000 births compared to the national average of 6.9 deaths.[^cite_note-137]

There were 2.6 physicians per 1,000 people compared to the national average of 3.2 physicians.[^cite_note-138] There was \$5,114 spent on health expenses per capita in the state, compared to the national average of \$5,283.[^cite_note-139] There were 26 percent of children and 13 percent of elderly living in poverty in the state, compared to 23 percent and 13 percent, respectively, doing so in the U.S.[^cite_note-140] There were 34 percent of children that were [overweight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overweight) or [obese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obese), compared to the national average of 32 percent.[^cite_note-141]

## Government and politics

Main article: [Government and politics of South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_and_politics_of_South_Carolina)

See also: [Political party strength in South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party_strength_in_South_Carolina)



[2024 U.S. presidential election results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_presidential_election_in_South_Carolina) by county in South Carolina&#x20;

&#x20; Democratic

&#x20; Republican

South Carolina's [state government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_governments_of_the_United_States) consists of executive, legislative, and judicial branches. The [governor of South Carolina](253089.md) heads the executive branch; the [South Carolina General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_General_Assembly) heads the legislative branch; and the [South Carolina Supreme Court](6985896.md) heads the judicial branch.

South Carolina is a largely conservative state. Since the [Declaration of Independence](31874.md), South Carolina's politics have been controlled by three main parties: the [Democratic-Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic-Republican_Party_\(United_States\)) in the early 1800s, the [Democratic Party](5043544.md) through most of the 19th and 20th centuries, and the [Republican Party](32070.md) in the 21st century. Since the mid-1990s, the [South Carolina General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_General_Assembly) has been controlled by the Republican party, and currently, eight of nine statewide offices are held by Republican officeholders and one by a Democratic officeholder.[^cite_note-142][^cite_note-143]

&#x20;At the federal level, South Carolina has voted Republican in every presidential election since the [1980 election of Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_presidential_election).[^cite_note-144] As of the [2024 presidential election](59568546.md), it is the only state located on the [Eastern Seaboard of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Seaboard_of_the_United_States) that has never been won by a Democratic Party presidential candidate in the 21st Century. The last [Democratic](5043544.md) candidate to carry South Carolina was [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) in [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_United_States_presidential_election). The most recent Democratic senator to serve was [Fritz Hollings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Hollings), who left office in 2005. South Carolina has seven representatives in the [United States House of Representatives](19468510.md), six of whom are Republican.&#x20;



[South Carolina State House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_State_House)

As of November 8, 2022, there were 3,740,743 registered voters.[^cite_note-145] In a 2020 study, South Carolina was ranked by the *[Election Law Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Law_Journal)* as the 7th hardest state for citizens to vote in.[^cite_note-146] South Carolina retains the [death penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_South_Carolina). Authorized methods of execution include by [electric chair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_chair) or firing squad.[^cite_note-147]

An April 2023 *[Winthrop University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winthrop_University)* poll found that an overwhelming majority of South Carolinians supported legalizing [medical marijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_cannabis_in_the_United_States) and believed that a [separation between church and state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Separation_of_church_and_state_in_the_United_States) was "critical". A large majority were also found to support [same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Same-sex_marriage_in_the_United_States), legalized [recreational marijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legality_of_cannabis_by_U.S._jurisdiction) and [sports gambling](1328050.md), along with an [independent commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redistricting_commission) system for [congressional redistricting](667093.md).[^cite_note-148]

## Culture

See also: [South Carolina literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_literature)

South Carolina has many venues for visual and performing arts. The [Gibbes Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gibbes_Museum_of_Art) in Charleston, the [Greenville County Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenville_County_Museum_of_Art), the [Columbia Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_Museum_of_Art), Spartanburg Art Museum, and the [South Carolina State Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Carolina_State_Museum) in Columbia among others provide access to visual arts to the state. There are also numerous historic sites and museums scattered throughout the state paying homage to many events and periods in the state's history from Native American inhabitation to the present day.

South Carolina also has performing art venues including the [Peace Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Center) in Greenville, the [Koger Center for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koger_Center_for_the_Arts) in Columbia, and the [Newberry Opera House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newberry_Opera_House), among others to bring local, national, and international talent to the stages of South Carolina. Several large venues can house major events, including [Colonial Life Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Life_Arena) in Columbia, [Bon Secours Wellness Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bon_Secours_Wellness_Arena) in Greenville, and [North Charleston Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Charleston_Coliseum).

One of the nation's major performing arts festivals, [Spoleto Festival USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoleto_Festival_USA), is held annually in Charleston. There are also countless local festivals throughout the state highlighting many cultural traditions, historical events, and folklore.

According to the South Carolina Arts Commission, creative industries generate \$9.2 billion annually and support over 78,000 jobs in the state.[^cite_note-149] A 2009 statewide poll by the University of South Carolina Institute for Public Service and Policy Research found that 67% of residents had participated in the arts in some form during the past year and on average citizens had participated in the arts 14 times in the previous year.

### Sports

Main article: [Sports in South Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_South_Carolina)

Although no major league professional sports teams are based in South Carolina, the [Carolina Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Panthers) have training facilities in the state and played their inaugural season's home games at Clemson's [Memorial Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Stadium_\(Clemson\)) in 1995. They now play at [Bank of America Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_America_Stadium) in [Charlotte, North Carolina](57447.md). The Panthers consider themselves "The Carolinas' Team" and refrained from naming themselves after Charlotte or either of the Carolinas, as they represent both states. The Panthers prominently display the South Carolina state flag at the stadium with co-equal prominence to the North Carolina flag.

The highest level of professional sports in the state is in soccer with the [Charleston Battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charleston_Battery) of the second-division [USL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Championship). Founded in 1993, the Battery also hold the designation as the oldest continuously operating professional soccer team in the United States, pre-dating Major League Soccer, too.

The state is also home to numerous minor league professional teams. College teams represent their particular South Carolina institutions, and are the primary options for [football](18951490.md), basketball and baseball attendance in the state. South Carolina is also a top destination for golf and [water sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_water_sports).

South Carolina is also home to one of [NASCAR](21114.md)'s first tracks and its first paved speedway, [Darlington Raceway](685467.md), located northwest of [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence,_South_Carolina).
