**Alix Didier Fils-Aimé** (Haitian Creole pronunciation: [\[aliks didje fis eme\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Haitian_Creole); born 14 November 1971) is a Haitian businessman and politician who has served as the acting [prime minister of Haiti](3972111.md) since 2024, and became the country’s sole leader following the dissolution of the [Transitional Presidential Council](76614454.md), starting on 7 February 2026.

## Early life and education

Fils-Aimé studied at [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University).[^cite_note-AP2-1] He owns a chain of [dry cleaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_cleaning) stores.[^cite_note-2]

## Business career

From 1999 to 2011 he was the president of Hainet, one of the internet providers in Haiti. The company became insolvent and sold its assets in 2013.[^cite_note-3] He is one of the founders of the *Association des Entreprises de Technologies de l'Information et de la Communication* (ATIC).[^cite_note-4]

Fils-Aimé was a member of the board of Banque de l’Union Haïtienne (BUH), where he replaced Eddy Deeb, until his nomination as Prime Minister of Haiti.[^cite_note-5]

He was the president of the [Chamber of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_commerce) of Haiti in the government of former president [Michel Martelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Martelly).[^cite_note-6] He ran for a seat in the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_\(Haiti\)) for the [Vérité](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A9rit%C3%A9_\(political_party\)) party in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Haitian_parliamentary_election).[^cite_note-AP2-1]

## Acting Prime Minister

On 10 November 2024, he succeeded [Garry Conille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garry_Conille) as acting Prime Minister of Haiti. Conille was fired by the Transitional Presidential Council the same day.[^cite_note-7]

On 23 January 2026, the Transitional Presidential Council attempted to fire Fils-Aimé, seeking a replacement within thirty days.[^cite_note-8] However, [Laurent Saint-Cyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Saint-Cyr), chairman of the Transitional Presidential Council, refused to sign and publish the motion on *[Le Moniteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Moniteur_\(Haiti\))* that would have removed him.[^cite_note-9] After the mandate of the Transitional Presidential Council expired on 7 February 2026, powers were handed over to Fils-Aimé.[^cite_note-SondeoLatino-10]

On 23 February 2026, the "National Pact for Stability and the Organization of Elections" was signed by a number of political parties and civil society groups to support Fils-Aimé as the sole executive of Haiti until the [next election](59684636.md) is held.[^cite_note-11]
