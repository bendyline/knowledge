The **Rwanda national football team** represents [Rwanda](25645.md) in international [football](10568.md) and is controlled by the [Rwandan Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwandan_Football_Federation), the [governing body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_governing_body) of [football in Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Rwanda), and competes as a member of the [Confederation of African Football](715265.md) (CAF), as well as the [Council for East and Central Africa Football Associations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CECAFA) (CECAFA), a CAF sub-confederation that governs football in East and Central Africa. The team bears the nickname *Amavubi* ([Kinyarwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinyarwanda) for *The Wasps*), and primarily plays its home games at the [Stade Amahoro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amahoro_Stadium) in [Kigali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kigali), the nation's capital. They have never qualified for a [World Cup](11370.md) finals, and reached their only [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Nations_Cup).

## History

Rwanda [qualified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Cup_of_Nations_qualification#Group_13) for its first ever [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) in the [2004 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_African_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-2] At the tournament, they lost their opening match 2–1 to [Tunisia](1029953.md) before winning their first ever point in the competition after a 1–1 draw against [Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinea_national_football_team).[^cite_note-3] Rwanda went on to beat [DR Congo](1030243.md) in their final group match by a 1–0 scoreline, but it wasn't enough, as elsewhere in the group, Guinea and Tunisia drew, meaning both teams progressed to the quarter-finals, and [Rwanda](25645.md) were eliminated.[^cite_note-4]

On 3 November 2007, [Josip Kuže](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Ku%C5%BEe) was appointed as head coach of Rwanda on a three-year deal,[^cite_note-5] but left just two months later to manage Japanese club [JEF United Chiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JEF_United_Chiba), having managed just six matches.[^cite_note-6] [Raoul Shungu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raoul_Shungu) was appointed as interim manager on 11 April 2008,[^cite_note-7] with [Branko Tucak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branko_Tucak) appointed on a permanent basis just over two months later.[^cite_note-8] After Rwanda finished bottom of their group in the [third round of 2010 FIFA World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CAF_third_round), Tucak was sacked in November 2009, with [Eric Nshimiyimana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Nshimiyimana) appointed as his replacement on an interim basis.[^cite_note-9] In February 2010, Ghanaian [Sellas Tetteh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sellas_Tetteh) was appointed as the new national team manager.[^cite_note-10] Under Tetteh, Rwanda qualified for the [2011 African Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_African_Nations_Championship),[^cite_note-11] but finished bottom of their group after losing all three group stage matches.[^cite_note-12] Tetteh resigned in September 2011.[^cite_note-13]

[Milutin Sredojević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milutin_Sredojevi%C4%87) was appointed as Rwanda manager in November 2011,[^cite_note-14] though he was sacked in April 2013.[^cite_note-15] Nshimiyimana was reappointed to succeed Sredojević,[^cite_note-16] though he was replaced by [Stephen Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Constantine) in May 2014.[^cite_note-17] Under Constantine, Rwanda appeared to qualify for the group stage of qualifying for the [2015 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), but were disqualified after fielding an ineligible player in a match against [Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_national_football_team).[^cite_note-18] Constantine resigned from his role in January 2015 however, in order to manage the [India national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_national_football_team).[^cite_note-19]

[Rwanda Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rwanda_Football_Federation) technical director [Lee Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Johnson_\(football_coach\)) became interim manager before [Johnathan McKinstry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnathan_McKinstry) was appointed as Constantine's replacement in March 2015, with Johnson leaving his role as technical director to become Constantine's assistant manager in India shortly after.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] In his first competitive game in charge, McKinstry guided Rwanda to their first away win in four years with a 1–0 victory over Mozambique in the opening round of 2017 African Nations Cup qualifying.[^cite_note-22] In December 2015, Rwanda finished as runners-up in the [CECAFA Senior Challenge Cup 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_CECAFA_Cup), having lost 1–0 to [Uganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_national_football_team) in the final.[^cite_note-auto3-23][^cite_note-auto4-24] In January 2016, Rwanda reached the knock-out stages of a major competition for the first time in their history[^cite_note-25] after topping their group at the [2016 African Nations Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_African_Nations_Championship).[^cite_note-26] Rwanda did however lose to eventual champions, [DR Congo](1030243.md), 2–1 after extra time in the quarter-final.[^cite_note-27] In March 2016 McKinstry agreed a new two-year contract,[^cite_note-28] though he was sacked just five months later, with [Jimmy Mulisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Mulisa) appointed on an interim basis.[^cite_note-29] The Rwanda Football Federation were later ordered by [FIFA](11049.md) to compensate McKinstry \$200,000 for unlawful dismissal, and though the Rwandan FA initially appealed this ruling, they later complied.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

In November 2023, [Torsten Spittler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torsten_Spittler) was appointed as head coach,[^cite_note-32] though he was relieved of his duties in January 2025.[^cite_note-33] [Adel Amrouche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adel_Amrouche) was appointed in his place in March 2025.[^cite_note-34]

## Team image

### Kit

In 2001, after adopting the new [flag of Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Rwanda), The Federation *(FERWAFA)* changed the color of the team kit. The new team kit consists of a yellow jersey, blue shorts and green socks for home matches, while their away kit is either all white or all blue. [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) has generally been the manufacturer for the Rwandan team since 2001.[^cite_note-35] However, between 2004 and 2009, Rwanda used L-sport as their outfitter, and in 2015 the side started wearing kit provided by AMS, an emerging Australian supplier.

### Names

Under the official [FIFA Trigramme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FIFA_country_codes) the team's name is abbreviated as **RWA**; this acronym is used by [FIFA](11049.md), the [CAF](715265.md) and the [CECAFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_East_and_Central_Africa_Football_Associations) to identify the team in official competitions.[^cite_note-36] However the team was more commonly known as the **RR**, the acronym for the country's official name, *Repubulika y'u Rwanda* or *République du Rwanda*, which the local press used when they referred to the team as the RR XI. The national team is often referred to as *Amavubi* (The [Wasps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasp)).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]
