**Alamogordo** ([/ˌæləməˈɡɔːrdoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is a city in and the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Otero County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otero_County,_New_Mexico), [New Mexico](21649.md), United States. A city in the [Tularosa Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tularosa_Basin) of the [Chihuahuan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chihuahuan_Desert), it is bordered on the east by the [Sacramento Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Mountains_\(New_Mexico\)) and to the west by [Holloman Air Force Base](1092369.md). The population was 31,384 as of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census). Alamogordo is widely known for its connection with the 1945 [Trinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_\(nuclear_test\)) test, which was the first-ever explosion of an [atomic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bomb).

Humans have lived in the Alamogordo area for at least 11,000 years. The present settlement, established in 1898 to support the construction of the [El Paso and Northeastern Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso_and_Northeastern_Railroad), is an early example of a [planned community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_community). The city was incorporated in 1912. Tourism became an important economic factor with the creation of [White Sands National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_National_Park) in 1933, which is still one of the biggest attractions of the city today. During the 1950s and 1960s, Alamogordo was an unofficial center for research on pilot safety and the development of the United States' space program.

Alamogordo is a [charter city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_city) with a [council-manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council-manager) form of government. City government provides a large number of recreational and leisure facilities for its citizens, including a large park in the center of the city, many smaller parks scattered through the city, a golf course, [Alameda Park Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alameda_Park_Zoo), a network of walking paths, [Alamogordo Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamogordo_Public_Library), and a senior citizens' center. [Gerald Champion Regional Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Champion_Regional_Medical_Center) is a nonprofit shared military/civilian facility that is also the hospital for Holloman Air Force Base.

## History



Aerial image of Alamogordo in 1951

For the history of aerospace sites outside of the city, see [White Sands Space Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_Space_Harbor), [White Sands Missile Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_Missile_Range), and [Holloman Air Force Base](1092369.md).

[Tularosa Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tularosa_Basin) has been inhabited for at least 11,000 years. There are signs of previous inhabitants in the area such as the [Clovis culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clovis_culture), the [Folsom culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folsom_culture), the peoples of the [Archaic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaic_period_in_the_Americas), and the [Formative stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formative_stage).[^cite_note-9] The [Mescalero Apache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mescalero_Apache) were already living in the Tularosa Basin when the Spanish came in 1534, and Mescalero oral history says they have always lived there.[^cite_note-State_Historian_Mescalero-10] In 1719, the Spanish built a chapel at [La Luz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Luz,_New_Mexico) (about 5 miles (8.0 km) from the future site of Alamogordo), although La Luz was not settled until about 1860.[^cite_note-Julyan-11][^cite_note-Gilbert_Territorial_Years-12]<sup>: 167 </sup>

The city of Alamogordo was founded in June 1898, when the [El Paso and Northeastern Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso_and_Northeastern_Railroad), headed by Charles Bishop Eddy, extended the railway to the town.[^cite_note-Centennial-13]<sup>: 4, 6–7 </sup> Eddy influenced the design of the community, which included large, wide thoroughfares and tree-lined irrigation canals.[^cite_note-14] Charles Eddy's brother, John Arthur Eddy, named the new city. He created a [neologism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neologism) adapted from the Spanish words for "large/fat cottonwood"[^cite_note-15] after a grove of stout [cottonwoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populus_sect._Aegiros) he remembered from the [Pecos River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecos_River) area.[^cite_note-Centennial-13]<sup>: x–1 </sup> However, the word "Alamogordo" was not used in the Spanish language.[^cite_note-16] When Alamogordo was laid out in 1898, the east–west streets were given numerical designations, while north–south streets were named after states. The present-day White Sands Boulevard was then called Pennsylvania Avenue.[^cite_note-Gilbert_Territorial_Years-12]<sup>: 42, 44–45 </sup>

With the creation of [White Sands National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_National_Park) in 1934, tourism began.[^cite_note-Centennial-13]<sup>: 53 </sup>

The [Works Progress Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Works_Progress_Administration), a government program created in 1935 in response to the Great Depression, was responsible for the construction of several government buildings in Alamogordo. These include the Otero County Administration Building at 1101 New York Avenue, a [Pueblo style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pueblo_Revival_Style_architecture) building originally constructed as the main U.S. Post Office in 1938. The building is listed in the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places). The main entrance portico features frescoes by [Peter Hurd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hurd), which were completed in 1942.[^cite_note-17]

In July 1941, the [Alamogordo Bombing and Gunnery Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamogordo_Bombing_and_Gunnery_Range) was established.[^cite_note-18]

The Post Office moved out in 1961, and the building was used by a succession of federal agencies and was known as the Federal Building. The last federal agency to occupy it was the [United States Forest Service](42652.md) who used it as the headquarters of the [Lincoln National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_National_Forest) until October 2008, when that agency moved to a newly constructed building.[^cite_note-Garcia_Forest_Service-19][^cite_note-20] In February 2009, ownership of the building was transferred to [Otero County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otero_County,_New_Mexico) government and many government offices were moved from the Courthouse to its new Administration Building.[^cite_note-London_Forest_Service-21][^cite_note-22]

In 1983, [Atari, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari,_Inc.) [buried](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_video_game_burial) more than 700,000 unsold [Atari 2600](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_2600) video game cartridges in Alamogordo's landfill.[^cite_note-23] Most notably, Atari discarded many copies of the unpopular *[E.T. the Extra-Terrestrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E.T._the_Extra-Terrestrial_\(video_game\))*. This event was often believed to be an urban legend, until it was confirmed by Atari and excavations at the landfill.[^cite_note-smithsonian-24]

Alamogordo briefly made international news in late 2001 when Christ Community Church held a public [book burning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_burning) of books in the [Harry Potter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Potter) series, and several other series, on December 30.[^cite_note-Matise2-25][^cite_note-BBC-26][^cite_note-NYT2002-27]

## Geography

As of 2010, Alamogordo had a total area of 19.3 square miles (50.0 km<sup>2</sup>), all land.[^cite_note-28] The city is located on the western flank of the [Sacramento Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento_Mountains_\(New_Mexico\)) and on the eastern edge of the [Tularosa Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tularosa_Basin). It lies within the [Rio Grande rift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Grande_rift)[^cite_note-29] and in the northernmost part of the [Chihuahuan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chihuahuan_Desert).[^cite_note-Jaeger-30]<sup>: 36 </sup> Tectonic activity is low in the Tularosa Basin.[^cite_note-Geologic_Hydrologic-31] Plants native to the area are typical of the southern New Mexico foothills and include [creosote bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creosote_bush), [mesquite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesquite), [saltbush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saltbush), [cottonwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populus_sect._Aegiros), [desert willow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_willow), and many species of [cactus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cactus) and [yucca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucca).[^cite_note-32]

The Tularosa Basin is an [endorheic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endorheic_basin), or closed, basin; that is, no water flows out of it.[^cite_note-Geologic_Hydrologic-31][^cite_note-33] Because of this and because of the geology of the region, water in the basin is [hard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_water): it has very high [total dissolved solids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_dissolved_solids) concentrations, in excess of 3,000 mg/L.[^cite_note-Geologic_Hydrologic-31][^cite_note-34] The Brackish Groundwater National Desalination Research Facility, a [Bureau of Reclamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Reclamation) laboratory doing research and development on [desalination](156787.md) of [brackish water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brackish_water), is located in Alamogordo.[^cite_note-35] The gypsum crystals of [White Sands National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_National_Park) are formed in [Lake Lucero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Lucero). Water drains from the mountains carrying dissolved gypsum and collects in Lake Lucero. After the water dries, the winds pick up the gypsum crystals and distribute them over the basin.[^cite_note-Jaeger-30]<sup>: 37 </sup>

### Climate

Alamogordo has a [semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BSk*), bordering a [desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) (*BWk*), with hot summers and mild winters. Rainfall is low and usually confined to [the monsoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Monsoon) from July to September, when half a typical year's rainfall of 10.63 in (270.0 mm) will occur – although December 1991 did see 5.45 in (138.4 mm). The wettest calendar year has been 1941 with 21.87 in (555.5 mm) and the driest 1952 with 4.85 in (123.2 mm), while the wettest month on record has been September 1941 when 6.94 in (176.3 mm) fell. September 1941 also saw the largest daily rainfall at Alamogordo with 2.60 in (66.0 mm) falling on the 22nd of that month.

Temperatures outside of monsoonal storms are very hot during the summer: 89.1 days exceed 90 °F (32.2 °C) and temperatures as high as 110 °F (43.3 °C) occurred on June 22, 1981, and July 8, 1951. During the winter, days are very mild and sunny, but nights are cold, with 32 °F (0 °C) reached on 55.1 mornings during an average winter, although only ten mornings have ever fallen to or below 0 °F (−17.8 °C),[^cite_note-36] with the coldest temperature recorded at Alamogordo being −13 °F (−25.0 °C) during a major cold wave on February 3, 2011. Snow is very rare, with a mean of no more than 4.1 in (10 cm) and a median very close to zero. The most snowfall in one month was 10.0 in (25 cm) in December 1960.

### 2020 census

As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), Alamogordo had a population of 30,898. The median age was 37.9 years. 21.9% of residents were under the age of 18 and 19.4% of residents were 65 years of age or older. For every 100 females there were 99.3 males, and for every 100 females age 18 and over there were 98.5 males age 18 and over.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-40][^cite_note-Census2020PL-41]

98.1% of residents lived in urban areas, while 1.9% lived in rural areas.[^cite_note-Census2020DHC-42]

There were 13,377 households in Alamogordo, of which 26.4% had children under the age of 18 living in them. Of all households, 39.8% were married-couple households, 24.1% were households with a male householder and no spouse or partner present, and 28.7% were households with a female householder and no spouse or partner present. About 34.3% of all households were made up of individuals and 13.5% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-40]

There were 15,118 housing units, of which 11.5% were vacant. The homeowner vacancy rate was 3.5% and the rental vacancy rate was 9.3%.[^cite_note-Census2020DP-40]

### 2000 census

As of the 2000 census, there were 13,704 households, and 9,728 families residing in the city. There were 15,920 housing units. The racial makeup of the city was 70.4% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_\(U.S._Census\)); 5.6% [African American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 1.1% [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_American_\(U.S._Census\)), 1.5% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_\(U.S._Census\)), 0.2% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_\(U.S._Census\)), 12.1% from [some other race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), and 4.2% from two or more races. [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_\(U.S._Census\)) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) of any race were 32.0% of the population.[^cite_note-2000_New_Mexico_DP-1-43]<sup>: 38 </sup>

There were 13,704 households, out of which 36.3% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 55.6% were married couples living together, 11.7% had a female householder with no husband present, and 29.0% were non-families. 25.2% of all households were made up of individuals, and 8.8% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.57 and the average family size was 3.07.[^cite_note-2000_New_Mexico_DP-1-43]<sup>: 38 </sup>

In the city the population was spread out, with 28.7% under the age of 18, 9.2% from 18 to 24, 29.7% from 25 to 44, 19.9% from 45 to 64, and 12.7% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 34 years. For every 100 females, there were 97.6 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 94.1 males.[^cite_note-2000_New_Mexico_DP-1-43]<sup>: 39 </sup>

In 1999, the median income for a household in the city was \$30,928, and the median income for a family was \$35,673. Males had a median income of \$28,163 versus \$18,860 for females. The per capita income for the city was \$14,662. About 13.2% of families and 16.5% of the population were below the poverty line, including 23.9% of those under age 18 and 11.8% of those age 65 or over.[^cite_note-2000_Alamogordo_DP-3-44]

### German community, 1992–2019



[Deutsche Schule Alamogordo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Schule_Alamogordo), a school for children of German Air Force service members and employees at the German Air Force Flying Training Center at Holloman Air Force Base

Previously Alamogordo had a German community due to the presence of the [German Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Air_Force) at [Holloman Air Force Base](1092369.md); in 1992 that air force made Holloman its main pilot training center in the United States.[^cite_note-45] Holloman was chosen due to its weather conditions.[^cite_note-WarStrains-46] There was a subdivision called "Little Germany" with houses that had German-style electrical outlets. The [Deutsche Schule Alamogordo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Schule_Alamogordo) educated German children, as did the local schools. Additionally area supermarkets had [German cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_cuisine).[^cite_note-47]

By 1999, there were about 1,110 German dependents and 900 German military personnel in Alamogordo.[^cite_note-48] By 2003 there were about 2,000 Germans in Alamogordo. That year there were tensions between Americans and Germans since Germany chose not to join the U.S. in the [Iraq War](5043324.md).[^cite_note-WarStrains-46]

In 2019, the German military withdrew from the base.[^cite_note-49]

Until the German Air Force left, [Oktoberfest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oktoberfest) was celebrated annually in late September, hosted by the German Air Force at Holloman Air Force Base. The public was invited, and shuttle buses ran between Alamogordo and the base.[^cite_note-50]

## Economy

Alamogordo is the economic center of Otero County,[^cite_note-Chamber_Community_Profile-51] with nearly half the Otero County population living within the city limits. Alamogordo today has very little manufacturing and has a primarily service and retail economy, driven by tourism, a large nearby military installation and a concentration of military retirees.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] In 2006 the per capita income in Otero County was \$22,377 versus per capita income in [New Mexico](21649.md) of \$29,346.[^cite_note-54]

### Economic history



Retail shops on New York Avenue



Buildings in Alamogordo: PreCheck headquarters, Aviator 10 Theater, and the US Department of Agriculture / Forest Service building.

Alamogordo was founded as a company town to support the building of the [El Paso and Northeastern Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso_and_Northeastern_Railroad),[^cite_note-Centennial-13]<sup>: 2 </sup> a portion of the transcontinental railway that was being constructed in the late 19th century. Initially its main industry was timbering for railroad ties.[^cite_note-Centennial-13]<sup>: 1 </sup> The railroad founders were also eager to found a major town that would persist after the railroad was completed; they formed the Alamogordo Improvement Company to develop the area,[^cite_note-Centennial-13]<sup>: 5 </sup> making Alamogordo an early example of a [planned community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planned_community). The Alamogordo Improvement Company owned all the land, platted the streets, built the first houses and commercial buildings, donated land for a college, and placed a restrictive covenant on each deed prohibiting the manufacture, distribution, or sale of intoxicating liquor.[^cite_note-Centennial-13]<sup>: 1, 9, 13, 44 </sup>

Tourism became an important part of the local economy from the creation of [White Sands National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_National_Park) in 1934.[^cite_note-Centennial-13]<sup>: 53 </sup> Construction began on the Alamogordo Army Airfield (the present-day [Holloman Air Force Base](1092369.md)) in 1942, and the Federal government has been a strong presence in Alamogordo ever since.[^cite_note-Centennial-13]<sup>: 39, 53 </sup> Education has also been an important part of the local economy. In addition to the [local school system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamogordo_Public_School_District), Alamogordo is home to the [New Mexico School for the Blind and Visually Impaired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico_School_for_the_Blind_and_Visually_Impaired), founded in 1903, and a branch of [New Mexico State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico_State_University) founded in 1958.[^cite_note-Centennial-13]<sup>: 44, 58 </sup> The largest non-government employer in the city is the Gerald Champion Regional Medical Center with 650 employees in 2008.[^cite_note-55]

### Military impact

Holloman Air Force Base, located approximately 3 miles (4.8 km) west of the city limits, is the largest employer of Alamogordo residents, and has a major effect on the local economy. According to some estimates, Holloman accounts for half of the Alamogordo economy.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] According to the 49th Fighter Wing Public Affairs office, as of January 2008 Holloman directly employs 6,111 personnel with a gross payroll of \$266 million. It indirectly creates another 2,047 jobs with a payroll of \$77 million. The estimated amount spent in the community, including payroll, construction projects, supplies, services, health care, and education, is \$482 million.[^cite_note-Holloman_Economic_Impact_Statement-58]

An estimated 6,700 military retirees live in the area. Counting both [USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF) and [German Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Air_Force) personnel there are 1,383 active military and 1,641 military dependents living on base and 2,765 active military and 2,942 military dependents living off base.[^cite_note-Holloman_Economic_Impact_Statement-58] After 27 years of training at Holloman, the German Air Force left in 2019. They relocated their pilot training to [Sheppard Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheppard_Air_Force_Base) in Texas.[^cite_note-59]

[Future Combat Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Combat_Systems) is a wide-ranging modernization project of the [US Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Army). Much of the work will be done at [Fort Bliss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Bliss), with some at [White Sands Missile Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_Missile_Range) and some at Holloman Air Force Base. Alamogordo is expected to get some economic benefit due to its proximity to these three bases.[^cite_note-60]

### Economic development

Otero County Economic Development Council is a nonprofit organization founded in 1984. Its focus has generally been on job creation and recruiting and expanding businesses in [Otero County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otero_County,_New_Mexico), including helping them satisfy business regulations in New Mexico and lining up funding.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] Its role expanded in 2000, when Alamogordo passed an Economic Development [Gross Receipts Tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_receipts_tax). OCEDC continues to work to attract businesses, but now it also helps develop the incentive packages that will be paid by the new tax, and a portion of the tax receipts go to fund OCEDC's operating expenses.[^cite_note-63] Formal economic development plans have been adopted by Alamogordo[^cite_note-64] and by Otero County.[^cite_note-65]

OCEDC has recruited several new employers by using financial incentives. A [1-800-Flowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-800-Flowers) [call center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_center) opened in November 2001 and received \$1.25 million in city rent abatements, a 50% reduction in property taxes from Otero County, and \$940,000 in plant training funds from the State of New Mexico.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] A Sunbaked Biscuits cookie factory opened in 2006 and received \$800,000 in job-training incentives from the state.[^cite_note-ADN_Homans_visit-68] When the company went out of business in 2007, Marietta Baking took over the cookie factory and received interest-free loans, job-training incentives, and partial forgiveness of indebtedness for job creation.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] A branch office of PreCheck Inc., a company performing background checks of health-care workers, opened in 2006. PreCheck received \$2.4 million in high-wage job creation tax credits, \$1.5 million in job-training subsidies, \$1.5 million in capital outlay money for roads and infrastructure, a \$625,000 allocation from City of Alamogordo for upgrading sewer lines in the area, and 20.8 acres of land from Heritage Group, a developer.[^cite_note-71]

The Otero County Film Office,[^cite_note-72] an office of Otero County Economic Development Council, promotes film-making in Otero County by publicizing potential locations in the county and New Mexico's film financial incentive programs[^cite_note-73] and by recruiting [extras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_\(actor\)) for film productions. It sponsors the Desert Light Film Competition for middle and high school students to encourage learning about the film industry.[^cite_note-74] The 2007 film *[Transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformers_\(film\))* spent \$5.5 million in New Mexico and \$1 million in Alamogordo.[^cite_note-75]

## Government



Alamogordo City Hall houses most of the city government administrative functions, as well as the Commission Chambers and the Municipal Court.

Alamogordo was incorporated in 1912.[^cite_note-Centennial-13]<sup>: 136 </sup> It is a [charter city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_city) (also called a home rule city[^cite_note-118]), and the charter is included as Part I of the Code of Ordinances.[^cite_note-Municipal_Code-119] It has a [Council-manager government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council-manager_government) form of government (called Commission/Manager in New Mexico).[^cite_note-Municipal_Code-119]<sup>: Article II </sup> There are seven city commissioners, each elected from a district within the city, on staggered 4-year terms.[^cite_note-Municipal_Code-119]<sup>: Article VII </sup> The city manager is considered the chief executive officer of the city and is tasked to enforce and implement the City Council's directives and policy.[^cite_note-120] The mayor is a member of the City Council. As of 2018, Richard Boss holds the position of mayor.[^cite_note-121]

Alamogordo's fiscal year ends on June 30 each year; thus Fiscal Year 2008 runs from July 1, 2007, through June 30, 2008. The FY 2008 budget projects income of \$61,454,402[^cite_note-FY_2008_budget-122]<sup>: 7 </sup> and expenditures of \$73,655,777.[^cite_note-FY_2008_budget-122]<sup>: 5 </sup> Sources of City government income and their percentages of the whole were:[^cite_note-FY_2008_budget-122]<sup>: 7 </sup> [gross receipts tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_receipts_tax) (31%), miscellaneous (23%), [grants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_\(money\)) (22%), [user fees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User_fees) (19%), and [property tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) (5%).

It has a religious format, and a weekly local news magazine broadcast Thursday through Saturday.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137] Cable television service is provided by Baja Broadband.[^cite_note-Chamber_Relocation-138]

There are two commercial radio broadcast companies, WP Broadcasting and Burt Broadcasting; each operates several stations in several formats.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141] There are two "listener-supported" radio stations that do not carry advertising but depend on sponsorships and donations. [KLAG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KLAG) has a gospel music [radio format](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_format) and some live coverage of local events, including many [remote broadcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_broadcast) from civic events.[^cite_note-142] [KALH-LP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KALH-LP) is a low-power FM station that carries a [variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(radio\)) radio format, network news on the hour, and local news on some hours.[^cite_note-143] Neither station is an [NPR](29697232.md) affiliate. The local NPR outlet is [KRWG-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KRWG_\(FM\)) in Las Cruces, which reaches Alamogordo through a local [relay transmitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relay_transmitter).[^cite_note-144]

Several major motion pictures were filmed in or near Alamogordo. The 2007 film *[Transformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformers_\(film\))* was shot primarily at [White Sands Missile Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_Missile_Range), with additional filming at Holloman Air Force Base, both in the Alamogordo area.[^cite_note-145] Its 2009 sequel *[Transformers: Revenge of the Fallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transformers:_Revenge_of_the_Fallen)* also prominently featured these two military bases.[^cite_note-146] The 2009 film *[Year One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_One_\(film\))* was shot partly at [White Sands National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_National_Park), near Alamogordo.[^cite_note-147] Alamogordo was one of the fourteen cities profiled in the 2005 documentary *14 Days in America*.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] The Otero County Film Office maintains a list[^cite_note-150] of films shot partly or wholly in Alamogordo and Otero County.

In May 2013, Alamogordo's City Commission approved a deal for Canada-based film production company Fuel Industries to excavate the Atari landfill site.[^cite_note-151] Fuel Entertainment partnered with Xbox Entertainment Studios and Lightbox to make [a documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari:_Game_Over) about the massive 1983 [Atari video game burial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari_video_game_burial), said to be one of the gaming culture's greatest urban legends. On April 26, 2014, video game archaeologists began sifting through years of trash from the old Alamogordo landfill.[^cite_note-Alamogordo_Daily_News-152] The first batch of E.T. games was discovered after about three hours of digging,[^cite_note-153] and hundreds more were found in the mounds of trash and dirt scooped by a backhoe.[^cite_note-154] In the deal between the City of Alamogordo and Fuel Entertainment regarding the excavation, Fuel Entertainment was to be given 250 games or 10 percent of what was found.[^cite_note-Alamogordo_Daily_News-152]

## Infrastructure

### Major highways

The major intercity surface routes from Alamogordo are [U.S. Highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Highway) 54, 70, and 82, all of which are four-lane roads. The major north–south street within the city is White Sands Boulevard. The Charlie T. Lee Memorial Relief Route, which is designated as U.S. Route 54 and 70, is a [bypass road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bypass_\(road\)) constructed to the west of the city in 2001 to relieve congestion on White Sands Boulevard.[^cite_note-Relief_Route_PR-155][^cite_note-156]

[U.S. Route 70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_70_in_New_Mexico) and [U.S. Route 54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_54_\(New_Mexico\)) traverses through the north and south ends of the city.[^cite_note-Relief_Route_PR-155] At the south end of the city, White Sands Boulevard is a major named street that merges into U.S. Route 54/Charles T. Lee Memorial Relief Route, running south to [El Paso, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Paso,_Texas). In the south part of the city, U.S. Route 70 splits from U.S. Route 54 in a southwestern direction towards [Holloman Air Force Base](1092369.md), [White Sands National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_National_Park), [White Sands Missile Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_Missile_Range), and [Las Cruces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Cruces,_New_Mexico). At the north end of the city, White Sands Boulevard and the Charles T. Lee Memorial Relief Route become a merged U.S. Route 54 and U.S. Route 70 running north to [Tularosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tularosa,_New_Mexico). [U.S. Route 82](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_82_\(New_Mexico\)) starts at the same point and runs east to [Cloudcroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloudcroft,_New_Mexico) and the mountain communities of [Otero County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otero_County,_New_Mexico), and then to [Artesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artesia,_New_Mexico). Meanwhile, in Tularosa, U.S. 70 and U.S. 54 both split in which U.S. 70 heads east through the mountains, and towards [Ruidoso](125916.md) and [Roswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roswell,_New_Mexico), while U.S. 54 heads north towards [Carrizozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrizozo,_New_Mexico) and keeps going north until it heads east again starting in [Vaughn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaughn,_New_Mexico).

### Other transportation



Alamogordo-White Sands Regional Airport provides scheduled commercial service and is used for general aviation.

[Alamogordo-White Sands Regional Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamogordo-White_Sands_Regional_Airport) is the municipal airport located in the Alamogordo area. It is primarily used for [general aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_aviation).[^cite_note-157] There is no longer scheduled commercial service from [New Mexico Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico_Airlines), previously operated under a subsidy from the [Essential Air Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essential_Air_Service) program.[^cite_note-158]

In 2008, [Greyhound Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines) offered intercity bus service to Alamogordo.[^cite_note-159] As of 2017, there was a daily shuttle van service between Alamogordo and [El Paso International Airport](269621.md).[^cite_note-160]

Z-Trans is the mass transit system, providing [paratransit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paratransit) and scheduled service within the city center and to White Sands Mall, Holloman Air Force Base and Inn of the Mountain Gods Resort & Casino in [Mescalero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mescalero,_New_Mexico). Z-Trans is unusual in that it is privately owned (by Zia Therapy Center, a non-profit), although it does get some local and state subsidies.[^cite_note-161]

[Union Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific) provides railroad freight services. Currently there is no intercity passenger train service.

As of 2003, the Alamogordo city government was building a network of [bike routes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bike_route) and walking routes. More information and maps are in the Alamogordo Comprehensive Plan.[^cite_note-Comprehensive_Plan-162]<sup>: 42–44 </sup> In 2005, the [New Mexico](21649.md) Rails-to-Trails Association operated a [Rails to Trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rails_to_Trails) project to convert old railroad beds to walking trails. Its trail system in Otero County, the Cloud Climbing Rail Trail, is planned to eventually surround Alamogordo.[^cite_note-Comprehensive_Plan-162]<sup>: 45 </sup>[^cite_note-163]

### Utilities

Electric power is supplied within the city by [PNM Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNM_Resources).[^cite_note-Chamber_Relocation-138] PNM also provides electrical power in the [Tularosa Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tularosa_Basin), while Otero County Electric Cooperative, a member cooperative of Tri-State Generation and Transmission Association and of [Touchstone Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touchstone_Energy), serves other areas of the county.[^cite_note-164] Natural gas is supplied within the city by [New Mexico Gas Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico_Gas_Company).[^cite_note-Chamber_Relocation-138] [Severn Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severn_Trent) operates both the water and [sewage treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage_treatment) facilities for the City of Alamogordo. Severn Trent maintains all water storage facilities, booster pump stations, city wells and treats the waste water to be re-used by the city to water the parks, Desert Lakes Golf Course and is sold to construction companies for dust control. Rural houses have individual wells.[^cite_note-165]

Alamogordo has a [dark sky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark-sky_movement) ordinance to reduce the amount of [light pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_pollution) in the night skies.[^cite_note-Municipal_Code-119]<sup>: Article 31 </sup> The ordinance was passed in 1990 to promote the growth and scientific productivity of [Apache Point Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apache_Point_Observatory).[^cite_note-166] City streetlights are high-pressure [sodium vapor lamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_vapor_lamp).[^cite_note-167]

### Healthcare



Gerald Champion Regional Medical Center serves Alamogordo and Holloman Air Force Base.

[Gerald Champion Regional Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerald_Champion_Regional_Medical_Center) is a private not-for-profit 99-bed [general hospital](18994221.md) that serves the Alamogordo area. The hospital is a shared military/civilian facility that is also the hospital for nearby Holloman Air Force Base.[^cite_note-168]

The Otero County Community Health Council prepares a detailed health profile[^cite_note-OCCHC_Health_Profile-169] each year with many facts and figures about health in Otero County. Otero County is ranked in the middle of most health rankings within the state. New Mexico is near the bottom of most national rankings; for example, it was 38th in the United Health Foundation 2007 report, but has been slowly improving (it was 40th in 2005).[^cite_note-170]

## Notable people

Among scientists, [Edward Condon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Condon), a [physicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicist) and a past director of [National Institute of Standards and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Institute_of_Standards_and_Technology), was born in Alamogordo.[^cite_note-171] [Alan Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Hale_\(astronomer\)), an [astronomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) and co-discoverer of [Comet Hale-Bopp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comet_Hale-Bopp); was raised in Alamogordo and resides in nearby [Cloudcroft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloudcroft,_New_Mexico).[^cite_note-172]

Among politicians, [Edwin L. Mechem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_L._Mechem), a past [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_New_Mexico) and [United States Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senator) from New Mexico, was born in Alamogordo,[^cite_note-173] as was [Cindy Chavez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cindy_Chavez), a past member of the [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_council) of [San Jose, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California).[^cite_note-174]

[Monica Kim Garza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Kim_Garza) is a painter who was born in Alamogordo.[^cite_note-175]

[Edward Lee Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Lee_Howard), a former [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA) case agent who allegedly gave classified material to the Russians and later defected to the Soviet Union, was an Alamogordo native.

In sports, professional soccer player [Adam Frye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Frye), jockey [Donna Barton Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Barton_Brothers), and former professional American football cornerback [Conrad Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_Hamilton) were all born in Alamogordo. Professional golf brothers [Brad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_Bryant) and [Bart Bryant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bart_Bryant) are from Alamogordo.

[Alexis Duprey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexis_Duprey), crowned [Miss New Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_New_Mexico) in 2013 and again in 2015, is from Alamogordo. [Mai Shanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mai_Shanley), who became [Miss USA 1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_USA_1984), represented the city as [Miss New Mexico USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_New_Mexico_USA).

The lead singer of the 2020 Grammy-nominated [Black Pumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Pumas), [Eric Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Burton_\(singer-songwriter\)), grew up in Alamogordo and graduated from Alamogordo High School.
