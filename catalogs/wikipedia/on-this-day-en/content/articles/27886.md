**Suffolk** ([/ˈsʌfək/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/04/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Suffolk.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Suffolk.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/04/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Suffolk.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Vealhurl-Suffolk.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*SUF-ək*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a [ceremonial county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_county) in the [East of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_of_England) and [East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Anglia). It is bordered by [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk) to the north, the [North Sea](21179.md) to the east, [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex) to the south, and [Cambridgeshire](6288.md) to the west. [Ipswich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipswich) is the largest settlement.

The county has an area of 3,798 km<sup>2</sup> (1,466 sq mi) and had an estimated population of 786,231 in 2024. Ipswich is in the south of the county, and its other principal towns include [Lowestoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowestoft) in the north-east, [Haverhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haverhill,_Suffolk) in the south-west, and [Bury St Edmunds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury_St_Edmunds) in the west. For [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_England) purposes Suffolk is a [non-metropolitan county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county) with five [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_district).

The Suffolk coastline, which includes parts of the [Suffolk & Essex Coast & Heaths National Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_&_Essex_Coast_&_Heaths_National_Landscape), is a complex [habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat), formed by [London Clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Clay) and [crag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crag_Group) underlain by [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk) and therefore susceptible to [erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion). It contains several deep [estuaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuaries), including those of the rivers [Blyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Blyth,_Suffolk), [Deben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Deben), [Orwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Orwell), [Stour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Stour,_Suffolk), and [Alde/Ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Alde); the latter is 25.5 km (15.8 mi) long and separated from the North Sea by [Orford Ness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orford_Ness), a large [spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spit_\(landform\)). Large parts of the coast are backed by [heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath) and wetland habitats, such as [Sandlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandlings).[^cite_note-4] The northeast of the county contains part of [the Broads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Broads), a network of rivers and lakes which is a national park. Inland, the landscape is flat and gently undulating, and contains part of [Thetford Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thetford_Forest) on the Norfolk border and [Dedham Vale National Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedham_Vale_National_Landscape) on the Essex border.

It is also known for its extensive farming and has largely [arable land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arable_land). [Newmarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newmarket,_Suffolk) is known for [horse racing](74711.md), and [Felixstowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felixstowe) is one of the largest [container ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Container_port) in Europe.[^cite_note-5]

## History

Main article: [History of Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Suffolk)

Artefacts dating to around 70,000 years ago found at [Pakefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakefield) and [Beeches Pit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beeches_Pit) are among the earliest evidence of human activity in northern Europe. In the [Neolithic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic_period) and [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) burial mounds, [hillforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Camp) and [causewayed enclosures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freston_causewayed_enclosure) were built reflecting Suffolk's role in prehistoric trade and agriculture.

At the start of the [Roman period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain) most of Suffolk was, along with [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk) inhabited by the [Iceni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceni) tribe.[^cite_note-6] The [Trinobantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinobantes) inhabited parts of southern Suffolk, as well as [Essex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essex). The Iceni unsuccessfully [revolted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boudican_revolt) under [Boudica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boudica) in AD 60–61. Although Suffolk remained predominantly rural in the Roman period there were [villas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Hill_Roman_villa), small towns and pottery kilns. By the late 4th century, however, archaeological evidence suggests significant depopulation in parts of [East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Anglia) possibly linked to [coastal raiding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saxon_Shore).[^cite_note-Dark39-7] [Anglian settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglian_settlement) followed in the 5th and 6th centuries with a distinctive Germanic [material culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material_culture) largely replacing a [Romano-British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romano-British) one.[^cite_note-8]

The area was mostly or entirely governed by the [Kingdom of East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_East_Anglia), with studies of trade patterns and subinfeudation suggesting that the [Stour Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Stour,_Suffolk) of what is now south-west Suffolk was part of the [Kingdom of the East Saxons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_East_Saxons).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-Kingdom_2015-10] Suffolk's most celebrated Anglo-Saxon site, [Sutton Hoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton_Hoo) was an elaborate royal [ship burial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship_burial) – probably that of the East Anglian King [Rædwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A6dwald_of_East_Anglia),[^cite_note-11] whose son [Sigeberht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigeberht_of_East_Anglia) [Christianised East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianisation_of_East_Anglia).

In the medieval period Suffolk was shaped by successive waves of conquest. It became part of the [Danelaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danelaw) following [Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age) incursions. The county of Suffolk was formed in the 10th or 11th Century.[^cite_note-Kingdom_2015-10] Suffolk was reorganised under Norman feudalism after 1066, with castles at [Framlingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framlingham_Castle), [Clare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Castle), and [Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_Castle). The [abbey at Bury St Edmunds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_at_Bury_St_Edmunds) became one of the wealthiest and most powerful religious institutions in medieval England. Later centuries saw Suffolk prosper from the cloth and [wool trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_English_wool_trade), with towns such as [Lavenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavenham) and [Hadleigh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadleigh,_Suffolk) flourishing in the 15th and 16th centuries. The county was also a site of Puritan and parliamentary strength during the [English Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Civil_War).

In modern times Suffolk's economy evolved with agriculture, [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_industry), and maritime trade, while the 19th century saw the development of industry at [Ipswich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipswich) and [Lowestoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowestoft). Coastal erosion and the decline of traditional industries changed the county's fortunes, but its historic sites, churches, and coastal landscapes continue to shape its identity.

A survey in 2020 named Suffolk the third best place in the UK for aspiring [archaeologists](18951655.md), and showed that the area was especially rich in finds from the Roman period, with over 1500 objects found in the preceding year.[^cite_note-12]

A formative episode in English archeology was in 1797 when [John Frere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Frere) found flint [hand axes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_axe), now known to date back 400,000 years in the [Hoxne Brick Pit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoxne_Brick_Pit), in a deposit twelve feet deep, and commented that "the situation in which these weapons were found may tempt us to refer them to a very remote period indeed; even beyond that of the present world".[^cite_note-cite-13][^cite_note-14] This is the earliest recognition that hand axes were made by early humans, and was over sixty years before the antiquity of humanity was widely appreciated. One of Frere's hand axes, which was probably a general cutting tool, is held in the [British Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Museum). The site also provides the type deposits of the [Hoxnian Stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoxnian_Stage), an [interglacial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interglacial) between around 474,000 and 374,000 years ago, which is named after the site.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-cite-13]

[West Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Suffolk_\(district\)), like nearby [East Cambridgeshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Cambridgeshire), is renowned for [archaeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological) finds from the [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age), the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), and the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age). Bronze Age [artefacts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artifact_\(archaeology\)) have been found in the area between [Mildenhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mildenhall,_Suffolk) and [West Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Row), in [Eriswell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eriswell) and in [Lakenheath](856919.md).[^cite_note-16]

In the east of the county is [Sutton Hoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton_Hoo), the site of one of England's most significant Anglo-Saxon archaeological finds, a ship burial containing a collection of treasures including a [sword of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sword_of_state), [helmet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton_Hoo_helmet), gold and silver bowls, jewellery and a [lyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyre).[^cite_note-17]

The [Hoxne Hoard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoxne_Hoard), to date the largest assembly of late [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) silver and gold discovered in Britain, was found near the village of [Hoxne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoxne) in 1992.

While carrying out surveys before installing a pipeline in 2014, archaeologists for [Anglian Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglian_Water) discovered nine skeletons and four cremation pits, at [Bardwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardwell,_Suffolk), [Barnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barnham,_Suffolk), [Pakenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakenham,_Suffolk) and [Rougham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rougham,_Suffolk), all near Bury St Edmunds. [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic), Bronze Age, Iron Age, Roman and [Medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval) items were also unearthed, along with the nine skeletons believed to be of the late or [Post-Roman Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Roman_Britain). Experts said the five-month project had recovered enough artefacts to fill half a shipping container, and that the discoveries had shed new light on their understanding of the development of small rural communities.[^cite_note-18]

In 2019 an excavation of a 4th-century [Roman burial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_burial) in [Great Whelnetham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Whelnetham) uncovered unusual burial practices. Of 52 skeletons found, a large number had been [decapitated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decapitated), which archaeologists claimed gave new insight into Roman traditions. The burial ground includes the remains of men, women and children who likely lived in a nearby settlement. The fact that up to 40% of the bodies were decapitated represents "quite a rare find".[^cite_note-19]

In July 2020, [metal detectorist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_detector) Luke Mahoney found 1,061 silver hammered coins, estimated to be worth £100,000, in Ipswich. The coins dated back to the 15th–17th century, according to experts.[^cite_note-20]

In 2020, archaeologists discovered a 7th-century [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon) cemetery with 17 cremations and 191 burials in [Oulton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oulton,_Suffolk), near Lowestoft. The graves contained the remains of men, women and children, as well as artefacts including small iron knives, silver pennies, wrist clasps, strings of [amber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber) and glass beads. According to Andrew Peachey, who carried out the excavations, the skeletons had mostly vanished because of the highly acidic soil. They were preserved as brittle shapes and "sand silhouettes".[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

## Governance

### Local government



The [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) of [Suffolk County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_County_Council)

Since 1974, Suffolk has been a two-tier non-metropolitan county, meaning the provision of local government services is divided between a county council and district councils.[^cite_note-\:0-23][^cite_note-24] The county has been divided into five districts since 2019: [Babergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babergh_District), [East Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Suffolk_\(district\)), [Ipswich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Ipswich), [Mid Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid_Suffolk), and [West Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Suffolk_District).[^cite_note-\:1-25][^cite_note-\:2-26] [Suffolk County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_County_Council) is based in Ipswich.

In March 2026, the government announced [local government reorganisation plans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upcoming_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England), which would have seen local government across Suffolk reorganised in 2028 into three [unitary authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England).[^cite_note-27] These proposals were withdrawn in September 2026.[^cite_note-28]

#### History

Historically, Suffolk was divided into four for the purposes of the [courts of quarter sessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_quarter_sessions), which met at [Beccles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beccles), [Bury St Edmunds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury_St_Edmunds), Ipswich and [Woodbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodbridge,_Suffolk).[^cite_note-29] In 1860, the Beccles, Ipswich and Woodbridge divisions were merged into an East Suffolk division, administered from Ipswich, and the Bury St Edmunds division became the West Suffolk division.[^cite_note-30] Under the [Local Government Act 1888](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1888), which established county councils, the two divisions formed the basis of the administrative counties of [East Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Suffolk_\(county\)) and [West Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Suffolk_\(county\)).[^cite_note-31]

On 1 April 1974, under the [Local Government Act 1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Act_1972), East Suffolk (with the exception of five parishes and parts of two more, which were transferred to Norfolk), the [county borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_borough) of Ipswich, and West Suffolk were merged to form a single [non-metropolitan county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county) of Suffolk.[^cite_note-\:0-23] The new county was originally divided into seven districts: Babergh, [Forest Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Heath), Ipswich, Mid Suffolk, [St Edmundsbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_St_Edmundsbury), [Suffolk Coastal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_Coastal), and [Waveney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waveney_District).[^cite_note-32] In 2019, Forest Heath and St Edmundsbury were merged to form [West Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Suffolk_\(district\)), and Waveney and Suffolk Coastal to form [East Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Suffolk_\(district\)).[^cite_note-\:1-25][^cite_note-\:2-26]

### Parliamentary constituencies

Further information: [Parliamentary constituencies in Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_constituencies_in_Suffolk)

Suffolk is divided into eight [parliamentary constituencies](1784582.md): one [borough constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_constituency) and seven [county constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_constituencies). Seven constituencies lie wholly within Suffolk, and [Waveney Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waveney_Valley) straddles the county border between Suffolk and [Norfolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk).

## Geography



River Stour at [Dedham Vale National Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dedham_Vale_National_Landscape)



Hand-drawn map of Suffolk by [Christopher Saxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Saxton) from 1573

Suffolk is also home to nature reserves, such as the [RSPB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RSPB) site at [Minsmere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsmere), and [Trimley Marshes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trimley_Marshes), a wetland under the protection of [Suffolk Wildlife Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_Wildlife_Trust). The clay plateau inland, deeply intercut by rivers, is often referred to as 'High Suffolk'.[^cite_note-33] The west of the county lies on more resistant [Cretaceous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous) [chalk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalk). This chalk is responsible for a sweeping tract of largely [downland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downland) landscapes that stretches from [Dorset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorset) in the south west to [Dover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dover) in the south east and north through [East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Anglia) to the [Yorkshire Wolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire_Wolds). The chalk is less easily [eroded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eroded) so forms the only significant hills in the county. The highest point in the county is [Great Wood Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Wood_Hill), with an elevation of 128 metres (420 ft).[^cite_note-34] The county flower is the [oxlip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxlip).[^cite_note-35]

According to estimates by the [Office for National Statistics](324029.md), the population of Suffolk in 2014 was 738,512, split almost evenly between males and females. Roughly 22% of the population was aged 65 or older, and 90.84% were [White British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_British).[^cite_note-36]

Historically, the county's population has mostly been employed as [agricultural workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture). An 1835 survey showed Suffolk to have 4,526 occupiers of land employing labourers, 1,121 occupiers not employing labourers, 33,040 labourers employed in agriculture, 676 employed in manufacture, 18,167 employed in retail trade or handicraft, 2,228 'capitalists, bankers etc.', 5,336 labourers (non-agricultural), 4,940 other males aged over 20, 2,032 male servants and 11,483 female servants.[^cite_note-37]

A traditional nickname for people from Suffolk is "Suffolk Fair-Maids", referring to the supposed beauty of its female inhabitants in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages).[^cite_note-38] Another is "Silly Suffolk", often assumed to be derived from the [Old English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_English) word *sælig* in the meaning "blessed", referring to the long history of Christianity in the county.[^cite_note-39] However, use of the term "Silly Suffolk" can actually be dated to no earlier than 1819, and its alleged medieval origins have been shown to be mythical.[^cite_note-40]

## Economy



The [Willis Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willis_Building_\(Ipswich\)) in Ipswich, a landmark office building in the town

The majority of agriculture in Suffolk is either [agronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agronomy) or [mixed farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_farming). Farm sizes vary from anything around 80 acres (32 hectares) to over 8,000. Soil types vary from heavy clays to light sands. Crops grown include [winter wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_wheat), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), [sugar beet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_beet), [oilseed rape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oilseed_rape), winter and [spring beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_beans) and [linseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linseed), although smaller areas of rye and oats can be found growing in areas with lighter soils along with a variety of vegetables.[^cite_note-41]

The continuing importance of agriculture in the county is reflected in the [Suffolk Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_Show), which is held annually in May at [Ipswich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipswich). Although latterly somewhat changed in nature, this remains primarily an [agricultural show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_show).[^cite_note-42]

Companies based in Suffolk include [Greene King Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greene_King_Brewery) and [Branston Pickle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branston_Pickle) in Bury St Edmunds. [Birds Eye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birds_Eye) has its largest UK factory in [Lowestoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowestoft), where all its meat products and frozen vegetables are processed. [Huntley & Palmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntley_&_Palmers) biscuit company has a base in [Sudbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudbury,_Suffolk). The UK [horse racing](74711.md) industry is based in [Newmarket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newmarket,_Suffolk). There are two [United States Air Force](32090.md) bases in the west of the county at [RAF Lakenheath](856907.md) and [RAF Mildenhall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Mildenhall). [Sizewell B nuclear power station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sizewell_B_nuclear_power_station) is at [Sizewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sizewell) on the coast near [Leiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiston). [Bernard Matthews Farms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Matthews_Farms) have some processing units in the county, specifically [Holton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holton,_Suffolk). [Southwold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwold) is the home of [Adnams Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adnams_Brewery). The [Port of Felixstowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Felixstowe) is the largest [container port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Container_port) in the United Kingdom. Other ports include [Port of Lowestoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Lowestoft) and [Port of Ipswich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Ipswich), run by [Associated British Ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Associated_British_Ports). [BT Group plc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BT_Group_plc) has its main research and development facility at [Martlesham Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martlesham_Heath).

## Education

See also: [List of schools in Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Suffolk)

### Primary, secondary and further education

Suffolk has a [comprehensive education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_education) system with fourteen independent schools. Unusually for the UK, some of Suffolk had a [3-tier school system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-tier_education) in place with primary schools (ages 4–9), [middle schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Schools_in_England) (ages 9–13) and upper schools (ages 13–16). However, a 2006 Suffolk County Council study concluded that Suffolk should move to the two-tier school system used in the majority of the UK.[^cite_note-48] For the purpose of conversion to two-tier, the three-tier system was divided into four geographical area groupings and corresponding phases. The first phase was the conversion of schools in Lowestoft and Haverhill in 2011, followed by schools in north and west Suffolk in 2012. The remainder of the changeovers to two-tier took place from 2013, for those schools that stayed within local government control, and did not become [Academies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_\(English_school\)) and/or [free schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_school_\(England\)). The majority of schools thus now (2019) operate the more common primary to high school (11–16).

Many of the county's upper schools have a [sixth form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_form) and most [further education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Further_education) colleges in the county offer [A-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-level) courses. In terms of school population, Suffolk's individual schools are large with the Ipswich district with the largest school population and Forest Heath the smallest, with just two schools. In 2013, a letter said that "...nearly a fifth of the schools inspected were judged inadequate. This is unacceptable and now means that Suffolk has a higher proportion of pupils educated in inadequate schools than both the regional and national averages."[^cite_note-49]

[The Royal Hospital School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Royal_Hospital_School) near Ipswich is the largest independent boarding school in Suffolk. Other boarding schools within Suffolk include Barnardiston Hall Preparatory School, [Culford School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culford_School), [Finborough School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finborough_School), [Framlingham College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framlingham_College), [Ipswich High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipswich_High_School,_Suffolk), [Ipswich School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipswich_School), [Orwell Park School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orwell_Park_School), [Saint Felix School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Felix_School) and [Woodbridge School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodbridge_School).

The Castle Partnership Academy Trust in Haverhill is the county's only All-through Academy Chain. Comprising [Castle Manor Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Manor_Academy) and Place Farm Primary Academy, the Academy Trust supports all-through education and provides opportunities for young people aged 3 to 18.

[Sixth form colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_form_college) in the county include [Lowestoft Sixth Form College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowestoft_Sixth_Form_College) and [One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_\(sixth_form_college\)) in Ipswich. Suffolk is home to considerably more education colleges, which include: [Lowestoft College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowestoft_College), [Easton & Otley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easton_&_Otley_College), [Suffolk New College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_New_College) and [Northgate Sixth Form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northgate_High_School,_Ipswich) (Ipswich), Abbeygate Sixth Form, [Thurston Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurston_Community_College) ([Beyton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyton) Campus) and [West Suffolk College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Suffolk_College) (Bury St Edmunds).

### Tertiary education

The county has one university, the [University of Suffolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Suffolk), which became an independent institution with degree awarding powers and university status in 2016.[^cite_note-50] The university operates at five sites, with its central hub in Ipswich. Others include Lowestoft, Bury St. Edmunds, and [Great Yarmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Yarmouth) in Norfolk.[^cite_note-uea-51] The university is organised in four academic schools[^cite_note-52] and in 2024/25 had 13,590 students. 88% of the student body are aged over 21 and 46% of university students are male.[^cite_note-53]

## Culture

### Arts



[Snape Maltings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snape_Maltings) Concert Hall; formerly a Victorian [maltings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltings), now converted into a world-famous concert venue

Founded in 1948 by [Benjamin Britten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Britten), the annual [Aldeburgh Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldeburgh_Festival) is one of the UK's major classical music festivals. Originating in [Aldeburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldeburgh), it has been held at the nearby [Snape Maltings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snape_Maltings) since 1967.[^cite_note-54] Since 2006, [Henham Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henham_Park), has been home to the annual [Latitude Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latitude_Festival). This mainly open-air festival, which has grown considerably in size and scope, includes popular music, comedy, poetry and literary events.

The [FolkEast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FolkEast) festival is held at [Glemham Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glemham_Hall) in August[^cite_note-55] and attracts international acoustic, folk and roots musicians whilst also championing local businesses, heritage and crafts. In 2015 it was also home to the first instrumental festival of musical instruments and makers.[^cite_note-56] More recently, [LeeStock Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeeStock_Music_Festival) has been held in [Sudbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudbury,_Suffolk).[^cite_note-57] A celebration of the county, "Suffolk Day", was instigated in 2017.[^cite_note-58]

### Dialect

The [Suffolk dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_dialect) is very distinctive. [Epenthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epenthesis) and [yod-dropping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yod-dropping) is common, along with non-conjugation of verbs.[^cite_note-59]

### Sport

#### Football

The county's sole professional [football](10568.md) club is [Ipswich Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipswich_Town). Formed in 1878, the club were [Football League First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division) champions in [1961–62](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_in_association_football), [FA Cup](11237.md) winners in [1977–78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_in_association_football) and [UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup) winners in [1980–81](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_in_association_football);[^cite_note-60] as of the [2025–26 season](79698535.md), Ipswich Town play in the [Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_Championship), the second tier of English football. The club has as part of its [crest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crest_\(sports\)) the [Suffolk Punch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_Punch), a now endangered breed of [draught horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draught_horse) native to the county. The next highest ranked teams in Suffolk are [AFC Sudbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Sudbury), [Bury Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury_Town_F.C.), [Leiston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiston_F.C.) and [Needham Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Needham_Market_F.C.), who all participate in the Southern League Premier Division Central, the seventh tier of English football.

#### Horse racing

Newmarket is the headquarters of [British horseracing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_horseracing) – home to the largest cluster of training yards in the country and many key horse racing organisations including the [National Stud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Stud),[^cite_note-61] and [Newmarket Racecourse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newmarket_Racecourse). [Tattersalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tattersalls) bloodstock auctioneers and the [National Horseracing Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Horseracing_Museum) are also in the town.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] [Point to point racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_to_point_racing) takes place at [Higham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higham,_Babergh) and [Ampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ampton).[^cite_note-64]

#### Speedway

[Motorcycle speedway](230182.md) racing has been staged in Suffolk since at least the 1950s, following the construction of the [Foxhall Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxhall_Stadium), just outside Ipswich, home of the [Ipswich Witches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ipswich_Witches). The Witches are currently members of the [Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_League_\(speedway\)), the UK's first division.[^cite_note-65] [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_\(speedway\)) team [Mildenhall Fen Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mildenhall_Fen_Tigers) are also from Suffolk.[^cite_note-66]

#### Cricket

[Suffolk County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffolk_County_Cricket_Club) compete in the Eastern Division of the [Minor Counties Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Counties_Championship).[^cite_note-67] The club has won the championship three times outright and has shared the title one other time as well as winning the [MCCA Knockout Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MCCA_Knockout_Trophy) once.[^cite_note-68] Home games are played in Bury St Edmunds, [Copdock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copdock), [Exning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exning), [Framlingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framlingham), Ipswich and Mildenhall.[^cite_note-69]

### Suffolk Pink



16th century cottage in Ixworth, with [pargetting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pargeting) and Suffolk Pink limewash

The exteriors of historic Suffolk buildings are often coloured pink, and "Suffolk Pink" has become associated with the county.[^cite_note-70]

There is little evidence that the county's buildings were coloured externally before the eighteenth century, with white or off-white predominating.[^cite_note-\:3-71] [Clapboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clapboard)ed buildings and barns were sometimes painted red, but a St Edmundsbury Borough Council conservation leaflet notes that "if there is such a thing as a traditional, external Suffolk colour it is lime white, which is a soft, translucent off-white."[^cite_note-72]

By the late nineteenth century, red and yellow ochre were added to limewash to produce pink and pale yellow respectively; a popular myth that pink was achieved by adding pig blood, ox blood, sloes, or damsons to limewash is probably apocryphal.[^cite_note-\:3-71] The popularisation of the colour may have occurred in the twentieth century as a result of commercial marketing.[^cite_note-73] It is not mentioned in the 1960 [*Shell Guide*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_Guides) to Suffolk or the 1961 edition of *[The Buildings of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Buildings_of_England)* covering the county, but is mentioned in the 1974 edition of the latter.[^cite_note-\:3-71]

## Notable people



Gainsborough's *[Mr and Mrs Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr_and_Mrs_Andrews)* (1748–49), housed at the [National Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery) in London, depicts the Suffolk landscape of his time.

In the arts, Suffolk is noted for having been home to two of England's best regarded painters, [Thomas Gainsborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Gainsborough)[^cite_note-85] and [John Constable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Constable) – the Stour Valley area is branded as "Constable Country"[^cite_note-86] – and one of its most noted composers, [Benjamin Britten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Britten).[^cite_note-87] Other artistic figures connected with Suffolk include: [Sir Alfred Munnings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Alfred_Munnings), [John Nash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Nash_\(artist\)), sculptor [Dame Elizabeth Frink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dame_Elizabeth_Frink), [Cedric Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedric_Morris) who ran the [East Anglian School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Anglian_School_of_Painting_and_Drawing), [Philip Wilson Steer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Wilson_Steer), and the cartoonist [Carl Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Giles) (a bronze statue of his character "Grandma" is located in Ipswich town centre); the poets [George Crabbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Crabbe)[^cite_note-88] and [Robert Bloomfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bloomfield) were both born in Suffolk;[^cite_note-89] as was folklorist [Margaret Helen James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Helen_James); farmer and writer [Adrian Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrian_Bell), writer and [literary editor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_editor) [Ronald Blythe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Blythe), [V. S. Pritchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._S._Pritchett), the authors Ralph [Hammond Innes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammond_Innes) and [Ruth Rendell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Rendell) all lived in the county.

The writer [M. M. Kaye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M._M._Kaye) spent her last years in Suffolk and died in [Lavenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavenham). Actors [Ralph Fiennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Fiennes),[^cite_note-90] [Bob Hoskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hoskins) and [Sam Claflin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Claflin), actress and singer [Kerry Ellis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerry_Ellis), American film and television actress [Rebecca De Mornay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_De_Mornay),[^cite_note-ind-91] musician and record producer [Brian Eno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Eno),[^cite_note-92] multi-award-winning singer-songwriter [Ed Sheeran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Sheeran), bassist in the band [Keane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keane_\(band\)) [Jesse Quin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Quin) and sopranos [Laura Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Wright_\(singer\)) and [Christina Johnston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Johnston)[^cite_note-93] are all connected with the county. [Glam rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glam_rock) band and three time [Brit Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brit_Award) winners [the Darkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Darkness_\(band\)) hail from Lowestoft.[^cite_note-94]

[Hip hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop) DJ [Tim Westwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Westwood) is originally from Suffolk and the influential DJ and radio presenter [John Peel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Peel) made the county his home.[^cite_note-95] Contemporary painter [Maggi Hambling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maggi_Hambling), was born and resides in Suffolk. [Norah Lofts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_Lofts), author of best-selling historical novels, lived for decades in Bury St. Edmunds. [Sir Peter Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Hall_\(director\)) the founder of the [Royal Shakespeare Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Shakespeare_Company) was born in Bury St. Edmunds, and [Sir Trevor Nunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Trevor_Nunn) the theatre director was born in Ipswich. The actor [Sir John Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Mills) spent periods of his youth in the county. The designer [David Hicks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Hicks_\(British_designer\)) lived for a number of years in Suffolk. Model [Claudia Schiffer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudia_Schiffer) and her husband, the film director [Matthew Vaughn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Vaughn), have owned a house in Suffolk since 2002.

Suffolk's contributions to sport include former [Formula One](10854.md) magnate [Bernie Ecclestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Ecclestone) and former [England national team](9904.md) footballers [Terry Butcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Butcher), [Kieron Dyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kieron_Dyer) and [Matthew Upson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Upson). Due to Newmarket being the centre of British [horse racing](74711.md) many jockeys have settled in the county, including [Lester Piggott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Piggott) and [Frankie Dettori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankie_Dettori). [MMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMA) fighter [Arnold Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_Allen) was born in Suffolk. British heavyweight boxing champion [Fabio Wardley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabio_Wardley) is also from Suffolk.

Significant ecclesiastical figures from Suffolk include [Simon Sudbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Sudbury), a former [archbishop of Canterbury](2345.md);[^cite_note-96] former [Lord Chancellor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Chancellor) [Thomas Wolsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Wolsey) hailed from Ipswich;[^cite_note-97] and author, poet and Benedictine monk [John Lydgate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lydgate).[^cite_note-98] [Richard Hakluyt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Hakluyt) the great recorder of exploration and voyages was a clergyman in [Wetheringsett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetheringsett-cum-Brockford). [Edward FitzGerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_FitzGerald_\(poet\)), the first translator of the [Rubaiyat of Omar Khayyam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubaiyat_of_Omar_Khayyam), was born in [Bredfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bredfield). The [abolitionists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionists) [Thomas Clarkson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Clarkson) and [Richard Dykes Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Dykes_Alexander) both lived near Ipswich. The [agriculturist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculturist) [Arthur Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Young_\(agriculturist\)) had a long-standing association with the county.

Other significant persons from Suffolk include the great [landscape designer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_architecture) [Humphry Repton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphry_Repton), [suffragette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragette) [Dame Millicent Garrett Fawcett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dame_Millicent_Garrett_Fawcett);[^cite_note-99] the captain of *[HMS Beagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Beagle)*, [Robert FitzRoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_FitzRoy);[^cite_note-100] Witch-finder General [Matthew Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Hopkins);[^cite_note-101] educationist [Hugh Catchpole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Catchpole);[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] and Britain's first female physician and mayor, [Elizabeth Garrett Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Garrett_Anderson).[^cite_note-104] The [tuberculosis](30653.md) treatment pioneer [Jane Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Harriet_Walker) ran the East Anglian Sanatorium above the banks of the [River Stour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Stour,_Suffolk), and charity leader [Sue Ryder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sue_Ryder) settled in Suffolk and based her charity in [Cavendish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavendish,_Suffolk).

The popular Victorian novelist [Henry Seton Merriman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Seton_Merriman) lived and died in the village of [Melton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melton,_Suffolk). Between 1932 and 1939 [George Orwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Orwell) lived at his parents' home in the coastal town of Southwold, where a mural of the author now dominates the entrance to Southwold Pier.[^cite_note-105] He is said to have chosen his pen name from Suffolk's [River Orwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Orwell). [Arthur Ransome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Ransome) lived alongside the river during the 1930s, sailing his boats from [Pin Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pin_Mill) and along the [Shotley Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shotley_Peninsula). The county was also home to [wild swimmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_swimmer) and environmentalist [Roger Deakin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Deakin). The Welshman [George Ewart Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Ewart_Evans) settled in Suffolk and did much to record the traditional rural ways of East Anglian life. [Flora Sandes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_Sandes), officially the only British woman to serve in a military capacity during the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) died in Suffolk in 1956.

## Edmund of East Anglia

King of [East Anglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Anglia) and Christian [martyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr) [St Edmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_the_Martyr), after whom the town of [Bury St Edmunds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bury_St_Edmunds) is named, was killed by invading [Danes](419786.md) in the year 869. St Edmund was the [patron saint](68055.md) of England until he was replaced by [St George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_George) in the 13th century. The year 2006 saw the failure of a campaign to have St Edmund named the patron saint of England. In 2007 he was named the patron saint of Suffolk, with St Edmund's Day falling on 20 November. [His flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Suffolk) is flown in Suffolk on that day.
