**Lagos State** ([Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language): *Ìpínlẹ̀ Èkó*, [Gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_language): *Ayìmátẹ̀n Awọnlìn tọ̀n*) is a [state](226734.md) in [southwestern Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_\(Nigeria\)). Of the 36 [states](226734.md), Lagos is the [first or second most populous state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_population) (depending on the source) but is the [smallest by area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_area). Bounded to the south by the [Bight of Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bight_of_Benin) and to the west by the [international border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin%E2%80%93Nigeria_border) with the [Republic of Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Benin), Lagos State borders [Ogun State](1436438.md) to the north and east, making it the only Nigerian state to border only one other state. Named for the city of [Lagos](85232.md) — the [most populous city in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urban_areas_in_Africa_by_population) — the state was formed from the [Western Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Region,_Nigeria) and the former Federal Capital Territory on 27 May 1967.[^cite_note-\:32-8][^cite_note-Creation2-9]

Geographically, Lagos State is dominated by bodies of water with nearly a quarter of the state's area being lagoons, creeks, and rivers.[^cite_note-10] The largest of these bodies are the [Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Lagoon) and [Lekki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki_Lagoon) lagoons in the state's interior with the [Ogun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogun_River) and [Osun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osun_river) rivers flowing into them. Many other rivers and creeks flow throughout the state and serve as vital means of transportation for people and goods. On land, non-urbanized areas are within the [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_moist_broadleaf_forests) [Nigerian lowland forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_lowland_forests) [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ecoregions_in_Nigeria) with natural areas containing threatened populations of [mona monkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mona_monkey), [tree pangolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree_pangolin), and [hooded vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hooded_vulture) along with a transitory population of [African forest elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_forest_elephant).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Offshore, the state is also biodiverse as there are large fish populations along with [African manatees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_manatee) and [crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocodile).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

Lagos State has been inhabited for years by various indigenous ethnic groups, primarily the majority [Yoruba people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_people) who live throughout the state but also the [Ewe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewe_people) and [Ogu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogu_people) peoples in the far west. As a result of migration since the nineteenth century, Lagos State also has large populations of non-native Nigerian ethnic groups with [Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo_people), [Efik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efik_people), [Fulani](102383.md), [Hausa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hausa_people), [Igbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igbo_people), [Ijaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijaw_people), [Ibibio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibibio_people), and [Nupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nupe_people) peoples among other Nigerian groups. There are also groups from outside of Nigeria's modern borders with the [Saro (Sierra Leonean)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saro_people) and [Amaro (Brazilian)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilians_in_Nigeria) groups being descendants of [formerly enslaved people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedman) who returned to Africa in the 1800s with a longstanding Middle Eastern Nigerian community (mainly Syrian and [Lebanese Nigerians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Nigerians)) also forming a significant part of Lagos' population along with recent immigrants from [Benin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_Republic), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_people_in_Nigeria), [Ghana](12067.md), [India](14533.md), [Togo](30138.md), and the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Nigerian).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Religiously, the state is also diverse, as there is a sizable number of [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian), [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) and [traditional ethnic religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions).[^cite_note-21]

In the [pre-colonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-colonial) period, the area that is now Lagos State was mainly [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing) villages and ports that at various points were controlled by states including the [Benin Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_Empire) and [Oyo Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyo_Empire) until the early 1800s when the city of [Lagos](85232.md) had developed into a major kingdom of its own right. In 1850, the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) successfully attacked the kingdom in the [Bombardment of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardment_of_Lagos) before installing [an ally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akitoye) as [Oba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oba_of_Lagos) and signing [a treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_Between_Great_Britain_and_Lagos,_1_January_1852) that established Lagos as being under British protection. Ten years later, the forced [Lagos Treaty of Cession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Treaty_of_Cession) led to the formal establishment of the [Lagos Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Colony). In 1906, the colony was incorporated into the new [Southern Nigeria Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Nigeria_Protectorate) which merged into [British Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Nigeria) in 1914 with the city of [Lagos](85232.md) as its capital. Upon independence in 1960, Lagos remained as the capital with much of the city forming the Federal Capital Territory while the rest of modern-day Lagos State was a part of the [Western Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Region,_Nigeria) until 1967 when the region was split and the area became Lagos State.[^cite_note-22]

Economically, Lagos State is one of the fastest-growing urban areas in the world. It contains the most populous city in Nigeria and one of the most important states in the country, a major financial centre and has one of the largest [economies in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\))[^cite_note-John_M._O._Ekundayo_2013_135-23] with a [gross domestic product](12594.md) of \$84 billion comparable with [Ghana](12067.md)'s \$75 billion, [Angola](701.md)'s \$70 billion, and [Ethiopia](187749.md)'s \$93 billion.[^cite_note-24] Lagos State is also a key culture, education, and transportation hub for [Nigeria](21383.md) and [Sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md). Additionally, the state also has the highest literacy rate in Nigeria. It is known for its vibrant culture, bustling markets, and significant economic activities.[^cite_note-25] Despite overcrowding and chronic debilitating traffic, Lagos State has the [highest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_Human_Development_Index#2019) [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) in Nigeria and numerous development projects.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## History

Main article: [History of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Lagos)

### Early history

Before the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) name of Lagos had been adopted, Lagos' initial name was Eko which referred mainly to the [Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island). The first to settle in Eko were the [Aworis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awori_tribe). Trade later drew various groups to the island, including the Portuguese in the 16th century.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The Awori hunters and fishermen had originally come from [Ile-Ife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ife) to the coast.[^cite_note-Lagos_State,_Nigeria-30][^cite_note-31]

It was in 1760 that the name Lagos was adopted by the Portuguese. Naming it after a city in Southern Portugal which was used as port for [slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_slavery).[^cite_note-32]

### British Involvement

Main article: [Reduction of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reduction_of_Lagos)

See also: [Lagos Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Colony), [Southern Nigeria Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Nigeria_Protectorate), and [Colonial Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Nigeria)



In late 1851, the British Royal Navy bombarded Lagos (in present-day Nigeria) in the "Reduction of Lagos." The attack aimed to suppress the Atlantic slave trade, which had thrived in Lagos since the arrival of the Portuguese. The British sought to depose Oba Kosoko, who resisted efforts to end the trade. After Kosoko fled, the British installed Oba Akitoye, a more cooperative ruler aligned with their anti-slavery efforts. In 1861, Oba Docemo was the one who signed the treaty making Lagos a [British colony](207114.md).[^cite_note-33]

### Post-colonial era

Lagos State was created on 27 May 1967 according to the *State Creation and Transitional Provisions Decree No. 14 of 1967*,[^cite_note-34] which restructured Nigeria into a [federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation) of 12 states.[^cite_note-Lagos_State_Information-35][^cite_note-36] Before the issuance of this Decree, [Lagos city](85232.md), which was the country's capital had been administered directly by the Federal Government through the *Federal Ministry of Lagos Affairs*.[^cite_note-Lagos_State_Information-35] However, [Ikeja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikeja), [Agege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agege), [Mushin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushin,_Lagos), [Ikorodu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikorodu), [Epe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epe,_Lagos_State), [Surulere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surulere), and [Badagry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badagry) were administered by the then Western Region Government.[^cite_note-Lagos_State_Information-35] Lagos, the city, along with these other towns were captured to create the state of Lagos, with the state becoming fully recognized as a [semi-autonomous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-autonomous)[^cite_note-37] administrative division on 11 April 1968.[^cite_note-Lagos_State_Information-35] Lagos served the dual role of being the State and Federal Capital until 1976 when the capital of the state was moved to [Ikeja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikeja).[^cite_note-Lagos_State_Information-35] After the full establishment of the [Federal Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Capital_Territory,_Nigeria), based on the recommendation of the [Akinola Àgùdà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akinola_Aguda)–led committee set up by General [Murtala Muhammed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtala_Muhammed) to review the need for a new capital for Nigeria in 1975. The seat of the Federal Government was formally relocated to [Abuja](56823.md) on 12 December 1991.[^cite_note-38] Nevertheless, Lagos remains the financial centre of the country, and even grew to become the most populous city in the state and the country.[^cite_note-Lagos_State_Information-35]

## Cities and towns

### Lagos

Main article: [Lagos](85232.md)



Lagos is the most populous city in Lagos State, Nigeria as a whole, and the continent of Africa. The [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation) is one of the most populous in the world.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] Lagos is a [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) which originated on islands separated by [creeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creek_\(tidal\)), such as Lagos Island, fringing the southwest mouth of [Lagos Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Lagoon) while protected from the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) by [barrier islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrier_island) and long [sand spits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_spit) such as [Bar Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Beach,_Lagos), which stretch up to 100 kilometres (62 miles) east and west of the mouth. The metropolitan area of Lagos includes [Ikeja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikeja) (which is the capital of Lagos State) and [Agege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agege) and [Mushin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushin,_Lagos).[^cite_note-Lagos_State_Information-35]

### Ikeja

Main article: [Ikeja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikeja)



Ikeja is the state capital of Lagos State. [Ikeja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikeja) is a planned residential and commercial town with shopping malls, pharmacies and government reserved areas. The [Murtala Mohammed International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtala_Muhammed_International_Airport) is in Ikeja. Ikeja is also home to Fela Kuti's African Shrine,[^cite_note-41] Late Chief [Gani Fawehinmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gani_Fawehinmi) house and Lagbaja's Motherland. It also has the largest shopping center on the mainland.[^cite_note-42]

### Lekki

Main article: [Lekki](44625841.md)



[Lekki](44625841.md) is a city in the south eastern part of the state. It is a naturally formed peninsula, it is still largely under construction. As of 2015, only phase 1 of the project had been completed, with phase 2 nearing completion. The peninsula is approximately 70 to 80 km long, with an average width of 10 km. Lekki currently houses several estates, gated residential developments, agricultural farmlands, areas allocated for a Free Trade Zone, an airport, and a sea port under construction. The proposed land use master plan for the Lekki envisages the Peninsula as a "Blue-Green Environment City",[^cite_note-Lekki-Lagos_State-43] expected to accommodate over 3.4 million residential population and an additional non-residential population of at least 1.9 million.[^cite_note-allafrica-44]

### Ikorodu

Main article: [Ikorodu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikorodu)



Ikorodu is a city located north-east of the state along the [Lagos Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Lagoon). It shares a boundary with [Ogun State](1436438.md). As of the 2006 Census, Ikorodu boosted population of about 535,619.[^cite_note-nigeriacongress2-45] It is the third largest city in the south-west after Ibadan and Lagos while it is the 12th largest city in Nigeria.[^cite_note-guardian.ng-46] In 2022, it had an estimated population of 1,041,066. The population of the city currently grows at 5.26% annually, and it is projected to reach 1.7 million by 2035.[^cite_note-guardian.ng-46]

### Eko Atlantic

Main article: [Eko Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eko_Atlantic)

Eko Atlantic is a planned city being constructed on land reclaimed from the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md).[^cite_note-47] It is located on the former Lagos' [Bar Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_Beach,_Lagos). Upon completion, the new island which is still under development is anticipating at least 250,000 residents and a daily flow of at least 150,000 commuters. The development will also have a positive environmental impact; its purpose is to stop the erosion of the Lagos coastline.[^cite_note-Dada-48] The [Eko Atlantic City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eko_Atlantic) project received global recognition in 2009, as the [Lagos State government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Lagos_State) and its private sector partners on the Project, South Energyx, received the [Clinton Global Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Foundation#Clinton_Global_Initiative_\(CGI\)) Commitment Certificate.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

### Badagry

Main article: [Badagry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badagry)

Badagry is a coastal town in the state. It is situated between Metropolitan Lagos, and the border of the [Republic of Benin](3459.md) at [Seme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seme_Border). As of the preliminary 2006 [census](6889.md) results, the municipality had a population of 241,093.[^cite_note-\:0-52] The area is led by a traditional king, Akran De Wheno Aholu Menu – Toyi 1,[^cite_note-53] who is also the permanent vice-chairman of obas and chiefs in Lagos State. It is known to hold the country's oldest storey building. Badagry is home to the [Ewe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewe_people) and [Egun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egun) people who are predominantly fishermen.[^cite_note-\:0-52]

### Epe

Main article: [Epe, Lagos State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epe,_Lagos_State)

Epe is a town located on the north side of the [Lekki Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki_Lagoon). It is popular for the fishing activities attributed to the city. Per the 2006 Census the population of Epe was 181,409.[^cite_note-54]

Epe is widely regarded as Lagos's fishing capital. Fishing is the main occupation of the people here, so it is no surprise that a sculpture of two giant fishes, erected at the Lekki-Epe T-Junction, welcomes you to Epe. The Epe Fish Market is regarded as Lagos's largest seafood market.

Epe town is well-known for its tranquilly. Aside from the adventure, sightseeing, and serenity, you also have access to low-cost animal protein.

Epe, like any other society, has special occasions and festivals where people come together to celebrate. Epe residents celebrate various festivals such as the Kayo-kayo festival,[^cite_note-55] the Ebi bi festival, Ojude-Oba,[^cite_note-56] and the Epe day.

Some campuses of popular higher education institutions in Lagos can be found within Epe. Lagos State University (LASU),[^cite_note-57] Pan-Atlantic University (PAU),[^cite_note-58] Yaba College of Technology,[^cite_note-59] and Michael Otedola College of Primary Education are among them (MOCOPED).

Epe is also the birthplace of notable individuals such as former Lagos State Governor Akinwunmi Ambode and Nigerian businessman Femi Otedola.[^cite_note-\:32-8]

### Ojo

Main article: [Ojo, Lagos State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojo,_Lagos_State)

Ojo is a town predominantly inhabited by the Awori people, with a population of 507,693. It is home to several notable institutions, including [Lagos State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_State_University) and the Lagos State University of Education, formerly known as [Adeniran Ogunsanya College of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adeniran_Ogunsanya_College_of_Education) ([AOCOED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adeniran_Ogunsanya_College_of_Education)). The town also hosts the [Federal Government College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Government_College,_Ijanikin), Ijanikin, and the renowned [Alaba International Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alaba_International_Market).

Lagos State, with a [Gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) based on [PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)) in 2025 estimated to be over [US\$](18717338.md)250 billion,[^cite_note-NairaMetrics-60] is the major economic centre of Nigeria. The state's dynamic economy, driven by its bustling port, thriving tech scene, and a robust manufacturing sector, is continuously expanding, positioning Lagos as a significant global economic hub. If Lagos were an independent nation, it would rank among the top 30 economies globally and it would be the fifth largest [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy) in Africa.[^cite_note-John_M._O._Ekundayo_2013_135-23] Lagos State houses headquarters of most conglomerates and commercial banks in Nigeria.[^cite_note-61] The state has the [lowest incidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_poverty_rate) of [extreme poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_poverty) (around 1.3% of the population against a national average of 31%) of all states in Nigeria, according to [World Bank](36752.md) data from 2018.[^cite_note-62] Despite that, [slums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum) and poverty are a major issue in the Lagos area.

Its total generated [revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue) in 2017 was around [₦](178686.md)334 billion (equivalent to US\$920 million), growing by 10.43% compared to 2016.[^cite_note-63] By the first half of 2021, the State's internally generated revenue (IGR) alone stood at over [₦](178686.md)267 billion.[^cite_note-64]

### Lekki Free Trade Zone

[Lekki Free Trade Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki_Free_Trade_Zone) (Lekki FTZ) is a free zone situated at the eastern part of Lekki, which covers a total area of about 155 square kilometres. ## Geography

### Water Borders

Lagos State's water boundaries are defined by the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md) to the south and the [Lagos Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_Lagoon) to the east. The state is also intersected by several rivers and [creeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream), including the [Ogun River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogun_River), which drains into the Lagoon. Major coastal areas include [Victoria Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Island,_Lagos), [Lekki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki_Peninsula), and [Epe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epe,_Lagos_State). Lagos’ strategic location along the Atlantic Ocean has made it a critical point for trade and commerce, with the Lagos Port Complex and Tin Can Island Port serving as Nigeria's busiest shipping hubs.

### Climate

Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Lagos State has a tropical monsoon climate (Am), characterized by distinct wet and dry seasons. The rainy season lasts from April to October, with an annual average rainfall of 1,800 to 2,000 mm. Humidity levels remain high throughout the year.

- Dry Season: November to March, characterized by harmattan winds, which bring dry and dusty air from the Sahara Desert.

- Rainy Season: Heavy rainfall and occasional thunderstorms from April to October.

Average daytime temperatures range between 28 °C to 34 °C (82 °F to 93 °F). The highest recorded temperature in Lagos was 38 °C (100 °F), while the lowest was 21 °C (70 °F).

Lagos has a Tropical wet and dry or savanna climate. The city's yearly temperature is 28.67 °C (83.61 °F) and it is -0.79% lower than Nigeria's averages. Lagos typically receives about 132.01 millimetres (5.2 inches) of precipitation and has 193.63 rainy days (53.05% of the time) annually.[^cite_note-68]

### Drainage

Lagos State is characterized by a complex network of lagoons, creeks, and rivers. The Lagos Lagoon serves as the main drainage basin for the state, connected to the Atlantic Ocean through the Commodore Channel. The Ogun River, which originates in Ogun State, flows southward into Lagos Lagoon. The Badagry Creek and the Five Cowrie Creek also contribute to the state's drainage system.

Flooding is a recurring challenge, particularly during the rainy season from April to October, as the low-lying topography and coastal location make Lagos vulnerable to waterlogging and storm surges.

### Environmental issues

- Water Pollution In Lagos, water contamination is a significant issue. Serious health concerns have been raised as a result of the unchecked discharge of raw sewage, sediment-carrying runoff, and effluents into the Lagoon system. People are suffering from deadly waterborne illnesses like cholera and diarrhea as a result. The number of Lagos inhabitants who have access to formal clean water is pitifully small, with most of them reliant on the unofficial sector made up of wells. Lagos has a tropical environment with over 2,000 millimetres (79 inches) of annual rainfall, is surrounded by water, however much of the water is unsafe to drink. The bulk of Lagos inhabitants rely on the unofficial sector, which consists of wells, boreholes, rivers, and rains, as there is very little access to formal clean water.

### Flora and Fauna

Lagos State's unique geography supports a wide range of ecosystems, including coastal wetlands, mangrove forests, and lowland tropical rainforests. Species such as mangroves, oil palms, coconut trees, and various species of tropical hardwoods are found in the region. The state's wetlands are home to water plants like water hyacinth and reeds. Lagos’ diverse fauna includes species such as: [African bush elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_bush_elephants), [antelopes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antelopes), and [African civet cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_civet). [Nile crocodiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nile_crocodiles), [monitor lizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitor_lizards), and [pythons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pythons). The state is home to over 250 species of birds, including the [African grey parrot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_grey_parrot), [pied kingfisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pied_kingfisher), and [hammerkop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hammerkop). The Lagos Lagoon and the Atlantic Ocean support a variety of marine life, including [tilapia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilapia), [catfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catfish), [crabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crabs), and [prawns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prawns). Urbanization and pollution have threatened several species, leading to conservation efforts within the state's reserves and protected areas, such as the [Lekki Conservation Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lekki_Conservation_Centre).

### Parks, Landmarks and Monuments

Lagos State has several protected conservation areas aimed at preserving its unique biodiversity:



- Lekki Conservation Centre: A 78-hectare (8,400,000 sq ft) reserve with the longest canopy walkway in Africa.

- Lufasi Nature Park: A sanctuary for endangered species and a center for environmental education.

- Omu Resort: A wildlife and amusement park featuring a zoo, aquarium, and other attractions.



Landmarks and Attractions include;&#x20;

- First Storey Building in Nigeria: Built in 1845 in Badagry.

- Slave Route: Badagry was a major departure point for enslaved Africans during the transatlantic slave trade.

- National Museum: Houses artifacts, including the famous Festac Mask from the 1977 Festival of African Culture (FESTAC).

- National Theatre: A cultural and arts center in Iganmu.

- Third Mainland Bridge: At 11.8 km, it is the longest bridge in West Africa, connecting Lagos Island to the mainland.

- Nike Art Gallery: The largest art gallery in West Africa, showcasing Nigerian and African art.

Freedom Park: A former colonial-era prison turned into a cultural and recreational center.

## Government

Main article: [Politics of Lagos State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Lagos_State)

Since its creation in 1967, the state has been administered either by a [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Governors_of_Lagos_State) and a [House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_State_House_of_Assembly) in civilian or quasi-civilian (under [Ibrahim Badamasi Babangida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_Badamasi_Babangida)'s administration) federal administrations, or by Sole-Administrators or Military Administrators in military dispensations. Since December 2007, [Yoruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoruba_language) has been the second official language of debate and discussion for the [House of Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Assembly) after English. The House of Assembly is headed by [the Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_Lagos_State_House_of_Assembly), an elected position.

## Official Languages of Lagos

Both English and Yoruba are the official languages of Lagos state, Yoruba is compulsory for admission into universities and is compulsory in all private and public schools in Lagos State. Yoruba is also used in governmental proceedings as well as in plenaries. Yoruba is also used in very popular newspapers including but not limited to "Alaroye" which means "the explainer" and "Iroyin Owuro" which means "Morning News." These newspapers are distributed across Yorubaland and are available online in order to preserve the Yoruba language as well as close the bridge gap between Yoruba people who cannot understand English well. No longer will people think of Yoruba as inferior to English but Yoruba now will also be seen as an important language, and not as "outdated or old" as some young people now see it . Both English and Yoruba are very important languages in Lagos State. Lagos' laws and rules are available in both Yoruba and English[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

## Electoral System

The electoral system of each state is selected using a modified two-round system. To be elected in the first round, a candidate must receive the plurality of the vote and over 25% of the vote in at least two -third of the State local government Areas. If no candidate passes threshold, a second round will be held between the top candidate and the next candidate to have received a plurality of votes in the highest number of local government areas.[^cite_note-92]

## Demographics

Lagos state is in Yorubaland and the Yoruba language is predominant. The state attracts other non-Yorubas who live there in search of greener pastures attracting both [Nigerians](13008384.md) and foreigners alike.[^cite_note-129]

Indigenous inhabitants include the [Awori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awori_tribe) and [Ogu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogu_people) a.k.a. Egun in the Ikeja and [Badagry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badagry) Divisions respectively, with the Egun being found mainly in Badagry.

There is also an admixture of other pioneer settlers collectively known as the Eko.[^cite_note-130]

The Alimosho and Ifako-Ijaiye Local Government areas are predominantly populated by the [Egba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egba_people) and [Egbado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egbado) Yoruba people.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] The area is rich in [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture), prominent amongst which are the Oro, Igunnu and [Egungun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egungun) annual festivals.

The indigenous people of the [Ikorodu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikorodu) and [Epe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epe,_Lagos) Divisions are mainly the [Ijebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijebu_people), with pockets of Eko-Awori settlers along the coastland and riverine areas.[^cite_note-133]

## Religion

The dominant religions in Lagos State are Christianity and Islam although a certain amount of traditional religion is still practiced.[^cite_note-134] Churches represented include Anglican, Baptist, Methodist, 25.6% Roman Catholic, and many local and spiritual churches. Islam and traditional Yoruba spiritism are also practised.

The [Anglican Province of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Province_of_Lagos) (2002) within the [Church of Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Nigeria) includes the four Dioceses of [Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Lagos) (1919) led by Bishop [Humphrey Bamisebi Olumakaiye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humphrey_Bamisebi_Olumakaiye) until he died 2022, [Badagry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Badagry) led by Bishop [Babatunde Joseph Adeyemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babatunde_Joseph_Adeyemi) (2005), [Lagos Mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Lagos_Mainland) led by Bishop [Akinpelu Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akinpelu_Johnson) (2014) and [Lagos West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagos_West) (1999) with 275 parishes led by Bishop [James Olusola Odedeji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Odedeji) (2013).

343,675 Catholics (2021) in [Archdiocese of Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese_of_Lagos) (1860 as the Vicariate Apostolic of Dahomey) with 184 parishes under Archbishop [Alfred Adewale Martins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Adewale_Martins?action=edit\&redlink=1) (2012).
