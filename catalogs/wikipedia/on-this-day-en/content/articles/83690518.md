A series of [wildfires](56106.md) have been burning throughout the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [Minnesota](19590.md). Minnesota is currently under a peacetime [state of emergency](298608.md) regarding the fires. The state of emergency was originally expended to continue into mid-September of 2026.[^cite_note-emergency-4][^cite_note-homes-5] As of July 20, 2026, at least 8,000 people had been evacuated.[^cite_note-more-6]

The Minnesota fires, combined the [2026 Canadian wildfires](83302792.md) have contributed to a day of widespread air quality issues across the Great Lakes region and northern [United States](3434750.md) into [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England).[^cite_note-7] The wildfires and conditions themselves across the region were said to be "unprecedented" by meteorologists and local firefighting agencies.[^cite_note-8]

## Background



Minnesota drought monitor on July 7

While "fire season" varies every year in Minnesota, most wildfires occur in April and May. On average, over 1,500 wildfires occur each year, and 75% of those occur in those months. However, there is another small peak in fall. This happens when snow melts, so vegetation begins to dry out, and more dry and windy conditions lead to more fire activity. While [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) can moisten vegetation, it quickly dries up within an hour. Dry winters can make vegetation much more vulnerable to wildfires.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-Several_wildfires_in_MN-11]

## State of Emergency

On July 15, 2026, [Minnesota Department of Natural Resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Department_of_Natural_Resources) (DNR) Commissioner [Sarah Strommen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Strommen) announced that 17 fires were active, and 55,000 acres had been burned.[^cite_note-emergency-4] The [Minnesota Pollution Control Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Pollution_Control_Agency) issued an air quality alert the same day, as smoke from the fires spread. An [AQI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Quality_Index) for [particulate matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate_matter) was recorded as high as 617. The maximum normal AQI is 66 and below. The air quality alert remained in effect in much Northern and Eastern Minnesota throughout all of July.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-watch-13] On the evening of July 16, 2026, [Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duluth,_Minnesota) recorded an AQI of 1030.[^cite_note-14] Effected areas, such as the [Boundary Waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_Waters_Canoe_Area_Wilderness), were quickly evacuated and closed to the public.

On July 17, 2026, the Bear Trap fire reached about one mile from public and private buildings, homes, and infrastructure. The [Minnesota National Guard](4562088.md) is one of multiple organizations fighting the fire. Priority had been forced to shift away from the Thumb fire due to resource constraints.[^cite_note-homes-5] Two new fires were also detected that same day due to over 13,000 lightning strikes from widespread thunderstorm activity, and despite dropping noticeable rain on the fire perimeters, the fires kept raging on.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The amount of acres burned also increased by 22,000 in only one day, now reported at greater than 77,000 acres burnt.[^cite_note-KSTP-17] At that time, it was estimated between 6,000 and 10,000 people have been evacuated.[^cite_note-KSTP-17]



Wildfire Smoke Intensity, July 17, 2026

As of July 18, 2026, efforts to stop the spread of the Bear Trap fire have been successful in temporarily stopping the fire's spread.[^cite_note-18] On July 20, 2026, it was reported that no new fires had started after a storm.[^cite_note-more-6] Due to improved weather, the spread of the fires was slowed, and some control lines have been set up, although containment continues to prove difficult. The fires have been found to be burning as deep as 20–30 feet underground, which further hinders attempts at containment.[^cite_note-19]

By July 23, 2026, more than 1,000 emergency personnel were involved in the firefighting. Rain has been able to slow the spread of the fires. Full containment was reported for all ten minor fires.[^cite_note-July_23-20] Heavy rain on July 30–31 brought relief, along with concerns that lightning may have started new fires.[^cite_note-watch-13] The [United States Forest Service](42652.md) announced that limited public access to about 40% of the Boundary waters would begin on July 30. [Superior National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_National_Forest) also re-opened to visitors.[^cite_note-21] On August 5, more of the Boundary waters was reopened, bringing the open areas to now cover two-thirds of the wilderness area. Continued rain was able to slow and calm the fires, however as little as five days without rain would be enough to bring the fires back into a much more dangerous state.[^cite_note-22] On August 11, Alaskan firefighter Leslie R. Washington died while fighting the Bear Trap fire, becoming the first (and so far, the only) fatality. Governor [Tim Walz](2216593.md) ordered flags to be flown at half-mast in his honor.[^cite_note-half-mast-3] On August 26, the Minnesota DNR lifted some burning restrictions, allowing campfires and other recreational fires to be allowed across the state, as fire risk was lowered to low and moderate across the state.[^cite_note-23]

On September 17, all fires were announced to be at 100% containment.[^cite_note-100%-24] On September 22, all fires except the Bear Trap and Thumb fires were put out.[^cite_note-25]
