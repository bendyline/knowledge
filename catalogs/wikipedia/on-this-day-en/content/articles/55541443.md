**Elena Andreyevna Rybakina**[^cite_note-3] (born 17 June 1999) is a Russian-born Kazakhstani professional [tennis](29773.md) player. She is currently ranked [world No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WTA_number_1_ranked_singles_tennis_players) in singles by the [Women's Tennis Association](57768.md) (WTA). Rybakina has won 14 [WTA Tour](1398173.md)–level singles titles, including three majors, two [WTA 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_1000) titles, and the [2025 WTA Finals](81306023.md). She is the first Kazakhstani player to win a major, and to be ranked world No. 1 and in the world's top 10.

A former junior world No. 3, Rybakina initially represented Russia before switching federations to Kazakhstan in 2018. She had a breakout year in 2020 when she played in five finals, the most of any player that year, and reached her first major quarterfinal in both singles and doubles at the [2021 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open). Rybakina won her first major title at the [2022 Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and finished runner-up at the [2023 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), which saw her debut in the top 10 in the singles rankings. In 2025, she won the WTA Finals, followed by [Australian Open](81715708.md) and [US Open](84044135.md) titles in 2026, which saw her achieve the No. 1 ranking for the first time.

Rybakina is noted for her excellent [serve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serve_\(tennis\)) and ability to generate high-powered groundstrokes, and plays primarily from the baseline. She is known for her calm demeanour on court, and is sometimes referred to by the nickname "Ice Queen".[^cite_note-4]

## Early life and background

Elena Rybakina was born on 17 June 1999 in Moscow to Andrey Rybakin and Ekaterina. Her father worked as a producer for the television channel [NTV Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NTV_Plus).[^cite_note-Father_Kazakhstan-5] She started playing sports with her older sister, Anna, from a very young age, originally focusing on [gymnastics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gymnastics) and [ice skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_skating).[^cite_note-get-to-know-6][^cite_note-7] Upon being told that she was too tall to become a professional in either of those sports, her father suggested she switch to tennis instead because of his interest in the sport.[^cite_note-8] Rybakina began playing tennis at the age of six.[^cite_note-get-to-know-6]

Rybakina moved from the [Dynamo Sports Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamo_Sports_Club) to the [Spartak Tennis Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spartak_Tennis_Club), where she had several accomplished coaches. She trained with former top-10 player [Andrey Chesnokov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Chesnokov) and former top-100 player [Evgenia Kulikovskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgenia_Kulikovskaya). One of her fitness coaches was [Irina Kiseleva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina_Kiseleva), a World Championship gold medallist in the [modern pentathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_pentathlon).[^cite_note-ubi-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-spartak-11]

Rybakina did not have individual training as a junior, instead practicing in a group of about eight players up until age 15 and a group of four players through age 18. She also only played tennis about two hours per day and trained in fitness for three hours a day. Her time for tennis was limited in part because she attended a regular high school not specialized for athletes and needed to balance tennis with schoolwork.[^cite_note-school-12]

## Juniors



Rybakina (right) and [Whitney Osuigwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Osuigwe) at the 2017 ITF Junior Masters

Rybakina is a former world No. 3 junior.[^cite_note-itf-juniors-13] She began playing on the [ITF Junior Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Junior_Circuit) in November 2013 at the age of 14. The following March, she won her first title at her second career event, the Grade-3 [Almetievsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almetievsk) Cup. She played her first Grade-2 event in June at the Ozerov Cup in Moscow, finishing runner-up to compatriot [Anna Blinkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Blinkova). She began playing Grade-1 events from the start of 2015, but did not have any success until she reached the final at the Belgian International Junior Championships in May, losing to [Katharina Hobgarski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katharina_Hobgarski).

Rybakina made her junior-major debut later in the year at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_US_Open_\(tennis\)), where she reached the third round. Following an opening-round loss at the [2016 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Australian_Open), she won back-to-back Grade-1 titles. She continued to struggle at the junior Grand Slam and other Grade-A events in singles for the rest of the year.[^cite_note-itf-junior-singles-14] Her best result of 2016 at the Grade A-events came in doubles when she finished runner-up to [Olesya Pervushina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olesya_Pervushina) and [Anastasia Potapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova) at the [Trofeo Bonfiglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trofeo_Bonfiglio), alongside [Amina Anshba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amina_Anshba) in an all-Russian final.[^cite_note-itf-junior-doubles-15]

The 2017 season was Rybakina's last year on the junior tour. In the middle of the season, she won her first and only Grade-A title at the Trofeo Bonfiglio, defeating [Iga Świątek](51838558.md) in the final.[^cite_note-\:0-16] She also fared better at the Grand Slam events compared to previous years, losing in the semifinals of the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Australian_Open) and the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_French_Open) to eventual champions [Marta Kostyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Kostyuk) and [Whitney Osuigwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitney_Osuigwe), respectively. She finished her junior career at the first round-robin edition of the [ITF Junior Masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Junior_Masters), the junior counterpart to the [WTA Finals](1356185.md). She won one match in her round-robin group and finished in seventh place.[^cite_note-itf-junior-singles-14]

## Professional

### 2014–18: First ITF titles, federation change

Rybakina began playing on the [ITF Women's Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Women's_Circuit) in December 2014, at the age of fifteen. While she was still playing on the junior circuit, she reached three ITF finals in singles and two in doubles, winning both of the doubles finals only in 2017.[^cite_note-itf-singles-17][^cite_note-itf-doubles-18] She also made her [WTA Tour](1398173.md) debut in October 2017 at the [Kremlin Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Kremlin_Cup), where she reached the main draw through qualifying but lost in the opening round to [Irina-Camelia Begu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina-Camelia_Begu).[^cite_note-19]

At her next WTA Tour tournament in February 2018, Rybakina won her first WTA Tour match at the [St. Petersburg Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_St._Petersburg_Ladies'_Trophy) against [Timea Bacsinszky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timea_Bacsinszky). She then [upset](1169845.md) world No. 7, [Caroline Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Garcia), in three sets, after saving a [match point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Match_point_\(tennis\)) in the second set.[^cite_note-20] Losing in the next round,[^cite_note-21] this quarterfinal appearance helped her rise from No. 450 to No. 268 in the world.[^cite_note-wta-rankings-22] In March, Rybakina won her first \$15k singles title in [Kazan](202295.md),[^cite_note-itf-singles-17] where she also won the doubles title.[^cite_note-itf-doubles-18] Her next significant rankings jump came in April when she finished runner-up to [Sabina Sharipova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabina_Sharipova) at the \$60k [Lale Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Lale_Cup) in Istanbul, bringing her to No. 215. She broke into the top 200 for the first time in late May.[^cite_note-wta-rankings-22]

Rybakina's family began to grow frustrated with the [Russian Tennis Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Tennis_Federation) over a perceived lack of financial support for Rybakina's career and various incidents involving the federation.[^cite_note-2016_US_Open-23] At the [girls' juniors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Girls'_singles) tournament of the [2016 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_US_Open_\(tennis\)), Rybakina fainted due to low [hemoglobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemoglobin) levels and was forced to retire from her first round match.[^cite_note-2016_US_Open-23] The RTF reportedly provided no assistance to Rybakina despite being present at the event, leading Rybakina's family to decide that she would no longer travel for tournaments without having a parent present.[^cite_note-2016_US_Open-23] As the family could not afford to travel with her full-time, they instead decided to focus on playing [college tennis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_tennis) in the United States or Canada.[^cite_note-2016_US_Open-23] In May 2018, having just turned 19 years old, Rybakina acquired Kazakhstani citizenship and switched federations from Russia to Kazakhstan. The [Kazakhstan Tennis Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan_Tennis_Federation) had offered her financial support to change her nationality, which she chose over various options to play college tennis and pause her professional career.[^cite_note-get-to-know-6][^cite_note-switch-24] Rybakina's father had contacted the Kazakhstan Tennis Federation about a nationality switch for Rybakina, as he was originally from Kazakhstan.[^cite_note-Father_Kazakhstan-5]

Playing for Kazakhstan, Rybakina entered her first Grand Slam tournament qualifying draw at the [2018 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_US_Open_\(tennis\)), but did not reach the main draw.[^cite_note-itf-singles-17][^cite_note-25]

### 2019: WTA 250 title, top 50



Rybakina at the 2019 French Open

After playing mostly ITF events in the first half of 2019, Rybakina began playing primarily on the WTA Tour in the second half of the season. During the first few months of the year, she won three ITF titles, including the \$60k [Launceston International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Launceston_Tennis_International). She qualified into her first [WTA](1398173.md) event at [Istanbul Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_%C4%B0stanbul_Cup_%E2%80%93_Singles), where she lost to [Barbora Strýcová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Str%C3%BDcov%C3%A1) in the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-26] She made her major debut at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_French_Open) as a qualifier,[^cite_note-27] losing to [Kateřina Siniaková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Siniakov%C3%A1). In her first tour event on grass, Rybakina made her first tour semifinal at the [Rosmalen Grass Court Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Rosmalen_Grass_Court_Championships).[^cite_note-28] Despite this success, she lost in qualifying at [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wimbledon_Championships).[^cite_note-wta-matches-29] Rybakina's breakthrough came in July when she won her maiden WTA Tour title at the [Bucharest Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Bucharest_Open), a month after turning 20 years old. During the event, she upset second seed [Viktória Kužmová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikt%C3%B3ria_Ku%C5%BEmov%C3%A1) before defeating [Patricia Maria Țig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_Maria_%C8%9Aig) in the final.[^cite_note-wta-matches-29][^cite_note-first-title-30] With this title, she made her top 100 debut in the WTA rankings at No. 65.[^cite_note-wta-rankings-22][^cite_note-31]

Rybakina qualified for her second main-draw Grand Slam tournament match of the year at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_US_Open_\(tennis\)) but again lost in the first round.[^cite_note-wta-matches-29][^cite_note-32] At her next tournament, she made her second WTA Tour tournament final of the year at the [Jiangxi International Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Jiangxi_International_Women's_Tennis_Open), defeating [Viktorija Golubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktorija_Golubic)[^cite_note-33] and [Peng Shuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peng_Shuai),[^cite_note-34] finishing runner-up to [Rebecca Peterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Peterson).[^cite_note-35] This result brought her into the top 50 for the first time.[^cite_note-wta-rankings-22] Rybakina closed out the year strong, reaching at least the quarterfinals at her last three events of the season. In particular, she reached the quarterfinals at the [Wuhan Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Wuhan_Open), her first career Premier-5 event. In the tournament, she defeated world No. 6, [Simona Halep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simona_Halep), who retired late in the first set with a lower back injury. She lost in the next round to eventual champion and world No. 14, [Aryna Sabalenka](50222577.md).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] She also reached the semifinals of [Luxembourg Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_BGL_Luxembourg_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles).[^cite_note-38] Rybakina finished the season at No. 37 in the world.[^cite_note-wta-rankings-22][^cite_note-39]

### 2020: WTA 250 title, top 20

Rybakina led the WTA Tour in finals during the 2020 season, and finished tied for second in match wins.[^cite_note-2020-stats-40][^cite_note-2020-summary-41] She reached the finals at four of her first five events. Before the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) led to the shutdown of the WTA Tour for more than five months, she had reached the final of every tournament except for the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Australian_Open) and the [Qatar Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Qatar_Total_Open), losing to world No. 1, [Ashleigh Barty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashleigh_Barty), in both instances, the latter in a [walkover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkover) due to an abductor strain in her leg.[^cite_note-barty-loss-1-42][^cite_note-43] Prior to the Australian Open, her two finals came at International events. She reached her first final of the year at the [Shenzhen Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_WTA_Shenzhen_Open) defeating [Karolína Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1),[^cite_note-44] but lost to [Ekaterina Alexandrova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekaterina_Alexandrova).[^cite_note-\:2-45] She defeated [Zhang Shuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Shuai) to win her second WTA title at the [Hobart International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Hobart_International).[^cite_note-\:3-46] At Melbourne, she recorded her first two Grand Slam main-draw match wins against [Bernarda Pera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernarda_Pera) and [Greet Minnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greet_Minnen),[^cite_note-47] before losing to world No. 1, Ashleigh Barty.[^cite_note-barty-loss-1-42] Following the tournament, she reached two Premier finals at the [St. Petersburg Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_St._Petersburg_Ladies'_Trophy) finishing runner-up to No. 8 [Kiki Bertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiki_Bertens), and at the [Dubai Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Dubai_Tennis_Championships) losing to No. 2 Simona Halep in the WTA Match of The Year.[^cite_note-\:5-48][^cite_note-49] At Dubai in particular, Rybakina defeated two top-ten players in No. 7 [Sofia Kenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Kenin) and No. 3 Karolína Plíšková, the latter of whom was the highest ranked player she had defeated so far.[^cite_note-kenin-50][^cite_note-\:4-51] These four finals helped her climb to No. 17 in the world at the time of the tour shutdown.[^cite_note-wta-rankings-22] She also became the first Kazakhstani player in the top 20 in history.[^cite_note-52] She later withdrew from the [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) after defeating [Sorana Cîrstea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorana_C%C3%AErstea) and [Alison Van Uytvanck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alison_Van_Uytvanck).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

During the bulk of the shutdown, Rybakina stayed in Moscow and did not have the opportunity to practice for two and a half months. She eventually resumed training in [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava), Slovakia for five weeks.[^cite_note-pandemic-interview-55] When the tour resumed in New York in August, she lost her return match to Alexandrova and only recorded one match win at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_US_Open_\(tennis\)).[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] Back in Europe, she finally defeated Alexandrova at the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Italian_Open_\(tennis\)) in her third opportunity of the year before squandering a chance to serve out the match in a third-round loss to compatriot [Yulia Putintseva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Putintseva).[^cite_note-58] At the [Internationaux de Strasbourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Internationaux_de_Strasbourg), Rybakina reached her fifth final of the year and first since the resumption of the tour, losing to No. 5, [Elina Svitolina](27630639.md).[^cite_note-59] However, she then lost to [Fiona Ferro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiona_Ferro) in the second round at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_French_Open).[^cite_note-60]

### 2021: French Open quarterfinal, Olympics fourth place



Rybakina at the 2021 French Open

Rybakina started her season at [Abu Dhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Abu_Dhabi_Women's_Tennis_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) losing to eventual champion Aryna Sabalenka in the quarterfinals.[^cite_note-61] She then lost to [Fiona Ferro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiona_Ferro) in the second round of [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). After an uneventful stretch of the year where she fell out of top 20, she won two consecutive matches for the first time since Abu Dhabi to reach the third round of [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). She then defeated [Elena Vesnina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elena_Vesnina) and [Serena Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serena_Williams) to reach her first major quarterfinal.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] At the same tournament, she also reached the quarterfinals in doubles, partnering [Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Pavlyuchenkova); incidentally, Pavlyuchenkova was the opponent who defeated her in the quarterfinals of the singles portion of the 2021 French Open.[^cite_note-64]

Rybakina recorded her first top-10 win on grass at the [Eastbourne International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Eastbourne_International) defeating second seed [Elina Svitolina](27630639.md) in straight sets.[^cite_note-65] She reached her first semifinal of the year defeating lucky loser [Anastasija Sevastova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasija_Sevastova) before falling to eventual champion [Jelena Ostapenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelena_Ostapenko).[^cite_note-66] Making her first appearance at the tournament, Rybakina stormed into the fourth round of [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) without dropping a set.[^cite_note-67] There she lost to world No. 2, Aryna Sabalenka, in a tough three-set battle.[^cite_note-68] She made her comeback in top 20 on the back of strong grass season.

Rybakina was the 15th seed of the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_at_the_2020_Summer_Olympics) tennis tournament, defeating [Samantha Stosur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samantha_Stosur),[^cite_note-69] [Rebecca Peterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebecca_Peterson),[^cite_note-70] [Donna Vekić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Veki%C4%87),[^cite_note-71] and [Garbiñe Muguruza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza)[^cite_note-72] all in straight sets to reach the semifinals, where she was defeated by Belinda Bencic.[^cite_note-73] In the bronze medal match, Rybakina was defeated in a comeback by Elina Svitolina.[^cite_note-74] She lost in the third round of [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) to 12th seed Simona Halep. She reached the semifnals of [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Chicago_Fall_Tennis_Classic) after the retirement of her opponent Belinda Bencic,[^cite_note-75] where she retired against [Ons Jabeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ons_Jabeur) because of gastrointestinal illness.[^cite_note-76] She also made the quarters of [Ostrava Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_J\&T_Banka_Ostrava_Open), losing to [Iga Świątek](51838558.md).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

On 1 November 2021, she made her debut in the top 15, at world No. 14, becoming the highest ranked Kazakhstani player in history.[^cite_note-79]

### 2022: Wimbledon champion

Main article: [2022 Elena Rybakina tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Elena_Rybakina_tennis_season)

Rybakina started her 2022 season at the [Adelaide International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Adelaide_International_1_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) where she defeated wildcard [Storm Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Sanders),[^cite_note-80] qualifier [Marie Bouzková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Bouzkov%C3%A1),[^cite_note-81] [Shelby Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelby_Rogers),[^cite_note-82] and [Misaki Doi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misaki_Doi)[^cite_note-83] enroute to the final where she was defeated by world No. 1, [Ashleigh Barty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashleigh_Barty).[^cite_note-84] Her success continued at the [Sydney Tennis Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Sydney_Tennis_Classic_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) with a lopsided first-round defeat of reigning US Open champion [Emma Raducanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Raducanu),[^cite_note-85] before she subsequently withdrew from the tournament citing a thigh injury.[^cite_note-86] She reached a career-high ranking of No. 12 on 17 January 2022.[^cite_note-#rankings-87]

Her remaining early hardcourt season saw little progress. She defeated [Zarina Diyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarina_Diyas),[^cite_note-88] before a second-round retirement against [Zhang Shuai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Shuai) at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) due to thigh injury.[^cite_note-89] She defeated [Varvara Gracheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varvara_Gracheva),[^cite_note-90] but withdrew before the second round at the [St. Petersburg Ladies' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_St._Petersburg_Ladies'_Trophy_%E2%80%93_Singles).[^cite_note-91] She lost in the first round to [Jacqueline Cristian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqueline_Cristian) at the [Qatar Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Qatar_Total_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles).[^cite_note-92] Her "Sunshine Double" ([Miami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Open_\(tennis\)) and Indian Wells) saw improvement with a quarterfinal appearance at the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) against [Maria Sakkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sakkari) and a third-round appearance to [Jessica Pegula](32944701.md) at the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

Her clay-court season began with a second-round loss to [Anhelina Kalinina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhelina_Kalinina) after a first-round bye at [Charleston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Credit_One_Charleston_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles). Following this, she represented [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan_Billie_Jean_King_Cup_team) as the team's top seed and won both of her singles matches in a tie against [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_Billie_Jean_King_Cup_team) securing a berth in the [finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Billie_Jean_King_Cup) later in the year. The remainder of her clay-court season saw little achievement as she failed to advance into the quarterfinals at the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles), [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Italian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), and [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles).



Rybakina with the [Venus Rosewater Dish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_Rosewater_Dish) at the 2022 Wimbledon Championships

Rybakina's grass-court season in the lead up to Wimbledon saw a second-round loss to [Shelby Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelby_Rogers) at the [Rosmalen Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Lib%C3%A9ma_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) followed by a first-round bye and a second-round loss to [Lesia Tsurenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesia_Tsurenko) at the [Eastbourne International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Eastbourne_International_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles). At the [Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she reached her second Grand Slam quarterfinal, defeating [CoCo Vandeweghe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoCo_Vandeweghe),[^cite_note-95] [Bianca Andreescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bianca_Andreescu),[^cite_note-96] [Zheng Qinwen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zheng_Qinwen)[^cite_note-97] and [Petra Martić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_Marti%C4%87).[^cite_note-98] She then defeated [Ajla Tomljanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajla_Tomljanovi%C4%87) in her quarterfinal match to reach her first major semifinal, becoming the first Kazakhstani singles player (male or female) to reach the semifinal of a Grand Slam.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] She then defeated Simona Halep in straight sets, to reach her first major final, becoming the youngest Wimbledon finalist since [Garbiñe Muguruza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garbi%C3%B1e_Muguruza) in 2015.[^cite_note-101] After dropping the first set, she defeated No. 3 seeded [Ons Jabeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ons_Jabeur) in three sets to secure her first major title.[^cite_note-espnrybakinachampion-102] She became the youngest women's champion since 21-year-old [Petra Kvitová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_Kvitov%C3%A1) in 2011. She was also the fourth-youngest active Grand Slam champion, older only than [Iga Świątek](51838558.md), Bianca Andreescu and Emma Raducanu.[^cite_note-103] Rybakina's Russian nationality and previous international representation of Russia became a matter of public discourse in the aftermath of her success at Wimbledon 2022, where Wimbledon had [banned athletes representing Russia and Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wimbledon_ban_on_Russian_and_Belarusian_players) due to [Russia's invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105] [Russian state media](15861222.md) celebrated Rybakina's win as a national victory despite her longstanding decision not to represent the country of her birth.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

The North American hardcourt season began with early losses at the [Silicon Valley Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Silicon_Valley_Classic_%E2%80%93_Singles) (first round) and [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_National_Bank_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) (second). Her US Open preparations continued at the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Western_&_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) where she advanced to the quarterfinals and was defeated by [Madison Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Keys). Her season continued next with an entry to the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) as the 25th seed; however, she lost in the first round to qualifier [Clara Burel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Burel).[^cite_note-108] She bounced back reaching the finals of [Portorož Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Zavarovalnica_Sava_Portoro%C5%BE_%E2%80%93_Singles) in Slovenia, where she lost to [Kateřina Siniaková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kate%C5%99ina_Siniakov%C3%A1) in three sets.[^cite_note-109] Since her Wimbledon win didn't reward any points, Rybakina failed to qualify for [WTA Finals](1356185.md) and finished the year ranked 22.[^cite_note-110]

### 2023: Australian Open final, two WTA 1000 titles, No. 3

Main article: [2023 Elena Rybakina tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Elena_Rybakina_tennis_season)



Rybakina at the 2023 US Open

Rybakina started the season at the [Adelaide International 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Adelaide_International_1_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), defeating [Danielle Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danielle_Collins) in three sets[^cite_note-111] before losing to [Marta Kostyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marta_Kostyuk) in the second round.[^cite_note-112] She followed it with another loss in the [Adelaide International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Adelaide_International_2_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) to Petra Kvitová, in straight sets. However, she reached the doubles final with partner [Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Pavlyuchenkova). At the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Rybakina defeated 2022 finalist Danielle Collins again in the third round,[^cite_note-113] and world No. 1, Iga Świątek, in the fourth round[^cite_note-114] becoming the first Kazakhstani woman to reach an Australian Open quarterfinal.[^cite_note-115] Next, she defeated former French Open champion Jeļena Ostapenko to reach her first Australian Open semifinal.[^cite_note-116] In the semifinals, she defeated former world No. 1 and two-time Australian Open champion, [Victoria Azarenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka), in straight sets, reaching the second Grand Slam final of her career.[^cite_note-117] However, despite winning the first set, Rybakina ultimately lost the championship match to Aryna Sabalenka in a high quality encounter.[^cite_note-118] She reached the top 10 on 30 January 2023 making her the first player representing Kazakhstan, male or female, ever to reach the top 10 on either the ATP or WTA rankings.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

In [Abu Dhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Abu_Dhabi_Open), defeating Karolina Plíšková,[^cite_note-121] she made the quarterfinals where she lost to [Beatriz Haddad Maia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatriz_Haddad_Maia).[^cite_note-122] In [Dubai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Dubai_Tennis_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), she reached the third round by beating [Bianca Andreescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bianca_Andreescu)[^cite_note-123] and [Marie Bouzková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Bouzkov%C3%A1)[^cite_note-124] in straight sets. She withdrew from her third round match against fifth seed [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff) due to a lower-back injury.[^cite_note-125] At [Indian Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), as the tenth seed, she made her second consecutive Indian Wells quarterfinal defeating [Sofia Kenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Kenin),[^cite_note-126] 21st seed Paula Badosa[^cite_note-127] and [Varvara Gracheva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varvara_Gracheva). Then, she defeated [Karolína Muchová](55266410.md) to reach her first WTA 1000 semifinal.[^cite_note-128] In the semifinals, she moved past the defending champion Świątek, in straight sets, for the second time in 2023 to reach her first WTA 1000 final.[^cite_note-129] In the final, she edged second seed Aryna Sabalenka in straight sets, reversing the result of their matchup in the Australian Open final for her first WTA 1000 title.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] This result pushed her singles ranking to a new career-high of world No. 7.[^cite_note-132]

Rybakina saved match point to defeat [Paula Badosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Badosa) in the third round of [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Miami_Open).[^cite_note-133] She then defetaed [Elise Mertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Mertens),[^cite_note-134] [Martina Trevisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Trevisan), and [Jessica Pegula](32944701.md)[^cite_note-135] to reach the final. In the final, she lost to 15th seed Petra Kvitová, preventing her from completing the Sunshine Double and ending her career-best 13 match winning streak.[^cite_note-136] She then recorded early second round exits at the [Stuttgart Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix_%E2%80%93_Singles) and the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Mutua_Madrid_Open) to [Beatriz Haddad Maia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatriz_Haddad_Maia) and [Anna Kalinskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Kalinskaya), respectively, and reached a new career-high of world No. 6 on 8 May 2023. She then reached the quarterfinals at the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Italian_Open) and moved to a new career high ranking of world No. 5 with a win over [Jasmine Paolini](46663470.md), Anna Kalinskaya by retirement and [Markéta Vondroušová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark%C3%A9ta_Vondrou%C5%A1ov%C3%A1). Next she defeated world No. 1, Iga Świątek (who retired during the deciding set due to injury) for the third time to reach her first Rome semifinal.[^cite_note-137] Rybakina reached her third WTA 1000 final of the season defeating Jeļena Ostapenko, becoming the third player in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era) to reach the final in the same season at the Australian Open, Indian Wells and Miami Open plus Rome, after [Monica Seles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monica_Seles) in 1991 and [Maria Sharapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sharapova) in 2012.[^cite_note-138] She won her first WTA 1000 clay title, after Anhelina Kalinina retired in the second set, and moved to a career-high ranking of world No. 4, on 22 May 2023.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] At the [2023 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open), despite her walkover due to respiratory illness in the third round,[^cite_note-141] she reached No. 3 in the world rankings on 12 June 2023.[^cite_note-142] Rybakina then played in the WTA 500-level Berlin [Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_WTA_German_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles) on grass, losing to [Donna Vekić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donna_Veki%C4%87) in the second round.[^cite_note-143] On 26 June 2023, Rybakina announced her withdrawal from the [Eastbourne International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastbourne_International) tournament with a viral illness.[^cite_note-144]

Returning to [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Wimbledon_Championships) as defending champion, Rybakina defeated [Shelby Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelby_Rogers),[^cite_note-145][Alizé Cornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aliz%C3%A9_Cornet),[^cite_note-146] home-favourite [Katie Boulter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Boulter),[^cite_note-147] and [Beatriz Haddad Maia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatriz_Haddad_Maia)[^cite_note-148] to reach the quarterfinals where she lost to Ons Jabeur in three sets in the rematch of last year's final.[^cite_note-149]

Following her quarterfinal loss at Wimbledon, she reached the semifinals at the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Open_\(tennis\)) for the first time in her career. She overcame [Jennifer Brady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Brady) and [Sloane Stephens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloane_Stephens) before defeating [Daria Kasatkina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daria_Kasatkina) in a match that lasted 3 hours and 27 minutes, the longest match of Rybakina's career.[^cite_note-150] The match finished at 2\:55am local time. In the semifinals Rybakina lost to [Liudmila Samsonova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liudmila_Samsonova) in three sets reporting that she felt 'destroyed' physically in the post-match press conference because of the scheduling issues she faced during the tournament.[^cite_note-151]

Rybakina reached the third round of the [Cincinnati Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Western_&_Southern_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), before retiring in the second set against [Jasmine Paolini](46663470.md).[^cite_note-152] She then reached the third round of the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) as the fourth seed, losing to [Sorana Cîrstea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorana_C%C3%AErstea) in three sets.[^cite_note-153]

At the [Beijing Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_China_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), Rybakina recorded a straight-sets win over top seed Sabalenka in the quarterfinals, before losing to [Liudmila Samsonova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liudmila_Samsonova) in the semifinals.[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155]

In November, Rybakina played in her first WTA Finals, where she was eliminated in the group stage.[^cite_note-156]

### 2024: Wimbledon semifinal, three WTA 500 titles, illnesses

Main article: [2024 Elena Rybakina tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Elena_Rybakina_tennis_season)



Rybakina at [Brisbane International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brisbane_International)

Rybakina started her 2024 season at the [Brisbane International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Brisbane_International) where she defeated qualifier [Olivia Gadecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivia_Gadecki),[^cite_note-157] [Elise Mertens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elise_Mertens),[^cite_note-158] to reach quarterfinals where her opponent [Anastasia Potapova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Potapova) retired in second set,[^cite_note-159] and then defeated [Linda Nosková](67929086.md)[^cite_note-160] to set up rematch of last year's Australian Open final against Aryna Sabalenka, which he lost. She avenged her loss defeating Sabalenka in straight sets to win the title without dropping a set in the entire tournament.[^cite_note-161] She saved set points to defeat former World No.1 [Karolína Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1) in straight sets,[^cite_note-162] before going out in the second round at the [Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Australian_Open) to [Anna Blinkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Blinkova) in a match that featured the longest tie-break in Grand Slam history.[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164]

Rybakina won her second title at the [Abu Dhabi Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Abu_Dhabi_Open) in February, beating Danielle Collins from a set down,[^cite_note-165] [Cristina Bucsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Bucsa),[^cite_note-166] Ludmilla Samsonova,[^cite_note-167] and [Daria Kasatkina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daria_Kasatkina).[^cite_note-168] She then defeated [Zhu Lin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Lin_\(tennis\)), [Emma Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Navarro),[^cite_note-169] [Leylah Fernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leylah_Fernandez), and [Anastasia Pavlyuchenkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_Pavlyuchenkova)[^cite_note-170] to reach the final of the WTA 1000 [Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_Qatar_Open), losing to Iga Świątek, and reached the quarterfinals of the Dubai Open before withdrawing due to illness.[^cite_note-171]

As defending champion of the [Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Wells_Open), Rybakina was forced to withdraw from the tournament shortly before her first match due to illness.[^cite_note-172] She then played in the [Miami Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Miami_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles), where she defeated qualifier [Clara Tauson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clara_Tauson) in a comeback three setter,[^cite_note-173] another qualifier [Taylor Townsend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Townsend) again in three sets, [Madison Keys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Keys) in straight sets to reach quarterfinals.[^cite_note-174]She then defeated [Maria Sakkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Sakkari)[^cite_note-175] and [Victoria Azarenka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Azarenka)[^cite_note-176] both in three sets to reach the finals for the second consecutive year before losing to Danielle Collins.[^cite_note-177]

In April, Rybakina secured her third title of the season at the [Porsche Tennis Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Porsche_Tennis_Grand_Prix) in Stuttgart, beating [Veronika Kudermetova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veronika_Kudermetova) in second round,[^cite_note-178] [Jasmine Paolini](46663470.md) in quarterfinals,[^cite_note-179] two-time defending champion Iga Świątek in the semifinals,[^cite_note-180] all in three sets and then Marta Kostyuk in straight sets in the final.[^cite_note-181] She then played at the [Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Mutua_Madrid_Open), beating [Lucia Bronzetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucia_Bronzetti),[^cite_note-182] [Mayar Sherif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayar_Sherif),[^cite_note-183] and qualifier [Sára Bejlek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A1ra_Bejlek) all in straight sets to make it to quarterfinals,[^cite_note-184] where she defeated compatriot [Yulia Putintseva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulia_Putintseva) while saving two match points,[^cite_note-185] before falling to second seed Aryna Sabalenka in three sets in the semifinals. She was forced to withdraw from the [Italian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Italian_Open_\(tennis\)) because of illness, where she was the defending champion.[^cite_note-186] She made it through to the quarterfinals at the [French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_French_Open), before losing to eventual runner-up Jasmine Paolini.[^cite_note-187]

During the grass-court swing of the year, Rybakina retired due to illness during her quarterfinal match against Victoria Azarenka at the [Berlin Ladies Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Berlin_Ladies_Open)[^cite_note-188] and then withdrew from the following week's [Eastbourne International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Eastbourne_International).[^cite_note-189] At [Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Wimbledon_Championships), she steamrollered former world No. 1 [Caroline Wozniacki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Wozniacki), in just 57 minutes and for the loss of only one game in the third round,[^cite_note-190] before winning her next match when opponent [Anna Kalinskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Kalinskaya) retired injured in the second set.[^cite_note-191] Rybakina then beat Elina Svitolina in the quarterfinals[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] before losing to [Barbora Krejčíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1) in the semifinals in a three-set match.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195]

Rybakina withdrew from the [Paris Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Olympics) two days before it got underway due to acute bronchitis.[^cite_note-196] She defeated qualifier [Destanee Aiava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destanee_Aiava) in the first round at the [US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_US_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles)[^cite_note-197] but then withdrew from the tournament due to unspecified injuries.[^cite_note-198] On 23 September, Rybakina announced she would miss the entire Asian part of the season due to a back injury.[^cite_note-199]

Rybakina played her first match since August at the [WTA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_WTA_Finals_%E2%80%93_Singles) in November, losing her opening group contest to Jasmine Paolini in straight sets.[^cite_note-200] She lost her second group match in three sets to Zheng Qinwen.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202] Despite defeating world No. 1, Aryna Sabalenka, in her final group match, Rybakina failed to reach the semifinals.[^cite_note-203]

### 2025: WTA Finals champion

Main article: [2025 Elena Rybakina tennis season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Elena_Rybakina_tennis_season)

Rybakina began her season at the [2025 United Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_United_Cup), reaching the semi-finals with team Kazakhstan. ## Playing style

Elena Rybakina serving a tennis ball at the 2024 Brisbane International

Rybakina serving at the 2024 Brisbane International

With a powerful serve, Rybakina is an [aggressive baseliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggressive_baseliner) who aims to finish points quickly, and whose high risk game style leads to an accumulation of both winners and unforced errors. She can generate effortless power, both on [groundstrokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groundstroke) and her [serve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serve_\(tennis\)).[^cite_note-baseliner-323][^cite_note-21-and-under-324][^cite_note-325] Her [forehand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forehand) and [backhand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backhand) are both hit flat, with relentless depth and speed, allowing her to generate excellent power with both her groundstrokes, and she can hit [winners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winner_\(tennis\)) with both shots.[^cite_note-profiler-326] Her serve, which is capable of reaching 127 mph (204 km/h),[^cite_note-espnrybakinachampion-102] allows her to serve a large number of [aces.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ace_\(tennis\)) She led the tour in the ace count in both 2020, with 192 aces, and in 2025 with 516, when she became the first player since [Karolína Plíšková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol%C3%ADna_Pl%C3%AD%C5%A1kov%C3%A1) in 2016 to hit over 500 aces in a single season.[^cite_note-327][^cite_note-stats-2020-328][^cite_note-329] She had hit 2,513 aces on WTA Tour in her career, 9th highest ace count on tour since 2008. She also has good movement given her height, although this is one of the few weak areas in her game.[^cite_note-now-330] Adriano Albanesi, a WTA coach, described her as "a right-handed [\[Petra\] Kvitová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_Kvitov%C3%A1)".[^cite_note-331] Rybakina plays with a very calm demeanour, and believes she can defeat any opponent.[^cite_note-kenin-50][^cite_note-21-and-under-324][^cite_note-calm-332]

## Coaches

Rybakina hired [Andrei Chesnokov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Chesnokov), whom she had already trained with at Spartak Tennis Club, to be her private coach in 2018 at the age of 18. This was the first time she had an individual coach. Chesnokov only coached in Moscow and did not travel with her to tournaments.[^cite_note-get-to-know-6][^cite_note-ubi-9] In February 2019, Rybakina switched coaches to [Stefano Vukov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefano_Vukov), a Croatian former tennis player who briefly competed mainly on the [ITF Futures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITF_Futures) tour.[^cite_note-school-12][^cite_note-333] With Vukov as her first travelling coach, Rybakina rapidly improved, rising from just inside the top 200 of the WTA rankings into the top 30 in about a year.[^cite_note-get-to-know-6][^cite_note-ubi-9] In November 2024, she announced [Goran Ivanišević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goran_Ivani%C5%A1evi%C4%87) as her new coach having split from Vukov after that year's US Open.[^cite_note-334] They worked together until her [2025 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Open) exit.[^cite_note-335]

In January 2025, Rybakina rehired Vukov as an additional coach; he was subsequently suspended by the [WTA](57768.md) under a code of conduct investigation,[^cite_note-336] and later banned for 12 months, barring him from gaining accreditation at WTA–sanctioned events.[^cite_note-337] In his absence, Rybakina hired Italian coach and former ATP tour player [Davide Sanguinetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Sanguinetti).[^cite_note-338] Vukov's ban was removed in August that same year, and he returned to coaching Rybakina.[^cite_note-339] According to *[the Athletic](54936152.md)*, Rybakina told several people that "her relationship with \[Vukov] had become personal and romantic"; they were said to have shared a hotel room in Melbourne during the Australian Open.[^cite_note-340]

At the end of 2025, Rybakina also added Ukrainian tennis coach and former player Stanislav Khmarskyi to her coaching team.[^cite_note-341]

Rybakina often practices in [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava), Slovakia during the European clay swing and grass season for several weeks each year, calling the city her 'second home' in an interview to *Slovak Daily*.[^cite_note-342][^cite_note-343][^cite_note-344]

## Rivalries

### Aryna Sabalenka

Main article: [Rybakina–Sabalenka rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rybakina%E2%80%93Sabalenka_rivalry)

Rybakina has a prominent rivalry with [Aryna Sabalenka](50222577.md), with the pair having faced off in three major finals. They have played each other 18 times since 2019, with Sabalenka leading their overall head-to-head 10–8 whereas Rybakina leads the record in finals 5–2.[^cite_note-345] Both players are known for their aggressive baseline play, resulting in fast-paced and intense matches. The rivalry is considered one of the best of its time.[^cite_note-346]

They faced off in three major finals, the [2023 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Australian_Open_-_Women's_singles),[^cite_note-347] won by Sabalenka, the [2026 Australian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Australian_Open_-_Women's_singles),[^cite_note-348] and the [2026 US Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_US_Open_-_Women's_singles),[^cite_note-#USO26Final-320] the latter two both won by Rybakina.

Their other notable encounters include the [2023 Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles)[^cite_note-349] final, [2024 Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Mutua_Madrid_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles)[^cite_note-350] semifinal, [2025 WTA Finals](81306023.md)[^cite_note-351] final, and the [2026 Indian Wells Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_BNP_Paribas_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles)[^cite_note-352] final.[^cite_note-353]

### Iga Świątek

Rybakina has also developed a competitive rivalry with [Iga Świątek](51838558.md). They have met 14 times since 2021, with Świątek leading their head-to-head at 8–6 in official competitions.[^cite_note-354] Their matches are highly anticipated due to their contrasting playing styles and high rankings.[^cite_note-355] Rybakina had led earlier in their careers before Świątek began to pull ahead. Świątek defeated Rybakina to win her seventh [WTA 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_1000_tournaments) title at the [2024 Qatar Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Qatar_TotalEnergies_Open_%E2%80%93_Singles).[^cite_note-356] After 4 consecutive defeats to Świątek in 2025, Rybakina came back from a set down to earn her 5th victory against her in the group stage of [2025 WTA Finals](81306023.md) in Riyadh.[^cite_note-357] Rybakina would again defeat Świątek, in straight sets, in the quarterfinals of the [2026 Australian Open](81715708.md),[^cite_note-358] which she would eventually win. In August 2026, Świątek defeated Rybakina in straight sets to win the [Canadian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_National_Bank_Open) in Toronto, her first WTA tour-level title of 2026.[^cite_note-359]

## Endorsements

Rybakina has been sponsored by [Yonex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yonex) for clothing and shoes since the [2023 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_French_Open).[^cite_note-360] She had previously been endorsed by [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas) from the start of 2020, and by [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.).[^cite_note-adidas-361] She uses a Yonex VCore 100 racket.[^cite_note-362]

From 2023 through 2025 she was sponsored by [Red Bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull),[^cite_note-363] and has been partnered with Swiss luxury watch manufacturer Vanguart since the beginning of 2026.[^cite_note-364]

On 24 January 2023, Rybakina became an ambassador for [Bank RBK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_RBK?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-365] Since September 2024, she has also served as the official brand ambassador for [Lexus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lexus). As a part of the collaboration, Rybakina teams up with the brand to support the 'Tennis for Life by Lexus' program.[^cite_note-366] She is also sponsored by Kazakhstani company IZI.[^cite_note-Instagram-367]

Rybakina was ranked as the 9th highest-paid female athlete in 2023.[^cite_note-368] After falling to 15th next year,[^cite_note-369] she made a comeback in the top 10 as 8th highest-paid in 2025 following her win at the [2025 WTA Finals](78728546.md), which earned her the single largest single paycheck in women's sporting history.[^cite_note-370][^cite_note-Instagram-367]

## Off the court

### Philanthropy



Rybakina on a 2023 stamp of Kazakhstan

Following her [2022 Wimbledon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Wimbledon_Championships) victory, Rybakina announced that she will donate a part of the bonus she received from [Kazakhstan Tennis Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstan_Tennis_Federation) (KTF) to support young tennis players.[^cite_note-371] In March 2023, Rybakina donated [₸](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakhstani_tenge)35 million to rising female tennis players in Kazakhstan. She, also donated money to StopOtlov volunteering movement which works for animals welfare.[^cite_note-372] President of KTF [Bulat Utemuratov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulat_Utemuratov) said she also has two charities in Kazakhstan, one funds young and promising tennis players while other supports less privileged people.[^cite_note-373]
