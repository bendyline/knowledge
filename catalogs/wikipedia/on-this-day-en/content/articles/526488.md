The gold, [silver](526647.md) and [bronze medals](526648.md) of the [1964 Olympic Winter Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Winter_Olympics) ([Olympic Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Museum)).

A **gold medal** is a [medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal) awarded for highest achievement in a non-military field. Its name derives from the use of at least a fraction of [gold](12240.md) in form of [plating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_plate) or [alloying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alloy) in its manufacture.

Since the eighteenth century, gold medals have been awarded in the arts, for example, by the [Royal Danish Academy of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Danish_Academy_of_Fine_Arts), usually as a symbol of an award to give an outstanding student some financial freedom. Others offer only the [prestige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reputation) of the award. Many organizations now award gold medals either annually or extraordinarily, including various academic societies.

While some gold medals are solid gold, others are [gold-plated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold-plated) or [silver-gilt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver-gilt), like those of the [Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_gold_medal), the [Lorentz Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorentz_Medal), the United States [Congressional Gold Medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Gold_Medal) and the [Nobel Prize](21201.md) medal. Nobel Prize medals consist of 18 [karat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karat_\(purity\)) [green gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_gold) plated with 24 karat gold. Before 1980, they were struck in 23 karat gold.

## Military origins

Before the establishment of standard [military awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_award), e.g., the [Medal of Honor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medal_of_Honor), it was common practice to have a medal specially created to provide national recognition for a significant military or naval victory or accomplishment. In the United States, [Congress](31756.md) would enact a resolution asking the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_President) to reward those responsible. The [commanding officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commanding_officer) would receive a gold medal and his [officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_\(military\)) would get [silver medals](526647.md).[^cite_note-1]

## Competition medals



A medal on a ribbon designed to be worn around the winner's neck.

Medals have historically been given as prizes in various types of competitive activities, especially athletics.

Traditionally, medals are made of the following metals:

1. [Gold](12240.md) (or another yellow metal, e.g., [brass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brass))

2. [Silver](27119.md) (or another grey metal, e.g., [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel))

3. [Bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze)

Rarely, [Platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum) medals can also be awarded.

These metals designate the first three [Ages of Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ages_of_Man) in [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology): the [Golden Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Age), when men lived among the gods, the [Silver Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_Age), where youth lasted a hundred years, and the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Heroic_Age), the era of heroes.

The custom of awarding the sequence of gold, [silver](526647.md), and [bronze medals](526648.md) for the first three highest achievers dates from at least the 19th century, with the National Association of Amateur Athletes in the United States awarding such medals as early as 1884.[^cite_note-2]

This standard was adopted for Olympic competition at the [1904 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics) in [St. Louis](27687.md). At the [1896 event](48644.md), silver was awarded to winners and bronze to runners-up, while in [1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_Summer_Olympics) other prizes were given, not medals.

## Olympic Games

Main article: [Olympic medal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_medal)



At the [1896 Summer Olympics](48644.md), the winners received a [silver medal](526647.md). Since [1904](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics), the winners receive a gold medal, the second-place finishers receive a [silver medal](526647.md) and the third-place finishers receive a [bronze medal](526648.md).

At the modern [Olympic Games](22576.md), winners of a sporting discipline receive a gold medal in recognition of their achievement.

At the [Ancient Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Olympic_Games), only one winner per event received an award, which was a [kotinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotinos), an olive wreath made of wild olive leaves from a sacred tree near the temple of Zeus at Olympia. [Aristophanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristophanes) in [Plutus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutus_\(play\)) makes a remark why victorious athletes are crowned with wreaths made of wild olive instead of gold.[^cite_note-3] [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) describes a story that explains why there were only a few Greek men at the [Battle of Thermopylae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Thermopylae) since "all other men were participating in the Olympic Games" and that the prize for the winner was "an olive-wreath". When [Tigranes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigranes), an Armenian general, learned this, he uttered to his leader: "Good heavens! What kind of men are these against whom you have brought us to fight? Men who do not compete for possessions, but for honour".[^cite_note-4]

At the [1896 Summer Olympics](48644.md), winners received a [silver medal](526647.md) and the second-place finisher received a [bronze medal](526648.md). In [1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1900_Summer_Olympics), most winners received cups or trophies instead of medals. The next three Olympics ([1904](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1904_Summer_Olympics), [1908](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1908_Summer_Olympics), [1912](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1912_Summer_Olympics)) awarded the winners solid gold medals, but the medals themselves were smaller. The use of gold rapidly declined with the onset of the [First World War](4764461.md) and also with the onset of the [Second World War](32927.md).[^cite_note-5] The last series of Olympic medals to be made of solid gold were awarded at the 1912 Summer Olympics in [Stockholm](26741.md), [Sweden](5058739.md).

Olympic gold medals are required to be made from at least [92.5% silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_silver), and must contain a minimum of 6 grams of gold.[^cite_note-history-6] All Olympic medals must be at least 70mm in diameter and 3mm thick.[^cite_note-7] [Minting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_\(coin\)) the medals is the responsibility of the Olympic host. From [1928](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1928_Summer_Olympics) through [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Summer_Olympics) the design was always the same: the obverse showed a generic design by [Florentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) artist [Giuseppe Cassioli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Cassioli) of Greek goddess [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike_\(mythology\)) with Rome's [Colosseum](49603.md) in the background and text naming the host city; the reverse showed another generic design of Nike saluting an Olympic champion.

From the [1972 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics) through [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics), Cassioli's design (or a slight modification) remained on the obverse with a custom design by the host city on the reverse. Noting that Cassioli's design showed a Roman amphitheater for what originally were Greek games, a new obverse design was commissioned for the [2004 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympics) in [Athens](1216.md). For the [2008 Beijing Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics) medals had a diameter of 70mm and were 6mm thick, with the front displaying a winged figure of victory and the back showed a Beijing Olympics symbol surrounded by an inset jade circle.

[Winter Olympics](34069.md) medals have been of more varied design. The silver and bronze medals have always borne the same designs.

## Other gold medal awards

The award of a gold medal, often coupled with the award of silver and bronze medals to the next place finishers, has been adopted in other sports competitions (such as the [Dr.M.A Wazed Miah Gold Medal](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_M_A_Wazed_Miah_Gold_Medal) ,[Mediterranean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_Games)), and in other competitive fields, such as music and writing, as well as some competitive games. Typically bronze medals are awarded only to third place, but in some contests there is some variety, such as International [barbershop music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbershop_music) contests where bronze medals are awarded for third, fourth, and fifth place.
