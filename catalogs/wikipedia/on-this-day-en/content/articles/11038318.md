**Methamphetamine**[^cite_note-25] is a [central nervous system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_nervous_system) (CNS) [stimulant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulant) that is primarily used as a [recreational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_drug_use) or [performance-enhancing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance-enhancing_substance) drug and less commonly as a [second-line treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-line_treatment) for [attention deficit hyperactivity disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attention_deficit_hyperactivity_disorder) (ADHD).[^cite_note-d-meth_review-26] It has also been researched as a potential treatment for [traumatic brain injury](1057414.md).[^cite_note-pmid25724762-7] Methamphetamine was discovered in 1893 and exists as two [enantiomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer): [levo-methamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levo-methamphetamine) and dextro-methamphetamine.[^cite_note-28] *Methamphetamine* properly refers to a specific chemical substance, the [racemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racemic_mixture) [free base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_base), which is an equal mixture of levomethamphetamine and dextromethamphetamine in their pure amine forms, but the [hydrochloride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrochloride) salt, commonly called crystal meth, is widely used. Methamphetamine is rarely prescribed over concerns involving its potential for misuse as an [aphrodisiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphrodisiac) and [euphoriant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoriant), among other concerns, as well as the availability of other drugs with comparable effects and treatment efficacy such as [dextroamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dextroamphetamine) and [lisdexamfetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisdexamfetamine).[^cite_note-d-meth_review-26] While pharmaceutical formulations of methamphetamine in the United States are labeled as methamphetamine hydrochloride, they contain dextromethamphetamine as the [active ingredient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_ingredient).[^cite_note-d-meth_review-26][^cite_note-D-meth_FDA_label-30] Dextromethamphetamine is a stronger CNS stimulant than levomethamphetamine.[^cite_note-d-meth_review-26]

Both racemic methamphetamine and dextromethamphetamine are illicitly trafficked and sold owing to their potential for recreational use and ease of manufacture. The highest prevalence of illegal methamphetamine use occurs in parts of Asia and Oceania, and in the United States, where racemic methamphetamine and dextromethamphetamine are classified as [Schedule II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Schedule_II_drugs_\(US\)) controlled substances. [Levomethamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levomethamphetamine) is available as an [over-the-counter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-the-counter) (OTC) drug for use as an inhaled [nasal decongestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_decongestant) in the United States and is seldom abused.[^cite_note-Barkholtz_2023-31][^cite_note-OTC_levmetamfetamine-34] Internationally, the production, distribution, sale, and possession of methamphetamine is restricted or banned in many countries, owing to its placement in schedule II of the [United Nations Convention on Psychotropic Substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_Psychotropic_Substances) treaty. While dextromethamphetamine is a more potent drug, racemic methamphetamine is illicitly produced more often, owing to the relative ease of [synthesis](#Synthesis) and regulatory limits of [chemical precursor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precursor_\(chemistry\)) availability.

The effects of methamphetamine are nearly identical to other [substituted amphetamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substituted_amphetamine).[^cite_note-Hart_2011-35] In low to moderate and [therapeutic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therapeutic_index) doses (5–25 mg [orally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_administration)),[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] methamphetamine produces typical [SNDRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin%E2%80%93norepinephrine%E2%80%93dopamine_releasing_agent) effects and may [elevate mood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphoria), increase alertness, concentration, and energy, [reduce appetite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anorexia_\(symptom\)), and [promote weight loss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anorectic). In [overdose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_overdose) or during extended [binges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_abuse), it may [induce psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulant_psychosis#Substituted_amphetamines), [breakdown of skeletal muscle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhabdomyolysis), [seizures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalized_epilepsy), and [bleeding in the brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_hemorrhage). Chronic high-dose use can precipitate unpredictable and rapid [mood swings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mood_swing), [stimulant psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulant_psychosis) (e.g., [paranoia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranoia), [hallucinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucination), [delirium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delirium), and [delusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delusion)), and [aggression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggression). Recreationally, methamphetamine's ability to [increase energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_energy) has been reported to lift mood and [increase sexual desire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphrodisiac) to such an extent that users are able to engage in sexual activity continuously for several days while binging the drug.[^cite_note-AP-NBC_2004-36] Methamphetamine is known to possess a high [abuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_abuse) liability (a high likelihood that extratherapeutic use will lead to [compulsive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsive_behavior) drug use) and high [psychological dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychological_dependence) liability (a high likelihood that [withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_withdrawal) symptoms will occur when methamphetamine use ceases). Discontinuing methamphetamine after heavy use may lead to a [post-acute-withdrawal syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-acute-withdrawal_syndrome), which can persist for months beyond the typical withdrawal period. At high doses, like other [substituted amphetamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substituted_amphetamine), methamphetamine is [neurotoxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotoxicity) to human [midbrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midbrain) [dopaminergic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopaminergic_pathways) [neurons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuron) and, to a lesser extent, [serotonergic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin) neurons.[^cite_note-pmid25861156-37][^cite_note-pmid19328213-38][^cite_note-Hart_2011-35] Methamphetamine neurotoxicity causes adverse changes in brain structure and function, such as reductions in [grey matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_matter) volume in several brain regions, as well as adverse changes in markers of metabolic integrity.[^cite_note-pmid19328213-38]

&#x20;Methamphetamine belongs to the [substituted phenethylamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substituted_phenethylamine) and [substituted amphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substituted_amphetamine) [chemical classes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_classification) and as a drug acts as a [serotonin–norepinephrine–dopamine releasing agent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin%E2%80%93norepinephrine%E2%80%93dopamine_releasing_agent). It is related to the other [dimethylphenethylamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimethylphenethylamine) as a [positional isomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positional_isomer) of these compounds, which share the common [chemical formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_formula) [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon)<sub>10</sub>[H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen)<sub>15</sub>[N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen).

## Uses

### Medical



Bottle of 5mg Desoxyn (methamphetamine hydrochloride) tablets

In the United States, methamphetamine hydrochloride, sold under the brand name **Desoxyn**, is [FDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDA)-approved for the treatment of attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD);[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29][^cite_note-pmid22089317-39] however, the FDA notes that the limited therapeutic usefulness of methamphetamine should be weighed against the risks associated with its use.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] To avoid toxicity and risk of side effects, FDA guidelines recommend an initial dose of methamphetamine at doses 5–10 mg/day for ADHD in adults and children over six years of age, which may be increased at weekly intervals of 5 mg, up to 25 mg/day, until optimum clinical response is found; the usual effective dose is around 20–25 mg/day.[^cite_note-d-meth_review-26][^cite_note-pmid25724762-7][^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] Methamphetamine is sometimes prescribed [off-label](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off_label) for [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity), [narcolepsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcolepsy), and [idiopathic hypersomnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idiopathic_hypersomnia).[^cite_note-d-meth_review-26][^cite_note-pmid8341891-40][^cite_note-41] In the United States, [methamphetamine's levorotary form](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levomethamphetamine) is available in some [over-the-counter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-the-counter) (OTC) [nasal decongestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasal_decongestant) products.[^cite_note-d-meth_review-26][^cite_note-OTC_levmetamfetamine-34]

Although the pharmaceutical name "methamphetamine hydrochloride" may suggest a [racemic mixture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racemic_mixture), Desoxyn contains [enantiopure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiopure_drug) dextromethamphetamine, which is a more potent stimulant than both levomethamphetamine and racemic methamphetamine.[^cite_note-d-meth_review-26][^cite_note-D-meth_FDA_label-30] This naming convention deviates from the standard practice observed with other stimulants, such as [Adderall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adderall) and [dextroamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dextroamphetamine), where the dextrorotary [enantiomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enantiomer) is explicitly identified as an [active ingredient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_ingredient) in both [generic](293885.md) and brand-name pharmaceuticals.[^cite_note-methamfetamine_INN-42][^cite_note-Adderall_IR-43][^cite_note-Dextroamphetamine_FDA_label-44]

As methamphetamine is associated with a high potential for misuse, the drug is regulated under the [Controlled Substances Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_Substances_Act) and is [listed under Schedule II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Schedule_II_drugs_\(US\)) in the United States.[^cite_note-\:USAS2-3] Methamphetamine hydrochloride dispensed in the United States is required to include a [boxed warning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxed_warning) regarding its potential for recreational misuse and [addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addiction) liability.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29]

**Desoxyn Gradumet** was an [extended-release](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extended-release) form of the drug. It is no longer produced.[^cite_note-45]

### Recreational

See also: [Party and play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_and_play) and the [Recreational routes of methamphetamine administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_and_culture_of_substituted_amphetamines#Recreational_routes_of_administration)

Methamphetamine is often used recreationally for its effects as a potent euphoriant and stimulant as well as [aphrodisiac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphrodisiac) qualities.[^cite_note-SF_Meth-46]

A subculture known as [party and play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_and_play) is based around sexual activity and methamphetamine use.[^cite_note-SF_Meth-46] Participants in this subculture, which consists mostly of homosexual male methamphetamine users, will typically meet up through [internet dating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_dating) sites and have sex.[^cite_note-SF_Meth-46] Because of its strong stimulant and aphrodisiac effects and inhibitory effect on [ejaculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ejaculation), with repeated use, these sexual encounters will sometimes occur continuously for several days on end.[^cite_note-SF_Meth-46] The crash following the use of methamphetamine in this manner is very often severe, with marked [hypersomnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersomnia) (excessive daytime sleepiness).[^cite_note-SF_Meth-46] The party and play subculture is prevalent in major US cities such as San Francisco and New York City.[^cite_note-SF_Meth-46][^cite_note-47]

## Contraindications

Methamphetamine is [contraindicated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraindicated) in individuals with a history of [substance use disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_use_disorder), [heart disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_disease), or severe [agitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irritability) or anxiety, or in individuals currently experiencing [arteriosclerosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arteriosclerosis), [glaucoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glaucoma), [hyperthyroidism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperthyroidism), or severe [hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertension).[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] The FDA states that individuals who have experienced [hypersensitivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersensitivity) reactions to other stimulants in the past or are currently taking [monoamine oxidase inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_oxidase_inhibitor) should not take methamphetamine.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] The FDA also advises individuals with [bipolar disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bipolar_disorder), [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_depressive_disorder), elevated [blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_pressure), liver or kidney problems, [mania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mania), [psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosis), [Raynaud's phenomenon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raynaud's_phenomenon), [seizures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seizure), [thyroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid) problems, [tics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tic), or [Tourette syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourette_syndrome) to monitor their symptoms while taking methamphetamine.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] Owing to the potential for stunted growth, the FDA advises monitoring the height and weight of growing children and adolescents during treatment.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29]

Main short and long term adverse physical and mental effects that may appear in methamphetamine use

### Physical

#### Cardiovascular

Methamphetamine is a [sympathomimetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympathomimetic_drug) drug that causes [vasoconstriction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasoconstriction) and [tachycardia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachycardia). Methamphetamine also promotes [abnormal extra heartbeats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ectopic_beat) and [irregular heart rhythms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrhythmia), which may be life-threatening.[^cite_note-51]

#### Other physical effects

The effects can also include [loss of appetite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anorexia_\(symptom\)), hyperactivity, [dilated pupils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilated_pupils), [flushed skin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flushing_\(physiology\)), [excessive sweating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diaphoresis), [increased movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychomotor_agitation), dry mouth and [teeth grinding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruxism) (potentially leading to condition informally known as *[meth mouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meth_mouth)*), headache, [rapid breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachypnea), [high body temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperthermia), diarrhea, constipation, [blurred vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blurred_vision), [dizziness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizziness), [twitching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasciculation), [numbness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numbness), [tremors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremor), dry skin, [acne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acne), and [pale appearance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pallor).[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29][^cite_note-Westfall-52] Long-term methamphetamine abuse compounds these effects; [xerodermia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xeroderma) may lead to the development of [sores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulcer_\(dermatology\)) on the skin,[^cite_note-NIH-What-53][^cite_note-Elkins-54] as well as [compulsive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsive_behavior) scratching due to [pruritis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itch) and/or [formication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formication).[^cite_note-NIH-What-53][^cite_note-Elkins-54] Numerous deaths related to methamphetamine overdoses have been reported.[^cite_note-55] Additionally, "\[p]ostmortem examinations of human tissues have linked use of the drug to diseases associated with aging, such as coronary atherosclerosis and pulmonary fibrosis",[^cite_note-ScienceDaily-56] which may be caused "by a considerable rise in the formation of [ceramides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramide), pro-inflammatory molecules that can foster cell aging and death."[^cite_note-ScienceDaily-56]

#### Dental and oral health ("meth mouth")

Main article: [Meth mouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meth_mouth)



A suspected case of [meth mouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meth_mouth)

Methamphetamine users, particularly heavy users, may lose their teeth abnormally quickly, regardless of the route of administration, from a condition informally known as [meth mouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meth_mouth).[^cite_note-pmid22782046-57] The condition is generally most severe in users who inject the drug, rather than swallow, smoke, or inhale it.[^cite_note-pmid22782046-57] According to the [American Dental Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dental_Association), meth mouth "is probably caused by a combination of drug-induced psychological and physiological changes resulting in [xerostomia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xerostomia) (dry mouth), extended periods of poor [oral hygiene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_hygiene), frequent consumption of high-calorie, carbonated beverages and bruxism (teeth grinding and clenching)".[^cite_note-pmid22782046-57][^cite_note-ADA-58] As dry mouth is also a common side effect of other stimulants, which are not known to contribute severe tooth decay, many researchers suggest that methamphetamine-associated tooth decay is more due to users' other choices. They suggest the side effect has been exaggerated and stylized to create a stereotype of current users as a deterrence for new ones.[^cite_note-pmid22089317-39]

#### Sexually transmitted infection

Methamphetamine use was found to be related to higher frequencies of unprotected sexual intercourse in both [HIV-positive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS) and unknown casual partners, an association more pronounced in HIV-positive participants.[^cite_note-STD-59] These findings suggest that methamphetamine use and engagement in unprotected anal intercourse are co-occurring risk behaviors, behaviors that potentially heighten the risk of HIV transmission among gay and bisexual men.[^cite_note-STD-59] Methamphetamine use allows users of both sexes to engage in prolonged sexual activity, which may cause genital sores and abrasions as well as [priapism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priapism) in men.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29][^cite_note-Patrick_Moore-60] Methamphetamine may also cause sores and abrasions in the mouth via bruxism, increasing the risk of sexually transmitted infection.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29][^cite_note-Patrick_Moore-60]

Besides the sexual transmission of HIV, it may also be transmitted between users who [share a common needle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Needle_sharing).[^cite_note-unsw-61] The level of needle sharing among methamphetamine users is similar to that among other drug injection users.[^cite_note-unsw-61]

### Psychological

The psychological effects of methamphetamine can include euphoria, dysphoria, changes in [libido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libido), [alertness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alertness), apprehension and [concentration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration), decreased sense of fatigue, [insomnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insomnia) or [wakefulness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefulness), [self-confidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-confidence), sociability, irritability, restlessness, [grandiosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grandiosity) and [repetitive and obsessive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fixation_\(psychology\)) behaviors.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29][^cite_note-Westfall-52][^cite_note-Merck_Manual_Amphetamines-62] Peculiar to methamphetamine and related stimulants is "[punding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punding)", persistent non-goal-directed repetitive activity.[^cite_note-NeurClin-63] Methamphetamine use also has a high association with [anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anxiety), [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_depressive_disorder), amphetamine psychosis, [suicide](16831059.md), and violent behaviors.[^cite_note-Darke-2008-64][^cite_note-Sword-65]

Methamphetamine is [neurotoxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurotoxic) to dopaminergic systems in lab animals and is associated with dopaminergic toxicity in humans.[^cite_note-pmid25861156-37][^cite_note-pmid19328213-38] [Excitotoxicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excitotoxicity), [oxidative stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidative_stress), metabolic compromise, UPS dysfunction, protein nitration, [endoplasmic reticulum stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoplasmic_reticulum_stress_in_beta_cells), [p53 expression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P53_expression) and other processes contribute to this neurotoxicity.[^cite_note-pmid25861156-37][^cite_note-pmid22392347-69][^cite_note-pmid19426289-4] In line with its dopaminergic neurotoxicity, methamphetamine use is associated with a higher risk of [Parkinson's disease](22228064.md).[^cite_note-Cisneros_2014_and_review-70] In addition to its dopaminergic neurotoxicity, a review of human studies indicated that chronic methamphetamine use is associated with [serotonergic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin) neurotoxicity.[^cite_note-pmid19328213-38] It has been demonstrated that a high core temperature is correlated with an increase in the neurotoxic effects of methamphetamine.[^cite_note-71] Withdrawal of methamphetamine in dependent persons may lead to [post-acute withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-acute-withdrawal_syndrome) which persists months beyond the typical withdrawal period.[^cite_note-pmid19426289-4]

[Magnetic resonance imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging) studies on human methamphetamine users have also found evidence of neurodegeneration, or adverse [neuroplastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroplastic) changes in brain structure and function.[^cite_note-pmid19328213-38] In particular, methamphetamine appears to cause [hyperintensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperintensity) and [hypertrophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertrophy) of [white matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_matter), marked shrinkage of [hippocampi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocampus), and reduced [gray matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_matter) in the [cingulate cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cingulate_cortex), [limbic cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limbic_cortex), and [paralimbic cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralimbic_cortex) in recreational methamphetamine users.[^cite_note-pmid19328213-38] Moreover, evidence suggests that adverse changes in the level of [biomarkers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomarker) of metabolic integrity and synthesis occur in recreational users, such as a reduction in [*N*-acetylaspartate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-acetylaspartate) and [creatine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creatine) levels and elevated levels of [choline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choline) and [myoinositol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myoinositol).[^cite_note-pmid19328213-38]

Methamphetamine has been shown to activate [TAAR1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAAR1) in human [astrocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrocyte) and generate [cAMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_AMP) as a result.[^cite_note-Cisneros_2014_and_review-70] Activation of astrocyte-localized TAAR1 appears to function as a mechanism by which methamphetamine attenuates membrane-bound [EAAT2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EAAT2) (SLC1A2) levels and function in these cells.[^cite_note-Cisneros_2014_and_review-70]

Methamphetamine binds to and activates both [sigma receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_receptor) subtypes, [σ<sub>1</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-1_receptor) and [σ<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-2_receptor), with micromolar affinity.[^cite_note-Sigma-68][^cite_note-SigmaB-72] Sigma receptor activation may promote methamphetamine-induced neurotoxicity by facilitating [hyperthermia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperthermia), increasing dopamine synthesis and release, influencing microglial activation, and modulating [apoptotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoptotic) signaling cascades and the formation of reactive oxygen species.[^cite_note-Sigma-68][^cite_note-SigmaB-72]

[alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholism), [cannabinoids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabinoid), [cocaine](7701.md), [methylphenidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methylphenidate), addictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_addiction), which are compulsive sexual behaviors that result from excessive sexual activity and amphetamine use.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-Natural_and_drug_addictions-90][^cite_note-Amph_and_sex_addiction-96] These sex addictions (i.e., drug-induced compulsive sexual behaviors) are associated with a methamphetamine.[^cite_note-Natural_and_drug_addictions-90][^cite_note-ΔFosB_reward-94][^cite_note-Amph_and_sex_addiction-96]

#### Epigenetic factors

Methamphetamine addiction is persistent for many individuals, with 61% of individuals treated for addiction relapsing within one year.[^cite_note-pmid24685563-97] About half of those with methamphetamine addiction continue with use over a ten-year period, while the other half reduce use starting at about one to four years after initial use.[^cite_note-pmid23313146-98]

The frequent persistence of addiction suggests that long-lasting changes in [gene expression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulation_of_gene_expression#Regulation_of_transcription_in_addiction) may occur in particular regions of the brain, and may contribute importantly to the addiction phenotype. In 2014, a crucial role was found for [epigenetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics) mechanisms in driving lasting changes in gene expression in the brain.[^cite_note-pmid23643695-93]

A review in 2015[^cite_note-Godino-99] summarized a number of studies involving chronic methamphetamine use in rodents. Epigenetic alterations were observed in the brain [reward pathways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolimbic_pathway), including areas like [ventral tegmental area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventral_tegmental_area), [nucleus accumbens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleus_accumbens), and dorsal [striatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striatum), the [hippocampus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocampus), and the [prefrontal cortex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefrontal_cortex). Chronic methamphetamine use caused gene-specific [histone acetylations, deacetylations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histone_acetylation_and_deacetylation) and [methylations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histone_methylation). Gene-specific DNA methylations in particular regions of the brain were also observed. The various epigenetic alterations caused [downregulations or upregulations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downregulation_and_upregulation) of specific genes important in addiction. For instance, chronic methamphetamine use caused [methylation of the lysine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histone_methylation#Function) in position 4 of histone 3 located at the [promoters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promoter_\(genetics\)) of the *[c-fos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-fos)* and the *[C-C chemokine receptor 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CCR2) (ccr2)* genes, activating those genes in the nucleus accumbens (NAc).[^cite_note-Godino-99] c-fos is well known to be important in [addiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addiction).[^cite_note-pmid25446457-100] The *ccr2* gene is also important in addiction, since mutational inactivation of this gene impairs addiction.[^cite_note-Godino-99]

In methamphetamine addicted rats, epigenetic regulation through reduced [acetylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetylation) of histones, in brain striatal neurons, caused reduced transcription of [glutamate receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutamate_receptor#Conditions_with_demonstrated_associations_to_glutamate_receptors).[^cite_note-pmid24239129-101] Glutamate receptors play an important role in regulating the reinforcing effects of addictive drugs.[^cite_note-pmid15120493-102]

Administration of methamphetamine to rodents causes [DNA damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_damage_\(naturally_occurring\)) in their brain, particularly in the [nucleus accumbens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleus_accumbens) region.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] During repair of such DNA damages, persistent chromatin alterations may occur such as in the [methylation of DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_methylation) or the acetylation or [methylation of histones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histone_methylation) at the sites of repair.[^cite_note-105] These alterations can be [epigenetic scars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics) in the [chromatin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromatin) that contribute to the persistent epigenetic changes found in methamphetamine addiction.

#### Treatment and management

Further information: [Addiction § Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Addiction#Research)

A 2018 systematic review and [network meta-analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_meta-analysis) of 50 trials involving 12 different psychosocial interventions for amphetamine, methamphetamine, or cocaine addiction found that [combination therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combination_therapy) with both [contingency management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contingency_management) and [community reinforcement approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_reinforcement_approach) had the highest efficacy (i.e., abstinence rate) and acceptability (i.e., lowest dropout rate).[^cite_note-Psychosocial_interventions_network_meta-analysis-106] Other treatment modalities examined in the analysis included [monotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monotherapy) with contingency management or community reinforcement approach, [cognitive behavioral therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognitive_behavioral_therapy), [12-step programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12-step_program), non-contingent reward-based therapies, [psychodynamic therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychodynamic_therapy), and other combination therapies involving these.[^cite_note-Psychosocial_interventions_network_meta-analysis-106]

As of December 2019, there is no effective [pharmacotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharmacotherapy) for methamphetamine addiction.[^cite_note-pmid24716825-107][^cite_note-SystRev-Meta_analysis_amphetamine_addiction_pharmacotherapy-108][^cite_note-pmid23039267-109] A systematic review and meta-analysis from 2019 assessed the efficacy of 17 different pharmacotherapies used in [randomized controlled trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Randomized_controlled_trial) (RCTs) for amphetamine and methamphetamine addiction;[^cite_note-SystRev-Meta_analysis_amphetamine_addiction_pharmacotherapy-108] it found only low-strength evidence that methylphenidate might reduce amphetamine or methamphetamine self-administration.[^cite_note-SystRev-Meta_analysis_amphetamine_addiction_pharmacotherapy-108] There was low-to moderate-strength evidence of no benefit for most of the other medications used in RCTs, which included antidepressants (bupropion, [mirtazapine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirtazapine), [sertraline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sertraline)), antipsychotics ([aripiprazole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aripiprazole)), anticonvulsants ([topiramate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topiramate), [baclofen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baclofen), [gabapentin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabapentin)), [naltrexone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naltrexone), [varenicline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varenicline), [citicoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citicoline), [ondansetron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ondansetron), [prometa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prometa), [riluzole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riluzole), [atomoxetine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomoxetine), dextroamphetamine, and [modafinil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modafinil).[^cite_note-SystRev-Meta_analysis_amphetamine_addiction_pharmacotherapy-108][^cite_note-110]

[Medication-Assisted Treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opioid_agonist_therapy) (MAT) combines FDA-approved medications with behavioral therapies to address substance use disorders. This approach aims to reduce cravings and withdrawal symptoms, supporting individuals in their recovery process.[^cite_note-111]

#### Dependence and withdrawal

[Tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_tolerance) is expected to develop with regular methamphetamine use and, when used recreationally, this tolerance develops rapidly.[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-Cochrane_Abuse-113] In dependent users, withdrawal symptoms are positively correlated with the level of drug tolerance.[^cite_note-Cochrane_Withdrawal-114] [Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depression_\(mood\)) from methamphetamine withdrawal lasts longer and is more severe than that of [cocaine](7701.md) withdrawal.[^cite_note-pmid17990840-115]

According to the current Cochrane review on [drug dependence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_dependence) and [withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_withdrawal) in recreational users of methamphetamine, "when chronic heavy users abruptly discontinue \[methamphetamine] use, many report a time-limited withdrawal syndrome that occurs within 24 hours of their last dose".[^cite_note-Cochrane_Withdrawal-114] Withdrawal symptoms in chronic, high-dose users are frequent, occurring in up to 87.6% of cases, and persist for three to four weeks with a marked "crash" phase occurring during the first week.[^cite_note-Cochrane_Withdrawal-114] Methamphetamine withdrawal symptoms can include anxiety, [drug craving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craving_\(withdrawal\)), dysphoric mood, [fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatigue_\(medical\)), [increased appetite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperphagia), [increased movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychomotor_agitation) or [decreased movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychomotor_retardation), [lack of motivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhedonia), [sleeplessness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insomnia) or [sleepiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersomnia), and [vivid or lucid dreams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucid_dream).[^cite_note-Cochrane_Withdrawal-114]

Methamphetamine that is present in a mother's [bloodstream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloodstream) can pass through the [placenta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Placenta) to a [fetus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetus) and be secreted into [breast milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_milk).[^cite_note-pmid17990840-115] Infants born to methamphetamine-abusing mothers may experience a [neonatal withdrawal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neonatal_withdrawal) syndrome, with symptoms involving of abnormal sleep patterns, poor feeding, tremors, and [hypertonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypertonia).[^cite_note-pmid17990840-115] This withdrawal syndrome is relatively mild and only requires medical intervention in approximately 4% of cases.[^cite_note-pmid17990840-115]

### Neonatal

Unlike other drugs, babies with [prenatal exposure to methamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drugs_in_pregnancy#Methamphetamine) do not show immediate signs of withdrawal. Instead, cognitive and behavioral problems start emerging when the children reach school age.[^cite_note-2020-01-03_ABC-116]

A [prospective cohort study](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prospective_cohort_study) of 330 children showed that at the age of 3, children with methamphetamine exposure showed increased emotional reactivity, as well as more signs of anxiety and depression; and at the age of 5, children showed higher rates of [externalizing disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Externalizing_disorder) and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD).[^cite_note-LaGasse_2012-117]

## Overdose

Methamphetamine overdose is a diverse term. It frequently refers to the exaggeration of the unusual effects with features such as irritability, agitation, hallucinations and paranoia.[^cite_note-Schep-5][^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] The cardiovascular effects are typically not noticed in young healthy people. Hypertension and tachycardia are not apparent unless measured. A moderate overdose of methamphetamine may induce symptoms such as: [abnormal heart rhythm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiac_dysrhythmia), confusion, [difficult or painful urination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysuria), high or low blood pressure, [high body temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperthermia), [over-active or over-responsive reflexes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperreflexia), [muscle aches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myalgia), severe [agitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychomotor_agitation), [rapid breathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tachypnea), [tremor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremor), [urinary hesitancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urinary_hesitancy), and [an inability to pass urine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urinary_retention).[^cite_note-Schep-5][^cite_note-Westfall-52] An extremely large overdose may produce symptoms such as [adrenergic storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrenergic_storm), [methamphetamine psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methamphetamine_psychosis), [substantially reduced or no urine output](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anuria), [cardiogenic shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardiogenic_shock), [bleeding in the brain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerebral_hemorrhage), [circulatory collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circulatory_collapse), [hyperpyrexia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperpyrexia) (i.e., dangerously high body temperature), [pulmonary hypertension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulmonary_hypertension), [kidney failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_failure), [rapid muscle breakdown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhabdomyolysis), [serotonin syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin_syndrome), and a form of [stereotypy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotypy#Associated_terms) ("tweaking").[^cite_note-121] A methamphetamine overdose will likely also result in mild [brain damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_damage) owing to [dopaminergic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopaminergic) and [serotonergic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin) neurotoxicity.[^cite_note-Malenka-122][^cite_note-pmid19328213-38] Death from methamphetamine poisoning is typically preceded by convulsions and [coma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coma).[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29]

### Psychosis

Main section: [Stimulant psychosis § Substituted amphetamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stimulant_psychosis#Substituted_amphetamines)

Use of methamphetamine can result in a stimulant psychosis which may present with a variety of symptoms (e.g., [paranoia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranoia), [hallucinations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallucination), [delirium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delirium), and [delusions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delusion)).[^cite_note-Schep-5][^cite_note-Cochrane-123] A [Cochrane Collaboration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cochrane_Collaboration) review on treatment for amphetamine, dextroamphetamine, and methamphetamine use-induced psychosis states that about 5–15% of users fail to recover completely.[^cite_note-Cochrane-123][^cite_note-Hofmann-124] The same review asserts that, based upon at least one trial, [antipsychotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipsychotic) medications effectively resolve the symptoms of acute amphetamine psychosis.[^cite_note-Cochrane-123] Amphetamine psychosis may also develop occasionally as a treatment-emergent side effect.[^cite_note-Berman-2009-125]

### Death from overdose

The CDC reported that the number of deaths in the United States involving psychostimulants with abuse potential to be 23,837 in 2020 and 32,537 in 2021.[^cite_note-126] This category code (ICD–10 of T43.6) includes primarily methamphetamine but also other stimulants such as amphetamine, and methylphenidate. The mechanism of death in these cases is not reported in these statistics and is difficult to know.[^cite_note-127] Unlike fentanyl which causes respiratory depression, methamphetamine is not a respiratory depressant. Some deaths are as a result of intracranial hemorrhage[^cite_note-128] and some deaths are cardiovascular in nature including flash pulmonary edema[^cite_note-129] and ventricular fibrillation.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

### Emergency treatment

Acute methamphetamine intoxication is largely managed by treating the symptoms and treatments may initially include administration of [activated charcoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Activated_charcoal) and [sedation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedation).[^cite_note-Schep-5] There is not enough evidence on [hemodialysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemodialysis) or [peritoneal dialysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peritoneal_dialysis) in cases of methamphetamine intoxication to determine their usefulness.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] [Forced acid diuresis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_acid_diuresis) (e.g., with [vitamin C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_C)) will increase methamphetamine excretion but is not recommended as it may increase the risk of aggravating acidosis, or cause seizures or rhabdomyolysis.[^cite_note-Schep-5] Hypertension presents a risk for [intracranial hemorrhage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intracranial_hemorrhage) (i.e., bleeding in the brain) and, if severe, is typically treated with intravenous [phentolamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phentolamine) or [nitroprusside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitroprusside).[^cite_note-Schep-5] Blood pressure often drops gradually following sufficient sedation with a [benzodiazepine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzodiazepine) and providing a calming environment.[^cite_note-Schep-5]

Antipsychotics such as [haloperidol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haloperidol) are useful in treating agitation and psychosis from methamphetamine overdose.[^cite_note-Richards_2015-132][^cite_note-133] [Beta blockers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta_blocker) with lipophilic properties and CNS penetration such as [metoprolol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metoprolol) and [labetalol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labetalol) may be useful for treating CNS and cardiovascular toxicity.[^cite_note-Medscape_meth_toxicity-134][^cite_note-135] The mixed [alpha-](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_blocker) and [beta-blocker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta-blocker) labetalol is especially useful for treatment of concomitant tachycardia and hypertension induced by methamphetamine.[^cite_note-Richards_2015-132] The phenomenon of "unopposed alpha stimulation" has not been reported with the use of beta-blockers for treatment of methamphetamine toxicity.[^cite_note-Richards_2015-132]

## Interactions

Methamphetamine is metabolized by the liver enzyme CYP2D6, so [CYP2D6 inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CYP2D6#Ligands) will prolong the [elimination half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_half-life) of methamphetamine.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29][^cite_note-DrugBank_Enzymes-136] Methamphetamine also interacts with [monoamine oxidase inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_oxidase_inhibitor) (MAOIs), since both MAOIs and methamphetamine increase plasma catecholamines; therefore, concurrent use of both is dangerous.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] Methamphetamine may decrease the effects of [sedatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedative) and [depressants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depressant) and increase the effects of [antidepressants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antidepressant) and other stimulants as well.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] Methamphetamine may counteract the effects of [antihypertensives](633467.md) and [antipsychotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antipsychotic) owing to its effects on the cardiovascular system and cognition respectively.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] The [pH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PH) of gastrointestinal content and urine affects the absorption and excretion of methamphetamine.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] Specifically, acidic substances will reduce the absorption of methamphetamine and increase urinary excretion, while alkaline substances do the opposite.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] Owing to the effect pH has on absorption, [proton pump inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proton_pump_inhibitor), which reduce [gastric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastric_acid), are known to interact with methamphetamine.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29] [Norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norepinephrine_reuptake_inhibitor) (NRIs) like [atomoxetine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomoxetine) prevent [norepinephrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norepinephrine) release induced by amphetamines and have been found to reduce the stimulant, euphoriant, and [sympathomimetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympathomimetic) effects of [dextroamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dextroamphetamine) in humans.[^cite_note-TreuerGauMéndez2013-137][^cite_note-HealSmithFindling2012-138][^cite_note-SofuogluPolingHill2009-139] Similarly, [norepinephrine–dopamine reuptake inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norepinephrine%E2%80%93dopamine_reuptake_inhibitor) (NRIs) like [methylphenidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methylphenidate) and [bupropion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bupropion) prevent norepinephrine and dopamine release induced by amphetamines and bupropion has been found to reduce the subjective and sympathomimetic effects of methamphetamine in humans.[^cite_note-ElkashefVocciHanson2008-140][^cite_note-HealSmithFindling2012-138][^cite_note-SimmlerWandelerLiechti2013-141][^cite_note-NewtonRoacheDeLaGarza2006-142]

An image of methamphetamine pharmacodynamics

This illustration depicts the normal operation of the [dopaminergic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopaminergic) terminal to the left, and the dopaminergic terminal in the presence of methamphetamine to the right. Methamphetamine reverses the action of the dopamine transporter (DAT) by activating [TAAR1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAAR1) (not shown). TAAR1 activation also causes some of the dopamine transporters to move into the presynaptic neuron and cease transport (not shown). At VMAT2 (labeled VMAT), methamphetamine causes dopamine efflux (release).

Methamphetamine has been identified as a potent [full agonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_agonist) of [trace amine-associated receptor 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAAR1) (TAAR1), a [G protein-coupled receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_protein-coupled_receptor) (GPCR) that regulates brain [catecholamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catecholamine) systems.[^cite_note-Miller-153][^cite_note-Meth_Targets-154] Activation of TAAR1 increases [cyclic adenosine monophosphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclic_adenosine_monophosphate) (cAMP) production and either completely inhibits or reverses the transport direction of the [dopamine transporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine_transporter) (DAT), [norepinephrine transporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norepinephrine_transporter) (NET), and [serotonin transporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serotonin_transporter) (SERT).[^cite_note-Miller-153][^cite_note-pmid11459929-155] When methamphetamine binds to TAAR1, it triggers transporter [phosphorylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorylation) via [protein kinase A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_kinase_A) (PKA) and [protein kinase C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein_kinase_C) (PKC) signaling, ultimately resulting in the [internalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocytosis) or reverse function of [monoamine transporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_transporter).[^cite_note-Miller-153][^cite_note-Xie_and_Miller_2009-156] Methamphetamine is also known to increase intracellular calcium, an effect which is associated with DAT phosphorylation through a [Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent protein kinase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca2+/calmodulin-dependent_protein_kinase) (CAMK)-dependent signaling pathway, in turn producing dopamine efflux.[^cite_note-TAAR1_IUPHAR-157][^cite_note-EAAT3-158][^cite_note-DAT_regulation_review-159] TAAR1 has been shown to reduce the [firing rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_potential) of neurons through direct activation of [G protein-coupled inwardly-rectifying potassium channels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_protein-coupled_inwardly-rectifying_potassium_channel).[^cite_note-GIRK-160][^cite_note-Genatlas_TAAR1-161][^cite_note-TAAR1-Paradoxical-162] TAAR1 activation by methamphetamine in [astrocytes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrocyte) appears to negatively modulate the membrane expression and function of [EAAT2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EAAT2), a type of [glutamate transporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutamate_transporter).[^cite_note-Cisneros_2014_and_review-70]

In addition to its effect on the plasma membrane monoamine transporters, methamphetamine inhibits synaptic vesicle function by inhibiting [VMAT2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VMAT2), which prevents monoamine uptake into the vesicles and promotes their release.[^cite_note-Meth_Transporters-163] Methamphetamine binds to VMAT2 via the [resperine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserpine) site, in contrast to amphetamine, which appears to bind at the [tetrabenazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetrabenazine) site.[^cite_note-164] This results in the outflow of monoamines from [synaptic vesicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synaptic_vesicle) into the [cytosol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytosol) (intracellular fluid) of the [presynaptic neuron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presynaptic_neuron), and their subsequent release into the synaptic cleft by the phosphorylated transporters.[^cite_note-E_Weihe-165] Other [transporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Membrane_transport_protein) that methamphetamine is known to inhibit are [SLC22A3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLC22A3) and [SLC22A5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLC22A5).[^cite_note-Meth_Transporters-163] SLC22A3 is an extraneuronal monoamine transporter that is present in astrocytes, and SLC22A5 is a high-affinity [carnitine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnitine) transporter.[^cite_note-Meth_Targets-154][^cite_note-pmid13677912-166]

Methamphetamine is also an [agonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agonist) of the [alpha-2 adrenergic receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha-2_adrenergic_receptor) and [sigma receptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma_receptor) with a greater [affinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Binding_affinity) for [σ<sub>1</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-1_receptor) than [σ<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigma-2_receptor), and inhibits [monoamine oxidase A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_oxidase_A) (MAO-A) and [monoamine oxidase B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_oxidase_B) (MAO-B).[^cite_note-Sigma-68][^cite_note-Meth_Targets-154][^cite_note-SigmaB-72] Sigma receptor activation by methamphetamine may facilitate its central nervous system stimulant effects and promote neurotoxicity within the brain.[^cite_note-Sigma-68][^cite_note-SigmaB-72] [Dextromethamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dextromethamphetamine) is a stronger [psychostimulant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychostimulant), but [levomethamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levomethamphetamine) has stronger [peripheral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peripheral_nervous_system) effects, a longer half-life, and longer perceived effects among heavy substance users.[^cite_note-Melega-167][^cite_note-Kuczenski-168][^cite_note-Mendelson-169] At high doses, both enantiomers of methamphetamine can induce similar [stereotypy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereotypy) and [methamphetamine psychosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methamphetamine_psychosis),[^cite_note-Kuczenski-168] but levomethamphetamine has shorter psychodynamic effects.[^cite_note-Mendelson-169]

### Pharmacokinetics

The [bioavailability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioavailability) of methamphetamine is 67% [orally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_administration), 79% intranasally, 67 to 90% via [inhalation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inhalational_administration) ([smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking)), and 100% intravenously.[^cite_note-pmid19426289-4][^cite_note-Schep-5][^cite_note-pmid25176528-6] Following oral administration, methamphetamine is well-absorbed into the bloodstream, with peak plasma methamphetamine concentrations achieved in approximately 3.13–6.3 hours post ingestion.[^cite_note-DrugBank_methamphetamine_metabolism-170] Methamphetamine is also well absorbed following inhalation and following intranasal administration.[^cite_note-Schep-5] Because of the high [lipophilicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipophilicity) of methamphetamine due to its methyl group, it can readily move through the [blood–brain barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood%E2%80%93brain_barrier) faster than other stimulants, where it is more resistant to degradation by [monoamine oxidase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoamine_oxidase).[^cite_note-Schep-5][^cite_note-DrugBank_methamphetamine_metabolism-170][^cite_note-pmid30731099-171] The amphetamine metabolite peaks at 10–24 hours.[^cite_note-Schep-5] Methamphetamine is excreted by the kidneys, with the rate of excretion into the urine heavily influenced by urinary pH.[^cite_note-Desoxyn_FDA_label-29][^cite_note-DrugBank_methamphetamine_metabolism-170] When taken orally, 30–54% of the dose is excreted in urine as methamphetamine and 10–23% as amphetamine.[^cite_note-DrugBank_methamphetamine_metabolism-170] Following IV doses, about 45% is excreted as methamphetamine and 7% as amphetamine.[^cite_note-DrugBank_methamphetamine_metabolism-170] The [elimination half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elimination_half-life) of methamphetamine varies with a range of 5–30 hours, but it is on average 9 to 12 hours in most studies.[^cite_note-Schep-5][^cite_note-pmid19426289-4] The elimination half-life of methamphetamine does not vary by [route of administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Route_of_administration), but is subject to substantial [interindividual variability](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interindividual_variability).[^cite_note-pmid19426289-4]

CYP2D6, [dopamine β-hydroxylase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dopamine_%CE%B2-hydroxylase), [flavin-containing monooxygenase 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavin-containing_monooxygenase_3), [butyrate-CoA ligase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butyrate-CoA_ligase), and [glycine N-acyltransferase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycine_N-acyltransferase) are the enzymes known to metabolize methamphetamine or its metabolites in humans.[^cite_note-methamphetamine_metabolism-178] The primary metabolites are amphetamine and [4-hydroxymethamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pholedrine);[^cite_note-DrugBank_methamphetamine_metabolism-170] other minor metabolites include: [4-hydroxyamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-hydroxyamphetamine), [4-hydroxynorephedrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-hydroxynorephedrine), [4-hydroxyphenylacetone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4-hydroxyphenylacetone), [benzoic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzoic_acid), [hippuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippuric_acid), [norephedrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norephedrine), and [phenylacetone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenylacetone), the metabolites of amphetamine.[^cite_note-FDA_Pharmacokinetics-10][^cite_note-DrugBank_methamphetamine_metabolism-170][^cite_note-Metabolites-172] Among these metabolites, the active [sympathomimetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sympathomimetics) are amphetamine, 4‑hydroxyamphetamine,[^cite_note-179] 4‑hydroxynorephedrine,[^cite_note-180] 4-hydroxymethamphetamine,[^cite_note-DrugBank_methamphetamine_metabolism-170] and norephedrine.[^cite_note-181] Methamphetamine is a CYP2D6 inhibitor.[^cite_note-DrugBank_Enzymes-136]

The main metabolic pathways involve aromatic para-hydroxylation, aliphatic alpha- and beta-hydroxylation, N-oxidation, N-dealkylation, and deamination.[^cite_note-FDA_Pharmacokinetics-10][^cite_note-DrugBank_methamphetamine_metabolism-170][^cite_note-Pubchem_Kinetics-182] The known metabolic pathways include:

#### Detection in biological fluids

Methamphetamine and amphetamine are often measured in urine or blood as part of a [drug test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_test) for sports, employment, poisoning diagnostics, and forensics.[^cite_note-Ergogenics-185][^cite_note-pmid9700558-186][^cite_note-pmid17468860-187][^cite_note-pmid8075776-188] Chiral techniques may be employed to help distinguish the source of the drug to determine whether it was obtained illicitly or legally via prescription or prodrug.[^cite_note-pmid15516295-189] Chiral separation is needed to assess the possible contribution of [levomethamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levomethamphetamine), which is an active ingredient in some OTC nasal decongestants,[^cite_note-OTC_levmetamfetamine-34] toward a positive test result.[^cite_note-pmid15516295-189][^cite_note-pmid14871155-190][^cite_note-191] Dietary zinc supplements can mask the presence of methamphetamine and other drugs in urine.[^cite_note-pmid21740689-192]

## Chemistry





Shards of pure methamphetamine hydrochloride, also known as crystal meth

Methamphetamine is a [chiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chirality_\(chemistry\)) compound with two enantiomers, [dextromethamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dextromethamphetamine) and [levomethamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levomethamphetamine). At room temperature, the [free base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_base) of methamphetamine is a clear and colorless liquid with an odor characteristic of [geranium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geranium) leaves.[^cite_note-Pubchem2-14] It is [soluble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soluble) in [diethyl ether](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diethyl_ether) and [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol) as well as [miscible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miscible) with [chloroform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chloroform).[^cite_note-Pubchem2-14]

In contrast, the methamphetamine hydrochloride salt is odorless with a bitter taste.[^cite_note-Pubchem2-14] It has a melting point between 170 and 175 °C (338 and 347 °F) and, at room temperature, occurs as white crystals or a white [crystalline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystallinity) powder.[^cite_note-Pubchem2-14] The hydrochloride salt is also freely soluble in ethanol and water.[^cite_note-Pubchem2-14] The crystal structure of either enantiomer is [monoclinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoclinic) with P2<sub>1</sub> [space group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_group); at 90 K (−183.2 °C; −297.7 °F), it has [lattice parameters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lattice_parameter) *a* = 7.10 [Å](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angstrom), *b* = 7.29 Å, *c* = 10.81 Å, and *β* = 97.29°.[^cite_note-193]

### Degradation

A 2011 study into the destruction of methamphetamine using bleach showed that effectiveness is correlated with exposure time and concentration.[^cite_note-194] A year-long study (also from 2011) showed that methamphetamine in soils is a persistent pollutant.[^cite_note-pmid21777940-195] In a 2013 study of bioreactors in [wastewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wastewater), methamphetamine was found to be largely degraded within 30 days under exposure to light.[^cite_note-pmid23886544-196]

### Synthesis

Further information on illicit amphetamine synthesis: [History and culture of substituted amphetamines § Illegal synthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_and_culture_of_substituted_amphetamines#Illegal_synthesis)

[Racemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racemic) methamphetamine may be prepared starting from [phenylacetone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenylacetone) by either the [Leuckart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leuckart_reaction)[^cite_note-Crossley_1944-197] or [reductive amination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reductive_amination) methods.[^cite_note-pmid19637924-198] In the Leuckart reaction, one equivalent of phenylacetone is reacted with two equivalents of [*N*-methylformamide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-methylformamide) to produce the formyl [amide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amide) of methamphetamine plus carbon dioxide and [methylamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methylamine) as side products.[^cite_note-pmid19637924-198] In this reaction, an [iminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iminium) cation is formed as an intermediate which is [reduced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redox) by the second equivalent of *N*-methylformamide.[^cite_note-pmid19637924-198] The intermediate formyl amide is then [hydrolyzed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrolyzed) under acidic aqueous conditions to yield methamphetamine as the final product.[^cite_note-pmid19637924-198] Alternatively, phenylacetone can be reacted with methylamine under reducing conditions to yield methamphetamine.[^cite_note-pmid19637924-198]

## History, society, and culture

Main article: [History and culture of substituted amphetamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_and_culture_of_substituted_amphetamines)

Several different methamphetamine tablet containers photographed on a table

Pervitin, a methamphetamine brand used by German soldiers during [World War II](32927.md), was dispensed in various forms, including tablet containers.



U.S. [drug overdose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_overdose) related fatalities in 2017 were 70,200, including 10,333 of those related to psychostimulants (including methamphetamine).

[^cite_note-NIDA-deaths-199]

[^cite_note-200]

Amphetamine, discovered before methamphetamine, was first synthesized in 1887 in Germany by Romanian chemist [Lazăr Edeleanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laz%C4%83r_Edeleanu) who named it *phenylisopropylamine*.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-Vermont-202] Shortly after, methamphetamine was synthesized from [ephedrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ephedrine) in 1893 by Japanese [chemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist) [Nagai Nagayoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagai_Nagayoshi).[^cite_note-Grobler_et_al_2011-203] Three decades later, in 1919, methamphetamine hydrochloride was synthesized by pharmacologist [Akira Ogata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Ogata) via [reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redox) of ephedrine using red [phosphorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phosphorus) and [iodine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iodine).[^cite_note-history-204]

From 1938, methamphetamine was marketed on a large scale in Germany as a nonprescription drug under the brand name *Pervitin*, produced by the Berlin-based [Temmler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temmler) pharmaceutical company.[^cite_note-CISP-205][^cite_note-206] It was used by all branches of the combined [armed forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht) of the [Third Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Reich), for its stimulant effects and to induce extended [wakefulness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakefulness).[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-pmid22849208-208] Pervitin became colloquially known among the German troops as "[Stuka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuka)-Tablets" (*Stuka-Tabletten*) and "[Herman-Göring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_G%C3%B6ring)-Pills" (*Hermann-Göring-Pillen*), as a snide allusion to Göring's widely-known addiction to drugs. However, the side effects, particularly the withdrawal symptoms, were so serious that the army sharply cut back its usage in 1940.[^cite_note-shooting_up-209] By 1941, usage was restricted to a doctor's prescription, and the military tightly controlled its distribution. Soldiers would only receive a couple of tablets at a time, and were discouraged from using them in combat. Historian Łukasz Kamieński says,

> A soldier going to battle on Pervitin usually found himself unable to perform effectively for the next day or two. Suffering from a drug hangover and looking more like a zombie than a great warrior, he had to recover from the side effects.

Some soldiers turned violent, committing war crimes against civilians; others attacked their own officers.[^cite_note-shooting_up-209] At the end of the war, it was used as part of a new drug: [D-IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D-IX).

[Obetrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obetrol), patented by Obetrol Pharmaceuticals in the 1950s and indicated for treatment of [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity), was one of the first brands of pharmaceutical methamphetamine products.[^cite_note-Real_Obetrol_Ad-210] Because of the psychological and stimulant effects of methamphetamine, Obetrol became a popular diet pill in the United States in the 1950s and 1960s.[^cite_note-Real_Obetrol_Ad-210] Eventually, as the addictive properties of the drug became known, governments began to strictly regulate the production and distribution of methamphetamine.[^cite_note-Vermont-202] For example, during the early 1970s in the United States, methamphetamine became a [schedule II controlled substance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_Substances_Act#Schedule_II_controlled_substances) under the [Controlled Substances Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_Substances_Act).[^cite_note-\:USAS2-3] As of January 2013, the Desoxyn trademark had been sold to Italian pharmaceutical company Recordati.[^cite_note-211]

## Trafficking

The [Golden Triangle (Southeast Asia)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Triangle_\(Southeast_Asia\)), specifically [Shan State](493376.md), Myanmar, is the world's leading producer of methamphetamine as production has shifted to [*ya ba*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaba_\(drug\)) and crystalline methamphetamine, including for export to the United States and across East and Southeast Asia and the Pacific.[^cite_note-212]

Concerning the accelerating synthetic drug production in the region, the Cantonese Chinese syndicate [Sam Gor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Gor), also known as The Company, is understood to be the main international crime syndicate responsible for this shift.[^cite_note-213] It is made up of members of five different triads. Sam Gor is primarily involved in drug trafficking, earning at least \$8 billion per year.[^cite_note-214] Sam Gor is alleged to control 40% of the Asia-Pacific methamphetamine market, while also trafficking [heroin](14034.md) and [ketamine](16948.md). The organization is active in a variety of countries, including Myanmar, Thailand, New Zealand, Australia, Japan, China, and Taiwan. Sam Gor previously produced meth in Southern China and is now believed to manufacture mainly in the Golden Triangle, specifically Shan State, Myanmar, responsible for much of the massive surge of crystal meth in circa 2019.[^cite_note-215] The group is understood to be headed by [Tse Chi Lop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tse_Chi_Lop), a gangster born in [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou), [China](5405.md) who also holds a Canadian passport.

[Liu Zhaohua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Zhaohua) was another individual involved in the production and trafficking of methamphetamine until his arrest in 2005.[^cite_note-\:0-216] It was estimated over 18 tonnes of methamphetamine were produced under his watch.[^cite_note-\:0-216]

## Legal status

Main article: [Legal status of methamphetamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_status_of_methamphetamine)

The production, distribution, sale, and possession of methamphetamine is restricted or illegal in many [jurisdictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction).[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-incb-218] In some jurisdictions, it is legally available as a prescription medication. Methamphetamine has been placed in schedule II of the [United Nations](31769.md) [Convention on Psychotropic Substances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convention_on_Psychotropic_Substances) treaty, indicating that it has limited medical use.[^cite_note-incb-218]

## Research

Animal models have shown that low-dose methamphetamine improves cognitive and behavioural functioning following TBI (traumatic brain injury).[^cite_note-pmid25724762-7] This is in contrast to high, repeated doses which cause neurotoxicity. These models demonstrate that low-dose methamphetamine increases neurogenesis and reduces apoptosis in the dentate gyrus of the hippocampus following TBI.[^cite_note-219] It has also been found that TBI patients testing positive for methamphetamine at the time of emergency department admission have lower rates of mortality.[^cite_note-220]

It has been suggested, based on animal research, that calcitriol, the active metabolite of [vitamin D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_D), can provide significant protection against the DA- and 5-HT-depleting effects of neurotoxic doses of methamphetamine.[^cite_note-221] Protection against methamphetamine-induced neurotoxicity has also been observed following administration of ascorbic acid (vitamin C),[^cite_note-222] cobalamin (vitamin B<sub>12</sub>),[^cite_note-223] and vitamin E.[^cite_note-224]
