The [Czech Republic](5321.md) is a [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic), in which the [president](410288.md) is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and the [prime minister](410283.md) is the [head of government](37570.md).[^cite_note-1] Executive power is exercised by the [Government of the Czech Republic](14380629.md), which reports to the [Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamber_of_Deputies). The legislature is exercised by the [Parliament](3991200.md). The Czech Parliament is [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameral): the upper house of the Parliament is the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Czech_Republic), and the lower house is the [Chamber of Deputies](3991120.md). The Senate consists of 81 members who are elected for six years. The Chamber of Deputies consists of 200 members who are elected for four years. The judiciary system is topped by the trio of the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_the_Czech_Republic), [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Czech_Republic) and [Supreme Administrative Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Administrative_Court_of_the_Czech_Republic).

The highest legal document is the [Constitution of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Czech_Republic), complemented by constitutional laws and the [Charter of Fundamental Rights and Freedoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_Fundamental_Rights_and_Freedoms). The current constitution went in effect on 1 January 1993,[^cite_note-ustava-2] after the [dissolution of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_Czechoslovakia).

The Czech Republic has a [multi-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system). Between 1993 and 2013, the two largest political parties were the centre-left [Czech Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Social_Democratic_Party) (ČSSD) and centre-right [Civic Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Democratic_Party_\(Czech_Republic\)) (ODS). This changed in early 2014, with the rise of a new major political party [ANO 2011](40808475.md), which has since led two cabinets.

The [Economist Intelligence Unit](15303116.md) rated Czech Republic a "[full democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracy_Index#Definitions)" in 2024.[^cite_note-democracy-index-3] According to the [V-Dem Democracy indices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V-Dem_Democracy_indices) the Czech Republic was 2023 the 16th most electoral democratic country in the world.[^cite_note-vdem_dataset-4]

## Executive branch

The president is the head of state, and the prime minister is the head of government. The majority of executive power is given to the Cabinet, which consists of the prime minister, deputy prime ministers and ministers (usually heads of the ministries).

### President

Main article: [President of the Czech Republic](410288.md)

The [president of the Czech Republic](410288.md) is elected by a direct vote for five years. They can only serve for two terms. The president is a formal [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) with limited executive powers specified in the articles 54 to 66 of the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Czech_Republic):[^cite_note-ustava-2]

- to appoint or dismiss the prime minister and other members of the Cabinet

- to appoint or dismiss the entire Cabinet

- to confirm or decline a resignation of the prime minister and other members of the Cabinet

- to summon a session of the Chamber of Deputies

- to dissolve the Chamber of Deputies when specific conditions described in the Constitution are met

- to pardon and mitigate penalties imposed by the court, order not to initiate criminal proceedings, suspend them if they are already initiated and to wipe previous criminal records

- to declare the date of elections to the Chamber of Deputies and the Senate

- to bestow state honors

- to appoint and promote generals

- to appoint judges

- to appoint the president and vice-president of the [Supreme Audit Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Audit_Office_\(Czech_Republic\))

- to appoint members of the [Board of the Czech National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_the_Czech_National_Bank?action=edit\&redlink=1)

- to appoint or dismiss heads of diplomatic missions

The president is also the commander in chief of the Armed Forces and ratifies all domestic laws and international agreements.

### Cabinet

Main article: [Government of the Czech Republic](14380629.md)



Prague Castle, the seat of the president of the Czech Republic



[Straka Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straka_Academy), the seat of the Cabinet, [Prague](23844.md)



[Wallenstein Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallenstein_Palace), the seat of the Senate, [Prague](23844.md)

The [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_Czech_Republic) is the supreme executive body in the Czech Republic. It makes its decisions as a body. It is held responsible by the Chamber of Deputies. The president appoints every new prime minister, who then chooses the ministers. All ministers of the Cabinet need to be approved by the president and within thirty days after the presidential approval they must ask the Chamber of Deputies for a vote of confidence.

#### Prime minister

Main article: [Prime Minister of the Czech Republic](410283.md)

The prime minister is the head of government. The prime minister organizes the work of the Cabinet, presides over it and acts in its name. The prime minister sets the agenda for most foreign and domestic policies but has to obtain the president's approval to hire or dismiss any other member of the Cabinet.[^cite_note-5]

#### Ministers

Ministers are any member of the Cabinet who are not the prime minister. They are usually the head of a [ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_\(government_department\)), but this is not required. A ministry – sometimes called government department – is a governmental organisation that manages a specific sector of public administration. The number of ministries varies depending on the particular Cabinet and is managed by the [Competence Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competence_Law?action=edit\&redlink=1). As of 2021, the Czech Republic had 13 ministers and 14 ministries.

## Legislative branch

Main article: [Parliament of the Czech Republic](3991200.md)



[Thun Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thun_Palace), the seat of the Chamber of Deputies, [Prague](23844.md)

The [Parliament](3991200.md) (*Parlament* in Czech) consists of [two houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism). The lower house is the Chamber of Deputies, and the upper house is the Senate.

### Chamber of Deputies

The [Chamber of Deputies](3991120.md) (*Poslanecká sněmovna* in Czech) has 200 members, elected for four-year terms by [proportional representation](23298.md) with a 5% [election threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_threshold). The Chamber of Deputies elections happen every four years, unless the reigning Cabinet prematurely loses the Chamber of Deputies' support. Candidates for every political party participating in the elections are split among 14 [electoral districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_districts), which are identical to the country's administrative [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_the_Czech_Republic). A citizen must be at least 21 years old to be eligible for candidacy.

The Chamber of Deputies was formerly known as the [Czech National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_National_Council). It has the same powers and responsibilities as the now-defunct [Federal Assembly of the Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Assembly_\(Czechoslovakia\)).

### Senate

The [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Czech_Republic) (*Senát* in Czech) has 81 members, each elected for a six-year term. Senate elections happen every two years and only a third of the seats is contested each time. All of the 81 Senate electoral districts are designed to contain roughly the same number of voters. The Senate elections use a [two-round system](25932.md), when the two most successful candidates from the first round face each other again in the second round usually a week later. Only citizens who have reached the age of 40 are eligible for candidacy. The senate's function is to be a stabilizing force and its influence is significantly lower than that of the Chamber of Deputies.

## Judicial branch

Main article: [Judiciary of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_the_Czech_Republic)

The Czech court system recognizes four categories of courts and the Constitutional Court, which stands outside of the court system.

### Constitutional Court

The [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_the_Czech_Republic)'s main purpose is to protect people's constitutional rights and freedoms. The decisions of the court are final, cannot be overturned and are considered a source of law. The court is composed of 15 justices who are named for a renewable period of 10 years by the president and approved by the Senate.[^cite_note-6] Its functionality is similar to that of the [Supreme Court of the United States](31737.md).

### Supreme courts

There are two supreme courts in the court system of the Czech Republic – the Supreme Court and the Supreme Administrative Court. Both reside in [Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno).



Simplified chart of the Czech political system

#### Supreme Court

The [Supreme Court of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Czech_Republic) is the court of highest appeal for almost all legal cases heard in the Czech Republic. The justices of the Supreme Court analyze and evaluate legally effective decisions of lower courts. They unify the Czech judicature.

#### Supreme Administrative Court

The [Supreme Administrative Court of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Administrative_Court_of_the_Czech_Republic) hears cases regarding unlawful decisions and procedures of state authorities. It examines objections to elections and has the authority to ban or suspend the activity of political parties. It resolves competence disputes between governmental organizations and also serves as disciplinary court for other members of the judiciary.

### High courts

There are two [high courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Courts_\(Czech_Republic\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the Czech Republic – one in [Prague](23844.md), the other in [Olomouc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olomouc). They serve as courts of appeal to Regional Courts in cases where the Regional Court acted as a court of first instance. Presidents of the high courts are appointed by the president for seven years. Vice-presidents are appointed by the [minister of justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Czech_Republic\)) and also serve a seven-year term. A justice is required by law to have at least eight years of experience before becoming a member of a High Court.[^cite_note-7]

### Regional courts

[Regional courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_courts_\(Czech_Republic\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) serve mainly as the courts of appeal to district courts and also as the only other administrative court beyond the Supreme Administrative Court. They can also act as courts of first instance in cases of more severe criminal charges, disputes between corporations, or disputes over intellectual property. There are eight regional courts in the Czech Republic. These courts are located in Brno, [Ostrava](464078.md), [Hradec Králové](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hradec_Kr%C3%A1lov%C3%A9), [Ústí nad Labem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Ast%C3%AD_nad_Labem), [Plzeň](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plze%C5%88), and [České Budějovice](149384.md), alongside two in [Prague](23844.md).

### District courts



Seat of the Constitutional Court, Brno

District courts serve as the courts of first instance in almost all civil or criminal proceedings. There are a total of 86 district courts in the Czech Republic. Notaries and executors are appointed by the [minister of justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Justice_\(Czech_Republic\)) to their jurisdictions.

## Regional government



Regions of the Czech Republic with their coats of arms

Main article: [Regions of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_the_Czech_Republic)

The Czech Republic is divided in 14 administrative [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_the_Czech_Republic), including one for the capital Prague. The older system of 73 administrative [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_the_Czech_Republic) (*okresy* in Czech) and 4 municipalities was abandoned in 1999 in an administrative reform. Each of the regions has a regional council with a varied number of regional councilors and a president of the regional cabinet (*hejtman* in Czech) as its formal head. The capital of Prague is the only exception to this, as the [City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_City_Council) acts both as regional and municipal governing body and led by a mayor. Regional councilors are elected for four-year terms similarly to deputies in the Chamber of Deputies. All adults eligible to vote are also eligible to be a candidate to a regional council.

## Recent political developments

From 1991, the Czech Republic, originally as part of Czechoslovakia and since 1993 in its own right, has been a member of the [Visegrád Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visegr%C3%A1d_Group) and from 1995, the [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development). The Czech Republic joined [NATO](21133.md) on 12 March 1999 and the [European Union](9317.md) on 1 May 2004. On 21 December 2007 the Czech Republic joined the [Schengen Area](16565139.md).[^cite_note-9]

Until 2017, either the [Czech Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Social_Democratic_Party) or the [Civic Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Democratic_Party_\(Czech_Republic\)) had led the governments of the Czech Republic. In October 2017, the right-wing populist movement [ANO 2011](40808475.md), led by the country's second-richest man, [Andrej Babiš](40378134.md), who won the [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Czech_legislative_election) with three times the vote of his closest rival, the centre-right Civic Democrats.[^cite_note-10] In December 2017, Czech President [Miloš Zeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Zeman) appointed Andrej Babiš as the new prime minister.[^cite_note-11]

On 28 November 2021, Czech President Miloš Zeman appointed opposition leader [Petr Fiala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Fiala) as the country's new prime minister. The centre-right coalition [Spolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spolu_\(Czech_Republic\)) (meaning Together) won tightly contested [legislative elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Czech_legislative_election) in October 2021 against Prime Minister Andrej Babiš and his ANO party.[^cite_note-12] In January 2023, Former NATO general [Petr Pavel](42272533.md) won the [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Czech_presidential_election) runoff over Andrej Babiš to succeed Miloš Zeman as the fourth president of the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-13] In December 2025, Babis returned as the Czech Republic's new prime minister,[^cite_note-14] following the ANO party's win in the [October parliamentary election](65703300.md).[^cite_note-15]
