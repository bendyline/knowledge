**Kent** is a city in [King County, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_County,_Washington), United States. It is part of the [Seattle metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_metropolitan_area) and had a population of 136,588 as of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-2020_Census_(City)-5] The population was estimated at 134,871 in 2025,[^cite_note-2025_Quickfacts-6] making it the fourth-most populous municipality in greater Seattle and the [sixth-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Washington) in [Washington](13015878.md). The city is connected to [Seattle](11388236.md), [Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Washington), and [Tacoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacoma,_Washington) via [State Route 167](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Route_167) in addition to [Interstate 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5_in_Washington), [Sounder commuter rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sounder_commuter_rail), and commuter buses.

Incorporated in 1890, Kent is the second-oldest incorporated city in the county, after the county seat of Seattle. It is generally divided into three areas: West Hill (mixed residential and commercial along Interstate 5), Valley (primarily industrial and commercial with some medium-density residential; significant parkland along the [Green River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_River_\(Duwamish_River_tributary\))), and [East Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Hill-Meridian,_Washington) (primarily residential with retail).

## History

The Kent area was first permanently settled by European Americans in the 1850s along the banks of what was then the [White River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_River_\(Puyallup_River\)). The first settler was Samuel Russell, who sailed the White and [Duwamish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duwamish_River) Rivers until he claimed a plot of land southeast of modern-day downtown Kent in the spring of 1853. Russell was followed by several other settlers, who quickly staked claims around the area.[^cite_note-8] The settlements were originally known as "White River" and later the town was called "Titusville" after an early settler by the name of James Henry Titus.[^cite_note-9] (There is still a "Titusville Station" sign on Gowe Street near First Avenue.)

In 1861, a post office was established under the name White River and was located at the farm of David and Irena Neely, who settled in modern-day Kent in 1854. In 1855, their farm was [attacked by Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Seattle_\(1856\)#Prelude) when David Neely served as a lieutenant in the territorial army. Another settler was [Henry L. Yesler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Yesler), who was the first sawmill operator in Seattle.[^cite_note-WR-10] By 1870, the population was 277 and all of the quality bottomland had been claimed.[^cite_note-11]

Throughout the 1860s and '70s, grain and forage crops such as wheat, barley, oats, hay, and [timothy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timothy_\(grass\)) accounted for much of the annual return of farmers in the valley. During the late 1870s, the town discovered [hops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hops) production as a major source of income.[^cite_note-12] Due to an [aphid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aphid) invasion, which affected hops crops in Europe,[^cite_note-13] hops from the [Puget Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget_Sound) area began to command high prices. Hops were shipped from Titusville either by the river or rail. In 1889, the town was renamed for [the County of Kent](16766.md), the major hops-producing region in England. [Ezra Meeker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezra_Meeker) was asked by the Northern Pacific Railroad to name its station. Meeker suggested that it be known as Kent as it was "Hop Capitol of the West".[^cite_note-WR-10] Hops production in the White River Valley came to an end soon after its own invasion of aphids in 1891.[^cite_note-HistoryLink-14] Kent was officially incorporated on May 28, 1890, with a population of 793, the second city incorporated in King County (after Seattle).[^cite_note-HistoryLink-14]

After the turn of the 20th century, the area turned to [dairy farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_farming) and was home to a [Carnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_\(brand\)) condensed milk plant.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Flooding from both the [Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_River_\(Duwamish_River_tributary\)) and the White Rivers was a constant problem. In 1906, flooding changed the course of the White River, which reduced the flood hazard by half. The Green River continued to present problems until the [Howard A. Hanson Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_A._Hanson_Dam) was built at Eagle Gorge in 1962.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] During and after the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), Kent was known as the "Lettuce Capital of the World".[^cite_note-19] After [World War II](32927.md), the city began to grow more rapidly. From 1953 to 1960, its size grew 12-fold. In 1965, [Boeing](18933266.md) began building in Kent, followed a few years later by other aerospace and high-technology companies.[^cite_note-20]

In keeping with the King County Annexation Initiative, which seeks to annex large urban unincorporated areas into city limits or incorporate new cities out of those areas,[^cite_note-21] the Panther Lake area (known officially as the Kent Northeast Potential Annexation Area)[^cite_note-22] was proposed for annexation to the city of Kent. The annexation was voted on by residents of the potential annexation area on November 3, 2009; the area was officially annexed on July 1, 2010.[^cite_note-AnnexFAQ-23] The city grew in area by approximately 5 square miles (13 km<sup>2</sup>) and 24,000 residents.[^cite_note-AnnexFAQ-23]

## Geography



Aerial view of Kent, looking west to east: All three main sections of the city are visible (West Hill, Valley, and East Hill), with downtown Kent near the center of the image.

Kent is located in southern King County and is part of Seattle's metropolitan area. The city is divided into three geographic sections: West Hill, the Kent Valley, and East Hill. Downtown Kent is located on the east side of the valley, about 16 miles from downtown Seattle and downtown Bellevue. Adjoining cities are [Renton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renton,_Washington) to the north, [Covington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covington,_Washington) to the east, [Auburn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auburn,_Washington) to the south, [Federal Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Way,_Washington) to the southwest, [Des Moines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Moines,_Washington) to the west, [SeaTac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaTac,_Washington) to the northwest, and [Tukwila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukwila,_Washington) to the north.[^cite_note-King_County_Maps-24] According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 34.167 square miles (88.49 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 0.583 sq mi (1.51 km<sup>2</sup>) (1.71%) is covered by water.[^cite_note-CenPopGazetteer2025-3]

Major waterways include the Green River, which flows north through Kent on its way to [Puget Sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puget_Sound). The largest lake is [Lake Meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Meridian) on the city's East Hill. [Clark Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Lake_\(Kent,_Washington\)) and [Lake Fenwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Fenwick) are both surrounded by city parks. [Mount Rainier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Rainier) is a prominent geographical landmark to the southeast.

## Economy

The economy of Kent consists of commuters traveling to the main urban centers of the Seattle metropolitan area (particularly [downtown Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Seattle)), extensive manufacturing and warehousing within the city, and retail/personal services catering to residents.[^cite_note-City_of_Kent_Comprehensive_Plan-28] The manufacturing and distribution industry in Kent and the surrounding area ranks 4th among markets in the United States.[^cite_note-29]

Kent is a hub for the [space industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_industry) with the corporate headquarters of [Blue Origin](887418.md) and [Stoke Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_Space), as well as facilities for [Cowboy Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowboy_Space_Corporation).[^cite_note-30] Other corporate headquarters in Kent include [Oberto Sausage Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberto_Sausage_Company), [Seattle Bicycle Supply](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torker), and [Omax Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omax_Corporation). [Amazon](90451.md), [Boeing](18933266.md), [Whirlpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whirlpool_Corporation), and [General Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Electric) operate sizable facilities in the city. Due to its central location within the metropolitan area, Kent is home to a large and growing warehouse district. Honoring the 100th anniversary of Oberto Sausage Company's presence there, the city designated a section of South 238th Street as Oberto Drive in May 2018.[^cite_note-31]

### Boeing



Aerial view of the Kent Space Center

The Boeing Kent Space Center was opened with a public dedication ceremony on October 24, 1964. Keynote speakers at the event were [William "Bill" Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_McPherson_Allen), chairman and CEO of the Boeing Company; future Washington Governor [Dan Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_J._Evans); and Alex Thorton, mayor of the City of Kent. The event featured public tours of the laboratories and facilities used to build the [Lunar Roving Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_Roving_Vehicle) used in the [Apollo program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_program).[^cite_note-32]

### Steel

Kent is home to a large steel industry dating back to the early 20th century.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] Steel and metal manufacturers include:

- Salmon Bay Steel Company operated in Kent for 50 years before closing. Birmingham Steel purchased Salmon Bay in 1991. Salmon Bay went on to buy Bethlehem Steel (Seattle Steel) in [West Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Seattle). Years after the purchase, complaints were made of pollution in the Green River Valley about pollution from the Salmon Bay melting facility, and the facility was shut down.

  [^cite_note-35]

- Puget Sound Steel is an independently owned and operated-unique specialty fabricator of reinforcing steel and a supplier of related reinforcement products since 1961. It has been the Northwest's select supplier of fabricated rebar and steel reinforcement to commercial, highway, industrial, and residential building contractors. Works include large-scale projects, including bridges and skyscrapers.

  [^cite_note-36]

  [^cite_note-37]

- Pacific Metal Company (PMC) in Seattle in 1947 opened a 19,000-square-foot plant. The business and facilities continued to grow for 30 years to meet local needs, as well as the emerging markets of [Alaska](624.md). Even the expanded 40,000 sq ft warehouse and sales office was deemed insufficient, and in 1979, an 80,000 sq ft facility was built south of the city of Seattle in the Kent Valley at [Tukwila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukwila,_Washington). In September 2010, PMC moved to a new location just 3 miles southeast in the city of Kent. PMC is a stocking distributor of nonferrous metals, specializing in stainless steel, copper, aluminum, and brass products, as well as ferrous products specializing in cold=rolled, coated (zinc and aluminum), and painted coils and sheets.

  [^cite_note-38]

- TMX Aerospace, a division of ThyssenKrupp Steel North America, provides materials including steel, brass, and copper, as well as exclusive supply-chain management support for the Boeing Commercial Airplanes group.

As of the 2024 [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey), an estimated 46,607 households were in Kent, with an average of 2.6 persons per household. The city has a median household income of \$92,497. About 14.4% of the city's population lives at or below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_the_United_States). Kent has an estimated 65.3% employment rate, with 30.3% of the population holding a bachelor's degree or higher and 87.8% holding a high-school diploma.[^cite_note-QF-43] The 49,952 housing units had an average density of 1,487.37 per square mile (574.3/km<sup>2</sup>). The median age in the city was 37.6 years.

### Sports and entertainment

In 2003, Kent was named *[Sports Illustrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_Illustrated)'*&#x73; Sportstown of the year for Washington. In January 2006, a shopping and entertainment center, known as Kent Station, opened in downtown Kent adjacent to the transit station of the same name.

The [accesso ShoWare Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accesso_ShoWare_Center) is a 6,500-seat indoor arena that hosts two minor-league sports teams: the [Seattle Thunderbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Thunderbirds) play ice hockey in the U.S. Division of the [Western Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Hockey_League),[^cite_note-59] and the [Tacoma Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacoma_Stars) play indoor soccer in the [Major Arena Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_Arena_Soccer_League).[^cite_note-60] The [Seattle Kraken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Kraken) of the [National Hockey League](21809.md) played a preseason game at the arena on October 2, 2021, losing to the [Calgary Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Flames) in front of a sellout crowd.[^cite_note-61] The arena hosted the [2012 Skate America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Skate_America) figure skating competition, which drew a total of 13,172 spectators over its three-day run,[^cite_note-62] and the 2015 [Junior Roller Derby World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Roller_Derby_Association).[^cite_note-63]

## Parks and recreation

Kent's park system includes 55 parks, miniparks, playfields, skateparks, greenbelts, and other related facilities. The parks range in size from as small as 4,300 square feet (400 m<sup>2</sup>) to over 310 acres (1.3 km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-64] Riverbend Golf Complex, featuring an 18-hole course which is one of the busiest in Washington, is located in Kent. An adjacent par-3 course was actively used by locals for years before being shut down in 2017 to make room for a mixed-used development.[^cite_note-65]

## Education

Public primary and secondary education in the vast majority of Kent and a number of neighboring cities and unincorporated areas is provided by the [Kent School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_School_District),[^cite_note-KingCoSDMap-66] which includes four high schools, seven middle schools, 28 elementary schools, and two academies. The Kent School District has an individualized graduation and degree program named iGrad, which is aimed at helping dropouts aged 16–21 who are willing to get back to school.[^cite_note-k12-67]

[Federal Way Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Way_Public_Schools), which includes a portion of Kent, has several schools within the city limits. Residents of far east Kent are zoned in the Tahoma School District. Other portions are in the [Highline Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highline_Public_Schools) and the [Renton School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renton_School_District).[^cite_note-KingCoSDMap-66] A branch of [Green River Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_River_Community_College) opened in Kent Station in 2007; it is now called Green River College.[^cite_note-68]

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

The main north–south highway serving Kent is [State Route 167](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Route_167), a freeway that connects the city to Renton and [Puyallup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puyallup,_Washington). The city includes portions of [Interstate 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5_in_Washington) to the west and [State Route 18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Route_18) to the east; both are major freeways that provide inter-regional connections. [State Route 516](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Route_516) travels east–west across Kent and connects all three freeways, while [State Route 181](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Route_181) and [State Route 515](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Route_515) provide further north–south connections as city streets.[^cite_note-69] The older portions of the city, primarily in downtown and the valley floor, are laid out with a continuous grid of arterial streets.[^cite_note-KentTMP-70]

The city is served by two [public transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) providers: [King County Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_County_Metro) and [Sound Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_Transit). The main hub is [Kent Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_station_\(Sound_Transit\)), a [Sounder commuter rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sounder_commuter_rail) station with service to [downtown Seattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Seattle), [Tacoma Dome Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tacoma_Dome_Station), and bus connections. Sound Transit operates Sounder service primarily during peak hours on weekdays and has three [Sound Transit Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_Transit_Express) bus routes connecting to [Bellevue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellevue,_Washington), [Redmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redmond,_Washington), and [Seattle–Tacoma International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle%E2%80%93Tacoma_International_Airport).[^cite_note-KentTMP-70][^cite_note-KentTMP-Appendix-71] King County Metro operates local and regional service from Kent Station and other hubs, including [park and ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_and_ride) facilities.[^cite_note-KentTMP-70] Metro's [RapidRide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RapidRide) bus rapid transit system includes the [A Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RapidRide_A_Line), which runs on [State Route 99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_State_Route_99) in western Kent; the [I Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RapidRide_I_Line), scheduled to open in 2027, will connect downtown Kent to Renton and Auburn.[^cite_note-KentTMP-70][^cite_note-72] The [Link light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Link_light_rail) system is planned to be extended to [Federal Way, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Way,_Washington) in late 2025, with intermediate stops at the [Kent Des Moines station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kent_Des_Moines_station) near Highline College and the [Star Lake station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Lake_station) at South 272nd Street.[^cite_note-73]

Heavy rail service includes two major north–south lines through the Kent Valley, with freight traffic operations by the [BNSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BNSF_Railway) and [Union Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad) railroads.[^cite_note-74]
