**Jenin** ([/ʒɪˈniːn/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*zhin-EEN*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): جنين, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Jinīn*, [locally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Arabic) pronounced [\[ʒɪˈniːn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0d/LL-Q55633582\_%28ajp%29-AdrianAbdulBaha-%D8%AC%D9%86%D9%8A%D9%86.wav/LL-Q55633582\_%28ajp%29-AdrianAbdulBaha-%D8%AC%D9%86%D9%8A%D9%86.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/0/0d/LL-Q55633582_%28ajp%29-AdrianAbdulBaha-%D8%AC%D9%86%D9%8A%D9%86.wav/LL-Q55633582_%28ajp%29-AdrianAbdulBaha-%D8%AC%D9%86%D9%8A%D9%86.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a city in the [West Bank](33209.md), [Palestine](241405.md), and is the capital of the [Jenin Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenin_Governorate). It is a hub for the surrounding towns.[^cite_note-2] Jenin came under [Israeli occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupied_territories) in 1967, and was put under the administration of the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) as [Area A of the West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord), a [Palestinian enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclave), in 1995.[^cite_note-Beinin-3]

The city had a population of approximately 50,000 people in 2017, whilst the [Jenin refugee camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenin_Camp) had a population of about 10,000, housing families of Palestinians who fled or were expelled from their homes during the [1948 Palestine War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Palestine_war).[^cite_note-PrelimCensus2017-1][^cite_note-PCBS_Jenin-4] The camp has since become a stronghold of [Palestinian militants](2646043.md), being the location of several incidents relating to the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md).

## Etymology

Jenin has been identified as the place "[Gina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_\(Canaan\))" or "Ginah" mentioned in the [Amarna letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letters) from the 14th century BCE, as a town in [Canaan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan).[^cite_note-\:4-5] Jenin is commonly identified with the later biblical city of [Ein-Ganim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anem_\(ancient_city\)),[^cite_note-\:1-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-\:34-9] from [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): עֵין גַּנִּים, meaning "the spring of gardens"[^cite_note-\:0-10] or "the spring of Ganim",[^cite_note-11] probably referring to the many springs located nearby. The present-day Arabic name is believed to preserve the city's ancient name.[^cite_note-\:1-6]

Jenin is identified with a number of important towns mentioned in ancient sources. Throughout history, it was referred to as "Ein Ganim", "Beth Hagan", "Ginah", and "Ginae", along other names.[^cite_note-\:4-5][^cite_note-\:34-9]

## History

Tell Jenin is believed to constitute the original settlement core of the city.[^cite_note-\:1-6][^cite_note-Kohlp339-12] In the early 20th century, the tell was occupied by a modern cemetery and a threshing floor,[^cite_note-fauna-13] and today it is located at the center of Jenin's business district.[^cite_note-\:1-6][^cite_note-Kohlp339-12]

The tell is also known as *Tell el-Nawar*, [Nawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_people#Al-Nawar) being an Arabic word for a number of nomadic peoples, especially the [Dom people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dom_people), Middle Eastern relatives of the [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) (gypsies). This name reflects the pre-1948 presence of seasonal nomadic encampments at the site.[^cite_note-fauna-13]

### Late Bronze Age

#### Late Bronze II

Further information: [Gina (Canaan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_\(Canaan\))

Jenin has been identified as the place [*Gina*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_\(Canaan\)) or *Ginah* mentioned in the [Amarna letters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amarna_letters) from the 14th century BCE. At the time, it was a vassal state of the [New Kingdom of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Egypt).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-\:4-5] The people of Gina managed to kill the warlord [Labaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labaya) during the reign of [Pharaoh](23294.md) [Akhenaten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhenaten).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDodson201681-17]

### Iron Age

Further information: [Anem (ancient city)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anem_\(ancient_city\))

Jenin is identical to *Ein-Ganim*, which the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible) describes as a [Levite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Levi) city belonging to the [Israelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israelites) [tribe of Issachar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_of_Issachar).[^cite_note-\:4-5][^cite_note-\:34-9][^cite_note-\:2-18] It has also been associated with *Beth-Haggan*, mentioned in [2 Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_Kings) in connection with [Ahaziah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahaziah_of_Judah)'s flight from [Jehu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehu), before he is wounded at [Ibleam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibleam) and later dies in [Megiddo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Megiddo).[^cite_note-\:4-5][^cite_note-\:34-9][^cite_note-19] The [Book of Judith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Judith) renders its name as *Gini*.[^cite_note-\:1-6][^cite_note-\:4-5]

### Roman and Byzantine periods

[Josephus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus), a [Roman-Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Rome) historian of the 1st-century CE, mentions *Ginae* as being in the great plain, on the northern border of [Samaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaria).[^cite_note-\:1-6][^cite_note-War-20][^cite_note-21] During the Roman period, *Ginae* was settled exclusively by [Samaritans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaritans). The [people of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilean) were disposed to pass through their city during the [annual pilgrimages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Pilgrimage_Festivals) to Jerusalem.[^cite_note-22] In 51 CE, a Galilean Jew was killed in Ginae by hostile Samaritans while en route to Jerusalem to celebrate [Sukkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukkot). With [Roman procurator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_administration_of_Judaea_\(AD_6%E2%80%93135\)) [Cumanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventidius_Cumanus) failing to respond, Jewish [Zealots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zealots) led by Elazar the Son of Deinaeus (Ben Dinai) sought vengeance, and several Samaritan villages in [Aqrabatene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqraba,_Nablus) were destroyed.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

Biblical commentator [F. W. Farrar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F._W._Farrar) raised the possibility that this Samaritan village, "the first village at which \[a traveler taking the road from Galilee to [Judea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judea) over [Mount Tabor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor)] would arrive", was the one which rejected the disciples of Jesus in [Luke's Gospel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke's_Gospel) at the point where Jesus and his followers begin his journey towards [Jerusalem](16043.md).[^cite_note-25]

Ceramics dating from the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) period have been found here.[^cite_note-26] There is no mention of Jenin in the reports of the [Muslim conquest of the Levant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Levant) from the Byzantines, which, according to the historian Moshe Sharon, "is not surprising, since it was a small place of minor importance".[^cite_note-Sharon172-27]

### Crusader, Ayyubid and Mamluk periods

Jenin came under [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem) rule in 1103.[^cite_note-Khalidi-28] The Crusaders called it *Le Grand Gerin*[^cite_note-Kedar-29] (Latin: *Garinum* or *Gallina Major*),[^cite_note-Sharon172-27] to distinguish it from the town of [Zir'in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zir'in), which they called *Le Petit Gerin*.[^cite_note-Kedar-29][^cite_note-Khalidi-28] Under the Crusaders it was a small [seigniory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seigniory), forming part of the [Principality of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Galilee) or the [Kingdom of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem).[^cite_note-Sharon172-27]

Shortly before the [Battle of Hattin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Hattin) in 1187, Jenin was captured by the [Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty) sultan [Saladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladin),[^cite_note-Sharon172-27] who destroyed the nearby fort, [Castellum Beleismum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tel_Dothan#Crusader_period).[^cite_note-Boas-30] In the 1220s, the geographer [Yaqut al-Hamawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaqut_al-Hamawi) described Jenin as "a small and beautiful town, lying between [Nabulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabulus) and [Beisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beisan), in the Jordan Province. There is much water, and many springs are found here, and often have I visited it."[^cite_note-31] In 1229, a peace was concluded between [Frederick II, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) and Sultan [al-Kamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Kamil), during the [Sixth Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_Crusade), whereby the city was given to the Crusaders, but Sultan [as-Salih Ayyub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/As-Salih_Ayyub) was able to control it permanently in 1244 after the [Battle of La Forbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_La_Forbie).

In 1255, it was agreed between the Ayyubid sultan in Syria, [an-Nasir Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-Nasir_Yusuf), and the first [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Egypt) sultan in Egypt, [Izz al-Din Aybak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izz_al-Din_Aybak), to give the latter all of the lands lying west of the [Jordan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_River), and thus Jenin entered into the possession of the Mamluks. It was one of eleven subdistricts of Mamlakat Safad (Province of [Safed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed)).[^cite_note-Sharon174-32] In the late 13th century, Mamluk [emirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir) (commanders) stationed at Jenin were ordered by Sultan [Qalawun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalawun) (r. 1279–1290) "to ride every day with their troops before the fortress of '[Akka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre,_Israel), so as to protect the coast and the merchants."[^cite_note-Ayalonp168-33] As one of the stations of the Mamluk *[barid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barid)* (postal route) between the Mamluk capital [Cairo](6293.md) and [Damascus](8914.md), it was one of the towns where fires were lit to warn of a [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) attack.[^cite_note-Sharon174-32] The geographer [al-Dimashqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Dimashqi_\(geographer\)) mentioned Jenin around 1300.[^cite_note-34] From the time of Qalawun's son, Sultan [an-Nasir Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An-Nasir_Muhammad) (r. 1299–1309, 1310–1341), it was a station on the route where ice was transported to Cairo for the sultans' drink houses.[^cite_note-Sharon174-32] The Mamluk historian [al-Qalqashandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Qalqashandi) (d. 1418) described Jenin as "an ancient spacious town which is riding on a shoulder of a nice valley in which there is a river of flowing water" north of [Qaqun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaqun) "on the top end of Marj Bani Amer \[Jezreel Valley]".[^cite_note-Sharon174-32] He also noted that it contained the mausoleum of [Dihyah al-Kalbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dihyah_al-Kalbi), a [companion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Companions_of_Muhammad) of the Islamic prophet [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad).[^cite_note-Sharon174-32]

### Ottoman period



Painting of Jenin by [David Roberts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Roberts_\(painter\)), 1839, in *[The Holy Land, Syria, Idumea, Arabia, Egypt, and Nubia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Holy_Land,_Syria,_Idumea,_Arabia,_Egypt,_and_Nubia)*

Street scene in Jenin, 1917. An Ottoman Army soldier (center left) with a local Arab (center right)

The [Ottomans](22278.md) conquered Mamluk Syria in 1516. Jenin became the administrative center of a *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiya)* (subdistrict) of the [Lajjun Sanjak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lajjun) (Lajjun District).[^cite_note-Sharon174-32] The sanjak was officially called the Iqta (Fief) of Turabay until 1559 when it became officially known as the Lajjun Sanjak.[^cite_note-35] The [Turabay dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turabay_dynasty) was the ruling house of the [Bedouin](61770.md) Banu Haritha tribe, whose members held the governorship of Lajjun from the start of Ottoman rule through 1677.[^cite_note-36]

The [tax registers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daftar) from 1548 to 1549 report that Jenin had a population of eight households, all Muslim. They paid a fixed tax rate of 25% on agricultural products, including wheat, barley, summer crops, goats and beehives, in addition to occasional revenues; a total of 2,000 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e). All of the revenue went to a *[waqf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqf)* (religious endowment) in the name of the Mamluk sultan [Qansuh al-Ghuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ashraf_Qansuh_al-Ghuri) (r. 1501–1516).[^cite_note-HA160-37] Turabay rule was occasionally interrupted, including in 1564, when a certain Kemal Bey was appointed *[sanjak-bey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjak-bey)* (district governor) by the Ottomans.[^cite_note-38] On 15 October 1564 Kemal Bey requested from the *[beylerbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beylerbey)* (provincial governor) of [Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus_Eyalet) that the stone [caravanserai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai) of Jenin be repaired, garrisoned and serve as the chief headquarters of the Lajjun *sanjak-bey* in order for Lajjun to prosper and for the road connecting Damascus to [Jerusalem](16043.md) and [Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt_Eyalet) to become secure. The official response was that the caravanserai be turned into a fortress; the fortress became ruined at some later point and 19th-century residents of Jenin used to claim that certain large rocks strewn in the village were the remnants of the 16th-century fortress.[^cite_note-39]

The Turabays, who remained nomads in the plain between [Mount Carmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Carmel) and [Caesarea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarea_Maritima), made Jenin the administrative headquarters of Lajjun and used the town's Izz al-Din Cemetery to bury their dead.[^cite_note-40] A large, domed mausoleum was built for the grave of one of the chiefs and *sanjak-beys* of the family, Turabay ibn Ali (d. 1601). Known as Qubbat al-Amir Turabay (Dome of the Emir Turabay), it was described in a 1941 report as a ruined structure, and Sharon, writing in 2017, notes that it "does not exist anymore".[^cite_note-Sharon178-41] No other graves of the Turabays in Jenin had survived into the 20th century.[^cite_note-Sharon178-41] During the conflict between [Fakhr al-Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhr_al-Din_II) of the [Ma'n dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'n_dynasty), who governed the sanjaks of [Sidon-Beirut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidon-Beirut_Sanjak) and [Safed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safad_Sanjak), and the Turabays, in 1623, Fakhr al-Din captured Jenin and stationed his men there. In 1624 the most prominent Turabay chief and *sanjak-bey* of Lajjun, Ahmad ibn Turabay, drove out the Ma'nid troops from Jenin and established his personal residence in the town.[^cite_note-42]

In the mid-18th century, Jenin was designated the administrative capital of the combined districts of Lajjun and [Ajlun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ajlun).[^cite_note-Doumanip39-43] There are indications that the area comprising Jenin and Nablus remained functionally autonomous under Ottoman rule and that the empire struggled to collect taxes there. During the [Napoleonic Campaign in Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Campaign_in_Egypt) which extended into Syria and Palestine in 1799, a local official from Jenin wrote a poem enumerating and calling upon local Arab leaders to resist Bonaparte, without mentioning the Sultan or the need to protect the Ottoman Empire.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEQuataert2005\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidOX3lsOrXJGcCpgPA107_107\]-44]

In the late 19th century, some members of the [Jarrar family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarrar_family), who formed part of the *mallakin* (elite land-owning families) in Jenin, cooperated with merchants in [Haifa](55606.md) to set up an export enterprise there.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEYazbak1998\[httpsbooksgooglecombooksidDPseCvbPsKsCdqjeninottomanpgRA1-PA150_150\]-45] During the Ottoman era, Jenin was plagued by local warfare between members of the same clan.[^cite_note-46] The French explorer [Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) visited in 1870.[^cite_note-47] In 1882, the [PEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s *[Survey of Western Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine)* described Jenin as "The capital of the district, the seat of a [Caimacam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caimacam), a town of about 3,000 inhabitants, with a small bazaar. The houses are well built of stone. There are two families of Roman Catholics; the remainder are Moslems. A spring rises east of the town and is conducted to a large masonry reservoir, near the west side, of good squared stonework, with a long stone trough. This reservoir was built by 'And el Hady, Mudir of Acre, in the first half of the century \[..], north of the town is the little mosque of 'Ezz ed Din, with a good- sized dome and a minaret."[^cite_note-48]

### British Mandate



View of the rubble in Jenin after a quarter of the town was dynamited by British forces in 1938, as a retaliatory attack after a British official was assassinated

According to a [census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted in 1922 by the [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), Jenin had a population of 2,637 (2,307 Muslims, 212 Hindus, 108 Christians, seven Jews, and three Sikhs).[^cite_note-census1922-49] A following [census in 1931](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) showed a slight increase to 2,706 (2,610 Muslims, 103 Christians, two Jews, and one [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze)) with another 68 in nearby suburbs (all Muslims).[^cite_note-50] From 1936, Jenin became a center of rebellion against the [British Mandatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine) authorities. By the summer of 1938, residents of the city embarked on "an intensified campaign of murder, intimidation and sabotage" that caused the British administration "grave concern", according to a British report to the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations);[^cite_note-51] the population had further increased to 3,100.[^cite_note-52] The city played an important role in the [1936–39 Arab revolt in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%9339_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine), prompted by the death of [Izz ad-Din al-Qassam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izz_ad-Din_al-Qassam) in a fire-fight with British colonial police at the nearby town of [Ya'bad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ya'bad) months prior to the start of the revolt. On 25 August 1938, the day after the British Assistant District Commissioner was assassinated in his Jenin office, a large British force with explosives entered the town. Despite having captured and killed the assassin, British forces ordered the inhabitants to leave, and blew up one quarter of the town as a form of punishment.[^cite_note-53]

Jenin was used by [Fawzi al-Qawuqji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fawzi_al-Qawuqji)'s [Arab Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Liberation_Army) as a base.

The [village statistics of 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945) list the population as 3,990 (3,840 Muslims and 150 Christians).[^cite_note-54]

### 1948 War

Further information: [Battle of Jenin (1948)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jenin_\(1948\))

In the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), the city was defended by the [Iraqi Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Army), then captured briefly by the forces from Israel's [Carmeli Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmeli_Brigade) during the "Ten Days' fighting" following the cancellation of the first cease-fire. Prior to the battle, the city's residents fled temporarily.[^cite_note-55] The offensive was actually a feint designed to draw Arab forces away from the critical [Battle for Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_for_Jerusalem), and gains in that sector were quickly abandoned when Arab reinforcements arrived.

### Jordanian occupation (1948-1967)

In the wake of the [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), and after the [1949 Armistice Agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Armistice_Agreements), Jenin came under [Jordanian](7515964.md) rule. It was [annexed by Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) in 1950.

The Jenin refugee camp was founded in 1953 by Jordan to house displaced Palestinians who fled or were expelled during the 1948 War. In 2014 the camp had a population of 16,000. For 19 years, the city was under Jordanian control. A war cemetery for Iraqi soldiers and local combatants is located on the outskirts of Jenin.

The [Jordanian](7515964.md) census of 1961 found 14,402 inhabitants in Jenin.[^cite_note-Jordan1961-56]

In Jenin

Jenin has been under [Israeli occupation](59076253.md) since the [Six-Day War](29328.md), in 1967.

#### First Intifada

On 14 May 1989, during the early months of the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada). Mohammad Jibrin, aged 45, died in [Ramallah](44132.md) Hospital after being beaten by [Israeli soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_army) in Jenin. Three months later, in response to a question from a member of the [Knesset](74656.md), Defence Minister [Yitzhak Rabin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yitzhak_Rabin) wrote that there was no investigation by the Military Police Investigator.[^cite_note-57]

#### Oslo Accords and Second Intifada

According to [Joel Beinin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joel_Beinin), Jenin was classified as under the administration of the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) as [Area A of the West Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bank_areas_in_the_Oslo_II_Accord), a [Palestinian enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclave), in 1995.[^cite_note-Beinin-3] In 1996, [Israel](9282173.md) handed over control of the city to the [Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_National_Authority) in keeping with the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords).

According to the [BBC](19344654.md), the city is "Known among Palestinians" as "the [Martyrs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr_\(shahid\))' capital", but the reason they gave was Israeli statistics about [suicide bombings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_suicide_attacks), the BBC did not identify any non-Israeli source for the name.[^cite_note-dirt-58] During the Second [Palestinian uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_uprising) (also known as the [Al-Aqsa Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Intifada)) the camp's militants, some 200 armed men, included members of [Al-Aqsa Martyrs Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Martyrs_Brigades), [Tanzim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanzim), [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](13093253.md) (PIJ) and [Hamas](13913.md).[^cite_note-BBCmartyrs-59][^cite_note-Bernanp434-60] By Israel's count, at least 28 [suicide bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_suicide_attacks) were dispatched from the Jenin camp from 2000 to 2003 during the [Second Intifada](71717.md).[^cite_note-dirt-58] Israeli army weekly *[Bamahane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamahane)* attributes at least 31 militant attacks, totaling 124 victims, to Jenin during the same period, more than any other city in the West Bank.[^cite_note-61]

During the [al-Aqsa Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Aqsa_Intifada), Israel launched [Operation Defensive Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Defensive_Shield) with the stated aim of dismantling militant infrastructure so as to curb suicide bombings and other militant activities. The army encircled and entered six major Palestinian population centers in the West Bank, among them Jenin. During the [Battle of Jenin (2002)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jenin_\(2002\)) in April 2002, 23 Israeli soldiers and 52 Palestinians, including civilians,[^cite_note-AFPJTimesJul2011-62][^cite_note-JPostJul2010-63]  were killed.[^cite_note-64] [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) reported that the refugee camp, which was the major battleground, suffered extensive damage. Witnesses stated unarmed people were shot and denied medical treatment, and as a result died. Human Rights Watch have regarded many killings to be unlawful such as the death of a 57-year-old wheelchair-using man who was shot and run over by a tank despite having attached a white flag on his wheelchair. A 37-year-old man who was paralysed was crushed under the rubble of his house, his family was not allowed to remove his body. A 14-year-old boy was killed as he travelled to purchase groceries during the temporary relief of the curfew that was imposed by the army. Medical staff were shot at (one nurse killed) while trying to reach the wounded even after clearly being in uniform displaying the red crescent symbol.[^cite_note-65] There have also been reports of Israeli soldiers using Palestinians as human shields, one father described how a soldier rested his rifle on his 14-year-old son's shoulder as he shot.[^cite_note-66] Israel denied the entry of rescue teams and journalists into Jenin even after they withdrew. Over the following years, Jenin was subject to extended [curfews](297681.md) and [targeted killings](33928336.md).

During a gun-battle with [Islamic Jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Jihad_Movement_in_Palestine) militants who Israel says were firing at troops from inside the UN compound, an [Israeli](9282173.md) military [sniper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sniper) shot and killed a [UN Relief and Works Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Relief_and_Works_Agency_for_Palestine_Refugees_in_the_Near_East) (UNRWA) employee, [Iain Hook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iain_Hook) (54) on November 22, 2002.[^cite_note-67] The sniper reportedly mistook a cellphone in Hook's hands for a gun or grenade.[^cite_note-nyt-68]

#### 2010s



Downtown Jenin

In the framework of the [Valley of Peace initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_of_Peace_initiative), a joint Arab-Israeli project is under way to promote tourism in the Jenin region.[^cite_note-69] In 2010, 600 new businesses opened in Jenin.[^cite_note-70] The [Canaan Fair Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canaan_Fair_Trade) is headquartered in Jenin.[^cite_note-71] Director of [The Freedom Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Freedom_Theatre) in Jenin, [Juliano Mer-Khamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliano_Mer-Khamis), was killed by masked gunmen in the city in April 2011. Mer-Khamis co-founded the theatre with [Zakaria Zubeidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakaria_Zubeidi), former military chief of the al-Aqsa Brigades who had renounced violence.[^cite_note-72]

#### 2020s and Gaza War

On 6 February 2020, a Palestinian policeman, Tarek Badwan, was shot dead by an Israeli sniper as he stood at the entrance to the Jenin police station and chatted with a colleague. No explanation has been forthcoming. The incident was recorded on video.[^cite_note-73]

On 17 June 2022, Israeli forces conducted a raid in the al-Marah area of the city. During the raid, Israeli forces opened fire on a car, killing three Palestinians and seriously injuring another.[^cite_note-74]



A street in Jenin

On 26 January 2023, Israeli forces killed nine in a clash with Islamic Jihad militants during [a raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/January_2023_Jenin_incursion) in the city and refugee camp of Jenin.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

On 3 July 2023, shortly after 1 a.m., Israeli forces [attacked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2023_Jenin_incursion) the city's refugee camp using drone-fired missiles and ground troops. Eight Palestinians died from injuries sustained during the attack and a further eighty were injured, nine of them critically.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] Fifty Palestinians, whom Israeli forces labeled "militants", were arrested. Israeli forces cut off [telecommunications](33094374.md) and electricity in the area and medical professionals struggled to reach the injured. Israel claimed that while they were targeting suspected members of the [Jenin Brigades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenin_Brigades), an armed group, they acknowledged that innocent people may have been injured or killed in the raid. [Yoav Gallant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoav_Gallant), the [Israeli Minister of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Minister_of_Defense), said "The operation is progressing as planned," and [Eli Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Cohen_\(politician_born_1972\)), the [Israeli Foreign Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Foreign_Minister), said claimed the refugee camp had become a "center for terrorist activity" thanks to funding from Iranian sources.[^cite_note-79] The refugee camp has a population of roughly 17,000 inhabitants and is about a quarter a square mile in size.

On 19 September 2023, four Palestinians were killed during another Israeli military invasion of the city – among them a 15-year-old Palestinian boy, chased and killed for having noticed the Israeli undercover soldiers sneaking into the Jenin refugee camp.[^cite_note-80]

During the [Gaza war](74998519.md), Israeli forces carried out [multiple operations in Jenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October-November_2023_Jenin_incursion). On October 22, 2023, the IDF conducted [an airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ansar_Mosque_airstrike) targeting an underground compound beneath the [Al-Ansar Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ansar_Mosque_\(Jenin\)) in the city. It was reported to be the first airstrike in the West Bank since the Second Intifada. The IDF stated that the strike aimed at operatives from Hamas and PIJ who were planning a terror attack.[^cite_note-81]

Jenin was severely damaged during the [Gaza war](74998519.md). By June 2024 repeated bombing attacks and incursions with bulldozers by Israeli forces razed every street and reduced every public square to rubble. In one incident on 21 May, according to journalist [Gideon Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gideon_Levy), an Israeli sniper shot dead bystanders, including surgeon Oussaid Jabareen, who was shot while on his way to work at the Jenin Government Hospital.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

Aftermath of Israel raid on Jenin, August 2024

In late August 2024 Israel launched a large-scale, multi-day raid on Jenin as part of [Operation Summer Camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Israeli_military_operation_in_the_West_Bank), with one resident saying that "The water is cut off. The electricity is cut off, the sewage system is no longer working. All the infrastructure is destroyed, we no longer have any services that work." The governor of Jenin said that "The Israelis are besieging the hospitals and cutting off the city from the refugee camp, which has become a military zone with no access...neither the civil defence, nor the ambulances, nor the journalists can go and see what is happening there." The IDF, on the other hand, says that this is operation is "not extremely different from regular activity".[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] At least 20 Palestinians were killed by the IDF in this attack,[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-\:3-87] including a Palestinian man in his 80s.[^cite_note-\:3-87] Additionally, the Israeli forces fired at ambulances carrying "a dead and a wounded person",[^cite_note-\:3-87] leading to the injury of 2 EMTs and a volunteer doctor.[^cite_note-88]

Also in August, Israeli bulldozers destroyed miles of the city, including homes, businesses and infrastructure; Israeli soldiers blocked emergency responders from assisting residents. News reports include videos of this attack, as well as assertions by the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) that it is rooting out terrorism; and that it "undertakes all feasible precautions to avoid damaging essential infrastructure”, while acknowledging that these “operations in the area have caused unavoidable harm to certain civilian structures".[^cite_note-89]

In December 2024, the Palestinian Authority launched an anti-militant operation in Jenin, the largest in three decades, called "[Protect the Homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/December_2024_Palestinian_Authority_operation_in_Jenin)". The operation aims to regain control of the Jenin refugee camp, targeting militants and those fueling instability.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

On January 21, 2025, the IDF launched its own operation in Jenin, dubbed [Iron Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Israeli_raid_on_Jenin), aimed at clearing Jenin of militants and putting a stop to light arms being smuggled into the city.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] Operations in Jenin were ongoing as of late February 2025. Around 100 homes had been demolished, residents were being prevented from returning to the area by military checkpoints, and Israeli tanks had been deployed.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95]

## Geography

Jenin is situated at the foot of the rugged northernmost hills ([Jabal Nablus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabal_Nablus)) of the [West Bank](33209.md), and along the southern edge of the [Jezreel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jezreel_Valley) (Marj Ibn Amer),[^cite_note-Zeitoun-96] which the city overlooks.[^cite_note-97] Its highest elevation is about 250 meters above sea level and its lowest areas are 90 meters above sea level.[^cite_note-98] Immediately southwest of Jenin is the [Sahl Arraba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahl_Arraba) plain (Dothan Valley), while further south is the [Marj Sanur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marj_Sanur) valley.[^cite_note-NID-99] About 1.5 kilometers to Jenin's east is [Mount Gilboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Gilboa) (Jabal Faqqua).[^cite_note-100]

Jenin is 42 kilometers north of [Nablus](233369.md), 18 kilometers to the south of [Afula](323344.md), and 51 kilometers southeast of [Haifa](55606.md).[^cite_note-101] The nearest localities are [Umm at-Tut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umm_at-Tut) and [Jalqamus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalqamus) to the southeast, [Qabatiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qabatiya) and [Zababdeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zababdeh) to the south, [Burqin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burqin,_Palestine) to the southwest, [Kafr Dan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafr_Dan) to the west, [Arranah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arranah), [Jalamah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalamah) and the Arab Israeli village of [Muqeible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muqeible) to the north, [Deir Ghazaleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_Ghazaleh) to the northeast, and [Beit Qad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Qad) and [Deir Abu Da'if](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_Abu_Da'if) to the east.

## Public institutions and landmarks



The Jenin Horse

Jenin is home to many institutions, landmarks, and works of art. The [Khalil Suleiman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalil_Suleiman) Hospital is located in the city. [The Freedom Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Freedom_Theatre) is a theatre and cinema in the [Jenin refugee camp](23460648.md). [The Jenin Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jenin_Horse) is a famous work of art made of scrap metal from cars destroyed by Israeli forces, built in 2003 by German artist Thomas Kilpper and young people from the city.[^cite_note-113] The horse was destroyed by the IDF in October 2023.[^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115]

There is a monument honoring German pilots shot down in Jenin during the [First World War](4764461.md), which incorporates an original wooden propeller.[^cite_note-116] An old British Mandate landing strip, [Muqeible Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muqeible_Airfield), is located in the city.

### Holy sites



[Fatima Khatun Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatima_Khatun_Mosque)

The main and largest [mosque](19894.md) of Jenin is the [Fatima Khatun Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatima_Khatun_Mosque), built in 1566.[^cite_note-117] Another mosque in Jenin is the Al-Ansar Mosque.[^cite_note-idc20231022-118]

The ancient cemetery in Jenin houses the tomb of Sheikh Izz al-Din, a [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) saint of the [Rifa'i order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rifa%CA%BDi). According to local traditions, he was a descendant of [Ali Zayn al-Abidin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Zayn_al-Abidin), the grandson of [Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad) and the sixth [Sh'ia Imam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shia_Imam). According to a local tradition, Sheikh Izz al-Din fought alongside [Saladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saladin) against the [Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades). The tomb possibly predates the cemetery, which served as a burial site for the people of Jenin and the Turabay dynasty. Another tomb in the same cemetery is al-Sheikh Tarabiya, where the Turabay emir Ahmad ibn Turabay ibn Ali al-Harithi is buried.[^cite_note-\:03-119]

## Education and culture



Arab American University in Jenin

The [Arab American University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_American_University) is located in Jenin's vicinity.

[Cinema Jenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_Jenin) is the largest movie theater in the area. The cinema, which reopened in 2010 after a 23-year intermission, has indoor and outdoor screens, a film library and educational facilities.[^cite_note-120]

[Strings of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strings_of_Freedom) is an orchestra in Jenin founded by an [Arab citizen of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_citizen_of_Israel), Wafaa Younis, who travels form her home in central Israel to teach music to the local youth.[^cite_note-Concert-121]
