[Guinea](12176.md) has had four [constitutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution) previous to the current one.[^cite_note-1] Following the [2021 coup](68651369.md), the 2010 constitution was suspended by the ruling junta.[^cite_note-2] The current constitution was approved on 21 September 2025.[^cite_note-yes2025-3]

## Background

A [constitutional referendum was held on 28 September 1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_Guinean_constitutional_referendum) as part of a [wider referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_French_constitutional_referendum) across the [French Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Union) (and [France](5843419.md) itself) on whether to adopt the new [French constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_France); colonies voting to accept it would become part of the new [French Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Community); if rejected, the territory would be granted independence.[^cite_note-HDoG2005-4][^cite_note-5] More than 95% of voters of [French Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guinea) voted against the constitution, with a turnout of 85.5%,[^cite_note-AED-6] making it the only colony to vote no.[^cite_note-7]

## History

Guinea became an independent nation on 2 October 1958. The first constitution was enacted immediately afterward[^cite_note-HDoG2005-4] and was written in some haste.[^cite_note-An-Na'im-8] A 15-person commission wrote a draft constitution in 10 days, which was approved by the new national assembly on 10 November 1958 after a mere two hours of debate.[^cite_note-An-Na'im-8] However, during the dictatorial reign of the first president, [Ahmed Sékou Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_S%C3%A9kou_Tour%C3%A9), it was routinely ignored or altered.[^cite_note-HDoG2005-4]

In 1982, bowing to both international and internal pressure, Guinea adopted a new constitution which included better protections for human rights.[^cite_note-An-Na'im-8] After Touré's death in 1984, the government was toppled by a military [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Guinean_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). Another [constitutional referendum, held on 23 December 1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Guinean_constitutional_referendum), was approved by 98.7% of the voters, paving the way for a third constitution.[^cite_note-9]

A [2001 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Guinean_constitutional_referendum), which was boycotted by the opposition, amended this constitution, removing presidential term limits and lengthening the term from five years to seven.[^cite_note-BBC-10] Critics accused then-President [Lansana Conté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansana_Cont%C3%A9) of seeking to remain in power longer.[^cite_note-BBC-10]

When Conté died in 2008, Captain [Moussa Dadis Camara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moussa_Dadis_Camara) seized power in a [coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Guinean_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), but was shot in the head in December 2010.[^cite_note-BBC-10] He left the country to receive medical care and recuperate.[^cite_note-BBC-10] He later agreed not to come back, and Guinea returned to civilian rule.[^cite_note-BBC-10]

The fourth constitution was approved by referendum on 19 April 2010 and formally adopted on 7 May.[^cite_note-11]

After the [2021 coup d'état](68651369.md), the [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Guinea_Armed_Forces) announced that they had dissolved the constitution.[^cite_note-12]

A draft constitution was released in June 2025[^cite_note-13] and was approved in a referendum held on 21 September 2025.[^cite_note-yes2025-3]
