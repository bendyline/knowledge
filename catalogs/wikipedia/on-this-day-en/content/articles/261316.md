# Energy crisis

This article is about energy crises in general. For other uses, see [Oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_crisis_\(disambiguation\)).

An **energy crisis** or **energy shortage** is any significant [bottleneck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottleneck_\(production\)) in the supply of [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) resources to an [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy). In literature, it often refers to one of the energy sources used at a certain time and place, in particular, those that supply [national electricity grids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_electricity_grid) or those used as fuel in industrial development. Population growth has led to a surge in the global [demand for energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_demand) in recent years. In the 2000s, this new demand – together with [Middle East](19323.md) tension, the falling value of the US dollar, dwindling oil reserves, concerns over [peak oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_oil), and [oil price speculation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_speculation) – triggered the [2000s energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000s_energy_crisis), which saw the [price of oil](5137675.md) reach an all-time high of \$147.30 per barrel (\$926/m<sup>3</sup>) in 2008.

Most energy crises have been caused by localized shortages, wars and market manipulation. However, the recent historical energy crises listed below were not caused by such factors.

## Causes



The gasoline shortages of [World War II](32927.md) brought about the resurgence of horse-and-wagon delivery.

Most energy crises have been caused by localized shortages, wars and market manipulation. Some have argued that government actions like tax hikes, nationalisation of energy companies, and regulation of the energy sector shift supply and demand of energy away from its economic equilibrium.[^cite_note-1] However, the recent historical energy crises listed below were not caused by such factors. [Market failure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_failure) is possible when [monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_monopoly) manipulation of markets occurs. A crisis can develop due to industrial actions like [union](17626.md) organized [strikes](155134.md) or government embargoes. The cause may be [over-consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over-consumption), aging [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure), [choke point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choke_point) disruption, or bottlenecks at [oil refineries](195137.md) or port facilities that restrict fuel supply. An emergency may emerge during very cold winters due to increased consumption of energy.

Large fluctuations and manipulations in future [derivatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_derivative) can impact price. Investment banks trade 80% of oil derivatives as of May 2012, compared to 30% a decade ago.[^cite_note-2] This consolidation of trade contributed to an improvement of global energy output from 117,687 TWh in 2000 to 143,851 TWh in 2008.[^cite_note-3] Limitations on free trade for derivatives could reverse this trend of growth in energy production. Kuwaiti Oil Minister Hani Hussein stated that "Under the supply and demand theory, oil prices today are not justified," in an interview with Upstream.[^cite_note-4]

Pipeline failures and other accidents may cause minor interruptions to energy supplies. A crisis could possibly emerge after infrastructure damage from [severe weather](11593538.md). Attacks by terrorists or [militia](20622.md) on important infrastructure are a possible problem for energy consumers, with a successful strike on a [Middle East](19323.md) facility potentially causing global shortages. Political events, for example, when governments change due to regime change, monarchy collapse, [military occupation](816214.md), and [coup](25914154.md) may disrupt oil and gas production and create shortages. Fuel shortage can also be due to the excessive and wasteful use of the fuels.

## Historical crises

- [North Korea](21255.md) has had energy shortages for many years.

- [Zimbabwe](34399.md) has experienced a shortage of energy supplies for many years due to financial mismanagement.

## Emerging oil shortage

Main articles: [Oil depletion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_depletion) and [Peak oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_oil)

"Peak oil" is the period when the maximum rate of global [petroleum](23195.md) [extraction](8082027.md) is reached, after which the rate of production enters terminal decline. It relates to a long-term decline in the available supply of petroleum. This, combined with increasing demand, significantly increases the worldwide prices of petroleum-derived products. Most significant is the availability and price of liquid fuel for transportation.

The US Department of Energy in the [Hirsch report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirsch_report) indicates that "The problems associated with world oil production peaking will not be temporary, and past 'energy crisis' experience will provide relatively little guidance."[^cite_note-22]

### Mitigation efforts

Main article: [Mitigation of peak oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitigation_of_peak_oil)

To avoid the serious [social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society) and [economic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) implications a global decline in oil production could entail, the 2005 Hirsch report emphasized the need to find alternatives, at least ten to twenty years before the peak, and to phase out the use of petroleum over that time. Such [mitigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitigation_of_peak_oil) could include energy conservation, fuel substitution, and the use of unconventional oil. Because mitigation can reduce the use of traditional petroleum sources, it can also affect the timing of peak oil and the shape of the [Hubbert curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubbert_curve).

[Energy policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy) may be reformed leading to greater [energy intensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_intensity), for example in [Iran](14653.md) with the [2007 Gas Rationing Plan in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Gas_Rationing_Plan_in_Iran), [Canada](5042916.md) and the [National Energy Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Energy_Program) and in the US with the *[Energy Independence and Security Act of 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Independence_and_Security_Act_of_2007)* also called the *[Clean Energy Act of 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Energy_Act_of_2007)*. Another mitigation measure is the setup of a cache of [secure fuel reserves](4478230.md) like the United States [Strategic Petroleum Reserve](167198.md), in case of [national emergency](298608.md). [Chinese energy policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy_of_China#Energy_conservation) includes specific targets within their 5-year plans.

[Andrew McKillop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_McKillop?action=edit\&redlink=1) has been a proponent of a contract and converge model or capping scheme, to mitigate both emissions of [greenhouse gases](21350772.md) and a peak oil crisis. The imposition of a [carbon tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_tax) would have mitigating effects on an oil crisis. The Oil Depletion Protocol has been developed by [Richard Heinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Heinberg) to implement a powerdown during a [peak oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubbert_Peak_Theory) crisis. While many [sustainable development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_development) and energy policy organisations have advocated reforms to [energy development](533487.md) from the 1970s, some cater to a specific crisis in energy supply including [Energy-Quest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy-Quest) and the [International Association for Energy Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_for_Energy_Economics). The [Oil Depletion Analysis Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_Depletion_Analysis_Centre) and the [Association for the Study of Peak Oil and Gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_for_the_Study_of_Peak_Oil_and_Gas?action=edit\&redlink=1) examine the timing and likely effects of peak oil.

Ecologist [William Rees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Rees_\(academic\)) believes that

> To avoid a serious energy crisis in coming decades, citizens in the industrial countries should actually be urging their governments to come to an international agreement on a persistent, orderly, predictable, and steepening series of oil and natural gas price hikes over the next two decades.

Due to a lack of political viability on the issue, government-mandated fuel prices hikes are unlikely and the unresolved [dilemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilemma) of fossil fuel dependence is becoming a [wicked problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wicked_problem). A global [soft energy path](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_energy_path) seems improbable, due to the [rebound effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebound_effect_\(conservation\)). Conclusions that the world is heading towards an unprecedented large and potentially devastating global energy crisis due to a decline in the availability of cheap oil lead to calls for a decreasing dependency on [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel).

Other ideas concentrate on design and development of improved, energy-efficient urban infrastructure in developing nations.[^cite_note-23] Government funding for alternative energy is more likely to increase during an energy crisis, so too are incentives for [oil exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_exploration). For example, funding for research into [inertial confinement fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_confinement_fusion) technology increased during the 1970s.

Kirk Sorensen and others[^cite_note-Richard_Martin-24] have suggested that additional nuclear power plants, particularly [liquid fluoride thorium reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_fluoride_thorium_reactor) have the [energy density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_density) to mitigate [global warming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) and replace the energy from [peak oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_oil), [peak coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_coal) and [peak gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_gas). The reactors produce electricity and heat so much of the transportation infrastructure should move over to electric vehicles. However, the high process heat of the [molten salt reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molten_salt_reactors) could be used to make [liquid fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_fuels) from any carbon source.

## Social and economic effects

Main articles: [Energy economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_economics) and [Renewable energy commercialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_commercialization)

The [macroeconomic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_and_the_Macroeconomy) implications of a [supply shock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_shock)-induced energy crisis are large, because energy is the resource used to exploit all other resources. Oil price shocks can affect the rest of the economy through delayed business investment,[^cite_note-25] sectoral shifts in the labor market,[^cite_note-26] or monetary policy responses.[^cite_note-27] When [energy markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_market) fail, an energy shortage develops. Electricity consumers may experience intentionally engineered [rolling blackouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_blackout) during periods of insufficient supply or unexpected [power outages](296636.md), regardless of the cause.

Industrialized nations are dependent on oil, and efforts to restrict the supply of oil would have an adverse effect on the economies of oil producers. For the consumer, the price of [natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_gas_prices), [gasoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Price_of_petroleum) (petrol) and [diesel](81761.md) for cars and other vehicles rises. An early response from stakeholders is the call for reports, investigations and commissions into the price of fuels. There are also movements towards the development of more [sustainable urban infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_urban_infrastructure).



In 2006, survey respondents in the [United States](3434750.md) were willing to pay more for a [plug-in hybrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_hybrid) car.



Global new investments in renewable energy, 2004–2010

[^cite_note-UNEP_GT_2011-28]

In the market, new technology and [energy efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Efficient_energy_use) measures become desirable for consumers seeking to decrease transport costs.[^cite_note-29] Examples include:

- In 1980 [Briggs & Stratton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Briggs_&_Stratton) developed the first gasoline [hybrid electric automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_automobile); also appearing are [plug-in hybrids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plug-in_hybrid).

- the growth of [advanced biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_biofuel).

- [innovations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innovation) like the [Dahon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahon), a [folding bicycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folding_bicycle)

- modernized and electrifying passenger transport

- [Railway electrification systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_electrification_system) and new engines such as the [Ganz-Mavag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganz-Mavag) locomotive

- [variable compression ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_compression_ratio) for vehicles

Other responses include the development of [unconventional oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconventional_oil) sources such as [synthetic fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_fuel) from places like the [Athabasca Oil Sands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athabasca_Oil_Sands), more [renewable energy commercialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_commercialization) and use of [alternative propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_propulsion). There may be a [relocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Localism_\(politics\)) trend towards [local foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_food) and possibly [microgeneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microgeneration_\(energy\)), [solar thermal collectors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_thermal_collector) and other [green energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_energy) sources.

[Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) trends and [gas-guzzler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas-guzzler) ownership varies with fuel costs. Energy shortages can influence public opinion on subjects from [nuclear power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_plants) to electric blankets. Building [construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction) techniques—improved [insulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_insulation), reflective roofs, thermally efficient windows, etc.—change to reduce heating costs.

See also: [Green building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_building) and [Zero-energy building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero-energy_building)

The percentage of businesses indicating that energy prices represent a barrier to investment has increased in 2022 (82%) as found in recent surveys, particularly for those who see it as a significant obstacle (59%). According to varied energy prices and energy intensity across nations and industries, various countries have different percentages of businesses that view energy costs as a key obstacle, ranging from 24% in [Finland](10577.md) to 81% in [Greece](12108.md) for example.[^cite_note-\:83-30]

### Crisis management

An electricity shortage is felt most acutely in heating, [cooking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking), and [water supply](684928.md). Therefore, a sustained energy crisis may become a [humanitarian crisis](1251700.md). If an energy shortage is prolonged a [crisis management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crisis_management) phase is enforced by authorities. [Energy audits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_audit) may be conducted to monitor usage. Various curfews with the intention of increasing [energy conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conservation) may be initiated to reduce consumption. For example, to conserve power during the Central Asia energy crisis, authorities in [Tajikistan](30108.md) ordered bars and cafes to operate by candlelight.[^cite_note-31]

In the worst kind of energy crisis [energy rationing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_rationing) and fuel [rationing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rationing) may be incurred. [Panic buying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panic_buying) may beset outlets as awareness of shortages spread. Facilities close down to save on heating oil; and factories cut production and lay off workers. The risk of [stagflation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagflation) increases.
