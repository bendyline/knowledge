**Pavel Talankin** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Павел Ильич Таланкин; born 11 March 1991)[^cite_note-7] is a [Russian](25391.md) [teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher) and documentary filmmaker who worked as a teacher-organizer and school videographer at [Karabash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabash,_Chelyabinsk_Oblast) Primary School No. 1 in Karabash, Chelyabinsk Oblast, Russia.[^cite_note-MediazonaProfile-4][^cite_note-MeduzaInterview2025-8] He is credited as co-director, cinematographer, and narrator of the anti-war [opposition](38212364.md) documentary feature *[Mr Nobody Against Putin](79060547.md)* (2025).[^cite_note-SundanceFilm-3][^cite_note-IMDbFilm-9]

*Mr. Nobody Against Putin* premiered in the World Cinema Documentary program at the [2025 Sundance Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Sundance_Film_Festival) and received the World Cinema Documentary Special Jury Award.[^cite_note-SundanceFilm-3][^cite_note-SundanceAwards2025-10] The film won the [BAFTA Award for Best Documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Documentary) and the [Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Documentary_Feature_Film) at the [98th Academy Awards](79369474.md).[^cite_note-BAFTADoc2026-5][^cite_note-Oscars98-11] Talankin left Russia in 2024 and emigrated to [Prague](23844.md) ([Czech Republic](5321.md)).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-MediazonaProfile-4][^cite_note-MdocBio-1]

## Early life and education

Talankin grew up in [Karabash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabash,_Chelyabinsk_Oblast), an industrial town in [Chelyabinsk Oblast](475230.md).[^cite_note-MeduzaInterview2025-8] He attended Karabash Primary School No. 1 as a child and later returned to work there.[^cite_note-SundanceFilm-3][^cite_note-SofiaDocuMENTAL-13] His mother worked at the same school as a librarian.[^cite_note-MeduzaInterview2025-8]

Published accounts describe Talankin as working with children since his student years, including at the Orlyonok camp near Lake Uvildy and later in a children’s home and a library in Karabash.[^cite_note-MeduzaInterview2025-8][^cite_note-MediazonaProfile-4][^cite_note-IMDbPerson-14][^cite_note-MdocBio-1][^cite_note-SundanceFilm-3]

## Career

At Karabash Primary School No. 1, Talankin organized extracurricular activities and filmed school events such as assemblies and ceremonies as part of his job as the school videographer.[^cite_note-MediazonaProfile-4][^cite_note-MeduzaInterview2025-8] He taught children practical video production skills, including [shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmmaking) and editing.[^cite_note-MountainfilmBio-2][^cite_note-BIDFbio-15] In public reporting, Talankin’s pre-film community work in Karabash included activities with students connected to media and youth programming, including a 2021 project building a detailed [Minecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minecraft) model of Karabash with students.[^cite_note-MeduzaInterview2025-8][^cite_note-74RU2025-16]

Talankin collaborated with filmmaker [David Borenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Borenstein) on *Mr. Nobody Against Putin* using footage recorded inside his school over a two-year period following [Russia’s 2022 invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), and he left Russia in June 2024 carrying recorded material on seven [hard drives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_disk_drive).[^cite_note-SundanceFilm-3][^cite_note-MediazonaProfile-4] The Sundance film listing credits Talankin as co-director and cinematographer and identifies the film as a [Denmark](76972.md)/[Czech Republic](5321.md) production with a 90-minute runtime in Russian.[^cite_note-SundanceFilm-3] International festival programs in Bulgaria, Serbia, and Hungary scheduled screenings of the film, listing Talankin as co-director and identifying him as a teacher associated with the school where the film was shot.[^cite_note-SofiaDocuMENTAL-13][^cite_note-Beldocs-17][^cite_note-BIDFbio-15] The film won the [Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Documentary_Feature_Film) at the [2026 Oscars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Oscars).[^cite_note-18]

## Style and themes

Talankin’s credited filmmaking work centers on first-person documentary narration and observational footage recorded within a Russian primary school setting in which he worked officially as a videographer.[^cite_note-SundanceFilm-3][^cite_note-CineuropaReview-19] Reviews have described the film’s approach as combining on-the-ground school footage with diary-like narration to depict the integration of ideological messaging into everyday school routines.[^cite_note-CineuropaReview-19][^cite_note-ScreenReview-20]

## Reception

Coverage of Talankin in Russian-language reporting has emphasized his former role inside Karabash Primary School No. 1 and the risks associated with removing filmed school material from Russia after the start of the [full scale invasion in 2022](70149799.md).[^cite_note-MediazonaProfile-4][^cite_note-MeduzaInterview2025-8] Reviews in European and North American outlets discussed Talankin’s footage as a rare inside-school record of how war-related messaging and rituals appeared in day-to-day educational life during the period when he was filming.[^cite_note-CineuropaReview-19][^cite_note-ScreenReview-20]

## At the 98th Academy Awards

The documentary is based on footage from a school in the city of Karabash, showing the process of ideological indoctrination of children. The film *Mr. Nobody Against Putin* became a sensation and caused a mixed reaction in Russia.[^cite_note-21] When presented with the award, Talankin made a call to stop wars, mentioning children.

## Legal issues in Russia

A [Chelyabinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelyabinsk) court banned the documentary from streaming platforms – [VK Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VK_Video?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Yandex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yandex), and Motion Video – on the grounds that it "propagates extremism and terrorism."[^cite_note-22] The court cited the use of the [White-blue-white flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-blue-white_flag) and associated it with the [Freedom of Russia Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Russia_Legion).[^cite_note-23]

On March 27, 2026 the [Russian Ministry of Justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Ministry_of_Justice) added Talankin to its list of [foreign agents](39572380.md).[^cite_note-24]
