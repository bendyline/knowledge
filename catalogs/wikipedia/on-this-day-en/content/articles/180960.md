**Tochigi Prefecture** (栃木県, *Tochigi-ken[^cite_note-3]*) is an inland [prefecture](74517.md) of [Japan](15573.md) located in the [Kantō region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kant%C5%8D_region) of [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu).[^cite_note-4] Tochigi Prefecture has a population of 1,897,649 (1 June 2023) and has a geographic area of 6,408 [km<sup>2</sup>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_kilometre) (2,474 [sq mi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Square_mile)). Tochigi Prefecture borders [Fukushima Prefecture](211914.md) to the north, [Gunma Prefecture](212632.md) to the west, [Saitama Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Prefecture) to the south, and [Ibaraki Prefecture](181029.md) to the southeast.

[Utsunomiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utsunomiya) is the capital and largest city of Tochigi Prefecture, with other major cities including [Oyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyama,_Tochigi), [Tochigi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochigi,_Tochigi), and [Ashikaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashikaga,_Tochigi).[^cite_note-5] Tochigi Prefecture is one of only eight [inland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked) prefectures and its mountainous northern region is a popular tourist region in Japan. The [Nasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasu_District,_Tochigi) area is known for its [onsens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onsen), local [sake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sake), and [ski](28478.md) resorts, the villa of the [Imperial Family](3177105.md), and the [Nasushiobara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasushiobara_Station) station of the [Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkansen) railway line. The city of [Nikkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikk%C5%8D,_Tochigi), with its ancient [Shintō shrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinto_shrine) and [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) [temples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple), is a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-6]

## Prefectural overview

Situated among the inland prefectures of the northern part of the Kantō region, Tochigi is contiguous with Ibaraki, [Gunma, Saitama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tochigi%E2%80%93Gunma%E2%80%93Saitama_border), and Fukushima Prefectures.

The climate of Tochigi may be classified as a humid temperate zone with broad variations in temperature. Winters are arid with dry winds, while summers are humid with frequent thunderstorms.

The population of Tochigi as of November 2010 is approximately 2,005,096.

Located in the center of the prefecture is the largest open plain in the Kantō region. Shirane (2,578 metres (8,458 ft)), Nantai (2,484 metres (8,150 ft)) and Nasudake (1,917 metres (6,289 ft)) mountain are in the northern part of the area. Kinugawa, Nakagawa, and Watarase River originate in this region, which flow across the Kanto plain before emptying into the Pacific Ocean. Tochigi is the 20th largest prefecture in Japan with a total area of 6,408.09 square km.

As of 1 April 2012, 21% of the total land area of the prefecture was designated as [Natural Parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Japan#History), namely [Nikkō National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikk%C5%8D_National_Park), [Oze National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oze_National_Park), and eight Prefectural Natural Parks.[^cite_note-7]

## History

See also: [Historic Sites of Tochigi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_Sites_of_Tochigi_Prefecture)

Before the [Meiji Restoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiji_Restoration), Tochigi was known as [Shimotsuke Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shimotsuke_Province).[^cite_note-8]

In the early 15th century, the [Ashikaga Gakkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashikaga_Gakk%C5%8D), Japan's oldest school of higher education, was re-established in the prefecture, holding over 3,000 students by the 16th century. Saint [Francis Xavier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Xavier) introduced Ashikaga to the world as the best university in Japan.

In the early 17th century, Japan was unified under the [shōgun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dgun) [Tokugawa Ieyasu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_Ieyasu). After his death, the [Nikko Tōshō-gū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikk%C5%8D_T%C5%8Dsh%C5%8D-g%C5%AB) shrine was built in [Nikkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikk%C5%8D) in 1617 on what the shōguns thought of as holy ground to protect and worship Ieyasu. Its establishment brought Nikkō to national attention. Ieyasu's successors as [Tokugawa shogun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokugawa_shogunate) developed the [Nikkō Kaidō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikk%C5%8D_Kaid%C5%8D) (日光街道, part of the major road connecting Nikkō with [Edo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edo)) and acquired lavish processions to worship Ieyasu.&#x20;

In the late 19th century, the Tokugawa shogunate fell and the new government established the [prefectures](74517.md). The prefectural capital was established in the city of Tochigi after the unification of Utsunomiya Prefecture and Tochigi Prefecture in 1873.[^cite_note-9] By 1884, however, the capital was transferred to Utsunomiya.

In March 2011, following the [Fukushima Daiichi nuclear disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukushima_Daiichi_nuclear_disaster), levels of [radioactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactivity) in Utsunomiya were 33 times higher than normal.[^cite_note-10]

## Industry and agriculture



Tochigi prefecture population pyramid in 2020

Located close to Tōkyō, Tochigi is home to many corporations and industrial zones, including the [Kiyohara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiyohara) [Industrial Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory), one of the largest inland industrial complexes in the country.

Industrial manufacturing accounts for 36.6% of the prefecture's total output. Vehicle parts and accessories are the primary products, followed by vehicles, radios and televisions, pharmaceuticals, and wireless communication equipment.

Below are goods manufactured in Tochigi with the highest market share in Japan:

| Product | Share |
| --- | --- |

(The 2004 industrial analysis report published by the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry)

The annual gross agricultural output in Tochigi is about 274 billion yen. Rice, vegetables, and livestock are produced in the region. Tochigi is also known for strawberries, Chinese chives, and Japanese pears sold throughout Japan and exported to other countries. Approximately 55% of Tochigi is covered by forests. Mushrooms, such as [Shiitake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiitake) mushrooms, make up half of the forest industry, with an output of approximately 5.6 billion yen.

## Tourism

Nikkō National Park is famous for its [UNESCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Educational,_Scientific_and_Cultural_Organization) [World Heritage Site](44940.md) which was registered as the 10th World Heritage Site in 1999. This encompasses Rinnō-ji, [Nikkō Tōshō-gū](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikk%C5%8D_T%C5%8Dsh%C5%8D-g%C5%AB), [Mount Nantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Nantai), and [Futarasan Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Futarasan_Shrine). The [Kegon Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kegon_Falls), also in Nikkō, is popular with tourists. To travel between the city and the falls, automobiles and buses take the Irohazaka, a road with dozens of switchbacks. In addition, 400-year-old [Japanese Cedars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptomeria) (about 13,000 in total) line the famous [Cedar Avenue of Nikkō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Avenue_of_Nikk%C5%8D) for roughly 35 km, making it the longest tree-lined avenue in the world.[^cite_note-12]



Statues in Nikkō



Three wise monkeys at Nikkō Tōshō-gū

A more recent and modern attraction is the [Twin Ring Motegi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Ring_Motegi) Circuit race course, which hosts the only [IndyCar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IndyCar_Series) race outside the United States. The track also hosts many other race events including [Super Formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Formula) and motorcycle races as well as festivals and fireworks events.

Tochigi has many traditional festivals and events such as Nikkō Tōshō-gū's 1000 Samurai Procession and Horseback Archery Festival, and the city of Tochigi's Autumn Festival where doll floats are pulled around the city once every five years.

## Transportation and access

### Roads

Traversing the prefecture along the north–south axis and connecting to the rest of the country are the [Tōhoku Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dhoku_Expressway) and the new and old [Route 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_National_Route_4). From east to west spans [Route 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_National_Route_50), connecting southern Tochigi with Ibaraki and Gunma Prefectures.

Also connecting Tochigi, Gunma, and Ibaraki is the [Kita-Kantō Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kita-Kant%C5%8D_Expressway), with the 18.5 km that connect the Tochigi-Tsuga Interchange and the Utsunomiya-Kaminokawa Interchange. Portions of the Kita-Kantō Expressway are still being constructed and is set to be fully completed by 2011. The highway will link the region's other main transport arteries, the Tōhoku, the [Jōban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C5%8Dban_Expressway) and the [Kan-Etsu Expressways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan-Etsu_Expressway), providing a link to the international port of Hitachinaka in Ibaraki.

### Rail



[JR Utsunomiya Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utsunomiya_Station)



[Tōbu Nikkō Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dbu_Nikk%C5%8D_Station)

The [Tōhoku Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dhoku_Shinkansen) and the JR [Utsunomiya Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utsunomiya_Line) are the main railways running north and south in Tochigi. Shinkansen runs from [Tokyo Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo_Station) to [Oyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oyama_Station) in south Tochigi in 43 minutes. [Utsunomiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utsunomiya_Station) can be reached by rail in as little as 48 minutes, and many parts of Tochigi are within commuting range of central Tokyo. To the east and west, the [Mito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mito_Line) and [Ryōmō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ry%C5%8Dm%C5%8D_Line) Lines connect Tochigi to Ibaraki and Gunma.

### Air travel

[Fukushima Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fukushima_Airport) is approximately an hour's drive from [Utsunomiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utsunomiya) on the [Tōhoku Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%8Dhoku_Expressway). International and national air transportation is through [Narita International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narita_International_Airport) to the east of Tokyo, approximately three hours by vehicle from Utsunomiya.
