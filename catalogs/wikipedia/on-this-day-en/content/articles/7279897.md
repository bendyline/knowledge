The **United States Department of Defense** (**DoD**),[^cite_note-5] also referred to as the **Department of War** (**DoW**),[^cite_note-exec-order-14347-3] is an [executive department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_executive_departments) of the [United States federal government](195149.md) charged with coordinating and supervising the [United States Armed Forces](32212.md)—the [Army](32087.md), [Navy](20518076.md), [Marines](17349325.md), [Air Force](32090.md), [Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force), and, for some purposes, the [Coast Guard](32223.md)—and related functions and agencies. Headquartered at [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) in [Arlington County, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlington_County,_Virginia), just outside Washington, D.C., the stated mission of the Department of Defense is "to provide the military forces needed to deter war and ensure our nation's security".[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-taskandpurpose_20180629-7]

The Department of Defense is headed by the [secretary of defense](43998.md), a [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Cabinet)-level official who reports directly to the president of the United States. The president is [commander-in-chief](2015257.md#Commander-in-chief) of the U.S. Armed Forces. Within the Department of Defense are three subordinate military departments: the [Department of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Army), the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy), and the [Department of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Air_Force). In addition, four [national intelligence services](501244.md) are part of the Department of Defense: the [Defense Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Intelligence_Agency), [National Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Agency) (NSA), [National Geospatial-Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Geospatial-Intelligence_Agency), and [National Reconnaissance Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Reconnaissance_Office).

Other [agencies in the Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Department_of_Defense_agencies) include the [Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA) (DARPA), [Defense Logistics Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Logistics_Agency), [Missile Defense Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Defense_Agency), [Defense Health Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Health_Agency), [Defense Threat Reduction Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Threat_Reduction_Agency), [Defense Counterintelligence and Security Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Counterintelligence_and_Security_Agency), [Space Development Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Development_Agency) and [Pentagon Force Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentagon_Force_Protection_Agency). Additionally, the [Defense Contract Management Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Contract_Management_Agency) is responsible for administering contracts for the Department of Defense. Military operations are managed by eleven regional or functional [unified combatant commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_combatant_command). The Department of Defense also operates several joint services schools, including the [Eisenhower School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower_School_for_National_Security_and_Resource_Strategy) and the [National War College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_War_College).

As of November 2022, the department has over 1.4 million active-duty uniformed personnel in the six armed services,[^cite_note-statista_20221111-8] and over 747,000 civilian employees. It also supervises over 778,000 [National Guard](37988.md) and reservist personnel.[^cite_note-workforce-4]

## History

Further information: [Military history of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_the_United_States)

See also: [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947)

Faced with rising tensions between the [Thirteen Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Colonies) and the [British government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Great_Britain), one of the first actions taken by the [First Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Continental_Congress) in September 1774 was to recommend that the colonies begin defensive military preparations. In mid-June 1775, after the outbreak of the [Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War), the [Second Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Continental_Congress), recognizing the necessity of having a national army that could move about and fight beyond the boundaries of any particular colony, organized the [Continental Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Army) on 14 June 1775.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-June_14-10] Later that year, Congress would charter the [Continental Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Navy) on 13 October,[^cite_note-Navy_Birthday-11] and the [Continental Marines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Marines) on 10 November.

### War Department and Navy Department

Main articles: [United States Department of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_War) and [United States Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy)

Upon the seating of the [1st U.S. Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_United_States_Congress) on 4 March 1789, legislation to create a military defense force stagnated as they focused on other concerns relevant to setting up the new government. President [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) went to Congress to remind them of their duty to establish a military twice during this time. Finally, on the last day of the session, 29 September 1789, Congress created the [War Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_War_Department).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The War Department handled naval affairs until Congress created the [Navy Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy) in 1798. The secretaries of each department reported directly to the president as cabinet-level advisors until 1949, when all military departments became subordinate to the Secretary of Defense.

### National Military Establishment



Seal of the Department of Defense with its statutory name



[President Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Truman) signs the National Security Act Amendment of 1949

After the end of [World War II](32927.md), President [Harry Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Truman) proposed the creation of a unified department of national defense. In a special message to the [Congress](31756.md) on 19 December 1945, the president cited wasteful military spending and interdepartmental conflicts. Deliberations in Congress went on for months focusing heavily on the role of the military in society and the threat of granting too much military power to the executive.[^cite_note-14]

On 26 July 1947, Truman signed the [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947), which "unified the Army and Navy within a single organization, the National Military Establishment, which was later renamed the Department of Defense."[^cite_note-15] It established the National Military Establishment (NME) and created the [National Security Council](44026.md), [National Security Resources Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Resources_Board), [United States Air Force](32090.md), and the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff).

The NME was placed under the control of the new post of [secretary of defense](43998.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPolmar200517-16][^cite_note-nibley-17][^cite_note-18]

The National Military Establishment formally began operations on 18 September, the day after the [Senate](24909346.md) confirmed [James V. Forrestal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_V._Forrestal) as the first secretary of defense.[^cite_note-nibley-17] The National Military Establishment was renamed the "Department of Defense" on 10 August 1949, and absorbed the three cabinet-level military departments, in an amendment to the original 1947 law.[^cite_note-\:0-19]

Under the [Department of Defense Reorganization Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense_Reorganization_Act) of 1958 ([Pub. L.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Congress#Public_law,_private_law,_designation) [85–599](https://uslaw.link/citation/us-law/public/85/599)), channels of authority within the department were streamlined while still maintaining the ordinary jurisdiction of the Military Departments to organize, train, and equip their associated forces. The Act clarified the overall decision-making authority of the secretary of defense concerning these subordinate military departments. It more clearly defined the operational [chain of command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_hierarchy) over U.S. military forces (created by the military departments) as running from the president to the secretary of defense, the service chief of the unified combatant commanders, and then to the unified combatant commanders.[^cite_note-20] Also provided in this legislation was a centralized research authority, the [Advanced Research Projects Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Research_Projects_Agency), eventually known as *DARPA*. The act was written and promoted by the Eisenhower administration and was signed into law on 6 August 1958.

### Name changes

By the [National Security Act of 1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Act_of_1947), the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy) and the [Department of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_War)[^cite_note-21] had a new single Secretary imposed over the top of their two previously independent Cabinet secretaries. The War Department also changed its name to the [Department of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Army) and split off the [Department of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Air_Force). The new Cabinet-level department was initially designated the National Military Establishment (NME). On 10 August 1949, the National Security Act of 1947 was amended; the amendment renamed the NME the Department of Defense. The renaming is alleged to be due to the NME's pronunciation as "enemy".[^cite_note-archives_10455766-22]

On 5 September 2025, Trump signed [an executive order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Order_14347) authorizing "Department of War" and "secretary of war" as secondary titles to the main titles of "Department of Defense" and "secretary of defense". The terms must be accommodated by [federal agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_federal_agencies) and are permitted in executive branch communications, ceremonial settings, and non-statutory documents.[^cite_note-defenseone_412676-23] However, only an [act of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Congress) can legally and formally change the department's name and secretary's title, so "Department of Defense" and "secretary of defense" remain legally official.[^cite_note-cbsnews_20250904-24][^cite_note-nbcnews_229217-25] Trump described his rebranding as an effort to project a stronger and more bellicose name and said the "defense" names were "[woke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke)".[^cite_note-nbcnews_229461-26] In June 2026, the House Armed Services Committee and Senate Armed Services Committee voted the proposed name change to "Department of War" out of committee as part of the 2027 National Defense Authorization Act.[^cite_note-militarytimes_20260605-27][^cite_note-militarytimes_20260611-28] On 22 July, the House voted to adopt the name change as part of its version of the bill.[^cite_note-apnews_647083-29] The Senate had not set a date for a vote on the bill before adjourning for a recess until mid-September.[^cite_note-thehill_6018324-30]

The proposed rebrand ignited a debate about its cost nationally and internationally, both financially for changing systems and agreements and psychologically by the message it sent.[^cite_note-cost-31] The [Congressional Budget Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Budget_Office) estimated that the change of name could cost between \$10 million and \$125 million, depending on the depth of implementation.[^cite_note-cbo_20260114-32]

## Organizational structure

Main article: [Organizational structure of the United States Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizational_structure_of_the_United_States_Department_of_Defense)

See also: [List of Department of Defense agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Department_of_Defense_agencies)



Organization of the Department of Defense following the creation of the United States Space Force



A December 2013 Department of Defense organizational chart

The [secretary of defense](43998.md), appointed by the president with the advice and consent of the [Senate](24909346.md), is by federal law ([10 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 113](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/10/113)) the head of the Department of Defense, "the principal assistant to the President in all matters relating to Department of Defense", and has "authority, direction, and control over the Department of Defense". Because the [Constitution](31644.md) vests all military authority in [Congress](31756.md) and the president, the statutory authority of the secretary of defense is derived from their constitutional authority. Since it is impractical for either Congress or the president to participate in every piece of Department of Defense affairs, the secretary of defense and the secretary's subordinate officials generally exercise military authority.

The Department of Defense is composed of the [Office of the Secretary of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Secretary_of_Defense), [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) and [Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Staff), [Office of the Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense_Office_of_Inspector_General), [Combatant Commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Combatant_Command), Military Departments ([Department of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Army), [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy) and [Department of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Air_Force)), [Defense Agencies and Department of Defense Field Activities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Department_of_Defense_agencies), [National Guard Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_Bureau), and such other offices, agencies, activities, organizations, and commands established or designated by law, or by the president or by the secretary of defense. Department of Defense Directive 5100.01 describes the organizational relationships within the department and is the foundational issuance for delineating the major functions of the department. The latest version, signed by then–secretary of defense [Robert Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Gates) in December 2010, is the first major re-write since 1987.[^cite_note-defense_205100-33][^cite_note-34]

### Office of the Secretary of Defense

Main article: [Office of the Secretary of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Secretary_of_Defense)



A 2008 Office of the Secretary of Defense organizational chart

The [Office of the Secretary of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Secretary_of_Defense) (OSD) is the secretary and their deputies, including predominantly civilian staff. OSD is the principal staff element of the Secretary of Defense in the exercise of policy development, planning, resource management, fiscal and program evaluation and oversight, and interface and exchange with other [U.S. federal government](195149.md) departments and agencies, foreign governments, and international organizations, through formal and informal processes. OSD also performs oversight and management of the Defense Agencies, Department of Defense Field Activities, and specialized [Cross Functional Teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_Defense-Empowered_Cross-Functional_Teams).

#### National intelligence agencies

Several defense agencies are members of the [United States Intelligence Community](501244.md). These are national-level intelligence services that operate under the Department of Defense jurisdiction but simultaneously fall under the authorities of the [Office of the Director of National Intelligence](456082.md). They fulfill the requirements of national policymakers and war planners, serve as [Combat Support Agencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_support_agency), and also assist and deploy alongside non-Department of Defense intelligence or law enforcement services such as the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) and the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md). The military services each have their intelligence elements that are distinct from but subject to coordination by national intelligence agencies under the Department of Defense. Department of Defense manages the nation's coordinating authorities and assets in disciplines of [signals intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signals_intelligence), [geospatial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geospatial_intelligence), and [measurement and signature intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measurement_and_signature_intelligence), and also builds, launches, and operates the Intelligence Community's satellite assets. Department of Defense also has its own [human intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_intelligence_\(intelligence_collection\)) [service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Clandestine_Service), which contributes to the CIA's human intelligence efforts while also focusing on military human intelligence priorities. These agencies are directly overseen by the [Under Secretary of Defense for Intelligence and Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Defense_for_Intelligence_and_Security).

### Joint Chiefs of Staff

Main article: [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff)



[Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) and Joint Staff organizational chart

The [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) is a body of senior uniformed leaders in the Department of Defense who advise the [secretary of defense](43998.md), the [Homeland Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Homeland_Security_Council), the [National Security Council](44026.md) and the president on military matters. The composition of the Joint Chiefs of Staff is defined by statute and consists of the [chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](364458.md), [Vice Chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Chairman_of_the_Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff), senior enlisted advisor to the chairman, the Military Service chiefs from the [Army](485507.md), [Marine Corps](7708.md), [Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Operations), [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_United_States_Air_Force), and [Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force), in addition to the chief of [National Guard Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_Bureau), all appointed by the president following [U.S. Senate](24909346.md) confirmation.[^cite_note-35] Each of the individual military service chiefs, outside their Joint Chiefs of Staff obligations, works directly for the secretary of the military department concerned: the [secretary of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Army), [secretary of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Navy), and [secretary of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_the_Air_Force).[^cite_note-archive_201303122222_10C305-36][^cite_note-archive_201303122222_10C505-37][^cite_note-archive_201303122222_10C506-38][^cite_note-archive_201303122222_10C805-39]

Following the [Goldwater–Nichols Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldwater%E2%80%93Nichols_Act) in 1986, the Joint Chiefs of Staff no longer maintained operational command authority individually or collectively. The act designated the chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff (CJCS) as the "principal military adviser to the president, the National Security Council, the Homeland Security Council, and the Secretary of Defense".[^cite_note-40] The remaining Joint Chiefs of Staff may only have their advice relayed to the president, National Security Council, the Homeland Security Council, or the secretary of defense after submitting it to the CJCS. By law, the chairman has to present that advice whenever he is presenting his own.[^cite_note-41] The chain of command goes from the president to the secretary of defense to the [commanders of the Combatant Commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Combatant_Command).[^cite_note-archive_201305290140-42] Goldwater–Nichols also created the office of the vice chairman, and the chairman is now designated as the *principal military adviser* to the secretary of defense, the Homeland Security Council, the National Security Council and to the president.[^cite_note-archive_201303122222_10C5-151b-43]

The Joint Staff is a headquarters staff at the [Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) made up of personnel from all five services that assist the chairman and vice chairman in discharging their duties. It is managed by the [director of the Joint Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Director_of_the_Joint_Staff) who is a [lieutenant general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_general_\(United_States\)) or [vice admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_admiral_\(United_States\)).[^cite_note-archive_201303122222_10C5-155-44]

### Military departments and services

There are three military departments within the Department of Defense:

1. the [Department of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Army), within which the [United States Army](32087.md) is organized.

2. the [Department of the Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Navy), within which the [United States Navy](20518076.md) and the [United States Marine Corps](17349325.md) are organized.

3. the [Department of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_the_Air_Force), within which the [United States Air Force](32090.md) and [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force) are organized.

The military departments are each headed by their secretary (i.e., [Secretary of the Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Army), [Secretary of the Navy](44001.md) and [Secretary of the Air Force](305210.md)), appointed by the president, with the advice and consent of the [Senate](24909346.md). They have the legal authority under [Title 10 of the United States Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_10_of_the_United_States_Code) to conduct all the affairs of their respective departments within which the military services are organized.[^cite_note-45] The secretaries of the military departments are (by law) subordinate to the [secretary of defense](43998.md) and (by SecDef delegation) to the [deputy secretary of defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Deputy_Secretary_of_Defense).

Secretaries of military departments, in turn, normally exercise authority over their forces by delegation through their respective service chiefs (i.e., [Chief of Staff of the Army](485507.md), [Commandant of the Marine Corps](7708.md), [Chief of Naval Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Naval_Operations), [Chief of Staff of the Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_United_States_Air_Force), and [Chief of Space Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Space_Operations)) over forces not assigned to a [Combatant Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Combatant_Command).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPolmar200520-46]

Military departments are tasked solely with "the training, provision of equipment, and administration of troops".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPolmar200520-46] The [Defense Reorganization Act of 1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Reorganization_Act_of_1958) removed the power of command over troops from secretaries of military departments and service chiefs.

### Unified Combatant Commands

Main article: [Unified combatant command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_combatant_command)

A [unified combatant command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_combatant_command) is a military command composed of personnel/equipment from at least two Military Departments, which has a broad, continuing mission.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhitley200944-47] They are responsible for the operational command of forces. Almost all operational U.S. forces are under the authority of a Unified Command.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPolmar200520-46] The DoD [Unified Command Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Command_Plan) lays out combatant commands' missions, geographical/functional responsibilities, and force structure.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhitley200944-47]

During military operations, the chain of command runs from the president to the [secretary of defense](43998.md) to the [combatant commanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combatant_commander) of the Combatant Commands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPolmar200520-46]

As of 2019, the United States has eleven Combatant Commands, organized either on a geographical basis (known as "[area of responsibility](9452512.md)", AOR) or on a global, functional basis:[^cite_note-48]

- [U.S. Northern Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Northern_Command) (USNORTHCOM)

- [U.S. Southern Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Southern_Command) (USSOUTHCOM)

- [U.S. Central Command](338482.md) (USCENTCOM)

- [U.S. European Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._European_Command) (USEUCOM)

- [U.S. Pacific Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Indo-Pacific_Command) (USPACOM)

- [U.S. Africa Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Africa_Command) (USAFRICOM)

- [U.S. Strategic Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Strategic_Command) (USSTRATCOM)

- [U.S. Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Special_Operations_Command) (USSOCOM)

- [U.S. Transportation Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Transportation_Command) (USTRANSCOM)

- [U.S. Cyber Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Cyber_Command) (USCYBERCOM)

- [U.S. Space Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Space_Command) (USSPACECOM)

## Budget

Main article: [Military budget of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_budget_of_the_United_States)



Defense spending as a percent of gross domestic product from 1792 to 2017



Total United States Defense outlays from 1962 to 2024 in \$millions (2019 to 2024 are estimated)

Department of Defense spending in 2017 was 3.15% of GDP and accounted for about 38% of the budgeted [global military spending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_military_expenditures) – more than the next seven largest militaries combined.[^cite_note-49] By 2019, the 27th secretary of defense had begun a line-by-line review of the defense budget; in 2020 the secretary identified items amounting to \$5.7 billion, out of a \$106 billion subtotal (the so-called "fourth estate" agencies such as missile defense, and defense intelligence, amounting to 16% of the defense budget),[^cite_note-mcleary2020-50][^cite_note-eaglen-51] He will re-deploy to the modernization of hypersonics, artificial intelligence, and missile defense.[^cite_note-mcleary2020-50] Beyond 2021 the 27th secretary of defense is projecting the need for yearly budget increases of 3 to 5 percent to modernize.[^cite_note-Chogath_Kog'sante-52]

The Department of Defense accounts for the majority of federal discretionary spending. In FY2017 ([U.S. fiscal year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._fiscal_year) 2017), the Department of Defense budgeted spending accounted for 15% of the U.S. federal budget, and 49% of federal [discretionary spending](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discretionary_spending), which represents funds not accounted for by pre-existing obligations. However, this does not include many military-related expenses that fall outside the Department of Defense budget, such as [nuclear weapons](21785.md) research, production, maintenance, and cleanup, which are covered in the [Department of Energy](62866.md) budget; Veterans Affairs expenses; payments from the Treasury Department for military retirees, widows, and their families; interest on debts incurred from past wars; or State Department financing for foreign arms sales and military-related development assistance. Additionally, it does not account for defense spending outside of military operations, including expenditures by the [Department of Homeland Security](58236.md), counter-terrorism funding by the FBI, and intelligence-gathering spending by the NSA.

In the [2010 United States federal budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_federal_budget), the Department of Defense was allocated a base budget of \$533.7 billion, with a further \$75.5 billion adjustment in respect of 2009, and \$130 billion for overseas contingencies.[^cite_note-53] The subsequent 2010 Department of Defense Financial Report shows the total budgetary resources for FY2010 were \$1.2 trillion.[^cite_note-DoD_FR_25-54] Of these resources, \$1.1 trillion were obligated and \$994 billion were disbursed, with the remaining resources relating to multi-year modernization projects requiring additional time to procure.[^cite_note-DoD_FR_25-54] After over a decade of [non-compliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_military_expenditure#GAO_audits), as part of the [National Defense Authorization Act for Fiscal Year 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act_for_Fiscal_Year_2010), [Congress](31756.md) established a deadline of FY2017 for the Department of Defense to achieve [audit readiness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audit#Accounting),[^cite_note-55] although this did not end up occurring.

In 2015 the allocation for the Department of Defense was \$585 billion,[^cite_note-56] the [highest level of budgetary resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_budget_of_the_United_States) among all federal agencies, and this amounts to more than one-half of the annual federal expenditures in the United States federal budget [discretionary budget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discretionary_spending).[^cite_note-57]

On 28 September 2018, President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) signed the Department of Defense and Labor, Health and Human Services, and Education Appropriations Act, 2019, and Continuing Appropriations Act, 2019 (H.R.6157) into law.[^cite_note-congress_20180928-58] On 30 September 2018, the FY2018 Budget expired and the FY2019 budget came into effect.

### FY2019

The FY2019 Budget for the Department of Defense is approximately \$686,074,048,000[^cite_note-59] (including Base + Overseas Contingency Operations + Emergency Funds) in discretionary spending and \$8,992,000,000 in mandatory spending totaling \$695,066,000,000.

[Undersecretary of Defense (Comptroller)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_Secretary_of_Defense_\(Comptroller\)) [David L. Norquist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Norquist) said in a hearing regarding the FY 2019 budget: "The overall number you often hear is \$716 billion. That is the amount of funding for national defense, the accounting code is 050 and includes more than simply the Department of Defense. It includes, for example, the Department of Energy and others. That large a number, if you back out the \$30 billion for non-defense agencies, you get to \$686 billion. That is the funding for the Department of Defense, split between \$617 billion in base and \$69 billion in [overseas contingency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Contingency_Operation)".[^cite_note-fy2019GreenBook-60]

The Department of Defense budget encompasses the majority of the National Defense Budget of approximately \$716.0 billion in discretionary spending and \$10.8 billion in mandatory spending for a \$726.8 billion total. Of the total, \$708.1 billion falls under the jurisdiction of the [House Committee on Armed Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_Committee_on_Armed_Services) and [Senate Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Armed_Services) and is subject to authorization by the annual [National Defense Authorization Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defense_Authorization_Act) (NDAA). The remaining \$7.9 billion falls under the jurisdiction of other congressional committees.[^cite_note-61]

The Department of Defense is unique because it is one of the few federal entities where the majority of its funding falls into the discretionary category. The majority of the entire federal budget is mandatory, and much of the discretionary funding in the budget consists of DoD dollars.

### FY2024

As of 10 March 2023 the FY2024 presidential budget request was \$842 billion.[^cite_note-FY2024Agreement-65] In January 2023, Treasury Secretary [Janet Yellen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Yellen) announced that the U.S. government would hit its \$31.4 trillion [debt ceiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt_ceiling) on 19 January 2023;[^cite_note-31teraUSD-66] the date on which the U.S. government would no longer be able to use [extraordinary measures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_debt_ceiling#Extraordinary_measures) such as issuance of [Treasury securities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_securities) is estimated to be in June 2023.[^cite_note-yellen-67] On 3 June 2023, the debt ceiling was suspended until 2025.[^cite_note-dorn-68] The \$886 billion [National Defense Authorization Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NDAA) is facing reconciliation of the [House](19468510.md) and [Senate](24909346.md) bills after passing both houses 27 July 2023; the conferees have to be chosen, next.[^cite_note-facingReconcil2023-69][^cite_note-ndaa2023-70][^cite_note-harris2023-71] As of September 2023, a [continuing resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuing_resolution) was needed to prevent a [government shutdown](2150997.md).[^cite_note-2023ShutdownThreat-72][^cite_note-demRepCoalitionLikely-73] A shutdown was avoided on 30 September for 45 days (until 17 November 2023),[^cite_note-houseCr23-74][^cite_note-senatCr23-75][^cite_note-Military_Kheel-76][^cite_note-defenseAuthExpected-77] with passage of the NDAA on 14 December 2023.[^cite_note-zengerle23-78] The Senate will next undertake negotiations on supplemental spending for 2024.[^cite_note-supplementalUpNext-79][^cite_note-8Jan24status-80] A government shutdown was averted on 23 March 2024 with the signing of a \$1.2 trillion bill to cover FY2024.[^cite_note-trillionBudgetIn2024-81][^cite_note-stopgapUntil8Mar24-82]

### Criticism of finances

See also: [Government Accountability Office investigations of the Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Office_investigations_of_the_Department_of_Defense)

A 2013 *Reuters* investigation concluded that [Defense Finance and Accounting Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_Finance_and_Accounting_Service), the primary financial management arm of the Department of Defense, implements monthly "unsubstantiated change actions"—illegal, inaccurate "plugs"—that forcibly make DoD's books match Treasury's books.[^cite_note-reuters_20131118-83] [Reuters](18998750.md) reported that [the Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) was the only federal agency that had not released annual audits as required by a 1992 law. According to Reuters, the Pentagon "annually reports to Congress that its books are in such disarray that an audit is impossible".[^cite_note-reuters_20130702-84][^cite_note-reuters_20131118-83]

In 2015, a Pentagon consulting firm performed an audit on the department's budget. It found that there was \$125 billion in wasteful spending that could be saved over the next five years without layoffs or reduction in military personnel. In 2016, *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* uncovered that rather than taking the advice of the auditing firm, senior defense officials suppressed and hid the report from the public to avoid political scrutiny.[^cite_note-washingtonpost_20161205-85] In June 2016, the [Office of the Inspector General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Inspector_General,_U.S._Department_of_Defense) released a report stating that the Army made \$6.5 trillion in wrongful adjustments to its accounting entries in 2015.[^cite_note-reuters_idUSKCN10U1IG-86] The Department of Defense failed its fifth audit in 2022, and could not account for more than 60% of its \$3.5 trillion in assets.[^cite_note-thehill_3740921-87]

In the latest [Center for Effective Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Effective_Government) analysis of 15 federal agencies which receive the most [Freedom of Information Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_Act_\(United_States\)) requests, published in 2015 (using 2012 and 2013 data, the most recent years available), the DoD earned 61 out of a possible 100 points, a D− grade. While it had improved from a failing grade in 2013, it still had low scores in processing requests (55%) and disclosure rules (42%).[^cite_note-archive_201908110442-88]
