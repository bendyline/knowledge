**Kermanshah** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): کرمانشاه, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *Kermânšâh*, pronounced [\[kʰʲeɾmɒːnˈʃɒːʰ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Persian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b9/Fa-Kermanshah.ogg/Fa-Kermanshah.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b9/Fa-Kermanshah.ogg/Fa-Kermanshah.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Southern Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Kurdish_language): کرماشان, romanized: *Kirmaşan*.[^cite_note-4]) is a city in the [Central District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_District_\(Kermanshah_County\)) of [Kermanshah](448078.md) province, Iran, serving as capital of the province, the county, and the district.[^cite_note-Kermanshah_Province_Structure-5] The city is 525 kilometres (326 miles) from [Tehran](57654.md) in the western part of the country. The 2016 [National Census](6889.md) measured the population of the city as 946,651[^cite_note-2016_Kermanshah_Province-2] (2025 estimate 1,117,000).[^cite_note-\:0-6]

## Etymology

"Kermanshah" derives from the [Sasanian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) title *Kirmanshah*, which translates as "King of Kerman".[^cite_note-Kia-7] This title was held by the son of [Shapur III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapur_III), Prince Bahram, who was bestowed with the title upon being appointed governor of the province of [Kirman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirman_\(Sasanian_province\)) (present-day [Kerman Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman_Province)).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-Kia-7] Later, in 390, when he had already succeeded his father as [ Bahram IV Kirmanshah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahram_IV) (r. 388–399), he founded the city and his title was applied to it, i.e. "(City of the) King of Kirman".[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

### Prehistory

Kermanshah is considered one of the [cradles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradle_of_civilization) of [prehistoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory) cultures. According to archaeological surveys and excavation, the Kermanshah area has been occupied by prehistoric people since the [Lower Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Paleolithic) period, and continued to later [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) periods until late [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) period. The Lower Paleolithic evidence consists of some [hand axes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_axe) found in the Gakia area to the east of the city. The [Middle Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Paleolithic) remains have been found in various parts of the province, especially in the northern vicinity of the city in Tang-e Kenesht, [Tang-e Malaverd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaverd) and near [Taq-e Bostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taq-e_Bostan).

[Neanderthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal) Man existed in the Kermanshah region during this period and the only discovered skeletal remains of this early human in Iran was found in three caves and [rock shelter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_shelter) situated in Kermanshah province.[^cite_note-12] The known Paleolithic caves in this area are [Warwasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warwasi), [Qobeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qosbeh-ye_Maniat), [Malaverd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaverd) and [Do-Ashkaft Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Do-Ashkaft_Cave). The region was also one of the first places in which human settlements including [Asiab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiab,_Omidiyeh), [Qazanchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazanchi), Sarab, [Chia Jani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chia_Jani), and [Ganj-Darreh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganj_Dareh) were established between 8,000 and 10,000 years ago.

This is about the same time that the first [potteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery) pertaining to Iran were made in [Ganj-Darreh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganj_Dareh), near present-day [Harsin](9590172.md).

In May 2009, based on a research conducted by the university of [Hamadan](43610555.md) and [UCL](52029.md), the head of Archeology Research Center of [Iran's Cultural Heritage and Tourism Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Cultural_Heritage,_Handicrafts_and_Tourism) announced that one of the oldest [prehistoric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric) villages in the [Middle East](19323.md) dating back to 9800 B.P. was discovered in [Sahneh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahneh_County), located west of Kermanshah.[^cite_note-ISNA-13][^cite_note-خبرگزاري_دانشجويان_ايران-14]

Remains of later village occupations and early [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) are found in a number of mound sites in the city itself.

The city contains 4 archaeological mound sites: Chogha Kaboud, [Chogha Golan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chogha_Golan), Morad Hasel, and [Tappa Gawri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tappa_Gawri).

### Sassanid Kermanshah

In ancient [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_folklore) [mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myth), construction of the city is attributed to [Tahmuras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahmuras), the third king of [Pishdadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pishdadian) dynasty. It is believed that the [Sasanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) have constructed Kermanshah and [Bahram IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahram_IV) (he was called Kermanshah, meaning king of [Kerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerman)) gave his name to this city.[^cite_note-15]

Kermanshah became the capital city of the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire), as well as being a significant [health center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_health_center) for Sasanian kings, serving as their summer resort. In AD 226, following a two-year war led by the Persian Emperor, [Ardashir I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir_I), against "[Kurdish](3979351.md)" tribes in the region. At the time, the term "[Kurd](17068.md)" was used as a social term, designating Iranian [nomads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nomad), rather than a concrete ethnic group.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] The word became an [ethnic identity](17068.md) in the 12th and 13th century.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-MartinIdentity-19] Within the dynasty known as the [House of Kayus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Kayus) (also *Kâvusakân*) remained a semi-independent kingdom lasting until AD 380 before [Ardashir II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir_II) removed the dynasty's last ruling member.

### Islamic era

Kermanshah was conquered by the [Arabs](2185.md) in 629 AD. Under [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuks) rule in the eleventh century, it became a cultural and commercial center in western [Iran](14653.md) and the southern [Kurdish](17068.md)-inhabited areas as a whole. It was sacked by the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) under [Hulegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hulegu_Khan) in 1257, shortly before the [sack of Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Baghdad_\(1258\)). The [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavids) fortified the town, and the [Qajars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajars) repulsed an attack by the [Ottomans](22278.md) during [Fath Ali Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fath_Ali_Shah)'s rule (1797–1834).

Kermanshah was occupied by [Ottomans](22278.md) between 1723–1729 and 1731–1732.

### Modern history



Queen [Farah Pahlavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farah_Pahlavi) on a visit to Kermanshah, 1963

Occupied by the [Imperial Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army) in 1914, followed by the [Ottoman Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Army_\(1861%E2%80%931922\)) in 1915 during [World War I](4764461.md), it was evacuated in 1917 when the [British forces](31730.md) arrived there to expel the Ottomans. Kermanshah played an important role in the [Iranian Constitutional Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Constitutional_Revolution) during the late [Qajar era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran) and the Republic Movement in the [Pahlavi era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahlavi_Iran). The city was harshly damaged during the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) in 1980 to 1988, and although it was rebuilt, it has not yet fully recovered.

On 3 January 2026, during the [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md) and [2026 Iran massacres](82036668.md), [Reza Ghanbari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Reza_Ghanbari), a 17-year-old Kurdish minor, was killed during protests in the Jafarabad neighborhood of Kermanshah. Security authorities reportedly pressured the family to describe Ghanbari as affiliated with the [Basij](2892082.md) and to attribute his death to protesters, which the family declined.[^cite_note-\:03-20][^cite_note-\:22-21] On 8 January, five protesters were killed by gunfire by government security forces and ten members of the [Kermanshah Nabi Akram Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermanshah_Nabi_Akram_Corps) were killed in Kermanshah.[^cite_note-Hengaw_5Kurdish_Kermanshah-22]

## Demographics

### Population

At the time of the 2006 census, the city's population was 784,602 in 202,588 households.[^cite_note-2006_Kermanshah_Province-23] The following census in 2011 counted 851,405 people in 242,311 households.[^cite_note-2011_Kermanshah_Province-24] The last census measured the population of the city as 946,651 in 2016[^cite_note-2016_Kermanshah_Province-2] and the 2025 estimate is around 1,117,000.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

### Language

The languages in Kermanshah are [Southern Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Kurdish)[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-\:1-29][^cite_note-30] The [Kurdish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language) spoken in the city is called Kermashani, which is a variety of Southern Kurdish.[^cite_note-31] People in the city speak [Kermanshahi Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermanshahi_Persian), a local dialect which differs from standard Persian.[^cite_note-32] The city has the largest Kurdish population in Iran.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] The predominance of Kurdish in the city has been the subject of several sociolinguistic studies documenting ongoing language maintenance and strong intergenerational transmission of the Kermashani dialect alongside bilingualism in Persian.[^cite_note-36] Recent studies show the Persian dominance on Kurdish in all different social contexts, including family, friendship, neighborhood, business, education, and office contexts in the city.[^cite_note-\:1-29] The *[Encyclopaedia Iranica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclopaedia_Iranica)* characterises Kermashani as the primary vernacular of the city and situates it within the broader continuum of Southern Kurdish dialects spoken across the Zagros region.[^cite_note-37]

### Religion

Most of the inhabitants of Kermanshah are [Shia](26961.md) Muslims, but there are also [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) Muslims, [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_in_Iran),[^cite_note-assistnews1-38] and followers of [Yarsanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarsanism).[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

## Climate



A view of Kermanshah in mid.-19th century- toward south, Farokhshad Mt. and Wasi Mt. are visible at background

Kermanshah has a [montane climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montane_climate);[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-zaban-42][^cite_note-b-43][^cite_note-a-44] heavily influenced by the proximity of the [Zagros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros) mountains, it is classified as a hot-summer [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Csa*) or a hot-summer [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) ([Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Dohk*). The city's altitude and exposed location relative to westerly winds makes precipitation a little bit high (more than twice that of [Tehran](57654.md)), but at the same time produces huge [diurnal temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diurnal_air_temperature_variation) swings especially in the virtually rainless summers, which remain extremely hot during the day. Kermanshah experiences rather cold winters and there are usually rainfalls in fall and spring. Snow cover is seen for at least a couple of weeks in winter.

### Taq-e Bostan



&#x20;[Anahita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anahita) on the left as the patron *[yazata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazata)* of the [Sasanian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_dynasty) behind Emperor [Khosrow II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khosrow_II) with [Ahura Mazda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahura_Mazda) presenting the [diadem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diadem) of sovereignty on the right. [Taq-e Bostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taq-e_Bostan).

Main article: [Taq-e Bostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taq-e_Bostan)

[Taq-e Bostan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taq-e_Bostan) is a series of large [rock reliefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_relief) dating to the [Sasanian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) (224–651 AD). This example of [Sasanian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_art) is located 5 km (3 mi) from the city center of Kermanshah in western [Iran](14653.md).[^cite_note-49] It is located in the heart of the [Zagros mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros_Mountains), where it has endured almost 1,700 years of wind and rain.

The carvings, examples of Persian sculpture under the Sasanians, include representations of the investitures of [Ardashir II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardashir_II) (379–383) and [Shapur III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shapur_III) (383–388). Like other Sasanian symbols, Taq-e Bostan and its relief patterns accentuate power, religious tendencies, glory, honor, the vastness of the court, game and fighting spirit, festivity, joy, and rejoicing.

Sasanian kings chose a beautiful setting for their rock reliefs along an historic [Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Road) caravan route [waypoint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waypoint) and campground. The reliefs are adjacent a sacred spring that empties into a reflecting pool at the base of a mountain cliff.

Taq-e Bostan and its rock relief are one of the 30 surviving Sasanian relics of the [Zagros Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros_Mountains). According to [Arthur Pope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Pope), the founder of Iranian art and archeology Institute in the US, "art was characteristic of the Iranian people and the gift which they endowed the world with."

One of the most impressive reliefs inside the largest grotto or *ivan* is the [equestrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrian_sculpture) figure of the Sasanian king [Khosrow II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khosrow_II) (591–628 AD) mounted on his charger, [Shabdiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabdiz). Both horse and rider are arrayed in full battle armor. The arch rests on two columns that bear delicately carved patterns showing the tree of life or the sacred tree. Above the arch and located on two opposite sides are figures of two winged angels with [diadems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diadem_\(personal_wear\)). A noticeable border with flower patterns has been intricately carved around the outer layer of the arch. These same patterns can be seen on the official costumes of Sasanian kings. Equestrian relief panel measured on 16.08.07 approx. 7.45 m across by 4.25 m high.

### Behistun

Main article: [Behistun Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun_Inscription)

The Behistun inscription is considered as a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md). The [Behistun Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun_Inscription) (also *Bisitun* or *Bisutun*, [Modern Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Persian): بیستون; [Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian): *Bagastana*, meaning "the god's place or land") is a multi-lingual inscription located on [Mount Behistun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Behistun).

The inscription includes three versions of the same text, written in three different [cuneiform script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform_script) languages: [Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian_language), [Elamite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elamite_language), and [Babylonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonian_language). A British army officer, [Henry Rawlinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Henry_Rawlinson,_1st_Baronet), had the inscription transcribed in two parts, in 1835 and 1843. Rawlinson was able to translate the Old Persian cuneiform text in 1838, and the Elamite and Babylonian texts were translated by Rawlinson and others after 1843. Babylonian was a later form of [Akkadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akkadian_language): both are [Semitic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semitic_languages). In effect, then, the inscription is to [cuneiform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuneiform_script) what the [Rosetta Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosetta_Stone) is to [Egyptian hieroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_hieroglyph): the document most crucial in the [decipherment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decipherment) of a previously lost [script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writing_system).

The inscription is approximately 15 metres high by 25 meters wide, and 100 meters up a [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) cliff from an ancient road connecting the capitals of [Babylonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylonia) and [Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes) ([Babylon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babylon) and [Ecbatana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecbatana)). It is extremely inaccessible as the mountainside was removed to make the inscription more visible after its completion. The Old Persian text contains 414 lines in five columns; the Elamite text includes 593 lines in eight columns and the Babylonian text is in 112 lines. The inscription was illustrated by a life-sized bas-relief of [Darius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great_of_Persia), holding a [bow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bow_\(weapon\)) as a sign of kingship, with his left foot on the chest of a figure lying on his back before him. The prostrate figure is reputed to be the [pretender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretender) [Gaumata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaumata). Darius is attended to the left by two servants, and ten one-metre figures stand to the right, with hands tied and rope around their necks, representing conquered peoples. [Faravahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faravahar) floats above, giving his blessing to the king. One figure appears to have been added after the others were completed, as was (oddly enough) Darius' beard, which is a separate block of stone attached with iron pins and [lead](17747.md).

### Qajar era monuments

See also: [Tekyeh Biglarbeygi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tekyeh_Biglarbeygi) and [Tekyeh Moaven al-molk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tekyeh_Moaven_al-molk)



Jame-Shafeie Mosque

[^cite_note-50]

During the [Qajar era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_Iran) (1789–1925), [Kermanshah Bazaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermanshah_Bazaar?action=edit\&redlink=1), mosques and [tekyehs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tekyeh) such as [Takyeh Beyglarbeygi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takyeh_Beyglarbeygi) and [Takyeh Moaven-ol-Molk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takyeh_Moaven-ol-Molk), and houses such as [Khajeh Barookh's House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khajeh_Barookh's_House?action=edit\&redlink=1) were built. Takyeh Beyglarbeygi was built during the Qajar era by the efforts of Abdollah khan Beyglarbeygi and is known for its [ayeneh-kari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayeneh-kari). Tekyeh Moaven-ol-Molk has pictures on the walls that relate to [shahnameh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahnameh), despite some of its more religious ones.

Khajeh Barookh's House is located in the old district of Faizabad, a [Jewish](25955086.md) neighborhood of the city. It was built by a Jewish merchant of the Qajar era, named Barookh/Baruch. The house, a historical depiction of Iranian architecture, was renamed "Randeh-Kesh House", after the last owner, is a "daroongara" (inward oriented) house and is connected through a vestibule to the exterior yard and through a corridor to the interior yard.[^cite_note-51] Surrounding the interior yard are rooms, brick pillars making the [iwans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwan) (porches) of the house, and step-like column capitals decorated with brick-stalactite work. This house is among the rare Qajar houses with a private bathroom.

### Bazaars

Kermanshah is home to at least one [bazaar](556904.md) dating back to the Qajar era. **Kermanshah Grand Bazaar** or ***Tarike Bazaar*** was built around 1820 when [Prince Mohammad Ali Mirza Dowlatshah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad-Ali_Mirza_Dowlatshah) of the [Qajar dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) governed Kermanshah and was the largest covered bazaar of the [Middle East](19323.md) at the time.[^cite_note-52]

### Higher education

More than 49 thousands students are educating in 9 governmental and private universities in the city. Established in 1968 as the *Kermanshah Graduate School of Nursing*, the [Kermanshah University of Medical Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kermanshah_University_of_Medical_Sciences) was the first university in the west part of Iran. The medical school as a division of *Razi University* was established in 1976 and admitted some students in general practice. [Razi University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Razi_University) established in 1972 was the second university in the west part of Iran and is the most-prominent higher education institute in Kermanshah province and also west part of the country.[^cite_note-53] In 2020, Razi University ranked 24th in Iran and 1300th in the world by the [U.S. News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._News_&_World_Report) in universities of all countries of the world based upon 13 factors.[^cite_note-54]

### Schools
