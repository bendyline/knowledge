**Shandong**[^cite_note-8] is a coastal [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) in [East China](683693.md)[^cite_note-9] which has played a major role in the development of the Chinese civilization and culture as it has served as a pivotal cultural and religious center for [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism), [Chinese Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Buddhism) and [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism). Shandong's [Mount Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tai) is the most revered mountain of Taoism and a site with one of the longest histories of continuous religious worship in the world.[^cite_note-10] The [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) temples in the mountains south of the provincial capital of [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan) were once among the foremost Buddhist sites in China.[^cite_note-11] The city of [Qufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qufu) was the birthplace of [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius), and later became the center of Confucianism.[^cite_note-12]

Shandong's location at the intersection of ancient and modern north–south and east–west trading routes has helped establish it as an economic center. After a period of political instability and economic hardship beginning in the late 19th century, Shandong has experienced rapid growth in recent decades. Home to over 100 million inhabitants, Shandong is the [world's sixth-most](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first-level_administrative_divisions_by_population) populous [subnational entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subnational_entity), and China's [second-most populous province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_population).[^cite_note-censuspop-13] The [economy of Shandong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Shandong) is China's [third-largest provincial economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) with a [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) of CN¥8.3 trillion (US\$1.3 trillion) in 2021, equivalent to the GDP of Mexico.[^cite_note-\:0-14][^cite_note-\:1-15] If considered among sovereign states, Shandong would rank as the [15th-largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) and the [15th-most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population) as of 2021.[^cite_note-\:2-16] Its GDP per capita is slightly above [the national average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP_per_capita).

Shandong is one of China's leading provinces in education and research. It has 153 [higher education institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Shandong), ranking second in East China after [Jiangsu](166412.md) and fourth among all Chinese first-level divisions after Jiangsu, [Guangdong](65109.md) and [Henan](166408.md).[^cite_note-\:03-17] As of 2025, the [Nature Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index) ranked two major cities in Shandong (Jinan #27 and [Qingdao](250171.md) #31) in the global top 35 cities by scientific research output.[^cite_note-\:3-18]

## Etymology

The name *Shandong* (山东; 山東) literally translates to "east of the mountain," from 'mountain' (山) and 'east' (东; 東), the name first came into being in [Jin Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_\(1115%E2%80%931234\)), when [Shandong East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_East_Circuit) and [West Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_West_Circuit) were created. The name refers to the province's location to the east of the [Taihang Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taihang_Mountains).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Its counterpart is [Shanxi](81262.md), which literally means 'west of the mountains' and locates in the western side of [Taihang Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taihang_Mountains).

A common nickname for Shandong is Qilu (齐鲁; 齊魯), from two major states [Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Qi) and [Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Lu) that existed in this region during the [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period). Whereas Qi was a major political power, Lu played only a minor political role but became renowned as the home of [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius), and its cultural influence came to eclipse that of Qi. The cultural legacy of Lu is reflected in the province's official abbreviation of *Lǔ* (鲁; 魯).[^cite_note-Roberts1-21]

The province's old spelling is Shantung, which can be seen in *[Shantungosuchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantungosuchus)*, *[Shantungosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantungosaurus)* and [Shantung fabric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantung_\(fabric\)).

## History

### Ancient history

See also: [Dawenkou culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawenkou_culture), [Longshan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longshan_culture), [Qi (state)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qi_\(state\)), [Lu (state)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu_\(state\)), and [Hundred Schools of Thought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Schools_of_Thought)



*Yachou* bronze axe (亞醜鉞) was forged by a tribe of [Dongyi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongyi_people), which probably called Yachou; its exquisite workmanship and valuable inscripts made it China's first-class national artifact and now preserved in [China National Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Museum)



Remains of [Ancient Linzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Linzi) city sewer passing underneath the former city wall

With its location on the eastern edge of the [North China Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_Plain), Shandong was home to a succession of [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) cultures for millennia, including the [Houli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houli_culture) (c. 6500–5500 BC), [Beixin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beixin_culture) (c. 5300–4100 BC), [Dawenkou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawenkou) (c. 4100–2600 BC), [Longshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longshan_culture) (c. 3000–2000 BC), and [Yueshi cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yueshi_culture) (c. 1900–1500 BC).

The [Shang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty) and [Zhou dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_dynasty) exerted varying degrees of control over western Shandong, while eastern Shandong was inhabited by [Dongyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongyi) peoples, who were considered barbarians by the inhabitants of the [Central Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhongyuan). Following the annexation the Dongyi state of [Lai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lai_\(state\)) by the [state of Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Qi) in 567 BC, the Dongyi gradually became [sinicized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinicized).

During the [Spring and Autumn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period) and [Warring States periods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period), power was accumulated by regional states; Shandong was home to the state of Qi based in [Linzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Linzi), and the [state of Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Lu) based in [Qufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qufu). Lu is famous for being the home of [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius); however, it was comparatively small, eventually being annexed by the neighboring [state of Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Chu) to its south. Meanwhile, Qi was a significant power throughout the entire period, and ruled cities including [Jimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimo,_Shandong), [Linzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linzi_District), and [Ju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ju_\(city\)).

### Imperial history



[Four Gates Pagoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Gates_Pagoda) was the only intact Sui dynasty pagoda in China

The [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty) conquered Qi and founded the first centralized Chinese state in 221 BC. The [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) that followed created several commanderies supervised by two inspectorates (刺史部) in what is now modern Shandong: [Qingzhou Inspectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingzhou_Inspectorate?action=edit\&redlink=1) (青州) in the north and [Yanzhou Inspectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanzhou_Inspectorate?action=edit\&redlink=1) (兗州) in the south. During the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) period, Shandong was part of the northern kingdom of [Cao Wei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cao_Wei), which ruled over northern China.

After the Three Kingdoms period, a brief period of unity under the [Western Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Jin_dynasty) gave way to invasions by nomadic barbarians from the north. Northern China, including Shandong, was overrun. Over the next century or so, Shandong changed hands several times, falling to the [Later Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Zhao), then [Former Yan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Yan), then [Former Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Qin), then [Later Yan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Yan), then [Southern Yan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Yan), then the [Liu Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Song_dynasty), and finally the [Northern Wei dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Wei_dynasty), the first of the Northern dynasties during the [Northern and Southern dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_and_Southern_dynasties) period. Shandong stayed with the Northern dynasties for the rest of this period.

In 412 AD, the Chinese [Buddhist](3267529.md) monk [Faxian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faxian) landed at [Laoshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laoshan_District), on the southern edge of the Shandong peninsula, and proceeded to [Qingzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingzhou) to edit and translate the scriptures he had brought back from [Pakistan](23235.md) and [India](14533.md).

The [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty) reestablished unity in 589, and the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) (618–907) presided over the next golden age of China. For the earlier part of this period, Shandong was ruled as part of [Henan Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henan_Circuit?action=edit\&redlink=1), one of the [circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_\(political_division\)) (a political division). Later on, China splintered into warlord factions, resulting in the [Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties_and_Ten_Kingdoms) period. Shandong was part of the Five Dynasties, all based in the north.

The [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) reunified China in the late tenth century. The classic novel *[Water Margin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Margin)* was based on folk tales of outlaw bands active in Shandong during the Song dynasty. In 1996, the discovery of over two hundred buried Buddhist statues at Qingzhou was hailed as a major [archaeological](18951655.md) find. The statues included early examples of painted figures and are thought to have been buried due to [Emperor Huizong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Huizong_of_Song_China)'s repression of [Buddhism](3267529.md) (he favored [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism)).

The Song dynasty was forced to cede northern China to the [Jurchen Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurchen_Jin_dynasty) in 1142. Shandong was administered by Jin as [Shandong East Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_East_Circuit) and [Shandong West Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_West_Circuit) – the first use of its current name.

### Early modern era



[Linqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linqing) City, with a view of the [Grand Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_\(China\)). Drawing by [William Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Alexander_\(painter\)), draughtsman of the [Macartney Embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macartney_Embassy) to China in 1793.

The modern province of Shandong was created by the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), where it had a more expansive territory, including the eastern of [Liaoning](155516.md) ([Liaodong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaodong_Peninsula)). In 1376, the capital of Shandong moved from [Qingzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingzhou_Prefecture) to [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_Prefecture), and since then, Jinan served as the provincial capital for seven centuries.

After [Emperor Yongle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Yongle) moved the capital to [Beijing](18603746.md) in 1421, the cities of [Jining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jining) and [Linqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linqing) along the [Grand Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_of_China) flourished due to the development of canal grain transport.[^cite_note-22] However, due to the [Little Ice Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Ice_Age) (approximately 1550–1770),[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] crop yields declined and famine persisted year after year,[^cite_note-25] and compounded by the harsh policies of the [imperial court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty),[^cite_note-26] peasant rebellions broke out continuously.[^cite_note-27]

In 1633, Ming generals [Kong Youde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kong_Youde) and [Geng Zhongming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geng_Zhongming) led their troops from [Dengzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Prefecture_\(Shandong\)), Shandong to [Liaodong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaodong) to surrender to the [Manchu Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_Qing), later joining the [Qing army's entry into China proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_conquest_of_China).[^cite_note-28] By 1640, peasant uprisings led by the [White Lotus Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Lotus_Society) in Shandong intensified. The Ming court dispatched [Zhu Datian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Datian?action=edit\&redlink=1) to suppress the revolt, causing Shandong's population to decline sharply once again.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

The [earthquake with an estimated magnitude of 8.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1668_Shandong_earthquake) and an [epicenter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicenter) just northeast of [Linyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linyi) devastated Dengzhou and the prefecture, and killed foreigners and locals, between 43,000 and 50,000 people are claimed to lost their lives.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

### Late Qing era

See also: [Boxer Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_Rebellion), [Juye Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juye_Incident), [British Weihaiwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Weihaiwei), and [Shandong Problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Problem)



Imperial German vice admiral [Alfred Meyer-Waldeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Meyer-Waldeck) and other colonial officers, together with [Sun Baoqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Baoqi), the [Qing Governor of Shandong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Shandong#Qing_dynasty_\(1644-1912\)), in [Kiautschou Bay Leased Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiautschou_Bay_Leased_Territory), April 1910

After the defeat in the [First Opium War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Opium_War), China became increasingly exposed to Western influence; as a coastal province, Shandong was significantly affected. After the [Second Opium War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Opium_War), Qing court opened [Cheefoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheefoo) as trade port, and then western powers like established consulates, missionaries, Christian schools, and factories in there. Due to its strategic position, every power wanted to gain its position in the province; in the 1880s, Shandong became the logistics and training base of [Beiyang Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beiyang_Fleet), and [Weihaiwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weihaiwei) served as the headquarter of the fleet. However, Beiyang fleet was disastrous defeated by [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) in 1894, and Japanese began to penetrate into Shandong. Three years later, two German catholic missionaries were killed in [Juye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juye_County), [Heze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heze), causing the [Juye Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juye_Incident), and Qing government had to cede [Qingdao](250171.md) to the German Empire. In 1898, [Weihaiwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weihaiwei_\(British_colony\)) was also given to Britain, as the result of pressure from [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).

Moreover, due to the annexation of [Outer Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Manchuria) by Russia in 1860, the Qing government revoked the prohibition and [encouraged settlement of Shandong people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuang_Guandong) to what remained of [Manchuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchuria). A large number of people from Shandong migrated to [Northeast China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_China), [Russian Far East](173135.md), and [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea) in search of better opportunities. Many settled in Northeast China (also known as Manchuria), contributing significantly to the region's demographic and agricultural development, engaging in farming, construction, and mining. Before the [extermination and forced deportation by Soviets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_deportations_of_Chinese_people), there were roughly 200 thousand Chinese labors in [Russian Far East](173135.md), 95% of whom are from Shandong.[^cite_note-33]

Shandong was one of the first places where the [Boxer Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_Rebellion) broke out, and became one of the centers of the uprising. In 1899, Qing general [Yuan Shikai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_Shikai) was appointed governor of the province to suppress the uprising. He held the post for three years.

Germany took control of the peninsula in 1898, leasing [Jiaozhou Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou_Bay) and its port of Qingdao under threat of force. Development was a high priority for the Germans: over 200 million [marks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_mark_\(1871\)) were invested in world-class [harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbor) facilities including [berths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berth_\(moorings\)), heavy machinery, rail yards, and a floating dry dock. Private enterprises worked across the Shandong Province, opening mines, banks, factories, and rail lines.[^cite_note-34]

As a consequence of the First World War, Japan [seized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Qingdao) German holdings in [Qingdao](250171.md) and Shandong. The 1919 [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles) transferred ownership to Japan instead of restoring Chinese sovereignty over the area. Popular dissatisfaction with this outcome, referred to as the [Shandong Problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Problem), led to the vehement student protests in the [May Fourth Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Fourth_Movement). Among the reservations to the Treaty that the [United States Senate Committee on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_Foreign_Relations) approved was "to give Shantung to China", the treaty with reservations was not approved. Finally, Shandong reverted to Chinese control in 1922 after the United States' mediation during the [Washington Naval Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Naval_Conference). [Weihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weihai) followed in 1930.[^cite_note-35]

### ROC era

See also: [Republic of China (1912–1949)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), [Jinan incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_incident), [Battle of Taierzhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Taierzhuang), and [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War)



Industrial zone of the Luda Company (魯大公司) in [Zichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zichuan,_Zibo) in 1929. The central Shandong coal basin, endowed with substantial coal reserves, was first systematically exploited by Germans, which laid the foundation for subsequent development of this region

Shandong's return to Chinese administration came during the [Warlord Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warlord_Era) of the Republic of China, when the [Zhili clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhili_clique) of warlords was given control, but after the [Second Zhili–Fengtian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Zhili%E2%80%93Fengtian_War) of 1924, the northeast China-based [Fengtian clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fengtian_clique) took over. In April 1925, the Fengtian clique installed the warlord [Zhang Zongchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Zongchang), nicknamed the "Dogmeat General", as military governor of Shandong Province. *Time* dubbed him China's "[basest](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/base#Adjective) warlord".[^cite_note-36] He ruled over the province until 1928 when he was ousted in the wake of the [Northern Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Expedition). He was succeeded by [Han Fuju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Fuju), who was loyal to the warlord [Feng Yuxiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feng_Yuxiang) but later switched his allegiance to the Nanjing government headed by [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek). Han Fuju [also ousted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han%E2%80%93Liu_War) the warlord [Liu Zhennian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Zhennian), nicknamed the "King of Shandong East", who ruled eastern Shandong Province, hence unifying the province under his rule.

In 1937 Japan began its invasion of China in the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), which would eventually become part of the Pacific theater. Han Fuju was made Deputy Commander in Chief of the 5th War Area and put in charge of defending the lower Yellow River valley. However, he abandoned his base and about 600,000 Ethnic Chinese civilians fled out Jinan city with only elderly too old to leave remaining[^cite_note-37] when Japanese troops crossed the Yellow River. He was executed shortly thereafter for not following orders.



The surrender ceremony of Japanese troops in Shandong Theater on 27 December 1945

During the Japanese occupation, with resistance continuing in the unoccupied areas especially by Muslim peasant villages, by 1945, communist [Chinese Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Red_Army) forces already held some parts of Shandong. Over the next four years of the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), they expanded their holdings, eventually driving the [Kuomintang](16903.md) (government of the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\))) out of Shandong to the island of Taiwan. In May 1947, during the [Menglianggu Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menglianggu_Campaign) in the Yimeng Mountains of central Shandong, the Chinese Communist [East China Field Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_China_Field_Army) annihilated the Nationalist 74th Corp, widely considered the most elite unit of the [National Revolutionary Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Revolutionary_Army).[^cite_note-38] In 1948, Communist forces captured [Weixian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weifang)—then known as the "fortress of central Shandong"—during the [Battle of Weixian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Weixian).[^cite_note-weifang-39][^cite_note-40]

On 2 June 1949, after the withdrawal of the Nationalist Army's 11th Pacification District and U.S. forces from Qingdao, the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) entered the city and incorporated it into Shandong's jurisdiction. On 12 August of the same year, the PLA's [Third Field Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Field_Army) secured victory in the [Changshan Islands Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changshan_Islands_Campaign?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-xinhua_2012-09-19-41] With this, the Communist forces had taken full control of Shandong Province.[^cite_note-xinhua_2012-09-19-41]

### In People's Republic

Under the new Communist government, parts of western Shandong were initially given to create the short-lived [Pingyuan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingyuan_Province), but this did not last. Shandong also acquired the Prefectures of [Xuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuzhou) and [Lianyungang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lianyungang), but this did not last either. For the most part, Shandong has kept the same borders that it has today.

Shandong suffrered heavily during the [Great Chinese Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Chinese_Famine) of 1959–62, about six million people starved to death.[^cite_note-42] On 25 August 1966, a confrontation known as the "Qingdao Incident" broke out between cadres and workers on one side and students from three major local universities on the other. This marked the beginning of the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution) in Shandong Province.[^cite_note-43]

Mao died in 1976, ending the era of restless political movement in China, two years later, the new leadership launched the [Reform and Opening Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_Opening_Up). Shandong, especially the eastern coastal region—experienced significant economic development. By 1986, for the first time, the total output value of township and village enterprises across the province surpassed that of agriculture.[^cite_note-44]

In 1996, Shandong became the first province in China to achieve full electrification of all households. In 1999, [Zhanhua County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhanhua_County), the province's last officially designated impoverished county, was declared poverty-free. In 2004, Shandong overtook [Guangdong](65109.md) in both industrial output and profits, becoming China's top province in industrial strength for the first time.[^cite_note-45]



Benefited from its coast, Shandong experienced rapid economic growth after 1978. Ports like [Qingdao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Port), [Yantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantai_Port), and [Rizhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizhao_port) are constantly ranked top 50 in the world [by container traffics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_container_ports)

In August and September 2008, Qingdao, as a partner city of [Beijing](18603746.md), hosted the [sailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing) competitions of the [29th Summer Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics) and the [13th Summer Paralympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Paralympics).[^cite_note-46]

In 2009, Jinan hosted the [11th National Games of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_National_Games_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China). On 16 July 2010, German Chancellor [Angela Merkel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angela_Merkel) visited China, during which both sides agreed to support the establishment of the [Sino-German Ecopark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-German_Ecopark?action=edit\&redlink=1) within the [Qingdao Economic and Technological Development Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Economic_and_Technological_Development_Zone).[^cite_note-47]

In 2011, the development plan for the [Shandong Peninsula Blue Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Peninsula) was officially approved.[^cite_note-48] On 3 June 2014, the [State Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) formally approved the establishment of the national-level [Qingdao West Coast New Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_West_Coast_New_Area?action=edit\&redlink=1) in Huangdao District.[^cite_note-49]

In August 2019, the State Council approved the establishment of the China (Shandong) Pilot Free Trade Zone, which includes three areas: Jinan, Qingdao, and Yantai. By 2020, the total population of the [Shandong Peninsula urban agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Peninsula) was expected to exceed 103 million, with an urban population of 67 million.[^cite_note-50]

## Geography



[Mount Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tai), with the height of 1,545 metres (5,069 ft), is [the highest point of Shandong Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_highest_point)



[Yellow River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River_Delta_National_Nature_Reserve), which is located 60 kilometres (37 mi) from downtown [Dongying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E4%B8%9C%E8%90%A5), is one of the largest [river delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta) in the world

[^cite_note-51]



View of [Rongcheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongcheng,_Shandong), the eastmost city of Shandong, seen from a hill

Shandong is on the eastern edge of the [North China Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_Plain) and in the lower reaches of the [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River), and extends out to sea as the [Shandong Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Peninsula). Shandong borders the [Bohai Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohai_Sea) to the north, [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei) to the northwest, [Henan](166408.md) to the west, [Jiangsu](166412.md) to the south, and the [Yellow Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Sea) to the northeast, east and southeast. It shares a short border with [Anhui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhui) between Henan and Jiangsu.

The northwestern, western, and southwestern parts of the province are all part of the vast [North China Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_Plain). The province's center is more mountainous, with [Mount Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tai) being the most prominent. The east of the province is the hilly [Shandong Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Peninsula) extending into the sea; [Miaodao Archipaelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changdao_County) to the north of Shandong Peninsula is the border of [Bohai Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohai_Sea) (west) and [Yellow Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Sea) (east). The highest peak of Shandong is [Jade Emperor Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jade_Emperor_Peak), with a height of 1,545 metres (5,069 ft), which is also the highest peak in the Mount Tai Ranges.

The [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River) passes through Shandong's western areas, since 1855, it has always been entering the sea to Shandong's northern coast; in Shandong, it flows on a [levee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levee), higher than the surrounding land, and dividing western Shandong into the [Hai He](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hai_He) watershed in the north and the [Huai River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huai_River) watershed in the south. The [Grand Canal of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Canal_of_China) enters Shandong from the northwest and leaves on the southwest. [Weishan Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nansi_Lake) is the largest lake in the province. Shandong's coastline is 3,000 kilometres (1,900 mi) long. [Shandong Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Peninsula) has a rocky coastline with cliffs, bays, and islands; [Laizhou Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laizhou_Bay), the southernmost of the 3 bays of the [Bohai Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohai_Sea), is bordering the northern coast between [Dongying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongying) and [Penglai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penglai_District); [Jiaozhou Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou_Bay), which is much smaller, is surrounded by [Qingdao](250171.md). The [Miaodao Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Changdao_County) extends northwards from the northernmost coast of the peninsula, separating the [Bohai Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohai_Sea) and the [Yellow Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_Sea).

With [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan) serving as the province's economic and cultural center, the province's economic prowess has led to the development of modern coastal cities located at [Qingdao](250171.md), [Weihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weihai), and [Yantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantai).

### Climate



[Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) map of Shandong, based on its climate from 1991 to 2020

Shandong has a [temperate climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_climate): [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental) (Köppen *Dwa*); it is bordering [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical) (C*wa* under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)) in the south. Generally, summers are hot (typical max 35 °C) and rainy (except for eastern parts of Jiaodong Peninsula (typical max 28 °C) and Mount Tai (typical max 20 °C)), while winters are cold and dry. Average temperatures are −9 to 1 °C (16 to 34 °F) in January and 18 to 28 °C (64 to 82 °F) in July. Annual precipitation is 550 to 950 mm (22 to 37 in), the vast majority of which occurs during summer, due to [monsoonal](57630.md) influences.

### Geology



*[Shantungosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantungosaurus)* was a flat-headed, crestless [hadrosaurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadrosaurid) [dinosaur](8311.md) that lived in [East Asia](19605700.md) during the [Late Cretaceous period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Cretaceous). It is the largest known non-sauropod herbivorous dinosaur in the world, with a body size that even exceeded that of large carnivorous dinosaurs such as [Tyrannosaurus](30467.md) and [Spinosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinosaurus)

Shandong is part of the Eastern Block of the [North China craton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_craton). Beginning in the [Mesozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesozoic), Shandong has undergone a crustal thinning that is unusual for a [craton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craton) and that has reduced the thickness of the crust from 200 km (120 mi) to as little as 80 km (50 mi). Shandong has hence experienced extensive volcanism in the [Tertiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_\(period\)).

Some geological formations in Shandong are rich in fossils. For example, [Zhucheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhucheng) in southeastern Shandong has been the site of discovering many dinosaur fossils. In 2008, about 7,600 dinosaur bones from *[Zhuchengtyrannus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuchengtyrannus)*, *[Ankylosaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankylosaurus)*, and other [genera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genus) were found, likely the largest collection ever discovered at one location.[^cite_note-53]

Shandong is rich in mineral resources, with 128 types of minerals discovered across the province—accounting for 78% of all known mineral types in China. Among the 74 minerals with proven reserves, over 30 rank among the top ten in the country. Of these, gold (rock gold), native sulfur (over 90% of the national reserve), and gypsum (about 70% of national reserves) rank first nationwide. [Jiaodong region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Peninsula) is the largest gold-producing area in China and the third-largest gold concentration zone in the world.[^cite_note-54] Within the province's maritime exclusive economic zone, 102 types of marine minerals have been discovered, 65 of which have proven reserves.

In 2015, Shandong had a total land area of 237 million mu (approximately 15.8 million hectares), including 173 million mu of agricultural land (with 114 million mu of [cultivated land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultivated_land)), 42.3 million mu of construction land, and 21.63 million mu of unused land.[^cite_note-55] The province features six major soil types: brown earth, cinnamon soil, fluvo-aquic soil, sandy loam black soil, saline-alkali soil, and [paddy soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_soil?action=edit\&redlink=1). Among these, fluvo-aquic, brown, and cinnamon soils occupy the largest areas, accounting for 48%, 24%, and 19% of the cultivated land, respectively.

According to the results of the ninth continuous national forest resource survey, Shandong had a forest coverage rate of 17.51%.[^cite_note-56] Vegetation in the province belongs to 80 families, 203 genera, and includes 615 species. The natural vegetation is mainly warm-temperate deciduous broadleaf forest, with the predominant genus being [Quercus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus) (oak), such as [Quercus variabilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_variabilis), [Quercus dentata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_dentata), and [Quercus aliena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quercus_aliena), with the first being the most common. Representative conifer species include the Japanese [red pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_pine).

Shandong is home to more than 400 species of terrestrial vertebrates, including nearly 50 mammal species, 356 bird species, 17 reptile species, and 2 amphibian species.[^cite_note-57] Additionally, there are over 600 species of marine economic organisms, including 260 species of fish and 90 species of shellfish.

The [Shandong Provincial Committee of the Chinese Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Provincial_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) is the leading organ of the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) (CCP) in Shandong Province. It is elected by the Shandong Provincial Congress of the CCP and, during the intersessional period of the congress, executes the directives of the Central Committee of the CCP and the resolutions of the provincial congress, leads the work of Shandong Province, and regularly reports its work to the Central Committee of the CCP.[^cite_note-58] [Lin Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin_Wu) is the current Secretary of the Shandong Provincial Party Committee.

The Shandong Provincial People's Congress is the organ of state power in Shandong Province.[^cite_note-59] It was established on 17 August 1954.[^cite_note-60] It currently consists of provincial deputies elected from the 16 prefecture-level cities of Shandong and the People's Liberation Army units stationed in the province.[^cite_note-61] [Lin Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lin_Wu) currently also serves as the Chairman of the Standing Committee of the Shandong Provincial People's Congress.

The [Shandong Provincial People's Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Provincial_People's_Government) is the State Administration in Shandong province. Its main officials are elected and appointed by The Shandong Provincial People's Congress.[^cite_note-62] The provincial government reports to Shandong Provincial People's Congress and [State Council of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China). The current [Governor of Shandong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Shandong) is [Zhou Naixiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_Naixiang).[^cite_note-63]

### Judicial system



Shandong Provincial Supreme Court

In ancient times, Shandong implemented a judicial system in which administrative and judicial powers were combined—local administrative chiefs also served as judicial officials. It was not until the late Qing dynasty that a modern judicial system began to take shape.[^cite_note-sfz-64] In August 1910, the province established the Office of the Judicial Commissioner (提法使司). In January 1911, the Shandong Provincial High Court and the High Prosecutor's Office were established, along with local courts and prosecutor's offices in the provincial capital, Jinan commercial port, and Yantai commercial port. In December of the same year, the Shandong Model Prison was founded in Jinan.

After the founding of the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), civil and criminal judicial institutions in Shandong were divided into primary, local, and high-level courts. After the establishment of the People's Republic of China, all levels of Shandong's judicial organs underwent judicial reform, and participated in movements such as the Three-Anti and Five-Anti Campaigns.[^cite_note-sfz-64]

Today, the Shandong High People's Court serves as the highest court in the province, under the supervision of the [Supreme People's Court of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_People's_Court_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China). The current President of the Court is Huo Min. As of February 2018, there are 18 intermediate courts in Shandong, including 16 municipal intermediate people's courts, as well as two specialized courts: the Jinan Railway Transport Intermediate Court and the Qingdao Maritime Court. The province also has 156 basic-level courts and 633 grassroots tribunals.[^cite_note-65]

The Shandong People's Procuratorate serves as the legal supervisory authority, comprising 16 municipal-level procuratorates, 1 railway transport procuratorate, and 161 grassroots procuratorates.[^cite_note-66]

### Military



[Dong Jun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Jun), a native from [Yantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantai), and former chief of staff of [North Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Sea_Fleet), now served as China's 14th [Minister of National Defense](352784.md)&#x20;

In February 1949, the [Shandong Military Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Military_Region?action=edit\&redlink=1) was established.[^cite_note-jsz-67] In 1955, it was reorganized into the [Jinan Military Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_Military_Region), which was responsible for the operational command of the land, sea, and air forces within the two provincial-level administrative regions of Shandong and [Henan](166408.md), as well as military, political, and logistical affairs of its subordinate units. It also oversaw the militia, conscription, mobilization, and battlefield construction within its jurisdiction. It served as the strategic general reserve force of the entire PLA. The military region headquarters was located in [Shizhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shizhong,_Jinan), [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan).[^cite_note-68]

In October 1961, based on the Mobilization Department of the Jinan Military Region, the [Shandong Provincial Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Provincial_Military_District) was established.[^cite_note-69] The [militia](20622.md) system was widely implemented across the province. By 1985, Shandong had approximately 1.6 million first-class reserve troops and about 6.4 million second-class reserve troops.[^cite_note-jsz-67]

Since 2012, Shandong's border defense has advanced the construction of an integrated land-sea control system, achieving notable results.[^cite_note-70] Currently, the commander of the provincial military district is [Qiu Yuechao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiu_Yuechao?action=edit\&redlink=1), and the political commissar is [Wang Aiguo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Aiguo?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-71]

After the establishment of the [Theater Commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_command) on 1 February 2016, Shandong came under the jurisdiction of the [PLA Northern Theater Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Theater_Command). The headquarters of the [Northern Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Theater?action=edit\&redlink=1) Army, the [Shandong Provincial Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Provincial_Military_District), and the [Shandong Armed Police Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Armed_Police_Corps?action=edit\&redlink=1) are stationed in Jinan. [Qingdao](250171.md) hosts the headquarters of the PLA Northern Theater Navy. The PLA Navy's first [destroyer](8779.md) unit was founded in Qingdao in 1954. The base for China's first aircraft carrier, the [Liaoning](155516.md), and its nuclear submarine base are also located in [Qingdao](250171.md).[^cite_note-72]

Other cities are also of military importance. The headquarter of the [PLA 80th Group Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/80th_Group_Army) is located in [Weifang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weifang).[^cite_note-73] The [PLA Rocket Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLA_Rocket_Force)'s 822nd Missile Brigade is stationed in [Laiwu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laiwu), equipped with [DF-21C medium-range ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DF-21),[^cite_note-74] and the [Rocket Force NCO School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Force_NCO_School) of [People's Liberation Army Rocket Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Army_Rocket_Force) was established in [Qingzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingzhou), [Weifang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weifang) in 2017. On 17 December 2019, the [aircraft carrier *Shandong*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_aircraft_carrier_Shandong) was officially commissioned.[^cite_note-75]

## Economy

As of 1832, Shandong was exporting fruits, vegetables, wine, drugs, and deerskin, often heading to [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou) to exchange clothing and fabrics.[^cite_note-Roberts1-21] The [economy of Shandong](#Economy) is China's [third largest provincial economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) with a [GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP) of [CNY](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNY)¥8.3 trillion in 2021 or [USD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USD)1.3 trillion in (nominal), which is equivalent to the GDP of Mexico.[^cite_note-\:0-14][^cite_note-\:1-15] Its GDP per capita is around [the national average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP_per_capita). Compared to a country, it would be the [15th-largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) and the [15th most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population) as of 2021.[^cite_note-\:2-16]

Shandong ranks first among the provinces in the production of a variety of products, including [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), and [garlic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garlic)[^cite_note-76] as well as precious metals such as [gold](12240.md) and [diamonds](8082.md). It also has one of the biggest [sapphire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapphire) deposits in the world.[^cite_note-77] Other important crops include [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum) and [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize). Shandong has extensive [petroleum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_industry_in_China) deposits as well, especially the [Shengli Oil Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shengli_Oil_Field)[^cite_note-\:12-78]<sup>: 39 </sup> (lit. Victory Oilfield) in the [Dongying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongying) area in the [Yellow River delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River_delta). Shandong also produces [bromine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromine) from underground wells and [salt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_salt) from seawater. It is the largest agricultural exporter in China.

Shandong is one of China's richest provinces, and its economic development focuses on large enterprises with well-known brand names. Shandong is the biggest industrial producer and one of the top manufacturing provinces in China. Shandong has also benefited from [South Korean](27019.md) and [Japanese](15573.md) investment and tourism, due to its geographical proximity to those countries.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-79] The richest part of the province is the [Shandong Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Peninsula), where the city of [Qingdao](250171.md) is home to three of the most well-known brand names of China: [Tsingtao Beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsingtao_Brewery), [Haier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haier) and [Hisense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisense). Besides, [Dongying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongying)'s oil fields and petroleum industries form an important component of Shandong's economy. Despite the primacy of Shandong's energy sector, the province has also been plagued with problems of inefficiency and ranks as the largest consumer of fossil fuels in all of China.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-79]

### Agriculture

Shandong Agricultural Product



[Vermicelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermicelli) of [Longkou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longkou)



[Peonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peonies) of [Heze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heze)



[Scallions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scallions) of [Zhangqiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangqiu)

Shandong is one of China's most important agricultural provinces. Its output value from farming, forestry, animal husbandry, by-products, and fisheries has consistently ranked first nationwide. The province is a major producer of grain, cotton, oilseeds, meat, eggs, and dairy. Shandong also has a highly developed agricultural industrialization system, and its agricultural exports have topped national rankings for decades.[^cite_note-87]

The province's grain crops are typically grown in two seasons: summer and autumn. Summer crops are dominated by winter wheat, while autumn crops include [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potatoes), [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybeans), [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum), and other minor grains. Among them, wheat, corn, and sweet potatoes are the province's three major staples.[^cite_note-sanong-88]

[Shouguang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shouguang) is known as the birthplace of modern solar-heated greenhouses in China and is the country's largest vegetable production and distribution center. It has been designated the "Hometown of Vegetables in China" by the State Council. Vegetables from Shouguang are distributed to over 200 large and medium-sized cities in more than 30 provinces and regions across China and are also exported to countries such as Japan, South Korea, and Russia.[^cite_note-89]

Shandong not only boasts abundant cultivated and livestock breeds, but also rich wild flora and fauna. In terms of plant resources, the province produces over 40 staple and cash crops such as [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn), [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potatoes), and [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut), over 60 varieties of [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetables) and [melons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melons), and more than 660 species of woody plants including [fruit trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit_trees), [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea), [mulberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulberry), and [oak](39696.md) trees. There are over 1,350 species of wild economic plants, including those used for starch, oils, fibers, aromatic oils, tannins, medicinal uses, and natural pesticides.

In 2013, the following agricultural products were awarded the title of "Top 10 Geographical Indication Trademarks of Shandong": [Zhanhua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhanhua) winter jujubes, [Zhangqiu scallions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangqiu_scallions?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Rizhao green tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizhao_green_tea?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Yantai apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantai_apples?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Jinxiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinxiang_County) garlic, [Rongcheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rongcheng,_Shandong) kelp, [Pingyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingyi) honeysuckle, [Jiaozhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou_City) cabbage, and [Feicheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feicheng) peaches.[^cite_note-90]

Animal resources include over 10 types of domesticated [livestock](25160767.md) and [poultry](23197.md), 55 species of small and medium-sized mammals, and more than 270 species of birds (resident, summer migratory, winter migratory, and transit). There are also 563 species of beneficial predators to agricultural pests and 763 species of agricultural pests. Additionally, inland aquatic resources include more than 30 species of vascular aquatic plants and over 70 species of freshwater fish.[^cite_note-sanong-88]

#### Fishery



Fishing fleet at [Longkou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longkou) port

Fisheries are a traditional strength of Shandong's economy. In 2014, the province's total aquatic product output exceeded 9 million tons, with a total fishery output value of ¥360 billion, and over 12 million mu (about 800,000 hectares) of aquaculture area. Dominant marine aquaculture industries include [sea cucumbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_cucumbers), [kelp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelp), [prawns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prawns), [flounder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flounder), [sole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sole_\(fish\)), [clams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clams), and [Chinese mitten crabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_mitten_crab). Inland aquaculture is rapidly developing local specialties such as [softshell turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softshell_turtles), [Siniperca chuatsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siniperca_chuatsi), [loach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loach), and [icefish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icefish_\(China\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-卫计委-91]

#### Wine industry

See also: [Wine in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine_in_China)



Shandong coastal vineyards

The production of wine is the second largest industry in the Shandong Province, second only to agriculture.

Geographically, the [coastal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal) areas remain relatively flat. Most of the soil is loose, well-ventilated, and rich in minerals and [organic matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_matter) that enable full development of the [root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Root) systems.

Presently, there are more than 140 wineries in the region, mainly distributed in the Nanwang Grape Valley and the Yan-Peng Sightseeing Highway (both are in [Yantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantai)). The region produced more than 40% of China's grape wine production.[^cite_note-92] Main varieties such as [Cabernet Sauvignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabernet_Sauvignon), [Cabernet Gernischt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabernet_Gernischt), [Merlot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlot), [Riesling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riesling) and [Chardonnay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chardonnay) are all at 20 years of age, considered to be the golden stage for these grapes. Most of them maintain an average saccharinity of above 20%.

### Service sector

In 2015, the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector) (services) accounted for 45.3% of Shandong Province's total economic output.[^cite_note-93] In 2012, the value-added of the service sector accounted for 40% of GDP. The value-added of the [financial sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_sector) reached ¥201.9 billion, representing a year-on-year increase of 19%; the [real estate sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate) contributed ¥201.6 billion, up 6.5%; total revenue from [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) was ¥451.97 billion; and software business revenue from large-scale [software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software) enterprises amounted to ¥173.79 billion.[^cite_note-94] In 2015, the financial sector's value-added accounted for 5% of Shandong's GDP, and its contribution to local tax revenue reached 10.7%, making it one of the province's pillar industries.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] By 2018, the province's three-sector economic structure had adjusted to a ratio of 6.5\:44.0\:49.5 (primary\:secondary\:tertiary), with the service sector contributing 60% to overall economic growth.[^cite_note-97]

### Economic and technological development zones

[Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan) High-tech Industrial Development Zone



[Inspur Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspur_Group) in Jinan High-tech Industrial Development Zone

Founded in 1991, the Jinan High-tech Industrial Development zone was one of the first of its kind approved by the [State Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China). The zone is located to the east of the city and covers a total planning area of 83 km<sup>2</sup> (32 sq mi) that is divided into a central area covering 33 km<sup>2</sup> (13 sq mi), an export processing district of 10 km<sup>2</sup> (3.9 sq mi), and an eastern extension area of 40 km<sup>2</sup> (15 sq mi). Since its foundation, the Jinan High-tech Industrial Development Zone has attracted enterprises as LG, Panasonic, Volvo, and Sanyo. In 2000, it joined the world science and technology association and set up a China-Ukraine High-tech Cooperation Park. The Qilu Software Park became the sister park of Bangalore park of India.

[Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan) Export Processing Zone

The export processing zone is located in the eastern suburbs of Jinan, east of the Jinan High-tech Industrial Development Zone, and to the north of the Jiwang highway. The distances to the Jiqing Highway and the [Jinan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_Airport) are 9 and 18 km (5.6 and 11.2 mi) respectively.[^cite_note-98]

[Qingdao](250171.md) Economic & Technological Development Area



[CR400AF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CR400AF) produced by [CRRC Qingdao Sifang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRRC_Qingdao_Sifang)

Approved by the State Council in October 1984, Qingdao Economic and Technical Development Zone has a plan of 12.5 km<sup>2</sup> (4.8 sq mi). In 2004 the local GDP was ¥27.51 billion, which increased by 28.9%; the total industrial output value is ¥60.6 billion, which increased by 31%. There have been 48 projects invested by companies listed among the Global [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) in the zone. With the fast development of reform and opening-up, [Haier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haier), [Hisense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisense), Aucma, [Sinopec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinopec), [CSIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Shipbuilding_Industry_Corporation), [CNOOC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNOOC), [CIMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIMC) etc. are all located in the zone.[^cite_note-99]

[Qingdao](250171.md) Free Trade Zone

The State Council established Qingdao Free Trade Zone in 1992. The zone is 60 km (37 mi) away from Qingdao Liuting Airport. It is also close to [Qingdao Qianwan Container Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Qianwan_Container_Terminal). At present, more than 40 foreign-invested enterprises have moved in, and 2000 projects have been approved. It is one of the special economic areas which enjoys the most favorable investment policies on customs, foreign exchange, foreign trade, and taxation in China.

[Qingdao](250171.md) High-tech Industrial Zone

The State Council approved Qingdao High-Tech Industrial Development Zone in 1992. The zone is located close to [Qingdao Liuting Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Liuting_Airport) and Qingdao Harbor. Encouraged industries include electronic information, biotechnology, medicine, new materials, new energy, advanced equipment manufacturing, marine science & technology, national defense technology.[^cite_note-100]

[Weifang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weifang) Binhai Economic & Technological Development Area (BEDA)

Established in August 1995, Weifang Binhai Economic & Technological Development Area (BEDA) is a national economic and technological development area approved by the State Council. Covering an area of 677 km<sup>2</sup> (261 sq mi), BEDA has a population of 100,000. BEDA possesses a large state-owned industrial land for use with an area of 400 km<sup>2</sup> (150 sq mi). The land can be transacted conveniently, guaranteeing the demand of any project construction and providing broad development space for the enterprises in the area. Continuously, BEDA has been accredited as National Demonstration Zone invigorating the Sea by Science and Technology, National Innovation Base for Rejuvenating Trade through Science and Technology and National Demonstration Eco-Industry Park.

[Weihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weihai) Economic & Technological Development Zone

Accurate population statistics for [Shandong Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Province) began during the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty), and its development since then can be divided into four stages. In 2 AD, the population in the area of present-day Shandong Province was over 12 million, and it exceeded 30 million for the first time by 1830—this was the first stage. From 1841 to 1949, the second stage, the population of Shandong grew steadily, reaching 45 million by 1949. During the 1950s and 1960s, Shandong experienced rapid population growth, followed by family planning policies in the 1970s and 1980s. By 1988, the population reached over 80 million.[^cite_note-114] According to the [2010 national census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_National_Population_Census_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), the permanent population of Shandong was 95.7931 million. In 2015, 1.2358 million people were born, and the year-end permanent population reached 98.4716 million. Among them, the 0–14 age group accounted for 16.62% of the total population, the 15–64 age group accounted for 71.17%, and those aged 65 and over accounted for 12.21%. With a population of more than 101,527,453 at the [2020 Chinese census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_census), the permanent population of Shandong exceeded 100 million, ranking second in China after Guangdong Province and slightly ahead of [Henan](166408.md).[^cite_note-115]

According to the 2010 national census, 8.3287 million people had attained a [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University) level of education, 13.3226 million had a [high school](554992.md) education, 38.4682 million had a [junior high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_high_school) education, and 23.9124 million had a [primary school](25058.md) education. The illiterate population was 4.7573 million, with an illiteracy rate of 4.97%. Shandong has achieved a high level of [compulsory education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_education): in 2016, the net enrollment rate for primary school-age children was 99.97%, and the retention rate for compulsory education was 97.2%.[^cite_note-教育公报-116]

By the end of 2014, there were 2.615 million people aged over 80 in the province, and 5,932 people aged over 100. The average life expectancy was 73.42 years in 2000, 76.46 years in 2010, and was projected to reach 78 years in 2016.[^cite_note-117] In 2009, [Laizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laizhou) was recognized by the China Gerontological Society as a "Longevity Town of China". It was the tenth such town in China, the first in Shandong Province, and also the first in [Northern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_China).[^cite_note-118]

Among the 16 prefecture-level cities, two had populations exceeding 10 million: [Linyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linyi) with 11.018 million, and [Qingdao](250171.md) with 10.072 million. Among the total resident population, 51.433 million were male (50.66%) and 50.095 million were female (49.34%). The overall [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sex_ratio) was 102.67, and the birth sex ratio was 111.95. The population aged 0–14 was 19.063 million (18.78%), those aged 15–59 were 61.244 million (60.32%), and those aged 60 and over were 21.221 million (20.90%), of whom 15.364 million (15.13%) were aged 65 and above. Among the total resident population, 14.603 million had a college education or above, and 14.553 million had a high school education (including technical secondary school). The average years of schooling among the population aged 15 and above was 9.75 years, and the illiteracy rate was 3.26%. 64.014 million people lived in urban areas (63.05%), while 37.513 million lived in rural areas (36.95%).[^cite_note-119]

Shandong citizens are also known to have the tallest average height of any Chinese province. As of 2010, 16-18-year-old male students in Yantai measured 176.4 centimetres (5 ft 9.4 in) while female students measured 164 cm (5 ft 5 in).[^cite_note-120]

### Ethnicity

As of March 2015, there were 55 ethnic minority groups in Shandong Province, with a total resident population of 720,000, accounting for 0.75% of the province's total population. Among them, the Hui ethnic group numbered 540,000, accounting for 75% of the total ethnic minority population in the province. There are four [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui) townships in the province, they are town of Jinling, [Zibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zibo), town of Shiliwang, [Dezhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dezhou), [town of Zhanglu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhanglu), [Liaocheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaocheng), and [town of Houji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houji,_Shandong), [Heze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heze).[^cite_note-121]

On 21 May 2021, the main data of [the seventh national population census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seventh_National_Population_Census_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) in Shandong Province was released. The data showed that the total resident population was 101.527 million, of which 100.622 million were Han Chinese, accounting for 99.11%, and 905,000 were ethnic minorities, accounting for 0.89%.

In addition, there are considerable [Korean diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_diaspora) in Shandong; for example, [Qingdao](250171.md) has a Korean population of over 100,000, accounting for about 7.19% of total [Koreans in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans_in_China).[^cite_note-122]

### Religion

Religion in Shandong

[^cite_note-Wang2015-125]

[\[note 4\]](#cite_note-127)

3. [Islam](6037917.md) (0.55%)

The predominant religions in Shandong are [Chinese folk religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_folk_religion), [Taoist traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism) and [Chinese Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Buddhism). According to surveys conducted in 2007 and 2009, 25.28% of the population believes in [ancestor veneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_ancestral_religion), while 1.21% of the population identifies as Christian, decreasing from 1.30% in 2004.[^cite_note-Wang2015-125] The Christians were 1.89% of the province's population in 1949, the largest proportion in China at that time.[^cite_note-Wang2015-125] According to a survey of the year 2010, Muslims constitute 0.55% of Shandong's population up from 0.14% in 1949.[^cite_note-2010-Islam-126][^cite_note-129]

The reports did not give figures for other types of religion; 80.05% of the population may be either irreligious or involved in [worship of nature deities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_folk_religion), Buddhism, [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism), Taoism, and [folk religious sects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_salvationist_religions). Shandong is the province where [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius) was born in the year 551 B.C.

The most well-known religion and/or philosophy of Shandong is [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism). Shandong is the birthplace of [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius) and his disciples such as [Mencius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mencius), [Zisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zisi), [Zengzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zengzi), and [Yan Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yan_Hui). Each year thousands of people come to Shandong to visit and learn about Confucius' culture. According to Chinese tradition, Confucius was a thinker, political figure, educator, and founder of the Confucianism of Chinese thought. His teachings, preserved in the Lunyu or Analects, form the foundation of much of subsequent Chinese speculation on the ideal man's education and comportment, how such an individual should live his life and interact with others, and the forms of society and government in which he should participate. Confucius also helped edit [The Five Classics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Books_and_Five_Classics) (五經), which include *[Classic of Poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_of_Poetry)*, *[Book of Documents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Documents)*, *[Book of Rites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Rites)*, *[I Ching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Ching)*, and *[Spring and Autumn Annals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_Annals)*.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

Taoism also has had a significant historical presence in Shandong, a province rich in religious and philosophical traditions. During the Han and Tang dynasties, Taoist practices flourished in the province, especially in the sacred [Mount Tai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tai), which has long been a center for Taoist pilgrimage and ritual. Taoist priests performed ceremonies there to communicate with the heavens and seek blessings for peace and prosperity. There are numerous Taoist temples, such as the [Dai Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_Temple) at the foot of Mount Tai built [during the Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_the_Song_dynasty). The coast part is associated with Taoist culture such as master [Qiu Chuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiu_Chuji) and [Eight Immortals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Immortals).

Due to the presence of [Hui people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people) (also called Muslim Han people), there are several [mosques](19894.md) in western Shandong. The history of Chrisitanity in Shandong began in the 1860s, when Qing government opened [Chefoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chefoo). The most influential churches are affiliated with the [Southern Baptist Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Baptist_Convention), [American Presbyterian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_\(USA\)), [BMS World Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMS_World_Mission) and [Society of the Divine Word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_the_Divine_Word). However, after the communist took power, foreign missionaries and clergies were expelled and the Christian church was suppressed. Today there are roughly 1.5 million Christians in Shandong; the exact number is hard to get, because there are a large number of people affiliated to [home church](357470.md).[^cite_note-132]

### Historical divisions



The commandery-county system of the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty), the basis of modern administrative divisions in Shandong

During the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty), the commandery-county system was implemented in Shandong. In the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty), two provinces (first-level divisions) were established in what is now Shandong: [Qing Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_Province) in the north and [Yan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yan_Province) in the south.[^cite_note-133] Most of Shandong belonged to [Henan Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henan_Circuit?action=edit\&redlink=1) during the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty). In the [Northern Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Song_dynasty), the area was part of the [Jingdong Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingdong_Circuit), which was later split into [Jingdong East Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingdong_East_Circuit) and [Jingdong West Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingdong_West_Circuit?action=edit\&redlink=1). In the eighth year of the Dading reign of the [Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jin_dynasty_\(1115%E2%80%931234\)) (1168), the military command offices of [Shandong East Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_East_Circuit) and [Shandong West Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_West_Circuit) were established. The term "Shandong" thus came into use as a formal administrative name.

In the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty), the province of Shandong was established, later renamed the Shandong Provincial Administration Commission (山東等處承宣布政使司), with its seat in Jinan Prefecture. Jinan has since remained the provincial capital. The Shandong Administration Commission governed 6 prefectures, 15 sub-prefectures (*zhou*s), and 89 counties.[^cite_note-134]

During the [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty), the Shandong Administration Commission was renamed Shandong Province, and its highest official was the [Provincial Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Shandong_Province) (*Xunfu*). The province was divided into [circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_\(administrative_division\)) (*dao*), [prefectures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefectures) (*zhou* and *fu*), and [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(China\)) (*xian*).[^cite_note-135] Additionally, some counties such as [Qingyun County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingyun_County), [Ningjin County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ningjin_County,_Shandong), and [Dongming County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongming_County) in [Zhili Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhili_Province) (present-day Hebei) are now part of Shandong.



Map of Shandong in 1917

After the founding of the Republic of China in 1912, the Shandong Province initially retained the Qing administrative system. In 1913, the Beijing government abolished the prefectures and sub-prefectures, instituting the dao–county system. In 1927, the [Nanjing government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing_government) abolished the [dao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_\(China\)) level, placing counties directly under provincial jurisdiction. In 1932, administrative inspectorates were established below the provincial level, functioning as provincial extensions. This marked the formal establishment of administrative inspectorate districts.[^cite_note-cnr-136]

In 1950, three administrative regions were abolished, reducing 16 prefectures to 11. In 1952, [Pingyuan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingyuan_Province) was dissolved; its eastern part was incorporated into Shandong. At the same time, five counties from [Hebei Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei_Province) were transferred to Shandong, while some counties under Shandong's jurisdiction were reassigned to Hebei and [Jiangsu](166412.md).

In 1967, prefectures (专区) were renamed regions (地区), and the province then had 9 regions including [Dezhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dezhou), [Huimin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huimin_County), and [Changwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weifang), as well as four provincial cities: [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan), [Qingdao](250171.md), [Zibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zibo), and [Zaozhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaozhuang).

On 26 December 2018, the State Council officially approved the administrative adjustment of [Laiwu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laiwu) under [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan)'s jurisdiction, abolishing Laiwu as a separate prefecture-level city and merging its territory into Jinan. As a result, the province now comprises 16 prefecture-level cities.[^cite_note-137]

The 16 prefecture-level cities of Shandong are subdivided into 137 [county-level divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China#County_level) (55 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_of_China), 26 [county-level cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County-level_cities), and 56 [counties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_\(People's_Republic_of_China\))). Those are in turn divided into 1941 [township-level divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China#Township_level) (1223 [towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_\(China\)), 293 [townships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townships_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), two [ethnic townships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_township), and 423 [subdistricts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdistricts_of_China)).

As of 2023, the province's urbanization rate reached approximately 64.2% (with 65.55 million people reside in cities), slightly below the national average but showing consistent growth.[^cite_note-142] Shandong features a comprehensive multi-centered urban system with 16 prefecture-level cities, including [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan), the provincial capital and a key administrative and transportation hub, and [Qingdao](250171.md), a major coastal city with a strong port economy and international presence. The province has made notable progress in narrowing the urban-rural divide through policies promoting integrated development, rural infrastructure extension, and the growth of small towns and county-level cities.

Linguists classify these dialects into three broad categories: [Ji Lu Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji_Lu_Mandarin) spoken in the northwest (as well as in neighboring [Hebei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebei)), such as the [Jinan dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_dialect); [Zhongyuan Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhongyuan_Mandarin) spoken in the southwest (as well as in neighboring [Henan](166408.md)); and [Jiao Liao Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiao_Liao_Mandarin) spoken in the [Shandong Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Peninsula) (as well as the [Liaodong Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaodong_Peninsula) (e.g., [Dalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalian), [Dandong](614115.md)) and the southeastern Jilin (e.g., [Baishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baishan), [Tonghua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonghua))), such as the [Weihai Dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weihai_dialect). When people speak of the "Shandong dialect" (山東話), it is generally the first or the second that is meant; the Jiao Liao dialects of Shandong are commonly called the "Jiaodong dialect" (膠東話).

### Cuisine

[Shandong cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_cuisine) (鲁菜) is one of the eight great traditions of [Chinese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_cuisine). It is known for its bread (corn-based) and fish dishes and characterized by its emphasis on fresh seafood, hearty soups, and bold, savory flavors achieved through techniques like braising and roasting, with a focus on preserving the natural taste of ingredients.[^cite_note-153] It can be further divided into three branches: inland branch (e.g. [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan) cuisine (济南菜)), the [seafood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafood)-oriented [Jiaodong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaodong) branch (e.g. [Fushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fushan,_Yantai) cuisine (福山菜)) in the peninsula, and [Confucius's Family branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius's_Family_branch?action=edit\&redlink=1) (孔府菜), with an elaborate tradition originally intended for imperial and other important feasts.

Shandong cuisine significantly influenced [Beijing cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_cuisine) due to historical migration and political factors, especially during the Ming and Qing dynasties when imperial chefs and officials in the capital mostly came from Shandong.[^cite_note-BJCRS1-154][^cite_note-BJCRS2-155][^cite_note-BJCRS3-156][^cite_note-BJCRS4-157] This influence is evident in Beijing cuisine's emphasis on hearty flavors, wheat-based staples, and cooking techniques such as braising, roasting, and deep-frying.&#x20;

### Literature and arts



*Ci of Shuyü* by [Li Qingzhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Qingzhao), who is an outstanding female poet from [Zhangqiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangqiu,_Jinan) in the 12th century

The literature of Shandong largely reflects [Confucianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism); [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius) proposed the poetic theory of "evocation, observation, socialization, and admonition" (興, 觀, 群, 怨), which laid the foundation for traditional Chinese poetic theory. Since the Qin and Han dynasties, notable literary figures from Shandong include [Kong Rong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kong_Rong) and [Wang Can](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Can) of the [Jian'an period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jian'an_poetry), [Zuo Si](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuo_Si) of the [Western Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Jin), [Wang Xizhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wang_Xizhi) of the [Eastern Jin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Jin), [Bao Zhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bao_Zhao) and [He Xun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/He_Xun?action=edit\&redlink=1) of the [Southern Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Dynasties), [Yan Zhitui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yan_Zhitui) of the [Northern Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Qi), [Duan Chengshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duan_Chengshi) from the [Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) to [Five Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties) period, [Chao Buzhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_Buzhi), [Li Qingzhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Qingzhao), and [Xin Qiji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xin_Qiji) of the [Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) and [Yuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty) periods, [Li Kaixian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Kaixian), [Li Panlong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Panlong), and [Kong Shangren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kong_Shangren) of the [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming) and [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing) dynasties, as well as novelists like [Luo Guanzhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luo_Guanzhong) and [Pu Songling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pu_Songling). Classic works from Shandong include the [*Book of Songs*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_of_Poetry), *[Spring and Autumn Annals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_Annals)*, *[Discourses of the States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Discourses_of_the_States)*, *[Zuo Zhuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuo_Zhuan)*, *[Analects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analects)*, and [*Mencius*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mencius_\(book\)).[^cite_note-158]

Representative works of Shandong folk songs (a branch of [Chinese folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_folk_music)) include the Han dynasty's *[Liangfuyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangfuyin?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (梁父吟),[^cite_note-159] the Qing dynasty collection *[Baixue Yiyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baixue_Yiyin?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (白雪遗音, lit "Echoes of Snow and Sorrow"),[^cite_note-160] and the modern folk song *Yimeng Mountain Minor* (沂蒙山小调). Local folk performance forms include [Shandong Bangzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Bangzi?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Shandong Kuaishu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Kuaishu?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Lü opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BC_opera),[^cite_note-161] while [Shandong Drum Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Drum_Song?action=edit\&redlink=1) (山東大鼓) was listed in the first batch of National Intangible Cultural Heritage of China. Famous Shandong operatic works include *[The Precious Sword](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Precious_Sword?action=edit\&redlink=1)* (寶劍記) by [Li Kaixian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Kaixian) and *[The Peach Blossom Fan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Peach_Blossom_Fan)* by [Kong Shangren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kong_Shangren).[^cite_note-162] [Shandong Bangzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Bangzi?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Lüju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BCju) are popular types of [Chinese opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_opera) in Shandong; both originated from southwestern Shandong.



The first [Nobel laureate in Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nobel_laureates_in_Literature) with Chinese nationality, [Mo Yan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mo_Yan), hailed from [Gaomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaomi), Shandong, which served as the fictional backdrop for much of his [literary work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literary_work)

### Media

Radio and television broadcasting in Shandong is divided into over-the-air radio, cable radio, and television broadcasting, all of which are state-owned enterprises. In May 1933, the first wireless broadcasting station was established in Jinan and named the [Shandong Provincial Capital Broadcasting Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Television). On 27 October 1950, the Shandong People's Radio Station officially began broadcasting to the entire province. By 1955, some counties and cities began to establish cable broadcasting stations. By the end of 1990, the province had 16 wireless radio stations and 34 television stations.[^cite_note-171] As of the end of 2019, the comprehensive coverage rates of radio and television in Shandong were 99.13% and 99.10%, respectively.[^cite_note-172] Among them, the Shandong Radio and Television Station has 21 channels, including 11 television channels such as [Shandong Satellite TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Satellite_TV?action=edit\&redlink=1) and 10 radio channels.[^cite_note-173] The Jinan Radio and Television Station currently has 8 television channels and 7 radio channels; the Qingdao Radio and Television Station has 7 television channels and 9 radio channels; and the Zibo Radio and Television Station has 5 television channels covering news, science education, public affairs, life, and urban topics, as well as 4 radio channels.[^cite_note-174]

During the [late Qing Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty), more than 20 newspapers were published successively in Shandong, including *Jinan Bulletin* (濟南匯報) founded in 1903 by [Governor of Shandong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Shandong) [Zhou Fu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_Fu), and later publications such as *Jinan Daily* (濟南日報), and *Shandong Official Gazette* (山東官報).

At the beginning of the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution), only Dazhong Daily and seven regional and municipal newspapers remained in circulation.

Following the [Reform and opening-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up), the newspaper industry in Shandong resumed growth. By the end of 1990, the number of officially registered and approved newspapers in the province had reached 119.[^cite_note-175] Today, some of the most well-known newspapers in Shandong include *[Qilu Evening News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qilu_Evening_News)*, *[Jinan Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_Times?action=edit\&redlink=1)* and *[Shandong Business Daily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Business_Daily?action=edit\&redlink=1)*.[^cite_note-176]

## Transport

### Rail



German engineers posed before a locomotive on the newly completed [Qingdao–Jinan railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao%E2%80%93Jinan_railway), which opened Shandong's interior to large-scale trade and resource extraction, connecting the port of Qingdao with the provincial capital Jinan and laying the groundwork for the region's rapid urban and industrial transformation



Train No. K1531 passes Longju Bridge over [Yellow River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_River) near [Lijin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lijin_County)

The history of railway in Shandong can be traced back to [Jiaozhou–Jinan Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou%E2%80%93Jinan_Railway), which was constructed by Germans between 1899 and 1904 through the Shantung Railway Company ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Schantung Eisenbahn Gesellschaft*). The railroad was backed by German capital and operated under German management; they also built braches linking industrial towns such as [Boshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boshan,_Zibo) and [Fangzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangzi,_Weifang).[^cite_note-177] The line stretched approximately 393 kilometers, connecting Jinan, the provincial capital located in the northwestern interior of Shandong, with the German-controlled port of [Qingdao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiautschou_Bay_Leased_Territory). This railway is still the most important east–west main line across Shandong Province.

As for north–south traffics, [Jingjiu railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingjiu_railway) ([Beijing](18603746.md)-[Kowloon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kowloon)) and [Jinghu railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinghu_railway) ([Beijing](18603746.md)-[Shanghai](27643.md)) are two major arterial railways that pass through the western part of Shandong. The Jingjiu passes through [Liaocheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaocheng) and [Heze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heze); the Jinghu passes through [Dezhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dezhou), [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan), [Tai'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tai'an), [Yanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanzhou) (the Jinghu high-speed railway will through [Qufu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qufu)) and [Zaozhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaozhuang).

The first high-speed railroad, [Qingdao–Jinan passenger railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao%E2%80%93Jinan_passenger_railway), operated in 2008, with maximum speed of 250 km. In the subsequent years, high-speed lines such as [Beijing–Shanghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing%E2%80%93Shanghai_high-speed_railway), [Qingdao-Rongcheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao-Rongcheng_Intercity_Railway), [Shijiazhuang–Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shijiazhuang%E2%80%93Jinan_high-speed_railway) and [Zhengzhou–Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhengzhou%E2%80%93Jinan_high-speed_railway) were all completed and commenced operation.[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179]

[Jinan West Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinanxi_Railway_Station) is the largest railway hub in Shandong Province, serving as the intersection point of the Jinan hub (comprising the [Beijing–Shanghai railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing%E2%80%93Shanghai_railway), [Jiaoji railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaoji_railway), and [Handan–Jinan railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handan%E2%80%93Jinan_railway) lines). Other major railway hubs include: the [Qingdao railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_railway_station) (connecting the Jiaoji, Jiaoxin, Jiaohuang, and Lanyan railways), the Dezhou hub (connecting the Beijing–Shanghai and Shide railways, under the administration of the [Beijing Railway Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Railway_Bureau)), the Yanzhou hub (Beijing–Shanghai, [Xinyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinxiang%E2%80%93Yanzhou_railway), and [Yanshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yanshi_Railway) railways), and the Heze hub (Beijing–Kowloon and [Xinyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinyan_Railway) railways).

According to the classification of the [Ministry of Railways of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Railways_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), Shandong currently has four top-tier (special-class) railway stations:

- [Jinan Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_Railway_Station) (passenger and freight)

- [Jinxi Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinxi_Railway_Station?action=edit\&redlink=1) (济西站, one of the nation's top ten network-level freight marshalling yards)

- [Jinan West Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_West_Railway_Station) (passenger station on the [Beijing–Shanghai High-Speed Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing%E2%80%93Shanghai_High-Speed_Railway))

- [Qingdao Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Railway_Station) (passenger station)

The [Jinan East Railway Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_East_railway_station) is the largest railway station in Shandong. The [Jinan Railway Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_Railway_Bureau), following its 2008 reorganization, manages the majority of Shandong's trunk and branch railway networks, as well as the [Bohai Train Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohai_Train_Ferry).[^cite_note-180]

As of the end of 2024, [Qingdao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Metro) and [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_Metro) are the only two cities in Shandong with a metro system.

Major civil airports in Shandong

By 2014, civil aviation in Shandong Province had formed a preliminary structure featuring two trunk airports, [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan_Yaoqiang_International_Airport) and [Qingdao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Jiaodong_International_Airport), alongside six regional airports: [Yantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantai_Penglai_International_Airport), [Jining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jining_Qufu_Airport), [Linyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linyi_Qiyang_International_Airport), [Weihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weihai_Dashuipo_International_Airport), [Dongying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongying_Shengli_Airport), and [Weifang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weifang_Airport).[^cite_note-qilu-182]

Additionally, [Penglai Shahekou Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penglai_Shahekou_Airport?action=edit\&redlink=1), which was completed and opened to air traffic in 2002, is primarily used for test flights, training, and tourism.[^cite_note-183] [Rizhao Shanzihe Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizhao_Shanzihe_Airport) opened on 22 December 2015,[^cite_note-184] and [Qingdao Jiaodong International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Jiaodong_International_Airport) officially commenced operations on 12 August 2021.

In 2014, Shandong's total annual passenger throughput exceeded 30 million for the first time, with total cargo and mail volume reaching 350,000 tons. Among them, [Qingdao Liuting International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Liuting_International_Airport) alone handled over 10 million passengers.[^cite_note-185]

Direct flights between Shandong and Taiwan were launched in 2008. There are currently four cities with cross-strait direct flights: Jinan, Qingdao, Yantai, and Weihai, operating 10 routes with 52 round-trip flights per week, carrying over 1,000 passengers daily.[^cite_note-qilu-182]

[Shandong Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Airlines) was founded in 1994 and currently operates more than 220 routes, with over 700 flights per week serving more than 40 major and medium-sized cities across China. As of March 2018, the airline had a fleet of 114 [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) series aircraft, with an average age of 5.0 years.

## Education

### History of education



Traditional Confucius academy of [Qingzhou Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingzhou_Prefecture) (青州府) in [Qingzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingzhou), [Weifang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weifang)



First cohort of graduates of Christian-led Tengchow College in [Cheefoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheefoo), [Yantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantai)



[Shandong University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_University), which is consistently considered the best university of the province, in [Licheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licheng,_Jinan), [Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan)

Shandong is considered one of China's leading provinces in education and research. Shandong hosts 153 [higher education institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Shandong), ranking second in the East China region after [Jiangsu](166412.md) and fourth among all Chinese provinces/municipalities after [Jiangsu](166412.md), [Guangdong](65109.md) and [Henan](166408.md).[^cite_note-\:03-17]

Before the late Qing dynasty, Shandong generally enjoyed a flourishing educational tradition, except during a few periods such as the Qin dynasty and the late Jin dynasty. During the Spring and Autumn period, [Confucius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucius) pioneered private education. In the [Northern Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Song_Dynasty), Shandong first introduced the "school field" system, which promoted institutional education. In the Ming and Qing periods, private schools, official schools, and academies of classical learning thrived. During the reign of [Emperor Yongzheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Yongzheng) in the Qing dynasty, there were 75 academies in Shandong, of which 61 were government-run. Following the [Hundred Days' Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hundred_Days'_Reform), Shandong established the [first provincial-level university in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_University#History).

During the late Qing period, Western missionary education had a significant influence on Shandong's educational development. For example, in 1866, [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Church_\(USA\)) missionary [Calvin Wilson Mateer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvin_Wilson_Mateer) founded the Wenxian Primary School (for boys) and Huiying Primary School (for girls) in Yuhuangding, [Yantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yantai), which were the first modern schools established in Yantai following its opening as a treaty port under the [Treaty of Tientsin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Tientsin) in 1862. These schools later developed into the Yantai Yuwen Business College, the predecessor of today's Yantai No. 2 High School. Mateer's wife, [Julia Mateer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Mateer?action=edit\&redlink=1), also established the first kindergarten in Yantai at Yuhuangding. Calvin Mateer went on to establish over 40 primary schools in areas including [Fushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fushan_District), [Muping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muping_District), [Qixia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qixia,_Shandong), [Laiyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laiyang), [Haiyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiyang), [Jimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimo_District), [Jiaozhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou_City), and urban Yantai. Fellow missionaries [Hunter Corbett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunter_Corbett) and his wife established the [Tengchow College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tengchow_college?action=edit\&redlink=1), the first modern higher education institution in China, which also became one of the earliest [Christian colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_college) in the country.

After the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) came to power, all institutions of higher education in the province ceased enrollment for five years starting in 1966 due to the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution), which severely disrupted educational order. Restoration did not begin until after 1976.[^cite_note-190] As of 2016, the province had 18,853 kindergartens, 12,951 compulsory education schools (including 10,027 elementary schools and 2,924 junior high schools), 580 regular high schools, 428 secondary vocational schools, 146 special education schools, and 155 higher education institutions (144 regular universities and colleges and 11 adult education institutions).[^cite_note-教育公报-116] There were 25 nationally designated key technical schools, with a provincial enrollment of 148,000 students in technical schools, and a graduate employment rate of 98%. Among them, the [Shandong Lanxiang Senior Technical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Lanxiang_Vocational_School) gained widespread attention after Western media reported its role in training technical non-commissioned officers for the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md).[^cite_note-nyt2010-191]

### Colleges and universities

See also: [List of universities and colleges in Shandong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Shandong)

As of 2016, Shandong had 141 officially accredited higher education institutions offering general degree programs, including 67 regular undergraduate institutions, 73 vocational colleges, and 1 branch campus. Among the regular undergraduate institutions, 44 were public, 12 were private, and 11 were independent colleges. Among the vocational colleges, 60 were public and 13 were private.[^cite_note-192] Shandong is home to three universities included in the [Double First-Class Construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_First-Class_Construction), namely [Shandong University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_University) (985), [Ocean University of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_University_of_China) (985), and [China University of Petroleum (East China)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_University_of_Petroleum_\(East_China\)) (211). Additionally, three Double First-Class universities registered in other provinces have campuses in Shandong: [Beijing Jiaotong University, Weihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing_Jiaotong_University) (211), [Harbin Institute of Technology, Weihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin_Institute_of_Technology,Weihai) (985), and [China Agricultural University, Yantai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Agricultural_University) (985).[^cite_note-193] Shandong ranks first in the nation in terms of marine science research capacity. As of the end of 2009, the province had nearly 60 national and provincial-level institutions engaged in marine science and education, 29 provincial and ministerial-level marine key laboratories, over 20 scientific research vessels, 10 national-level demonstration bases for marine science and technology, and more than half of China's marine science personnel—including 23 academicians of the Chinese Academy of Sciences and Chinese Academy of Engineering.[^cite_note-194]

As of 2025, two major cities in the province ranked in the top 35 cities in the world ([Jinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinan) #27 and [Qingdao](250171.md) #31) by scientific research output, as tracked by the [Nature Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index).[^cite_note-\:3-18]

## Sports



[Qingdao International Sailing Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_International_Sailing_Centre)



A game between [Palmeiras](187243.md) and [Shandong Luneng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Luneng)

Before the 20th century, traditional sports were the main form of physical activity in Shandong, while modern competitive sports gradually became dominant. The 1903 Hetan Games (阖滩运动会) in Yantai was one of the most prominent early modern athletic competitions in China. After the outbreak of the [Second Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War), most sports activities came to a halt. Following the [founding of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founding_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), mass sports movements gained momentum in Shandong. In October 1953, the province established the Shandong Provincial Sports Committee. During the Cultural Revolution, all sports activities were suspended and administrative agencies ceased functioning. After the Reform and Opening-Up, Shandong successfully hosted over 70 national competitions, including championships, league matches at various levels, regional tournaments, title contests, and cup competitions.[^cite_note-195] Today, Shandong is home to several professional sports clubs such as [Shandong Taishan F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Taishan_F.C.), [Qingdao Hainiu F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Hainiu_F.C.), [Qingdao West Coast F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_West_Coast_F.C.), [Qingdao Red Lions F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Red_Lions_F.C.), [Shandong Hi-Speed Kirin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shandong_Hi-Speed_Kirin), and [Qingdao Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qingdao_Eagles).[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198]

Shandong has hosted numerous major sports events, including the [National Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Games_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), the [World Table Tennis Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Table_Tennis_Championships), and the [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup). Qingdao also served as a co-host city for the [2008 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics) sailing events. In 2009, Shandong hosted the [11th National Games of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_National_Games_of_China), ranking first nationwide in terms of gold medals, total medals, and overall points.[^cite_note-199] Moreover, since 2009, Shandong has achieved five consecutive championship at the [National Games of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Games_of_China), leading both the gold medal and total medal counts[^cite_note-200][^cite_note-201].

The year 2015 is considered the "inaugural year" of marathon events in Shandong. Major marathons have since been held in cities such as Dongying, Yantai, Weifang, Tai'an, Qingdao, Linyi, Zibo (Gaoqing), Tengzhou, and Jining. Among them, Dongying, Yantai, Tai'an, Qingdao (Laixi), Qingdao (High-tech Zone), and Linyi host international marathons.[^cite_note-202]

Prominent athletes from Shandong include table tennis world champion [Zhang Jike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Jike), billiards player [Pan Xiaoting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Xiaoting) (nine-ball), Olympic shooting champion [Du Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Du_Li), Olympic weightlifter [Liu Chunhong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Chunhong), long-distance runner [Xing Huina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xing_Huina), and gymnast [Xing Aowei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xing_Aowei).[^cite_note-203]
