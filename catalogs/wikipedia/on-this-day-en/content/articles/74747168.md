# Foreign relations of Russia since the Russian invasion of Ukraine

|  | Parts of this article (those related to article) need to be **updated**. Please help update this article to reflect recent events or newly available information. *(September 2026)* |
| --- | --- |

On 24 February 2022, [Russia](25391.md) launched a [large-scale invasion](79400268.md), prompting the imposition of substantial [economic and political sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) by the EU, the UK, the US, Canada, and other countries. The Russian government has a specified "[Unfriendly Countries List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfriendly_Countries_List)" which indicates those countries with which relations are now strained (or non-existent). A Foreign Policy Concept approved by president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) in 2023 identified Russia as a [Eurasian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia) [civilization state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilization_state); aligning the country more closely with [Asia](689.md), the [Islamic world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_world), [Africa](5334607.md), [Latin America](18524.md), and the rest of the [Global South](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_South). Russia is also seeking the end of the [Western hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_imperialism) in the [international order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_order).[^cite_note-1]

## Europe

### Positive relations

#### *Abkhazia* [Abkhazia](18933375.md)[^cite_note-2]

President [Aslan Bzhaniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aslan_Bzhania) stated that Russia's invasion was "absolutely justified."[^cite_note-oc-media.org-3]

#### *Artsakh* [Republic of Artsakh](1000530.md)[^cite_note-4]

President of Artsakh [Arayik Harutyunyan](13280780.md) welcomed Russia's declaration of the independence of the [Donetsk](42425747.md) and [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic) People's Republics.[^cite_note-armenpress.am-5] The Artsakh government sent 14 tons of humanitarian aid to the residents of the Kyiv and Zaporizhzhia regions.[^cite_note-6]

#### Azerbaijan [Azerbaijan](746.md)

The government of Azerbaijan has taken a relatively neutral position on the conflict, and maintained cooperation with both Moscow and Kyiv owing to its security, political, and economic ties and dependence on regional players.[^cite_note-7]

Azerbaijan President [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md) has previously expressed his support for territorial integrity of Ukraine, and urged his Ukrainian counterparts to "never to agree to the violation of your territorial integrity".[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] He offered to organize talks between Ukraine and Russia,[^cite_note-11] instructed Azerbaijan's State Oil Company [SOCAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOCAR) to provide free fuel to Ukraine's ambulances and [State Emergency Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Emergency_Service_of_Ukraine)[^cite_note-12] and sent humanitarian aid to Ukraine.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Before the war, Russia and Azerbaijan signed a joint wide-ranging political-military agreement which President Aliyev described as bringing "relations to the level of an alliance", and the two countries later entered into a new intelligence-sharing agreement.[^cite_note-15] However, relations between the two countries have since declined significantly, and have been described as "probably their worst crisis since Azerbaijan gained independence in 1991", following the Russian downing of passenger plane [Flight 8243](78712170.md), as well as tensions over the [Azerbaijani-Armenian border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93Azerbaijan_border_crisis_\(2021%E2%80%93present\)) and other tensions.[^cite_note-16]

In April 2026, Ukrainian President Zelensky visited Azerbaijan where he signed agreements with President Aliyev on enhanced co-operation between the two countries, including in the areas of the defence-industry, regional security, and the energy sector.[^cite_note-17] [^cite_note-18]

#### Belarus [Belarus](3457.md)

In February 2022, Russian forces were permitted to stage part of the [invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) from Belarusian territory.[^cite_note-auto-19] President [Alexander Lukashenko](156589.md) stated that Belarusian troops could take part in the invasion if needed.[^cite_note-reuters.com-20] Belarus stated that Russia could bring its nuclear weapons onto Belarusian soil.[^cite_note-21] In March 2022, Oleksandr Kamyshin, head of [Ukrainian Railways](3149341.md), said that the railway connection between Ukraine and Belarus had closed, so Russian equipment from Belarus could not be delivered.[^cite_note-22] On 25 March 2023, Russian president Vladimir Putin announced that Russia would station tactical nuclear operations in Belarus.[^cite_note-23]

#### Serbia [Serbia](29265.md)

While condemning the invasion, Serbia refused to back sanctions against Russia.[^cite_note-timesofisrael.com-24] Serbia did not impose sanctions. Serbian President [Aleksandar Vučić](13349267.md) said the National Security Council concluded the Republic of Serbia considers "very wrong the violation of territorial integrity of a number of countries including Ukraine."[^cite_note-Reuters-25][^cite_note-Dragojlo-26] Vučić also said that he would condemn Russia's recognition of the independence of separatist regions in eastern Ukraine only if Zelenskyy condemned the [NATO bombing of Yugoslavia](322473.md) in 1999 on public television.[^cite_note-27]

Serbian Foreign Minister [Nikola Selaković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikola_Selakovi%C4%87) said that "Russia was Serbia's biggest support in its battle to preserve its territorial integrity and sovereignty and avoid the stigmatisation of the entire Serb people." Serbian Interior Minister [Aleksandar Vulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandar_Vulin) stressed that Serbia would not join NATO: "While Aleksandar Vučić is the head of the state, you should have no dilemma as to what our attitude toward the NATO alliance is."[^cite_note-Euractiv-2022-02-25-28] Serbia expressed regrets over events, describing both Russia and Ukraine as friendly states and underlining full support for the Ukraine's territorial integrity.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] The initial weak response by the government was criticised by some commentators in the country and the region.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

The Serbian organization [Women in Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Black) organised anti-war demonstrations in [Belgrade](55904.md), and the [Serbian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church) organised a collection of humanitarian aid.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

On 2 March Serbia voted 'yes' to the UN's resolution condemning the Russian attack on Ukraine.[^cite_note-35]

On 16 May Serbia signed the "Tirana declaration" and deplored in the strongest terms the illegal and unjustified aggression by the Russian Federation against Ukraine in violation of Article 2(4) of the UN Charter.[^cite_note-36]

[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist) classified Serbia as "neutral".[^cite_note-37]

#### *South Ossetia* [South Ossetia](318381.md)[^cite_note-38]

The Ministry of Foreign Affairs issued a statement: "The Republic of South Ossetia expresses its support for the decision of the Russian Federation to conduct the special military operation to protect the residents of Donbas from the current nationalist regime of Kyiv."[^cite_note-mfa.rsogov.org-39] On 26 March 2022, South Ossetian President [Anatoly Bibilov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Bibilov) began sending troops to Ukraine to assist Russia with the invasion.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

#### *Transnistria* [Transnistria](376581.md)[^cite_note-42]

On 26 February, President [Vadim Krasnoselsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadim_Krasnoselsky), asserted that Transnistria was a peaceful state and never had plans to attack its neighbours. Krasnoselski noted the large ethnically [Ukrainian](145144.md) population in Transnistria and that [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language) is taught in Transnistrian schools and is one of its official languages.[^cite_note-Antonescu-43]

### Other relations

#### Armenia [Armenia](10918072.md)

The Armenian MFA spokesman on 23 February expressed "hope that the existing problems between the two friendly states would be resolved through diplomatic dialogue", and declared Armenia's readiness to accept refugees.[^cite_note-Martirosyan-44][^cite_note-mfa.am-45] On 1 June 2023, during an interview, prime minister of Armenia [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) stated, "Armenia is not an ally of Russia in the war in Ukraine."[^cite_note-Radio_Free_Europe_/_Radio_Liberty-46]

#### Bosnia and Herzegovina [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md)

Tripartite Presidency Member [Željko Komšić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BDeljko_Kom%C5%A1i%C4%87) said Bosnia would support Ukraine.[^cite_note-prishtinainsight.com-47] Croat and Bosniak members of the tripartite presidency, Željko Komšić and [Šefik Džaferović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0efik_D%C5%BEaferovi%C4%87), issued separate statements condemning the Russian invasion. The Serb member [Milorad Dodik](4218129.md) did not, instead stating that Bosnia and Herzegovina was neutral. He'd said the previous day that events showed it was good that Bosnia and Herzegovina had not joined NATO, and that the country would not support sanctions.[^cite_note-EWB-48] Foreign Minister [Bisera Turković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bisera_Turkovi%C4%87) said: "Bosnia and Herzegovina remains firmly committed to Ukraine's sovereignty and territorial integrity, and we call for an immediate end to fighting and shelling! OSCE principles, security and international law are under attack today. Hostility and suffering of innocent civilians must end immediately". Turković called on Russia and Belarus to refrain from using force in the interests of peace, and regional and global stability.[^cite_note-49]

Dodik repeatedly visited Russia, including attending Eastern Economic Summit.

#### Hungary [Hungary](13275.md)

Prime Minister [Victor Orban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Orban) had made strong arguments related to NATO expansion to the East, and stated that Ukraine never could join either EU nor NATO. Orban defended Russia and Hungarian relations. Hungary was one of a few European countries to maintain relations with Russia, despite many sanctions or obstacles faced between bilateral relations of Russia and Hungary.

#### Slovakia [Slovakia](26830.md)

Prime Minister [Eduard Heger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduard_Heger) stated that "The Russian imperialism has been restored in front of our eyes in its aggressive, militant form" and about Russian president Vladimir Putin added "All victims of this war will be his victims and he will be responsible for them in the eyes of the global public."[^cite_note-spectator.sme.sk-50] On 24 February 2022, [Bratislava Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava_Castle) and the seat of President of Slovakia [Grassalkovich Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grassalkovich_Palace) in Bratislava was lighted in blue and yellow in solidarity with Ukraine.[^cite_note-51] Slovakia provided Ukraine with an [S-300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-300_missile_system) air defense system.[^cite_note-52]

#### Turkey [Turkey](11125639.md)

On 3 February 2022, President [Recep Tayyip Erdoğan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recep_Tayyip_Erdo%C4%9Fan) volunteered to organize a Ukraine-Russia conference during a visit to Ukraine, as EU leaders increased outreach to the Kremlin to calm worries of a [Russian invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-53] On 24 February, Erdoğan expressed his support for Ukraine.[^cite_note-54] On 25 February, on the other hand, the Republic of Turkey abstained from voting on Russia's suspension from the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), instead calling for open dialogue between the parties under any circumstances.[^cite_note-55] Turkish Foreign Minister [Mevlut Cavusoglu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mevlut_Cavusoglu) reiterated Turkey's "readiness to host negotiations that could take place between the Russian Federation and Ukraine".[^cite_note-56]

On 27 February 2022, Cavusoglu shifted its terminology to refer to Russia's assault on Ukraine as a "war," and committed to enforce elements of the [1936 Montreux Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreux_Convention_Regarding_the_Regime_of_the_Straits)'s international pact that allowed Turkey to prohibit all warships of the belligerent forces, including those of the Russian navy, from entering the Bosporus and Dardanelles and thus hinder Russian vessels' transit from the Mediterranean to the Black Sea.[^cite_note-57] On 28 February, Erdoğan confirmed that the straits would be closed to prevent escalation, while pledging to maintain relations with both Ukraine and Russia.[^cite_note-58] On 10 March, Russian Foreign Minister [Sergei Lavrov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Lavrov) and [Dmytro Kuleba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmytro_Kuleba) met in [Antalya](361527.md) in Turkey, the first high-level contact between the two sides since the invasion. Turkish Foreign Minister [Mevlut Cavusoglu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mevlut_Cavusoglu) also participated. Mr Cavusoglu said the aim of the diplomatic meeting was "to pave the way," for a meeting between the Russian and Ukrainian presidents, which would be facilitated by Turkey's president.

Turkey actively participated in mediation efforts.[^cite_note-59] Ukraine asked both Israel and Turkey to set up talks with Russia.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

Turkey had been providing Ukraine with [Bayraktar drones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayraktar_UAV) since 2019, which played a significant role in deterring Russian advances in the early stages of the invasion, but did not impose sanctions on Russia.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

On 17 March 2022, Cavusoglu expressed hope for a humanitarian ceasefire in the southern Ukrainian port city of Mariupol, where he claimed more than 100 Turkish citizens remained.[^cite_note-64]

On 20 March 2022, Cavusoglu stated that Russia and Ukraine were close to agreement on "important" issues, and that he hoped for a ceasefire.[^cite_note-65]

#### Vatican City [Vatican City](32408.md)

[Pope Francis](1687680.md) stated that the events in Ukraine had caused "great pain in his heart". The Pope called for 2 March, [Ash Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_Wednesday), to be a day of prayer and fasting for peace. In an unprecedented departure from diplomatic protocol, the Pope went to the Russian embassy on 25 February to relay his concern.[^cite_note-Pullella-66]

The Pope said to Russian youth, "Never forget your heritage. You are the descendants of great Russia: the great Russia of saints, rulers, the great Russia of Peter I, Catherine II, that empire – educated, great culture and great humanity. Never give up on this heritage." This comment was received negatively by the government of Ukraine.[^cite_note-67]

### Strained relations

#### European Union [European Union](9317.md)

The EU launch a group of sanctions against Russia, which added all EU countries to the list of "[unfriendly nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfriendly_Countries_List)".[^cite_note-CTV_News-68]

#### Albania [Albania](738.md)

In mid February 2022, Lavrov accused Albania and two other Balkan countries of sending mercenaries to the Donbass conflict in Ukraine.[^cite_note-IsSin-69] The claims were rejected by Albanian officials.[^cite_note-IsSin-69]

Albanian President [Ilir Meta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilir_Meta),[^cite_note-70] Prime Minister [Edi Rama](277805.md),[^cite_note-prishtinainsight.com-47] Minister for Europe and Foreign Affairs [Olta Xhaçka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olta_Xha%C3%A7ka),[^cite_note-71] and Ambassador to the UN [Ferit Hoxha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferit_Hoxha)[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] made statements condemning the invasion. Russia's recognition of the separatist regions in the Ukrainian Donbass as independent was condemned by Albania as a violation of the [Minsk Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk_Protocol), international law, and of Ukraine's statehood and borders.[^cite_note-Albcon-74]

In late February 2022, Albania and the US tabled a co-written resolution condemning the invasion at the [UN Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Security_Council), but Russia vetoed it.[^cite_note-VoUkr-75] Albania cosponsored another resolution with the US for an [emergency General Assembly session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_emergency_special_session_of_the_United_Nations_General_Assembly).[^cite_note-Lederer-76][^cite_note-Falk-77] The resolution passed.[^cite_note-Lederer-76][^cite_note-Falk-77] At the session, Albania voted in favour of a [successful resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_ES-11/1) that condemned the invasion and demanded its withdrawal from Ukraine.[^cite_note-Genas-78]

Albania imposed sanctions on Russia targeting close political and business Putin associates, on sectors related to energy, finance, technology and transport, and denying access to Russian aircraft.[^cite_note-Sinoruka-79][^cite_note-Sinfreukr-80] The name of a Tirana street where the Russian embassy sits was changed to "Free Ukraine" by Mayor [Erion Veliaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erion_Veliaj).[^cite_note-albstr-81][^cite_note-Sinfreukr-80] These actions led to Russia including Albania on its official list of "unfriendly countries".[^cite_note-Kajosevic-82][^cite_note-Starrpub-83][^cite_note-Exikhar-84] Albania's honorary consulate in [Kharkiv](2788558.md) was shelled and destroyed by Russian forces. There were no casualties as its staff had evacuated the building.[^cite_note-Euralb-85][^cite_note-Exikhar-84][^cite_note-Sinfreukr-80]

In mid March, Albania and five other countries at the UN Security Council accused Russia of [war crimes](48626.md).[^cite_note-BoSa-86] In Albania, President Meta, Foreign Minister Xhaçka and the Speaker of Parliament [Lindita Nikolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindita_Nikolla) all condemned Russia for the [Bucha massacre](70455343.md) and called for an international response and independent investigation.[^cite_note-Albpol-87] Albania voted for a successful [UN General Assembly resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_ES-11/3) to suspend Russia from the [UN Human Rights Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN_Human_Rights_Council).[^cite_note-Alvot-88] In late September 2022, Russia vetoed a UN Security Council resolution tabled by Albania and the US opposing the [Russian annexation of occupied areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_annexation_of_Donetsk,_Kherson,_Luhansk_and_Zaporizhzhia_oblasts).[^cite_note-LewGar-89]

#### Austria [Austria](26964606.md)

Austria supported the EU sanctions, while maintaining its [military neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_country).[^cite_note-Österreichistsolidarisch-90] Austria joined other countries in spring 2022 in [declaring a number of Russian diplomats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_expulsions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) *[persona non grata](521241.md)*.

#### Belgium [Belgium](3343.md)

Belgium joined other countries in spring 2022 in [declaring a number of Russian diplomats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_expulsions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) *persona non grata*.

#### Bulgaria [Bulgaria](3415.md)

On 24 March 2022, the Bulgarian prime minister recalled the Bulgarian ambassador in Moscow for consultations, in the wake of "undiplomatic, sharp and rude" comments reportedly spouted by the Russian ambassador to Bulgaria, [Eleonora Mitrofanova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleonora_Mitrofanova).[^cite_note-91]

On the evening of 27 June the Russian embassy in Sofia launched a charity appeal for Bulgarians to support the invasion.[^cite_note-bi28.06-92] One day later, Petkov announced the expulsion of 70 Russian diplomats over concerns of espionage.[^cite_note-rus70-93] The [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Bulgaria\)) closed its diplomatic mission in [Yekaterinburg](66524.md) and expected Russia to halt the activities of its own mission in [Ruse, Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruse,_Bulgaria).[^cite_note-tso28-94] All Russian embassy services were halted. Bulgaria stipulated that Russia must conform to the official standard of limiting their numbers to 23 diplomatic staff and 25 administrative staff.[^cite_note-stbi70-95] Misinformation promulgated by Russia in Bulgaria was countered by the government.[^cite_note-96]

Russia is suspected of an explosion at an arms factory in June 2023, which followed similar incidents at arms depots housing ammunition meant for export to Ukraine.[^cite_note-97] In June 2023 Bulgaria began to sell weapons to Ukraine and in July endorsed a decision to join the [European Defence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Defence_Agency)'s Collaborative Procurement of Ammunition project in support of Ukraine.[^cite_note-98] Prime Minister [Nikolai Denkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Denkov) stated "Russia must definitely withdraw from the territory of Ukraine, recognize its borders, and be held accountable for the crimes it has committed," at a July meeting with Zelensky.[^cite_note-99]

Bulgaria took over the Rosenets oil terminal at the port of [Burgas](86758.md), run by [Lukoil](1027881.md), ending its long term concession.[^cite_note-100]

#### Croatia [Croatia](5573.md)

Croatia [declared multiple Russian diplomats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_expulsions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) *persona non grata*.[^cite_note-tasslist-101][^cite_note-direktnolist-102]

President [Zoran Milanović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoran_Milanovi%C4%87) made pro-Russian statements, stating in January 2023 "Crimea will never again be part of Ukraine".[^cite_note-103] In January 2023 Croatia joined the [Schengen Area](16565139.md) then requiring Russian citizens to apply for a visa to enter Croatia.[^cite_note-104]

A Russian drone crashed in [Zagreb](55906.md), in March 2022.[^cite_note-\:0-105][^cite_note-\:1-106][^cite_note-\:2-107]

#### Cyprus [Cyprus](5593.md)

Cyprus supported EU actions.[^cite_note-CTV_News-68]

#### Czech Republic [Czech Republic](5321.md)

The Czech embassy in Moscow were limited to 19 Russian employees.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109]

#### Denmark [Denmark](76972.md)

The self-governing Faroe Islands, although not part of the EU, implemented similar sanctions.[^cite_note-110] In April 2022, Denmark expelled 15 Russian diplomats from Denmark, and the following month Russia expelled 4 Danish diplomats and 3 other Danish embassy workers from Russia.[^cite_note-111] Relative to its GDP, Denmark donated among the most to Ukraine (combined [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_military_aid_to_Ukraine_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) and [humanitarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_humanitarian_aid_to_Ukraine_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) aid).[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-KielInstitute_9th_release-113]

#### Estonia [Estonia](28222445.md)

Estonia joined other countries in [declaring a number of Russian diplomats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_expulsions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) *persona non grata*. Estonia banned Russian language media channels.[^cite_note-114]

In September 2022, Poland, Lithuania, Latvia, and Estonia closed entry for Russian citizens with [Schengen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schengen) visas, including those issued by third countries.[^cite_note-euroweeklynews.com-115]

On 18 October 2022, the Estonian parliament voted to officially recognize Russia as a terrorist state. The [Riigikogu](794927.md) called on the international community to adopt a similar position.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117]

On 6 December 2022 suspected [FSB](385990.md) officer Vadim Konoshchenok was arrested at the border with high tech electronic items and ammunition sourced in the US. Additional goods were found in a warehouse Konoshchenok was renting. The USA sought his extradition,[^cite_note-118] which was granted and completed in July 2023.[^cite_note-119]

In January 2023, Estonian Foreign Ministry spokesman Mihkel Tamm announced Estonia's intention to seize \$21.4 million in Russian assets in Estonia and deliver it to Ukraine. Estonia worked with European Commission on plans to seize Russian assets frozen in the European Union which are estimated to be in the hundreds of billions of dollars.[^cite_note-120]

Estonia announced the expulsion of 21 Russian diplomats and technical staff in January 2023 and encouraged other European Union countries to follow suit.[^cite_note-121] In response, Russia downgraded its relations with Estonia and expelled the Estonian ambassador, Estonia responded in kind.[^cite_note-122]

#### Finland [Finland](10577.md)

On 6 June 2023 Finland expelled nine Russian diplomats, believed to be working for an intelligence service. In July 2023 Russia ordered the closure of Finland's St Petersburg consulate and expelled nine diplomats. Entry into Finland for Russian citizens was indefinitely limited.[^cite_note-123]

Finland would end up joining [NATO](21133.md).

#### France [France](5843419.md)

On 5 June 2022, French finance minister [Bruno Le Maire](20472835.md) stated that France was in talks with the United Arab Emirates, to replace Russian oil imports.

After Macron took power in France, Russian-French relations remained at a standstill. According to Macron, "the war will continue" and he urged the French people "to prepare for the scenario where we all have to go without Russian gas." Macron's government urged a "sobriety plan" to conserve energy.[^cite_note-124]

#### Georgia [Georgia (country)](48768.md)

President [Salome Zourabichvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salome_Zourabichvili) stated that "We are participating in all kinds of international financial sanctions and that's quite something for the Georgian financial sector. At the same time, we are participating in all the international resolutions that have been taken to support Ukraine. We share \[with Ukraine] a common two-century history of Russian aggression and we know what that means"[^cite_note-125]

The Georgian government contributed 1 million GEL from its reserve budget to help Ukrainians harmed by Russia's military assault. Prime Minister [Irakli Garibashvili](37868165.md) made the decision and signed the decree authorizing the aid. The funding was for medical supplies for Georgia's Ministry of Internally Displaced Persons, Labor, Health, and Social Affairs.[^cite_note-agenda.ge-126]

On 24 February, the Chairman of the Parliament of Georgia [Shalva Papuashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalva_Papuashvili) expressed his solidarity with Ukraine and called on the international community to take steps to "stop Russia escalating into a full-scale conflict and ensure the protection of international norms."[^cite_note-127]

On 27 February 100 tons of humanitarian aid were delivered to Ukraine via Poland.[^cite_note-agenda.ge-126]

On 4 March, The Georgian Health Ministry declared that all Ukrainian citizens in Georgia would receive free emergency medical services.[^cite_note-agenda.ge-126]

Garibashvili said that Georgia's government could not impose separate sanctions against Russia.[^cite_note-128]

Vice Prime Minister/Minister of Foreign Affairs of Georgia, [David Zalkaliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Zalkaliani) stated that "Military aggression launched by Russia against Ukraine is totally intolerable". He called on international partners to give a strict response to the violation of international law by Russia and ensure the de-escalation of the situation.[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130]

A Parliamentary delegation visited two Ukrainian cities where Papuashvili opposed Russia's invasion. The opposition United National Movement (UNM), Georgia's main opposition party, paid a surprise visit to Kyiv on the same day, sending a separate delegation that included party chairman Nika Melia and former Georgian President [Giorgi Margvelashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgi_Margvelashvili).[^cite_note-131]

#### Germany [Germany](11867.md)

The [2022 invasion](79400268.md) led to a near complete reversal of German-Russian relations under the new German Chancellor, [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz). He ordered the immediate transfer of thousands of missiles to the Ukrainian military.

Germany was dependent on Russia for [natural gas](22131.md) and was less willing to sanction that sector. Germany agreed to halt the [Nord Stream 2](50335419.md) gas pipeline. The pipeline provided a significant portion of Germany's petroleum imports.[^cite_note-132] Russia gradually reduced flows of gas. The pipeline was attacked on 26 September, ending transport.



Putin's hypochondria on display as he meets with German chancellor [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz) on 15 February 2022

Germany joined other countries in spring 2022 in [declaring a number of Russian diplomats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_expulsions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) *persona non grata*.

In April 2022, the German government said it would send 1 billion euros in [military aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_military_aid_to_Ukraine_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) to Ukraine.[^cite_note-133] On 17 May 2022, German Finance Minister [Christian Lindner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Lindner) said he is "politically open to the idea of seizing" the frozen [foreign-exchange reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_foreign-exchange_reserves) of the [Central Bank of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bank_of_Russia) to offset the costs of rebuilding Ukraine after the war. Russian Deputy Foreign Minister [Alexander Grushko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Grushko) remarked that it would amount to "complete lawlessness", and that the measure would hurt Germany if adopted.[^cite_note-134]

German [Riol Chemie GmbH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riol_Chemie_GmbH) was alleged to have illegally delivered chemicals to Russia, including a precursor for [Novichok](1766982.md).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

In late 2022, Germany announced its first [trade deficit](4810.md) since 1991, after it halted permits for [Nord Stream 2](50335419.md), and introduced a Russian oil embargo. The country suffered an acute energy shortage. The government offered subsidies to protect households from soaring energy bills. In fall 2022, Russia had halted gas flows via the [Nord Stream 1](3498125.md) pipeline several times, blaming [sanctions](70323386.md), while the value of the [Euro](9472.md) slipped against all major currencies until February 2023.[^cite_note-137] Russia blamed the United States for Germany's energy crisis, by pushing its leaders towards a "suicidal" step,[^cite_note-138] despite previous [Russian-Ukrainian gas disputes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russia%E2%80%93Ukraine_gas_disputes) disrupting Russia's natural gas supply to Europe in 2006 and 2009.[^cite_note-139]

The discussion on the legitimacy of economic sanctions against Russia had a significant impact on Germany's political landscape. Parties to the right ([AfD](38708229.md)) and to the left ([Die Linke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Linke)) split on whether economic sanctions were effective to stop the conflict, and how they impacted the German economy. The right wanted to support the [Nord Stream 2](50335419.md) pipeline, while leftists have voiced similar concerns with regard to Germany's economic viability.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141]

In April 2023, Germany expelled 50 Russian diplomats, reportedly to "reduce the presence of Russian intelligence in Germany".[^cite_note-142] Russia responded by expelling 34 German diplomats, stating that Germany "continues to demonstratively destroy the entire array of Russian-German relations".[^cite_note-143] In May 2023, the [German Foreign Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Foreign_Office) stated that hundreds of Germans would be expelled from Moscow at the beginning of June, after Russia capped the number of German workers in the country.[^cite_note-144]

"If your presence in Russia is not essential, we advise that you consider leaving by remaining commercial routes."[^cite_note-246]

On 11 March the UK imposed sanctions on 386 members of Russia's lower house of parliament and attempted to prohibit the export of luxury products to Russia.[^cite_note-247]

On 12 March, France, the United Kingdom, and Germany cautioned Russia that its demands for economic guarantees with Iran could jeopardize an almost-completed nuclear deal.[^cite_note-248]

On 17 March, the United Kingdom said it had "very, very strong evidence" of war crimes in Ukraine, and that Russian President Vladimir Putin was orchestrating them.[^cite_note-249]



The UK is on Russia's "[Unfriendly Countries List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unfriendly_Countries_List)" (red). Countries and territories on the list have imposed or joined [sanctions against Russia](70323386.md).

[^cite_note-250]

On 24 March, the [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin) declared Johnson to the most active anti-Russian leader. Downing Street rejected these claims and stated that the Prime Minister was "anti-Putin" and had no issue with the Russian people.[^cite_note-251] On 3 May, Russia aired a segment titled *The Sinkable Island.* During the segment, hosted by [Dmitry Kiselyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Kiselyov), a simulation showing a hypothetical nuclear attack on Great Britain was shown.[^cite_note-252]

On 29 October, Russia accused the UK of involvement in the [Nord Stream pipeline sabotage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nord_Stream_pipeline_sabotage), which it claimed was carried out by the Royal Navy, in addition to involvement in the drone strikes on the [Sevastopol Naval Base](59166849.md). The UK Ministry of Defence released a statement denouncing the claims and stated that Russia was "peddling lies on an epic scale".[^cite_note-nordstream-253] Earlier in the month, Russia had accused the UK of involvement in the [Crimean Bridge explosion](71948884.md).
