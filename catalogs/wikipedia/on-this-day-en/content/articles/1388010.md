**Makiivka** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Макіївка, IPA: [\[mɐˈkijiu̯kɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Ukrainian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e4/Uk-%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BA%D1%96%D1%97%D0%B2%D0%BA%D0%B0.ogg/Uk-%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BA%D1%96%D1%97%D0%B2%D0%BA%D0%B0.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/e4/Uk-%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BA%D1%96%D1%97%D0%B2%D0%BA%D0%B0.ogg/Uk-%D0%9C%D0%B0%D0%BA%D1%96%D1%97%D0%B2%D0%BA%D0%B0.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>, [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Макеевка, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Makeyevka*), formerly **Dmytriivsk** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Дмитріївськ) until 1931, is an industrial city in [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md), eastern [Ukraine](31750.md), located 15 kilometers (9.3 mi) east from [Donetsk](562951.md). The two cities are practically a [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation). It has a population of 338,968 (2022 estimate).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-3] It hosts the administration of [Makiivka urban hromada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makiivka_urban_hromada).

Makiivka is a metallurgical and coal-mining centre of the [Donets Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas), with [heavy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_industry) and coking plants supporting the local [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_industry) and [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_industry) industries. The city was captured by pro-Russian separatists in 2014 at the start of the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)) and is currently [occupied](42425747.md) by Russia.

## History

### Ancient prior settlement

The rough area of the city has been inhabited for millennia.[^cite_note-10] [Kurgans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurgan) from the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), [Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythian) civilization, and the 800s-1200s AD have been excavated in the surrounding area.[^cite_note-EOU-11]

### Founding



View from 1912

Makiivka was founded in 1690 under the name Yasinovka, as a Cossack settlement. In 1777, the free settlement was reorganized into a military settlement by order of the tsarist government. It was first mentioned in documents at that time.[^cite_note-EOU-11] In 1875–1877, several minor mines were opened nearby.[^cite_note-EOU-11] In 1899, a metallurgical settlement was founded nearby called Dmitriyevsk (Dmytriivsk), named after Dmitry Ilovaisky, son of [count Ilovaysky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilovaysky) - the landlord of the region. Makiivka was only a small village when it was combined with nearby Dmytriivsk. Dmytriivsk subsequently developed as one of the largest coal-mining and industrial centres of the [Donets Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets_Basin) coalfield.

During the [Ukrainian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence), from 1917 to 1920, it passed between various factions. Afterwards, it was administratively part of the [Donets Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets_Governorate) of Ukraine.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In 1931, Dmytriivsk was renamed Makiivka.[^cite_note-15]

### Industrialization and the Soviet era

The city became increasingly industrialized throughout the 1930s, with its population rising from 79,000 in 1926 to 242,000 in 1939.[^cite_note-EOU-11] In 1939, the [Jewish](25955086.md) population of Makiivka was 8,000.



Monument to the victims of Nazism

During [World War II](32927.md), the town was under [German](21212.md) occupation from 22 October 1941 until 6 September 1943.[^cite_note-16] In the Operational Situation Report (USSR No. 177) of German Chief of the Security Police dated from 6 March 1942, it is stated that as a result of the measures carried out by [Einsatzkommando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzkommando) 6, both the [Horlivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horlivka) and Makiivka districts had been made "[free of Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judenfrei)". [Nazis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazis) and [Ukrainian collaborators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_collaboration_with_Nazi_Germany) executed a total of 493 people here, among them 80 political agitators, 44 saboteurs and looters, and 369 [Jews](25955086.md). The Germans operated the Dulag 102 and Dulag 123 transit [prisoner-of-war camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) in the city in 1942 and 1943, respectively.[^cite_note-17]

After the end of the war, Makiivka was rebuilt. By 1959, its population had reached 381,000.[^cite_note-EOU-11]

### Ukrainian independence

In September 2006, the first synagogue was consecrated in Makiivka after almost 70 years. The house at 51 Lva Tolstogo street serves not only a synagogue, but also a community center for a [Jewish](25955086.md) community of Makiivka containing 2,000 members. The chief rabbi of Makiivka is [Eliyahu Kremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliyahu_Kremer?action=edit\&redlink=1). Makiivka Jewish community chairman is Alexander-Mikhoel Katz.

Early on 20 January 2011, two explosions took place in Makiivka, one near the coal plant, and the other near a shopping mall. There were no injuries or deaths, but a note was found near one of the blast sites, demanding [€](9472.md)4,200,000 (equivalent to [€](9472.md)5,407,275 in 2023) from well-known local people.[^cite_note-terror-18] [Security Service of Ukraine](709222.md) chief [Valeriy Khoroshkovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeriy_Khoroshkovsky) opened a criminal case on the blasts the same day, under the article on [terrorism](30636.md). Two suspects - Anton Voloshyn and Dmytro Onufrak - were detained on 15 February 2011. Voloshyn and Onufrak were later found guilty and sentenced to eight and fifteen years in prison, respectively.[^cite_note-terror-18]

### Russo-Ukrainian War

During the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md) the city town hall was taken over by pro-Russian separatists on 13 April 2014.[^cite_note-19] Since then, Makiivka has been controlled by the self-proclaimed [Donetsk People's Republic](42425747.md).[^cite_note-20]

On 1 January 2023, a military quarters in the city was [shelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makiivka_military_quarters_shelling). It was reported by both Russian and Ukrainian sources that a Ukrainian strike on Russian military forces based at a vocational school in Makiivka resulted in significant casualties, particularly among conscripts.[^cite_note-21] First Deputy Minister of Information of the Donetsk People's Republic Daniil Bezsonov stated that the strike took place at exactly 00\:01 [Moscow Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Time) and made use of the [M142 HIMARS](147011.md) rocket system.[^cite_note-22] Russian officials claimed that at least 25 HIMARS rockets were fired at the school, resulting in at least 15 casualties.[^cite_note-\:0-23] Officials of the Donetsk People's Republic stated that the reason for the strike was the use of mobile phones by Russian serviceman at the school, which revealed their location to the Ukrainian military.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-\:1-25] The [Armed Forces of Ukraine](42799055.md) announced on the same day that 400 Russian forces had been killed in the strike, with a further 300 wounded, resulting in 700 total casualties.[^cite_note-26] [Igor Girkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Girkin), the former commander of separatist forces in the Donbas, said about the attack, "the number of dead and wounded runs into many hundreds".[^cite_note-\:0-23] This number was, however, also challenged by others; Russian presenter [Vladimir Solovyov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Solovyov_\(TV_presenter\)) claimed that while casualties were high, they were not close to 400.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-\:1-25] An unnamed source in Donetsk told [Reuters](18998750.md) that fewer than 100 people had died in the attack.[^cite_note-\:0-23]

### Industry



Yasynivka coke plant near Makiivka



Kirov Foundry, Makeevka on a 1947 stamp

There are many coal mines in and around the city. Makiivka's modern industries include one of the largest integrated [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) and [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel) works in [Ukraine](31750.md). There are also other [metalworking](266443.md) and [coke-chemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cracking_\(chemistry\)) plants and factories for [pneumatic machinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pneumatic_machinery?action=edit\&redlink=1), [shoemaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoemaking), and food processing. The city is rather dispersed, with numerous residential communities surrounding individual industrial plants over an extensive area. It is gradually extending to form a single metropolitan area with the nearby city of [Donetsk](562951.md), which lies just a few miles to the southwest. Makiivka is home to the [Donbas National Academy of Civil Engineering and Architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas_National_Academy_of_Civil_Engineering_and_Architecture).

The largest enterprises in Makiivka are: State enterprise "Makeyevugol" - open joint-stock company "Makeyevsky Metallurgical Works" - open joint-stock company, "Yasinovsky Coke-chemical Plant", and the limited company "Makeyevcoke".

There are also many machine-building enterprises within the city, with the most significant being: open joint-stock company "Granit", open joint-stock company "Stroymash", and the closed joint-stock company "Makeyevsky Mine's Automatic Machinery plant".

#### Makiivka metallurgical plant

The Makiivka metallurgical plant produced 1.029 million tons of [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel) and 825,000 tons of [pig iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_iron) in 2005. It increased production of rolled steel 1.56-fold to over 700,000 tons in the January–July period of 2006, compared with the corresponding period of last year. It aims to increase its sales revenues to ₴1.265 billion in 2006. The [Nucor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucor) company ([United States](3434750.md)) intends to sign a contract with the Makiivka metallurgical plant on delivery of [pig iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pig_iron) to the company's enterprises in the [United States](3434750.md).

### Transport

Makiivka is crossed by several railway lines: one is the *Yasynuvata-Krynichna* line ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Ясиноватая-Криничная), and the other is the *Mospyno-Makiivka freight* line ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Моспино — Макеевка грузовая). The city also contained a [tram](30733.md) line (since 1925, but there are no tram routes now since 2006) and a [trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) system (from 1969).

### Sport

Makiivka has 5 stadiums, 4 swimming pools, 90 sport gyms, 15 [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_\(soccer\)) fields, 5 children's sport schools, and 36 fitness rooms. There is also a sport school for physically disabled people.

Within the city, 35 different forms of sport are played, and there are a total of 35 sport organisations. There are also many campuses of the [oblast](494889.md)'s sport schools in Makiivka, including schools for: [kickboxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kickboxing), [volleyball](32558.md), heavy athletics, [boxing](4243.md), some other forms of [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling), and [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo).
