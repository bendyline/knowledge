**Michael Bakari Jordan**[^cite_note-1] ([/bɑːˈkɑːri/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*bah-KAR-ee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); born February 9, 1987)[^cite_note-biography.com-2] is an American actor, producer, and director. [His accolades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_awards_and_nominations_received_by_Michael_B._Jordan) include an [Academy Award](324.md), three [Actor Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Awards), and a [Producers Guild Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Producers_Guild_of_America), in addition to nominations for a [British Academy Film Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Film_Award), a [Golden Globe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Globe_Award) and two [Emmy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Awards). Jordan was named one of the [100 most influential people in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_100) by *[Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_\(magazine\))* in 2020 and 2023,[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_\(magazine\))*'s [Sexiest Man Alive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_\(magazine\)#Sexiest_Man_Alive) in 2020,[^cite_note-5] and in 2020 was ranked 15th on *[The New York Times](30680.md)* list of the 25 greatest actors of the 21st century.[^cite_note-6]

Jordan initially gained recognition in television, playing [Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_\(The_Wire\)) in the first season of the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO) crime drama series *[The Wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wire)* (2002). He starred in the [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Broadcasting_Company) soap opera *[All My Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_My_Children)* (2003–2006) and the [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) sports drama series *[Friday Night Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_Night_Lights_\(TV_series\))* (2009–2011). He later starred in and produced the [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO) television film [*Fahrenheit 451*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit_451_\(2018_film\)) (2018), for which he was nominated for the [Primetime Emmy Award for Outstanding Television Movie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primetime_Emmy_Award_for_Outstanding_Television_Movie).

Jordan's film breakthrough came as [Oscar Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Oscar_Grant) in [Ryan Coogler](38348303.md)'s biopic *[Fruitvale Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruitvale_Station)* (2013), his performance in which received critical praise. He earned further acclaim for his performances in Coogler's subsequent films, including *[Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_\(film\))* (2015), *[Black Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_\(film\))* (2018), and *[Sinners](75895455.md)* (2025); the latter earned him the [Academy Award for Best Actor](23245410.md).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-forbes-8][^cite_note-businessinsider.com-9][^cite_note-10] Jordan reprised his role of [Donnie Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Creed) in *[Creed II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_II)* (2018) and *[Creed III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_III)* (2023), the latter of which also marked his directorial debut. His other films include *[Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicle_\(film\))* (2012), *[That Awkward Moment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/That_Awkward_Moment)* (2014), *[Fantastic Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantastic_Four_\(2015_film\))* (2015), and *[Just Mercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Mercy)* (2019).

## Early life and education

Michael Bakari Jordan was born on February 9, 1987, in [Santa Ana, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana,_California),[^cite_note-tap-dancing-11] to Donna and Michael A. Jordan, a supervisor at the [John F. Kennedy International Airport](97218.md) who later started a catering business and was involved in [Pan-Africanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Africanism).[^cite_note-12] He has an older sister and a younger brother.[^cite_note-Herzog-13] His family lived in Santa Ana for two years before moving to [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), where Jordan grew up.[^cite_note-tap-dancing-11][^cite_note-NJ-14] He attended [Newark Arts High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_Arts_High_School), where his mother worked as a teacher, and where he also played basketball.[^cite_note-Herzog-13][^cite_note-15]

## Career

### 1999–2012: Beginnings

Jordan worked as a child model for several companies and brands, including [Modell's Sporting Goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modell's_Sporting_Goods) and [Toys "R" Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toys_%22R%22_Us), before deciding to embark on a career as an actor.[^cite_note-blackfilm-16][^cite_note-17] He launched his career as a professional actor in 1999, when he appeared briefly in single episodes of the television series *[Cosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosby_\(TV_series\))* and *[The Sopranos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sopranos)*.[^cite_note-NJ-14] His first principal film role followed in 2001, when he was featured in *[Hardball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardball_\(film\))*, which starred [Keanu Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keanu_Reeves). In 2002, he gained more attention by playing the small but pivotal role of [Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallace_\(The_Wire\)) in the first season of HBO's *[The Wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Wire)*.

In March 2003, he joined the cast of *[All My Children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_My_Children)*, replacing [Chadwick Boseman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chadwick_Boseman), playing Reggie Montgomery, a troubled teenager, until June 2006, when Jordan was released from his contract.[^cite_note-18] Jordan's other credits include guest-starring appearances on *[CSI: Crime Scene Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSI:_Crime_Scene_Investigation)*,[^cite_note-19] *[Without a Trace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Without_a_Trace)*, and *[Cold Case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cold_Case)*. Thereafter, he had a lead role in the independent film *Blackout* and starred in *[The Assistants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Assistants)* on [The-N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The-N). In 2008, Jordan appeared in the music video "[Did You Wrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Did_You_Wrong)" by [rhythm and blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhythm_and_blues) artist [Pleasure P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleasure_P).



Jordan in 2011

In 2009, Jordan began starring in the [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) drama *[Friday Night Lights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_Night_Lights_\(TV_series\))* as quarterback Vince Howard, and lived in an apartment in [Austin](1998.md), where the show was filmed.[^cite_note-20] He played the character for two seasons until the show ended in 2011.[^cite_note-21] In 2009, he guest-starred on *[Burn Notice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burn_Notice)* in the episode "[Hot Spot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burn_Notice_\(season_2\))", playing a high-school football player who got into a fight and is being hunted by a local gangster. In 2010, he was considered one of the 55 faces of the future by [*Nylon*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nylon_\(magazine\)) in its "Young Hollywood" issue.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

In 2010, he guest-starred in the *[Law & Order: Criminal Intent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_&_Order:_Criminal_Intent)* episode "Inhumane Society", as a boxer involved in a [Michael Vick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Vick)–inspired [dog-fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog_fighting_in_the_United_States) scandal. That year, he landed a recurring role on the NBC show *[Parenthood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parenthood_\(2010_TV_series\))* playing Alex (Haddie Braverman's love interest).[^cite_note-24] This marked his second collaboration with [showrunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Showrunner) [Jason Katims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Katims), who was in charge of *Friday Night Lights*. [BuddyTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BuddyTV) ranked him number 80 on its list of "TV's Sexiest Men of 2011".[^cite_note-25] Jordan voiced Jace in the [Xbox 360](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_360) game *[Gears of War 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gears_of_War_3)*.[^cite_note-26] In 2012, Jordan appeared in the [George Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lucas)-produced film *[Red Tails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Tails)*,[^cite_note-27] and played lead character Steve Montgomery in *[Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronicle_\(film\))*, a film about three teenaged boys who develop superhuman abilities.[^cite_note-28] He also guest-starred in an episode of *[House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_\(TV_series\))*'s final season, playing a blind patient.[^cite_note-29]

### 2013–2018: Rise to prominence

In 2013, Jordan starred as shooting victim [Oscar Grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Oscar_Grant) in *[Fruitvale Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruitvale_Station)*, directed by [Ryan Coogler](38348303.md). His performance garnered critical acclaim, with [Todd McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_McCarthy) of *[The Hollywood Reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hollywood_Reporter)* stating that Jordan reminded him of "a young [Denzel Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denzel_Washington)".[^cite_note-30] Following his role in *Fruitvale Station*, Jordan was named an "actor to watch" by *[People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_\(magazine\))* and *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))*.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] *Time* magazine named him, with Coogler, one of 30 people under 30 who are changing the world, and he was also named one of 2013's breakout stars by *[Entertainment Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entertainment_Weekly)* and *[GQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GQ)*.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

In 2015, he starred as [Johnny Storm, the Human Torch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Torch), in *[Fantastic Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fantastic_Four_\(2015_film\))*.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] The film was universally panned by critics, holding a 9% approval rating on [Rotten Tomatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotten_Tomatoes), and was a bust at the box office.[^cite_note-39] Jordan rebounded with critical acclaim later that year when he starred as [Donnie Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donnie_Creed), the son of boxer [Apollo Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_Creed), in the spinoff of the *[Rocky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_\(film_series\))* film, *[Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_\(film\))*, his second collaboration with Coogler, which co-starred [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone).[^cite_note-40] Jordan prepared for his role as a boxer in *Creed* by undertaking one year of rigorous physical training and a stringent, low-fat diet.[^cite_note-\:0-41] He did not have a body double during filming, and was "routinely bloodied, bruised, and dizzy" when fighting scenes were being filmed.[^cite_note-\:0-41]



Jordan promoting *[Black Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_\(film\))* at the 2017 [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con)

In 2016, Jordan featured in the popular sports game *[NBA 2K17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_2K17)*, portraying Justice Young, a teammate of the [player-character](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Player_character) in the game's MyCareer mode. In October 2017, Jordan was announced as being cast in a supporting role as Mark Warren in the upcoming [Netflix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netflix) superhero series *[Raising Dion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raising_Dion)*.[^cite_note-42] In February 2018, Jordan starred as the villain [Erik Killmonger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Killmonger) in the [Marvel Cinematic Universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marvel_Cinematic_Universe) film *[Black Panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther_\(film\))*; this marked Jordan's third collaboration with Coogler.[^cite_note-43] His performance in *Black Panther* received critical acclaim, with Dani Di Placido of *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* stating that Jordan "steals the show", while Jason Guerrasio of *[Business Insider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_Insider)* wrote that the actor "plays a Killmonger fueled with hate and emptiness – we won't give away why – but he also delivers it with a swagger that's just a joy to watch ... the movie takes off more in story and viewing enjoyment whenever Jordan is on screen."[^cite_note-forbes-8][^cite_note-businessinsider.com-9] In an interview with *The Hollywood Reporter,* Jordan shared how his preparation for the role of Killmonger left an impact on him, for which he sought out therapy.[^cite_note-44]

Later in 2018, Jordan starred in *[Fahrenheit 451](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit_451_\(2018_film\))* with [Michael Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Shannon) and [Sofia Boutella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia_Boutella). The television film was distributed on [HBO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO) by [HBO Films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Films).[^cite_note-45] That same year, Jordan reprised his role as boxer Adonis "Donnie" Creed in *[Creed II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_II)*, a sequel to *[Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_\(film\))* (2015) and the eighth installment in the [*Rocky* film series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_\(film_series\)). *Creed II* was released in the United States by [Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-Goldwyn-Mayer) on November 21, 2018. The film received generally positive reviews from critics, and it went on to debut to \$35.3 million in its opening weekend (a five-day total of \$55.8 million), marking the biggest debut ever for a live-action release over Thanksgiving.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47]

### 2019–present: Established actor and awards success

He also voiced the character Julian Chase in [Rooster Teeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooster_Teeth)'s animated series *[Gen\:Lock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen\:Lock)*, since January 2019, which he also co-produced through his production company, Outlier Society Productions.[^cite_note-48] Jordan portrayed attorney [Bryan Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Stevenson) in a legal drama, *[Just Mercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just_Mercy)*, which he also co-produced. The film, based on a real-life story, was released in December 2019 to positive reviews.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Jordan starred in *[Without Remorse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Without_Remorse_\(film\))*, based on the [book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Without_Remorse) by [Tom Clancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Clancy), as [John Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Clark_\(Ryanverse_character\)), a former Navy SEAL and director of the elite counterterrorism unit Rainbow Six. Originally planned for release on September 18, 2020, it was released on April 30, 2021, due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-51]

Jordan reprised his Marvel role as Erik "Killmonger" Stevens in two episodes of the first season of *[What If...?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/What_If...%3F_\(TV_series\))* (2021), and in *[Black Panther: Wakanda Forever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Panther:_Wakanda_Forever)* (2022),[^cite_note-What_If...?-52][^cite_note-53] and made a cameo appearance in *[Space Jam: A New Legacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Jam:_A_New_Legacy)* (2021). He also starred in *[A Journal for Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Journal_for_Jordan)* (2021), directed by [Denzel Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denzel_Washington), as a soldier who "kept a journal full of poignant life lessons for their newborn son, Jordan, while deployed overseas."[^cite_note-54] Jordan made his [directorial debut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_directorial_debuts) with *[Creed III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_III)*, a sequel to *Creed II*, in addition to producing and reprising his starring role as boxer Adonis "Donnie" Creed. It was released on March 3, 2023.[^cite_note-\:1-55] He next reteamed with Coogler in the period horror film *[Sinners](75895455.md)* (2025), playing twin brothers in a [dual role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_role) performance. He received the [Academy Award for Best Actor](23245410.md) and the [Actor Award in the same category](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actor_Award_for_Outstanding_Performance_by_a_Male_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role), becoming the seventh [Black performer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_black_Academy_Award_winners_and_nominees#Best_Actor_in_a_Leading_Role) in history to win an Oscar in the lead acting category.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

Jordan is slated to work with Coogler for the fifth time in *Wrong Answer*, a film based on the [Atlanta Public Schools cheating scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlanta_Public_Schools_cheating_scandal).[^cite_note-\:0-41] He is also set to direct, produce, and star in a second remake of *[The Thomas Crown Affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Thomas_Crown_Affair_\(2027_film\))*,[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] as well as starring in the vampire film *Blood Brothers*.[^cite_note-61] His production company Outlier Society signed a first-look deal with Amazon,[^cite_note-62] and is also developing *[Val-Zod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-Zod)*, an [HBO Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HBO_Max) series featuring a black version of the [DC Comics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DC_Comics) character [Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman).[^cite_note-63] As of March 2022, Jordan was to produce and star in the sequel of *[I Am Legend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Am_Legend_\(film\))* with [Will Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Smith).[^cite_note-64]

In March 2026, Prime Video has officially ordered *Delphi*, a new Michael B. Jordan–produced drama series set in the *Rocky*/*Creed* universe that follows young fighters training at the iconic Delphi Boxing Academy from the films.[^cite_note-65]

In April 2026, Jordan is announced to produce and may star in the film adaptation of the Electronic Arts video game, *Battlefield.*[^cite_note-66]

In May 2026, Deadline announced Jordan will be executive producing the adaptation of Rebecca Yarros' bestselling novel *[Fourth Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Wing)* is moving forward with a series order by Prime Video.[^cite_note-67]

## Personal life



Jordan in 2018

Jordan has resided in Los Angeles since 2006.[^cite_note-68] He grew up in a religious household and considers himself to be spiritual.[^cite_note-69] As of 2018, he lived with his parents in a [Sherman Oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherman_Oaks) home that he purchased.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

Jordan is a fan of [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime), citing *[Naruto: Shippuden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naruto:_Shippuden)* and the *[Dragon Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball)* franchise as his favorite series and *[Princess Mononoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princess_Mononoke)* as his favorite film.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] Having grown up in [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), he is a lifelong supporter of the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants).[^cite_note-75] He is also a supporter of [Premier League](11250.md) club [Everton FC](91155.md).[^cite_note-76]

Michael B. Jordan considers [Tom Cruise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Cruise) and [Will Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Will_Smith) his career idols, citing their ability to build global careers and become movie stars. Jordan has also sought advice from Smith and Cruise and has received endorsements from Cruise, indicating a mentorship and respect among the three actors.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

Jordan cites [Ryan Coogler](38348303.md), [Steven Spielberg](26940.md), [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese), [Alejandro G. Iñárritu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Gonz%C3%A1lez_I%C3%B1%C3%A1rritu), [Stanley Kubrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Kubrick), [Alfonso Cuarón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_Cuar%C3%B3n), [George Lucas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Lucas), and [Christopher Nolan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Nolan) as his influences in film making.[^cite_note-80]

In November 2020, Jordan began dating model [Lori Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lori_Harvey), adoptive daughter of television personality [Steve Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Harvey).[^cite_note-81] In June 2022, the couple announced they had ended their relationship.[^cite_note-82]

In December 2023, Jordan wrecked his [Ferrari 812 Superfast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_812_Superfast) when he crashed it into a parked [Kia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kia) on [Sunset Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset_Boulevard) while racing a [Ferrari 488](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_488). He was uninjured and police investigated the incident for street racing.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] Another driver who was near the incident said they were "pacing" but insists they were not racing.[^cite_note-86] Jordan was eventually not charged "due to lack of evidence".[^cite_note-87] His 812 was later bought by Youtuber [TheStradman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheStradman) in March 2024.[^cite_note-88]

### Sports ownership

In December 2022, Jordan was announced as part-owner of English soccer club [AFC Bournemouth](451121.md). The club was taken over by the consortium group the Black Knights Football Club, led by fellow American businessman [Bill Foley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Foley_\(businessman\)).[^cite_note-89] Jordan led the minority ownership group with Kosmos founder Nullah Sarker.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

He is also an investor in the [Alpine F1 Team](65240620.md) alongside fellow actors [Ryan Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds) and [Rob McElhenney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_McElhenney).[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]
