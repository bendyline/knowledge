The **Farabundo Martí National Liberation Front** ([Spanish](26825.md): *Frente Farabundo Martí para la Liberación Nacional*, abbreviated **FMLN**) is a [Salvadoran](9356.md) [left-wing](18499.md) [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party) and former [guerrilla rebel group](12720.md).

The FMLN was established on 10 October 1980 as the merged of four [leftist](18499.md) guerrilla groups: the [Farabundo Martí Popular Liberation Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farabundo_Mart%C3%AD_Popular_Liberation_Forces) (FPL), the [People's Revolutionary Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Revolutionary_Army_\(El_Salvador\)) (ERP), the [National Resistance Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Resistance_Armed_Forces) (FARN), and the [Armed Liberation Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Liberation_Forces?action=edit\&redlink=1)/[Communist Party of El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_El_Salvador) (FAL/PCES); the [Revolutionary Party of the Central American Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Party_of_the_Central_American_Workers_%E2%80%93_El_Salvador) (PRTC) joined in December 1980. In 1981, the FMLN joined forces with the [Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Democratic_Front) (FDR) coalition of [mass organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_organization) and were sometimes collectively referred to as the **FMLN–FDR**. The FMLN fought against the Salvadoran government during [the country's civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Civil_War) that lasted from 1979 to 1992. The FMLN launched three major offensives against the government in [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_offensive_of_1981_\(El_Salvador\)), [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_offensive_of_1982?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_offensive_of_1989). The group signed the [Chapultepec Peace Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapultepec_Peace_Accords) with the government that ended the civil war. As a part of the peace accords, the FMLN was [demobilized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demobilization) and registered as a political party.

For three decades from the mid 1990s to the late 2010s, the FMLN dominated Salvadoran politics in a [two-party system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-party_system) alongside the right-wing [Nationalist Republican Alliance](542175.md) (ARENA). The party often finished in first or second place in the country's legislative and municipal elections. The FMLN lost the first three presidential elections it participated in, when it nominated various former civil war commanders. The party won the [2009 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Salvadoran_presidential_election) when it nominated journalist [Mauricio Funes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Funes) for [President](466532.md). Funes was succeeded in 2014 by Vice President [Salvador Sánchez Cerén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_S%C3%A1nchez_Cer%C3%A9n), a former guerrilla commander who narrowly won the [2014 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Salvadoran_presidential_election). The FMLN lost the next two presidential elections, and after the [2024 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Salvadoran_legislative_election), it lost all its seats in the [Legislative Assembly](1608500.md). Throughout the FMLN's history as a political party, several splinter parties have emerged from it such as the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(El_Salvador\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Renewal Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewal_Movement), and the [Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Democratic_Front_\(2006\)) (unrelated to the civil war-era group).

The FMLN is El Salvador's largest left-wing political party. During the civil war, the FMLN espoused [Marxism–Leninism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism) but moderated its position between 1989 and 1991 to embrace [democratic socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialism). The FMLN is a member of the [Parliamentary Group of the Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Group_of_the_Left) political faction in the [Central American Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Parliament). It is also a member of the [São Paulo Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_Forum) and [COPPPAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COPPPAL), two alliances of leftist Latin American political parties. [Manuel Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Flores_\(Salvadoran_politician\)), the FMLN's [2024 presidential candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Salvadoran_presidential_election) and a former legislator, has served as the party's [secretary-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary-general) since August 2024. As of 2024, the FMLN has around 16,000 members. In recent years, it has received criticism from leftist voters for corruption and perception of being out of touch with its base, as well as seeing a collapse in legislative seats during the [2024 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Salvadoran_legislative_election).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemán2024-1]

## History

### Background

#### Splintering of leftist groups during the 1970s

The [Communist Party of El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_El_Salvador) (PCES) was established in the 1930.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWhite197397-2] In 1970, PCES general-secretary [Cayetano Carpio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayetano_Carpio) split from the party and founded the [Farabundo Martí Popular Liberation Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farabundo_Mart%C3%AD_Popular_Liberation_Forces) (FPL)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990237-3] as he viewed the party's reliance on the electoral process to transform [El Salvador](9356.md) as futile;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016719-4] he instead called for a "[prolonged popular war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_war)" modeled on the [Viet Cong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viet_Cong) to overthrow [the military dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dictatorship_in_El_Salvador)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990237-3] that had ruled El Salvador since 1931.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChing19972–3_&_491-5] In 1971, the [People's Revolutionary Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Revolutionary_Army_\(El_Salvador\)) (ERP), led by [Joaquín Villalobos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaqu%C3%ADn_Villalobos), was established by dissidents of the PCES, FPL, and [Christian Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Party_\(El_Salvador\)) (PDC). Villalobos called for a popular insurrection modeled on the [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) (FSLN).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990237-3]

Throughout the 1970s, the FPL and ERP carried out [hit-and-run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit-and-run) attacks against the Salvadoran government, and allied [mass organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_organization) organized [protests](266137.md) and [strikes](155134.md) to create conditions that would start a [revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty199030-6][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELeoGrandeRobbins19801088-7] In 1975, [José Sancho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferm%C3%A1n_Cienfuegos) established the [National Resistance Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Resistance_Armed_Forces) (FARN) as a splinter group from the ERP after Villalobos ordered the [assassination](2963.md) of [Roque Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_Dalton)—the leader of the ERP's political wing.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990237–238-8] In 1977, PCES and FARN dissidents led by [Francisco Jovel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Jovel_\(communist\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) created the [Revolutionary Party of the Central American Workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Party_of_the_Central_American_Workers_%E2%80%93_El_Salvador) (PRTC).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990238-9] In 1979, [Schafik Hándal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schafik_H%C3%A1ndal), Carpio's successor as PCES general-secretary, established the [Armed Liberation Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Liberation_Forces?action=edit\&redlink=1) (FAL) as the PCES's militant wing after the party adopted an "armed struggle" doctrine under Hándal's leadership.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990239-10]

On 15 October 1979, the [reformist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformist) elements of the [Armed Forces of El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_El_Salvador) (FAES) overthrew General [Carlos Humberto Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Humberto_Romero), the [president of El Salvador](466532.md), in [a coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Salvadoran_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) and established the civil-military [Revolutionary Government Junta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Government_Junta_of_El_Salvador) (JRG).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty199034-11] Some leftist groups such as the PCES initially supported the JRG while others such as the ERP opposed it and renewed calls for an [insurrection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurrection). Eventually, negotiations between the JRG and some communist groups fell apart after Colonel [Adolfo Arnoldo Majano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolfo_Arnoldo_Majano) lost his influence in the JRG and the junta itself being unable to curb violence by far-right [death squads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_squads_in_El_Salvador).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty199039-12]

#### Negotiations and unification

In December 1979, Salvadoran leftist leaders met in [Havana](49719.md), Cuba to negotiate the establishment of a united coalition to oppose the JRG. They viewed the FSLN's victory in the [Nicaraguan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Revolution) as a sign that they needed to set aside ideological differences and create a united [vanguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanguard) to launch a successful revolution. Cuban leader [Fidel Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidel_Castro) personally took part in the negotiations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty199039–40-13] In May 1980, the FPL, FARN, and FAL/PCES established the [Unified Revolutionary Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Revolutionary_Directorate?action=edit\&redlink=1) (DRU) as a united coalition for political and military planning.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990240-14] DRU initially excluded the ERP due to lingering animosity over Dalton's assassination, but it later allowed the ERP to join following pressure from Castro.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016719-4]

On 10 October 1980,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPaszyn1996137-15] the DRU established the Farabundo Martí National Liberation Front (FMLN), a merger of the DRU's members and the [Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Democratic_Front) (FDR), a coalition of the DRU member's affiliated mass organizations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty199040_&_240-16] The FMLN and FDR were sometimes referred to collectively as the Farabundo Martí National Liberation Front–Revolutionary Democratic Front (FMLN–FDR).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990xx-17] The FMLN was an umbrella group consisting of the DRU groups as its militant wing and the FDR as its political front.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990240-14] The FMLN was named after [Farabundo Martí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farabundo_Mart%C3%AD), a peasant and communist leader who led a rebellion against the government of Brigadier General [Maximiliano Hernández Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximiliano_Hern%C3%A1ndez_Mart%C3%ADnez) in 1932 and was executed during subsequent reprisal killings known as *[La Matanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Matanza)*[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhanem202397-18] that killed up to 40,000 people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETulchinBland1992167-19] The PRTC later joined the FMLN in December 1980.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016719-4] The FMLN was governed by five-member executive directorate that consisted of the [Salvador Sánchez Cerén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_S%C3%A1nchez_Cer%C3%A9n) (representing the FPL), Villalobos (ERP), Sancho (FARN), Hándal (FAL/PCES), and Jovel (PRTC).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990240-14]

### Guerrilla coalition (1980–1992)

#### Early military offensives (1980–1983)

The FMLN launched its first military offensive—known as the [final offensive of 1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_offensive_of_1981_\(El_Salvador\))—on 10 January 1981 when it carried out 43 attacks against various government and military positions including the [Ilopango International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilopango_International_Airport) and the headquarters of the [Treasury Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_Police_\(El_Salvador\)?action=edit\&redlink=1). Carpio issued *FMLN General Order Number 1* that called on the Salvadoran people to join the FMLN in a national uprising.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBosch199983-20] Although the FMLN had captured 82 cities and villages, mostly in northern El Salvador, by 17 January,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrandall2016177-21] the JRG launched a counteroffensive and Hándal called for a "temporary tactical retreat".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBosch1999100-22] By 26 January, the offensive failed and the FMLN acknowledged that it did not spark the national uprising it hoped for.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBosch1999102-23] Although the offensive failed, the FMLN proved itself to be a competent fighting force and retained control of some territory.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrandall2016177-21]

On 28 March 1982, the date of the [1982 Constitutional Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Salvadoran_Constitutional_Assembly_election), the FMLN launched the [general offensive of 1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_offensive_of_1982?action=edit\&redlink=1) that intended to disrupt the election's conduct, but the offensive failed to achieve this goal[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBosch1999110-24] and all 60 seats on the Constitutional Assembly were decided.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKrennerich2005281-25] That year, many FMLN leaders believed that a negotiated settlement was the best way to obtain their goals, but this approach was strongly rejected by Carpio who described it as a betrayal of the revolution.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhanem202398–99-26] In January 1983, the FPL Central Committee voted Carpio out of power and replaced him with [Mélida Anaya Montes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Mar%C3%ADa). In April 1983, Carpio had Montes assassinated in [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua), Nicaragua and he committed [suicide](16831059.md) days later when the assassins were captured.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990241-27][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECentral_Intelligence_Agency198410-28]

Map of FMLN members' controlled territories and five fronts during the civil war

Carpio's suicide diminished the FPL's influence within the FMLN and the coalition's members moved towards further cooperation with each other led by the ERP.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990241-27] The FMLN divided its forces into five fronts: the Feliciano Ama Western Front (in [Ahuachapán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahuachap%C3%A1n_Department), [La Libertad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Libertad_Department_\(El_Salvador\)), [Santa Ana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana_Department), and [Sonsonate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonsonate_Department)), the Anastasio Aquino Para-Central Front (in [San Vicente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Vicente_Department) and parts of [Cabañas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caba%C3%B1as_Department) and [La Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Paz_Department_\(El_Salvador\))), the Modesto Ramírez Central Front (in [Cuscatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuscatl%C3%A1n_Department), [San Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Salvador_Department), and parts of Cabañas and La Paz), the Francisco Sánchez Eastern Front (in [La Unión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Uni%C3%B3n_Department), [Morazán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moraz%C3%A1n_Department), [San Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel_Department_\(El_Salvador\)), and [Usulután](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usulut%C3%A1n_Department)), and the Apolinario Serrano Northern Front (in [Chalatenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalatenango_Department)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990242–243-29]

#### Peace negotiations (1983–1989)

A black-and-white photograph of an FMLN solidarity meeting in Halle (Saale)

A 1982 FMLN solidarity meeting in [Halle (Saale)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_\(Saale\)), East Germany

Carpio's death also led to the FMLN approaching the Salvadoran government to enter negotiations ahead of the [1984 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Salvadoran_presidential_election) in the hopes of joining a [coalition government](6036.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhanem202399-30] In 1983, the FMLN entered private negotiations with the government of President [Álvaro Magaña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Maga%C3%B1a).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990244–245-31] After the 1984 election, the FMLN entered open negotiations with the PDC government of President [José Napoleón Duarte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Napole%C3%B3n_Duarte).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhanem202399-30] The FMLN and Duarte's government held several meetings between 1984 and 1988 but were unable to come to an agreement to end the civil war due to continued FMLN attacks.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990245-32]

While in negotiations, the FMLN continued to carry out hit-and-run attacks against the government including assassinations, [kidnappings](169376.md), and bombings. FMLN militants particularly attacked American military personnel[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990242_&_244-33] as the [United States](3434750.md) was supporting the Salvadoran government with weapons, supplies, and advisors.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcClintock1985326-34] The FMLN also destroyed infrastructure, blockaded public transportation, and imposed "war taxes" on businesses (such as kidnappings and bombings) that cost the Salvadoran government US\$2 billion between 1979 and 1988.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990244-35]

Throughout the civil war, Cuba trained hundreds of FMLN personnel and Nicaragua subsidized the FMLN's operation of *[Radio Venceremos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Venceremos)*, the group's clandestine radio station.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEColl198511–13-36] There were unsubstantiated rumors during the civil war of direct support from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) such as an alleged sighting of a Soviet submarine near [La Unión](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Uni%C3%B3n,_El_Salvador) or a Soviet soldier in [Chalatenango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalatenango,_El_Salvador).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBosch199968-37] The FMLN received materiel support from communist countries such as [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Bulgaria), [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Socialist_Republic), [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), [Ethiopia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derg), [Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_People's_Republic), the Soviet Union, and [Vietnam](202354.md), much of which was sent to the FMLN through Nicaragua.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhanem202398-38][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEColl198511_&_16-39] The FMLN also received financial support from sympathetic solidarity committees in the United States, [Mexico](3966054.md), and [Europe](9239.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhanem202398-38] FMLN membership varied throughout the civil war: it was as low as 4,000 members in 1981[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBosch199961-40] to as high as 15,000 members in 1991.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAcostaRogers2020379-41]

In December 1987, [Rubén Zamora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub%C3%A9n_Zamora) and [Guillermo Ungo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Ungo), two FDR leaders, announced the creation of [Democratic Convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Convergence_\(El_Salvador\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (CVD), a left-wing political party formed from the merger of the [Popular Social Christian Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Social_Christian_Movement?action=edit\&redlink=1) (MPSC), the [National Revolutionary Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Revolutionary_Movement_\(El_Salvador\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (MNR), and the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(El_Salvador\)) (PSD).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990171–172-42] The CVD was allowed to participate in the [1989 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Salvadoran_presidential_election) with Ungo as its presidential candidate,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990xxiv-43] and the CVD's electoral participation was approved by the FMLN.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990172-44] Ungo finished in fourth place with 3.8 percent of the vote.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKrennerich2005290-45]

#### Last offensive and peace accords (1989–1992)

A black-and-white photograph of guerrilla combatants

ERP combatants in [Perquín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perqu%C3%ADn) in 1990

Duarte was succeeded by [Alfredo Cristiani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Cristiani) of the right-wing [Nationalist Republican Alliance](542175.md) (ARENA), the winner of the 1989 election. Cristiani was more moderate than ARENA's previous nominee, Major [Roberto D'Aubuisson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_D'Aubuisson), and was open to continuing negotiations[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhanem202399-30] because he believed that FAES was unable to induce a decisive victory against the FMLN to end the civil war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChávez20151788-46] In November 1989, the FMLN launched the [final offensive of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_offensive_of_1989) in a bid to overthrow Cristiani's government and ignite a popular uprising, but once again the offensive failed and the popular uprising did not materialize. Although the FMLN was weakened by the military defeat together with the FSLN's electoral defeat in the [1990 Nicaraguan general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Nicaraguan_general_election), the [United States Congress](31756.md) voted to reduce funding to the Salvadoran government which also put it in a weaker position. In April 1990, the FMLN and Cristiani's government resumed peace negotiations mediated by the [United Nations](31769.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhanem202399-30] Additionally, by 1991, the FMLN abandoned Marxism–Leninism in favor of [democratic socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialism) as the group received diplomatic support from European and Latin American social democratic governments that pressured it to negotiate peace.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChávez20151787–1788-47]

On 16 January 1992, the FMLN and Salvadoran government signed the [Chapultepec Peace Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapultepec_Peace_Accords) that ended the civil war. The peace accords implemented various political and military reforms within the Salvadoran government. In exchange, the FMLN agreed to demobilize, being subsequently legalized as a [political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChávez20151793-48][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhanem2023100-49] According to the 1990s [Truth Commission for El Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truth_Commission_for_El_Salvador), around 5 percent of [human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses) during the civil war were committed by the FMLN, in contrast to 85 percent committed by the government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChávez20151792–1793-50] According to the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh)'s Mitchell A. Seligson and Vincent McElhinny, between 58,382 and 92,823 people were killed during the civil war, of whom, between 12,274 and 23,840 were FMLN combatants.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESeligsonMcElhinny1996214–217-51]

### Political party (1992–present)

#### Transition to a political party (1992–1994)

On 30 June 1992, the Legislative Assembly formally legalized the FMLN, and on 14 December, the [Supreme Electoral Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Electoral_Court_\(El_Salvador\)) (TSE) registered the FMLN as a political party.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPeace_Accords_Matrix-52] Although the FMLN was now a registered political party, its now-demobilized component militant groups continued to exist.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAcostaRogers2020380-53] The ERP (rebranded as the Renewed Expression of the People) and FARN wanted to moderate the FMLN and move it towards [social democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy). Conversely, the FPL and PCES criticized those who wanted to moderate the FMLN, and PCES member [Dagoberto Gutiérrez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagoberto_Guti%C3%A9rrez) argued that the FMLN should continue the civil war but as an opposition political party.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016720–721-54]

Ahead of the [1994 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Salvadoran_general_election), the first election held after the end of the civil war,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court19945-55] the ERP and FARN proposed that the party should nominate a [center-right](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-right_politics) reformist politician for president, but the FPL, PCES, and PRTC rejected this and instead nominated Zamora with [Francisco Roberto Lima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Roberto_Lima) as his running mate.[^cite_note-1994c-56][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016720–721-54] Zamora advanced to the [second round](25932.md) where he lost to ARENA's [Armando Calderón Sol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armando_Calder%C3%B3n_Sol) with 31.7 percent of the vote.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKrennerich2005290-45] The FMLN also won 21 of 84 seats in the [Legislative Assembly](1608500.md) (surpassing the PDC as the legislature's second largest political party),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court199443–50-57]15 of 262 [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_El_Salvador),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court199451-58] and 4 of El Salvador's 20 seats in the [Central American Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_Parliament) (PARLACEN).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court199437-59] The FMLN was most successful in territories it controlled during the civil war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAcostaRogers2020380-53]

#### In opposition and political splits (1994–2009)

The FMLN's elected officials assumed office in May 1994. The FMLN's legislators affiliated with the ERP and FARN voted in favor of ARENA's national budget resulting in the party suspending their membership. The ERP and FARN also wanted to dissolve the FMLN's five lingering factions, but the FPL, PCES, and PRTC rejected this. In December, 7 legislators and 14 mayors affiliated with the ERP and FARN left the FMLN and later founded the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(El_Salvador\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (PD) in March 1995.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016721-60] That same year, the FPL, PCES, and PRTC dissolved and fully merged into the FMLN.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAcostaRogers2020380-53]

In the [1997 legislative and municipal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Salvadoran_legislative_election), the FMLN won 27 seats in the Legislative Assembly[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court199737-61] and 49 municipalities.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court199745–48-62] [San Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Salvador), El Salvador's capital city, was among the FMLN's municipal victories.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAcostaRogers2020380-53] Meanwhile, the PD lost all its seats and was deregistered by the [Supreme Electoral Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Electoral_Court_\(El_Salvador\)) (TSE) for its poor performance, and the FMLN recuperated the seats that it had lost when the PD split from it. Ahead of the [1999 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Salvadoran_presidential_election), FMLN leaders such as Hándal and Sánchez Cerén wanted to maintain the party's "orthodox" [revolutionary socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_socialism) identity while leaders such as Jovel, [Facundo Guardado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facundo_Guardado?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Raúl Mijango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C3%BAl_Mijango?action=edit\&redlink=1) wanted to make the party resemble European parties that supported social democracy. Jovel, Guardado, and Mijango represented the party's [Renewal Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewal_Movement) (MR).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016722–723-63] The party ultimately nominated Guardado[^cite_note-1999c-65] and he finished in second behind ARENA's [Francisco Flores Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Flores_P%C3%A9rez) with 29 percent of the vote.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court199975-66] In the [2000 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Salvadoran_legislative_election), the FMLN became the legislature's largest party winning 31 seats.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court2000104-67]

A photograph of Schafik Hándal's supporters

A woman at an FMLN 2004 presidential campaign rally

The FMLN held a party convention in May 2001 to hold its internal leadership election, but Guardado and Jovel boycotted the revolutionary socialist-dominated convention and held their own rival convention to get social democrats elected to party leadership positions. For this, the FMLN expelled both from the party in October. In March 2002, they and five FMLN legislators registered the Renewal Movement as a political party with the TSE. At the [2003 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Salvadoran_legislative_election), the FMLN won 31 seats in the Legislative Assembly while the MR failed to surpass the 3 percent threshold as had its party registration canceled by the TSE.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016723-68]

Ahead of the [2004 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Salvadoran_presidential_election), Hándal vetoed the pre-candidacy of journalist [Mauricio Funes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Funes) as he was not a historic FMLN member and defeated reformist [Óscar Ortiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Ortiz_\(Salvadoran_politician\)) in the primary election.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELópez_Bernal2016113-69][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016724-70] Hándal lost the presidential election to ARENA's [Antonio Saca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Saca) with 35.7 percent of the vote.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court2004110-71] The revolutionary socialists winning the FMLN's 2004 internal leadership election led to another split in the party. In June 2005, 7 FMLN legislators left the party and formed the [Revolutionary Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Democratic_Front_\(2006\)) (FDR, no relation to the civil war-era organization). In the [2006 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Salvadoran_legislative_election), the FDR lost all its seats in the Legislative Assembly[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016724-70] while the FMLN won 32 seats.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court2006226_&_228-72]

Ahead of the 2009 elections, Ortiz proposed Funes as the FMLN's presidential candidate. The party's leadership accepted Funes' candidacy on the condition that Sánchez Cerén was his running mate.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELópez_Bernal2016113–114-73] Critics such as ARENA's candidate, [Rodrigo Ávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodrigo_%C3%81vila), claimed that Sánchez Cerén would be the [power behind the throne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_behind_the_throne) and that Funes would be little more than a puppet.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBooth2009-74][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBremerBarrera2009-75] The FMLN won 35 seats in the [January legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Salvadoran_legislative_election);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court2009196-76] Funes won the [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Salvadoran_presidential_election) two months later with 51.3 percent of the vote.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court200987-77] Funes' victory coincided with the Latin American [pink tide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_tide).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoodfriend2019-78] The FDR performed poorly in the same election and was deregistered by the TSE.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016724-70]

#### Control of the presidency (2009–2019)

A photograph of Mauricio Funes wearing the presidential sash with Salvador Sánchez Cerén

[Mauricio Funes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Funes) (wearing the [presidential sash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_sash)) and [Salvador Sánchez Cerén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_S%C3%A1nchez_Cer%C3%A9n), the two FMLN presidents of El Salvador, 2015

Funes assumed office on 1 June 2009[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoodfriend2019-78] becoming the FMLN's first president after 15 years of participating in elections.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016724–725-79] Some of Funes' presidential acts included reestablishing diplomatic relations with Cuba,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFrance_242009-80] implementing social reforms and anti-poverty programs,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECenter_for_Democracy_in_the_Americas20097–8-81] and apologizing for past state atrocities from the civil war[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalkin2010-82][^cite_note-FOOTNOTERenteria2010-83] and other eras of Salvadoran history.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEl_Salvador_Noticias2010-84] Additionally, the FMLN entered into a coalition government with the [National Coalition Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Coalition_Party_\(El_Salvador\)) (PCN) and [Grand Alliance for National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Alliance_for_National_Unity) (GANA) in the Legislative Assembly, and in February 2011, the FMLN's [Sigfrido Reyes Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigfrido_Reyes_Morales) became the [president of the Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Legislative_Assembly_of_El_Salvador) as a part of the coalition agreement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaires_Quezada2011-85] Funes' coalition with the right-wing GANA and PCN was criticized by more hardline FMLN members.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUlloa2010-86] The FMLN–PCN–GANA coalition lost control of the legislature after the [2012 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Salvadoran_legislative_and_local_elections)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEl_Faro2012-87] but negotiated with ARENA to create a grand coalition government, and Reyes retained his position as president of the legislature.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAguilarLabradorCarías2012-88]

Funes' government implemented a [controversial truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%932014_Salvadoran_gang_truce) with the country's largest [street gangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_gang), [MS-13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS-13) and [18th Street gang](293711.md), in a bid to lower the country's homicide rate. Mijango and Funes' defense minister, Divisional General [David Munguía Payés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Mungu%C3%ADa_Pay%C3%A9s), were two of the truce's principal negotiators,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECerón2023-89][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELooft2012-90] but Sánchez Cerén denied that the FMLN itself was responsible for the truce.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECórdoba2014-91] The truce was a major campaign topic during the [2014 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Salvadoran_presidential_election).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERobbins2013-92] The FMLN nominated Sánchez Cerén and Ortiz as its presidential and vice presidential candidates, respectively.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016725-93] Sánchez Cerén narrowly defeated ARENA's [Norman Quijano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Quijano) in the second round with 50.1 percent of the vote.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court2014144-94] Gutiérrez split from the FMLN in 2013 and attempted to get the splinter New Country Movement (MNP) registered with the TSE in time for the election, but the party failed to accumulate enough signatures to be registered.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016725-93]

Sánchez Cerén was inaugurated on 1 June 2014 becoming the first former guerrilla to serve as President of El Salvador.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBBC_News2014-95] During his presidency, he attempted to remove restrictions on [El Salvador's abortion ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abortion_in_El_Salvador) (but this was blocked by right-wing parties),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEL'Orient–Le_Jour2017-96] banned [metal mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_in_El_Salvador) for environmental and public health concerns,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDeutsche_Welle2017-97] and established [diplomatic relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93El_Salvador_relations) with [China](5405.md) over [Taiwan](25734.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHorton2018-98] The gang truce also collapsed by the time Sánchez Cerén assumed office[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUnivision2014-99] and homicides spiked during his presidency, peaking at a rate of 104 homicides per 100,000 people in 2015.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReed2016-100] The FMLN retained control of the Legislative Assembly after the [2015 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Salvadoran_legislative_election); it formed a coalition with GANA and [Lorena Peña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorena_Pe%C3%B1a) served as the legislature's president from 2015 to 2016 as a part of the coalition agreement.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELa_Prensa_Gráfica2015-101][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDiario_Co_Latino2016-102]

In 2017, the FMLN expelled [Nayib Bukele](46452145.md), then the [mayor of San Salvador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_San_Salvador), from the party after allegedly throwing an apple at a fellow party member.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELaguan2017-103] During the [2018 legislative and municipal elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Salvadoran_legislative_election), Bukele called on his supporters to [spoil their vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoilt_vote) or not vote instead of voting for the FMLN.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECohen2024137-104] The FMLN had its worst electoral showing since 1994 and won only 23 seats. Meanwhile, ARENA gained control of the Legislative Assembly and right-wing parties were able to override Sánchez Cerén's presidential veto.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoodfriend2018-105] The FMLN's [Hugo Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Mart%C3%ADnez_\(politician\)) finished in third place in the [2019 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Salvadoran_presidential_election) behind ARENA's [Carlos Calleja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Calleja) and GANA's Bukele, who won the election as a [third-party candidate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-party_candidate).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPalumboMalkin2019-106] After Bukele's victory, Roberto Lorenzana, the presidential communications secretary, stated that the FMLN's expulsion of Bukele was a mistake that cost the party votes.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEÚltima_Hora2019-107]

#### Return to opposition (2019–present)

A photograph of an FMLN campaign rally

An FMLN campaign rally ahead of the 2024 elections

Bukele's 2019 victory ended the FMLN–ARENA [duopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-party_system) on controlling the presidency.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGans201986-108][^cite_note-FOOTNOTENagovitch2019-109][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZainoHerrera20243-110] The FMLN opposed Bukele's government in the Legislative Assembly. In February 2021, FMLN and ARENA legislators briefly considered removing Bukele from office for being "mentally unfit" after he accused the FMLN of staging the deaths of two of its supporters at a rally prior to [that month's midterm election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Salvadoran_legislative_election).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarrison2021-111][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlemán2021-112] In the election, Bukele's allies won a [supermajority](638313.md) in the Legislative Assembly[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarrison2022-113] while the FMLN won only 4 seats.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court2021213-114]

The FMLN remained in the opposition for the duration of the 2021–2024 legislative term.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarrison2022-113] The party opposed various electoral and constitutional reforms ahead of the 2024 elections such as the TSE allowing Bukele to seek immediate re-election,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELemus2023-115] the reduction of seats in the Legislative Assembly from 84 to 60, and the reduction of municipalities from 262 to 44. Ortiz, then the secretary-general of the FMLN, described the reductions as "institutional fraud" by Bukele's government.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPeñate2023-116] The FMLN nominated former legislator [Manuel Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Flores_\(Salvadoran_politician\)) for president;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarrison2024-117] he [finished in second place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Salvadoran_presidential_election) with 6.4 percent of the vote behind Bukele.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEl_Mundo2024-118] In the [legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Salvadoran_legislative_election), the FMLN lost all its seats in the Legislative Assembly becoming an [extraparliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra-parliamentary_opposition) party for the first time since participating in elections in 1994.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEContra_Punto2024-119] It furthermore failed to win any municipal races.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECampos_Madrid2024-120]

On 26 July 2026, the FMLN held its primary election to select candidates for the 2027 [presidential](80593140.md), [legislative, and municipal](76008090.md) elections. The FMLN selected physician [Rafael Aguirre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Aguirre_\(physician\)) and nurse Madaí Santos as its presidential and vice presidential candidates, respectively.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEl_Mundo2026-121]

## Ideology

### Civil war-era ideology

A photograph of Salvador Sánchez Cerén in front of a mural of Schafik Hándal

[Salvador Sánchez Cerén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_S%C3%A1nchez_Cer%C3%A9n) with a mural of [Schafik Hándal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schafik_H%C3%A1ndal) in 2015

When the FMLN was established, its members had a wide range of [center-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_politics) to [far-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-left_politics) political positions,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGans201990-122] but it was largely dominated by its more far-left components.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGans201984-123] The PCES adhered to [Marxism–Leninism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxism%E2%80%93Leninism),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty199015-124] the FPL was [Maoist](46636.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty199030-6] the ERP followed Marxism–Leninism and [Che Guevara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che_Guevara)'s *[foquismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foco)*, the FARN had close relations with social democrats and [liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_and_conservatism_in_Latin_America), and the PRTC was [Trotskyist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trotskyist) (although it later moderated to Marxism–Leninism after it joined the FMLN).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty1990238-9] The Havana negotiations and subsequent formations of the DGU and FMLN were done to counteract ideological infighting among the leftist militant organizations and unite themselves under a single vanguard.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHaggerty199039–40-13] Under the FMLN, its member militant groups united under the banner of Marxism–Leninism.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhanem202396-125]

Although the FMLN was largely united, some disagreements remained between the various factions. Questions of whether to attain victory through a prolonged war or a rapid insurrection or on how much of the struggle should be based on political or military action continued to divide the FMLN, but many of these disagreements faded following Carpio's suicide in 1983.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAllison2016720-126] The vast majority of FMLN components also opposed receiving support from the Soviet Union, viewing it as [corrupt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corruption_in_the_Soviet_Union) and [oppressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_repression_in_the_Soviet_Union). Most groups instead opted for support from Cuba and Nicaragua. Only FAL/PCES and some hardline FPL members supported Soviet aid, while the ERP promoted [nationalism](16066160.md) and was suspicious of Cuban support.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPaszyn1996160-127]

The FMLN's members were largely in agreement on supporting the implementation of a [mixed economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_economy) rather than a [command economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Command_economy), and most also supported [non-alignment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPaszyn1996160-127] In 1984, the FMLN stated that it was in a "struggle against [colonialism](7299.md), [neocolonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neocolonialism), [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism), [racial discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_discrimination), and [apartheid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apartheid)".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECentral_Intelligence_Agency198412-128] The FMLN also utilized [liberation theology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_liberation_theology) to rally support from [the country's Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_El_Salvador) population.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrandall201656–57-129]

### Post-civil war ideology

Between 1988 and 1991, the FMLN gradually began to abandon Marxism–Leninism. By 1991, the group embraced democratic socialism. Ignacio Ellacuría, a [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesuit) priest and civil war observer, described the FMLN's shift towards democratic socialism as its own version of *[aggiornamento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggiornamento)* or a [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChávez20151787–1788-47] Although the FMLN is traditionally described as [left-wing](18499.md),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGoodfriend2019-78][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZainoHerrera20243-110] it is sometimes still described as far-left.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEO'Grady2012-130]

During Funes' presidency, the FMLN embraced [left-wing populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_populism). El Salvador did not join the Venezuelan-led [Bolivarian Alliance for the Peoples of Our America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivarian_Alliance_for_the_Peoples_of_Our_America) (ALBA) as Funes did not want to follow *[Chavismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavismo)*, but some local FMLN chapters created ALBA Petróleo that sold discounted gas from ALBA members.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUlloa2010-86]

## Party structure

A photograph of Manuel Flores

[Manuel Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Flores_\(Salvadoran_politician\)), the FMLN's 2024 presidential candidate and leader since August 2024

The FMLN's headquarters is located at 27 Poniente Street No. 1316 in Colonia Layco, San Salvador, the country's capital city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESupreme_Electoral_Court2026-131] Since since 4 August 2024, Flores and Marleni Funes have served as the party's secretary-general and deputy secretary-general, respectively.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGuzmán2024-132] As of 2024, the FMLN has around 16,000 members.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEChávez2024-133] The FMLN Youth is the party's [youth wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_wing)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDiario_Co_Latino2019-134] and "Anthem of the FMLN" ("*Himno del FMLN*") is the [party's anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_party_songs).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFarabundo_Martí_National_Liberation_Front-135]

In PARLACEN, the FMLN is a member of the [Parliamentary Group of the Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Group_of_the_Left).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECentral_American_Parliament-136] The FMLN is also a member of [COPPPAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COPPPAL) and the [São Paulo Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Paulo_Forum), two coalitions of left-wing Latin American political parties.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECOPPPAL-137][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEContra_Punto2016-138]

## Electoral history
