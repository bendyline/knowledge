The **United States attorney general** (**AG**) is the head of the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md) (DOJ) and serves as the chief law enforcement officer of the [federal government](195149.md). The attorney general acts as the principal legal advisor to the [president of the United States](24113.md) on all legal matters. The attorney general is also a statutory member of the [Cabinet of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States) and a member of the [United States National Security Council](44026.md). Additionally, the attorney general is seventh in the [presidential line of succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_line_of_succession). The attorney general is the only cabinet department head who is not given the title [Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_secretary).

Under the [Appointments Clause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appointments_Clause) of the [United States Constitution](31644.md), the officeholder is nominated by the president of the United States, and, following a confirmation hearing before the [Senate Judiciary Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_the_Judiciary), will take office if confirmed by the majority of the full [United States Senate](24909346.md). The attorney general is supported by the Office of the Attorney General, which includes executive staff and several deputy attorneys general.

The attorney general is a [Level I position in the Executive Schedule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_Schedule#Level_I)[^cite_note-4] and thus earns the salary prescribed for that level: \$250,600, as of January 2025.

## Name

The title Attorney General is an example of a noun (*attorney*) followed by a [postpositive adjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postpositive_adjective) (*general*).[^cite_note-\:1-5] "General" is a description of the type of attorney, not a title or rank in itself ([as it would be in the military](148537.md)).[^cite_note-\:1-5] Even though the attorney general (and the similarly titled [solicitor general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solicitor_General_of_the_United_States)) is occasionally referred to as "General" or "General \[last name]" by senior government officials, this is considered incorrect in standard [American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English) usage.[^cite_note-\:1-5][^cite_note-\:2-6] For the same reason, the correct American English plural form is "attorneys general" rather than "attorney generals".[^cite_note-\:2-6]

## History

See also: [United States Department of Justice § History](52563.md#History)

[Congress](31756.md) passed the [Judiciary Act of 1789](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_Act_of_1789) which, among other things, established the Office of the Attorney General. The original duties of this officer were "to prosecute and conduct all suits in the [Supreme Court](31737.md) in which the United States shall be concerned, and to give his advice and opinion upon [questions of law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Questions_of_law) when required by the president of the United States, or when requested by the heads of any of the departments".[^cite_note-7] Some of these duties have since been transferred to the [United States solicitor general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Solicitor_General) and the [White House counsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Counsel).

The [Department of Justice](52563.md) was established in 1870 to support the attorneys general in the discharge of their responsibilities. In 1841 the first volume of *The Official Opinions of the Attorneys General of the United States*[^cite_note-8] was printed on the authorization of Congress and contained the legal justifications of the President's official actions.[^cite_note-9]

The [secretary of state](32293.md), the [secretary of the treasury](43338.md), the [secretary of defense](43998.md), and the attorney general are regarded as the four most important [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_of_the_United_States) officials in the United States because of the size and importance of their respective departments.[^cite_note-10]

## Duties and responsibilities

The attorney general's duties and responsibilities as the chief law enforcement officer of the federal government include overseeing the [United States Department of Justice](52563.md), enforcing [federal laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_law_\(United_States\)), and providing both formal and informal legal advice and opinions to the president of the United States, the cabinet, and the heads of executive departments and agencies. The attorney general represents the federal government in legal matters and supervises the administration and operation of the Department of Justice, which includes the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md), the [Drug Enforcement Administration](146720.md), the [Bureau of Alcohol, Tobacco, Firearms and Explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureau_of_Alcohol,_Tobacco,_Firearms_and_Explosives), the [Office of Justice Programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Justice_Programs), [U.S. attorneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Attorney), and the [United States Marshals Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Marshals_Service).[^cite_note-11]

Additionally, the attorney general advises the president of the United States on appointments to federal judicial positions and Department of Justice roles, including U.S. Attorneys and U.S. Marshals. While the attorney general may represent the United States in the [Supreme Court](31737.md) and other courts, this is typically handled by the solicitor general.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The attorney general also performs or supervises other duties as required by statute or executive order.

## Presidential transition

It is the practice for the attorney general, along with the other Cabinet secretaries and high-level [political appointees of the president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_appointments_in_the_United_States), to tender a resignation with effect at noon on the [Inauguration Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_inauguration) (January 20) of a new president. The [deputy attorney general](2933681.md) is also expected to tender a resignation, but is commonly requested to stay on and act as the attorney general, pursuant to [28 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_28_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 508](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/508), pending the [confirmation by the Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advice_and_consent#United_States) of the new attorney general.

For example, upon the inauguration of President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) at noon on January 20, 2017, then-Attorney General [Loretta Lynch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loretta_Lynch) left her position, so then-deputy attorney general [Sally Yates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sally_Yates), who had also tendered her resignation, was asked to stay on to serve as the acting attorney general, pursuant to [28 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_28_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 508](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/508), until the confirmation of the new attorney general [Jeff Sessions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Sessions), who had been nominated for the office in November 2016 by then-[president-elect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President-elect_of_the_United_States) Donald Trump.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-16]

## Line of succession

### Presidential succession

The Attorney General is 7th in the [presidential line of succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_line_of_succession) following the [Secretary of Defense](43998.md) and preceding the [Secretary of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_the_Interior).[^cite_note-57]

### Attorney General succession

[28 U.S.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Title_28_of_the_United_States_Code) [§ 508](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/28/508) establishes the first two positions in the line of succession, while allowing the attorney general to designate other high-ranking officers of the Department of Justice as subsequent successors.[^cite_note-58] Furthermore, the most recent [Executive Order](210541.md) pertaining to the line of succession, [Executive Order](210541.md) 14136 titled "Providing an Order of Succession Within the Department of Justice" that was signed by President [Joe Biden](145422.md) on January 3, 2025, and published in the [Federal Register](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Register) on January 13, 2025,[^cite_note-\:29-52] but was revoked by President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) on January 20, 2025[^cite_note-59] and has yet to be replaced with another executive order pertaining to the line of succession, defines subsequent positions. The most recent line of succession was:

1. [United States Deputy Attorney General](2933681.md)

2. [United States Associate Attorney General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Associate_Attorney_General)

3. Other officers potentially designated by the attorney general (in no particular order):&#x20;

   - [Solicitor General of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solicitor_General_of_the_United_States)

   - Assistant Attorney General, [Antitrust Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice_Antitrust_Division)

   - Assistant Attorney General, [Civil Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice_Civil_Division)

   - Assistant Attorney General, [Civil Rights Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice_Civil_Rights_Division)

   - Assistant Attorney General, [Criminal Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice_Criminal_Division)

   - Assistant Attorney General, [National Security Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Justice_National_Security_Division)
