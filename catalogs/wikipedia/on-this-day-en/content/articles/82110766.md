The [pairs'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_skating) figure skating competition at the [2026 Winter Olympics](38416091.md) was held on 15 and 16 February 2026 at the [Milano Ice Skating Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_di_Milano) in [Milan, Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan,_Italy), and featured 19 teams from 14 nations. [Riku Miura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riku_Miura) and [Ryuichi Kihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryuichi_Kihara) of Japan won the gold medals, [Anastasiia Metelkina](67224137.md) and [Luka Berulava](62856231.md) of Georgia won the silver, and [Minerva Fabienne Hase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minerva_Fabienne_Hase) and [Nikita Volodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Volodin) of Germany won the bronze. Miura and Kihara had been part of the Japanese team that earlier finished second in the [team event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2022_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Team_event). In addition to their gold medal win here, Miura and Kihara set a new [world record score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest_scores_in_figure_skating) in the [free skate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_skating). This also marked Japan's first Olympic medal in pair skating, as well as Georgia's first ever Winter Olympic medal in any sport.

## Background

In October 2023, the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) suspended the [Olympic Committee of Russia](25045856.md).[^cite_note-2] The skating federations of Russia and Belarus were each permitted to nominate one skater or team from each discipline to participate at the [Skate to Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Skate_to_Milano) as a means to qualify for the [2026 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) as [Individual Neutral Athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_Neutral_Athletes_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics). Each nominee was required to pass a special screening process to assess whether they had displayed any active support for the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) or had any contractual links to the Russian or Belarusian military.[^cite_note-3] No pairs teams from either Russia or Belarus competed at the 2026 Winter Olympics.[^cite_note-ISU09202025-4]

The [pair skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_skating) event was one of five figure skating competitions contested at the 2026 Winter Olympics in [Milan, Italy](36511.md). It was held at the [Milano Ice Skating Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_di_Milano) over two days: the [short program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_program_\(figure_skating\)) took place on 15 February and the [free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_skating) [skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_skating) took place on 16 February.[^cite_note-Schedule-1] The competition featured 19 teams from 14 nations.[^cite_note-PairsResults-5] [Daria Danilova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daria_Danilova) and [Michel Tsiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Tsiba) made history as the first Dutch pair skating team to qualify for the Winter Olympics.[^cite_note-6] Having won both segments of the [team event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Team_event), [Riku Miura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riku_Miura) and [Ryuichi Kihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryuichi_Kihara) of Japan were seen as the most likely candidates to win the gold.[^cite_note-7] Miura and Kihara were two-time [world champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Figure_Skating_Championships) and also won two silver medals in the team events at the [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2022_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Team_event) and [2026 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2022_Winter_Olympics_%E2%80%93_Team_event), winning both pairs' segments in the latter.[^cite_note-8]

At age 42, [Deanna Stellato-Dudek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deanna_Stellato-Dudek) of Canada was the oldest woman to compete in figure skating at the Olympics.[^cite_note-COC02132026-9] She had competed in [single skating](7291274.md) as a teenager, winning a gold medal at the [1999 Junior Grand Prix Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Junior_Grand_Prix_Final) and a silver medal at the [2000 World Junior Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_World_Junior_Figure_Skating_Championships), before retiring in 2001 due to injury.[^cite_note-10] She returned to skating as a pair skater in 2016, first with [Nathan Bartholomay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_Bartholomay) and competing for the United States, and then with [Maxime Deschamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxime_Deschamps) and competing for Canada. In 2024, she became the oldest woman to win a gold medal at the World Championships.[^cite_note-11] At the [2025 John Nicks Pairs Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_CS_John_Nicks_International_Pairs_Competition), Stellato-Dudek performed a [backflip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Backflip_\(figure_skating\)) with an assist from Deschamps. Stellato-Dudek, becoming the first woman to perform the backflip since it became a legal element in figure skating in 2024.[^cite_note-12] On 2 February, [Skate Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skate_Canada) announced that Stellato-Dudek and Deschamps would not compete in the team event after Stellato-Dudek suffered an injury during practice, but that no decision had been made with regards to the pairs' event.[^cite_note-13] She was later medically cleared to compete. After their arrival in Milan, it was confirmed that she and Deschamps would not perform the backflip as part of their short program.[^cite_note-COC02132026-9]

## Required performance elements

Pairs performed their [short programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_program_\(figure_skating\)) on 15 February.[^cite_note-Schedule-1] Lasting 2 minutes 40 seconds (+/− 10 seconds),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTES\&P/ID202482-18] the short program had to include the following elements: one [pair lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_lifts#Pair_lifts), one [double or triple twist lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_lifts#Twist_lifts), one [double or triple throw jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_skating#Throw_jumps), one [double or triple solo jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pair_skating#Solo_jumps), one [solo spin combination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_spins#Solo_spin_combinations) with a change of foot, one [death spiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_spiral_\(figure_skating\)), and one [step sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Step_sequence) using the full ice surface.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTES\&P/ID2024119-19]

The sixteen highest-scoring pairs after the short program advanced to the [free skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_skating) on 16 February.[^cite_note-Schedule-1] Lasting 4 minutes (+/− 10 seconds),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTES\&P/ID202482-18] the free skate had to include the following elements: three pair lifts, of which one had to be a twist lift; two different throw jumps; one solo jump; one jump combination or sequence; one [pair spin combination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_spins#Pair_spin_combinations); one death spiral; and one [choreographic sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choreographic_sequence).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTES\&P/ID2024122-20]

## Judging

See also: [ISU Judging System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Judging_System)

Skaters were judged according to the required technical elements of their program (such as [jumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_jumps) and [spins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_spins)), as well as the overall presentation of their program, based on three program components (skating skills, presentation, and composition). Each technical element in a figure skating performance was assigned a predetermined base point value and scored by a panel of nine judges on a scale from −5 to +5 based on the quality of its execution.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTES\&P/ID202432,_83–84-21] Each Grade of Execution (GOE) from –5 to +5 was assigned a value as indicated on the Scale of Values.[^cite_note-ISU-2707-22] For example, a [triple Axel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axel_jump) was worth a base value of 8.00 points, and a GOE of +3 was worth 2.40 points, so a triple Axel with a GOE of +3 earned 10.40 points.[^cite_note-ISU-2707-22] The judging panel's GOE for each element was determined by calculating the [trimmed mean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean#Truncated_mean) (the average after discarding the highest and lowest scores). The panel's scores for all elements were added together to generate a [Total Elements Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Judging_System#Total_Elements_Score_\(TES\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTES\&P/ID202415–16-23] At the same time, the judges evaluated each performance based on the five aforementioned program components and assigned each a score from 0.25 to 10 in 0.25-point increments.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTES\&P/ID202484–85-24] The judging panel's final score for each program component was also determined by calculating the trimmed mean. Those scores were then multiplied by the factor shown on the chart below; the results were added together to generate a total [Program Component Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISU_Judging_System#Program_Component_Score_\(PCS\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTES\&P/ID202416–17-25]

### Short program

The [pairs' short program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_program_\(figure_skating\)) was held on 15 February. [Minerva Fabienne Hase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minerva_Fabienne_Hase) and [Nikita Volodin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Volodin) of Germany finished in first place, scoring a new personal-best score of 80.01, which was three points higher than their previous personal best. Hase and Volodin's score was five points ahead of [Anastasiia Metelkina](67224137.md) and [Luka Berulava](62856231.md) of Georgia. Metelkina and Berulava had a small error on their [triple flip throw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Throw_jump) and finished in second place, while [Lia Pereira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lia_Pereira) and [Trennt Michaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trennt_Michaud) of Canada finished in third place. [Riku Miura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riku_Miura) and [Ryuichi Kihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryuichi_Kihara) of Japan, who had been projected as the favorites to win the pairs event, finished in fifth place, seven points behind Hase and Volodin, after a mistake on a [lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_lift). Speaking afterward, Kihara said: "I’m not sure what happened, but it is what it is. We have to think positive. But it’s not like we’re in bad form, so tomorrow, we hope to be our usual selves."[^cite_note-Olympics03212026-30] Meanwhile, [Deanna Stellato-Dudek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deanna_Stellato-Dudek) and [Maxime Deschamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxime_Deschamps) of Canada finished in fourteenth place after Deschamps failed to properly land his [triple toe loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toe_loop_jump) and Stellato-Dudek lost her balance coming out of a lift.[^cite_note-Olympics03212026-30]

### Free skating

The [pairs' free skating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_skating) was held on 16 February. After finishing in fifth place after the short program, Riku Miura and Ryuichi Kihara rallied back to a first-place finish, beating Anastasiia Metelkina and Luka Berulava by over nine points, setting a new [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest_scores_in_figure_skating) in the free skate and winning Japan's first ever Olympic medal in pair skating. "We still can't believe that this has happened after yesterday's performance \[in the short program]," Kihara said. "We were quite disappointed... but we're really happy with how we skated today. It's a little bit of a disbelief that we were able to get a medal for Japan in pair skating for the first time ever."[^cite_note-NBC02162026-32]

Metelkina and Berulava finished in second place; their silver medals were the first Winter Olympic medals in Georgian history. "It's the best day in my life," Berulava said afterward. "Amazing moment for my country."[^cite_note-NBC02162026-32] Minerva Fabienne Hase and Nikita Volodin of Germany began the free skate with some errors on their [side-by-side Salchows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salchow_jump), but finished their performance successfully, winning the bronze. "It's a medal at the Olympics. I think it doesn't matter which color; it's just an amazing effort we put out there," Hase stated. "We fought to the end... We didn't stop after the big mistake on the Salchow, and we can bring home a bronze medal on our first Olympics together."[^cite_note-NBC02162026-32]

[Maria Pavlova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Pavlova_\(figure_skater\)) and [Alexei Sviatchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Sviatchenko) of Hungary delivered another consistent performance, finishing in third place in the free skate and fourth overall. [Sui Wenjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_Wenjing) and [Han Cong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Cong) of China, who were the reigning Olympic champions, finished in fifth place. Deanna Stellato-Dudek and Maxime Deschamps of Canada finished the competition in eleventh place. Speaking about her Olympic appearance, Stellato-Dudek commented: "Ironically, back in Italy, because that was the Olympics I was going for originally in [2006 \[in Turin\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figure_skating_at_the_2006_Winter_Olympics), so it's very ironic that it came full circle, and twenty years later, I'm back, perhaps where I belonged."[^cite_note-33]
