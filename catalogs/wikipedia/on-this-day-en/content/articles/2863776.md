# Tank truck

Not to be confused with [Tank transporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_transporter), [Gas van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_van), [Tanker (ship)](2506366.md), [Tank car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_car), or [Tank trailer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_trailer).



A Cape Cod Oil truck

A **tank truck**, **gas truck**, **fuel truck**, or **tanker truck** ([American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English)) or **tanker** ([British English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English)) is a [motor vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motor_vehicle) designed to carry [liquids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid) or [gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas) on roads. The largest such vehicles are similar to [railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad) [tank cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank_car), which are also designed to carry liquid loads. Many variants exist due to the wide variety of liquids that can be transported. Tank trucks tend to be large; they may be [insulated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_insulation) or non-insulated; [pressurized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pressurized) or non-pressurized; and designed for single or multiple loads (often by means of internal divisions in their tank). Some are [semi-trailer trucks](29381.md). They are difficult to drive and highly susceptible to [rollover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_rollover) due to their high center of gravity and, when they are partially filled, to the [free surface effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_surface_effect) of liquid sloshing in the tank.[^cite_note-1]

### Oil

Prior to tank distribution, oil was delivered in [cans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerrycan).[^cite_note-2] From the 1880s, it was distributed in horse-drawn tanks.[^cite_note-3] In 1910, [Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil) began using motor tankers.[^cite_note-4] [Anglo American Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esso#United_Kingdom) introduced underground tanks and delivery tankers to the UK in 1920.[^cite_note-5] [Pickfords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickfords) took over an oil tanker company in 1921 and soon had 4,500-litre (1,200 US gal; 1,000 imp gal) tankers, with 16,000 litres (4,300 US gal; 3,600 imp gal) by the mid 1930s.[^cite_note-6] Elsewhere, development was slower. For example, in New Zealand, Sir [Robert Waley Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Waley_Cohen), of [British Imperial Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_Australia#History), first proposed use of petrol tankers in 1925[^cite_note-7] and the first (910 litres (240 US gal; 200 imp gal)) tanker from [Auckland](18660332.md)[^cite_note-8] to arrive in [Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton,_New_Zealand) was greeted by a brass band in 1927.[^cite_note-9]

## Size and volume



A tank truck for a milk delivery parked in front of the Satamaito dairy in [Pori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pori), [Finland](10577.md)

Tank trucks are described by their size or volume capacity. Large trucks typically have capacities ranging from 21,000 to 44,000 litres (5,500 to 11,600 US gal; 4,600 to 9,700 imp gal). In Australia, [road trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_train) up to four trailers in length (known as Quad tankers) carry loads in excess of 120,000 litres (32,000 US gal; 26,000 imp gal). Longer road trains transporting liquids are also in use.

A tank truck is distinguished by its shape, usually a cylindrical tank lying horizontally upon the vehicle. Some less visible distinctions amongst tank trucks have to do with their intended use: compliance with human food regulations, refrigeration capability, acid resistance, pressurization capability, and more. The tanks themselves sometimes contain multiple compartments or [baffles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slosh_baffle) to prevent load movement destabilizing the vehicle known as the "[Wave effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slosh_dynamics#Sloshing_in_road_tank_vehicles)", a phenomenon caused by the movement of liquid within the tank due to the truck's momentum. This movement can create waves that exert pressure against the tank's walls, leading to instability during sharp turns or sudden braking and chances of rollover accidents.

## Common large tank trucks

Large tank trucks are used for example to transport [gasoline](23639.md), [diesel](81761.md), and [liquefied petroleum](361384.md) or [natural gas](832128.md) to [filling stations](61260.md). They also transport a wide variety of liquid goods such as [liquid sugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_sugar), [molasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molasses), [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk), [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wine), [juices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juices), [water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water), and [industrial chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_chemicals).

Tank trucks are constructed of various materials depending on what products they are hauling. These materials include [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium), [carbon steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_steel), [stainless steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stainless_steel), and [fibre-reinforced plastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibre-reinforced_plastic) (FRP).

Some tank trucks are able to carry multiple products at once due to compartmentalization of the tank into multiple tank compartments. This allows for an increased number of delivery options. These trucks are commonly used to carry different grades of gasoline to service stations to carry all products needed in one trip.

## Common small tank trucks

Smaller tank trucks with a capacity under 11,000 litres (3,000 US gal; 2,500 imp gal) are typically used to deal with light liquid cargo within a local community. For example, [vacuum trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vacuum_truck) are used to empty [septic tanks](217773.md) and then deliver the collected [fecal sludge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septage) to treatment sites. Such tank trucks typically have a maximum capacity of 11,000 litres (3,000 US gal; 2,500 imp gal). They are equipped with a pumping system to serve their particular need.

Another common use is to deliver fuel such as [liquified petroleum gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquified_petroleum_gas) (LPG) to households, businesses, and industries. The smallest of these trucks usually carry about 3,800 litres (1,000 US gal; 830 imp gal) of LPG under pressure. Typically, LPG tank trucks carry up to 3,499 US gallons (13,250 L; 2,914 imp gal) of product (usually liquid propane), on a 2-axle bobtail truck. 3,500 US gallons and greater requires a 3-axle truck (tank wagon). Some companies use lightweight steel to carry more gallons on single-axle trucks. Notably, one US manufacturer has built a 3,700 US gallons (14,010 L; 3,081 imp gal) tank truck, fitting it on a single axle.[^cite_note-10]

Tank trucks are also used to fuel aircraft at airports.
