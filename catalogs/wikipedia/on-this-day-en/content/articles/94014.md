**Golders Green** is a suburb in the [London Borough of Barnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Barnet) in north West London, 5.6 miles (9 km) northwest of Charing Cross. Its historical origins lie in a medieval small suburban [linear settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_settlement) that was sited near a farm and public grazing area green. The bulk of Golders Green forms a late Victorian/early Edwardian suburb centered on a commercial crossroads, the rest being of a later build.&#x20;

It was founded as a medieval [hamlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamlet_\(place\)) in the large [parish](23623.md) of [Hendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendon), [Middlesex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middlesex). The parish was superseded by [Hendon Urban District in 1894 and by the Municipal Borough of Hendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_Borough_of_Hendon) in 1932, abolished in 1965. In the early 20th century, it grew rapidly in response to the opening of a [tube station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_tube_station) of the [London Underground](17839.md), adjacent to the [Golders Green Hippodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_Hippodrome), which was home to the [BBC Concert Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Concert_Orchestra) for many years. The area has a wide variety of housing and a busy main shopping street, Golders Green Road.

It is known for its large [Jewish](5380456.md) population, as well as for being home to the largest Jewish [kosher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosher) hub (located west of Hoop Lane after the rail bridge) in the United Kingdom, which attracts many Jewish tourists.

## History

&#x20;



Extract of OS Map of 1868–1883. Click on map to view wider sheet.

The name Golders comes from a family named Godyere who lived in the area, and Green alludes to the manorial common at a cross roads next to which the settlement was built.[^cite_note-2] Golders Green has been a place in the parish and manor of [Hendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendon) since around the 13th century. The earliest reference to the name of the adjacent district of "Temple Fortune" is on John Rocque's map of 1754, which also shows what is now Golders Green Road as Groles Green. However the name Temple Fortune reveals a much earlier history. It is likely that the name refers to the [Knights of St John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitaller), who had land here (c. 1240). Fortune may be derived from a small settlement (tun) on the route from [Hampstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead) to [Hendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendon). Here a lane from [Finchley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finchley), called Ducksetters Lane (c. 1475), intersected. It is likely that the settlement was originally the Bleccanham estate (c. 10th century). By the end of the 18th century Temple Fortune Farm was established on the northern side of Farm Close.

The building of Finchley Road (c. 1827) replaced Ducksetters Lane as a route to Finchley, and resulted in the development of a small hamlet. Hendon Park Row (c. 1860s) is of this period, and consisted of around thirty small dwellings built by a George Stevens, which were, with two exceptions, demolished around 1956. A small [dame school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dame_school) and prayer house run by [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) [deaconesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deaconess) existed in the 1890s and 1900s, and developed to become St. Barnabas (1915). Along Finchley Road were a number of villas (c. 1830s), joined by the Royal Oak public house (c. 1850s). By the end of the 19th century there were around 300 people living in the area, which included a [laundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laundry) and a small hospital for children with skin diseases. The principal industry was brick making.

In 1895, [Golders Green Jewish Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_Jewish_Cemetery) was established adjacent to Hoop Lane,[^cite_note-3] with the first burial in 1897. [Golders Green Crematorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_Crematorium) was opened in 1902 (although much of it was built after 1905).[^cite_note-4] A significant moment in Temple Fortune's development into a suburban area occurred in 1907, when transport links were vastly improved by the opening of [Golders Green Underground station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_tube_station).

Although the area had been served by horse-drawn [omnibuses](4146.md) (since at least the 1880s) and later motor buses (from 1907), the tram line of 1910, connecting Finchley Church End with Golders Green Station, led to the development of the area west of Finchley Road. The establishment of [Hampstead Garden Suburb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead_Garden_Suburb) brought major changes to the area east of Finchley Road. Temple Fortune Farm was demolished and along the front of the road the building of the Arcade and Gateway House (c. 1911) established the Hampstead Garden Suburb's retail district.

In 1913, the [Golders Green Hippodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_Hippodrome), former home of the [BBC Concert Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Concert_Orchestra), and the [police station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_station) opened.[^cite_note-5] The 3,000 seater, Orpheum Theatre (1930) was intended to rival the Hippodrome in Golders Green and it enjoyed a royal visit from [Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_II), in 1972, but closed its doors in 1974, and was completely demolished in May 1982.[^cite_note-6]

In March 2026, four ambulances owned by Hatzalah, a Jewish volunteer service, were [set on fire](82760663.md).[^cite_note-7] A month later, two [Jewish locals were stabbed](83065835.md) in a separate incident, which was condemned by the prime minister, [Keir Starmer](18681491.md). Both incidents were characterised as [antisemitic](1078.md) by the Metropolitan Police.[^cite_note-8] On 14 May 2026, King [Charles III](125248.md) paid a visit to the community and met with both victims of the stabbings, together with the [first responders](500273.md) to the incident. The King voiced his concerns about the rise of antisemitism in the area.[^cite_note-9] The following month, a communal stairwell in a residentual apartment building on Bridge Lane, mainly inhabited by Jewish familied was set on fire. Residents confronted the suspect and extinguished the fire, preventing any damage or injuries. A suspect fled the scene.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## Geography

See also: [London Borough of Barnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Borough_of_Barnet)

For local elections *Golders Green* [ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wards_of_the_United_Kingdom) covers the north and west of the area; Childs Hill the south to the centre and *Garden Suburb*, named after Hampstead Garden Suburb: most of the east. Councillors are elected from across 21 wards. Nationally it votes for the MP for seat of [Finchley and Golders Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finchley_and_Golders_Green_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), which encompasses the parts of the NW11 and NW2 postcode districts it contains.[^cite_note-12] The same boundaries are used for the Golders Green, Childs Hill and Garden Suburb wards of the [Metropolitan Police Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Police_Service).[^cite_note-13]

It is about 160 feet (50 m) to 260 feet (80 m) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_above_sea_level).

The area is adjacent to the Heath Extensions part of [Hampstead Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead_Heath).

## Demography

Golders Green is often referred to as a Jewish area, and 2021 Census data shows that 49.9% (7,358 of 14,744)[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] of residents identified as Jewish, making it the epicentre of the largest Jewish population in Europe alongside surrounding areas, including Hampstead, Hendon and Finchley within the Borough of Barnet, which is home to 55,000 Jews, the highest percentage Jewish population in any Borough of the United Kingdom.[^cite_note-auto-16]

Ethnically, the Golders Green ward was 64% White (43% British, 21% Other, 1% Irish). Indians, Other Asians and Black Africans made up 5% each, while 6% claimed 'Any other ethnic group'.[^cite_note-auto-16]

### Jewish community

There has been a prominent [Jewish](25955086.md) community in Golders Green since the early 20th century, following the building of the station at the end of the Hampstead railway.[^cite_note-17] [Golders Green United Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_United_Synagogue) was the first purpose-built synagogue in the suburb, with its foundation stone laid on 16 October 1921. The Jewish community grew substantially after [Hitler's rise to power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler's_rise_to_power) drove many [German Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Jews) to England, the first German Jewish immigrants to the area forming the [Golders Green Beth Hamedrash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_Beth_Hamedrash). Soon after, [Galician Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galician_Jews) immigrants formed other synagogues. With it came the formation of Jewish schools such as Menorah Primary School before the onset of [World War II](32927.md).

There are close to fifty kosher restaurants and eateries under rabbinical supervision in Golders Green, and more than 40 synagogues throughout the area continuing into neighbouring [Hendon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hendon), as well as 30 Jewish schools (some in outlying areas owing to space restriction), many of them independent.

## Economy

Golders Green has restaurants with many cuisines including [Indian](14533.md), [Israeli](9282173.md), [Thai](30128.md), [Turkish](11125639.md), [Japanese](15573.md), [Chinese](5405.md), [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea) and [Italian](14532.md) eateries. These are over a dozen [coffee bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee_bar); together with a number of niche food stores. The area is home to several leading banks.

## Transport

[Golders Green station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_tube_station) is a [London Underground](17839.md) tube station on the [Northern line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_line) in London fare zone 3. It is the first surface station on the Edgware branch when heading north. On the station's forecourt is Golders Green [bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_station). This is a major hub for [London Buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Buses) in [North London](380308.md).[^cite_note-18] [National Express coaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Express_Coaches) also stop at the bus station before/after central London.

Many bus routes pass through Golders Green, including the 210 to Finsbury Park, the 139 to Waterloo, the 13 to Victoria and the 102 to Edmonton Green, and from Golders Green station the 240 to Edgware, the 183 to Pinner, and both the 83 and the 245 to Alperton begin their routes.

## Education

There are six state aided primary schools in Golders Green; these include: Brookland Infant & Junior, [Garden Suburb Infant & Junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Garden_Suburb_School?action=edit\&redlink=1), Menorah Primary School and Wessex Gardens.[^cite_note-19]

[The King Alfred School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Alfred_School,_London) is located on North End Road and [Henrietta Barnett School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrietta_Barnett_School) is located in nearby Hampstead Garden Suburb.

## Places of worship

### Churches



Golders Green Parish Church (Church of England)



Golders Green Synagogue

The [Anglican parish church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_England_parish_church) of [St. Alban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Alban) the Martyr in North End Road was designed by [Sir Giles Gilbert Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Giles_Gilbert_Scott), and in 1933 replaced the original eponymous church, on the site, which is now the [parish hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish_hall). The latter was built in 1910 and made a [parish church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parish_church) in 1922.[^cite_note-20]

[St Edward the Confessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Edward_the_Confessor_Church,_Golders_Green), a [Roman Catholic church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_England_and_Wales), was built in 1915 and consecrated in 1931.[^cite_note-21] A [Carmelite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmelites) [monastery](45856.md) was established in [Bridge Lane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_Lane?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1908[^cite_note-22] and sold in 2007.[^cite_note-23]

There is a [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Archdiocese_of_Thyateira_and_Great_Britain) cathedral on Golders Green Road,[^cite_note-24] and a [Coptic Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria) church,[^cite_note-25] both having been [Anglican churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_churches) for most of the 20th century. The Greek Orthodox church was built in 1914.

The Golders Green [Unitarian Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Assembly_of_Unitarian_and_Free_Christian_Churches), built in 1925 on Hoop Lane, is [Grade II listed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Listed_building); [Historic England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historic_England) singles out for mention a "notable temera mural by [Ivon Hitchens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivon_Hitchens)", among other works. The congregation seceded from [Rosslyn Hill Unitarian Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosslyn_Hill_Unitarian_Chapel) ([Hampstead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead)) in 1903 but took a generation to build its own place of worship.[^cite_note-26] St Ninian's Presbyterian Church on Helenslea Avenue was built in 1911 by [T. P. Figgis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._P._Figgis), noted for designing stations on the Northern line. The congregation merged with Golders Green Methodist Church (now Trinity Church, Hodford Road) in 1979. The building found new life as a Hindu temple, but was decommissioned in 2013.[^cite_note-27]

### Synagogues

There are several [synagogues](48559.md) in the area. The first, [Golders Green United Synagogue (Dunstan Road)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_United_Synagogue_\(Dunstan_Road\)), started in 1915 and its current building in Dunstan Road opened in 1922.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The Golders Green Beth Hamedrash opened in Golders Green in 1934, moving to the Riding in 1956. The Sassover Beis Hamedrash Helenslea Avenue moved to Golders Green from the [East End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_End) in 1939 as well as the Beis Yissochor Dov (Hager's) currently in Highfield Avenue.

The [Beth Shmuel Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Shmuel_Synagogue?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1942 in Oakfields Road and relocated at 169–171 Golders Green Road in 1945[^cite_note-30] and is a prominent synagogue in North West London, with Rabbi Elchonon Halpern its longest serving Rabbi for 72 years up until his death in 2015. In about 1960 the [Eastern Jewish Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi_Jews) established the [Ohel David Eastern Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohel_David_Eastern_Synagogue) at the Lincoln Institute, the former site of the Golders Green Beth Hamedrash.[^cite_note-31] The [Machzike Hadath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machzike_Hadath) Synagogue moved to Golders Green in the 1970s, opening its present building in 1983.[^cite_note-32]

### Other religious buildings

The former [Golders Green Hippodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_Hippodrome) theatre became an [El Shaddai International Christian Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Shaddai_International_Christian_Centre) in 2007 and then an Islamic centre in 2017, and now is a [Hillsong Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsong_Church).[^cite_note-33]

## Community facilities



Water Garden in Golders Hill Park

[Golders Hill Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Hill_Park), adjoining the West Heath of [Hampstead Heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hampstead_Heath), is a formal park, which includes a small (free) zoo, a walled horticultural garden, pinetum, duckponds, a water garden and a very popular café. During the summer, children's activities are organised and there is often live music on the bandstand. Close to the park, also adjoining the West Heath is the Hill, a formal garden with an extensive and imposing [pergola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pergola).

Nearby [Golders Green Crematorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golders_Green_Crematorium) has an extensive garden with features such as a special children's section and a pond, in keeping with the distinct [Italianate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italianate) air.

## Appearances in popular culture

In his 1946 book "[The Great Divorce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Divorce)", [C.S. Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C._S._Lewis) has a character from Golders Green. Sarah Smith and her husband are the last characters to enter the novel. She appears with great pomp and circumstance, arrayed in splendor and arriving behind a procession complete with music. She is beautiful and one of the "great ones" in heaven, but on earth had led an anonymous life in Golders Green.[^cite_note-34]
