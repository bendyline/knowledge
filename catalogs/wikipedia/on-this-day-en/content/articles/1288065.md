**Taganrog** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Таганрог, IPA: [\[təɡɐnˈrok\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Russian)) is a [port city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_city) in [Rostov Oblast](478744.md), Russia, on the north shore of [Taganrog Bay](3032433.md) in the [Sea of Azov](78019.md), several kilometers west of the mouth of the [Don River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_\(river\)). It is in the [Black Sea](3386.md) region. Population: 245,120 ([2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census));[^cite_note-2021Census-13] 257,681 ([2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Russian_census));[^cite_note-2010Census-8] 281,947 ([2002 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Russian_census));[^cite_note-PopCensus-14] 291,622 ([1989 Soviet census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Soviet_census)).[^cite_note-Census1989-15]

Located at the site of an ancient Greek and medieval Italian colony, modern Taganrog was founded in 1698. Contested by various factions during [World War I](4764461.md) and the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War), the city served as the temporary Soviet Ukrainian capital in 1918.

## History

Main article: [History of Taganrog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Taganrog)

The history of the city goes back to the late [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age)–early [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age). Later, it became the earliest [Greek settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_colonies) in the northeastern [Black Sea](3386.md) region and was probably mentioned by the Greek historian [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) as [emporion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emporium_\(antiquity\)) Kremnoi (Κρήμνοι, meaning cliffs).[^cite_note-16] It had contacts as well to the other Greek colonies around the Black Sea as well as to the indigenous communities of the hinterland.[^cite_note-17]

In the 13th century, [Pisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Pisa) merchants founded a colony, Portus Pisanus, which was however short-lived.[^cite_note-EB1911-18][^cite_note-sgk-19]

Taganrog was founded by [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) on 12 September 1698.[^cite_note-HolidayEst-3] In 1712, it passed to the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) and the fort was destroyed. In 1769, it was recaptured by Russia.[^cite_note-sgk-19] The first [Russian Navy](383258.md) base, it hosted the [Azov Flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Flotilla) of [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great) (1770–1783), which subsequently became the Russian [Black Sea Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Sea_Fleet). Taganrog was granted city status in 1775.[^cite_note-gr-4] From 1775 it was administratively located in the [Azov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Governorate), and then from 1784 in the [Yekaterinoslav Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yekaterinoslav_Governorate).[^cite_note-sgk-19]



Bombardment of Taganrog in 1855

By the end of the 18th century, Taganrog had lost its importance as a military base after [Crimea](163045.md) and the entire [Sea of Azov](78019.md) were absorbed into the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). In 1802, [Tsar Alexander I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Russia) granted the city [special status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Taganrog), which lasted until 1887. In 1825, the [Alexander I Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_Palace) in Taganrog was used as his summer residence, and he died there in November 1825. Also in Taganrog is the [House of Teacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_teacher), a mansion where numerous artists have performed.

Although it was [besieged by Anglo-French fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Taganrog) in 1855 during the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War),[^cite_note-EB1911-18] Taganrog became important as a commercial port, used for the import of [grain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain) by the end of the 19th century until the early 20th century. [Industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialization) increased in the city when [Belgian](3343.md) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) investors founded a boiler factory, an iron and steel [foundry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foundry), a [leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather) factory, and an [oil press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_press) factory. By 1911, fifteen foreign [consulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consulates) had opened in the city.[^cite_note-20]



Plaque commemorating the foundation of the [Communist Party of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Ukraine_\(Soviet_Union\)) in 1918 in the former Bristol Hotel

During [World War I](4764461.md), Taganrog served as the temporary capital of the [Ukrainian Soviet Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Republic) in March–April 1918.[^cite_note-21] Afterwards it was occupied by the troops of the [German Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Army_\(German_Empire\)) from May to August 1918. In 1919, General [Anton Denikin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Denikin) established his headquarters at the Avgerino mansion in the city while commanding [White Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Russians_\(Russian_Civil_War\)) troops fighting in [South Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Russia_\(1919%E2%80%931920\)) during the [Russian Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War). When the White Russians were defeated and [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) power was established in the city on 25 December 1919, Denikin's remaining troops and the British Consulate were evacuated by [HMS *Montrose*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Montrose_\(D01\)). Full power was granted to the executive committee of The City Soviet Workers' council on 17 December 1920, and Taganrog joined the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR) as the [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) of Taganrog Okrug. It was transferred to the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_SFSR) along with [Shakhty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakhty) Okrug on 1 October 1924.

During [World War II](32927.md), Taganrog was occupied by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) from 1941 to 1943 during [Operation Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Barbarossa), when two [SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel) divisions entered the city on 17 October 1941, followed by the [Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht). The city suffered extensive damage. Under German occupation the [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government) system was replaced by a German-style *Bürgermeisteramt* (Mayor's Office), which governed the city until it was liberated by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) on 30 August 1943.

## Administrative and municipal status

Within the [framework of administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Administrative_divisions), it is incorporated as Taganrog [Urban Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_federal_subject_significance)—an administrative unit with the status equal to that of the [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Rostov_Oblast).[^cite_note-Ref133-1] As a [municipal division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Municipal_divisions), this administrative unit also has urban okrug status.[^cite_note-Ref873-10]

Taganrog is the leading industrial center of Rostov Oblast. Local industry is represented by aerospace, machine-building, automobile, military, iron and steel industry, engineering, metal traders and processors, timber, woodwork, pulp and paper, food, light, chemical and construction materials. The city is one of the major ports of the [Sea of Azov](78019.md).

The biggest company currently operating in Taganrog is [Taganrog Iron & Steel Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taganrog_Iron_&_Steel_Factory), (publicly traded company Tagmet), which manufactures steel, steel pipe, for oil and gas industry and consumer goods. The other major employer is Taganrog Auto Factory ([TagAZ Ltd.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TagAZ)), which originated from Taganrog Combine Harvester Factory. The plant manufactures automobiles licensed by Hyundai. The production line includes Hyundai Accent compact sedan, mid-size Hyundai Sonata, sport utility vehicle Santa Fe, and Hyundai Porter pickup truck. Taganrog is also home to the aircraft design bureau [Beriev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beriev).

The area around Taganrog has a large industrial potential, a diversified agricultural industry, production plants, and a modern infrastructure. The location of Taganrog on the intersection of traffic routes and the seaport facilitate access to the emerging [CIS](36870.md) markets.

Taganrog's main trading partners are the [CIS](36870.md) countries, [South Korea](27019.md), [Turkey](11125639.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Greece](12108.md), and [Egypt](8087628.md).

## Military

The [Taganrog air base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taganrog-Central_\(air_base\)) is six kilometres (3 nmi) northwest of the city and hosts the [Taganrog Aviation Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taganrog_Aviation_Museum). The city also hosts the [Taganrog military museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taganrog_military_museum).

## Taganrog in literature



The Assumption Cathedral in Taganrog (1818–1938), where [Anton Chekhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Chekhov) was christened on February 10, 1860

[Anton Chekhov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Chekhov), a native of Taganrog, featured the city and its people in many of his works, including *[Ionych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionych)*, *The House with an Attic*, *[The Man in a Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Man_in_the_Case)*, *[Van'ka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanka_\(short_story\))*, *[Three Years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Years)*, *Mask*, and *My Life*. It is believed that Taganrog may have been the *[Lukomorye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukomorye)* (fairy tale land) in which [Alexander Pushkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Pushkin)'s *[Ruslan and Lyudmila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruslan_and_Lyudmila)* (1820) was set.[^cite_note-Mikhailov-24] The city also appeared in the novels of [Ivan Vasilenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Vasilenko) and [Konstantin Paustovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstantin_Paustovsky) and in the poems of [Nikolay Sherbina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolay_Sherbina) and [Valentin Parnakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin_Parnakh).

The legend of "Elder [Fyodor Kuzmich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fyodor_Kuzmich)" is cited in the book *[Roza Mira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roza_Mira)* by Russian mystic [Daniil Andreyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Andreyev). According to this legend, the Russian tsar Alexander I did not die in Taganrog, but instead left his crown and the status of monarch to continue his life as a traveling hermit.[^cite_note-25]

In foreign literature, the city was mentioned in the titles of *Der Tote von Taganrog* by [Eberhard von Cranach-Sichart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eberhard_von_Cranach-Sichart?action=edit\&redlink=1) and *Taganrog* by [Reinhold Schneider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinhold_Schneider).

In 2004 [Sabine Wichert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabine_Wichert) published a collection of poems entitled *Taganrog*.

In [Maria Kuncewiczowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Kuncewiczowa)'s 1945 novel *[The Stranger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cudzoziemka)* (New York, LB Fischer publisher), the city of Taganrog plays an essential role as a place of nostalgic happiness for the uprooted Polish musician and matriarch, Rose.
