**Mombasa** ([/mɒmˈbæsə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*mom-BASS-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); also [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/-ˈbɑːsə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*-⁠BAH-sə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a coastal city in southeastern [Kenya](188171.md) along the [Indian Ocean](14580.md). It was the first capital of [British East Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_Africa), before [Nairobi](21482.md) was elevated to capital status in 1907. It now serves as the capital of [Mombasa County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_County). Buildings in the Central Business District are blue and white, representing the Indian Ocean.[^cite_note-6] It is the country's oldest (c. 900 A.D.) and second-largest [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Kenya)[^cite_note-CIA_World_Factbook,_2012-7] after Nairobi, with a population of about 1,208,333 people according to the 2019 census.[^cite_note-KNBS-1]

Mombasa's location on the Indian Ocean made it a historic trading centre,[^cite_note-8] and it has been controlled by many countries because of its strategic location. Kenyan school history books place the founding of Mombasa as 900 AD.[^cite_note-\:2-9] It must have already been a prosperous trading town in the 12th century, as the Arab geographer [al-Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Idrisi) mentions it in 1151. It was a part of the [Kilwa Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilwa_Sultanate) from approximately the early 14th century until the dissolution of the sultanate in 1513. The oldest stone mosque in Mombasa, Mnara, was built c. 1300. The [Mandhry Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandhry_Mosque), built in 1570, has a minaret that contains a regionally specific [ogee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogee) arch. The city later came under the occupation and control of the [Omani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omani_Empire) in the late 17th century.

In the late pre-colonial period, it was the metropolis of a plantation society, which became dependent on slave labour based around the ivory trade. Throughout the early modern period, Mombasa was a key node in the complex and far-reaching Indian Ocean trading networks. Its key exports then were ivory, [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), [sesamum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesamum) and [coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut).

Today, Mombasa is a tourism-based town, home to one of the [state houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_House_\(Kenya\)), with an extra-large [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaport) and an [international airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moi_International_Airport).

### Ancient and medieval

The founding of Mombasa is associated with two rulers: Mwana Mkisi and Shehe Mvita. According to legend, Mwana Mkisi is the original ancestor of Mombasa's oldest lineages within *Thenashara Taifa* (or Twelve Nations). Families associated with the Twelve Nations are still considered the original inhabitants of the city. Mwana Mkisi was a queen from the pre-Islamic era, who founded Kongowea, the original urban settlement on Mombasa Island.

Significantly, the names of both the queen and the city have linguistic and spiritual connections with [Central Africa](539606.md). "Mkisi" is considered the personification of *"ukisi"*, which means "the holy" in [kiKongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KiKongo). "Kongowea" can similarly be interpreted as the Swahili locative of *"kongo"*, which denotes the essence of civilizational order in central Africa. These legends can be read as an acknowledgement of the [Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bantu_languages)-speaking origins of the [Swahili people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_people).

Shehe Mvitaff superseded the dynasty of Mwana Mkisi and established the first permanent stone mosque on Mombasa Island. Mombasa's oldest extant stone mosque, Mnara, was built c. 1300. Shehe Mvita is remembered as a Muslim of great learning and so is connected more directly with the present ideals of Swahili culture that people identify with Mombasa. The ancient history associated with Mwana Mkisi and Shehe Mvita and the founding of an urban settlement on Mombasa Island is still linked to present-day peoples living in Mombasa. The Thenashara Taifa (or Twelve Nations) Swahili lineages recount this ancient history today and are the keepers of local Swahili traditions.[^cite_note-10]

Most of the early information on Mombasa comes from the writings of [Portuguese chroniclers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) in the 16th century.

The famous Moroccan scholar and traveller [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta) (1304 – 1368/1369) visited the area during his travels to the [Swahili Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_Coast). He noted the city, although he stayed only one night. He wrote that the people of Mombasa were [Shafi'i](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shafi'i) Muslims, religious people, trustworthy and righteous. Their mosques were made of wood, expertly built.[^cite_note-11]

The exact founding date of the city is unknown, but it has a long history. Kenyan school history books place the founding of Mombasa as 900.[^cite_note-\:2-9] It must have been already a prosperous trading town in the 12th century, as the Arab geographer [al-Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Idrisi) mentions it in 1151. The oldest stone mosque in Mombasa, Mnara, was built c. 1300. The Mandhry Mosque, built in 1570, has a minaret that contains a regionally specific ogee arch. This suggests that Swahili architecture was an indigenous African product rather than being adopted from non-African Muslims who brought stone architecture to the Swahili Coast.[^cite_note-12]

During the pre-modern period, Mombasa was an important centre for the trade in spices, gold, and [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory). Its trade links reached as far as India and China. Oral historians today can still recount this period of local history. Indian history shows that there were trade links between Mombasa and [Cholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholas) of [South India](678583.md). Throughout the early modern period, Mombasa was a key node in the complex and far-reaching Indian Ocean trading networks. Its key exports were ivory, [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), [sesamum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesamum) and [coconuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coconut).

Ivory caravans remained a major source of economic prosperity. Mombasa became the major port city of pre-colonial Kenya in the Middle Ages and was used to trade with other African port cities, the [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Iran), the Arabian Peninsula, India and China.[^cite_note-13]

Sixteenth-century Portuguese voyager [Duarte Barbosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duarte_Barbosa) wrote,&#x20;

> "\[Mombasa] is a place of great traffic and has a good harbour in which there are always moored small craft of many kinds and also great ships, both of which are bound from [Sofala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofala) and others which come from [Cambay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khambhat) and [Melinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malindi) and others which sail to the island of [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar)."[^cite_note-14]

See also: [Battle of Mombasa (1505)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mombasa_\(1505\)) and [Battle of Mombasa (1528)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mombasa_\(1528\))



Mombasa was under [Portuguese rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) from 1593 to 1698 and again from 1728 to 1729. Portuguese presence in Kenya lasted from 1498 until 1730.

[Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama) was the first known European to visit Mombasa, receiving a chilly reception in 1498.[^cite_note-15] Two years later, the town was sacked by the Portuguese. In 1502, the [sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate) became independent from [Kilwa Kisiwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilwa_Kisiwani) and was renamed as Mvita (in [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language)) or Manbasa ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language)).

The [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) had since had encounters with the city several times; first under [Francisco de Almeida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_Almeida) in 1505, later under [Afonso de Albuquerque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afonso_de_Albuquerque) in 1522 to quell an attempted mutiny by the sultan's nephew in Pemba and Zanzibar, and finally the destruction of the city under [Nuno da Cunha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuno_da_Cunha) again in 1528 after the Malindi sultan failed to pay [tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribute).[^cite_note-\:02-16]

In 1585, a military expedition of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), led by Emir 'Ali Bey, successfully captured Mombasa, and other coastal cities in Southeast Africa from the Portuguese.[^cite_note-17] However, [Malindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malindi) remained loyal to Portugal. The [Zimba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zimba_\(East_Africa\)) overcame the towns of Sena and Tete on the Zambezi, and in 1587 they took [Kilwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilwa), killing 3,000 people. At Mombasa, the Zimba slaughtered the Muslim inhabitants, but they were halted at Malindi by the Bantu-speaking [Segeju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segeju) and went home. This stimulated the Portuguese to take over Mombasa a third time in 1589, and four years later they built Fort Jesus to administer the region. Between [Lake Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Malawi) and the [Zambezi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zambezi) mouth, Kalonga Mzura made an alliance with the Portuguese in 1608 and fielded 4,000 warriors to help defeat their rival Zimba, who were led by chief Lundi.

After the building of [Fort Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Jesus), Mombasa was put by the Portuguese under the rule of members of the ruling family of Malindi. In 1631 [Dom Jeronimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_ibn_al-Hasan), the ruler of Mombasa, slaughtered the Portuguese garrison in the city and defeated the relief force sent by the Portuguese. In 1632 Dom Jeronimo left Mombasa and became a pirate. That year the Portuguese returned and established direct rule over Mombasa.[^cite_note-18]

### Omani rule

With the [capture of Fort Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Fort_Jesus) in 1698, the town came under the influence of the [Imamate of Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imamate_of_Oman), subordinate to the Omani rulers on the island of [Unguja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unguja), prompting regular local rebellions. Oman appointed three consecutive Governors (Wali in Arabic, [Liwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwali) in [Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language)):

- 12 December 1698 – December 1698: Imam Sa'if ibn Sultan

- December 1698 – 1728: Nasr ibn Abdallah al-Mazru'i

- 1728–12 March 1728: Shaykh Rumba

Mombasa was briefly returned to Portuguese rule by captain-major Álvaro Caetano de Melo Castro (12 March 1728 – 21 September 1729), then four new Omani Liwali until 1746, when the last of them made it independent again (disputed by Oman), as the first of its recorded Sultans:

- 1746–1755: 'Ali ibn Uthman al-Mazru'i

- 1755–1773: Masud ibn Nasr al-Mazru'i

- 1773–1782: Abdallah ibn Muhammad al-Mazru'i

- 1782–1811: Ahmad ibn Muhammad al-Mazru'i (born 17–died 1814)

- 1812–1823: 'Abdallah ibn Ahmad al-Mazru'i (died 1823)

- 1823–1826: Sulayman ibn 'Ali al-Mazru'i



C. 1953 – A newly constructed East African Railways Beyer Garratt locomotive being unloaded at Mombasa

From 9 February 1824 to 25 July 1826, there was a [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) [protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate) over Mombasa, represented by governors. Omani rule was restored in 1826; seven *liwalis* where appointed. On 24 June 1837, it was nominally [annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexed) by [Said bin Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_bin_Sultan) of [Muscat and Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscat_and_Oman).

### British rule and independence

On 25 May 1887 Mombasa was relinquished to the British East Africa Association, later the [Imperial British East Africa Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_British_East_Africa_Company). It came under British administration in 1895.[^cite_note-19] It soon became the capital of the [British East Africa Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_East_Africa_Protectorate) and the sea terminal of the [Uganda Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Railway), construction of which was started in 1896. Many workers were brought in from [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) to build the railway, and the city's fortunes revived. The [Sultan of Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_of_Zanzibar) formally presented the town to the British in 1898.

Mombasa became the capital of the [Kenya Colony Protectorate of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Africa_Protectorate), sometime between 1887 and around 1906.[^cite_note-20] The capital was later moved because medical officers warned that the ground was swampy, and urged Sir [James Hayes Sadler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hayes_Sadler), then Commissioner of the East Africa Protectorate, to plead with London to move the town elsewhere to mitigate potential disease. [Nairobi](21482.md) has since been Kenya's capital to date.[^cite_note-21]

The [Mombasa tusks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_tusks), one of the city's best-known monuments, were originally constructed in 1952 by the British administration of the [Kenya Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Colony), commemorating the visit of [Queen Elizabeth II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_II) to the city.[^cite_note-std-22][^cite_note-nat-23]

In 2018, as part of an effort to increase tourism, Mombasa County Governor Hassan Joho issued a directive requiring that all buildings in the Old Town and the Central Business District be painted white with [Egyptian blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_blue) trim and banned all signs from their walls or canopies. Transport, Infrastructure and Public Works County Executive Tawfiq Balala stated that the city wanted to be "the most photographed in Africa".[^cite_note-tubei-businessinsider-24]

## Geography



Map of Mombasa's extent.

Being a [coastal town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_town), Mombasa is characterised by a flat topography. The town of Mombasa is centred on [Mombasa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_Island), but extends to the mainland. The island is separated from the mainland by two [creeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creek_\(tidal\)), [Port Reitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Reitz) in the south and [Tudor Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_Creek) in the north.

## Climate

Mombasa has a [tropical wet and dry climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wet_and_dry_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen-Geiger_climate_classification_system): *As*; [Trewartha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trewartha_climate_classification): *Asha*). The amount of rainfall essentially depends on the season. The rainiest months are April and May, while rainfall is minimal between January and February.

Located near the equator, Mombasa has only a slight seasonal temperature variation, with high temperatures ranging 28.8–33.7 °C (83.8–92.7 °F).

As a seaport, Mombasa is subject to detrimental consequences of a fluctuating climate. In October 2006, Mombasa experienced a large flood that affected 60,000 people.[^cite_note-\:1-25]

[Like the rest of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Kenya), climate change is already creating challenges for the city: [coastal erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_erosion) has become a problem for infrastructure in Mombasa. Due to [rising sea levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level_rise), the coastline has been eroding at 2.5–20 cm (0.98–7.87 in) per year. This has increased the number of annual floods.[^cite_note-\:1-25]

Mombasa is located on [Mombasa Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_Island) and sprawls to the surrounding mainlands. The island is separated from the mainland by two [creeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creek_\(tidal\)): [Tudor Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudor_Creek) and [Kilindini Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilindini_Harbour). It is connected to the mainland to the north by the [Nyali Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyali_Bridge), to the south by the [Likoni Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likoni_Ferry), and to the west by the [Makupa Causeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makupa_Causeway), alongside which runs the [Kenya-Uganda Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya-Uganda_Railway). The port serves both Kenya and countries of the interior, linking them to the ocean. The city is served by [Moi International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moi_International_Airport) located in the northwest mainland suburb of Chaani.[^cite_note-30]

### North Coast



Seafront of Nyali Beach, north coast (from the Voyager Resort).

[Nyali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyali), also considered a prime and up-market residential area, it is on the mainland north of the island and is linked by the [New Nyali Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Nyali_Bridge). It has numerous beach front hotels in the area known as the "North Coast". Nyali has two distinct sections – the upmarket Old Nyali and the upcoming New Nyali. For many residents, Nyali has now become a self-contained residential area, with two [Nakumatts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakumatt), a multiplex cinema, shopping malls, banks, schools and post offices. This often eliminates the need for residents to cross the bridge and to go into the congested Mombasa city centre. Nyali is home for the Nyali Cinemax complex, Mamba Village, the Nyali Golf Club, and some of the most prestigious academic institutions of the Coast Province.

Kongowea is a densely populated area with 15 villages, two sub-locations and an estimated population of 106,180 residents.[^cite_note-31] Kongowea is a cosmopolitan settlement mainly inhabited by people from mainland who migrated into the city in search of employment, mainly in service and manufacturing sector. The area is adjacent to the rich suburb of Nyali which employs a portion of the village residents. They are mainly hired as cheap labour as watchmen, gardeners, masons for up coming houses and house help. The most well known villages inside Kongowea include Kisumu Ndogo, Shauri Yako and Mnazi Mmoja, despite being located in this prime area, many residents live under extreme conditions – poor sanitation, high crime rate and lack of basic essential amenities like schools, hospitals and tap water. Kongowea is also home to one of the largest open-air markets in the African Great Lakes.

[Bamburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamburi) is an outlying township (fifteen minutes drive) along the Malindi road. It is home to [Bamburi Cement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamburi_Cement) factory, the largest cement plant in the East African region. Other notable features in the area are the Jomo Kenyatta public beach, commonly known as Pirates, and [Haller Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haller_Park_\(Kenya\)), a nature trail and wildlife conservatory. Kiembeni Estate, also in the Bamburi area, hosts around 100,000 residents. The estate has its own supermarket, several retail shops, salons and boutiques, and a number of licensed drinking dens. The establishments include The Shilla Bar, Turkey Base, Stars Garden and Sensera pub. Kiembeni is arguably the largest estate in Mombasa, and growing even faster.

Other areas include, [Shanzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanzu), Mkomani, [Bombolulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombolulu), [Kisauni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisauni) and, across the Mtwapa creek, the popular area of [Mtwapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mtwapa), which is already located in Kilifi county.

The North Coast has an entertainment industry which attracts locals and tourists.

## Tourism

Modern tourism in Mombasa developed during the colonial period. In the 1920s and 1930s, European residents of Kenya's interior began taking holidays on the coast, initially in Mombasa. At independence in 1963, coastal tourism was still based largely on European residents of Kenya. International tourism grew after 1962 with package holidays and charter flights to Mombasa. Hotel development later spread along the beaches north and south of the city.[^cite_note-Sindiga2000-35]

Fort Jesus and the adjoining Old Town are historical attractions.[^cite_note-Sindiga2000-35] The [National Museums of Kenya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museums_of_Kenya) sought World Heritage listing for both places in the mid-2000s.[^cite_note-36] Fort Jesus was added to the World Heritage List in 2011, with Old Town as its buffer zone; Old Town was not separately inscribed. The [International Council on Monuments and Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Council_on_Monuments_and_Sites) questioned whether the entire buffer zone had legal protection and called for better staffing and financing of its conservation bodies, together with stronger implementation of conservation measures. Its evaluation cited a 2003 survey in which 25 per cent of Old Town's urban fabric was assessed as being in poor condition.[^cite_note-37] High-rise construction has since been reported as a threat to Old Town's historic character.[^cite_note-38] A 2024 study of building changes in Mji wa Kale, Kibokoni and Makadara cautioned that the existence of a conservation plan did not by itself demonstrate effective protection.[^cite_note-39]

Jumba la Mtwana, about 15 kilometres (9.3 mi) north of Mombasa in Mtwapa, is a 14th-century Swahili settlement on the North Coast tourism circuit. Its remains include houses, three mosques and a tomb.[^cite_note-40] Haller Park in Bamburi occupies a former limestone quarry. Ecological restoration began in 1971, and the park opened to the public in 1985. The restored quarry is used for ecotourism, recreation and conservation education.[^cite_note-41]

Mama Ngina Waterfront has a 2.1-kilometre (1.3 mi) promenade and space for food vendors. Street foods include fried cassava, *kachiri*, *mkate wa mayai*, fish and coconut water.[^cite_note-42] Its harbourfront setting has been compared with that of the night food market at Forodhani Gardens in Zanzibar.[^cite_note-43]

Conference tourism has expanded alongside Mombasa's beach market.[^cite_note-44] In June 2026, opening ceremony and plenary sessions of the eleventh Our Ocean Conference was held at Tembo International Convention Centre in [Shanzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanzu).[^cite_note-45] The conference was the first in the series to be held in Africa and drew more than 5,000 participants.[^cite_note-46]

Short-term rentals, including listings on Airbnb, have expanded. About 443 listings were added in 2025, while operators in the city reported lower occupancy and increased competition.[^cite_note-47]

## Economy



Downtown Mombasa



Biashara Street, Mombasa



The Market Hall in Mombasa, Kenya, where especially spices are sold.

Mombasa is an important economic centre in Kenya. In addition to the coffee trade, the food and chemical industries, there is a steel mill, an aluminum rolling mill, an oil refinery and a cement plant. The city is home to the most important seaport in East Africa, [Kilindini Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilindini_Harbour), which is also used by the neighbouring countries Tanzania and Uganda for their imports and exports.

*Kilindini* is an old Swahili term meaning "deep". The port is so-called because the channel is naturally very deep. Kilindini Harbour is an example of a natural geographic phenomenon called a [ria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ria), formed at the end of the [last glacial period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_glacial_period) when the sea level rose and engulfed a river that was flowing from the mainland.

Mombasa is a centre of coastal tourism in Kenya. Mombasa Island itself is not a main attraction, although many people visit the Old Town and [Fort Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Jesus). The [Nyali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyali), [Bamburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamburi), and [Shanzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanzu) beaches are located north of the city. The [Shelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shelly_Beach,_Kenya?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Tiwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiwi,_Kenya), and [Diani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diani_Beach) beaches are located south of Mombasa. Several luxury hotels exist on these beaches, while the less expensive hotels are located further away.

Mombasa's northern shoreline is renowned for its vibrant 24-hour entertainment offers, including both family entertainment (water parks, cinemas, [bowling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowling), etc.), sports ([watersports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watersports), [mountain biking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_biking) and [gokarting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gokarting)), culinary offers (restaurants offering a wide range of specialties from Kenya, China, Japan, India, Italy, Germany and other countries) and nightlife (bars, pubs, clubs, discothèques, etc.).

Other local industries include an [oil refinery](195137.md) with a capacity of 80,000 barrels (13 ML) a day,[^cite_note-Mombasa_Refinery-50] and a cement factory capable of producing over 1.1 million tons per year.[^cite_note-51] The major intercontinental undersea telecom cables reach shore next to Mombasa, connecting the [African Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes) to the rest of the world and supporting a fast-growing [call centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Call_centre) business in the area. The estimated real GDP growth for Kenya in 2016 is 5.7-6.0%. This growth will be in response to the construction of a railway system from Nairobi to Mombasa which will aid in trade and transportation between Kenya's two major cities.[^cite_note-\:0-52]

Mombasa will become a Special Economic Zone (SEZ) in which certain industries such as tea, garments, and footwear will be exempt from certain taxes to promote domestic growth. This is in response to the deficiencies in Export Processing Zones (EPZ).[^cite_note-\:0-52]

The Kenyan Dock Worker's Union is situated in Mombasa and has roughly 5,000 members.[^cite_note-\:0-52]

President Kenyatta has made it a priority to deepen economic ties with Asia at the onset of his presidency. Japan has played a role in financially sponsoring the expansion of the Mombasa port in phase one and two of the expansion project.[^cite_note-\:0-52]

At 44%, the rate of youth unemployment in Mombasa is more than double the national average of 21% (2016).[^cite_note-53]

### Rail

Mombasa has a modern railway station on the [Mombasa–Nairobi Standard Gauge Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa%E2%80%93Nairobi_Standard_Gauge_Railway) that replaced the [narrow-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-gauge_railway) [Uganda Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda_Railway) completed in 1901 under British colonial rule.[^cite_note-56] Completed in 2017 and located at [Miritini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miritini), the [Mombasa Terminus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_Terminus) station links Mombasa to Nairobi. The station, situated about 20 kilometres from the city centre, is accessible through the newly built (2018) highway, being the first phase of the Dongo Kundu bypass. [Kenya Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Railways) transports passengers and cargo through the Standard Gauge Railway between Nairobi to Mombasa.[^cite_note-57]

### Road

Driving in Mombasa is straightforward and the majority of the roads are tarmacked. The [Mombasa–Nairobi Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa%E2%80%93Nairobi_Expressway) connects Mombasa to the capital city Nairobi.[^cite_note-58]

Within Mombasa, most local people use *[matatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matatu)* (mini-buses) which are extremely common in Kenya, to move around the city and its suburbs. The *[tuk-tuk](593765.md)*—a motor vehicle with three wheels—is widely used as transport around the city and its suburbs. No more than three passengers may be carried. A *[boda-boda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boda-boda)* is originally a bicycle taxi but have long since been replaced by motorcycles.[^cite_note-59]

### Sea

The port of Mombasa is the largest in East Africa, with 19 [deep water berths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep-water_port) with two additional berths nearing completion and two [oil terminals](3385681.md).[^cite_note-Kenyan_Port_Authority-60] The port is connected by rail and road to the interior.[^cite_note-Kenyan_Port_Authority-60] At present there is little or no scheduled passenger service from the port, however, international cruise ships frequent the port. The port is part of the [21st Century Maritime Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Maritime_Silk_Road) that runs from the Chinese coast to the Upper Adriatic region. In connection with the Silk Road Initiative, China has started infrastructure development projects in Kenya and is building roads, railways and public buildings on credit.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-Kenya_Port_Authority-66][^cite_note-67]

#### Ferry



Mombasa ferry

There is no bridge between Mombasa Island and the south coast, instead the route is served by [ferries](50771.md) operated by the Kenya Ferry Service from Kilindini and Mtongwe to [Likoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likoni) in the south coast of Mombasa. The last major accident occurred in 1994 when a ferry serving Mtongwe route sank killing more than 270 people.[^cite_note-68]

As a result of the increase in more luxurious hotels along the south coast and a lack of a direct bridge linking with Mombasa island, visiting tourists have the option of flying directly from Nairobi into the South Coast airstrip at Ukunda.

The [Dongo Kundu Bypass Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongo_Kundu_Bypass_Highway) is under construction (as at 2018). With a total of three bridges, it will finally connect the mainland to the south coast easing the burden on the ferry services. [Mombasa Gate Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_Gate_Bridge?action=edit\&redlink=1) will connect the mainland to Likoni, this will eliminate the usage of the unsafe ferry which has claimed hundreds of lives.

Many Hindus visit Mombasa to pray at a number of naturally formed [Lingam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingam) that have formed in the [Nyali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyali) beach Gombeshwar Caves. According to local folklore, the cave-temple was found a long time ago when a group of local herders were puzzled after they noticed that one of their cows regularly released all of her milk at a specific yet random spot on the hills. They are then said to have approached some Hindu families living in the area to ask if they had any idea about the strange behaviour of the cow. The group then teamed up, made their way to the spot and started digging. They thus encountered the cave where they found a 'Shiva [Lingam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingam)' – an abstract representation of the Hindu God Lord Shiva, whose vehicle is [Nandi (mythology)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandi_\(mythology\)). Hindus worship Lord Shiva by offering milk to a [Lingam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingam).[^cite_note-74]

## Culture



Askari monument Mombasa

A major cultural hub in [Kenya](188171.md) and the [African Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes), Mombasa's proximity to [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar), [Nairobi](21482.md) and the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent), as well as its large shipping and maritime industries gives it a diverse mosaic of cultures. [Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Kenya) is a main feature of Mombasa's culture.

### Music

[Taarab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taarab) music, which originates from [Zanzibar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanzibar), has a prominent local presence.[^cite_note-National_Geographic-75] Styles of music native to Mombasa include the smooth and mellow [Bango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bango_\(music\)), fast-paced [Chakacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakacha) and traditional [Mwanzele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mwanzele).

Musicians of note are [Mombasa Roots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_Roots), [Safari Sounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safari_Sound_Band), and [Them Mushrooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Them_Mushrooms). Mombasa has been the home or base for former greats such as [Fundi Konde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fundi_Konde), known for his song "Tausi"; [Fadhili Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadhili_Williams) and [Nyota Ndogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyota_Ndogo).

Recently, [hip hop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hip_hop), [reggae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggae), [soul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_music), [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues), [salsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salsa_music) and (among the Indian community) [bhangra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhangra_\(music\)) have become popular, especially amongst the youth. Mombasa is mainly a tourism centre with hundreds of entertainment spots of all kinds including night clubs, bars, hotels, and fancy restaurants.

### Sports

Mombasa is represented in the [Kenyan Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyan_Premier_League) by [Bandari F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandari_F.C._\(Kenya\)), which plays at the Mbaraki Sports Grounds. Also, the Congo United FC, Promoted and dropped in 2011, are in the second tier Nationwide Super League with 4 other hometown clubs – [Admiral F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_F.C.); Magongo Rangers; Sparki Youth and Coast United.[^cite_note-76] Derbies between Mombasa teams have become intriguing affairs recently.[^cite_note-77] Another team, [Coast Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Stars), were relegated several years ago from the league. The only Mombasa-based team to win the league is [Feisal F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feisal_F.C.), the 1965 champions. Kiziwi leopards was a popular team in the 1980s as was Mombasa Wanderers decades before. There are several cricket teams in Mombasa; one is [Mombasa Sports Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_Sports_Club) (MSC), whose ground was given [ODI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_International) status in 2006. MSC has also a [rugby union](25405.md) team playing in the [Kenya Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenya_Cup) League, the premier rugby competition in Kenya. Mvita XI men and MSC ladies represent Mombasa in Kenyan field hockey leagues.

Mombasa is represented in the nationwide rugby league by Mombasa RFC. The city is also host to a leg of the national rugby sevens circuit, being one of only six city hosts. The Mombasa leg is referred to as the Driftwood sevens, and the annual tournament is extremely popular, attracting thousands of fans from across the country.

The [2007 World Cross Country Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_IAAF_World_Cross_Country_Championships) were held in Mombasa. [Mombasa Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_Marathon) is competed annually in Mombasa. The town also hosts the biennial classic edition of [Safari Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safari_Rally) and annually a Kenya National Rally Championship round.

Scuba diving takes place mostly within the [Mombasa Marine National Park and Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_Marine_National_Park_and_Reserve), which is managed and maintained by [Kenya Wildlife Service](11102157.md). The park has a length of about 8 km (5.0 mi).

## In popular culture

Mombasa is the subject of the popular song and music video of the same name, "Mombasa" by *[Jabali Afrika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabali_Afrika)* feat. *[Jason Dunford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Dunford)* aka Samaki Mkuu released on July 17, 2020.[^cite_note-88]

In the film *[Out of Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Out_of_Africa_\(film\))*, Mombasa is the train destination as the seaport for voyages to Europe via the [Suez Canal](29323.md), and Mombasa is indicated as downriver ("This water must go home to Mombasa").

Mombasa is featured in [Paul Schrader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Schrader)'s 2014 film *[Dying of the Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dying_of_the_Light_\(film\))* as the hiding place of terrorist Muhammad Banir. It is also a prominent setting in Schrader's 2017 re-cut version, *Dark*.

A futuristic Mombasa is a pivotal setting in the popular *[Halo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_\(series\))* franchise, with much of *[Halo 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_2)*, *[Halo 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_3)*, and all of *[Halo 3: ODST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_3:_ODST)* taking place in the city. The games are set in the year 2552 and Mombasa serves as the capital of the fictional East African Protectorate. The city is a [megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalopolis) divided into "Old Mombasa" and "New Mombasa", filled with immense skyscrapers and an [orbital elevator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_elevator). New Mombasa is the main battleground of the [Covenant's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Covenant_\(Halo\)) invasion of Earth, as they focus their campaign in and around the city in search of a massive, technologically advanced [Forerunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factions_of_Halo) artifact buried nearby. Old Mombasa is largely destroyed by "glassing" (bombarded by [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_\(physics\)) until melted) during the battle and excavation process; it likely is glassed again shortly after (along with much of modern-day [Kenya](188171.md) and [Tanzania](30118.md)) during the events of *[Halo 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_3)*. Reconstruction efforts begin at some point after *[Halo 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_3)*. More recently, New Mombasa has appeared as the setting of the *[Halo Infinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halo_Infinite)* multiplayer maps "Bazaar" and "Streets".

Mombasa is featured in the 2010 movie, *[Inception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inception)*, where Cobb meets Eames and Yusuf before the job takes place.

In the [Warren Zevon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_Zevon) song "[Roland the Headless Thompson Gunner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roland_the_Headless_Thompson_Gunner)", Mombasa is one of the key locales related to the [protagonist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protagonist)'s quest.

The [Finnish](10577.md) pop hit "[Mombasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mombasa_\(song\))" (by [Taiska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiska)) is about the city.[^cite_note-89]

In the US, the [Walt Disney World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walt_Disney_World) resort recreated a [Kenyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenyan) village in the Africa section of the [Disney's Animal Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney's_Animal_Kingdom) theme park named "Harambe", which is modelled after Mombasa. The village features a store called the "Mombasa Marketplace".[^cite_note-90]

In the Indian movie *[Mr. India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._India_\(1987_film\))*, Mombasa is mentioned in the popular song "Hawa Hawaii".[^cite_note-91]

The Indian Bollywood movie Company was shot partly in Mombasa.

Most of the events in the 2017 story "Consummation in Mombasa" (by [Andrei Gusev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Gusev)) take place in Mombasa and in the nearest district [Mtwapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mtwapa).[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

*[One-Way Ticket to Mombasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-Way_Ticket_to_Mombasa) (Menolippu Mombasaan)* is a 2002 Finnish film directed by Hannu Tuomainen.[^cite_note-95]

Popular blackgaze band [Deafheaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deafheaven) titled the last song of its 2021 album, *[Infinite Granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_Granite)*, with the name of the city. The lyrics make reference to the beaches and general uplifting scenery of Mombasa.

In *[The Thief and the Cobbler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Thief_and_the_Cobbler)*, [Richard Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Williams_\(animator\))' unfinished movie, the antagonist Zigzag brings King Nod "a plaything from far south of Gaza, a bountiful maiden from Mombasa" at the beginning of the film.
