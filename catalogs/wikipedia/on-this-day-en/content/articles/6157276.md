&#x20;This is a partial list of temperature phenomena that have been labeled as [heat waves](293270.md), listed in order of occurrence.

- [1540 European drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_year-round_heat_and_drought_of_1540_in_Europe) – Extreme [drought](37335.md) and heatwave lasting 11 months in Europe.

- July 1743 heatwave in China – Beijing reached 44.4 °C (111.9 °F) on 25 July, higher than any modern records. 11,400 people reportedly died.

## 20th century

- 1901 – the [1901 eastern United States heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1901_eastern_United_States_heat_wave) killed 9,500 in the [Eastern United States](509486.md).

- 1906 – the [1906 United Kingdom heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1906_British_Isles_heatwave) began in August and lasted into September broke numerous records. On 2 September temperatures reached 35.6 °C (96.1 °F), which still holds the September record, but some places beat their local record during September 1911 and September 2016.

- 1911 – the [1911 Eastern North America heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1911_Eastern_North_America_heat_wave) killed between 380 and 2,000 people.

- 1911 – the [1911 United Kingdom heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1911_United_Kingdom_heatwave) was one of the most severe periods of heat to hit the country with temperatures around 36 °C (97 °F). The heat began in early July and did not let up until mid-September where even in September temperatures were still up to 33 °C (91 °F). It took 79 years for temperature higher to be recorded in the United Kingdom during [1990 United Kingdom heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_United_Kingdom_heat_wave).

- 1911 – 41,072 deaths were reported during a heat wave in [France](5843419.md).

- 1913 – in July, the hottest heat wave ever struck California. During this heat wave, [Death Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_Valley) recorded a record high temperature of 57 °C (134 °F) at [Furnace Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furnace_Creek,_California), which still remains the highest ambient air temperature recorded on Earth.

  [^cite_note-WorldHighTemp-6]

  [^cite_note-El_Azizia-7]

- 1921 – hottest July on record across Eastern Canada and parts of the Northeastern US, part of a very warm year in those places. Parts of the [United Kingdom](31717.md) also saw recording breaking heat, also part of a very warm year. The [Central England Temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_England_Temperature) for July was 18.5 °C (65.3 °F), which was the 8th warmest since records began in 1659, and the warmest since 1852. The year of 1921 was the warmest on record at the time but has since been eclipsed by 15 other years.

  [^cite_note-8]

- 1923–1924 – during a period of 160 such days from 31 October 1923 to 7 April 1924, the [Western Australian](33613.md) town of [Marble Bar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble_Bar,_Western_Australia) reached 38 °C (100 °F).

  [^cite_note-climateextremes-9]

- 1930s – almost every year from 1930 to 1938 featured historic heat waves and droughts somewhere in North America, part of the [Dust Bowl](59749.md) years. Many mid-Atlantic/Ohio valley states recorded their highest temperatures during July 1934. The longest continuous string of 38 °C (100 °F) or higher temperatures was reached for 101 days in Yuma, Arizona during 1937 and the highest temperatures ever reached in Canada were recorded in two locations in [Saskatchewan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskatchewan) in July 1937.

- 1935 – in the summer months this heatwave was characterized by unusually high temperatures across the [United Kingdom](31717.md).

  [^cite_note-10]

- 1936 – the [1936 North American heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_North_American_heat_wave) during the [Dust Bowl](59749.md) followed one of the coldest winters on record—the [1936 North American cold wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_North_American_cold_wave) - and was particularly brutal. 13 states and hundreds of cities across the Northeast, Midwest, and Great Plains in the U.S. recorded record high temperatures with over 5,000 people dying from the heat.

  [^cite_note-11]

  [^cite_note-12]

- 1947 – record breaking temperature of 37.6 °C (99.7 °F) in Paris recorded on 26 June 1947.

  [^cite_note-13]

- 1950s – prolonged severe drought and heat wave occurred in the early 1950s throughout the central and southern United States. Every year from 1952 to 1955 featured major heat waves across North America. In some areas it was drier than during the Dust Bowl and the heat wave in most areas was within the top five on record. The heat was particularly severe in 1954 with 22 days of temperatures exceeding 38 °C (100 °F) covering significant parts of eleven states. On 14 July, the thermometer reached 47 °C (117 °F) at [East St. Louis, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_St._Louis,_Illinois), which remains the record highest temperature for that state.

  [^cite_note-14]

  [^cite_note-15]

  [^cite_note-16]

- October 1952 – Romania was hit by very hot weather. Temperatures reached 39.0 °C (102.2 °F) on 2 October, with Bucharest reaching 35.2 °C (95.4 °F). Temperatures on the night of 2–3 October were also just under 26 °C (79 °F).

- 1955 – the [1955 United Kingdom heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_United_Kingdom_heatwave) was a period of hot weather that was accompanied by drought. In some places it was the worst drought on record, more severe than 1976 and 1995.

- 1960 – on 2 January, [Oodnadatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oodnadatta), South Australia hit 50.7 °C (123.3 °F) degrees, the highest temperature ever recorded in the [Southern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere) and Oceania.

- 1972 – heat waves of 1972 in New York and Northeastern United States were significant. Almost 900 people died; the heat conditions lasted almost 16 days, aggravated by very high humidity levels.

- 1976 – the [1976 United Kingdom heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_British_Isles_heatwave) was one of the hottest in living memory, with temperatures exceeding 32 °C (90 °F) somewhere in the country for over two consecutive weeks. The heatwave was also accompanied by one of the worst droughts in British history, and reservoirs reached historic low levels during the heat wave that would not be seen again until the heat waves of the 21st century. The maximum recorded temperature of the heat wave, 35.9 °C (96.6 °F) at [Cheltenham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheltenham), [Gloucestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloucestershire) was at the time the third highest reliably recorded temperature in British history, with only the 1911 heat wave achieving higher reliably recorded temperatures.

- 1980 – an estimated 1,000 people died in the [1980 United States heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_heat_wave) and drought, which impacted the central and eastern United States. Temperatures were highest in the southern plains. From June through September, temperatures remained above 32 °C (90 °F) all but two days in [Kansas City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri). The [Dallas/Fort Worth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas/Fort_Worth) area experienced 42 consecutive days with high temperatures above 38 °C (100 °F), with temperatures reaching 47 °C (117 °F) at [Wichita Falls, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wichita_Falls,_Texas), on 28 June. Economic losses were \$20 billion (1980 dollars).

  [^cite_note-ncdc-17]

- 1981 – August 1981 heat wave in the Pacific Northwest.

- 1983 – during the summer of 1983 temperatures over 38 °C (100 °F) were common across [Iowa](26810748.md), [Missouri](19571.md), [Illinois](14849.md), [Michigan](18859.md), [Wisconsin](33127.md), [Indiana](21883857.md), [Ohio](22199.md), [Minnesota](19590.md), [Kansas](16716.md), [Nebraska](21647.md), and certain parts of [Kentucky](16846.md); the summer of 1983 remains one of the hottest summers ever recorded in many of the states affected. The hundred-degree readings were accompanied by [very dry conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_United_States_drought) associated with drought affecting the [Corn Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_Belt) States and [Upper Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Midwest). The heat also affected the [Southeastern U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeastern_U.S.) and the [Mid-Atlantic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_states) as well that same summer. New York Times represented articles about the heat waves of 1983 affecting the [central United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest).

  [^cite_note-18]

  &#x20;This heat wave was associated with the [I-94 derecho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-94_derecho).

- 1983 – France recorded a record 26 days of Heat-wave conditions, the longest such defined period. The United Kingdom recorded the hottest month ever by average temperature until it was beaten in August 1995.

  

  Temperature difference in Europe from the average during the [European heat wave of 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_European_heat_wave)

- 1987 – prolonged heat wave from 20 to 31 July in [Greece](12108.md), with more than 1,000 deaths in the area of [Athens](1216.md). The maximum temperature measured was 41.9 °C (107.4 °F) at 23 July at the center of Athens and in the suburb of [Nea Philadelphia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nea_Philadelphia), 8 kilometres (5.0 mi) northeast was 43.6 °C (110.5 °F) on 27 July, and were combined with high minima, with the highest being 30.2 °C (86.4 °F) in the center of Athens at 27 July and 29.9 °C (85.8 °F) at 24 July at Nea Philadelfia. The lowest minimum was 25.6 °C (78.1 °F) at the center of Athens. Moreover, humidity was high and wind speeds low, contributing to human discomfort, even during the night.

  [^cite_note-19]

- 1988 – intense heat spells in combination with the [drought of 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988%E2%80%931990_North_American_drought), reminiscent of the dust bowl years caused deadly results across the United States. Official estimates report that 5,000 to 10,000 people died because of constant heat across the United States. Some estimates put total deaths at close to 17,000.

  [^cite_note-ncdc-17]

- 1990 – cities across the United Kingdom broke their all-time temperature records in the dramatic [1990 United Kingdom heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_United_Kingdom_heatwave). Temperatures peaked at 37.1 °C (98.8 °F) at Cheltenham, Gloucestershire. This led to one of the hottest Augusts on record going back to 1659. Also in France a several day heat wave is reported (*France-Soir*, 4 September 1990) with temperatures exceeding 40 degrees Celsius.

- 1994 – Intense heat wave in [Poland](22936.md) between July and August, with maximum temperature 39.5 °C (103.1 °F). This heat caused 1076 additional deaths in 10 largest Polish cities.

  [^cite_note-20]

- 1995 – During the first week of February 1995, California, including Los Angeles, experienced a record-breaking heat wave, and on 20 February 1995, it was 35 °C (95 °F) in Los Angeles during a [Presidents' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Day) heat wave.

- 1995 – the [1995 Chicago heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Chicago_heat_wave) produced record high dew point levels and heat indices in the [Chicago area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_area) and [Wisconsin](33127.md); temperatures reached as high as 41 °C (106 °F). The lack of emergency cooling facilities and inadequate response from civic authorities to the senior population, particularly in lower income neighborhoods in Chicago and other Midwestern cities, lead to at least 778 deaths—mostly which were African American Chicagoans. A [series of damaging derechos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_wave_of_1995_derecho_series) occurred on the periphery of the hot air dome.

- 1995 – the United Kingdom experienced its third hottest summer since at least 1659. August was the hottest on record since 1659 and also the hottest month ever recorded until July 2006. The summer was also the driest on record since at least 1766. Temperatures peaked at 35.2 °C (95.4 °F) on 1 August at [Boxworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxworth), [Cambridgeshire](6288.md), which did not break the all-time record.

- 1996 – During the first week of February 1996 California, including Los Angeles, experienced a heat wave, with a high recorded temperature of 28.9 °C (84.0 °F) on 7 February 1996.

  [^cite_note-21]

  [^cite_note-22]

- 1997 – the United Kingdom experienced its third major heatwave in 7 years with August 1997 being one of the hottest on record.

- 1999 – a heat wave and drought in the eastern United States during the summer of 1999. Rainfall shortages resulted in worst drought on record for Maryland, Delaware, New Jersey, and Rhode Island. The state of West Virginia was declared a disaster area. 3.81 million acres (15,400 km

  2

  ) were consumed by fire as of mid-August. Record heat throughout the country resulted in 502 deaths nationwide.

  [^cite_note-23]

  &#x20;There were many deaths in urban centers of the Midwest.

- 2000 – in late Summer 2000, a [heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Southern_United_States_heat_wave) occurred in the southern United States, breaking many cities' all-time maximum temperature records.

## 21st century



### 2001–2009

- In early August 2001 [an intense heatwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_eastern_North_America_heat_wave) hit the eastern seaboard of the United States and neighboring southeastern Canada. For over a week, temperatures climbed above 35 °C (95 °F) combined with stifling high humidity. [Newark, New Jersey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark,_New_Jersey), tied its all-time record high temperature of 41 °C (106 °F) with a [heat index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_index) of over 50 °C (122 °F).

  [^cite_note-24]

- In April 2002 a summer-like heat wave in spring affected much of the Eastern United States.

- In July 2002 a heatwave in China killed at least 7 people and resulted in hospitalization of over 3500 people.

  [^cite_note-25]

- During April 2003 there was a summer-like heatwave that affected the United Kingdom, mainly [England](9316.md) and [Wales](69894.md), where temperature records were broken.

- The [European heat wave of 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_European_heatwave) affected much of western Europe, breaking temperature records. Much of the heat was concentrated in Spain, England and in France, where nearly 15,000 people died.

  [^cite_note-26]

  &#x20;In Portugal, the temperatures reached as high as 47 °C (117 °F) in the south.

- The [European heat wave of 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_European_heatwave) was the second massive heat wave to hit the continent in four years, with temperatures rising to 36.7 °C (98.1 °F) in Paris; in Ireland, which has a moderate maritime climate, temperatures of over 32 °C (90 °F) were reported. Temperatures of 35 °C (95 °F) were reached in the [Benelux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benelux) and Germany (in some areas 38 °C (100 °F)), while Great Britain recorded 36.5 °C (97.7 °F). Many heat records were broken (including the hottest ever July temperature in Great Britain) and many people who experienced the heat waves of 1976 and 2003 drew comparisons with them. The highest average July temperatures were recorded at many locations in Great Britain, Netherlands, Denmark, Sweden and Germany, and in the UK, July 2006 was the hottest month ever recorded and remains so today, even though the all-time temperature records of August 1990 and August 2003 were not reached.

- The [2006 North American heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_North_American_heat_wave) affected a wide area of the United States and parts of neighboring Canada during July and August 2006. Over 220 deaths were reported. Temperatures in some parts of South Dakota exceeded 46 °C (115 °F). Also, California experienced temperatures that were extraordinarily high, with records ranging from 38 to 54 °C (100 to 130 °F). On 22 July, the [County of Los Angeles](79734.md) recorded its highest temperature ever at 48 °C (119 °F). Humidity levels in California were also unusually high, although low compared with normal gulf coast/eastern seaboard summer humidity they were significant enough to cause widespread discomfort.

  [^cite_note-27]

  &#x20;Additionally, the heat wave was associated a [series of derechos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_wave_of_2006_derecho_series) that produced widespread damage.

- The [European heat wave of 2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_European_heatwave) affected primarily south-eastern Europe during late June through August. Bulgaria experienced its [hottest year on record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Bulgarian_heat_wave), with previously unrecorded temperatures above 45 °C (113 °F). The [2007 Greek forest fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Greek_forest_fires) were associated with the heat wave.

- During the [2007 Asian heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Asian_heat_wave), the Indian city of [Datia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datia) experienced temperatures of 48 °C (118 °F).

- In January 2008, [Alice Springs](183935.md) in Australia's [Northern Territory](21638.md) recorded ten consecutive days of temperatures above 40 °C (104 °F) with the average temperature for that month being 39.8 °C (103.6 °F). In March 2008, [Adelaide](1148.md), [South Australia](26716.md) experienced maximum temperatures of above 35 °C (95 °F) for fifteen consecutive days, seven days more than the previous longest stretch of 35 °C (95 °F) days. The March 2008 heat wave also included eleven consecutive days above 38 °C (100 °F).

  [^cite_note-28]

  &#x20;The heat wave was especially notable because it occurred in March, an autumn month, in which Adelaide averages only 2.3 days above 35 °C (95 °F).

  [^cite_note-29]

- The eastern United States experienced an early summer heat wave from 6–10 June 2008 with record temperatures.

  [^cite_note-30]

  [^cite_note-31]

  &#x20;There was a heat wave in Southern California beginning late June,

  [^cite_note-32]

  &#x20;which contributed to widespread fires. On 6 July, a renewed heat wave was forecast, which was expected to affect the entire state.

  [^cite_note-33]

  [^cite_note-34]

- In early 2009, [Adelaide](1148.md), [South Australia](26716.md) was hit by a [heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_southeastern_Australia_heat_wave) with temperatures exceeding 40 °C (104 °F) for six days in a row, while many rural areas experienced temperatures hovering around 45 °C (113 °F). Kyancutta on the Eyre Peninsula endured at least one day at 48 °C (118 °F), with 46 and 47 being common in the hottest parts of the state. [Melbourne](17306237.md), in neighbouring [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)) recorded 3 consecutive days over 43 °C (109 °F), and also recorded its highest ever temperature 8 days later in a secondary heatwave, with temperatures peaking at 46.4 °C (115.5 °F). During this heat wave Victoria suffered from [large bushfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Saturday_bushfires) which killed 173 people and destroyed more than 2,500 homes. There were also over half a million people without power as the heatwave blew transformers and the power grid was overloaded.

- In August 2009, [Argentina](18951905.md) experienced a period of unusual and exceptionally hot weather during 24–30 August, during the Southern Hemisphere winter, just a month before Spring,

  [^cite_note-35]

  &#x20;when an unusual and unrecorded [winter heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2009_Argentine_winter_heat_wave) hit the country. A shot of [tropical heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat) drawn unusually far southward hiked temperatures 22 °C (72 °F) above normal in the city of [Buenos Aires](255919.md) and across the northern-centre regions of the country. Several records were broken. Even though normal high temperatures for late August are in the lower 15 °C (59 °F), readings topped 30 °C (86 °F) degrees at midweek, then topped out above 32 °C (90 °F) degrees during the weekend.

  [^cite_note-36]

  &#x20;Temperatures hit 33.8 °C (92.8 °F) on 29 August and finally 34.6 °C (94.3 °F) on 30 August in Buenos Aires, making it the hottest day ever recorded in winter breaking the 1996 winter record of 33.7 °C (92.7 °F). In the city of [Santa Fe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe,_Argentina), 38.3 °C (100.9 °F) degrees on 30 August were registered, well above the normal highs of around 15 °C (59 °F). As per the [Meteorological Office of Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servicio_Meteorol%C3%B3gico_Nacional_\(Argentina\)), August 2009 has been the warmest month during winter since official measurements began.

### 2010

- The [Northern Hemisphere summer heat wave of 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere_summer_heat_wave_of_2010) affected many areas across the [Northern Hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere), especially parts of Northeastern China and European Russia.

  [^cite_note-38]

- Starting in May 2010, records were being set. On 26 May, at [Mohenjo-daro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohenjo-daro), [Sindh](28972.md) province in Pakistan a national record high temperature of 53.5 °C (128.3 °F) occurred.

- In June 2010, Eastern Europe experienced very warm conditions. [Ruse, Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruse,_Bulgaria) hit 36.6 °C (97.9 °F) on the 13th making it the warmest spot in Europe. Other records broken on the 13th include [Vidin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidin), [Bulgaria](3415.md) at 35.8 °C (96.4 °F), [Sandanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandanski), Bulgaria hitting 35.5 °C (95.9 °F), [Lovech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lovech) and [Pazardzhik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pazardzhik), Bulgaria at 35.1 °C (95.2 °F) as well as the capital, [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia), hitting 33.3 °C (91.9 °F). The heat came from the [Sahara desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahara_desert) and was not associated with rain. This helped the situation with high water levels in that part of the continent.

  [^cite_note-39]

  &#x20;On the 14th, several cities were once again above the 35 °C (95 °F) mark even though they did not break records. The only cities in Bulgaria breaking records were [Musala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musala) peak hitting 15.2 °C (59.4 °F) and [Elhovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elhovo) hitting 35.6 °C (96.1 °F).

  [^cite_note-40]

  &#x20;On the 15th, Ruse, Bulgaria peaked at 37.2 °C (99.0 °F). Although it was not a record, this was the highest temperature recorded in the country. Five Bulgarian cities broke records that day: [Ahtopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahtopol) hit 28.6 °C (83.5 °F), [Dobrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobrich) was 33.8 °C (92.8 °F), [Karnobat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnobat) hit 34 °C (93 °F), [Sliven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sliven) hit 35 °C (95 °F) and [Elhovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elhovo) recorded 36.1 °C (97.0 °F).

  [^cite_note-41]

- From 4 to 9 July 2010, the majority of the American East Coast, from the Carolinas to Maine, was gripped in a severe heat wave. Philadelphia, New York, Baltimore, Washington, Raleigh, and even Boston eclipsed 38 °C (100 °F). Many records were broken, some of which dated back to the 19th century, including Wilmington, Delaware's temperature of 39 °C (103 °F) on Wednesday, 7 July, which broke the record of 36 °C (97 °F) from 1897. Philadelphia and New York eclipsed 38 °C (100 °F) for the first time since 2001. Frederick, Maryland, and Newark, New Jersey, among others exceeded 38 °C (100 °F) for four days in a row.

### 2012



Land surface temperature anomalies of 8–15 March 2012. [Land surface temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_temperature_measurements) are distinct from the air temperatures that meteorological stations typically measure.

- In March 2012, the [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest) experienced one of the most anomalous and [largest heat waves of all time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_2012_North_American_heat_wave).

- In late June 2012, [much of North America began experiencing a heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_2012_North_American_heat_wave), as heat spread east from the [Rocky Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountains). S. without power on 30 June.

- Around mid-February, a jet-stream of Sahara dust brought a [winter heatwave in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_winter_heatwave) with daily temperatures nearly similar to max high during spring. In Berlin, a high temperature of 20 °C (68 °F) was reported on Wednesday and the next day it reached 19 °C (66 °F). Paris reported the same high temperature of 20 °C (68 °F) while Warsaw and London had it around 18 °C (64 °F). In Asia, a record-high winter temperature was declared in Beijing on 21 February at 25.6 °C (78.1 °F).

  [^cite_note-123]

- On 20 May, the May record 31.9 °C (89.4 °F) was reported north of the Arctic Circle at 67.6° North, 53° East.

  [^cite_note-124]

  &#x20;On 20 June, the land surface temperature had widely exceeded 35 °C (95 °F) across Siberia.

  [^cite_note-125]

  &#x20;The [2021 Russia heatwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russia_heatwave) and drought caused the [2021 Siberia wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Siberia_wildfires).

  [^cite_note-126]

- From 3 to 6 June the northern [Great Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plains) and southern [Canadian Prairies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Prairies) experienced a heat wave. On 4 June, [Gretna, Manitoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gretna,_Manitoba), reached a temperature of 41.3 °C (106.3 °F), the highest recorded temperature in Manitoba since the 1980s and the earliest in the year occurrence of above 40 °C (104 °F) temperatures in Canada.

  [^cite_note-127]

- In mid-June, record temperatures were recorded in multiple parts of the [Southwestern United States](179571.md), reaching a maximum of 53 °C (128 °F) at Death Valley, California, on 17 June.

- In late June, the [2021 Western North America heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Western_North_America_heat_wave) occurred, causing temperatures to soar above 38 °C (100 °F) in the [Pacific Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest). All-time record high temperatures were recorded in cities such as [Portland](23503.md) 47 °C (116 °F) and [Seattle](11388236.md) 42 °C (108 °F). [Lytton, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lytton,_British_Columbia), reached 49.6 °C (121.3 °F), surpassing the day prior 47.9 °C (118.2 °F), which had exceeded the all-time high temperature ever recorded in [Canada](5042916.md).

  [^cite_note-128]

- From 18 June to 18 July in [Kouvola](382715.md) Finland, the [Finnish heat wave record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_extreme_temperatures_in_Finland) broke: already 31 consecutive heat days came full.

- In the last week of July, a [heat wave](293270.md) began in Turkey, Greece, Italy and other countries in the region.

  [^cite_note-129]

  [^cite_note-130]

  &#x20;On 11 August, 48.8 °C (119.8 °F), the highest temperature ever in Europe, was recorded in [Floridia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floridia), [Sicily](27619.md).

  [^cite_note-131]

- In July, a heat wave combined with drought, low natural gas production, and COVID-19 delays to cause widespread power outages across the Middle East, with protests in Iraq, Iran, and Lebanon.

  [^cite_note-132]

### 2022

Main article: [2022 heat waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_heat_waves)



A temperature anomaly map of Europe in July 2022

- During mid-January 2022, several countries of South America, including Argentina, certain parts of Brazil, Paraguay, and Uruguay, experienced a record-breaking heat wave, with temperatures over 44 °C (111 °F) and with Argentina being the most affected country.

  [^cite_note-133]

- During the second week of February 2022, multiple cities in California, including San Francisco, Sacramento, Los Angeles, and San Diego experienced a record-breaking heat wave with temperatures over 24 °C (75 °F) and with Palm Springs being the most affected city.

- Starting in late March 2022, [India began experiencing one of the hottest March–April periods on record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_India%E2%80%93Pakistan_heat_wave).

  [^cite_note-134]

- A major heat wave affected the United States throughout May. Three residents in a senior building died on 14 May in Chicago due to the intense heat, because the AC wouldn't turn on. On 19 May in Memphis, as temperatures soared to near record highs of 33 °C (91 °F), a toddler died after being left in a car. On 21 May, intense heat surged into the Mid-Atlantic, causing a near record hot [Preakness Stakes](62370.md), with Baltimore and Philadelphia hitting 35 °C (95 °F), and temperatures of 33 °C (92 °F) in Washington DC, and 32 °C (90 °F) in New York City.

- During mid-June 2022, a record-breaking heat wave affected half of the United States. Record-high temperatures were set from California to Texas on 13 June. On 14 June, dangerous heat spread to the Midwest, South, and the Plains. On 15 June, St. Louis reached a record-tying temperature of 38 °C (101 °F).

- In late June 2022, [Japan saw the worst heatwave in 150 years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Japan_heat_wave).

  [^cite_note-135]

- The [2022 European heat waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_European_heatwaves) affected much of Western Europe and the United Kingdom. Temperatures in Spain reached 45.7 °C (114.3 °F).

  [^cite_note-136]

  &#x20;The highest temperature recorded was 47.0 °C (116.6 °F) in [Pinhão](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alij%C3%B3), Portugal, on 14 July.

  [^cite_note-137]

  &#x20;The United Kingdom saw the first red extreme heat warning to ever be issued in the country, causing it to be declared a national emergency on 15 July.

  [^cite_note-138]

  &#x20;A report from the Met Office suggests that temperatures may have reached 40.3 °C (104.5 °F) at [Coningsby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coningsby) on 19 July, which is the first time the United Kingdom has exceeded 40 °C (104 °F).

  [^cite_note-139]

  &#x20;[Ireland](147575.md) also recorded its hottest day since 1887 with temperatures exceeding 33.1 °C (91.6 °F) in [Dublin](8504.md).

  [^cite_note-140]

  &#x20;Unconfirmed temperatures of over 35 °C (95 °F) were also recorded in other parts of the country.

- China suffered [several heat waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_China_heat_wave) in 2022.

  [^cite_note-141]

### 2023

Main article: [2023 heat waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_heat_waves)



2023's June–July–August season was the warmest on record globally by a large margin, as El Niño conditions continued to develop.

[^cite_note-Copernicus_202309-142]



September 2023 was the warmest September on record globally, with an average surface air temperature 0.5 °C above the temperature of the previous warmest September (2020).

[^cite_note-CopernicusSept_20231005-143]

- During mid-April 2023, [heat waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Asia_heat_wave) occurred in several countries in [South Asia](21566765.md), [Indochina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indochina), and parts of China. In what has been described as the "worst April heatwave in Asian history", several cities in the region have reported record temperatures. In [Luang Prabang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luang_Prabang), [Laos](17752.md), temperatures reached 42.7 °C (108.9 °F), the highest in Laos' recorded history, while neighboring [Thailand](30128.md) reached a record-tying 44.6 °C (112.3 °F). At least 13 people died in [Maharashtra](20629.md) state, India, as a result of heatstroke.

  [^cite_note-144]

  &#x20;In May, [Vietnam](202354.md) recorded its highest ever temperature of 44.1 °C (111.4 °F).

  [^cite_note-145]

- A [heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Western_North_America_heat_wave) affected the Pacific Northwest region of North America in May 2023.

  [^cite_note-146]

  [^cite_note-Canon_2023-147]

- Starting on 10 July 2023, a record-breaking [heat wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_European_heatwaves) affected many European countries, with effects felt most severely in [Greece](12108.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Spain](26667.md), and parts of [Southeast Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Europe). The extreme weather event [became named](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_named_heat_waves) "Cerberus" after the [hounds of Hades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerberus) from Greek mythology by the [Italian Meteorological Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Servizio_Meteorologico).

  [^cite_note-148]

  &#x20;A new heatwave in the third week of July 2023 was then called [Charon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charon).

  [^cite_note-149]

  &#x20;In [Greek mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_mythology), **Charon** or **Kharon** ([/ˈkɛərɒn, -ən/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*KAIR-on, -⁠ən*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Χάρων) is a [psychopomp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychopomp), the ferryman of [Hades, the Greek underworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_underworld).

  [^cite_note-150]

- September 2023 European heatwave: From the beginning of September, much of western Europe experienced unseasonably high temperatures. The UK recorded seven consecutive days above 30.2 °C (86.4 °F), beating the previous longest run in September of four days. Temperatures peaked at 33.5 °C (92.3 °F) at [Faversham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faversham), [Kent](16766.md). Temperatures in France were unseasonably high too with Paris recording 36.5 °C (97.7 °F), a new all-time record for the month. Ireland experienced its second warmest September on record, behind only September 2021. Temperatures reached into the high 20s in parts of southern Ireland, with temperatures in the low 30s recorded in parts of the [Midland Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Region,_Ireland).
