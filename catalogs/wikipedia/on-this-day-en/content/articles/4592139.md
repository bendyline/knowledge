**Lodhran District** ([Saraiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraiki_language): ضِلع لودهراں), is a [district](639402.md) in the province of [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_\(Pakistan\)), Pakistan, with the city of [Lodhran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodhran) as its capital. Located on the northern side of the River [Sutlej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutlej), it is bounded to the north by the districts of [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan_District), [Khanewal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanewal_District) and [Vehari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehari_District), to the south by [Bahawalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahawalpur_District), to the east lie the districts of Vehari and Bahawalpur; while district Multan lies on the western side.

Lodhran was split off as a separate district from [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan_District) in 1991.[^cite_note-Dawn-3] It has the lowest Human Development Index of all districts in Punjab, and is among the thirty poorest districts in Pakistan.[^cite_note-Mughal2014-4]<sup>: 85 </sup> It is a well-known cotton-growing area.[^cite_note-Dawn-3]

## Administrative divisions

Lodhran District is spread over an area of 2,778 square kilometres and is subdivided into three [tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) ([Lodhran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodhran_Tehsil), [Kahror Pakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahror_Pacca_Tehsil) and [Dunyapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunyapur_Tehsil)) which contain a total of 73 [Union Councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Councils_of_Pakistan):[^cite_note-NRB-5]

As of the [2023 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census), Lodhran district has 323,866 households and a population of 1,928,299. The district has a sex ratio of 107.26 males to 100 females and a literacy rate of 51.68%: 60.63% for males and 42.12% for females.[^cite_note-2023table1-1][^cite_note-2023table12-10] 540,664 (28.07% of the surveyed population) are under 10 years of age.[^cite_note-2023table5-11] 325,053 (16.86%) live in urban areas.[^cite_note-2023table1-1]

Languages of Lodhran district (2023)

[^cite_note-2023table11-17]

1. [Saraiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraiki_language) (73.8%)

2. [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) (15.2%)

3. [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) (6.41%)

4. [Mewati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mewati_language) (2.19%)

5. Others (2.36%)

At the time of the 2023 census, 73.81% of the population spoke [Saraiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraiki_language), 15.23% [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language), 6.41% [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu), and 2.19% [Mewati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mewati_language) as their first language.[^cite_note-2023table11-17]

The most widely spoken first language is [Saraiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraiki_language) (70%), which is used by the major indigenous social groups of the Joya, Baloch, Awan, [Arain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arain), Kanju, Uttera/Uttero, Ghallu, Bhutta, Lodhra, Metla, Chaner Syed, [Qureshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qureshi), [Tareen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tareen) and [Pathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathans_of_Punjab). Additionally, [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) is spoken by about 19%. The percentage of the district's population who declared [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) as their language at the 1998 census was 9%; this includes these Haryanvi speakers as well as other, smaller, groups of Muhajirs such as the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_tribe). Additionally, the nomadic Od people are speakers of the [Od language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Od_language), while [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto) (0.2%) is spoken by [Pashtuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns).[^cite_note-city-18][^cite_note-Mughal2014-4]<sup>: 83 </sup>
