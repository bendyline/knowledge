**McDonald's Corporation** is an American multinational [fast food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_food) restaurant chain. Founded in 1940 as a restaurant operated by brothers [Richard and Maurice McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_and_Maurice_McDonald) in [San Bernardino, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino,_California), the company has grown into one of the world's largest restaurant chains. At the end of 2025, the McDonald's system comprised 45,356 restaurants in more than 100 countries, approximately 95% of which were operated by [franchisees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franchisee).[^cite_note-MCD2025-1]

The McDonald brothers introduced their streamlined food-production system, later known as the Speedee Service System, during the 1940s. The [Golden Arches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Arches) design was introduced in 1953.[^cite_note-Hughes2008-2] Businessman [Ray Kroc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Kroc) became a McDonald's franchise agent in 1955 and opened a restaurant in [Des Plaines, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Des_Plaines,_Illinois). He purchased the brothers' ownership stake in the company in 1961 and oversaw its domestic and international expansion.[^cite_note-3]

McDonald's generates revenue from sales at company-operated restaurants and from rent, royalties, and fees paid by franchisees. At the end of 2025, the company owned approximately 56% of the land and 80% of the restaurant buildings within its consolidated markets.[^cite_note-MCD2025-1] McDonald's directly employed more than 150,000 people, while more than two million additional people worked at independently operated franchised restaurants.[^cite_note-MCD2025-1]

The company's menu is centered on hamburgers and French fries, with other offerings including chicken, breakfast items, soft drinks, coffee, desserts, and products adapted to local markets. Its best-known products include the [Big Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Mac) and its French fries.[^cite_note-4] The company has faced [criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criticism_of_McDonald's) concerning the nutritional content of its food, treatment of animals, environmental impact, and employee wages and working conditions.[^cite_note-5]

## History

### Origin

Siblings [Richard and Maurice McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_and_Maurice_McDonald), with the goal of making \$1 million before they turned 50,[^cite_note-Gilpin_1998-6] opened the first McDonald's at 1398 North E Street at West 14th Street\[^cite_note-[www.mcdonalds.com-7](http://www.mcdonalds.com-7/)] in [San Bernardino, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino,_California), on May 15, 1940.[^cite_note-8]\[^cite_note-[www.mcdonalds.com-7](http://www.mcdonalds.com-7/)]

#### Speedee Service System

The McDonald brothers introduced the "Speedee Service System" in 1948, putting into expanded use the principles of the modern [fast-food restaurant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_food#Overview) that predecessor [White Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Castle_\(restaurant\)) had put into practice more than two decades earlier.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] When the brothers made the Speedee Service System, they fired their 20 [carhops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carhop), removed their [silverware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silverware_\(household\)) and plates for paper wrappings and cups so that they no longer needed a [dishwasher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dishwasher), and simplified their menu to just nine items: hamburgers, cheeseburgers, three [soft drink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_drink) flavors in one 12-ounce size, [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk), [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), [potato chips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato_chips) and [pie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pie). "Our whole concept was based on speed, lower prices, and volume," Richard McDonald later said.[^cite_note-11]

#### Mascots

The original mascot of McDonald's was a hamburger-headed chef who was referred to as "Speedee".[^cite_note-12] In 1962, the [Golden Arches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Arches) replaced Speedee as the universal mascot.[^cite_note-Hughes2008-2] [Clown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clown) mascot [Ronald McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_McDonald) was introduced in 1963 to market the chain to children.[^cite_note-13]

### Ray Kroc



Logo from 1940 until 1948



Logo from 1948 until 1953



Logo from 1953 until 1960

The present corporation credits its founding to [franchised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franchising) businessman [Ray Kroc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Kroc) on April 15, 1955.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] This was the ninth opened McDonald's restaurant overall, although this location was demolished in 1984.[^cite_note-16] Kroc was recorded as being an aggressive business partner, driving the McDonald brothers out of the industry.[^cite_note-Smith2009-17]

Kroc and the McDonald brothers fought for control of the business, as documented in Kroc's autobiography. In 1961, he purchased the McDonald brothers' equity in the company and began the company's worldwide reach.[^cite_note-18] The sale cost Kroc \$2.7 million (worth almost \$29,089,644 in the current day).[^cite_note-Smith2009-17] The San Bernardino restaurant was eventually torn down in 1971, and the site was sold to the [Juan Pollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Pollo) chain in 1998. This area serves as headquarters for the Juan Pollo chain, and a McDonald's and [Route 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_66) museum.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] With the expansion of McDonald's into many international markets, the company has become a symbol of [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization) and the spread of the [American way of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_way_of_life).[^cite_note-21]

### Changes

&#x20;In 1994, McDonald's [banned smoking](386818.md) at the 1,400 United States locations it wholly owned.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]



An American McDonald's in [Franklin, North Carolina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin,_North_Carolina), in 2024; it is an example of the "new" look of American McDonald's restaurants

In 2006, McDonald's introduced its "Forever Young" brand by redesigning all of its restaurants. The restaurants were turned into cafe-esque space, being mostly terracotta, grey, and olive. The redesigned restaurants were also primarily furnished with wood furniture.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

As a result of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), McDonald's closed most seating and all play areas in its United States restaurants.[^cite_note-26] It transitioned to drive-through and curbside orders at locations and [online food ordering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Online_food_ordering) delivery services.[^cite_note-27]

### Menu



French fries, a soft drink, McSpicy Chicken Fillet, and [tomato ketchup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato_ketchup) packet

McDonald's predominantly sells [hamburgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger), [cheeseburgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheeseburger), various types of [chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_as_food), [chicken sandwiches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_sandwich), [French fries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_fries), soft drinks, [shakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milkshake), breakfast items, and desserts. In most markets, McDonald's offers [salads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salad) and vegetarian items, [wraps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrap_\(food\)) and other localized fare. On a seasonal basis, McDonald's offers the [McRib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McRib) sandwich.[^cite_note-28] During March of each year, McDonald's offers a [Shamrock Shake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shamrock_Shake) to honor [Saint Patrick's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Patrick's_Day).[^cite_note-29] The company often introduces items temporarily or brings them back after long absences.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

McDonald's has offered the [Happy Meal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Meal), a [kids' meal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kids'_meal), since June 1979, typically including a small toy or book packaged with the food.

In addition, the chain also sells some items within the United States on a regional basis; for example, the Hatch Green Chile Double Cheeseburger, which is topped with [New Mexico green chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico_green_chile), is only available in the southwestern state of [New Mexico](21649.md).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In 2021, the company cut value meals and cheaper items from its menu as part of a focus on higher-priced items.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

### Health improvements

When [Steve Easterbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Easterbrook) became CEO of the company in 2015, McDonald's streamlined the menu which in the United States contained nearly 200 items. The company looked to introduce healthier options and removed [high-fructose corn syrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-fructose_corn_syrup) from [hamburger buns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bun). The company removed [artificial preservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preservative) from [Chicken McNuggets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_McNuggets),[^cite_note-36] replacing chicken skin, safflower oil and citric acid found in Chicken McNuggets with pea starch, rice starch, and powdered lemon juice.[^cite_note-37]

In September 2018, McDonald's USA announced that they no longer use artificial preservatives, flavors and colors in seven classic burgers sold in the U.S., including the hamburger, cheeseburger, double cheeseburger, McDouble, Quarter Pounder with Cheese, double Quarter Pounder with Cheese and the Big Mac.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] The pickles will still be made with an artificial preservative, although customers can choose to opt out of getting pickles with their burgers.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

### McPlant

In November 2020, McDonald's announced [McPlant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McPlant), a plant-based burger, along with plans to develop additional meat alternative menu items that extend to chicken substitutes and breakfast sandwiches.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-Yahoo_Finance_-_McPlant_Announcement-43] This announcement came after the successful testing of [Beyond Meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beyond_Meat) plant based meat substitutes.[^cite_note-Pochin2022-44]

In late 2022, McDonald's announced the addition of the Double McPlant at all restaurants in the United Kingdom and Ireland starting January 4 due to the success of the McPlant.[^cite_note-Pochin2022-44] In 2024, select McDonald's in the UK were testing vegan ice cream called Choco Scoop and Strawberry Scoop and receiving positive reviews.[^cite_note-45]

### International menu variations

See also: [International availability of McDonald's products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_availability_of_McDonald's_products)

#### Asia

McDonald's menu is customized to reflect consumer tastes in their respective countries. Restaurants in several countries, particularly in Asia, serve [soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soup). This local deviation from the standard menu is a characteristic for which the chain is particularly known and one which is employed either to abide by regional [food taboos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_taboo) (such as the [religious prohibition of beef consumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetarianism_and_religion) in India) or to make available foods with which the regional market is more familiar (such as the sale of McRice in Indonesia, or [Ebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebi_korokke) (prawn) Burger in Singapore and Japan). McDonald's restaurants in China include fried buns and soybean milk on their breakfast menus.[^cite_note-46]

In partnership with the [Central Food Technological Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Food_Technological_Research_Institute) in [India](14533.md), McDonald's has introduced [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet)-based [buns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bun) on September 4, 2024. Three major ([Pearl millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_millet), [Sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum), and [Finger millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_millet)) and two minor ([Proso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proso_millet) and *[Paspalum scrobiculatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paspalum_scrobiculatum)*) millets are used which makes up 22% of the bun. It provides more calcium, iron, and protein in addition to a small amount of complex carbs to aid in satiety more quickly.[^cite_note-47]

#### Europe

&#x20;Throughout Europe, the McDonald's menu may be altered or feature country-exclusive items to suit national tastes. For example, a cheeseburger in Switzerland may include [Gruyère cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruy%C3%A8re_cheese),[^cite_note-48] and [caldo verde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldo_verde) is served in Portugal.[^cite_note-49]



A Croque McDo from France

In France, McDonald's is colloquially called "McDo". They sell [macaroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaroon), Croque McDos, [croissants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croissant), [pain au chocolat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pain_au_chocolat), and [beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer).[^cite_note-50] In Germany and some other Western European countries, McDonald's also sells beer. In Greece, the signature hamburger, Big Mac, is changed by adding Tzatziki sauce and packaging in a pita.[^cite_note-51]

#### Oceania

In New Zealand, until 2020, McDonald's sold [meat pies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat_pie_\(Australia_and_New_Zealand\)) after local affiliate [McDonald's New Zealand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonald's_New_Zealand) (known locally as "Macca's"[^cite_note-52]) partially relaunched the [Georgie Pie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgie_Pie) fast food chain it bought out in 1996.[^cite_note-53] In Australia, the "McOz" burger, containing beetroot, is perennially available, as are fries dusted in [Vegemite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegemite).[^cite_note-54]

#### North America

After limited regional trials, McDonald's began offering a partial breakfast menu during all hours its restaurants were open in the United States and Canada in 2015[^cite_note-55] and 2017,[^cite_note-56] respectively. All-day breakfast was phased out from menus in the United States at the start of the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) in 2020. The all-day breakfast menu is still available in select locations across Canada.[^cite_note-57]

In the United States, most standalone McDonald's restaurants offer both [counter service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter_service) and [drive-through](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drive-through) service, with indoor and sometimes [outdoor seating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outdoor_dining).[^cite_note-Mohapatra2012-62]

### McDrive

Drive-Thru, Auto-Mac, Pay and Drive, or "McDrive" as it is known in many countries, often has separate stations for placing, paying for, and picking up orders while the customer remains in their vehicle;[^cite_note-Mohapatra2012-62] it was first introduced in [Sierra Vista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Vista,_Arizona), Arizona in 1975,[^cite_note-64] following the lead of other fast-food chains. The first such restaurant in Britain opened at [Fallowfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallowfield), Manchester, in 1986.[^cite_note-65] In some countries, McDrive locations near [highways](48519.md) offer no counter service or seating. In contrast, locations in high-density city neighborhoods often omit drive-through service.[^cite_note-66] There are also a few locations, mostly in downtown districts, that offer a "Walk-Thru" service in place of Drive-Thru.[^cite_note-67]

### McCafé

Main article: [McCafé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCaf%C3%A9)



A McCafé-branded mobile coffee truck in China, 2024. McDonald’s has expanded the McCafé concept through modular and mobile retail formats in parts of Asia.

McCafé is a [café](370815.md)-style accompaniment to McDonald's restaurants. The concept was created by McDonald's Australia, starting with [Melbourne](17306237.md) in 1993.[^cite_note-68] In China, McCafé has expanded through small-format retail concepts such as kiosks, walk-up stands, and mobile coffee trucks. These formats support high-density areas and office districts where full restaurant build-outs are impractical.

### McDonald's Next

McDonald's Next uses open-concept design and offers "Create Your Taste" [digital ordering](9611.md). The concept store also offers free mobile device charging and table service after 6\:00 pm. The first store opened in Hong Kong in December 2015.[^cite_note-cnn-20160111-69] The buildings are not colorful, and instead use more natural colors, like from [concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete) and unpolished oak.[^cite_note-70]

### PlayPlaces

Main article: [PlayPlace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayPlace)



A McDonald's in [Panorama City, Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panorama_City,_Los_Angeles), California with a Playplace designed to promote a family-friendly image

McDonald's playgrounds used to be called Playlands and were outside. After concerns were raised that the playgrounds were dangerous, McDonald's moved them inside and began to make them out of softer [materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Material), such as plastic instead of metal. They renamed them PlayPlaces in 1987.[^cite_note-71]

### McExpress and McStop

Some locations are connected to [gas stations](61260.md) and convenience stores,[^cite_note-72] while others called McExpress have limited seating or menu or may be located in a shopping mall. Other McDonald's are located in [Walmart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walmart) stores. McStop is a location targeted at truckers and travelers who may have services found at [truck stops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truck_stop).[^cite_note-73]

### CosMc's

Main article: [CosMc's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CosMc's)

In July 2023, the company announced it was working toward a new fast-food brand called [CosMc's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CosMc's), which would be tested at 10 sites. The first location was opened in December 2023 in [Bolingbrook, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolingbrook,_Illinois). The outlet had a smaller real-estate footprint than regular McDonald's restaurants and focused on selling coffee and other drinks to afternoon customers.[^cite_note-74]

The name for the new brand came from CosMc, a McDonaldland mascot that appeared in advertisements in the late 1980s and early 1990s.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

Although four additional locations were eventually opened in Texas, McDonald's announced in May 2025 that all locations would be shuttered and the associated app removed by the end of June of that year. Some CosMc's beverage flavors would be tested in other U.S. McDonald's locations.[^cite_note-77]

McDonald's restaurants are in 120 countries and territories and serve 68 million customers daily.[^cite_note-daily-79] There are 41,800 McDonald's locations as of 2023, and 45,000 employees in the United States.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

The McDonald's on-demand delivery concept, which began in 2017 with a partnership with [Uber Eats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber_Eats) and added [DoorDash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DoorDash) in 2019 (with select locations adding [Grubhub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grubhub) in 2021), accounts for up to 3% of all business as of 2019.[^cite_note-82]

The \$100 billion in sales generated by McDonald's company-owned and franchise restaurants in 2019 accounts for almost 4% of the estimated \$2.5 trillion global restaurant industry.[^cite_note-83]

#### Stock holdings

Focusing on its core brand, McDonald's began [divesting](981983.md) itself of other chains it had acquired during the 1990s. The company owned a majority stake in [Chipotle Mexican Grill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chipotle_Mexican_Grill) until October 2006, when McDonald's fully divested from Chipotle through a stock exchange.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-mcds-dbj-85] Until December 2003, it owned [Donatos Pizza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donatos_Pizza), and it owned a small share of [Aroma Café](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aroma_Caf%C3%A9), from 1999 to 2001. On August 27, 2007, McDonald's sold [Boston Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Market) to [Sun Capital Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Capital_Partners).[^cite_note-Chogath_Kog'sante-86]

McDonald's has increased shareholder dividends for 25 consecutive years,[^cite_note-ReutersOnMcDividend-87] making it one of the [S\&P 500 Dividend Aristocrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S\&P_500_Dividend_Aristocrats).[^cite_note-seekAlphaFullListDivArist-88] The company is ranked 131st on the [*Fortune* 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) of the [largest United States corporations by revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_companies_in_the_United_States_by_revenue).[^cite_note-89] In October 2012, its monthly sales fell for the first time in nine years.[^cite_note-90] In 2014, its quarterly sales fell for the first time in seventeen years, when its sales dropped for the entirety of 1997.[^cite_note-91]

#### Monetary loss

In the United States, it is reported that drive-throughs account for 70 percent of sales.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93] McDonald's closed down 184 restaurants in the United States in 2015, which was 59 more than what they planned to open.[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-cutstores-95] This move was the first time McDonald's had a net decrease in the number of locations in the United States since 1970.[^cite_note-cutstores-95]

[Ray Kroc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Kroc) joined the chain in 1954 and built it into a global franchise, making it the most successful fast food corporation in the world.

#### Ownership

McDonald's owns the land on which many of its restaurants are situated,[^cite_note-Budzyn_2017-121] which is valued at an estimated \$42 billion.[^cite_note-122] The company earns a significant portion of its revenue from rental payments from franchisees. These rent payments rose 26 percent between 2010 and 2015, accounting for one-fifth of the company's total revenue at the end of the period.[^cite_note-123] In recent times, there have been calls to spin off the company's U.S. holdings into a potential [real estate investment trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate_investment_trust), but the company announced at its investor conference on November 10, 2015, that this would not happen. CEO [Steve Easterbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Easterbrook) discussed that pursuing the REIT option would pose too large a risk to the company's business model.[^cite_note-124]

McDonald's trains its franchisees and management at one of the [Hamburger Universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_University). As of 2015, the Hamburger Universities reside in [Oak Brook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oak_Brook,_Illinois), [Tokyo](30057.md), [London](17867.md), [Sydney](27862.md), [Munich](19058.md), [São Paulo](390875.md), [Shanghai](27643.md), and [Moscow](19004.md).[^cite_note-125] In other countries, McDonald's restaurants are operated by joint ventures of McDonald's Corporation and other, local entities or governments.[^cite_note-126]

Quality, Service, Cleanliness & Value is a corporate [motto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motto) adopted by McDonald's to describe the company's philosophy for operating restaurants.[^cite_note-127] The motto is codified in the procedures McDonald's uses to evaluate its [franchisees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franchisees).[^cite_note-128]

#### Workers

According to *[Fast Food Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_Food_Nation)* by [Eric Schlosser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Schlosser) (2001), nearly one in eight workers in the U.S. have at some time been employed by McDonald's. Employees are encouraged by McDonald's Corp. to maintain their health by singing along to their favorite songs to relieve stress, attending [church services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_service) to have a lower [blood pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_pressure), and taking two vacations annually to reduce the risk for [myocardial infarction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myocardial_infarction).[^cite_note-129]

*Fast Food Nation* states that McDonald's is the largest private operator of playgrounds in the U.S; as well as the single largest purchaser of beef, pork, potatoes, and apples. The selection of meats McDonald's uses varies to some extent based on the host country's culture.[^cite_note-130]

#### International influence

McDonald's has become a symbol of [globalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization), sometimes referred to as the "[McDonaldization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonaldization)" of society. *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* newspaper uses the "[Big Mac Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Mac_Index)": the comparison of the cost of a [Big Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Mac) in various world [currencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Currency) can be used to informally judge these currencies' [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity). Switzerland has the most expensive Big Mac in the world as of July 2015, while the country with the least expensive Big Mac is India[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-144] (albeit for a [Maharaja Mac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharaja_Mac)—the next cheapest Big Mac is Hong Kong).[^cite_note-145] The northernmost McDonald's restaurant in the world is located in [Tromsø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troms%C3%B8), Norway (since January 2024);[^cite_note-146] and the southernmost is in [Invercargill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invercargill), New Zealand.[^cite_note-147]

[Thomas Friedman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Friedman) said that no country with a McDonald's had gone to war with another.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149]

McDonald's suspended operations in its corporate-owned stores in Crimea after Russia annexed the region in 2014.[^cite_note-150] On August 20, 2014, as tensions [between the United States and Russia](4482509.md) strained over [the Russian annexation of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), and the [resultant U.S. sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War), the Russian government temporarily shut down four McDonald's outlets in Moscow, citing sanitary concerns. The company has operated in Russia since 1990 and, in August 2014, had 438 stores across the country.[^cite_note-McDonaldsMoscow-151] On August 23, 2014, Russian Deputy Prime Minister [Arkady Dvorkovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkady_Dvorkovich) ruled out any government move to ban McDonald's and dismissed the notion that the temporary closures had anything to do with the sanctions.[^cite_note-McDonaldsRussia-152]

Some observers have suggested that the company should be given credit for increasing the service standard in markets it enters. A group of anthropologists in a study entitled *Golden Arches East*[^cite_note-153] looked at McDonald's impact on East Asia and Hong Kong, particularly. When it opened in Hong Kong in 1975, McDonald's was the first restaurant to consistently offer clean restrooms, driving customers to demand the same of other restaurants and institutions. On June 20, 2006, McDonald's partnered with [Sinopec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinopec) to open drive-throughs at its Chinese petrol stations, tapping into the nation's rising car culture.[^cite_note-154]

#### Technology uses

Since the late 1990s, McDonald's has attempted to replace employees with [electronic kiosks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interactive_kiosk) that would perform actions such as taking orders and accepting money. In 1999, McDonald's first tested "E-Clerks" in suburban [Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago,_Illinois), and [Wyoming, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyoming,_Michigan), with the devices being able to "save money on live staffers" and attracting larger purchase amounts than average employees.[^cite_note-ECLERK-179]

In 2013, the [University of Oxford](31797.md) estimated that in the succeeding decades, there was a 92% probability of food preparation and serving becoming automated in fast food establishments.[^cite_note-180] By 2016, McDonald's "Create Your Taste" electronic kiosks were seen in some restaurants internationally where customers could custom order meals.[^cite_note-181]

In 2017, McDonald's launched an app in the United States that allows customers to skip the ordering line inside or drive through and order online. Many McDonald's locations have special parking spaces for such orders.[^cite_note-182]

In September 2019, McDonald's purchased an AI-based start-up, Apprente, to replace human servers with voice-based technology in its US drive-throughs.[^cite_note-183]

In early 2023, McDonald's opened its first largely automated restaurant in [Fort Worth, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Worth,_Texas). The restaurant in question would de-emphasize human contact when ordering, with employees available if there were problems but who would otherwise be absent from the ordering process (along with others working in the kitchen or other back-of-the-house roles). There was no seating in this restaurant.[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185]

#### Worker complaints



McDonald's workers striking against coercive labor practices and low wages, 2018

In March 2015, McDonald's workers in 19 U.S. cities filed 28 health and safety complaints with the U.S. [Occupational Safety and Health Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_Safety_and_Health_Administration), which allege that low staffing, lack of protective gear, poor training, and pressure to work fast resulted in injuries. The complaints allege that, because of a lack of [first aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_aid) supplies, workers were told by management to treat [burn injuries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burn_injuries) with condiments such as [mayonnaise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayonnaise) and [mustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_\(condiment\)).[^cite_note-186] The Fight for \$15 labor organization aided the workers in filing the complaints.[^cite_note-187]

From 2007 to 2011, fast food workers in the U.S. drew an average of \$7 billion of [public assistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_assistance) annually resulting from receiving low wages.[^cite_note-188] The McResource website advised employees to break their food into smaller pieces to feel fuller, seek refunds for unopened holiday purchases, sell possessions online for quick cash, and "quit complaining" as "stress hormone levels rise by 15 percent after ten minutes of complaining."[^cite_note-189] In December 2013, McDonald's shut down the McResource website amidst negative publicity and criticism. McDonald's plans to continue an internal telephone help line through which its employees can obtain advice on work and life problems.[^cite_note-190]

The [Roosevelt Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roosevelt_Institute), a [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_\(politics\)) [think tank](37101.md), accuses some McDonald's restaurants of actually paying less than the minimum wage to entry positions due to "rampant" [wage theft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wage_theft).[^cite_note-191]

### Nutrition

In 1990, activists from a small group known as [London Greenpeace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Greenpeace) (no connection to the international group [Greenpeace](12233.md)) distributed leaflets entitled *What's wrong with McDonald's?*, criticizing its environmental, health, and labor record. The corporation wrote to the group demanding they desist and apologize, and, when two of the activists refused to back down, sued them for [libel](28661.md) leading to the "[McLibel case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLibel_case)", one of the longest cases in English civil law. [A documentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLibel_\(film\)) film of the McLibel Trial has been shown in several countries.[^cite_note-\:1-205]

[Morgan Spurlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Spurlock)'s 2004 documentary film *[Super Size Me](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Size_Me)* claimed that McDonald's food was contributing to the increase of [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity) in society and that the company was failing to provide nutritional information about its food for its customers. Within weeks of the film's premiere, McDonald's announced that it was eliminating the super size option[^cite_note-206] and was creating the adult [Happy Meal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Meal).[^cite_note-207]

In response to public pressure, McDonald's has sought to include more healthy choices in its menu, announcing in May 2008 that, in the United States and Canada, it has switched to using cooking oil that contains no [trans fats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_fat) for its french fries, and [canola](71016451.md)-based oil with corn and soy oils, for its baked items, pies, and cookies, by the end of 2018.[^cite_note-208] The company introduced a new slogan to its recruitment posters: "Not bad for a [McJob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McJob)."[^cite_note-mcjob-209]

### Animal welfare

McDonald's has been criticized for using factory farmed fast growing chicken breeds in their supply chain.[^cite_note-210] These breeds are more likely to suffer from welfare issues such as broken or fractured bones, leg deformities and heart failure.[^cite_note-211][^cite_note-212]

Since McDonald's purchases over 2 billion eggs per year, which accounts for about 4 percent of eggs produced in the United States, the switch to cage-free eggs is expected to have a major impact on the egg industry. It is part of a general trend toward cage-free eggs driven by consumer concern over the harsh living conditions of hens.[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-Wired2016-214] In 2014, McDonald's pledged to stop using eggs from [battery cage](6693736.md) facilities in restaurants in Australia by 2017, which was met in 2018.[^cite_note-215][^cite_note-216]

McDonald's has been the subject of animal rights activism. In 2021, [Animal Rising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_Rising) blockaded four UK distribution centers using bamboo towers, leading to food shortages at restaurants.[^cite_note-217] They cited "suffering of animals" in the McDonald's supply chain as a motivating factor.[^cite_note-218]

### Legal cases

Main article: [McDonald's legal cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonald's_legal_cases)

#### European Union

In April 2017, Irish fast-food chain [Supermac's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermac's) submitted a request to the European Union Property Office to cancel McDonald's owned trademarks within the European Union, claiming that McDonald's engaged in "trademark bullying; registering brand names... which are simply stored away in a war chest to use against future competitors", after the trademarks had prevented Supermac's from expanding out of Ireland. The EUIPO ruled in Supermac's favor, finding that McDonald's "has not proven genuine use" of many trademarks, canceling McDonald's owned trademarks such as "Big Mac" and specific "Mc"-related trademarks within the European Union.[^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221]

[Burger King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burger_King) responded by "trolling" McDonald's by giving their burgers names that included the words "Big Mac" that also mocked the original burger, which included "Like a Big Mac But Juicier", "Like a Big Mac, But Actually Big" and "Big Mac-ish But Flame-Grilled of Course."[^cite_note-222]

In 2023, the EUIPO Board of Appeal partially annulled their decision after McDonald's filed 700 pages of additional evidence despite objections.[^cite_note-223] However, on further appeal to the [European Court of Justice](87187.md), the court ruled in 2024 that McDonald's had failed to prove the use of the Big Mac trademark in relation to chicken products or services associated with operating restaurants, meaning Supermac's and other businesses are now free to use the "Mac" in their business names and in names for poultry products in Europe. McDonald's retains the Big Mac trademark solely for its beef burgers.[^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225][^cite_note-226]

The McDonald's group has had proceedings taken against it by the French Tax Authorities, with possible charges of criminal tax fraud. In July 2022, the group reached an agreement with the French judicial authorities to end criminal proceedings for tax fraud.[^cite_note-227]

#### Malaysia

On September 8, 2009, McDonald's Malaysian operations lost a lawsuit to prevent another restaurant from calling itself McCurry. McDonald's lost in an appeal to Malaysia's highest court, the Federal Court.[^cite_note-228]

On December 29, 2016, McDonald's Malaysian issued a statement that said only certified halal cakes are allowed inside its restaurants nationwide.[^cite_note-229]

#### United Kingdom

The longest-running legal action of all time in the UK was the [McLibel case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McLibel_case) against two defendants who criticized several aspects of the company. The trial lasted ten years and called 130 witnesses. The [European Court of Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Court_of_Human_Rights) deemed that the unequal resources of the litigants breached the defendants' rights to freedom of speech and biased the trial. The result was widely seen as a "PR disaster" for McDonald's.[^cite_note-\:1-205]

In 2021, it emerged that at least 50 employees had filed charges against the chains regarding harassment in the workplace in five years, leading to the company instituting anti-harassment training. Some of the complainants also stated that they were verbally and physically harassed in retaliation for their complaints.[^cite_note-230] In 2023, the BBC conducted an investigation, and reported that more than 100 current and recent UK workers at outlets of the fast-food chain allege there is a continuing toxic culture of sexual assault, harassment, racism and bullying.[^cite_note-231]

In January 2025, it was reported that over 700 young workers had filed lawsuits against McDonald's UK, alleging widespread harassment and discrimination. The claims, handled by the law firm Leigh Day, involved current and former employees under the age of 20 and spanned more than 450 restaurants. Following a BBC investigation in July 2023, testimonies highlighted issues such as racism, homophobia, ableism, and harassment. McDonald's UK, dismissed 29 workers for sexual harassment in 2023 and established a specialist unit to investigate such allegations. However, unions argued that these measures did not resolve the issues. Employees shared accounts of inappropriate behavior, including homophobic remarks, sexual advances, and unwanted physical contact from managers. Similar allegations had been raised in 2019, with the Bakers, Food and Allied Workers Union reporting over 1,000 cases of harassment involving female employees.[^cite_note-232] In March 2025, the Equality and Human Rights Commission wrote to all 1,400 McDonald's branches telling them they must comply with their legal duties, or risk enforcement action.[^cite_note-233]

In June 2025, it was reported that McDonald's was attempting to block the expansion of the Irish chain [Supermac's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermac's) into the UK market and intended to proceed with IPO trademark court proceedings in the UK, despite having been unsuccessful in similar proceedings against Supermac's in the EU in 2024.[^cite_note-234]

#### United States

The 1994 court case *[Liebeck v. McDonald's Restaurants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liebeck_v._McDonald's_Restaurants)* examined a McDonald's practice of serving coffee so hot that when spilled, it caused [third degree burns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_degree_burns) requiring weeks of hospitalization and [skin grafting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_grafting) surgery.[^cite_note-Goodman_2011-235] The trial outcome was an award of \$2.86 million (equivalent to \$5.47 million in 2024) for the plaintiff, 81-year old Stella Liebeck.[^cite_note-Goodman_2011-235] The amount was later reduced to \$640,000 (equivalent to \$1.2 million in 2024).&#x20;

In 2019, a McDonald's employee, Jenna Ries, sued the restaurant chain over allowing [sexual harassment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_harassment) in the workplace and described the [working environment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workplace) as "toxic".[^cite_note-236]

In 2023, an investigation by the [United States Department of Labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Labor) found [child labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_labor_in_the_United_States) violations at McDonald's franchises in the state of Kentucky, which involved over 300 children, two of them 10-year-olds. A total of \$212,000 was levied against three franchises for the violations.[^cite_note-237] Further investigations uncovered child labor violations involving 83 minors at 16 different locations in Louisiana and Texas, with imposed fines amounting to \$77,572.[^cite_note-238]

### Commercial surveillance

McDonald's has become a significant actor in the landscape of [commercial surveillance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surveillance_capitalism), leveraging extensive data collection to drive customer retention and revenue. Through its loyalty program, the company aggregates granular [behavioral data](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behavioral_analytics) — including purchase history, promotional interactions, location data and "online activity on other websites, applications or social media"[^cite_note-\:2-239] — to feed predictive algorithms that estimate the customer lifetime value and probability of future visits. The scale of this monitoring was highlighted in 2024 when a reporter obtained a 515-page dossier on his own profile, through [California’s Consumer Privacy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Consumer_Privacy_Act), revealing a depth of personal tracking that was associated with intelligence agencies rather than private enterprises. This practice exemplifies how non-tech companies have incentives to collect, process and aggregate as much data as possible to increase their profits. Critics from CyLab Security and Privacy Institute and the [Center for Digital Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Digital_Democracy?action=edit\&redlink=1) argue that such corporate profiling, normalizes massive data collection and undermines consumers privacy.[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241][^cite_note-\:2-239][^cite_note-242]

## Marketing and advertising

Main article: [McDonald's advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonald's_advertising)

McDonald's has maintained several advertising campaigns for decades. In addition to the usual media (television, radio, and newspaper), the company makes use of billboards and signage. Also, it sponsors sporting events ranging from [Little League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_League_Baseball) to the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) and [Olympic Games](22576.md).[^cite_note-243] Television has been central to the company's advertising strategy.[^cite_note-244] To date, McDonald's has used 23 different slogans in United States advertising, as well as a few other slogans for select countries and regions.[^cite_note-245]

In 2026, [Wired](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_\(magazine\)) published a report on data McDonald's collects on customers who are a part of the loyalty program. The data collected includes purchases, loyalty-points history, activity, and any promotional offers associated with an account. According to McDonald's, this data is used with the purpose of helping provide personalized deals and messages. These data is also used to predict McDonald's-related behavior, including predicting what a customer will eat, when a customer will visit, the [odds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odds) that a customer will visit a restaurant ("Customer Attrition Likelihood"), and how much will be spent on average.[^cite_note-246][^cite_note-247]

### Children's advertising

Main articles: [Ronald McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_McDonald) and [McDonaldland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonaldland)

McDonald's uses [playgrounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playground), games, and [toys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toy) to make children want to go to McDonald's.[^cite_note-248]

### Celebrity endorsements

#### Sports

In 1992, basketball player [Michael Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jordan) became the first celebrity to have a McDonald's value meal named after him. The "McJordan" was available at Chicago franchises. The exclusivity of the meal lead to fans in other regions being envious, more notably leading to a man buying a gallon of the McJordan BBQ sauce for 10,000 dollars.[^cite_note-249]

[LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James) was a spokesman for McDonald's from 2003 to 2017,[^cite_note-250] while co-endorsing Coca-Cola-Sprite since early in his career.[^cite_note-251]

In January 2025, [WNBA](57721.md) player [Angel Reese](70605969.md) became the first woman to partner with McDonald's, creating the Angel Reese Special.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253] Ordering the meal as a double Quarter Pounder was promoted in reference to Reese's consistent [double-doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double-double) during games.[^cite_note-254]

#### Music

In September 2020, McDonald's partnered with rapper [Travis Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Scott) to release the "Travis Scott Meal" nationwide. Scott designed new uniforms for McDonald's employees and released Cactus Jack merchandise using vintage visuals from the fast food chain's history.[^cite_note-255] The company followed up with the "J Balvin Meal" in a partnership with [reggaeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggaeton) singer [J Balvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_Balvin).[^cite_note-256]

In 2021, McDonald's partnered with Korean [boy group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boy_band) [BTS](39862325.md) to release the "[BTS Meal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTS_Meal)" in 50 countries around the world, starting on May 26 in select countries.[^cite_note-257]

### Space exploration

McDonald's and [NASA](18426568.md) explored an advertising agreement for a planned mission to the asteroid [449 Hamburga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/449_Hamburga); however, the spacecraft was eventually canceled.[^cite_note-258]

### Sponsorship in NASCAR



The McDonald's-sponsored car of [Jamie McMurray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_McMurray) in 2016



[Bubba Wallace](35761776.md) in a McDonald's-sponsored [racing suit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_suit) in 2022

McDonald's entered the [NASCAR Cup Series](56571945.md) in 1977, sponsoring [Richard Childress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Childress) for one race. Between 1977 and 1986, McDonald's only sponsored a handful of races in a season. In 1993, McDonald's became the full-time sponsor for the No. 27 [Junior Johnson & Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junior_Johnson_&_Associates) [Ford](30433662.md), driven by [Hut Stricklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hut_Stricklin).[^cite_note-Daytona_500_champ_gets_McDonald's_sponsorship-259] In 1994, Stricklin was replaced in the car by [Jimmy Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Spencer), who went on to win twice that season. The following season McDonald's would move over to the No. 94 [Bill Elliott Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Elliott_Racing) Ford, driven by team-owner [Bill Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Elliott).[^cite_note-260] McDonald's stayed with Elliott until the 2001 season when they moved again, this time to the No. 96 [PPI Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PPI_Motorsports) Ford, driven by rookie [Andy Houston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Houston). However, when the team failed to field a car for the entire season, McDonald's became absent from NASCAR until 2004, when it joined [Evernham Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evernham_Motorsports) as a part-time sponsor for drivers Elliott, [Kasey Kahne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasey_Kahne), [Elliott Sadler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Sadler), [A. J. Allmendinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._J._Allmendinger), and [Reed Sorenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_Sorenson) until 2010.[^cite_note-Daytona_500_champ_gets_McDonald's_sponsorship-259]

During the 2010 season, McDonald's entered its longest partnership with a team at [Chip Ganassi Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chip_Ganassi_Racing), sponsoring the No. 1 [Chevrolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet) driven by [Jamie McMurray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamie_McMurray) until his final race in the [2019 Daytona 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Daytona_500).[^cite_note-261][^cite_note-262] McDonald's moved to CGR's No. 42 of [Kyle Larson](36163081.md), whom the company sponsored until his suspension in 2020,[^cite_note-263] and also had a one-race partnership with [Richard Petty Motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evernham_Motorsports)' No. 43 Chevrolet driven by [Bubba Wallace](35761776.md) in 2019 and 2020.[^cite_note-264][^cite_note-265] McDonald's continued working with the No. 42 under new driver [Ross Chastain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ross_Chastain) in 2021 and also joined Wallace's new team [23XI Racing](65517461.md) as a "founding partner".[^cite_note-266][^cite_note-267]

## Charity



A Ronald McDonald House collection box in [Framingham, Massachusetts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framingham,_Massachusetts)

McHappy Day is an annual event at McDonald's, during which a percentage of the day's sales go to charity. It is the signature fundraising event for [Ronald McDonald House Charities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_McDonald_House_Charities) (RMHC).[^cite_note-268] According to the Australian McHappy Day website, McHappy Day raised \$20.4 million in 2009. The goal for 2010 was \$20.8 million.[^cite_note-269]

Ronald McDonald House Charities is a global network of more than 260 Chapters that is made to help families access [medical care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_care) for their children and family support services in more than 60 countries and regions throughout the world. In 2022, over \$182 million were donated to Ronald McDonald House Charities from McDonald's, its franchisees, and its customers.[^cite_note-270]

In 1995, [St. Jude Children's Research Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Jude_Children's_Research_Hospital) received an anonymous letter postmarked in Dallas, Texas, containing a \$1 million winning [McDonald's Monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonald's_Monopoly) game piece. McDonald's officials came to the hospital, accompanied by a representative from the accounting firm Arthur Andersen, who examined the card under a jeweler's eyepiece, handled it with plastic gloves, and verified it as a winner.[^cite_note-271]

## Future plans

McDonald's wants to add 900 McDonald's restaurants in the U.S. and 10,000 more worldwide (totaling 50,000 restaurants globally) by 2027.[^cite_note-Farrell-272][^cite_note-273]

McDonald's has committed to eliminating [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) from its [global supply chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_supply_chain) by 2030 for beef and four other commodities. The company also has a 2050 [net-zero carbon emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_zero_carbon_emissions) commitment, to bring global carbon emissions down.[^cite_note-274] Digitizing the Arches is a new initiative of McDonald's.\[^cite_note-[www.nrn.com-275](http://www.nrn.com-275/)] From this, McDonald's announces a partnership with [Google](1092923.md) to automate restaurants using [artificial intelligence](1164.md),[^cite_note-Farrell-272] and [Google Cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Cloud_Platform) technology.[^cite_note-Corporation-276] They also plan to expand the McDonald's loyalty program from 150 million to 250 million 90-day active users by 2027.[^cite_note-Corporation-276]\[^cite_note-[www.nrn.com-275](http://www.nrn.com-275/)] McDonald's is also planning to expand its digital ordering system to 30% of deliveries originating from their app by 2027.[^cite_note-Farrell-272]
