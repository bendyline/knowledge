The **Danube** ([/ˈdæn.juːb/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*DAN-yoob*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); see also [other names](#Names_and_etymology)) is a [river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River) in Europe, the [second-longest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_rivers_of_Europe#Longest_rivers) after the [Volga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga).[^cite_note-9] It flows through Central and Southeastern Europe, from the [Black Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Forest) of Germany[^cite_note-\:0-10] south through the [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta) in [Romania](25445.md) into the [Black Sea](3386.md).[^cite_note-\:0-10] A large and historically important river, it was once a frontier of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire). In the 21st century, it connects ten European countries, running through their territories or marking a border. Originating in Germany, the Danube flows southeast for 2,850 km (1,770 mi), passing through or bordering Austria, Slovakia, Hungary, Croatia, Serbia, Romania, Bulgaria, Moldova, and Ukraine.[^cite_note-11] Among the many [cities on the river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_on_the_river_Danube) are four national capitals: [Vienna](55866.md), [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava),[^cite_note-12] [Budapest](36787.md),[^cite_note-13] and [Belgrade](55904.md). Its drainage basin amounts to 817,000 km<sup>2</sup> (315,000 sq mi) and extends into nine more countries.&#x20;

The Danube's longest headstream, the [Breg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breg_\(river\)), rises in [Furtwangen im Schwarzwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furtwangen_im_Schwarzwald), while the river carries its name from its source confluence in the [palace park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaueschingen_Palace) in [Donaueschingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaueschingen) onwards. Since ancient times, the Danube has been a traditional trade route in Europe. Today, 2,415 km (1,501 mi) of its total length are navigable. The Danube is linked to the [North Sea](21179.md) via the [Rhine–Main–Danube Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine%E2%80%93Main%E2%80%93Danube_Canal), connecting the Danube at [Kelheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelheim) with the [Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_\(river\)) at [Bamberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamberg). The river is also an important source of [hydropower](14073.md) and drinking water.&#x20;

The Danube river basin is home to such fish species as [pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_pike), [zander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zander), [huchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huchen), [wels catfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wels_catfish), [burbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbot) and [tench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tench). It is also home to numerous diverse [carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carp) and [sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturgeon), as well as [salmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salmon) and [trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trout). A few species of [euryhaline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euryhaline) fish, such as European [seabass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_seabass), [mullet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullet_\(fish\)), and [eel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_eel), inhabit the Danube Delta and the lower portion of the river.&#x20;

## Names and etymology

Today the river carries its name from its source confluence in [Donaueschingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaueschingen), Germany, to its discharge into the [Black Sea](3386.md) via the [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta) in Romania and Ukraine.&#x20;

The river was known to the [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) as the *Istros* (Ἴστρος)[^cite_note-14] from a root possibly also encountered in the ancient name of the [Dniester](376633.md) (*Danaster* in Latin, *Tiras* in Greek) and akin to [Iranic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranic_languages) *turos* 'swift' and [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) *iṣiras* (इषिरस्) 'swift', from the [PIE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language) \**isro-*, \**sreu* 'to flow'.[^cite_note-Felecan_&_Felecan_2015-15]&#x20;

In the Middle Ages, the Greek *Tiras* was borrowed into Italian as *Tyrlo* and into Turkic languages as *Tyrla*; the latter was further borrowed into Romanian as a regionalism (*Turlă*).[^cite_note-Felecan_&_Felecan_2015-15]&#x20;

The Thraco-[Phrygian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phrygian_language) name was *Matoas*,[^cite_note-16] "the bringer of luck".[^cite_note-Šašel2009-17]&#x20;

The [Middle Mongolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Mongol_language) name for the Danube was transliterated as *Tho-na* in 1829 by [Jean-Pierre Abel-Rémusat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Abel-R%C3%A9musat).[^cite_note-Abel-Remusat-18]&#x20;

### Etymology

*Danube* is an [Old European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_European_hydronymy) river name derived from the [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_languages) '[Danu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danu_\(Irish_goddess\))' or '[Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dylan_ail_Don)'[^cite_note-Bromwich-22] (both of them being [Celtic deities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_deities)), which itself derived from the [Proto-Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Indo-European_language) \**deh₂nu*. Other European river names from the same root include the [Dunajec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunajec), [Dzvina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzvina)/[Daugava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daugava), [Don](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_River_\(Russia\)), [Donets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donets), [Dnieper](380138.md), [Dniestr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dniestr), [Dysna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysna) and [Tana/Deatnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tana_\(Norway\)). In [Rigvedic Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigvedic_Sanskrit), *[Danu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danu_\(Hinduism\))* (दनु) means "fluid, dewdrop" (which is also a [Vedic goddess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_Vedic_religion)) and *Danuja* (दनुज) means "born from *[Danu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danu_\(Hinduism\))*" or "born from dewdrops". In [Avestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avestan_language), the word *Dānu* means "river". The [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_language) word for Danube is *[Tonava](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Tonava#Finnish)*, which is most likely derived from the name of the river in [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), *Donau*. Its [Sámi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sami_languages) name *Deatnu* means "Great River". It is possible that *Dānu* in [Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythian_language) as in [Avestan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avestan) was a generic word for "river": *Dnieper* and *Dniestr*, from *Danapris* and *Danastius*, are presumed to continue Scythian \**dānu apara* "far river" and \**dānu nazdya-* "near river", respectively.[^cite_note-Mallory,_J.P_2000._p._106-23]&#x20;

In Latin, the Danube was variously known as *Danubius*, *Danuvius*, *Ister*[^cite_note-24] or *Hister*. The Latin name is masculine, as are all its [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages) names, except [Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_language) (the name of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) is also masculine in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin), most of the [Slavic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages), as well as in [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language)). The German *Donau* ([Early Modern German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Modern_German) *Donaw*, *Tonaw*,[^cite_note-25] [Middle High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_High_German) *Tuonowe*)[^cite_note-26] is feminine, as it has been re-interpreted as containing the suffix *[-ouwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aue_\(toponymy\))* "wetland".&#x20;

[Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language) differs from other surrounding languages in designating the river with a feminine term, *Dunărea* (IPA: [\[ˈdunəre̯a\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Romanian)).[^cite_note-Felecan_&_Felecan_2015-15] This form was not inherited from Latin, although Romanian is a Romance language.[^cite_note-Vékony-20] To explain the loss of the Latin name, scholars who suppose that Romanian developed near the large river propose[^cite_note-Vékony-20] that the Romanian name descends from a hypothetical [Thracian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracian_language) \**Donaris*. The Proto-Indo-European root of this presumed name is related to the Iranic word "*don-*"/"*dan-*", while the supposed suffix *-aris* is encountered in the ancient name of the [Ialomița River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ialomi%C8%9Ba_\(river\)), *Naparis*, and in the unidentified *Miliare* river mentioned by [Jordanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanes) in his [Getica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getica).[^cite_note-Felecan_&_Felecan_2015-15] Gábor Vékony says that this hypothesis is not plausible, because the Greeks borrowed the *Istros* form from the native [Thracians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thracians).[^cite_note-Vékony-20] He proposes that the Romanian name is a loanword from a [Turkic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages) ([Cuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuman_language) or [Pecheneg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecheneg_language)).[^cite_note-Vékony-20]&#x20;

## Geography



The [Danube basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_basin)



The hydrogeographical [source of the Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_of_the_Danube) at [St. Martin's Chapel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Martin%27s_Chapel,_Furtwangen) in [Furtwangen im Schwarzwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furtwangen_im_Schwarzwald) Germany: the *Bregquelle*, the source of the Danube's longest headstream, the [Breg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breg_\(river\)), where the Danube is symbolized by the Roman allegory for the river, *Danuvius*.



The symbolical source of the Danube in [Donaueschingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaueschingen): the source of the [Donaubach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaubach) (*Danube Brook*), which flows into the [Brigach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigach).

Classified as an [international waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_waterway), it [originates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Source_of_the_Danube) in the town of [Donaueschingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaueschingen), in the [Black Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Forest) of [Germany](11867.md), at the [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of the rivers [Brigach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigach) and [Breg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breg_\(river\)). The Danube then flows southeast for about 2,730 km (1,700 mi), passing through four capital cities ([Vienna](55866.md), [Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratislava), [Budapest](36787.md), and [Belgrade](55904.md)) before emptying into the [Black Sea](3386.md) via the [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta) in [Romania](25445.md) and [Ukraine](31750.md).&#x20;

### Drainage basin

In addition to the bordering countries (see above), the [drainage basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin) includes parts of nine more countries: [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) (4.6% of the basin area), the [Czech Republic](5321.md) (2.9%), [Slovenia](27338.md) (2.0%), [Montenegro](20760.md) (0.9%), [Switzerland](26748.md) (0.2%), [Italy](14532.md) (<0.15%), [Poland](22936.md) (<0.1%), [North Macedonia](23564616.md) (<0.1%) and [Albania](738.md) (<0.1%).[^cite_note-icpdr-27] The total drainage basin is 801,463 km<sup>2</sup> (309,447 sq mi) in area,[^cite_note-DE-BY_GV-29][^cite_note-Danube_12-30] and is home to 83 million people.[^cite_note-ICPDR-Basin-31] The highest point of the drainage basin is the summit of *[Piz Bernina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piz_Bernina)* at the [Italy–Switzerland border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy%E2%80%93Switzerland_border), at 4,049 m (13,284 ft).[^cite_note-32] The Danube river basin is divided into three main parts, separated by "gates" where the river is forced to cut through mountainous sections:[^cite_note-ICPDR-Basin-31]&#x20;

- **Upper Basin**, from the [headwaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headwaters) to the [Devín Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dev%C3%ADn_Gate).

- **Middle Basin**, usually called the [Pannonian Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Basin) or Carpathian Basin, between the [Devín Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dev%C3%ADn_Gate) and the [Iron Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gates). It includes the Hungarian plains [Kisalföld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisalf%C3%B6ld) and [Alföld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf%C3%B6ld).

- **Lower Basin**, from the [Iron Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gates) to the [river mouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_mouth), including the [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta).

Main article: [List of tributaries of the Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tributaries_of_the_Danube)

## Sectioning

- Upper Section: From spring to [Devín Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dev%C3%ADn_Gate), at the border of Austria and Slovakia. Danube remains a characteristic mountain river until [Passau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passau), with average bottom gradient 0.12% (1200 ppm), from Passau to Devín Gate the gradient lessens to 0.06% (600 ppm).

- Middle Section: From Devín Gate to [Iron Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_\(Danube\)), at the border of Serbia and Romania. The riverbed widens and the average bottom gradient becomes only 0.006% (60 ppm).

- Lower Section: From Iron Gate to [Pătlăgeanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C4%83tl%C4%83geanca), with average gradient as little as 0.003% (30 ppm).

- Delta: From Pătlăgeanca to [Sulina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulina), where the Danube meets the Black Sea. The river splits into three main distributaries.

## Modern navigation

The Danube in [Budapest](36787.md)



Fisherman in the [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta)

[Barge](4177.md) being pushed on the Danube near [Vienna](55866.md)

The Danube is navigable by ocean ships from the Black Sea to [Brăila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Br%C4%83ila) in Romania (the maritime river sector), and further on by river ships to [Kelheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelheim), [Bavaria](3764.md), Germany; smaller craft can navigate further upstream to [Ulm](189455.md), [Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W%C3%BCrttemberg), Germany. About 60 of its tributaries are also navigable.&#x20;

Since the completion of the German [Rhine–Main–Danube Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Main-Danube_Canal) in 1992, the river has been part of a trans-European waterway from [Rotterdam](26049.md) on the [North Sea](21179.md) to [Sulina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulina) on the Black Sea, a distance of 3,500 km (2,200 mi). In 1994 the Danube was declared one of ten [Pan-European transport corridors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_corridors), routes in Central and Eastern Europe that required major investment over the following ten to fifteen years. The amount of goods transported on the Danube increased to about 100 million tons in 1987. In 1999, transport on the river was made difficult by the [NATO bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NATO_bombing_of_Yugoslavia) of three bridges in Serbia during the [Kosovo War](16760.md). Clearance of the resulting debris was completed in 2002, and a temporary [pontoon bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontoon_bridge) that hampered navigation was removed in 2005.&#x20;

At the [Iron Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_\(Danube\)), the Danube flows through a [gorge](78478.md) that forms part of the boundary between Serbia and Romania; it contains the [Iron Gate I Hydroelectric Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_I_Hydroelectric_Power_Station) dam, followed at about 60 km (37 mi) downstream (outside the gorge) by the [Iron Gate II Hydroelectric Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_II_Hydroelectric_Power_Station). On 13 April 2006, a record peak discharge at Iron Gate Dam reached 15,400 m<sup>3</sup>/s (540,000 cu ft/s).&#x20;

There are three artificial waterways built on the Danube: the [Danube-Tisa-Danube Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube-Tisa-Danube_Canal) (DTD) in the [Banat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banat) and [Bačka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C4%8Dka) regions ([Vojvodina](176801.md), northern province of Serbia); the 64 km (40 mi) [Danube-Black Sea Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube-Black_Sea_Canal), between [Cernavodă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cernavod%C4%83) and [Constanța](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constan%C8%9Ba) (Romania) finished in 1984, shortens the distance to the Black Sea by 400 km (250 mi); the [Rhine–Main–Danube Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine%E2%80%93Main%E2%80%93Danube_Canal) is about 171 km (106 mi), finished in 1992, linking the North Sea to the Black Sea.[^cite_note-44] A Danube-Aegean canal has been proposed.[^cite_note-45]&#x20;

[Cruising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_cruise) on the Danube is a popular sightseeing activity, especially between [Passau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passau), Germany, to [Budapest](36787.md), Hungary.[^cite_note-46]&#x20;

## Piracy

In 2010–12, shipping companies, especially from Ukraine, claimed that their vessels suffered from "regular [pirate attacks](50715.md)" on the Serbian and the Romanian stretches of the Danube.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] However, the transgressions may not be considered acts of piracy, as defined according to the [United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea](31940.md), but rather instances of "river robbery".[^cite_note-50]&#x20;

Media reports suggest that transport crews frequently misappropriate cargo, selling it for personal gain while attributing the losses to pirate attacks. These incidents are often characterised not as piracy, but as opportunistic theft and smuggling conducted along the river.[^cite_note-51]&#x20;

## Danube Delta

Main article: [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta)



Russian-speaking [Lipovans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipovans) in the Danube Delta

The Danube Delta ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Delta Dunării* pronounced [\[ˈdelta ˈdunərij\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Romanian); [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Дельта Дунаю, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Del'ta Dunayu*) is the largest [river delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta) in the [European Union](9317.md). The greater part of the Danube Delta lies in Romania (Tulcea county), while its northern part, on the left bank of the Chilia arm, is situated in Ukraine (Odesa Oblast). The approximate surface is 4,152 km<sup>2</sup> (1,603 sq mi), of which 3,446 km<sup>2</sup> (1,331 sq mi) are in Romania. If one includes the lagoons of [Razim-Sinoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Razelm) (1,015 km<sup>2</sup> (392 sq mi) of which 865 km<sup>2</sup> (334 sq mi) water surface), which are located south of the delta proper, but are related to it geologically and ecologically (their combined territory is part of the World Heritage Site), the total area of the Danube Delta reaches 5,165 km<sup>2</sup> (1,994 sq mi).&#x20;

The Danube Delta is also the best-preserved river delta in Europe, a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md) (since 1991) and a [Ramsar Site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramsar_Convention). Its lakes and marshes support 45 freshwater fish species. Its wetlands support vast flocks of migratory birds of over 300 species, including the endangered [pygmy cormorant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pygmy_cormorant) (*Phalacrocorax pygmaeus*). These are threatened by rival canalization and drainage schemes such as the [Bystroye Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bystroye_Canal).[^cite_note-52]&#x20;

## International cooperation

### Ecology and environment

Main article: [International Commission for the Protection of the Danube River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Commission_for_the_Protection_of_the_Danube_River)



[Pelicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pelicans) in the [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta), Romania

The International Commission for the Protection of the Danube River (ICPDR) is an organization that consists of 14 member states (Germany, Austria, the Czech Republic, Slovakia, Slovenia, Hungary, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Serbia, Bulgaria, Romania, Moldova, Montenegro, and Ukraine) and the [European Union](9317.md). The commission, established in 1998, deals with the whole Danube river basin, which includes tributaries and groundwater resources. Its goal is to implement the Danube River Protection Convention by promoting and coordinating sustainable and equitable water management, including conservation, improvement, and rational use of waters and the implementation of the EU [Water Framework Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Framework_Directive) and the [Danube Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Strategy).&#x20;

### Navigation

Main article: [Danube Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Commission)

The Danube Commission is concerned with the maintenance and improvement of the river's navigation conditions. It was established in 1948 by seven countries bordering the river. Members include representatives from Austria, Bulgaria, Croatia, Germany, Hungary, Moldova, Slovakia, Romania, Russia, Ukraine, and Serbia; it meets regularly twice a year. It also convenes groups of experts to consider items provided for in the commission's working plans.&#x20;

The commission dates to the Paris Conferences of 1856 and 1921, which established for the first time an international regime to safeguard free navigation on the Danube. Today the Commission include riparian and non-riparian states.&#x20;

## Geology



&#x20;[Iron Gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_\(Danube\)), Serbia-Romania border



[Iron Gate II Hydroelectric Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_II_Hydroelectric_Power_Station), Romania-Serbia

Although the [headwaters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headwater) of the Danube are relatively small today, geologically, the Danube is much older than the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), with which its catchment area competes in today's southern Germany. This has a few interesting geological complications. Since the Rhine flows north towards the North Sea, a [continental divide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_divide) beginning at [Piz Lunghin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piz_Lunghin) divides large parts of southern Germany, which is sometimes referred to as the [European Watershed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Watershed).&#x20;

Before the last [ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_age) in the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene), the Rhine started at the southwestern tip of the Black Forest, while the waters from the Alps that today feed the Rhine were carried east by the so-called *Urdonau* (original Danube). Parts of this ancient river's bed, which was much larger than today's Danube, can still be seen in (now waterless) canyons in today's landscape of the [Swabian Alb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swabian_Alb). The erosion of the [Upper Rhine valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Rhine_valley) led to [stream capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_capture); waters from the Alps changed their direction and began feeding the Rhine. Today's upper Danube is thus an [underfit stream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underfit_stream).&#x20;



The [Iron Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_\(Danube\)), on the Serbian-Romanian border ([Iron Gates natural park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gates_natural_park) and [Đerdap national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90erdap_national_park))

Since the Swabian Alb is largely shaped of porous [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), and since the Rhine's level is much lower than the Danube's, today subsurface rivers carry much water from the Danube to the Rhine. On many days in the summer, when the Danube carries little water, it completely sinks into these underground channels at two locations in the Swabian Alb, which are referred to as the Donauversickerung ([Danube Sink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Sinkhole)). Most of this water resurfaces only 12 km (7.5 mi) south at the [Aachtopf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aachtopf), Germany's wellspring with the highest flow, an average of 8,500 L/s (300 cu ft/s), north of [Lake Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Constance)—thus feeding the Rhine. The European Water Divide applies only for those waters that pass beyond this point, which only occurs during the days of the year when the Danube carries enough water to survive the sinkholes in the Donauversickerung.&#x20;

Since such large volumes of underground water erode much of the surrounding limestone, it is predicted that the Danube upper course will one day disappear entirely in favor of the Rhine, an event called [stream capturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stream_capture).&#x20;

The hydrological parameters of Danube are regularly monitored in Croatia at [Batina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batina), [Dalj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalj), [Vukovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vukovar) and [Ilok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilok).[^cite_note-53]&#x20;

## History



Combat between Russian and Turkish forces on the Danube in 1854, during the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) (1853–1856)

The Danube basin was the site of some of the earliest human cultures. The [Danubian Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danubian_culture) cultures include the [Linear Pottery cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linear_Pottery_culture) of the mid-Danube basin. Many sites of the sixth-to-third millennium BCE [Vinča culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin%C4%8Da_culture) (Vinča, Serbia), are sited along the Danube. The third millennium BCE [Vučedol culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vu%C4%8Dedol_culture) (from the Vučedol site near [Vukovar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vukovar), [Croatia](5573.md)) is famous for its ceramics.&#x20;

[Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great), [king of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), crossed the river in the late 6th century BCE to [invade European Scythia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Scythian_campaign_of_Darius_I) and to subdue the [Scythians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythians).&#x20;

[Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) defeated the Triballian king [Syrmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrmus) and the northern barbarian Thracian and Illyrian tribes by advancing from Macedonia as far as the Danube in 336 BCE.&#x20;

Under the Romans, the Danube formed the border of the Empire with the tribes to the north almost from its source to its mouth. At the same time, it was a route for the transport of troops and the supply of settlements downstream. From 37 CE to the reign of the Emperor [Valentinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentinian_I) (364–375) the [Danubian Limes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danubian_Limes) was the northeastern border of the Empire, with occasional interruptions such as the fall of the Danubian Limes in 259. The crossing of the Danube into [Dacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Dacia) was achieved by the *Imperium Romanum*, first in two battles in 102 and then in 106 after the construction of a bridge in 101 near the garrison town of [Drobeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drobeta_Turnu_Severin) at the Iron Gate. This victory over Dacia under [Decebalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decebalus) enabled the Province of [Dacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacia_\(province\)) to be created, but in 271 it was abandoned by emperor [Aurelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelian).&#x20;

[Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avar_Khaganate) used the river as their southeastern border in the 6th century.&#x20;

### Ancient cultural perspectives of the lower Danube

Part of the rivers Danubius or Istros was also known as (together with the Black Sea) the *Okeanos* in ancient times, being called the *Okeanos Potamos* (Okeanos River). The lower Danube was also called the *Keras Okeanoio* (Gulf or Horn of Okeanos) in the *[Argonautica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argonautica)* by [Apollonius Rhodos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollonius_of_Rhodes) (Argon. IV. 282).&#x20;

At the end of the *Okeanos Potamos*, is the holy island of Alba (Leuke, Pytho Nisi, [Isle of Snakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snake_Island_\(Ukraine\))), sacred to the Pelasgian (and later, Greek) Apollo, greeting the sun rising in the east. [Hecateus Abderitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecateus_Abderitas) refers to Apollo's island from the region of the Hyperboreans, in the Okeanos. It was on Leuke, in one version of his legend, that the hero Achilles was buried (to this day, one of the mouths of the Danube is called Chilia). Old Romanian folk songs recount a white monastery on a white island with nine priests.[^cite_note-Densusianu-55]&#x20;

### Rivalry and trade along the Danube



The [Holy League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_League_\(1684\)) took [Ottoman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Hungary)-held [Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda) after a long [siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Buda_\(1686\)) in 1686.

Between the late 14th and late 19th centuries, the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) competed first with the [Kingdom of Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Serbia_\(medieval\)), [Second Bulgarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Bulgarian_Empire), [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary), [Principality of Wallachia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Wallachia), [Principality of Moldavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Moldavia) and later with the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy), [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth), and [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) for controlling the Danube (طونه, *Tuna* in [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language)), which became the northern border of the Ottoman Empire for centuries. Many of the [Ottoman–Hungarian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Hungarian_Wars) (1366–1526) and [Ottoman–Habsburg wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Habsburg_wars) (1526–1791) were fought along the river.&#x20;

The most important [wars of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_wars_in_Europe) along the Danube include the [Battle of Nicopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nicopolis) (1396), the [Siege of Belgrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Belgrade_\(1456\)) (1456), the [Battle of Mohács](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moh%C3%A1cs) (1526), the first Turkish [Siege of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Vienna_\(1529\)) (1529), the [Siege of Esztergom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Esztergom_\(1543\)) (1543), the [Long War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_War_\(Ottoman_wars\)) (1591–1606), the [Battle of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vienna) (1683), the [Great Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Turkish_War) (1683–1699), the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) (1853–1856) and the [Russo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1877%E2%80%931878\)) (1877–1878).&#x20;

In the 19th century the [beaver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver) was hunted to extinction along the Danube in [Bavaria](3764.md).[^cite_note-56] The [Beluga sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beluga_\(sturgeon\)) population also collapsed, Danube sturgeons had been commercially exploited for meat and [caviar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caviar) since the 5th century BC. River regulation schemes commenced in the 19th century and continued into the 20th century. The Danube river has a total of 18 major dams, including [Melk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melk) and Freudenau in [Vienna](55866.md).[^cite_note-57]&#x20;

### Second World War

During the 2011 renovation of the [Margaret Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Bridge), [Budapest](36787.md), human remains were discovered. The mostly Jewish remains were victims of the [far-right](221220.md) [Arrow Cross Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow_Cross_Party), who briefly governed Hungary from 1944.[^cite_note-58]&#x20;

### Record-low levels and remains discovery

In July and August 2026, a prolonged [heatwave](83266395.md) and [drought](83908768.md) across Europe caused water levels along the Danube and other major rivers, including the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), to fall to record lows in some areas. Near [Ryahovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryahovo) in northern Bulgaria, the receding water exposed the remains of a [mammoth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammoth), including a jawbone, two tusks, and rib bones, believed to belong to a juvenile individual. Local officials said the area had long been thought to be a marshland where mammoths perished thousands of years ago.[^cite_note-59]&#x20;

Near [Prahovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prahovo), Serbia, more than 20 German shipwrecks were exposed, which were scuttled in 1944 during the German retreat in [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-60] Near [Gigen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigen), Bulgaria, the low water revealed parts of the foundations of [Constantine's Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantine%27s_Bridge_\(Danube\)), a Roman bridge over the Danube.[^cite_note-61] Near [Virt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virt), Slovakia, another large British World War II bomb surfaced and was later destroyed in a controlled explosion; it was probably connected to the [Royal Air Force](25679.md)'s 1944 river-mining campaign, [Operation Gardening](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Operation_Gardening\&action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-62]&#x20;

In [Budapest](36787.md), sections of the former Franz Joseph Bridge, destroyed in 1945, also became visible beneath the [Liberty Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberty_Bridge_\(Budapest\)).[^cite_note-hungarytoday-63] Near Budapest, the exposed riverbed revealed a German [DKW NZ 350-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=DKW_NZ_350-1\&action=edit\&redlink=1) motorcycle, the remains of two *[Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht)* soldiers, and several World War II-era explosives, including anti-tank mines.[^cite_note-64] Near [Kisoroszi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kisoroszi), Hungary, a 460-kilogram British World War II [naval mine](22102.md) was discovered and safely removed by bomb-disposal experts.[^cite_note-hungarytoday-63] In addition, wrecks of World War II-era warships were exposed near [Mohács](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moh%C3%A1cs) and [Baja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja,_Hungary).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]&#x20;

The [Paks Nuclear Power Plant](1835713.md), Hungary's largest nuclear power plant, has been cutting output as record-low Danube water levels reduced the availability of cooling water, which normally supplies nearly half of the country's electricity.[^cite_note-67] Hungarian authorities planned to build a rock [bed sill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bed_sill) near Paks to raise the Danube level by up to 1 metre (3 ft 3 in), with two [barges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barges) potentially adding another 20 centimetres (7.9 in) at the plant's cooling pumps.[^cite_note-68]&#x20;

In Romania, the [Cernavodă Nuclear Power Plant](3844451.md) was also affected by the drought. Romanian naval forces used 180 kg of explosives in a controlled blast to move a large rock restricting the flow of Danube water toward the plant's cooling system.[^cite_note-69] Serbia also suffered from severe drought and low Danube water levels. With around 65% of its electricity generated by coal and 25% by hydropower, prolonged dry periods posed a major challenge. The Iron Gate hydropower plants [I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_I_Hydroelectric_Power_Station) and [II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_II_Hydroelectric_Power_Station) were producing far below average, while the [Kostolac A and B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TPP_Kostolac) coal plants had to reduce output due to limited cooling water.[^cite_note-70]&#x20;

## Economics

### Drinking water

Along its course, the Danube is a source of drinking water for about 20 million people.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] In [Baden-Württemberg](66401.md), [Germany](11867.md), almost 30 percent (as of 2004) of the water for the area between [Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuttgart), [Bad Mergentheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Mergentheim), [Aalen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalen) and [Alb-Donau (district)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alb-Donau_\(district\)) comes from purified water of the Danube. Other cities such as [Ulm](189455.md) and [Passau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passau) also use some water from the Danube.&#x20;

In [Austria](26964606.md) and [Hungary](13275.md), most water is drawn from ground and spring sources, and only in rare cases is water from the Danube used. Most states also find it too difficult to clean the water because of extensive pollution; only parts of Romania where the water is cleaner still obtain drinking water from the Danube on a regular basis.[^cite_note-73]&#x20;

### Navigation and transport



Fishing from a [Zille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zille_\(boat\)) on the Danube in Lower Austria, 1982

In the 19th century, the Danube was an important waterway but was, as *The Times* of London put it, "annually swept by ice that will lift a large ship out of the water or cut her in two as if she were a carrot."[^cite_note-74]&#x20;

Today, as ["Corridor VII"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-European_corridors) of the [European Union](9317.md), the Danube is an important transport route. Since the opening of the [Rhine–Main–Danube Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine-Main-Danube_Canal), the river connects the [Port of Rotterdam](4410985.md) and the industrial centers of Western Europe with the [Black Sea](3386.md) and, also, through the [Danube – Black Sea Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_%E2%80%93_Black_Sea_Canal), with the [Port of Constanța](13823555.md).&#x20;

The waterway is designed for large-scale inland vessels (110 × 11.45 m) but it can carry much larger vessels on most of its course. The Danube has been partly canalized in Germany (5 locks) and Austria (10 locks). Proposals to build a number of new locks to improve navigation have not progressed, due in part to environmental concerns.&#x20;

Downstream from the [Freudenau](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Freudenau\&action=edit\&redlink=1) locks in Vienna, canalization of the Danube was limited to the [Gabčíkovo dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gab%C4%8D%C3%ADkovo_-_Nagymaros_Dams) and locks near Bratislava and the two double [Iron Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gates) locks in the border stretch of the Danube between Serbia and Romania. These locks have larger dimensions. Downstream of the Iron Gate, the river is free flowing all the way to the Black Sea, a distance of more than 860 kilometres (530 mi).&#x20;

The Danube connects with the Rhine–Main–Danube Canal at [Kelheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelheim), with the [Donaukanal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donaukanal) in Vienna, and with the Danube–Black Sea Canal at [Cernavodă](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cernavod%C4%83).&#x20;

Apart from a couple of secondary navigable branches, the only major navigable rivers linked to the Danube are the [Drava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drava), [Sava](587845.md) and [Tisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tisza). In Serbia, a canal network also connects to the river; the network, known as the [Danube–Tisa–Danube Canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube%E2%80%93Tisa%E2%80%93Danube_Canal), links sections downstream.&#x20;

In the Austrian and German sections of the Danube, a type of flat-bottomed boat called a [Zille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zille_\(boat\)) was developed for use along the river. Zillen are still used today for fishing, ferrying, and other transport of goods and people in this area.&#x20;

### Fishing

The importance of fishing on the Danube, which was critical in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), has declined dramatically. Some fishermen are still active at certain points on the river, and the [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta) still has an important industry. However, some of the river's resources have been managed in an environmentally unsustainable manner in the past, leading to damage by pollution, alterations to the channel, and major infrastructure development, including large hydropower dams.[^cite_note-75]&#x20;

The [sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sturgeon) stocks associated with the Danube river basin have, over the centuries, formed the basis of a large and significant commercial fishery, renowned throughout the world. The construction of the dams, besides overfishing and river pollution, has a significant role in sturgeon population decline because it creates a barrier for fish migratory species that usually spawn in the upper parts of the river.[^cite_note-76] The spawning areas of migratory fishes species has been dramatically reduced by the construction of hydropower and navigation systems at Iron Gates I (1974) and Iron Gates II (1984).[^cite_note-77] The initial design of these dams has not included any fish passage facility.[^cite_note-78] The possibility of building a human-made [fish pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_pass) enabling migration for fish species including the sturgeon, is currently under review by projects such as We Pass.[^cite_note-79]&#x20;

The Upper Danube [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion) alone has about 60 fish species and the Lower Danube–[Dniester](376633.md) ecoregion has about twice as many.[^cite_note-FEW-80] Among these are an exceptionally high diversity of sturgeon, a total of six species ([beluga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beluga_\(sturgeon\)), [Russian sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_sturgeon), [bastard sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastard_sturgeon), [sterlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterlet), [starry sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starry_sturgeon) and [European sea sturgeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_sea_sturgeon)), but these are all threatened and have largely–or entirely in the case of the European sea sturgeon–disappeared from the river.[^cite_note-FEW-80] The [huchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huchen), one of the largest species of salmon, is [endemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) to the Danube basin, but has been [introduced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Introduced_species) elsewhere by humans.[^cite_note-81]&#x20;

### Tourism



The ruins of [Aggstein Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggstein_Castle) above the Danube



[Wachau Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wachau_Valley) near [Spitz, Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spitz,_Austria)

Important tourist and natural spots along the Danube include the [Wachau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wachau) Valley, the [Nationalpark Donau-Auen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalpark_Donau-Auen) in Austria, [Gemenc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemenc) in Hungary, the [Naturpark Obere Donau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturpark_Obere_Donau) in Germany, [Kopački rit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopa%C4%8Dki_rit) in Croatia, [Iron Gate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Gate_\(Danube\)) in Serbia and Romania, the [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta) in Romania, and the [Srebarna Nature Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srebarna_Nature_Reserve) in Bulgaria.&#x20;

Also, leisure and travel cruises on the river are of significance. Besides the often frequented route between Vienna and Budapest, some ships even go from [Passau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passau) in Germany to the [Danube Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_Delta) and back. During the peak season, more than 70 cruise liners are in use on the river, while the traffic-free upper parts can only be discovered with canoes or boats.&#x20;

In Austria alone, there are more than 14 million overnight stays and about 6.5 million arrivals per year.[^cite_note-kaiserundkoenige-82]&#x20;

The Danube Banks in [Budapest](36787.md) are a part of Unesco World Heritage sites, they can be viewed from a number of sightseeing cruises offered in the city.&#x20;

The Danube Bend is also a popular tourist destination.&#x20;

#### Donausteig



Resting area along the *Donausteig* hiking trail near [Bad Kreuzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Kreuzen)

In 2010, the [Donausteig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donausteig_\(Danube_Trail\)), a hiking trail from Passau to [Grein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grein,_Austria), was opened. It is 450 km (280 mi) long and it is divided into 23 stages. The route passes through five Bavarian and 40 Austrian communities. A landscape and viewpoints, which are along the river, are the highlights of the *Donausteig*.[^cite_note-84]&#x20;

#### The Route of Emperors and Kings

The Route of Emperors and Kings is an international touristic route leading from [Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regensburg) to Budapest, calling in Passau, Linz and Vienna.[^cite_note-85] The international consortium *ARGE Die Donau-Straße der Kaiser und Könige*, comprising ten tourism organisations, shipping companies, and cities, strives for the conservation and touristic development of the Danube region.[^cite_note-kaiserundkoenige-82]&#x20;

In medieval Regensburg, with its maintained old town, [stone bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Bridge_\(Regensburg\)) and [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regensburg_Cathedral), the Route of Emperors and Kings begins. It continues to [Engelhartszell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelhartszell_an_der_Donau), with the only [Trappist monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engelszell_Abbey) in Austria. Further highlight-stops along the Danube, include the "Schlögener Schlinge", the city of Linz, which was [European Capital of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Capital_of_Culture) in 2009 with its contemporary art richness, the [Melk Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melk_Abbey), the university city of [Krems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krems_an_der_Donau) and the cosmopolitan city of Vienna. Before the Route of Emperors and Kings ends, you pass Bratislava and Budapest, the latter of which was seen as the twin town of Vienna during the times of the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary). Since [ancient Roman times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), famous emperors and their retinue traveled on and along the Danube and used the river for travel and transportation. While traveling on the mainland was quite exhausting, most people preferred to travel by ship on the Danube. So the Route of Emperors and Kings was the setting for many important historical events, which characterize the Danube up until today.&#x20;

The route got its name from the [Holy Roman Emperor Frederick I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_I,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) of Barbarossa and the [crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) as well as from [Richard I of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_I_of_England) who had been jailed in the [Dürnstein Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgruine_D%C3%BCrnstein), which is situated above the Danube. The most imperial journeys throughout time were those of the [Habsburg family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy). Once crowned in [Frankfurt](10992.md), the emperors ruled from Vienna and also held in Regensburg the [Perpetual Diet of Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpetual_Diet_of_Regensburg). Many famous castles, palaces, residences, and state-run convents were built by the Habsburger along the river. Nowadays they still remind the people who live near the Danube of the bold architecture of the "Donaubarock".&#x20;

Today, people can not only travel by boat on the Danube but also by train, by bike on the Danube Bike Trail or walk on the "Donausteig" and visit the [UNESCO World Heritage cities](44940.md) of Regensburg, [Wachau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wachau) and Vienna.[^cite_note-86]&#x20;

## In folklore and literature



16th-century Danube landscape near [Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regensburg), by [Albrecht Altdorfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albrecht_Altdorfer) – a member of the Danube school

- The Danube is mentioned in the title of a famous [waltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltz) by Austrian composer [Johann Strauss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Strauss_II), *[The Blue Danube Waltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Blue_Danube_Waltz)* (*On the Beautiful Blue Danube*). This piece is well known across the world and is also used widely as a [lullaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lullaby). *[The Waves of the Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Waves_of_the_Danube)* ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Valurile Dunării*) is a waltz by the Romanian composer [Iosif Ivanovici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iosif_Ivanovici) (1845–1902); as the [Anniversary Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anniversary_Song), it has been performed by many vocalists, such as [Al Jolson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Jolson), [Rosemary Clooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosemary_Clooney), [Vera Lynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vera_Lynn), [Tom Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Jones_\(singer\)), and countless others. It is most commonly known as the [Anniversary Waltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waves_of_the_Danube), though that is actually a different song and melody. [Joe Zawinul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Zawinul) wrote a [symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony) about the Danube called *[Stories of the Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stories_of_the_Danube)*. It was performed for the first time at the 1993 [Bruckner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Bruckner) festival, at [Linz](55856.md).

- The Danube figures prominently in the [Bulgarian National Anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mila_Rodino), as a symbolic representation of the country's natural beauty. In Lithuanian folklore songs, the appearance of Danube (Dunojus, Dunojėlis) is more common than the appearance of the longest Lithuanian river [Neman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neman).

- The German and Austrian tradition of landscape painting, the [Danube school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danube_school), developed in the 16th century Danube valley by artists who worked autonomously.

  [^cite_note-87]

- One of [Claudio Magris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Magris)'s masterpieces is called *Danube* ([ISBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISBN_\(identifier\)) [1-86046-823-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special\:BookSources/1-86046-823-3)). The book, published in 1986, is a large cultural-historical essay, in which Magris travels the Danube from the first sources to the delta, tracing the European ethnic and cultural heritage, literary and ideological history.

- *The Great Danube Adventure* was published in 1838 as epic travel diary.

- [Jules Verne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jules_Verne)'s *[The Danube Pilot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Danube_Pilot)* (1908) (*Le Pilote du Danube*) depicts the adventures of fisherman Serge Ladko as he travels down the river.

- [Algernon Blackwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algernon_Blackwood)'s short story "[The Willows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Willows_\(story\))" mostly takes place on the river.
