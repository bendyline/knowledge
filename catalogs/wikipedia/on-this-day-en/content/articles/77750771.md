***Clair Obscur: Expedition 33*** [^cite_note-4] is a 2025 [role-playing video game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Role-playing_video_game) developed by French studio [Sandfall Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandfall_Interactive) and published by [Kepler Interactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler_Interactive). Set in a [dark fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_fantasy) [Belle Époque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_%C3%89poque)-inspired universe, the game follows the members of Expedition 33 as they set out to destroy the Paintress, a mysterious entity causing the yearly "[Gommage](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/gommage)", which results in those at or above an ever-decreasing age being erased from existence. In *Expedition 33*, the player controls a party of characters from a [third-person perspective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-person_\(video_games\)), exploring areas and engaging in combat. Its [turn-based mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn-based_role-playing_game) feature [real-time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timekeeping_in_games#Real-time) aspects such as [quick time events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_time_event) and timed actions in combat.&#x20;

The project originated in 2019 as Guillaume Broche, a [Ubisoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubisoft) employee who left the company in 2020 during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) to form Sandfall Interactive with friends and contacts, began steadily establishing a core team of 30 developers that came to be supported by various production partners. Inspired by [Japanese RPGs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_RPG) that shaped their youth, such as the *[Final Fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Fantasy)* and *[Persona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_\(series\))* series, the developers at Sandfall sought to create a high-fidelity turn-based role-playing game, a genre they felt [AAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AAA_\(video_game_industry\)) studios had neglected. Development began with [Unreal Engine 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Engine_4) and later switched to [Unreal Engine 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Engine_5), providing rendering improvements.

*Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* was released for [PlayStation 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PlayStation_5), [Windows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windows) and [Xbox Series X/S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Series_X/S) on 24 April 2025, and was available day one on [Xbox Game Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox_Game_Pass). The game received universal acclaim from critics for its narrative and themes, art direction, game direction, [original soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Clair_Obscur:_Expedition_33), gameplay, and performances. As of 24 April 2026, it has sold over 8 million units. *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* received numerous accolades, and topped various publications' lists of best games of 2025. It became the most awarded title in [The Game Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_Awards) history, winning nine of the record thirteen nominations it received at [The Game Awards 2025](81382602.md), including the paramount [Game of the Year](62662783.md) recognition. It became the first debut title and second overall title after *[Baldur's Gate 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldur's_Gate_3)* to win [Game of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Game_of_the_Year_awards) from all five major game award ceremonies ([Golden Joystick Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Joystick_Awards), [The Game Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Game_Awards), [D.I.C.E. Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.I.C.E._Awards), [GDC Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Developers_Choice_Awards), and [BAFTA Games Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Academy_Games_Awards)).

## Gameplay



In this screenshot, Lune, Gustave, and Maelle are fighting against an enemy. On each turn, players can perform a basic melee attack, consume an item, use action points to shoot an enemy or use a skill.

*Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* is a [third-person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-person_perspective) [turn-based role-playing game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turn-based_role-playing_video_game) with real-time elements. The player controls a party of Expeditioners exploring a fantasy world. On their turns, players select whether to use an item, perform a melee attack to gain Ability Points, or spend accumulated Ability Points to use ranged attacks or Skills.[^cite_note-5] Ranged attacks are aimed freely, similar to a [third-person shooter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third-person_shooter). When using a Skill, a [quick time event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_time_event) can be completed for improved effects.[^cite_note-6] During enemy turns, the player can dodge, [parry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parry_\(fencing\)), or jump over attacks in real time to avoid damage.[^cite_note-7] Parrying requires more precise timing than dodging but provides Ability Points and the opportunity to counterattack.[^cite_note-reserve-8] A stamina system allows players to "Break" enemies, temporarily stunning them.[^cite_note-9] As players progress, the game introduces new attacks and parries, including Gradient Attacks, Gradient Counters, and Gradient Skills, all of which deal significant damage. The use of gradient attacks is monitored by the party using a shared gauge. It will slowly fill up when the party uses skills in combat.[^cite_note-10] The Expeditioners and enemies can also apply various status effects on each other, boosting or impairing either side's combat efficiency. Some enemies have weak points that can be targeted, or may be vulnerable to certain elemental attacks.[^cite_note-11] If the combat party is defeated, reserve characters may be called in to continue fighting. Combat ends when either side has no remaining combatants,[^cite_note-reserve-8] or when the player decides to flee the battlefield when they are facing minor enemies.[^cite_note-12]

The game features six playable characters, with each having unique [skill trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skill_trees), weapons and gameplay mechanics. The [mage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magician_\(fantasy\)) Lune's Skills generate [elemental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_element) "Stains", which can be spent to enhance her Skills. The [fencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fencing) Maelle switches between Stances, which alter her Skills, damage and defense. The [scythe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythe)-wielding warrior Sciel can use a set of magical cards to apply and stack "Foretell" to enemies, and then consume them to inflict maximum damage on enemies. Gustave and Verso deal more damage to enemies the more they attack them. Gustave, in particular, gains charge based on the amount of attacks he's done, which can be expelled, doing increased damage depending on the number of charges. Verso, on the other hand, gains "Perfection" ranks upon landing hits, parries, and how little he gets damaged; which will increase his damage output the higher his Perfection rank becomes. The Gestral Monoco can transform into enemies and use their abilities against them. Some of his abilities are further enhanced by his "Bestial Wheel", which spins whenever Monoco uses his abilities.[^cite_note-13] When the party is not exploring or fighting enemies, they can rest at a camp. While playing as Verso, players can converse with other members of Expedition 33 and raise their relationship levels, unlocking new [cinematics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutscene), skills and quests.[^cite_note-14]

Combat encounters reward [experience points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experience_point), currency, and upgrades. Each time a character levels up, they gain three attribute points, which can be spent on increasing five core [character stats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistic_\(role-playing_games\)): Vitality (maximum [health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_\(gaming\))), Might (attack power), Agility (frequency of attack), Defense (damage reduction), and Luck (critical rate).[^cite_note-15] The abilities of the Expeditioners are further customized with equipable "Pictos" which add a variety of [perks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experience_point#Perks) and further improve a character's stats. There are a total of 210 unique Pictos items.[^cite_note-16] Each character can equip three Pictos at a time. Players can 'master' the use of Pictos after using it in combat four times, unlocking its passive bonuses (known as Luminas) for other Expeditioners to use.[^cite_note-poly_tricks-17] Each character also has Lumina Points, which dictate how many Luminas they can equip at a time. As players level up, their maximum Lumina Points will increase, and players can also find additional Lumina Points as collectibles during exploration.[^cite_note-18] As players explore, they will also collect "Chroma Catalysts", which can be used to upgrade weapons to higher levels at the camp.[^cite_note-19] Higher-level weapons deal more base damage to enemies and offer additional gameplay perks.[^cite_note-20]

&#x20;While the game is set in linear levels, players can explore off the beaten path where they will find hidden resources, [side quests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side_quest), [collectibles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collectible), character outfits, Gestral merchants, and optional [bosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boss_\(gaming\)).[^cite_note-gbolt-21] Each level also has several Expedition Flags, where players can heal their party, [fast travel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_travel), restock items, and allocate attribute and skill points. Resting at an Expedition Flag respawns most enemies.[^cite_note-poly_tricks-17] Levels in the game are connected by an [overworld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overworld) map known as "The Continent".[^cite_note-22] Eventually, the Expeditioners meet Esquie, a mythical creature who helps the party to travel across The Continent. Esquie acquires new traversal abilities as players progress, granting the ability to swim, fly, and dive underwater.[^cite_note-gbolt-21] These new traversal abilities are essential for players to advance the story and access optional zones. The game has multiple [difficulty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Difficulty_level) options[^cite_note-23] and a [New Game Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Game_Plus) mode, allowing the player to replay the game with increased difficulty while retaining all character progression from their first playthrough.[^cite_note-24]

## Synopsis

### Setting

*Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* takes place in a [dark fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_fantasy) [Belle Époque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_%C3%89poque) setting. 67 years prior to the events of the game, a disaster known as the "Fracture" occurred, isolating the city of Lumière from the rest of the continent. The people of Lumière have since suffered from an annual event called the "Gommage",[^cite_note-26] where an entity known as "the Paintress" ([Tracy Wiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracy_Wiles)), paints an ever-decreasing number on an enormous rock formation across the continent known as the Monolith, and all humans with an age equal to or above that number disappear. After the yearly Gommage, Lumière sends an expedition of volunteers to head to the mainland in an effort to slay the Paintress before she can paint a new number. Expedition 33 is the latest to set out. The fractured continent is populated by hostile monsters called "Nevrons", who are the primary cause of failed past expeditions, as well as the "Gestrals" and "Grandis", mostly friendly creatures who have settled across the mainland.[^cite_note-Eurogamer_28_August_2024_Nightingale-27][^cite_note-PC_Gamer_28_August_2024_Jones-28]

Members of Expedition 33 include: Gustave ([Charlie Cox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlie_Cox)), a resourceful engineer with a mechanical arm; Maelle ([Jennifer English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_English)), the youngest member of the Expedition and Gustave's foster sister; Lune (Kirsty Rider), a brilliant scholar and mage; and Sciel (Shala Nyx), a calm and cheerful farmer turned teacher.[^cite_note-29] As the expedition traverses the mainland, they encounter various individuals, including: Renoir ([Andy Serkis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Serkis)), a mysterious elderly man who ruthlessly hunts them upon making landfall; Verso ([Ben Starr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Starr_\(actor\))), a stranger who keeps watch over them from afar; Monoco ([Rich Keeble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rich_Keeble)), a Gestral associated with Verso; and Esquie (Maxence Cazorla), a mythical creature who serves as their transportation across the mainland.[^cite_note-IGN_28_August_2024_Purslow-30][^cite_note-VG247_28_August_2024_Donaldson-31][^cite_note-Windows_Central_29_August_2024_Young-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

### Plot

On the day of the Gommage, Gustave bids farewell to his 33-year-old ex-lover, Sophie. With only a year left to live, he joins Expedition 33. Upon making landfall, most expeditioners are killed by Renoir. Gustave survives and locates the three other survivors: Lune, Sciel, and Maelle, the last of whom was rescued by a mysterious entity known as the Curator, who aids the expeditioners. Continuing their expedition, they enlist Esquie's help while Maelle begins having visions of a masked girl. Renoir attacks again and kills Gustave, who was trying to defend Maelle. Verso intervenes, allowing the other expeditioners to escape.&#x20;

Joining the expedition, Verso reveals that he was a member of the first expedition and Renoir—his father—was their commander; furthermore, the masked girl is his sister, Alicia. They all received [immortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortality) from the Paintress; thus, Renoir guards her to protect his family, but Verso is tired of eternal life and wishes to kill her to end it. After recruiting Monoco, the expedition reaches Renoir's mansion to find and destroy the Paintress' heart, a being bearing a striking resemblance to the Curator, to disable a barrier protecting her. Unfortunately, the plan fails when Renoir teleports the mansion and the heart away. Lune devises a new plan: have the Curator forge a weapon to pierce the barrier from the hearts of powerful beings called Axons.

After destroying the barrier, the expedition is confronted by the Paintress' heart and taken inside the Monolith, where they defeat Renoir and the Paintress herself. Afterward, the expedition returns to Lumière as heroes. However, unbeknownst to anyone except Verso, the Curator was the true culprit behind the Gommage, and the Paintress was actually stalling him. With her protection gone, the Curator then kills Lumière's entire population, except Verso.

The story flashes back to the Dessendre family in early 20th-century [Paris](22989.md): mother Aline, father Renoir, son Verso, eldest daughter Clea, and youngest daughter Alicia. They are artists capable of creating worlds within magical paintings; Lumière resides within one such canvas, made by Verso Dessendre. However, a rival faction set fire to their mansion, resulting in Verso Dessendre's death and Alicia Dessendre's disfigurement. Grief-stricken, Aline entered her son's canvas and created copies of her family, becoming the Paintress. Since extended stays inside canvases are unhealthy, Renoir Dessendre attempted to force her out, and their clash caused the Fracture. To shift the stalemate in her father's favor, Clea created the Nevrons and tasked her sister with aiding him. However, upon entering the canvas, Alicia Dessendre got overwhelmed by Aline's power and [reincarnated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reincarnation) as Maelle without her memories.

Back in the present, following her "death", Maelle regains her memories and revives herself. Reuniting with Verso, he comes clean about tricking the expedition into expelling Aline from the canvas. She then approaches the Curator—revealed to be Renoir Dessendre—hoping that he would revive everyone. However, seeing that his daughter has chosen to identify as Maelle rather than as her true self as Alicia Dessendre, he instead intends to erase the canvas. Disagreeing, Maelle revives the other expeditioners, and a battle ensues for control of the canvas. In the end, with Aline's reentering the canvas, Renoir Dessendre is defeated and relents before leaving with his wife.&#x20;

Realizing that, like Aline, Maelle intends to stay inside the canvas indefinitely, Verso approaches the last fragment of Verso Dessendre's soul, which maintains the painting. Verso wants it to pass on, which would erase the canvas and force Maelle out, while she tries to stop him.[^cite_note-35]

- If the player sides with Maelle, the Canvas is saved, and she rebuilds Lumière and revives its people, including Gustave and Sophie. However, Maelle also recreates Verso against his will, and her health begins to decline from staying in the Canvas for too long.

- If the player sides with Verso, he banishes Maelle before convincing Verso Dessendre's soul remnant to stop maintaining the canvas, erasing it along with himself and the remaining expeditioners in the process. Back in reality, the Dessendre family reconciles after coming to terms with Verso Dessendre's death.

## Development

### Pre-production

Guillaume Broche

Tom Guillermin

Sandfall Interactive was founded by director Guillaume Broche (left), technical director Tom Guillermin (right), and producer François Meurisse.

The ideas behind *Expedition 33* originated in 2019 with Guillaume Broche, an employee of [Ubisoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubisoft), not long before the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) began;[^cite_note-auto-36] it soon grew into a passion project inspired by his childhood favorites, most notably the *[Final Fantasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Fantasy_\(series\))* series.[^cite_note-bbc-37] He sent out some requests for help to craft a demo to a group of other developers he knew, along with requests on [Reddit](3829005.md) in April 2020 looking for voice actors for said demo. In order to focus on his project full-time, Broche would leave Ubisoft that year and form Sandfall Interactive, alongside François Meurisse, an old friend, and fellow Ubisoft developer Tom Guillermin. The three co-founders would soon be joined by Lorien Testard, Nicholas Maxson-Francombe and Jennifer Svedberg-Yen; the six forming the kick-off team.[^cite_note-bbc-37][^cite_note-auto-36] Lorien Testard, the composer, was discovered by Broche through a post on a French indie video game forum where he linked a track from his [SoundCloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoundCloud) page.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-bbc-37][^cite_note-aftermath_soundtrack-39] Maxson-Francombe, the game's art director, was discovered and recruited off ArtStation by Broche. Svedberg-Yen, one of the voice actors who had stumbled upon Broche's Reddit post and was cast for the original demo, gained a more prominent role as development progressed, becoming the game's lead writer.[^cite_note-bbc-37]

Nicholas Maxson-Francombe

Florian Torres

Art director Nicholas Maxson-Francombe (left) and senior gameplay programmer Florian Torres (right)

After inking a partnership with Kepler Interactive, which was officially announced in early 2023, and securing funding from said publisher, Sandfall grew into a studio of about thirty developers, three of whom—including Broche and Guillermin—were former Ubisoft employees.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-RPS_size-41] The funding also allowed Sandfall to expand the manpower contributing to the project beyond this core team, having outsourced gameplay combat animation to a team of eight South Korean freelance animators and [quality assurance](305224.md) (QA) to a few dozen QA testers from the firm QLOC, as well as receiving assistance from a half-dozen developers from Ebb Software to [port the game to consoles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porting_of_video_games). The studio also hired a couple of [performance capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_capture) artists; brought in musicians for [the soundtrack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Clair_Obscur:_Expedition_33) recording sessions; contracted with translators from Riotloc for [language localization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_localization); and partnered with Side UK and Studio Anatole as to voice casting and production in English and French respectively.[^cite_note-RPS_size-41][^cite_note-42] Finally, the partnership with Kepler Interactive enabled Sandfall to pay for noted professional voice actors, including Charlie Cox, Andy Serkis and Ben Starr.[^cite_note-bbc-37][^cite_note-aftermath_soundtrack-39] Cox has jokingly stated he was in the studio for barely four hours to record his lines and felt like a fraud over being lauded for his performance; though Svedberg-Yen clarified on her Instagram account that the task had taken around 8 hours, and praised his professionalism and efficiency.[^cite_note-43]

### Production

Broche stated that the purpose of *Expedition 33* was to create a high fidelity turn-based RPG, which he felt had been neglected by [AAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AAA_\(video_game_industry\)) game developers.[^cite_note-Eurogamer_28_August_2024_Nightingale-27] Besides *Final Fantasy*, *Expedition 33* took inspiration from other [Japanese role-playing games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_role-playing_games), including the *[Persona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_\(series\))* series;[^cite_note-PC_Gamer_28_August_2024-44] Broche notably praised *[Persona 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persona_5)* for its user interface and use of camera work during battles, "making it feel like you're watching a movie".[^cite_note-45] Broche also considered *[Lost Odyssey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Odyssey)* and *[Blue Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Dragon_\(video_game\))*, JRPGs developed for Microsoft's drive to help market the [Xbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xbox) consoles in Japan, as an influence, particularly their use of [quick time events](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_time_event) during combat.[^cite_note-RPG_august_2024-46] According to producer François Meurisse, the game drew inspiration from [SquareSoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SquareSoft)'s *[Final Fantasy VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Fantasy_VIII)*, *[Final Fantasy IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Fantasy_IX)* and *[Final Fantasy X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Fantasy_X)* in particular, while the dodge and parry mechanics were influenced by [FromSoftware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FromSoftware)'s *[Sekiro: Shadows Die Twice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekiro:_Shadows_Die_Twice)*.[^cite_note-47]

Development began with [Unreal Engine 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Engine_4), switching to [Unreal Engine 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unreal_Engine_5) due to its improvements in rendering and animation.[^cite_note-PC_Gamer_28_August_2024_Jones-28][^cite_note-Windows_Central_29_August_2024_Young-32] The engine's Nanite and Lumen features allowed for higher-fidelity assets and more-realistic lighting, respectively.[^cite_note-Creative_Bloq_18_February_2025-48] The adoption of Lumen necessitated reworking the lighting for most environments.[^cite_note-Creative_Bloq_18_February_2025-48] Additionally, UE5 had more support for character creation—an advantage over Reallusion's Character Creator, previously being used in this regard.[^cite_note-Creative_Bloq_18_February_2025-48] Sandfall relied on ready-made assets for background objects such as rocks, enabling them to focus on creating "hero assets", i.e., large-scale assets that make an impression on the viewer.[^cite_note-Creative_Bloq_18_February_2025-48] Broche credits the development of the game to the simplicity of modern engines.[^cite_note-bbc-37] The total budget was less than \$10 million.[^cite_note-49]

### Art and narrative



Screenshot of a demo trailer, released via a [Reddit](3829005.md) post by Broche in 2020, for *We Lost*, which would later become *Expedition 33*. The game's setting originally took inspiration from the [steampunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steampunk) subgenre.

Development initially began under the codename "Project W",[^cite_note-50] and was first known as *We Lost* around the time that Broche sought help on Reddit. The initial demo showcased a [steampunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steampunk) setting inspired by [Victorian era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_era) England, with more science fiction elements, including zombies and aliens. About six months into this approach, potential investors suggested that Broche should "think bigger" and ponder what he would want to do if they weren't restricted by their limited resources. This led him to reset the entire story, opting for the [Belle Époque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_%C3%89poque)—a period the French team was naturally well acquainted with and which they deemed to be a more distinctive setting—as well as taking inspiration from the [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco) movement for the visual world design.[^cite_note-51] The new narrative was based on a painting Broche admired, which led him to think of a giantess and a doomsday clock, while also taking some inspiration from the French fantasy novel *[La Horde du Contrevent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Damasio#Novels)* by [Alain Damasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Damasio). The latter featured a horde of adventurers, trained since childhood, undertaking an odyssey to reach the mythical "Extrême-Amont", the source of all winds.[^cite_note-52] Broche's premise was then associated with a short story unrelated to the project Svedberg-Yen had written on her own for fun, in which a painter capable of traveling through her own works got lost in one, prompting her daughter's endeavor to save her.[^cite_note-auto1-53][^cite_note-54]

Svedberg-Yen stated one of the game's core themes, the loss of loved ones, originates with Broche's mother and represented the "final piece". As the two were stuck on the draft, Broche asked his mother what would be the worst thing that could happen to her; she answered the loss of any of her children. This notably became the foundation for Aline's character and became the catalyst for her decision to dwell in what Svedberg-Yen and Broche subsequently conceived as her departed son's canvas.[^cite_note-auto1-53][^cite_note-thegamer_svedberg-yen-55] While other aspects of the narrative were crafted as the game progressed, Svedberg-Yen asserted that the ending of Act I, featuring the death of Gustave, was something she and Broche had set early on, as part of the emotional journey they wanted for the characters.[^cite_note-thegamer_svedberg-yen-55] The team kept some of the characters they had envisioned for *We Lost*, such as Maelle and Lune, but their design and characterization were reimagined for Expedition 33. The characters Noco and Monoco derive their names from the Swedish energy drink [Nocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NOCCO). Broche and Guillermin met during their stint at a subsidiary of Ubisoft in [Malmö, Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6), where a refrigerator was reportedly filled with the beverage. The studio's dog, Monoco, who's credited as Sandfall's "Happiness Manager" on the studio's website, is named after the character;[^cite_note-auto1-53] meanwhile, Svedberg-Yen's own dog, Trunks, was an inspiration for the latter's character design, notably his mop-like hair style.[^cite_note-56]

### Music

Lorien Testard

Composition of the game's music and lyric writing were undertaken by Lorien Testard; he was later joined by Alice Duport-Percier, who composed the vocal lines and was the primary vocalist during recording. Testard used a variety of [leitmotifs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leitmotifs) through the score for various character themes, and wrote towards a range of musical genres.[^cite_note-RPGSite-57] The music was released on music streaming platforms along with a multi-vinyl record limited edition.

The game's soundtrack reached the top spot on [Billboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_charts)'s Classical Albums Chart as well as Classical Crossover Albums Chart during the first week of May 2025, maintaining the No. 1 position for six consecutive weeks.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] In early October 2025, it was announced that within the 5 months since the game's release, the soundtrack had amassed over 333 million track streams across digital music platforms; had spent over 10 weeks at No. 1 on the two Billboard charts aforementioned; and had reached No. 1 on iTunes Top 100 Albums Chart in nine countries.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

## Release and sales

The game [went gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Went_gold) on 22 March 2025, coincidentally 33 days before the game's release.[^cite_note-64] The physical version of the game was distributed by [Maximum Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_Entertainment) in North America and [Bandai Namco Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandai_Namco_Entertainment) in Europe as well as Asia, with the exception of Japan where distribution was handled by [Sega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sega).[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] *Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* sold 500,000 copies within 24 hours after its release on 24 April 2025, 1 million copies within 3 days,[^cite_note-67] 2 million copies within 12 days[^cite_note-68] and 3.3 million copies within 33 days.[^cite_note-69] By October 2025, the game had sold over 5 million copies worldwide. To mark the milestone, the developers announced a free content update featuring a new location, additional late-game bosses, new character costumes, and expanded language support. The update was released after the game won the [Game of the Year](62662783.md) award at [The Game Awards 2025](81382602.md).[^cite_note-70] In December 2025, Xbox Wire reported the game was the biggest third-party title launch on Xbox Game Pass that year.[^cite_note-71] On 24 April 2026, the date marking the game's first anniversary, Sandfall announced it had sold over 8 million units.[^cite_note-72]

Broche has expressed the studio aims to develop sequels or games similar in spirit, confirming *Clair Obscur* is the name of an intended franchise and *Expedition 33* one of its titles; though he was unsure whether these would narratively continue fro&#x6D;*&#x20;Expedition 33*.[^cite_note-73] In January 2025, a live-action adaptation of the game was announced to be in development by Story Kitchen in collaboration with Sandfall.[^cite_note-74]

*Clair Obscur: Expedition 33* received "universal acclaim" from critics according to [review aggregator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Review_aggregator) website [Metacritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metacritic).[^cite_note-MCPC-77][^cite_note-MCPS5-75][^cite_note-MCXSXS-79] This sentiment was also echoed by the users, with the game attaining the highest Metacritic userscore rating of all time.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] Fellow review aggregator [OpenCritic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenCritic) reported that 98% of critics recommended the game.[^cite_note-OC-81] On [Steam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_\(service\)), it reached an "Overwhelmingly positive" review score of 95%, with well over 100,000 users leaving favorable feedback.[^cite_note-101] In Japan, four critics from [Famitsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famitsu) each awarded the game a score of 9 out of 10, for a total score of 36 out of 40.[^cite_note-Famitsu-85]

*Expedition 33*'s art direction and visual world design were praised by reviewers.[^cite_note-euro_review-84][^cite_note-ign_review-90][^cite_note-102] *[Edge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_\(magazine\))* described them as "dreamily intoxicating",[^cite_note-Edge_review-83] a sentiment echoed by Richard Wakeling of *[GameSpot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GameSpot)*, who nonetheless criticized the world for being "mechanically uninteresting \[...] to explore".[^cite_note-spot_review-87] Additional criticism was directed at the absence of a [mini-map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini-map), which can lead to occasional disorientation.[^cite_note-informer_review-86][^cite_note-euro_review-84][^cite_note-pcn_review-92][^cite_note-gr+_review-88]

The story received critical acclaim, with particular praise for the voice acting, which was noted for bringing the characters to life and effectively supporting the narrative.[^cite_note-spot_review-87][^cite_note-vg_review-98][^cite_note-pcn_review-92] *[IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN)*'s Michael Higham commended the game for the way it "frames mortality, grief, and the small moments of joy we find".[^cite_note-ign_review-90] Liam Croft of *[Push Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Push_Square)* noted that "every [cutscene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutscene) remains gripping, with an already intriguing plot heightened by \[...] excellent voice acting".[^cite_note-ps_review-93]

Reviewers were impressed by the soundtrack and particularly praised its adaptability.[^cite_note-spot_review-87][^cite_note-euro_review-84][^cite_note-ign_review-90] *[Game Informer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_Informer)'*&#x73; Kyle Hilliard highlighted the contrast between the "somber piano music and haunting vocals", which underscore the characters' emotional experiences, and the combat themes that "bring the energy back up at precisely the right moment".[^cite_note-informer_review-86] *Edge* described the score as immense and "operatic".[^cite_note-Edge_review-83]

Critics also noted the game's modern approach to traditional Japanese-style turn-based combat.[^cite_note-spot_review-87][^cite_note-informer_review-86][^cite_note-vg_review-98] Malindy Hetfeld of *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* noted that "a successful counter felt deeply satisfying every time".[^cite_note-guardian_review-97] Ed Nightingale of *[Eurogamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurogamer)* further highlighted the variety in character fighting styles, each one introducing "a new system to learn", and the extensive options for character customization.[^cite_note-euro_review-84] Josh Brown for *[PCGamesN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCGamesN)* described the game as a "hyper-stimulating take on a genre once deemed too slow and stale for the HD era".[^cite_note-pcn_review-92]

*Expedition 33* was thus regarded as a major success by reviewers despite the minor shortcomings highlighted. *Edge* underlined that the ability to undertake side quests could lead to the player being overleveled and cause subsequent boss fights to be much less challenging and gratifying, but asserted it was "imprudent" to suggest an alteration to what they felt was a "masterpiece".[^cite_note-Edge_review-83] Wakeling criticized the underwhelming exploration and small quality-of-life issues, but concluded that "these missteps aren't enough to significantly detract from a game with all the makings of an RPG classic".[^cite_note-spot_review-87]

On reaching the 1 million sales milestone, the [French President](24899.md) [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) congratulated the studio on the achievement and highlighted the game's success as "a shining example of French audacity and creativity".[^cite_note-103] Macron later congratulated Sandfall on its multiple accolades at the Game Awards in December 2025, stating: "A historic first for a French title! \[...] Great pride for Montpellier and for France. Congratulations to the Sandfall Interactive team. For future generations and those that follow!".[^cite_note-104] [Michaël Delafosse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micha%C3%ABl_Delafosse), the mayor of [Montpellier](73369.md), where the studio is based, also commended the team for their accomplishment in late May in a LinkedIn post, stating it will be "a reference in the international history of video games".[^cite_note-105] Sandfall's developers were knighted under the *[Ordre des Arts et des Lettres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordre_des_Arts_et_des_Lettres)* (Order of Arts and Letters) in February 2026 by the French [Ministry of Culture](3354559.md) in recognition of their contribution to the nation's culture.[^cite_note-106]

High-profile developers, including [Hideo Kojima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hideo_Kojima), [Neil Druckmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_Druckmann), [Ken Levine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Levine_\(game_developer\)) have voiced praise for *Expedition 33*, whether for the flexibility demonstrated by the small development team, the game's story, or the ability to achieve a product of great quality while avoiding the pitfalls of excessive monetization that can plague games developed and/or published by established [AAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AAA_\(video_game_industry\)) companies.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-gibson-110] [Ari Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ari_Gibson) believed that it would win Game of the Year over [his own game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollow_Knight:_Silksong), which was a fellow nominee for this accolade at The Game Awards 2025.[^cite_note-gibson-110] Both [Phil Spencer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Spencer_\(business_executive\)) and [Todd Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Howard) picked *Expedition 33* as their personal Game of the Year for 2025.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

Members of Sandfall Interactive at the Game Developers Conference 2026
