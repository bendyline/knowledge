The **Kurultai**,[^cite_note-1] officially known in English as the **Kuryltai of the Republic of Kazakhstan**,[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] is the [unicameral](204304.md) [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) of [Kazakhstan](16642.md), established in June 2026 following constitutional reforms that replaced the previous bicameral [Parliament of Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Kazakhstan), consisting of the [Mäjilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A4jilis) and the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Kazakhstan). The body serves as the sole legislative authority of the state, responsible for adopting laws, approving the [state budget](4360909.md), and exercising oversight of the [executive branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)).

The Kurultai consists of [145 members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Kurultai), all of whom are elected under a proportional system.[^cite_note-azattiq1-4] The current, [1st convocation](83747507.md), was elected following the [2026 Kazakh legislative election](83118601.md).

## History

### Background

The term *[kurultai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurultai)* originates from [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_languages) and [Mongolic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolic_languages), derived from the verbal root *qur-* or *quri-* meaning "to gather" or "to assemble" The suffix *-tai* or *-tay* denotes a collective or formal gathering, giving the term the meaning of a "great assembly" or "national council". Historically, *kurultai* referred to councils of political and military leaders convened in nomadic and semi-nomadic societies of the [Eurasian steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Steppe), including those in the territory of present-day [Kazakhstan](16642.md).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

In steppe political tradition, assemblies identified as *kurultai* were associated with large-scale decision-making among ruling elites. Such gatherings are often linked in historical sources to the Mongol imperial period, when they were used for matters such as the proclamation of rulers and coordination of military campaigns. Similar forms of council-based decision-making also existed in later Turkic polities, including the [Kazakh Khanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_Khanate) (15th–18th centuries), where [khans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_\(title\)), [sultans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan), and [biys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biy), and clan elders convened to address matters of war, peace, customary law, and inter-tribal relations.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] In Kazakh history, such assemblies were convened periodically during major political or military crises, including coordination efforts against external threats in the 18th century. These councils functioned as consultative and consensus-based institutions rather than permanent legislatures.[^cite_note-\:0-9][^cite_note-10]

Following incorporation into the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) in the 19th century and subsequent Soviet administration in the 20th century, traditional steppe governance structures ceased to operate as formal political institutions. In the early 20th century, national congresses associated with the [Alash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alash_\(party\)) national liberation movement briefly revived the idea of collective assemblies in a modern political context, but these were replaced by Soviet administrative systems.[^cite_note-\:0-9]

After Kazakhstan gained independence in 1991, the country adopted a new [constitutional framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Constitution_of_Kazakhstan) in 1995 establishing a [bicameral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicameralism) [Parliament of Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Kazakhstan) consisting of the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Kazakhstan) and the [Mäjilis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A4jilis).[^cite_note-11] This system functioned as the national legislature until its dissolution in 2026.[^cite_note-12]

### Establishing

In 2026, following a constitutional reform approved by [national referendum](82396297.md), Kazakhstan restructured its legislative branch into a [unicameral parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameral_parliament) named the Kurultai. The reform abolished the previous bicameral parliament and established the Kurultai as the sole legislative body of the Republic of Kazakhstan, adopting a historical term to designate the new institution.[^cite_note-azattiq1-4][^cite_note-kursiv-13][^cite_note-azattiq2-14] The Constitutional Law "On the Kurultai of the Republic of Kazakhstan and the Status of Its Deputies" was subsequently ratified by Parliament and [signed into law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_\(law\)) by President [Kassym-Jomart Tokayev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kassym-Jomart_Tokayev) on 5 June 2026.[^cite_note-15] The law established the structure, powers, electoral system, and status of [deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Kurultai) of the new legislature and entered into force on 1 July 2026, simultaneously with the [new constitution](83368720.md), formally establishing the Kurultai as Kazakhstan's national legislature.

## Electoral system

The Kurultai consists of 145 deputies elected on the basis of a [proportional representation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proportional_representation_system) within the territory of a single nationwide [electoral district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district). A citizen of Kazakhstan who has reached the age of 25 and has resided in the country for the past 10 years may be elected as a [deputy chairman of the Kurultai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_chairman_of_the_Kurultai). The term of office of deputies of the Kurultai is 5 years.

## Leadership

### Chairman

Main article: [Chairman of the Kurultai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Kurultai)

The Kurultai is headed by the [Chairman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Kurultai). He is elected by secret ballot from among the [deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_the_Kurultai) who are fluent in the Kazakh language by a majority vote of the total number of Kurultai deputies. The [President of Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Kazakhstan) nominates the Chairman; if the Kurultai refuses to elect the Chairman twice, the President has the right to dissolve the Kurultai.[^cite_note-konstsot-16]

### Deputy Chairman

Main article: [Deputy Chairman of the Kurultai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Chairman_of_the_Kurultai)

The [deputy chairmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_chairman_of_the_Kurultai) are elected by the deputies of the Kurultai, and their candidacies are proposed by the [chairmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chairman_of_the_Kurultai) themselves.[^cite_note-konstsot-16]
