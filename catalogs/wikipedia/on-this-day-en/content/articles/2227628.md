**Borno**[Listen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/6a/LL-Q56475_%28hau%29-Eunice_Ameh-Borno.wav/LL-Q56475_%28hau%29-Eunice_Ameh-Borno.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> is a [state](226734.md) in the [North-East geopolitical zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_\(Nigeria\)) of [Nigeria](21383.md). It is bordered by [Yobe](2139960.md) to the west, [Gombe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gombe_State) to the southwest, and [Adamawa](905832.md) is to the south, while its eastern border forms part of the national [border with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon-Nigeria_border) [Cameroon](5447.md). Its northern border forms part of the national [border with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger%E2%80%93Nigeria_border) [Niger](21373.md) and its northeastern border forms all of the national [border with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad%E2%80%93Nigeria_border) [Chad](5488.md). It is the only Nigerian state to border up to three countries. It takes its name from the historic [emirate of Borno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borno_Emirate), with the emirate's old capital of [Maiduguri](2137952.md) serving as the capital city of Borno State. The state was formed in 1976 when the former [North-Eastern State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-Eastern_State) was broken up. It originally included the area that is now [Yobe State](2139960.md), which became a distinct state in 1991.[^cite_note-4]

Borno is the [second largest in area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_area) of the [36 states](226734.md), only behind [Niger State](1723431.md). Despite its size, the state is the [eleventh most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_population) with an estimated population of about 5.86 million as of 2016.[^cite_note-5] Geographically, the state is divided between the [semi-desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-desert) [Sahelian savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahelian_savanna) in the north and the [West Sudanian savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Sudanian_savanna) in the centre and south with a part of the montane [Mandara Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandara_Plateau_mosaic) in the southeast. In the far northeast of the state is the Nigerian portion of [Lake Chad](59314.md) and the [Lake Chad flooded savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Chad_flooded_savanna) [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ecoregions_in_Nigeria); the lake is fed by the [Yobe River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yobe_River) which forms the state's [border with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niger%E2%80%93Nigeria_border) [Niger](21373.md) until it reaches the lakebed. In the centre of the state is part of the [Chad Basin National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Basin_National_Park), a large [national park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Nigeria) that contains populations of [black crowned crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_crowned_crane), [spotted hyena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_hyena), [patas monkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patas_monkey), and [roan antelope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roan_antelope) along with transient herds of some of Nigeria's last remaining [African bush elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_bush_elephant). However, a section of the park, the [Sambisa Forest](42612125.md), was taken over during the [Boko Haram insurgency](27868253.md) in the early 2010s forcing many fauna to flee;[^cite_note-6] large animals were not seen until 2019 and 2020 when a massive herd of migratory elephants returned to Borno.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Borno State as a territory has been inhabited for centuries by various ethnic groups, including the [Dghwede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dghwede_language), [Glavda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glavda_language), [Guduf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guduf-Gava_language), [Laamang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamang_language), [Mafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafa_people), and [Mandara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandara_people) in the central region; the [Afade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afade_language), [Yedina (Buduma)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buduma_people), and [Kanembu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanembu_people) in the extreme northeast; the [Waja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waja_language) in the extreme south; and the [Kyibaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyibaku_language), [Kamwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamwe_language#People), [Kilba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilba_people), and [Margi groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marghi_people) in the south while the [Kanuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanuri_people) and [Shuwa Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuwa_Arabs) live throughout the state's north and centre. Religiously, the vast majority of the state's population (\~85%) are [Muslim](19541.md) with smaller [Christian](18337522.md) and [traditionalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_African_religions) minorities (especially in the south) at around 7% each.

From the 700s, what is now Borno State was within the territory of the [Kanem Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem_Empire), an empire spanning from modern-day southern [Libya](17633.md) ([Fezzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fezzan)) south through most of now-[Chad](5488.md) into modern-day Borno State. In the late 1300s, the [Kanem Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem_Empire) was forced to move after unsuccessful wars, becoming the [Bornu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornu_Empire) before regaining strength and ruling the wider area for the next 500 years. It was not until the early 1800s when the [Fulani jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulani_jihad) significantly weakened the Empire, that Bornu began to decline. Much of modern-day southern Borno State was seized in the wars and incorporated into the [Adamawa Emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamawa_Emirate) under the [Sokoto Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sokoto_Caliphate). About 80 years later, [Rabih az-Zubayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabih_az-Zubayr), a [Sudanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Sudan) [warlord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warlord), conquered the Empire and ruled until he was killed by French forces in the 1900 [Battle of Kousséri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kouss%C3%A9ri). The Adamawa Emirate was also defeated by colonial powers, losing the [Adamawa Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamawa_Wars) to [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Kamerun) and the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire). Both Rabih's lands (later reconstituted as the [Borno Emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borno_Emirate)) and the Adamawa Emirate were then divided among colonial powers with modern-day Borno State being split between [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Kamerun) and the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire).

The British-controlled area was incorporated into the [Northern Nigeria Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Nigeria_Protectorate) which later merged into [British Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Nigeria) before becoming independent as [Nigeria](21383.md) in 1960. The German-controlled area (territory along the modern-day [border with](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon-Nigeria_border) [Cameroon](5447.md)) formed Deutsch-Bornu as a part of [German Kamerun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Kamerun) until [allied forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) invaded and occupied Kamerun during the [Kamerun campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamerun_campaign) of [World War I](4764461.md). After the war, what is now the eastern periphery of Borno State became a part of the [Northern Cameroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Cameroons) within the [British Cameroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Cameroons) until 1961, when a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1961_British_Cameroons_referendum) led to a merger with [Nigeria](21383.md). Originally, modern-day Borno State was a part of the post-independence [Northern Region](2292852.md) until 1967 when the region was split and the area became part of the [North-Eastern State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North-Eastern_State). After the North-Eastern State was split, Borno State was formed on 3 February 1976 alongside ten other states. Fifteen years after statehood, a group of LGAs in the state's west was broken off to form the new [Yobe State](2139960.md). Years later, the state became the epicentre of the Islamist group [Boko Haram](23769768.md) since it began its [insurgency](27868253.md) in 2009. From 2012 to 2015, the insurgency escalated dramatically with much of the state falling under the control of the group, which soon became the world's deadliest terror group in 2015 and forced millions from their homes.[^cite_note-9] Following a [2015 mass multinational offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_West_African_offensive) along with infighting within the terrorists between the original [Boko Haram](23769768.md) group and the [Islamic State – West Africa Province](46529902.md) breakaway, the group was forced from its strongholds into the [Sambisa Forest](42612125.md) and some islands in [Lake Chad](59314.md) by 2017; however, terrorists continue to be a threat statewide with frequent attacks on both civilian and military targets.[^cite_note-10]

As a partially agriculturally-based state, the rural Borno State economy relied heavily on livestock and crops prior to the [Boko Haram insurgency](27868253.md) while the state capital [Maiduguri](2137952.md) is a major regional trade and service center.[^cite_note-11] However, after years of the insurgency affecting development and forcing farmers from rural areas in the state, Borno has the [thirteenth lowest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Nigerian_states_by_Human_Development_Index#2019) [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) in the country but as the insurgency has slightly abated since 2016, development has renewed.[^cite_note-HDI-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## History

For precolonial history, see [Bornu (historical region)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bornu_\(historical_region\)).



Dancers in Borno state attire

The state has a predominance of [Kanuri people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanuri_people), while other ethnic groups such as [Lapang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapang?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur_people)/[Bura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bura_people), [Mafa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafa_people) and [Marghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marghi_people) are also found in the southern part of the state. [Shuwa Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuwa_Arabs) are mainly the descendants of [Arab people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_people)[^cite_note-15] and are an example of the endurance of traditional political institutions in some areas of [Africa](5334607.md). The emirs of the former [Kanem–Bornu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem%E2%80%93Bornu_Empire) have played a part in the politics of this area for nearly 1,000 years.[^cite_note-16]

The current [Kanemi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanemi_dynasty) gained control of the [Borno Emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borno_Emirate) in the early 19th century after the Fulani jihad of [Usman dan Fodio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usman_dan_Fodio). Conquered by [Rabih](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabih_az-Zubayr) in 1893,[^cite_note-17] Borno was invaded by the British, French and Germans at the beginning of the 20th century. In 1902, the [British](31717.md) officially incorporated Borno into the [Northern Nigeria Protectorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Nigeria_Protectorate)[^cite_note-18] and in 1907 established a new capital at [Maiduguri](2137952.md), which remains the capital to this day.[^cite_note-19]

After [Nigerian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigerian_Independence) in 1960, Borno remained fairly autonomous until the number of states in Nigeria expanded to 12 in 1967. Local government reform in 1976 further reduced the power of the emirs of the former dynasty, and by the time of [Nigeria's return to civilian rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nigeria_\(1979-1999\)#Second_Republic) in 1979, the emirs' jurisdiction has been restricted solely to cultural and traditional affairs. [Mala Kachallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mala_Kachallah) was elected governor of Borno State in 1999 under the flagship of the then APP (All Peoples Party), later renamed the [All Nigeria People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Nigeria_People's_Party) (ANPP). [Ali Modu Sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Modu_Sheriff) was elected governor of Borno State in Nigeria in April 2003.[^cite_note-20]

Boko Haram's insurgency began in 2009, with Borno being the worst-affected area. On 14 May 2013, [President](516088.md) [Goodluck Jonathan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodluck_Jonathan) declared a [state of emergency](298608.md) in northeastern Nigeria,[^cite_note-21] including Borno State along with the neighboring states of [Adamawa](905832.md) and [Yobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yobe).[^cite_note-22] This happened after fighting between [Boko Haram](23769768.md) and the state armed forces [killed 200 people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Baga_massacre) in the town of [Baga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baga,_Nigeria). A spokesman for the [armed forces](21391.md) declared that the offensive would continue "as long as it takes to achieve our objective of getting rid of insurgents from every part of Nigeria."[^cite_note-23]

In July 2014, the state's governor [Kashim Shettima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashim_Shettima) said that "176 teachers had been killed and 900 schools destroyed since 2011."[^cite_note-audujune26-24] After the [Chibok schoolgirls kidnapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibok_schoolgirls_kidnapping) in April 2014, most schools in Borno State were closed.[^cite_note-25]

In November 2014, [UNICEF](19698110.md) reported it has increased its Community Management of Acute Malnutrition (CMAM) centres in Borno State "from 5 to 67."[^cite_note-26] In Borno State, the agricultural sector has suffered mostly because of the insurgency, and many people have experienced acute [food insecurity](216361.md).[^cite_note-27]

## Climate

The climate of Borno state is characteristic of rainfall variability, with a strong latitudinal zone, which is drier in this northeastern state.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The commencement of the rainy season in this northeast state is around June/July of every year, which is far behind the southeastern states. The [trade wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_winds), also regarded as the [harmattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmattan) season is often experienced in the state between the months of December and February. There is a reduction in rainfall from 3,800 mm to below 650 mm in the state, hence it rains in the state between 4 and 5 months annually.[^cite_note-30] The state experiences high [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humidity) annually. The hottest period in the state is in the month of May, with an average of 34<sup>o</sup>C while the month of January is the coldest with an annual average of 23<sup>o</sup>C. The wettest month is August with an average of 118.6 mm while the windiest month is December with an average of 11 km/h.[^cite_note-31]

An IDP camp outside [Maiduguri](2137952.md) from 2018, where internally displaced people come to escape harassment from the [Boko Haram](23769768.md) insurgency.

[Internally displaced person](427408.md) [camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_internally_displaced_persons_camps_in_Nigeria) in Borno State, [Nigeria](21383.md) were centers accommodating [Nigerians](13008384.md) who had been [forced to flee](477535.md) their homes but remain within the country's borders. Displaced persons camps in [Maiduguri](2137952.md) accommodated from 120,000 to 130,000 people, while those in [local government areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_areas_of_Nigeria) ranged above 400,000.[^cite_note-Internally_displaced_persons_camps_in_Borno_\:0-43] There were over two million displaced persons in the state.[^cite_note-Internally_displaced_persons_camps_in_Borno_\:1-44] Internal Displacement Monitoring Centre (IDMC) suggested the figure of internally displaced persons in the state to be 1,434,149, the highest in [Northern Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Nigeria).[^cite_note-45]

## Religion

[Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Nigeria) continues to be the dominant faith practised in Borno State, with much smaller numbers of adherents of [Christianity](12241851.md) and other faiths spread throughout and living within the region.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] [Sharia](28840.md) operates as the primary foundation for the development, interpretation, and enforcement of most civic codes and laws. The [Roman Catholic Diocese of Maiduguri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Maiduguri) has its seat in the State. The [Anglican Diocese of Maiduguri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Maiduguri) (1990) within the [Province of Jos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Jos), is led by Bishop [Emmanuel Morris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmanuel_Morris) (2017). [Ekklesiar Yan'Uwa A Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Brethren_in_Nigeria) (EYN) buildings in Maiduguri were destroyed by Boko Haram[^cite_note-50] as a part of their [uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Boko_Haram_uprising), which were later rebuilt.
