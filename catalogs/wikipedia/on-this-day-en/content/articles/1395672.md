# Commuter rail in North America

"Commuter railroad" redirects here. For commuter railroads in general, see [Commuter rail](6286.md).



[NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit) has an extensive [commuter rail](6286.md) system connecting [New Jersey](21648.md) to [New York City](645042.md) and [Philadelphia](50585.md).



[GO Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GO_Transit) operates a vast [commuter rail system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GO_Transit_rail_services) connecting [Toronto](64646.md) to the [Greater Golden Horseshoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Golden_Horseshoe) region.



A [Metra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metra) train in [West Chicago, Illinois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Chicago,_Illinois).

[Commuter rail](6286.md) services in [Canada](5042916.md), [Costa Rica](5551.md), [Cuba](5042481.md), [Mexico](3966054.md), [Panama](22997.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) provide [common carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_carrier) passenger transportation along railway tracks, with scheduled service on fixed routes on a non-reservation basis, primarily for short-distance (local) travel between a [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) and adjacent suburbs and regional travel between cities of a [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation). It does *not* include [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) or [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) service.

## Services

Many, but not all, newer commuter railways offer service during peak times only, with trains into the [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district) during morning rush hour and returning to the outer areas during the evening rush hour. This mode of operation is, in many cases, simplified by ending the train with a special passenger carriage (referred to as a [cab car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cab_car)), which has an operating cab and can control the locomotive remotely, to avoid having to turn the train around at each end of its route. Other systems avoid the problem entirely by using bi-directional [multiple units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_unit).

Other commuter rail services, many of them older, long-established ones, operate seven days a week, with service from early morning to after midnight. On these systems, patrons use the trains not just to get to and from work or school, but also for attending sporting events, concerts, theatre, and the like. Some also provide service to popular weekend getaway spots and recreation areas. The [Long Island Rail Road](37139.md) (LIRR) is the only commuter railroad that operates 24 hours a day, 7 days a week in North America.

Almost all commuter rail services in North America are operated by government entities or quasi-governmental organizations. Most share tracks or rights-of-way used by longer-distance passenger services (e.g. [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak), [Via Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Rail)), freight trains, or other commuter services. The 600-mile-long (970 km) [electrified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_electrification_system) [Northeast Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Corridor) in the United States is shared by commuter trains and [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak)'s [Acela Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acela_Express), regional, and intercity trains.

Commuter rail operators often sell reduced-price multiple-trip tickets (such as a monthly or weekly pass), charge specific station-to-station fares, and have one or two [railroad stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railroad_station) in the central business district. Commuter trains typically connect to [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) or bus services at their destination and along their route.

After the completion of [SEPTA Regional Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEPTA_Regional_Rail)'s [Center City Commuter Connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_City_Commuter_Connection) in 1981, which allowed through-running between two formerly separate radial networks, the term "regional rail" began to be used to refer to commuter rail (and sometimes even larger [heavy rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_rail) and [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail)) systems that offer bidirectional all-day service and may provide useful connections between suburbs and [edge cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_city), rather than merely transporting workers to a [central business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_business_district).[^cite_note-1] This is different from the European use of "[regional rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_rail)", which generally refers to services midway between commuter rail and [intercity rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_rail) that are not primarily commuter-oriented.

Some transit lines in the NYC metropolitan areas have commuter lines that act like a regional rail network, as lines often converge at one point and pass as a main line to the destination station. They also pass through large business areas (ie Harlem, Jamaica, Stamford, Metropark), and some lines operate every 5–10 minutes during peak hours, and roughly every 15 minutes during off hours.

## Spread



[South Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Station) in [Boston](24437894.md), [Massachusetts](1645518.md) is a major [transportation hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_hub) for the [MBTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massachusetts_Bay_Transportation_Authority)'s [commuter rail services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBTA_Commuter_Rail).

The two busiest passenger rail stations in the United States are [Pennsylvania Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Station_\(New_York_City\)) and [Grand Central Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Central_Terminal), which are both located in the Borough of Manhattan in New York City, and which serve three of the four busiest commuter railroads in the United States (the [Long Island Rail Road](37139.md) and [NJ Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NJ_Transit_Rail_Operations) at Penn Station, and the [Metro-North Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-North_Railroad) and the [Long Island Rail Road](37139.md) at Grand Central Terminal). The commuter railroads serving the [Chicago](6886.md) area are [Metra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metra) (the fourth-busiest commuter railroad in the United States) and the [South Shore Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Shore_Line) (one of the last surviving [interurbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interurban)). Other notable commuter railroad systems include [SEPTA Regional Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEPTA_Regional_Rail) (fifth-busiest in the US), serving the [Philadelphia](50585.md) area; [MBTA Commuter Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBTA_Commuter_Rail) (sixth-busiest in the US), serving the [Greater Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Boston)-[Providence](19356538.md) area; [Caltrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caltrain), serving the area south of [San Francisco](49728.md) along [the peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Peninsula) as far as [San Jose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose,_California); and [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)), serving the 5-county [Los Angeles](18110.md) area.

There are only three commuter rail agencies in Canada: [GO Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GO_Transit) in [Toronto](64646.md), [Exo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exo_commuter_rail) in [Montreal](7954681.md) (eighth-busiest in North America), and [West Coast Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Express) in [Vancouver](32706.md). GO Transit's [rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GO_Transit_rail_services) is the third busiest commuter rail system in North America. The two busiest rail stations in Canada are [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Toronto\)) in Toronto and [Gare Centrale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Central_Station) in Montreal.



A suburban train in [Bejucal, Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bejucal,_Cuba)

Commuter rail networks outside of densely populated urban areas like the Washington D.C., New York, Chicago, Philadelphia, Boston, San Francisco, Montreal, and Toronto metropolitan areas have historically been sparse. Since the 1990s, however, several commuter rail projects have been proposed and built throughout the United States, especially in the [Sun Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Belt) and other regions characterized by [urban sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl) that have traditionally been underserved by public transportation. Since then, commuter rail networks have been inaugurated in Dallas–Fort Worth, Los Angeles, San Diego, Minneapolis, Denver, Salt Lake City, and Orlando, among other cities. Several more commuter rail projects have been proposed and are in the planning stages.

## Rolling stock

Commuter trains are either powered by diesel-electric or electric [locomotives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locomotive), or else use [self-propelled cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_unit) (some systems, such as the New York area's [Metro-North Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro-North_Railroad), use both). A few systems, particularly around New York City, use electric power, supplied by a [third rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_rail) and/or [overhead catenary wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhead_line), which provides quicker acceleration, lower noise, and fewer air-quality issues. Philadelphia's [SEPTA Regional Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SEPTA_Regional_Rail) uses exclusively electric power, supplied by overhead catenary wire.

[Diesel-electric locomotives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel-electric_locomotive) based on the [EMD F40PH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EMD_F40PH) design as well as the [MP36PH-3C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MPI_MPXpress) are popular as motive power for commuter trains. Manufacturers of coaches include [Bombardier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Transportation), [Kawasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kawasaki_Heavy_Industries), [Nippon Sharyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Sharyo), and [Hyundai Rotem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Rotem). A few systems use [diesel multiple unit vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_multiple_unit), including [WES Commuter Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WES_Commuter_Rail) near Portland and Austin's [Capital MetroRail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_MetroRail). These systems use vehicles supplied by [Stadler Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadler_Rail) or [US Railcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Railcar) (formerly [Colorado Railcar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Railcar)).
