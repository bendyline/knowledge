The [Islamic State](9087364.md) is waging an [ongoing](2346678.md) [insurgency](589841.md) against the [Taliban](30635.md) regime in [Afghanistan](737.md).[^cite_note-50] The conflict initially began when both operated as rival insurgent groups in [Nangarhar](457263.md); since the formation of the Taliban's state in 2021, IS members have enacted a campaign of [terrorism](30636.md) targeting both civilians and assassinating Taliban members using [hit-and-run tactics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit-and-run_tactics).[^cite_note-\:0-51] The group have also caused incidents and attacks across the border in [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-AP_11/4/22-7]

The conflict began in 2015 following the creation of the [Islamic State – Khorasan Province](48029263.md) (IS-KP), a regional branch of the [Islamic State](9087364.md). IS-KP and the Taliban started to clash over control of territory during the [war in Afghanistan (2001–2021)](19666611.md), mostly in eastern Afghanistan but also through cells in the north-west and south-west.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-newlinesmag.com-53] The [Haqqani network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haqqani_network), [al-Qaeda](1921.md) and others supported the Taliban, while IS was supported by the [Mullah Dadullah Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullah_Dadullah_Front) and the pro-ISIS faction of the [Islamic Movement of Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Movement_of_Uzbekistan). IS-KP had lost territory to both the Taliban and [the former republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) government, with American support, by 2020.[^cite_note-newlinesmag.com-53] Following the [Taliban capture of Kabul](68481047.md) in 2021, IS-KP have continued its insurgent attacks against the new Taliban regime.[^cite_note-\:0-51]

As of October 2024 IS-KP is the largest and most powerful of the terror groups active in Afghanistan,[^cite_note-crisisgroup.org-54] with the Taliban appearing to view IS-KP as the primary threat to their rule.[^cite_note-Terrorist_Groups_in_Afghanistan-55] As of June 2026, [BBC Monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Monitoring) reported that IS-KP is severely crippled and only a "[shadow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadow) of its former self.'[^cite_note-\:1-56]

## Background

During the original stint in power of the [Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan) in the late 1990s, the ruling [Taliban](30635.md) had pursued a policy of suppressing [Salafism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafism); motivated by strict [Deobandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deobandi) tenets. During this period, the main issue of Salafist scholars was that Taliban was led by [Maturidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maturidi) Sufis. As a result of the unofficial Taliban bans on *[Ahl-i Hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahl-i_Hadith)* during the 1996–2001 era, several Salafis had shifted to [Peshawar](66230.md). However, after the [US-led invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US-led_invasion_of_Afghanistan) in 2001, Taliban and *[Ahl-i Hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahl-i_Hadith)* allied to wage a common [Jihad](16203.md) to resist the invasion. The Afghan Salafists decided to put aside their differences with the Taliban to join them in the "[greater jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_jihad)" against the United States. Several Arab Salafis in [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) rank and file would mediate the disputes between Afghan Salafists and Taliban; enabling them to unify for the more important religious duty of fighting against the [U.S and its allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Participants_in_Operation_Enduring_Freedom) in Afghanistan. Many Salafi commanders and *Ahl-i Hadith* organisations participated in the [Taliban insurgency (2001–2021)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_insurgency) under Afghan Taliban's command.[^cite_note-islamic-state-khorasan-provinces-peshawar-seminary-attack-and-war-against-afghan-taliban-hanafis-57][^cite_note-58]

During the Taliban insurgency, in January 2015, IS established itself in [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan) and formed IS-K.[^cite_note-nyt-59] The main objective of IS-K was to occupy the land of Khorasan, which includes the country of Afghanistan.[^cite_note-60] Even though the initial IS-K was formed by Taliban as well as [Tehrik-i-Taliban Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehrik-i-Taliban_Pakistan) (TTP) defectors and thus ideologically similar, it became dominated by [Salafists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESayed202110–11-61] The disgruntled members of TTP would establish [IS-KP](48029263.md) and shifted to the [Nangarhar](457263.md) province. After its founding [Pakistani](28171281.md) leaders who defected from TTP were killed in US drone strikes, Afghan Salafists took charge of TTP.[^cite_note-62] The conflict escalated when militants affiliated with IS-KP killed Abdul Ghani, a senior Taliban commander in [Logar Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logar_Province) on 2 February 2015.[^cite_note-turf-18]

The emergence of IS-K provided militant Afghan Salafists with an opportunity to set up a rival force, although Salafist support for the group waned as it proved ideologically "too extreme and brutal" for most Afghan Salafis.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESayed202111–12-63] As a result, the majority of Afghan Salafis have remained supportive of the Taliban. In March 2020, major [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun) *Ahl-i Hadith* *[ulema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulema)* convened in [Peshawar](66230.md) under the leadership of Shaikh Abdul Aziz Nooristani and Haji Hayatullah to pledge *[Bay'ah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay'ah)* (oath of loyalty) to the Taliban and publicly condemn IS-K. The scholars also requested protection from the Afghan Taliban for the *Ahl-i Hadith* community.[^cite_note-islamic-state-khorasan-provinces-peshawar-seminary-attack-and-war-against-afghan-taliban-hanafis-57]

After Taliban victory in the [War in Afghanistan](19666611.md) and [restoration of the Islamic Emirate](68481047.md), hundreds of *Ahl-i Hadith* *[ulema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulama)* would gather to announce their *[Bay'ah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay'ah)* (pledge of allegiance) to the [Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan](737.md). Numerous *Ahl-i Hadith* clerics and their representatives held gatherings across various provinces of Afghanistan to re-affirm their backing of the Taliban and officially declare their support to the Taliban crackdown on IS-K.[^cite_note-64] Several members of the [Afghan intelligence agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Directorate_of_Security) and the [Afghan national army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_national_army) have also joined the Islamic State – Khorasan Province.[^cite_note-Former-1-42][^cite_note-former-41] The [High Council of the Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Council_of_the_Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan), previously a breakaway Taliban faction, announced that they have pledged allegiance to the Taliban and will dissolve.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-\:4-66] In February 2022, Pakistani officials acknowledged that ongoing violence was destabilizing the region.[^cite_note-regional-67]

### Opposing forces

By 2016 IS-K mostly consisted of eastern Afghans, Pakistanis, and foreign fighters from Central Asia. The latter were mainly former members of the [Islamic Jihad Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Jihad_Union) and the [Turkistan Islamic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkistan_Islamic_Party). In addition, there were a small number of Arabs.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson20162-46] Throughout its existence, IS-K has operated in a very limited area, mainly concentrated in select provinces in eastern Afghanistan,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson20162-46] most importantly [Nangarhar](457263.md) and [Kunar](2159600.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson20163-68] By 2016, it had appointed shadow governors in other regions as well, but not exerted much influence outside its traditional bases.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson20162-46] The group is known to receive support by the Islamic State's central command in form of money[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson20161-11] and combat trainers from Iraq and Syria.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson20163-68] IS-K's combat strength has fluctuated greatly over the years, but has mostly remained in the low thousands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohnson20162-46]

## During the Taliban insurgency

### 2015

On 2 February militants affiliated with IS-K killed Abdul Ghani, a Taliban commander, in [Logar province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logar_province).[^cite_note-turf-18]

On 26 May Asif Nang, governor of [Farah province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farah_province), said the Taliban have been fighting against IS militants for the past three days in Farah province. The clash left ten Taliban and 15 IS militants dead.[^cite_note-69]

In May IS-K militants captured Maulvi Abbas, a Taliban commander who was leading a small squad of insurgent fighters in [Nangarhar province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nangarhar_province).[^cite_note-clash-1-70]

In June IS-K militants beheaded ten Taliban fighters who were fleeing an Afghan military offensive according to a spokesman of Afghan army corps responsible for the region.[^cite_note-clash-1-70]

On 9 November fighting had broken out between different Taliban factions in the [Zabul Province](362239.md) of [Afghanistan](737.md). Fighters loyal to the new Taliban leader [Akhtar Mansour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akhtar_Mansour) began to fight a pro-IS faction, led by Mullah [Mansoor Dadullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansoor_Dadullah). According to Afghan security and local officials, Akhtar Mansour had sent as many as 450 Taliban fighters to crush Mullah Mansoor and Islamic State elements in Zabul.[^cite_note-Zabul_offensive-12] Dadullah's faction received support from IS during the clashes, and IS fighters also joined in on the fighting alongside Dadullah, including foreign fighters from [Chechnya](6095.md) and [Uzbekistan](31853.md). Dadullah and IS were eventually defeated by Mansour's forces.[^cite_note-71] Hajji Momand Nasratyar, the district governor of Arghandab, said the fighting took place in three districts of Zabul province and 86 IS militants and 26 Taliban fighters were killed in the clash. Taliban also reported to have killed several IS militants who were responsible for beheading of seven [Hazara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazaras) civilians a few days back.[^cite_note-Zabul_offensive-12]

Hajji Atta Jan, the Zabul provincial council chief, said the offensive by Mullah Mansour's fighters was so intense, that at least three Islamic State commanders, all of them ethnic Uzbeks, had surrendered. They were also asking others IS militants to do the same.[^cite_note-Zabul_offensive-12] [Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe/Radio_Liberty), while quoting sources from Southern Afghanistan, reported that some 70 IS militants were also captured in the clash by the Taliban.[^cite_note-72]

On 13 November Ghulam Jelani Farahi, an Afghan police chief, said that Mullah Mansoor Dadullah was killed in a clash with Taliban.[^cite_note-Dadullah-35]

### 2016

In January hundreds of Taliban fighters launched an assault against IS bases in eastern Afghanistan. Taliban fighters were successful in capturing two districts from IS in eastern Afghanistan, but it failed to drive the group out of their stronghold in the [Nazyan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazyan_District) in Nangarhar province.[^cite_note-73] Ataullah Khogyani, a spokesman for the provincial governor, said that 26 IS militants and five Taliban fighters were killed in the clashes in Nangarhar.[^cite_note-74]

On 2 February US carried out airstrikes targeting IS radio station in eastern Afghanistan. The strike destroyed the radio station and killed 29 IS militants.[^cite_note-75]

In March Taliban factions led by [Muhammad Rasul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_Rasul) and opposed to Mansoor, began to fight against his loyalists in the group. During the fighting, dozens were reported killed.[^cite_note-\:0a-76]

On 26 April Hazrat Hussain Mashriqwal, a provincial police spokesman, said that ten IS militants, including an IS commander, and six Taliban fighters were killed in a clash in Nangarhar. Fifteen IS militants and four Taliban fighters were also wounded during the same clash according to the spokesman.[^cite_note-77]

On 19 May local government officials reported that a clash took place between IS and Taliban in [Achin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achin_District) and [Khogyani District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khogyani_District) of Nangarhar province. Fifteen IS militants and three Taliban fighters were killed in Achin district, and the remaining were killed in Khogyani. Four Taliban commanders were also among the dead.[^cite_note-78]

On 13 August US defence officials said that ISIL's top leader, [Hafiz Saeed Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz_Saeed_Khan), was killed in a drone strike on 26 July in Nangarhar province.[^cite_note-79]

On 30 October Ajmal Zahid, a governor of [Golestan district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulistan_District), said that ISIL's commander, Abdul Razaq Mehdi, was killed by Taliban fighters in [Farah province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farah_province).[^cite_note-80]

### 2017

On 13 April 2017 the United States dropped[^cite_note-81] the largest non-[nuclear](21785.md) bomb, known as the [GBU-43/B Massive Ordnance Air Blast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBU-43/B_Massive_Ordnance_Air_Blast) (MOAB) Mother of All Bombs near [Momand village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momand_Dara_District)[^cite_note-GuardiaRasmussen-82] upon a [Nangahar](457263.md)'s [Achin District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achin_District) village in eastern Afghanistan to destroy tunnel complexes used by the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant – Khorasan Province (ISIL-KP or ISIS–K).[^cite_note-CBS_US_Drops-83][^cite_note-Fox_US_Drops-84][^cite_note-85] The *Guardian* reported that following the strike, US and Afghan forces conducted clearing operations and airstrikes in the area and assessed the damage.[^cite_note-86]

On 26 April a [fight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Darzab_\(2017\)) occurred after IS captured three drug dealers who were involved in selling [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium) for the Taliban in [Jowzjan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jowzjan_Province). An [Afghan National Police](13327465.md) spokesman stated that the Taliban attacked IS in response, saying "The clashes erupted when group of armed Taliban attacked Daesh militants \[to secure] the release of three drug smugglers who came here to pay 10 million [afghanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_afghani) \[\$14,780] to the Taliban for a deal." The Taliban's spokesman, [Zabihullah Mujahid](18935255.md) had also confirmed clashes were ongoing with IS at the time, without providing details on the nature of the fight or reasons.[^cite_note-87] Mohammad Reza Ghafori, a spokesman for the provincial governor, said that the clashes between Taliban and IS-K had left 76 Taliban and 15 IS militants dead. IS militants also seized two districts from the Taliban, according to the spokesman.[^cite_note-88]

On 24 May a clash between the Taliban and IS occurred, and at that time, it had reportedly been the largest clash between the two with 22 casualties, 13 of which were IS fighters, and nine Taliban fighters, according to a Taliban official. The clashes occurred near [Iran](14653.md)'s border with Afghanistan. The Taliban had attacked an IS camp in the area, an IS commander, who was formerly a Taliban member, said that there was an agreement between the Taliban and IS not to attack each other until there was a dialogue. The commander claimed that the Taliban had violated the agreement and attacked the IS camp. The IS commander also claimed the attack was coordinated with the [Iranian military](14305018.md), and that there were Iranians filming dead IS fighters. The Taliban splinter faction [Fidai Mahaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fidai_Mahaz) has also criticized the Taliban for its relationship with Iran. Days before the battle, the Taliban reportedly met with Iranian officials to discuss regional issues. A spokesman for Fidai Mahaz claimed the meeting was held at the request of the Taliban, as it was weary of the expansion of IS in the country, which also concerned the Iranian government. The spokesman also said that the Taliban received US\$3 million in cash, 3,000 arms, 40 trucks, and the ammunition from Iran's intelligence services, to fight IS near the Iranian border, although a Taliban spokesman denied the allegations.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90]

On 27 November Taliban executed one of its senior commanders for colluding with IS. A week before, IS fighters were mass executed by their fellow militants in Achin district, according to a provincial government spokesman. However, the spokesman did not provide any additional detail, and neither did IS release any official statement on killing its own members.[^cite_note-91]

### 2018

Further information: [Battle of Darzab (2018)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Darzab_\(2018\))



Surrendered Islamic State fighters after the [Battle of Darzab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Darzab_\(2018\)).

On 20 June after the talks between the Russian government and the Taliban, US assistant secretary of state [Alice Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Wells) condemned the Russian government's position on the Taliban that included backing for the group against IS, stating it gave the Taliban legitimacy and challenged the recognized Afghan government.[^cite_note-92]

In July the Taliban launched an [offensive](15077993.md) against IS in the Jowzjan province. According to a surrendered IS commander, the Taliban had amassed 2,000 fighters for the offensive against IS. The fighters from the [Islamic Movement of Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Movement_of_Uzbekistan), who had sworn allegiance to IS, were also present fighting alongside IS against the Taliban. During the fighting, 3,500 to 7,000 civilians were displaced. By the end of July, IS's hold in the region was reduced to two villages, all thanks to the Taliban's campaign. In response, they requested support from the [Afghan government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_government), and also agreed to put down their arms in exchange for protection from the Taliban. The [Afghan Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Air_Force) later carried out airstrikes against the Taliban in exchange for IS's surrender in the region. The agreement between the Afghan government and IS created controversy afterwards.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] On 17 July, IS militants killed 15 Taliban militants and injured five others during a raid on a house belonging to a Taliban commander in [Sar-e Pol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sar-e_Pol_Province). Abdul Qayuom Baqizoi, the police chief of Sar-e Pol, told [Associated Press](18935732.md) that Taliban and IS fighters have been fighting each other in [Jowzjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jowzjan_Province) and Sar-e Pol for more than two months, killing hundreds on both sides.[^cite_note-95]

In August Maulawi Habib Ur Rehman, together with 152 militants, surrendered to the 209th Shaheen Corps of the Afghan Military in Jawzjan province.[^cite_note-96] During the negotiations between the [US government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_government) and the Taliban in [Doha](26214389.md), the Taliban had requested that the US ends airstrikes on the Taliban, as well as provide support to the group to fight IS.[^cite_note-97]

In December the press office of 201 Seylab Division announced that Daesh spokesman Sultan Aziz Azam has been killed in an airstrike by foreign forces in Vazirtangi area, Khogyani District, Nangarhar Province.[^cite_note-98]

### 2020

In March 2020 the Afghan Salafist Council under its emir, Shaikh Abdul Aziz Nooristani, met with Taliban leaders and pledged loyalty to their movement. Salafists had previously provided crucial support to IS-K, but recognized that the latter's position had greatly declined after its defeats in Nangarhar and Kunar.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESayed20219-104] The Salafist Council, represented by 32 scholars and military leaders, stated that they were in no way loyal to IS-K, and wanted to be left out of the Islamic State–Taliban conflict. The Taliban leadership accepted the pledge of loyalty, exploiting it in its propaganda.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESayed20219–10-105]

In October 2020 former [Politico](9172097.md) reporter Wesley Morgan revealed that United States special operations forces, longtime foes of the Taliban, had been conducting drone strikes against IS-K to give the Taliban an advantage in the field. According to Morgan, the operators were jokingly referred to as the "Taliban Air Force" and instead of communicating directly with Taliban commanders, they would monitor Taliban communications and decide when was the best time to strike.[^cite_note-106] On 10 December 2020, General [Kenneth McKenzie Jr.](55230452.md), head of [US Central Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Central_Command), confirmed that the US had assisted the Taliban via opportunistic drone strikes, saying that they did not coordinate operations with the Taliban, but took advantage of them fighting a "common enemy" to conduct their own operations. Gen. McKenzie said the strikes occurred several months prior when IS-K was holding ground in Nangarhar Province and elsewhere in eastern Afghanistan.[^cite_note-107]

## After the Taliban's takeover of Afghanistan

### Renewed Islamic State attacks and anti-Salafist purge

Further information: [2021 Kabul airport attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Kabul_airport_attack) and [2021 Kunduz mosque bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Kunduz_mosque_bombing)

The Taliban finally succeeded in taking over Afghanistan from the [Islamic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) during a [large-scale offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive) in summer 2021. [Kabul fell](68481047.md) on 15 August 2021, prompting the leaders of the IS-K to denounce the Taliban takeover of Afghanistan.[^cite_note-108] The Taliban immediately moved to contain or purge potential opponents, including Islamic State supporters and Salafists. Across the country, the Taliban ordered the closure of Salafist mosques and seminaries, and tried to arrest prominent Salafist scholars, prompting many to go-into-hiding. Among those targeted by the new Taliban authorities were Salafi [clerics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clergy) whom had previously publicly opposed IS-K.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESayed202110-109] Researcher Abdul Sayed argued that the purge was probably organized by hardline anti-Salafist elements within the Taliban, but more motivated by long-time resentment than fears about Salafi support for a future IS-K insurgency.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESayed202112-110] On 16 August, the Taliban claimed to have killed around 150 IS-K fighters, including its former chief, Abu Umar Khurasani, whilst prisoners were being released from a jail in Kabul.[^cite_note-Pakdef-34] However, many IS-K militants were able to rejoin their IS-K ranks after a string of [prison-breaks](3902454.md) across the country were organized by the Taliban.[^cite_note-Pakdef-34]

On 26 August 2021, a suicide bombing and a mass shooting occurred near Abbey Gate at [Hamid Karzai International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_International_Airport) in Kabul.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114][^cite_note-115] The attack began mere hours after the [US State Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_State_Department) advised Americans outside the airport to leave due to terrorism threats.[^cite_note-116] At least 185 people were killed in these attacks, including 13 US service members.[^cite_note-117] The Taliban condemned the attack, saying "evil circles will be strictly stopped".[^cite_note-118] The Taliban, later, announced that they would take every possible measure to capture IS-KP leader Shahab al-Muhajir.[^cite_note-119] The same day, Saifullah Mohammed (Taliban's CID chief) told *[The Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Times)* that they had captured six militants belonging to IS-K, following a gun battle in west Kabul.[^cite_note-120]

Taliban militants kidnapped the influential Salafi cleric Mullah Abu Obaidullah Mutawakil on 28 August; he was "brutal\[ly]" murdered one week later.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESayed20219-104] Taliban spokesman [Zabiullah Mujahid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zabiullah_Mujahid) denied the Taliban's role in the killing, but also did not condemn the act.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESayed20219-104] Even though Mutawakil was described as an IS-K sympathiser, and a large number of his students were a part of IS-K,[^cite_note-Pakdef-34] he had not officially backed the Islamic State. IS-K did not offer prayers for him after his demise, stating that he had "not been loyal" to the Islamic State.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESayed202110-109] On 9 November 2021, Reuters journalist James MacKenzie stated that "frequent, smaller atrocities" in the conflict are "less commonly reported."[^cite_note-beyondnangarhar-121] Aside from the ISIS stronghold of [Nangarhar](457263.md), other affected areas include [Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazni_Province) in central Afghanistan, [Herat](312559.md) in the west, [Balkh](457218.md) in the north, and [Paktia](457274.md), [Paktika](457294.md) and [Khost](457249.md) in the southeast.[^cite_note-beyondnangarhar-121]

#### 2023

On 1 January at least ten people were killed and eight others were wounded by an explosion at the entrance to the military airport in Kabul, the Afghan capital, on New Year's morning (1 January 2023), the Afghan Interior Ministry reported without providing further details or the exact number of victims.[^cite_note-199] The jihadist group Islamic State (IS) today claimed responsibility for the attack against a surveillance post in the military zone of the Kabul airport and identified the suicide bomber, the same one who attacked a hotel frequented by Chinese citizens three weeks ago in the Afghan capital.[^cite_note-200]

On 5 January a Taliban spokesperson stated that its forces have killed eight Islamic State insurgents, including foreign nationals, accusing them of having a "main role in the [attack on \[Longan\] hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kabul_hotel_attack)". These killings, along with seven arrests, took place in a series of raids in Kabul and the western Nimruz Province.[^cite_note-201]

On 11 January at least 20 people were killed in a suicide bombing outside the Ministry of Foreign Affairs in [Kabul](16826.md) according to Information Ministry official Ustad Fareedun. Responsibility for the attack was claimed by the [Islamic State](48029263.md).[^cite_note-202]

On 27 February the Taliban forces killed two Islamic State militants who they described as "key commanders".[^cite_note-203]

On 9 March, three people, including Mohammad Dawood Muzamil, the Taliban-appointed governor of Balkh Province, were killed by an explosion at his office.[^cite_note-Barrons_Taliban_Governor_Blast-24]

On 17 March, the Taliban killed several Islamic State-Khorasan Province insurgents and seized weapons and ammunition during raids on hideouts in Mazar-i-Sharif, Balkh Province.[^cite_note-204]

On 4 April six Islamic State members were killed during a raid by the Taliban in Balkh Province.[^cite_note-205]

In 2023 Taliban security forces killed the head of the ISIS cell responsible for the [2021 Kabul airport attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Kabul_airport_attack).[^cite_note-206]

On 6 June ISIS–K released a statement claiming responsibility for a car bombing which killed the Taliban-appointed Deputy Governor of Badakhstan.[^cite_note-207] It was also reported that the Deputy Governor who was killed was in fact Badakhstan's acting Governor.[^cite_note-208]

On 8 June 2023 a bombing took place during a mourning service at a mosque in Fayzabad, Badakhshan Province, northern Afghanistan. At least 13 people were killed in the blast, while more than 30 more were injured. The deputy governor of the province, Nisar Ahmad Ahmadi, who was killed that week in a car bombing, was remembered during the memorial service. The bombing happened close to the Nabawi Mosque in Fayzabad, the province's capital.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211] ISIS claimed responsibility for the attack, which was aimed at Taliban officials who were attending the service, and also claimed at least 20 senior Taliban officials were killed and 50 others were injured, a higher figure than what the Taliban reported.[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-213]

On 22 June 2023 ISIS magazine *[Khurasan Ghag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khurasan_Ghag)* released an issue titled "Shari'a Evaluation of Mullah Hibbatullah's Deceptive Words," which accused the Taliban of actually being "proxy fighters" of "unbeliever intelligence agencies" and also dubbed the Afghan Taliban's "Islamic System" government to not be in compliance with [Sharia](28840.md) law.[^cite_note-kgjun2023-214] The magazine further accused Western powers of aiding the Taliban's return to power because they assumed that Ashraf Ghani's government would collapse and sought to maintain influence with a regime change, pointing out the Taliban's efforts to build relations with the United States and how the change in government did not end the nation's reliance on American dollars.[^cite_note-kgjun2023-214]

On 13 October 2023, an Islamic State suicide bomber attacked a Shia mosque in Puli Khumri, killing seven and injuring fifteen.[^cite_note-215]

#### 2024

On 6 January, ISIS–K detonated an explosive on a minibus in the Shia majority Dasht-e-Barchi, Kabul, killing five passengers and injuring dozens.[^cite_note-216]

On 11 January, a grenade explosion outside a commercial center in Dasht-e-Barchi, Kabul killed two people and wounded twelve. No group claimed responsibility for the attack, though ISIS–K is suspected to have conducted it.[^cite_note-217]

Main article: [2024 Kandahar New Kabul Bank bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Kandahar_New_Kabul_Bank_bombing)

On 21 March, a suicide bomber attacked a private bank in [Kandahar](17260.md), resulting in the deaths of at least three people and injuring twelve others. [Inamullah Samangani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inamullah_Samangani), head of the government's Kandahar Information and Culture Department, reported that the victims were people who had gathered at the branch of New Kabul Bank to collect their monthly salaries, emphasizing that the victims were civilians. According to medical personnel at the [Mirwais Regional Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirwais_Hospital) in Kandahar, about 50 others were injured, but the Taliban disputed these higher casualty numbers and insisted that the situation was "under control." Samangani attempted to minimize the severity of the incident, telling journalists; "There is no such issue, and the wounded people are not in serious condition; they have superficial injuries." Initial investigations by Taliban officials at the [Ministry of Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Interior_Affairs_\(Afghanistan\)) pointed towards ISIS–K as the perpetrator, a claim later reiterated by the group on their Telegram channel.[^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219][^cite_note-220]

On 20 April, a sticky bomb detonated while attached to a car, killing the driver and wounding three others. The attack occurred in the Shia majority Kot-e-Sangi neighbourhood of Kabul. The culprits of the attack are believed to be ISIS–K.[^cite_note-221]

On 29 April, a gunman opened fire on worshippers in a Shia mosque in Guzara district, Herat. Taliban officials said the attack occurred at 9\:00 pm. AFT (UTC+04\:30) and killed six people. ISIS–K claimed responsibility for the shooting the next day.[^cite_note-222]

On 8 May, a motorcycle bombing killed three Taliban security personnel in Faizabad, Badakhshan. ISIS–K claimed responsibility for the attack.[^cite_note-223]

Main article: [2024 Bamyan shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bamyan_shooting)

On 17 May, a group of foreign tourists was [attacked by gunmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bamyan_shooting) at a market in [Bamiyan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamiyan_Province). Three Spaniards and three Afghans were killed, including a Taliban member, and eight were injured. ISIS–K claimed responsibility for the attack.[^cite_note-224]

Main article: [2024 Kabul bus bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Kabul_bus_bombing)

On 11 August a bomb planted on a minibus in Dasht-e Barchi, Kabul killed one person and injured 11. No group claimed responsibility though ISIS–K is suspected to have conducted the attack.[^cite_note-225]

Main article: [2024 Qala Bakhtiar bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Qala_Bakhtiar_bombing)

On 2 September a suicide bomber killed six people outside a government building in Kabul. ISIS–K claimed the attack the next day, citing it as a response to the Taliban reactivating the prison in [Bagram Airfield](197197.md).[^cite_note-226]

Main article: [2024 Afghanistan bus shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Afghanistan_bus_shooting)

On 12 September ISIS-K gunmen opened fire on a group of Shia Hazara civilians in Daykundi returning form pilgrimage in Karbala, killing 14 and injuring six.[^cite_note-227]

On 22 November a suspected ISIS-K gunman killed ten people at a Sufi shrine in Nahrin district, Baghlan.[^cite_note-228]

The Afghan Minister of Refugees and Repatriation, [Khalil Rahman Haqqani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalil_Rahman_Haqqani), died on 11 December 2024, in a suicide attack at his ministry offices in the capital Kabul, a Taliban government source indicated. A spokesman later explained that the Islamic State, a rival group of the Taliban, was responsible for the explosion that killed the official.The Islamic State claimed responsibility for the explosion that killed Khalil ur Rahman Haqqani, head of Refugees and Repatriation, who was in his office.[^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230][^cite_note-231] The de facto government of the Taliban in Afghanistan held the funeral of the Minister of Refugees, Khalilur Rahman Haqqani on 12 December 2024, amid strict security measures. The attack was claimed by the jihadist group Islamic State (IS).[^cite_note-232]

#### 2025

On 11 February five people were killed—including Taliban security forces—and seven others injured during a suicide bombing outside [New Kabul Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kabul_Bank) in [Kunduz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunduz).[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234]

In 2025 the Diplomat reported that the [Islamic State – Khorasan Province](48029263.md) is losing momentum and influence in Afghanistan due crackdown of many ISIS members in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-235]

In August 2025 the Khorasan Diary reported that a key ISIS-K leader called Abdul Malik Afridi was assassinated by [Lashkar-e-Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashkar-e-Islam) (a Taliban affiliated group).

#### 2026

On 19 January 2026 seven people including one Chinese national were killed and twenty others were injured in a bombing at a Chinese restaurant in Shahr-e Naw, Kabul, Afghanistan. The Islamic State – Khorasan Province claimed responsibility.[^cite_note-236][^cite_note-237]

On June 2026 the BBC Monitoring reported that [Islamic State – Khorasan Province](48029263.md) is severely crippled and only a "shadow of its former self.'[^cite_note-\:1-56]

During the [2026 Afghanistan–Pakistan war](82482902.md) the Afghan Taliban claimed to have carried out several drone strikes on [Islamic State – Khorasan Province](48029263.md) hideouts in Pakistan, but this could not be independently verified.[^cite_note-238]
