**Maastricht** ([/ˈmɑːstrɪxt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*MAH-strikht*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English) also [/mɑːˈstrɪxt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*mah-STRIKHT*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key),[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Dutch: [\[maːˈstrɪxt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/19/145_Maastricht.ogg/145_Maastricht.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/1/19/145_Maastricht.ogg/145_Maastricht.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Limburgish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburgish_language): *Mestreech* [\[məˈstʀeːx\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA)) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) and a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_the_Netherlands) in the southeastern [Netherlands](21148.md). It is the [capital](181337.md) and largest city of the province of [Limburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburg_\(Netherlands\)). Maastricht is located on both sides of the [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse) ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Maas*), at the point where the river is joined by the [Jeker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeker). [Mount Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Saint_Peter) (*Sint-Pietersberg*) is largely situated within the city's municipal borders. Maastricht is adjacent to the border with [Belgium](3343.md) and is part of the [Meuse-Rhine Euroregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse-Rhine_Euroregion), an international metropolis with a population of about 3.9 million, which includes the nearby German and Belgian cities of [Aachen](1520.md), [Liège](28341707.md), and [Hasselt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasselt).

Maastricht developed from a [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic) settlement (*Trajectum ad Mosam*) to a medieval river trade and religious centre. In the 16th century it became a garrison town and in the 19th century an early industrial centre.[^cite_note-11] Today, the city is a thriving cultural and regional hub. It became well known through the [Maastricht Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty) and as the birthplace of the [euro](9472.md).[^cite_note-12] Maastricht has 1,677 national heritage buildings (*[rijksmonumenten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijksmonument)*), the second highest number in the Netherlands, after [Amsterdam](844.md). The city is visited by tourists for shopping and recreation, and has a large international student population.

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Maastricht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Maastricht).

### Toponymy

Maastricht is mentioned in ancient documents as *\[Ad] Treiectinsem \[urbem]* ab. 575, *Treiectensis* in 634, *Triecto*, *Triectu* in 7th century, *Triiect* in 768–781, *Traiecto* in 945, *Masetrieth* in 1051.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The place name *Maastricht* is an [Old Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Dutch) compound *Masa-* (> *Maas* "the [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse) river") + Old Dutch *\*treiekt*, itself borrowed from Gallo-Romance \*TRA(I)ECTU cf. its Walloon name *li trek*, from Classical Latin *trajectus* ("[ford](30432825.md), passage, place to cross a river") with the later addition of *Maas* "Meuse" to avoid the confusion with the *-trecht* of [Utrecht](50333.md) having exactly the same original form and etymology. The Latin name first appears in medieval documents and it is not known whether *\**Trajectu(s)** was Maastricht's name during Roman times.

A resident of Maastricht is referred to as *Maastrichtenaar* while in the local dialect it is either *Mestreechteneer* or, colloquially, *[Sjeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sjeng_\(name\))* (derived from the formerly popular French name *Jean*).

### Early history



Roman sanctuary in the basement of Hotel Derlon

[Neanderthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal) remains have been found to the west of Maastricht (Belvédère excavations). Of a later date are [Palaeolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaeolithic) remains, between 8,000 and 25,000 years old. [Celts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) lived here around 500 BC, at a spot where the river [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse) was shallow and therefore easy to cross.

It is not known when the Romans arrived in Maastricht, nor whether the settlement was founded by them. The Romans built a bridge across the Meuse in the 1st century AD, during the reign of [Augustus Caesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augustus_Caesar). The bridge was an important link in the main road between [Bavay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavay) and [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonia_Claudia_Ara_Agrippinensium). Roman Maastricht was relatively small. Remains of the Roman road, the bridge, a religious shrine, a [Roman bath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_bath), a [granary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granary), some houses and the 4th-century [castrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castrum) walls and gates, have been excavated. Fragments of provincial Roman sculptures, as well as coins, jewelry, glass, pottery and other objects from Roman Maastricht are on display in the exhibition space of the city's public library (*Centre Céramique*).

According to legend, the [Armenian](10918072.md)-born [Saint Servatius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Servatius), [Bishop of Tongeren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishopric_of_Tongeren), died in Maastricht in 384 where he was interred along the Roman road, outside the castrum. According to [Gregory of Tours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_of_Tours) it was bishop [Monulph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monulph) who around 570 built the first stone church on the grave of Servatius, a precursor of the present-day [Basilica of Saint Servatius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Servatius). The city remained an early [Christian](5211.md) [diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) until it lost the distinction to nearby [Liège](28341707.md) in the 8th or 9th century.

### Middle Ages

In the early [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) Maastricht, along with [Aachen](1520.md) and [Liège](28341707.md), formed part of what is considered the heartland of the [Carolingian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_dynasty). At this time, the town was an important centre for river trade and manufacturing. [Merovingian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merovingian) coins minted in Maastricht have been found throughout Europe. In 881 the town was plundered by the [Vikings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vikings). In the 10th century it briefly became the capital of the [duchy of Lower Lorraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Lower_Lorraine).

During the 11th and 12th centuries the town flourished culturally. Several [provosts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provost_\(religion\)) of the chapter of Saint Servatius held important positions in the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). The two [collegiate churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_church) were largely rebuilt and redecorated during this era. Maastricht [Romanesque stone sculpture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_art#Sculpture) and [silversmithing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silversmithing) are regarded as highlights of [Mosan art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosan_art). Maastricht painters were praised by [Wolfram von Eschenbach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfram_von_Eschenbach) in his [Parzival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parzival). Around the same time, the poet [Henric van Veldeke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henric_van_Veldeke) wrote a legend of Saint Servatius, one of the earliest works in [Dutch literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_literature). The two main churches acquired a wealth of [relics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relic) and the septennial [Maastricht Pilgrimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Pilgrimage) became a major event that drew up to 100,000 pilgrims.

Unlike most Dutch towns, Maastricht did not receive [city rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_rights_in_the_Netherlands) at a certain date. These gradually developed during its long history. In 1204 the city's [dual authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condominium_\(international_law\)) was formalised in a treaty, with the [prince-bishop of Liège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-bishop_of_Li%C3%A8ge) and the [duke of Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Brabant) holding joint sovereignty over the city. Soon afterwards the first ring of medieval walls were built. In 1275, the old Roman bridge collapsed under the weight of a procession, allegedly killing 400 people. A replacement bridge, funded by church [indulgences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indulgence), was built slightly to the north and survives until today, the [Sint Servaasbrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sint_Servaasbrug).[^cite_note-15]

Throughout the Middle Ages, the city remained a centre for trade and manufacturing principally of [wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool) and [leather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leather) but gradually economic decline set in. After a brief period of economic prosperity around 1500, the city's economy suffered during the [wars of religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_wars_of_religion) of the 16th and 17th centuries, and recovery did not happen until the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution) in the early 19th century.

### 16th to 18th centuries



The [Siege of Maastricht (1579)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Maastricht_\(1579\)) as depicted in the [Palace of Aranjuez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Aranjuez)

The strategic location of Maastricht at a major river crossing necessitated the construction of an array of fortifications around the city during this period. The Spanish and Dutch [garrisons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrison) became an important factor in the city's economy. In 1579 the city was sacked by the Spanish army led by the [Duke of Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Farnese,_Duke_of_Parma) ([Siege of Maastricht, 1579](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Maastricht_\(1579\))). For over fifty years the Spanish crown took over the role previously held by the dukes of Brabant in the joint sovereignty over Maastricht. In 1632 [the city was conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Maastricht) by Prince [Frederick Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Henry,_Prince_of_Orange) of [Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Orange) and the Dutch [States General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_States-General) replaced the Spanish crown in the joint government of Maastricht. There was an attempt in 1634 of Spanish forces to [recapture the city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Maastricht_\(1634\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), but to no avail.

Another [Siege of Maastricht (1673)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Maastricht_\(1673\)) took place during the [Franco-Dutch War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Dutch_War). In June 1673, [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV#Early_wars_in_the_Low_Countries) laid siege to the city because French supply lines were being threatened. During this siege, [Vauban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vauban), the famous French military engineer, developed a new tactic in order to break down the strong fortifications surrounding Maastricht. His systematic approach remained the standard method of attacking fortresses until the 20th century. On 25 June 1673, while preparing to storm the city, captain-lieutenant [Charles de Batz de Castelmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Batz_de_Castelmore), also known as the *comte [d'Artagnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Artagnan)*, was killed by a musket shot outside the Tongerse Poort. This event was embellished in the [Alexandre Dumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Dumas) novel *[The Vicomte de Bragelonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vicomte_de_Bragelonne)*, part of the [D'Artagnan Romances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Artagnan_Romances). French troops occupied Maastricht from 1673 to 1678.

In 1748 the French again conquered the city at what is known as the [Second French Siege of Maastricht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Maastricht_\(1748\)), during the [War of Austrian Succession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Austrian_Succession). After each siege the city's fortifications were restored and expanded. The French revolutionary army failed to take the city in 1793 but a year later they succeeded. The condominium was dissolved and Maastricht was annexed to the [French First Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_First_Republic), later the [First French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire). For almost twenty years (1795–1814/15) Maastricht was the capital of the French [département](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9partement_in_France#Napoleonic_Empire) of [Meuse-Inférieure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse-Inf%C3%A9rieure).

### 19th and early 20th century



19th-century industry: Maastricht potteries in Boschstraat

After the [Napoleonic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_era), Maastricht became part of the [United Kingdom of the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_the_Netherlands) in 1815. It was made the capital of the newly formed [Province of Limburg (1815–1839)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Limburg_\(1815%E2%80%931839\)). When the southern provinces of the newly formed kingdom [seceded in 1830](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgian_Revolution), the Dutch garrison in Maastricht remained loyal to the Dutch king, [William I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_I_of_the_Netherlands), even when most of the inhabitants of the town and the surrounding area sided with the Belgian revolutionaries. In 1831, arbitration by the [Great Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Powers) allocated the city to the Netherlands. However, neither the Dutch nor the Belgians agreed to this and the arrangement was not implemented until the 1839 [Treaty of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_London,_1839). During this period of isolation Maastricht developed into an early industrial town.



Plate commemorating the liberation, 14 September 1944

Because of its eccentric location in the southeastern Netherlands, as well as its geographical and cultural proximity to Belgium and Germany, integration of Maastricht and Limburg into the Netherlands did not come about easily. Maastricht retained a distinctly non-Dutch appearance during much of the 19th century and it was not until the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War) that the city was forced to look northwards.

Like the rest of the Netherlands, Maastricht remained neutral during World War I. However, being wedged between Germany and Belgium, it received large numbers of refugees, putting a strain on the city's resources. Early in [World War II](32927.md), the city was taken by the [Germans](21212.md) by surprise during the [Battle of Maastricht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maastricht) of May 1940. On 13 and 14 September 1944 it was the first Dutch city to be liberated by [Allied forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) of the US [Old Hickory Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)). The three Meuse bridges were destroyed or severely damaged during the war. As elsewhere in the Netherlands, the majority of Maastricht [Jews](25955086.md) died in [Nazi concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps).[^cite_note-16]

### After World War II



Prime minister [Dries van Agt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dries_van_Agt) presiding over the 1981 [European Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) in the town hall

During the latter half of the century, traditional industries (such as Maastricht's [potteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery)) declined and the city's economy shifted to a [service economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_economy). [Maastricht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_University) was founded in 1976. Several European institutions found their base in Maastricht. In 1981 and 1991 [European Councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council) were held in Maastricht, the latter one resulting a year later in the signing of the [Maastricht Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Treaty), leading to the creation of the [European Union](9317.md) and the [euro](9472.md).[^cite_note-17] Since 1988, [The European Fine Art Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_European_Fine_Art_Fair), regarded as the world's leading art fair, annually draws in some of the wealthiest art collectors.

Since the 1990s, large parts of the city have been refurbished, including the areas around the main railway station and the Maasboulevard [promenade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promenade) along the Meuse, the Entre Deux and Mosae Forum shopping centres, as well as some of the main shopping streets. A prestigious quarter designed by international architects and including the new [Bonnefanten Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnefanten_Museum), a public library, and a theatre was built on the grounds of the former Société Céramique factory near the town centre. Further large-scale projects, such as the redevelopment of the area around the [A2 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A2_motorway_\(Netherlands\)), the Sphinx Quarter and the Belvédère area are under construction.

In the early 2000s, Maastricht launched several campaigns against drug-dealing in an attempt to stop foreign buyers taking advantage of the liberal Dutch legislation and causing trouble in the downtown area.[^cite_note-18]

## Geography

### Neighbourhoods



Typical street in the [Jekerkwartier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jekerkwartier), part of the city centre



Dutch topographic map of Maastricht, March 2014

4. North-West (093503): [Boschpoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boschpoort), Bosscherveld, Frontenkwartier, Belvédère, Lanakerveld

Maastricht's [city limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_limits) have an international border with Belgium. ### Climate

Maastricht features the same climate as most of the Netherlands (*Cfb*, [Oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate)), however, due to its more inland location in between hills, summers tend to be warmer (especially in the Meuse valley, which lies 70 m \[230 ft] lower than the meteorological station) and winters a bit colder. The highest temperature recorded was on 25 July 2019 at 39.6 °C (103.3 °F).

### Languages

Maastricht is a city of linguistic diversity, partly as a result of its location at the crossroads of multiple language areas and its international student population.

- [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) is the national language and the language of elementary and secondary education (excluding international institutions) as well as administration. Dutch in Maastricht is often spoken with a distinctive Limburgish [accent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accent_\(dialect\)), which should not be confused with the Limburgish language.

- [Limburgish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburgish) (or *Limburgian*) is the overlapping term of the [tonal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonal_languages) dialects spoken in [the Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburg_\(Netherlands\)) and [the Belgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburg_\(Belgium\)) provinces of Limburg. The [Maastrichtian dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastrichtian_dialect) (*Mestreechs*) is only one of many variants of Limburgish. It is characterised by stretched [vowels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vowel) and some French influence on its vocabulary. In recent years the Maastricht dialect has been in decline (see [dialect levelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialect_levelling)) and a language switch to Standard Dutch has been noted.

  [^cite_note-Dialect_of_Maastricht-25]

- [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) used to be the language of education and culture in Maastricht. In the late 18th century the language gained a powerful position as the judicial and administrative language, and throughout the following century it was the preferred language of the upper classes. Between 1851 and 1892 a Francophone newspaper (*Le Courrier de la Meuse*) was published in Maastricht.

  [^cite_note-Kessels-van_der_Heijde-26]

  &#x20;The language is often part of secondary school curricula. Many proper names are French and the language has left many traces in the local dialect.

- [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language), like French, is often part of secondary school curricula. Due to Maastricht's geographic proximity to Germany and the great number of German students in the city, German is widely spoken.

- [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) has become an important language in education. At [Maastricht University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_University) and [Hogeschool Zuyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogeschool_Zuyd) it is the language of instruction for many courses. Many foreign students and [expatriates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expatriates) use English as a [lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca). English is also a mandatory subject in Dutch secondary schools.

### Sights of Maastricht

Maastricht is known in the Netherlands and beyond for its lively squares, narrow streets, and historic buildings. The city has 1,677 national heritage buildings (*[rijksmonumenten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijksmonument)*), more than any Dutch city outside Amsterdam. In addition to that there are 3,500 locally listed buildings (*gemeentelijke monumenten*). The entire city centre is a conservation area (*beschermd stadsgezicht*) and largely traffic-free. The tourist information office ([VVV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_the_Netherlands)) is located in the basement of Dinghuis, a late-medieval courthouse overlooking Grote Staat. Maastricht's main sights include:

- **[Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse) ([Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Maas*) river**, with several parks and promenades along the river, and some interesting bridges:&#x20;

  - [Sint Servaasbrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sint_Servaasbrug), partly from the 13th century; the oldest bridge in the Netherlands;

  - [Hoge Brug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoge_Brug) ("High Bridge"), a modern pedestrian bridge designed by René Greisch.

- **City fortifications**, including:&#x20;

  - Remnants of the first and second medieval [city wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_wall) and several towers (13th and 14th centuries);

  - Helpoort ("Hell's Gate"), an imposing gate with two towers, built around 1230, the oldest city gate in the Netherlands;

  - Wycker Waterpoort, a medieval gate in Wyck, used for accessing the city from the Meuse, demolished in the 19th century but rebuilt shortly afterwards;

  - Hoge Fronten (or: Linie van Du Moulin), remnants of 17th and 18th-century [fortifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications), including a number of well-preserved [bastions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastions), [couvrefaces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couvreface), [lunettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunette_\(fortification\)) and dry [moats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moat);

  - Fort Sint-Pieter, an early 18th-century [fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress) on the flanks of [Mount Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Saint_Peter), offering guided tours and panoramic views of the city; and Fort Willem I, an early 19th-century fortress on the Caberg elevation;

  - [Casemates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casemate), an underground network of tunnels, built as sheltered emplacements for guns and cannons. These connected tunnels built of brick and limestone run for around fourteen kilometres underneath the city's fortifications. Guided tours are available.

- **Binnenstad**: inner-city pedestrianized district with popular shopping streets Grote and Kleine Staat, high-end shopping streets Stokstraat and Maastrichter Smedenstraat, and two indoor shopping centres. Several main sights in Maastricht as well as a large number of cafés, pubs and restaurants are centred around the three main squares in Binnenstad:&#x20;

  - [Vrijthof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vrijthof), the largest and possibly best-known square in Maastricht, with many well-known pubs and restaurants. Other sights include:&#x20;

    - [Basilica of Saint Servatius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint_Servatius), a predominantly Romanesque church with an imposing [westwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westwork) and important 12th and 13th-century sculptures; most notably the westwork interior figurative [capitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(architecture\)), the westwork [reredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reredo), and the sculpted South Portal. The tomb of [Saint Servatius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Servatius) in the crypt is a favoured place of [pilgrimage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_pilgrimage). The church has an important [church treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_of_the_Basilica_of_Saint_Servatius);

    - [Sint-Janskerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_John_Church_\(Maastricht\)), a Gothic church dedicated to [Saint John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_John_the_Baptist), the city's main Protestant church since 1632, adjacent to the Basilica of Saint Servatius, with a distinctive limestone tower painted red;

    - Spaans Gouvernement ("Spanish Government Building"), a 16th-century former canon's house, later used as a residence for the [Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Brabant) and [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Netherlands) rulers, now housing the [Fotomuseum aan het Vrijthof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fotomuseum_aan_het_Vrijthof);

    - Hoofdwacht, an 18th-century military guard house, built in the style of the [Dutch Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Baroque), used for exhibitions;

    - Generaalshuis ("General's House"), a [Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) mansion, now the city's main theater (Theater aan het Vrijthof).

  - [Onze Lieve Vrouweplein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onze_Lieve_Vrouweplein), a tree-lined square with a number of pavement cafes. Main sights:&#x20;

    - [Basilica of Our Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady,_Maastricht), a partly 11th-century church, one of the Netherlands' most significant Romanesque buildings with an imposing Mosan westwork and an important [church treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_treasury). Perhaps best known for the shrine of [Our Lady, Star of the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Our_Lady,_Star_of_the_Sea) in an adjacent Gothic chapel;

    - Derlon Museumkelder, a permanent exhibition of ancient Roman remains in the basement of Hotel Derlon.

  - Markt, the town's historic market square. Sights include:&#x20;

    - The [Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_City_Hall), built in the 17th century by [Pieter Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_Post) and considered one of the highlights of [Dutch Baroque architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Baroque_architecture). Nearby is Dinghuis, the late medieval town hall and courthouse with an early [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_in_the_Low_Countries) façade;

    - Mosae Forum, a shopping centre and civic building designed by [Jo Coenen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo_Coenen) and [Bruno Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Albert?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the early 2000s. Inside the Mosae Forum parking garage there is a small exhibition of [Citroën](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn) miniature cars;

    - Entre Deux, a rebuilt shopping centre in [Postmodern style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodern_architecture), which has won several international awards.

      [^cite_note-30]

      &#x20;It includes a bookstore located inside a former 13th-century Dominican church. In 2008, British newspaper *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* proclaimed this the world's most beautiful bookshop.

      [^cite_note-31]

- **[Jekerkwartier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jekerkwartier)**, a neighbourhood named after the small river [Jeker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeker), which pops up between old houses and remnants of city walls. The western part of the neighbourhood (named the Maastricht [Latin Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Quarter)) is dominated by university buildings and (performing) arts schools. Sights include:&#x20;

  - several churches and monasteries: the 13th-century First Franciscan Monastery, the 17th-century "Veiled Sisters" and Bonnefanten monasteries, and the 18th-century Second Franciscan Monastery and Walloon and Lutheran churches;

  - [Maastricht Natural History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Natural_History_Museum), a small museum of natural history in a former monastery;

  - Grote Looiersstraat ("Great Tanners' Street"), a former canal that was filled in during the 19th century, lined with elegant houses, the city's [poorhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poorhouse) (now part of the university library) and Sint-Maartenshofje, a typically Dutch [hofje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofje).

- **Kommelkwartier**, **Statenkwartier** and **[Boschstraatkwartier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boschstraatkwartier)**, three relatively quiet inner city neighbourhoods with several monasteries, university buildings and industrial heritage building:&#x20;

  - [Crosier Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crosier_Monastery,_Maastricht) in Kommelkwartier, a well-preserved Gothic monastery, now a five-star hotel;

  - Sint-Matthiaskerk, a 14th-century parish church dedicated to [Saint Matthew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Matthew);

  - Sphinx Quarter, an upcoming neighbourhood and cultural hotspot in the north of the city centre. Several of the industrial buildings of the former Sphinx glass, crystal and ceramics factories have been transformed for new uses;

  - Bassin, a restored early 19th-century inner harbor surrounded by industrial heritage buildings, re-used as cultural venues, bars and restaurants.

- **[Wyck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wyck,_Maastricht)**, the old quarter on the right bank of the river Meuse.&#x20;

  - Saint Martin's Church, a [Gothic Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival) church designed by [Pierre Cuypers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Cuypers) in 1856;

  - Rechtstraat and Hoogbrugstraat are the oldest streets in Wyck with many historic buildings and a mix of specialty shops, art galleries and restaurants;

  - Stationsstraat and Wycker Brugstraat are elegant streets with the majority of the buildings dating from the late 19th century. At the east end of Stationsstraat stands the [Maastricht railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_railway_station) from 1913.

- **Céramique**, a modern neighbourhood on the site of the former Société Céramique potteries, including a park along the river Meuse (Charles Eyckpark) and a showcase of architectural highlights:&#x20;

  - Wiebengahal, one of the few remaining industrial buildings in the neighbourhood and an early example of [modernist architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernist_architecture) in the Netherlands, dating from 1912;

  - [Bonnefanten Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnefanten_Museum) by [Aldo Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_Rossi), featuring a landmark rocket-shaped tower;

  - Centre Céramique, a public library and exhibition space by [Jo Coenen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo_Coenen);

  - La Fortezza, a red brick office and apartment building by [Mario Botta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Botta);

  - Siza Tower, a residential tower clad with zinc and white marble, by [Álvaro Siza Vieira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Siza_Vieira);

  - Other buildings in Céramique by [MBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBM_\(architecture_firm\)), [Cruz y Ortiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruz_y_Ortiz), [Luigi Snozzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Snozzi), [Aurelio Galfetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurelio_Galfetti), [Herman Hertzberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herman_Hertzberger), [Wiel Arets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiel_Arets), [Hubert-Jan Henket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubert-Jan_Henket), [Charles Vandenhove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Vandenhove) and [Bob Van Reeth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Van_Reeth).

- **[Sint-Pietersberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sint-Pietersberg)** ("Mount Saint Peter"): modest hill and nature reserve south of the city, peaking at 171 metres (561 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level). It serves as Maastricht's main recreation area and a viewing point. The main sights include:&#x20;

  - Fort Sint-Pieter, an early 18th-century military fortress fully restored in recent years;

  - [Caves of Maastricht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caves_of_Maastricht) aka *Grotten Sint-Pietersberg*, an underground network of man-made tunnels ("caves") in limestone quarries. Guided tours are available;

  - [ENCI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ENCI) Quarry: a former quarry and nature reserve with several lakes, accessible via a spectacular staircase with viewing platforms;

  - Slavante, a 19th-century former gentlemen's club on the site of a Franciscan monastery (of which parts are still standing), now a popular hang-out, offering panoramic views over the Meuse valley;

  - Lichtenberg, a ruined medieval castle keep and an adjacent 18th-century farmstead;

  - D'n Observant ("The Observer"), an artificial hilltop, made with the spoils of a nearby quarry, now a nature reserve.

### Museums in Maastricht

- [Bonnefanten Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnefanten_Museum) is the foremost museum for old masters and contemporary fine art in the province of Limburg. The collection features medieval sculpture ([The Virgin and Child with St. Anne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Virgin_and_Child_with_St._Anne_\(van_Steffeswert\))), early Italian painting ([Giovanni del Biondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_del_Biondo), [Domenico di Michelino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenico_di_Michelino), [Jacopo del Casentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacopo_del_Casentino), [Sano di Pietro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sano_di_Pietro), [Pietro Nelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Nelli)), [Southern Netherlandish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Netherlandish) and German Renaissance painting ([Colijn de Coter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colijn_de_Coter), [Roelandt Savery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roelandt_Savery), [Pieter Coecke van Aelst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_Coecke_van_Aelst), [Pieter Brueghel the Younger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pieter_Brueghel_the_Younger), [Lucas Cranach the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Cranach_the_Elder)), and contemporary art ([Sol LeWitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sol_LeWitt), [Robert Mangold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Mangold), [Richard Serra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Serra), [Luciano Fabro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Fabro), [Marcel Broodthaers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Broodthaers), [Joseph Beuys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Beuys), [Neo Rauch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo_Rauch), [Gilbert and George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_and_George), [Peter Doig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Doig), [Gary Hume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Hume), [Grayson Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grayson_Perry), [Luc Tuymans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luc_Tuymans), [Ai Weiwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ai_Weiwei)).

- The [Treasury of the Basilica of Saint Servatius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treasury_of_the_Basilica_of_Saint_Servatius) includes religious artifacts from the 4th to 20th centuries, notably those related to [Saint Servatius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Servatius). Highlights include the [shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrine_of_Saint_Servatius), the key and the [crosier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crosier) of Saint Servatius, and the reliquary bust donated by [Alexander Farnese, Duke of Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Farnese,_Duke_of_Parma).

- The [Treasury of the Basilica of Our Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Our_Lady,_Maastricht#Treasury_Basilica_of_Our_Lady) contains religious art, textiles, reliquaries, liturgical vessels and other artifacts from the Middle Ages and later periods.

- Derlon Museumkelder is a preserved archeological site in the basement of a hotel with Roman and pre-Roman remains.

- The [Maastricht Natural History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Natural_History_Museum) exhibits collections relating to the [geology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geology), [paleontology](23084.md) and [flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora) and [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna) of [Limburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburg_\(Netherlands\)). Highlights in the collection are several fragment of skeletons of [Mosasaurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosasaur) found in a quarry in [Mount Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Saint_Peter).

- [Fotomuseum aan het Vrijthof](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fotomuseum_aan_het_Vrijthof) is a local museum of photography housed in the 16th-century Spanish Government building, featuring some period rooms and temporary exhibitions of photographers.

### Parks

There are several city parks and recreational areas in Maastricht:[^cite_note-32]

- Stadspark, the main public park in Maastricht, partly 19th-century, with remnants of the medieval city walls, a branch of the [Jeker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeker) river, a mini-zoo and several public sculptures (e.g. the statue of [d'Artagnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Artagnan) in Aldenhofpark, a 20th-century extension of Stadspark). Other extensions of the park are called Kempland, Henri Hermanspark, Monseigneur Nolenspark and Waldeckpark. From 2014 onwards, the grounds of the former Tapijn military barracks will be gradually added to the park;

- Jekerpark, a new park along the river Jeker, separated from Stadspark by a busy road;

- Frontenpark, a new park west of the city centre, incorporating parts of the [fortifications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications) of Maastricht from the 17th to 19th centuries;

- Charles Eykpark, a modern park between the public library and [Bonnefanten Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonnefanten_Museum) on the east bank of the Meuse river, designed in the late 1990s by Swedish landscape architect [Gunnar Martinsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunnar_Martinsson).

- Griendpark, a modern park on the east bank of the river with an inline-skating and skateboarding course.

- Geusseltpark in eastern Maastricht and J.J. van de Vennepark in western Maastricht, both with elaborate sports facilities.

### Natural areas

- The Meuse river and its green banks in outlying areas. In the northern areas around [Itteren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itteren) and [Borgharen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borgharen) 'new nature' is being created in combination with river protection measures and gravel mining.

  [^cite_note-33]

- Pietersplas, an artificial lake between Maastricht and [Gronsveld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gronsveld) that was the result of gravel pits on the banks of the Meuse river. There is a beach on the northern slope of the lake and a [marina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina) near Castle Hoogenweerth. The eastern riverbed between Pietersplas and the provincial government building is a nature reserve (Kleine Weerd).

- The Jeker Valley, along the river [Jeker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeker), starts near the city centre in Stadspark and leads via Jekerpark to an area with green meadows, fertile fields, some vineyards on the slopes of Cannerberg, several water mills and [Château Neercanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%A2teau_Neercanne), and continues further south into Belgium.

- The green flanks of [Mount Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Saint_Peter), including many footpaths.

  [^cite_note-34]

- Dousberg and Zouwdal, a modest hill and valley surrounded by urban development on the western edge of the city, partly in Belgium. A large part of the hill is now in use as an international golf course (Golfclub Maastricht).

  [^cite_note-35]

- Landgoederenzone, an extended area in the northeast of Maastricht (partly in [Meerssen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meerssen)) consisting of around fifteen country estates, such as Severen, Geusselt, Bethlehem, Mariënwaard, Kruisdonk, Vaeshartelt, Meerssenhoven, [Borgharen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borgharen) and Hartelstein. Some of the castles, villas and stately homes are surrounded by industrial areas or quarries.

- Bike paths through agricultural areas in several outlying quarters (like "Biesland" and "Wolder").

The municipal government of Maastricht consists of a [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_council), a [mayor](101146.md) and a number of [aldermen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldermen). The city council, a 39-member legislative body directly [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Dutch_municipal_elections) every four years, appoints the aldermen on the basis of a coalition agreement between two or more parties after each election.

### Aldermen and mayors

The mayor and aldermen make up the executive branch of the municipal government.

The mayor from 2002, [Gerd Leers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerd_Leers) ([CDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Appeal)), resigned in January 2010 following allegations of irregularities in a holiday villa project in Bulgaria owned by Leers.[^cite_note-39] He was replaced by [Onno Hoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onno_Hoes), a Liberal ([VVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VVD)), the only male mayor in the country officially married to a man. In 2013 Hoes was subject to controversy after disclosures of intimate affairs with several other men, although he remained mayor.[^cite_note-40] After a new affair in 2014, Hoes eventually stepped down.[^cite_note-41]

From July 2015, [Annemarie Penn-te Strake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annemarie_Penn-te_Strake) became mayor.[^cite_note-42] She was an independent serving no political party, although her husband was a former chairman[^cite_note-1lim_Olaf-43] of the Maastricht Seniorenpartij.[^cite_note-44] She had served in the Dutch judicial system for many years, and during her tenure as mayor she still served as attorney general.[^cite_note-45] In July 2023 Wim Hillenaar [(CDA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Appeal) took over as mayor.[^cite_note-46]

### Cannabis

One controversial issue which dominated Maastricht politics for many years was the city's approach to soft drugs. Under the [Dutch soft drug policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_policy_of_the_Netherlands), individuals may buy [cannabis](1481886.md) from ['coffeeshops'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_coffee_shop) under certain conditions. From the 1980s, Maastricht saw a growing influx of '[drug tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drug_tourism)', mainly from neighbouring Belgium, France and Germany. The city government attempted to reduce negative side effects, including illegal sale of hard drugs in the city centre and anti-social behaviour.



Two ['coffeeshop'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannabis_coffee_shop) boats at Maasboulevard

A 2008 proposal to relocate the coffeeshops to the outskirts of the city[^cite_note-47] was opposed by neighbouring municipalities (some in Belgium) and by the Dutch and Belgian parliaments. In December 2010, a Maastricht law to restrict entry to coffeeshops to local residents was upheld by the [Court of Justice of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Justice_of_the_European_Union),[^cite_note-48] with the Dutch government introducing a similar national law in 2012.[^cite_note-49] The new system led to a reduction in drug tourism in Maastricht's cannabis shops, but an increase in drug dealing on the streets.[^cite_note-50] A 2018 Maastricht University study showed a substantial decline in drug nuisance since 2012, although criminal drug networks had grown due to police budget cuts.[^cite_note-51]

### By bike

Maastricht is bikeable. People biking are able to cross the river using every bridge in the city.

### By car

Maastricht is served by the [A2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A2_motorway_\(Netherlands\)) and [A79](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A79_motorway_\(Netherlands\)) motorways. The city can be reached from [Brussels](3708.md) and [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) in approximately one hour and from [Amsterdam](844.md) in about two and a half hours.

The A2 motorway runs through Maastricht in a double-decked tunnel. Before 2016, the A2 motorway ran through the city; heavily congested, it caused air pollution in the urban area. Construction of a two-level tunnel designed to solve these problems started in 2011 and was opened (in stages) by December 2016.[^cite_note-52]

In spite of several large underground car parks, parking in the city centre forms a major problem during weekends and bank holidays because of the large numbers of visitors. Parking fees are deliberately high to encourage visitors to use public transport or [park and ride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Park_and_ride) facilities away from the centre.

### By train

Maastricht is served by three rail operators, all of which call at the main [Maastricht railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_railway_station) near the centre and two of which call at the smaller [Maastricht Randwyck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Randwyck_railway_station), near the business and university district. Only Arriva also calls at [Maastricht Noord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Noord_railway_station), which opened in 2013. Intercity trains northwards to [Amsterdam](844.md), [Eindhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eindhoven), [Den Bosch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Den_Bosch) and [Utrecht](50333.md) are operated by [Dutch Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Railways). The line to [Heerlen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heerlen), [Valkenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkenburg_aan_de_Geul) and [Kerkrade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerkrade) is operated by [Arriva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arriva). The [National Railway Company of Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Railway_Company_of_Belgium) runs south to [Liège](28341707.md) in [Belgium](3343.md). The westbound railway to [Hasselt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasselt) (Belgium) closed in 1954. The former railway to [Aachen](1520.md) was closed down in the 1980s. However, Aachen can still be reached via Heerlen.

### By bus

Regular bus lines connect the city centre, outer areas, business districts and railway stations. The regional [Arriva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arriva#Netherlands) bus network extends to most parts of [South Limburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Limburg) and [Aachen](1520.md) (Germany). Regional buses by [De Lijn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Lijn) connect Maastricht with [Hasselt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasselt), [Tongeren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongeren) and [Maasmechelen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasmechelen), and one bus connects Maastricht with [Liège](28341707.md), operated by [TEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_R%C3%A9gionale_Wallonne_du_Transport). [FlixBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FlixBus) provides [intercity bus services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_bus_service) from Maastricht to many European destinations.

### By air

Maastricht is served by the nearby [Maastricht Aachen Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastricht_Aachen_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **MST**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **EHBK**), in nearby [Beek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beek), and it is informally referred to by that name. The airport is located about 10 kilometres (6 miles) north of the city centre. The airport is primarily served by low-cost airline [Wizz Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizz_Air).[^cite_note-53] The nearest airports are both located in nearby Germany which are [Düsseldorf Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf_Airport), located 112 km (70 mi) north east and [Cologne Bonn Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne_Bonn_Airport), located 117 km (73 mi) east of Maastricht. However, residents in the city also use [Amsterdam Schiphol Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amsterdam_Airport_Schiphol) as an international hub. The airport is located 217 km (135 mi) north west of the city. Maastricht has an [area control centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_control_centre) for air traffic 24,500 feet (7,500 m) in altitude and above over [Benelux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benelux) and northwest Germany.[^cite_note-54]

### By boat

Maastricht has a river [port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port) (*Beatrixhaven*) and is connected by water with [Belgium](3343.md) and the rest of the Netherlands through the river [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse), the [Juliana Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juliana_Canal), the [Albert Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Canal) and the [Zuid-Willemsvaart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuid-Willemsvaart). Although there are no regular boat connections to other cities, various organized boat trips for tourists connect Maastricht with Belgium cities such as [Liège](28341707.md).

## Local anthem

In 2002 the municipal government officially adopted a local anthem ([Limburgish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburgish) ([Maastrichtian variant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastrichtian_dialect)): *Mestreechs Volksleed*, [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language): *Maastrichts Volkslied*) composed of lyrics in [Maastrichtian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastrichtian_dialect), written by [Alfons Olterdissen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfons_Olterdissen). The theme was originally composed by his brother, Guus Olterdissen, though the theme is similar to the theme "[Pe al nostru steag e scris Unire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pe-al_nostru_steag_e_scris_Unire)" of the Romanian composer [Ciprian Porumbescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciprian_Porumbescu) (1853–1883). It is unknown if the Olterdissen brothers were aware of the Romanian piece.[^cite_note-Municipality_of_Maastricht-55]
