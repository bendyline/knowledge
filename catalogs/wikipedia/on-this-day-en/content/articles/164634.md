**Pune** ([Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language): *Puṇē*, pronounced [\[ˈpuɳeː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Marathi) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f6/Pune_pronunication.ogg/Pune_pronunication.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/f/f6/Pune_pronunication.ogg/Pune_pronunication.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), previously spelled in English as **Poona** ([the official name until 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_renamed_Indian_cities_and_states#Maharashtra)),[^cite_note-timesofindia-30Sep16-17][^cite_note-Chakravorty-18] is a city in the state of [Maharashtra](20629.md) in the [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau) in [Western India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_India). It is the administrative headquarters of the [Pune district](3123898.md), and of [Pune division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_division). In terms of the total amount of land under its jurisdiction, Pune is the largest city in Maharashtra by area, with a geographical area of 516.18km<sup>2</sup>,[^cite_note-19] though [by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_India_by_population) it comes in a distant second to [Mumbai](19189.md). The city of Pune is part of [Pune Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Metropolitan_Region), which according to the [2011 Census of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India), has 7.2 million residents, making it the [seventh-most populous metropolitan area in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_metropolitan_areas_in_India).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-Cities1Lakhandabove-21] Pune is one of the largest [IT hubs in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IT_hubs_in_India).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] It is also one of the most important [automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_India) and [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_India) hubs of India. Pune is often referred to as the "[Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxford) of the East" because of [its educational institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_educational_institutions_in_Pune).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] It has been ranked "the most liveable city in India" several times.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

Pune at different points in time has been ruled by the [Rashtrakuta dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_dynasty), [Ahmadnagar Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadnagar_Sultanate), the [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughals), and the [Adil Shahi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adil_Shahi_dynasty). In the 18th century, the city was part of the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy), and the seat of the [Peshwas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa), the prime ministers of the Maratha Empire.[^cite_note-29] Pune was seized by the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) in the [Third Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Maratha_War); it gained [municipal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Municipal_Corporation) status in 1858, the year in which [Crown rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) began. Many historical landmarks like [Shaniwarwada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaniwar_Wada), [Shinde Chhatri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinde_Chhatri), and [Vishrambaug Wada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishrambaug_Wada) date to this era. Historical sites from different eras dot the city.

Pune has historically been a major cultural centre, with important figures like [Dnyaneshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnyaneshwar), [Shivaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivaji), [Tukaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukaram), [Baji Rao I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baji_Rao_I), [Balaji Baji Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaji_Baji_Rao), [Madhavrao I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhavrao_I), [Nana Fadnavis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nana_Fadnavis), [Mahadev Govind Ranade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahadev_Govind_Ranade), [Gopal Krishna Gokhale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopal_Krishna_Gokhale), [Mahatma Jyotirao Phule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Jyotirao_Phule), [Savitribai Phule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savitribai_Phule), [Gopal Ganesh Agarkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopal_Ganesh_Agarkar), [Tarabai Shinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarabai_Shinde), [Dhondo Keshav Karve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhondo_Keshav_Karve), and [Pandita Ramabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandita_Ramabai) doing their life's work in Pune City or in an area that falls in [Pune Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Metropolitan_Region). Pune was a major centre of resistance to the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj), with people like [Gopal Krishna Gokhale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopal_Krishna_Gokhale) and [Bal Gangadhar Tilak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal_Gangadhar_Tilak) playing leading roles in the [struggle for Indian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement).&#x20;

## Etymology

Further information: [Puneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puneri)

The earliest reference to Pune is an inscription on a [Rashtrakuta Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_Dynasty) copper plate dated 937 CE, which refers to the town as Punya-[vishaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishaya).[^cite_note-30] By the 13th century, it had come to be known as Punawadi.[^cite_note-31]

During the Rashtrakuta dynasty, the city was referred to as Punnaka and Punyapur. The copper plates of 758 and 768 CE show that the [Yadava dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seuna_\(Yadava\)_dynasty) had renamed the city as Punakavishaya and Punya Vishaya. 'Vishaya' means land, while 'Punaka' and 'Punya' mean holy. The city was known as Kasbe Pune during the reign of the Maratha king, [Shivaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivaji)'s father, [Shahaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahaji). Mughal emperor [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb) renamed a small neighbourhood in central part of the old city as Muhiyabad (the only divergent naming) some time between 1703 and 1705 in memory of his great-grandson Muhi-ul-Milan, who died there. The name Muhiyabad was reverted soon after Aurangzeb's death.[^cite_note-timesofindia-30Sep16-17] Anglicized to Poona in 1857 by the English during British rule, the city's name was changed to Pune in 1978.

## History

Main article: [History of Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pune)

### Early and medieval period



The circular Nandi *[mandapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandapa)* at the [Pataleshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pataleshwar) cave temple built in the [Rashtrakuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta) era (753–982)

Copper plates dated 858 and 868 CE show that by the 9th century an agricultural settlement known as Punnaka existed at the location of the modern Pune. The plates indicate that this region was ruled by the [Rashtrakuta dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta_dynasty). The [Pataleshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pataleshwar) rock-cut temple complex was built during this era.[^cite_note-32] Pune was part of the territory ruled by the [Seuna Yadavas of Devagiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seuna_Yadavas_of_Devagiri) from the 9th century to 1327. Pune was under control of various [Muslim](19541.md) sultanates until the late 1600s.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-Kantak_489-495-34]

### The Maratha Empire

Main articles: [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) and [Battle of Poona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poona)

Pune was part of the [Jagir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagir) (fiefdom) granted by the [Nizamshahi (Ahmadnagar Sultanate)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizamshahi) to [Maloji Bhosale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maloji_Bhosale) in 1599.[^cite_note-Sardesai1957-35] [Maloji Bhosale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maloji_Bhosale) passed on [Jagir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagir) of Pune to his son [Shahaji Bhosale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahaji_Bhosale). The town was destroyed by Murar Jagdeo, a general from the rival [Adil Shahi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adil_Shahi_dynasty) in 1636. After the Mughal-Nijamshahi war, Shahaji soon joined Adilshahi as a political compulsion and regained the Pune jagir. At that time he selected the town for the residence of his wife [Jijabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jijabai), and young son Shivaji, the future founder of the Maratha empire. The [Lal Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Mahal) residence of Jiajabai and Shivaji was completed in 1640 AD.[^cite_note-\:6-36] Shivaji spent his young years at the Lal Mahal. [Jijabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jijabai) is said to have commissioned the building of the [Kasba Ganapati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasba_Ganapati) temple. The [Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha) idol consecrated at this temple has been regarded as the presiding deity (*[Gramadevata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gramadevata)*) of the city.[^cite_note-37]



The [Sinhagad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhagad) fort is known for [Battle of Sinhagad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sinhagad) on 4 February 1670

Pune changed hands between the [Mughals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) and the [Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) many times during the rest of the 1600s. Recognizing the military potential of Pune, the Mughal general [Shaista Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaista_Khan) and later, the emperor [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb) further developed the areas around the town.[^cite_note-38] Pune was occupied again by Marathas in June 1670, four months to the battle of [Sinhagad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhagad) and remained in the hands of Marathas. From 1703 to 1705, towards the end of the 27-year-long [Mughal–Maratha Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal%E2%80%93Maratha_Wars), the town was occupied by Mughal emperor [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb) and its name was changed temporarily to Muhiyabad.[^cite_note-timesofindia-30Sep16-17][^cite_note-39] This name was abandoned and reverted to its original name soon after Aurangzeb's death.

### Peshwa rule

In 1720, [Baji Rao I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baji_Rao_I) was appointed [Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) (prime minister) of the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) by [Shahu I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahu_I), the fifth [Chhatrapati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatrapati) of the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire).[^cite_note-Mehta2005-40] As the Peshwa, Bajirao moved his base from [Saswad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saswad) to Pune in 1728, marking the beginning of the transformation of what was a *[kasbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasbah)* into a city.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-gokhale1-42] He also commissioned the construction of the [Shaniwar Wada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaniwar_Wada) on the high grounds of right bank of the [Mutha River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutha_River). The construction was completed in 1730, ushering in the era of Peshwa control of the city. Bajirao's son and successor, Nanasaheb constructed a lake at [Katraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katraj) on the outskirts of the city and an underground aqueduct to bring water from the lake to Shaniwar Wada and the city.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] The aqueduct was still in working order in 2004.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]



[Sangamwadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mula-Mutha_River), Poona in 1809



British defence plan during Battle of Koregaon

The patronage of the Maratha Peshwas resulted in a great expansion of Pune, with the construction of around 250 temples and bridges in the city, including the Lakdi Pul[^cite_note-47] and the temples on [Parvati Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parvati_Hill)[^cite_note-48] and many [Maruti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman), [Vithoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vithoba), [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu), [Mahadeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva), [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama), [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna), and [Ganesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesh) temples. The building of temples led to religion being responsible for about 15% of the city's economy during this period.[^cite_note-gokhale1-42][^cite_note-49] Pune prospered as a city during the reign of [Nanasaheb Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanasaheb_Peshwa). He developed [Saras Baug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saras_Baug), Heera Baug, Parvati Hill and new commercial, trading, and residential localities. [Sadashiv Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadashiv_Peth,_Pune), [Narayan Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayan_Peth,_Pune), [Rasta Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasta_Peth,_Pune) and [Nana Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nana_Peth,_Pune) were developed. The Peshwa's influence in India declined after the defeat of Maratha forces at the [Battle of Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Panipat_\(1761\)) but Pune remained the seat of power. In 1802 Pune was captured by [Yashwantrao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yashwantrao_Holkar) in the [Battle of Poona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poona), directly precipitating the [Second Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Maratha_War) of 1803–1805. The Peshwa rule ended with the defeat of Peshwa [Bajirao II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baji_Rao_II) by the [British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) in 1818.[^cite_note-Naravane2006-50]

Historian [Govind Sakharam Sardesai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govind_Sakharam_Sardesai) lists 163 prominent families that held high ranks and played significant roles in politics, military, and finance in 18th century Pune. Of these 163 families, a majority(80) were [Deshastha Brahmins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deshastha_Brahmins), 46 were [Chitpawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitpawan), 15 were [Chandraseniya Kayastha Prabhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandraseniya_Kayastha_Prabhu) ([CKP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CKP)) whereas [Karhade Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karhade_Brahmin) and [Saraswat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraswat) accounted for 11 families each.[^cite_note-Gokhale1988-51]

### British rule (1818–1947)



Map of Poona in 1911.



Dapoorie railway bridge in [Dapodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dapodi) in 1858.



Deccan College in Poona in India Around 1875

The [Third Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Anglo-Maratha_War) broke out between the Marathas and [the British East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) in 1817. The [Peshwas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) were defeated at the [Battle of Khadki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Khadki) (then spelled Kirkee) on 5 November near Pune and the city was seized by the British. It was placed under the administration of the [Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency) and the British built a large military [cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Cantonment) to the east of the city (now used by the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army)). The old city and the cantonment areas followed different patterns of development, with the latter being developed more on European lines to cater for the needs of the British military class. The old city had narrow lanes and areas segregated by caste and religion.[^cite_note-52] The settlement of the regiments of the 17 [Poona Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poona_Horse) cavalry, the [Lancashire Fusiliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire_Fusiliers), the [Maratha Light Infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Light_Infantry), and others, led to an increase in the population. Due to its milder weather, the city became the "Monsoon capital" of the Governor of Bombay, thus making it one of the most important cities of the [Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-55] The [Southern Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Command_\(India\)) of the [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army), established in 1895, is headquartered in [Pune cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Camp).[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

The city of Pune was known as Poona during British rule. Poona Municipality was established in 1858. A railway line from Bombay to the city opened in 1858, run by the [Great Indian Peninsula Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Indian_Peninsula_Railway) (GIPR).[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] [Navi Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navi_Peth,_Pune), [Ganj Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganj_Peth) (now renamed [Mahatma Phule Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Phule_Peth)) were developed during the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj).[^cite_note-60]

#### Centre of social reform and nationalism



Main street,Pune (Now known as M.G. road) in early 1900s



A Street in [Budhwar Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budhwar_Peth,_Pune)

Pune was prominently associated with the [struggle for Indian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement). In the period between 1875 and 1910, the city was a centre of agitation led by [Gopal Krishna Gokhale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopal_Krishna_Gokhale) and [Bal Gangadhar Tilak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal_Gangadhar_Tilak). The city was also a centre for social reform led by [Gopal Ganesh Agarkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopal_Ganesh_Agarkar), [Mahatma Jyotirao Phule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Jyotirao_Phule), feminist [Tarabai Shinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarabai_Shinde), [Dhondo Keshav Karve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhondo_Keshav_Karve) and [Pandita Ramabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandita_Ramabai). They demanded the abolition of caste prejudice, equal rights for women, harmony between the [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) and [Muslim](19541.md) communities, and better schools for the poor.[^cite_note-61] [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) was imprisoned at the [Yerwada Central Jail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerwada_Central_Jail) several times and placed under house arrest at the [Aga Khan Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aga_Khan_Palace) between 1942 and 1944, where both his wife [Kasturba Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasturba_Gandhi) and aide [Mahadev Desai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahadev_Desai) died.[^cite_note-Severance1997-62] [Savarkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinayak_Damodar_Savarkar) used to study in [Fergusson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fergusson_College) and performed the "[Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi)" of foreign items near [Mutha river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutha_river) bank .

### Pune since Indian independence

After [Indian independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement) from the British in 1947, Pune saw enormous growth, transforming it into a modern metropolis. The Poona Municipal Council was reorganised to form the [Pune Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Municipal_Corporation) (PMC) in 1950.[^cite_note-63] The education sector in the city continued its growth in the post-independence era with the establishment of the University of Pune (now, [Savitribai Phule Pune University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savitribai_Phule_Pune_University)) in 1949, the [National Chemical Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Chemical_Laboratory) in 1950 and the [National Defence Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_Academy_\(India\)) in 1955.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66]

The establishment of [Hindustan Antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustan_Antibiotics) in 1954 marked the beginning of industrial development in the [Hadapsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadapsar), [Bhosari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhosari), and [Pimpri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimpri) areas.[^cite_note-Kolekar1993-67][^cite_note-nic-68] [MIDC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Industrial_Development_Corporation) provided the necessary infrastructure for new businesses to set up operations.[^cite_note-hindu2-69] In the 1970s, several engineering companies were set up in the city, allowing it to vie with [Chennai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] In the 1990s, Pune began to attract foreign capital, particularly in the information technology and engineering industries. IT parks were established in [Aundh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aundh_\(Pune\)), [Viman Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viman_Nagar), [Hinjawadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinjawadi), [Wagholi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wagholi,_Pune), [Kharadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharadi) and [Balewadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balewadi)-[Baner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baner) region. As a result, the city saw a huge influx of people due to opportunities offered by the manufacturing, and lately, the software industries.

The breach in the [Panshet dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panshet_Dam) and the resulting [flood of 1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadakwasla_Dam#Flood_of_1961) led to severe damage and the destruction of housing close to the river banks.[^cite_note-72] The mishap spurred the development of new suburbs and housing complexes.[^cite_note-73] To integrate urban planning, the [Pune Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Metropolitan_Region) was defined in 1967 covering the area under PMC, the [Pimpri-Chinchwad Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimpri-Chinchwad_Municipal_Corporation), the three cantonments, and the surrounding villages.[^cite_note-74]

In 1998 work on the six-lane [Mumbai-Pune expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mumbai-Pune_expressway) began; it was completed in 2001.[^cite_note-75] In 2008, the [Commonwealth Youth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Youth_Games) took place in Pune, which encouraged development in the northwest region of the city.[^cite_note-76] On 13 February 2010, [a bomb exploded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Pune_bombing) at the German Bakery in the upmarket [Koregaon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koregaon_Park) neighbourhood in eastern Pune, killing 17 and injuring 60.[^cite_note-cnn-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] Evidence suggested that the [Indian Mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Mujahideen) terrorist group carried out the attack.[^cite_note-Pant2015-80]

### 21st-century

Pune has evolved greatly since Indian Independence, from notable universities, colleges and management schools, earning it the nickname of the 'Oxford Of The East', to being one of the most important automobile manufacturing hub.[^cite_note-Pune_Autohub-81][^cite_note-82] Pune is also home to the world's largest vaccine manufacturer, [Serum Institute of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serum_Institute_of_India).[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

## Geography



[Vetal Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vetal_Hill) Panorama, a prominent hill in Pune. Elevation c. 800 m.

Pune is situated at approximately 18° 32" north latitude and 73° 51" east longitude. The area of Pune district is 15,642 km<sup>2</sup>,[^cite_note-85] and the municipal corporation area covers 518 km<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-86] By road Pune is 149 km (93 mi) south-east of [Mumbai](19189.md), 580 km (360 mi) south-west of [Indore](365670.md), 695 km (432 mi) west of [Nagpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagpur), 1,173 km (729 mi) south of Delhi, 734 km (456 mi) north of [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore), 562 km (349 mi) north-west of [Hyderabad](37534.md). Pune lies on the western margin of the [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau), at an altitude of 560 m (1,840 ft) above sea level. It is on the [leeward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_and_leeward) side of the [Sahyadri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahyadri) mountain range, which forms a barrier from the [Arabian Sea](2747.md). It is a hilly city, with [Vetal Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vetal_Hill_in_Pune) rising to 800 m (2,600 ft) above sea level. The [Sinhagad fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhagad) is at an altitude of 1,300 metres (4,300 feet).

The old city of Pune is at the confluence of the [Mula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mula_River_\(India\)) and [Mutha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutha_River) rivers. The [Pavana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavana_River), a tributary of Mula River and [Indrayani River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indrayani_River), a tributary of the [Bhima River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhima_River), traverse the northwest [Neighbourhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_neighbourhoods_in_Pune) of Pune.

### Cityscape

See also: [List of neighbourhoods in Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_neighbourhoods_in_Pune)



A view of the Kharadi Locality from Keshav Nagar

The modern city of Pune has many distinct [neighbourhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_neighbourhoods_in_Pune). These include the numerous [peths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peths_in_Pune) of the old city on the eastern bank of the Mutha river, the cantonment areas of [Khadki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadki) and [Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Camp) established by the British, and numerous suburbs.[^cite_note-nic-68] There are several Peths in usual localities of the Pune city.[^cite_note-87] The industrial growth in the [Pimpri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimpri), [Chinchwad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinchwad), [Akurdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akurdi,_Mawal), Nigdi and nearby areas allowed these areas to incorporate a new governing municipal corporation.[^cite_note-hindu2-69][^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89][^cite_note-RT_Dec2007-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

The [Pune Metropolitan Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Metropolitan_Region) (PMR), initially defined in 1967, has grown to 7,256 km<sup>2</sup> made up of the ten *[talukas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil)* of the [Pune district](3123898.md).[^cite_note-\:2-94] The areas of PMC and PCMC along with the three cantonment areas of Camp, Khadki, and Dehu Road form the urban core of the PMR, which also includes seven [municipal councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagar_Palika) and 842 villages.[^cite_note-\:2-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-\:3-96]

Rapid industrialisation since the 1960s has led to large influx of people into the city. Housing supply has not kept pace with demand, causing the number of [slum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum) dwellings to increase.[^cite_note-97] Approximately 36% of the population lives in 486 slum areas. Of these, 45% slum households do not have in-house toilet facilities and 10% do not have electricity. One third of the slums are on mixed ownership land. The living conditions in slums varies considerably, depending on their status (formal/informal) and in how far non-governmental organisations (NGOs), [community organisations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_organisation) (CBOs) and government agencies are involved and committed to improving local living conditions.[^cite_note-mdpi.com-98] Since the 1990s a number of landmark integrated townships and gated communities have been developed in Pune such as [Magarpatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magarpatta), [Nanded city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanded_City,_Pune), [Amanora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanora_Park_Town), [Blue Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ridge_Town_Pune), Life Republic and [Lavasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavasa).[^cite_note-99] They also offer business opportunities and access to infrastructure. According to the [PMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Municipal_Corporation), six townships with up to 15,000 housing units existed in Pune in 2012 and 25 more were in the planning process.[^cite_note-mdpi.com-98]

The Mercer 2017 Quality of Living Rankings evaluated living conditions in more than 440 cities around the world and ranked Pune at 145, second highest in India after [Hyderabad](37534.md) at 144.[^cite_note-100] The same source highlights Pune as being among evolving business centres and as one of nine emerging cities around the world with the citation "Hosts IT and automotive companies".[^cite_note-101] The 2017 Annual Survey of India's City-Systems (ASICS) report, released by the Janaagraha Centre for Citizenship and Democracy, adjudged Pune as the best governed of 23 major cities.[^cite_note-102]

#### Peths in Pune

Main article: [Peths in Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peths_in_Pune)

Peth is a general term in the [Marathi language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language) for a locality in Pune. Seventeen peths are located in Pune, which today constitute the old city of Pune. Most were established during the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) era under the [Maratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha) and [Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) rule of the city in the 18th century, before the arrival of the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj).[^cite_note-103] Pune is home to many distinctive peths, or place names, for various neighbourhoods. The majority of them bore the names of their founders and days of the week.[^cite_note-104]

### Climate

Pune has a [tropical wet and dry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_savanna_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Aw*) climate, closely bordering upon a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) (Köppen *BSh*) with average temperatures ranging between 20 and 28 °C (68 and 82 °F).[^cite_note-105] Pune experiences three seasons: summer, [monsoon](57630.md), and winter. Typical summer months are from mid-March to mid-June, with maximum temperatures sometimes reaching 42 °C (108 °F). The warmest month in Pune is May. The city often has heavy dusty winds in May, with humidity remaining high. Even during the hottest months, the nights are usually cool due to Pune's high altitude. The highest temperature recorded was 43.3 °C (109.9 °F) on 30 April 1897.[^cite_note-106]

The monsoon lasts from June to October, with moderate rainfall and temperatures ranging from 22 to 28 °C (72 to 82 °F). Most of the 822 mm (32.36 in) of annual rainfall in the city falls between June and September, and July is the wettest month of the year. Hailstorms are not unheard of. For most of December and January the daytime temperature hovers around 29 °C (84.2 °F) while overnight temperatures are below 12 °C (53.6 °F). On 11 May 2023, [Koregaon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koregaon_Park) recorded a temperature of 44.4 °C (112 °F)[^cite_note-107]

### Seismology

Main article: [List of earthquakes in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_India)

Pune is 100 km (62 mi) north of the seismically active zone around [Koyna Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koyna_Dam).[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] The [India Meteorological Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_Meteorological_Department) has assessed this area as being in [Zone 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earthquake_hazard_zoning_of_India#Zone_3), on a scale of 2 to 5, with 5 being the most prone to earthquakes.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116] Pune has experienced some moderate – and many low – intensity earthquakes in its history.

The city has a population of 3,124,458; while 5,057,709 people reside in the Pune Urban Agglomeration as of the 2011 census.[^cite_note-punepages.com-117] The latter was c. 4,485,000 in 2005. According to the [Pune Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Municipal_Corporation) (PMC), 40% of the population lived in [slums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slums) in 2001.[^cite_note-118]

Since Pune is a major industrial metropolis, it has attracted migrants from all parts of India. The number of people migrating to Pune rose from 43,900 in 2001 to 88,200 in 2005.[^cite_note-119] The sharp increase in population during the decade 1991–2001 led to the absorption of 38 fringe villages into the city.[^cite_note-REVISED_ACTION_PLAN_FOR_CONTROL_OF_AIR_POLLUTION_IN_Pune-120] The top five source areas of migrants are [Karnataka](16880.md), Uttar Pradesh, [Andhra Pradesh](2377.md), Gujarat, and [Rajasthan](26291.md). The [Sindhis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhis) in the city are mostly refugees and their descendants, who came to the area after the partition of India in 1947.[^cite_note-121] Initially they settled in the Pimpri area, which is still home to a large number of [Sindhi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhis_in_India). However, they are also present in other parts of the city.[^cite_note-122] As agriculture has dwindled in recent decades, immigration of the erstwhile rural peoples now accounts for 70 per cent of the population growth.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124]

The average [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) of Pune was 86.15% in 2011 compared to 80.45% in 2001.[^cite_note-125]

[Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) is the major religion, practised by a little under 80% of people in Pune. Other religions with a significant presence include [Islam](6037917.md), [Buddhism](3267529.md), [Jainism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism), Christianity, [Sikhism](27964.md) and [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism).[^cite_note-Religion-126][^cite_note-127]

Of the many Hindu temples in the city, the Parvati temple complex on [Parvati Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parvati_Hill) and at least 250 others date back to the 18th century.[^cite_note-128] These temples were commissioned by the [Peshwas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa), who ruled the city at the time, and are dedicated to various deities including [Maruti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman), [Vithoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vithoba), [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu), [Mahadeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva), [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama), [Krishna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna) and [Ganesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesh).[^cite_note-129][^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132] The historic temples of [Kasba Ganapati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasba_Ganapati), the [Tambadi (Red) Jogeshwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budhwar_Peth,_Pune) are considered the guardian deities of the city.[^cite_note-Kantak_489-495-34][^cite_note-State1885-133] [Dagadusheth Halwai Ganapati Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagadusheth_Halwai_Ganapati_Temple) is the richest Ganesh temple in Pune. Pune district has two of the most important pilgrimage centres of the [Varkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varkari) sect of the [Bhakti movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhakti_movement) in Maharashtra, namely [Alandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alandi) where the *[samadhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samadhi)* of 13th century Saint [Dnyaneshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnyaneshwar) is located and [Dehu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dehu) where the 17th century Saint [Tukaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukaram) lived. Every year in the [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_calendar) month of *[Ashadh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashadh)* (June/July), the *[Paduka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paduka)* (symbolic sandals) of these saints are carried in a [pilgrimage](23763.md), the *[Pandharpur Vari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandharpur_Vari)*, to meet Vithoba. The procession makes a stopover in the city on its way to [Pandharpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandharpur) attracting hundreds of thousands of *Varkaris* and devotees. Other important Hindu pilgrimage sites in [PMR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Metropolitan_Region) or the district include [Jejuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jejuri), and five of [Ashtavinayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashtavinayak) Ganesh temples. The Shrutisagar Ashram houses the Vedanta Research Centre and a unique temple of [Dakshinamurthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakshinamurthy).

Prominent mosques include Roshan Masjid, Chand Tara Masjid, Jama Masjid, and Azam Campus Masjid, Manusha Masjid. Chand Tara Masjid, located in [Nana Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nana_Peth,_Pune), is one of the biggest and most important mosques in Pune as it is the city headquarters (*markaz*) for the [Tablighi Jamaat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tablighi_Jamaat). Pune is also the birthplace of [Meher Baba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meher_Baba), although his followers usually travel to [Meherabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meherabad) to visit his tomb. [Hazrat Babajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazrat_Babajan), identified by Meher Baba as one of the five [perfect masters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_Master_\(Meher_Baba\)), has a shrine (*[Dargah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dargah)*) erected in her honour under a [neem tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadirachta_indica) in [Pune Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Camp).[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135]

Pune has a distinct Christian community comprising [Roman Catholic](606848.md), [Pentecostals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostalism), [CNI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_North_India), [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist_Church_in_India), [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterianism), Christian Missionaries helped in setting up schools and colleges all over and also spread the message of faith.[^cite_note-136] The city has several churches dedicated to different Christian denominations such as St. Anthony's Shrine and Vineyard Worker's of Christ Church, Dapodi. [St. Patrick's Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Patrick's_Cathedral,_Pune) built in 1850 is the seat of the bishop of the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Poona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Poona).[^cite_note-137]

Pune has [Jain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jainism) temples dating back to the Peshwa era. At present, there are more than one hundred Jain temples in [PMR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Metropolitan_Region) with the one at [Katraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katraj) being the largest.[^cite_note-Kelting2001-138] Pune has over 20 Gurdwaras, with Gurdwara Guru Nanak Darbar in [Pune Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Camp) and Gurdwara Shri Guru Singh Sabha in [Ganesh Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesh_Peth,_Pune) being the ones situated in the heart of the city. The 19th-century [Ohel David Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohel_David_Synagogue), known locally as Lal Deval, is said to be one of the largest synagogues in Asia outside Israel.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140] The Sir Jamsetjee Jejeebhoy Agiary is a prominent Zoroastrian temple.

Pune has been associated with several significant recent spiritual teachers. The controversial Guru [Rajneesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajneesh), also known as Osho, lived and taught in Pune for much of the 1970s and 1980s. The [Osho International Meditation Resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osho_\(Bhagwan_Shree_Rajneesh\)#Legacy), one of the world's largest spiritual centres, is located in [Koregaon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koregaon_Park) and attracts visitors from over a hundred countries. The meditation resort organises music and meditation festival every year during monsoon, known as [Osho Monsoon Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osho_Monsoon_Festival). A number of well known artists around the world participates in the event.[^cite_note-141]

### Civic administration



[Pune Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Municipal_Corporation) building

[Pune Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Municipal_Corporation) and [Pimpri-Chinchwad Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimpri-Chinchwad_Municipal_Corporation) are the civic bodies responsible for [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_governance_in_India). It comprises two branches, the executive branch headed by the Municipal Commissioner, an [IAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) officer appointed by the [Government of Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Maharashtra), and an elected deliberative branch, the general body, headed by the [Mayor of Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Pune).[^cite_note-\:4-143] Municipal elections are held every five years to elect councillors, commonly known as "corporators", who form the general body. The current general body of the PMC elected in February 2017 has 162 corporators representing 41 multi-member wards (39 with 4 corporators each and 2 with 3 each).[^cite_note-PMC2017winningcandidates-144] The general body, in turn, elects the mayor and the deputy mayor. The mayor has a ceremonial role as the first citizen and ambassador of the city while the actual executive power lies with the municipal commissioner. For policy deliberations, corporators form several committees. Perhaps the most important of these is the 16-member Standing Committee, half of whose members retire every year.[^cite_note-\:5-145] The Standing Committee and the 15 ward committees are in charge of financial approvals.[^cite_note-\:4-143] PMC was ranked 8th out of 21 Indian cities for best governance and administrative practices in 2014. It scored 3.5 out of 10 compared to the national average of 3.3.[^cite_note-146]

The [Pune City Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Police) is the law enforcement agency for the city of Pune. It is a division of the [Maharashtra Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Police) and is headed by the [Police Commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_Commissioner_of_Pune), an officer of the [Indian Police Service](451884.md). The Pune Police Department reports to the State Ministry of Home Affairs. A separate police commissionerate was announced for [PCMC, Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimpri-Chinchwad) in April 2018 to be carved out of the historic Pune Police Department.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148] The new commissionerate took charge on 15 August 2018.[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150]

[Pune Metropolitan Region Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Metropolitan_Region_Development_Authority) (PMRDA) was formed on 31 March 2015 and is responsible for the integrated development of the PMR.[^cite_note-151] Currently its jurisdiction extends over 7,256.46 km<sup>2</sup> (2,802 sq mi) and includes two [municipal corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporations_in_India), three cantonment boards, seven municipal councils, 13 [census towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_town#India) and 842 villages.[^cite_note-\:2-94][^cite_note-\:3-96]

### Municipal finance

According to audited financial statements published on the CityFinance Portal of the Ministry of Housing and Urban Affairs, the [Pune Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Municipal_Corporation) reported total revenue receipts of ₹94.26 billion (US\$1,136 million) and total expenditure of ₹78.36 billion (US\$944 million) for the financial year 2022–23.[^cite_note-152] The CityFinance Portal publishes audited financial statements of urban local bodies across India.

### Utility services

The PMC supplies the city with potable water that is sourced from the [Khadakwasla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadakwasla) Reservoir. There are five other reservoirs in the area that supply water to the city and the greater metropolitan area.[^cite_note-tirthkar-153] The city lacks the capacity to treat all the sewage it generates, which leads to the Mutha river containing only sewage outside the monsoon months.[^cite_note-154] In 2009 only 65% of sewage generated was treated before being discharged into the rivers.[^cite_note-tirthkar-153] According to Anwesha Borthakur and Pardeep Singh, unplanned and haphazard development has turned the Mula-Mutha river into a dead river. The Pune municipal corporation has undertaken plans to restore life into the rivers.[^cite_note-155] PMC is also responsible for collecting solid waste. Around 1,600 tons of solid waste is generated in Pune each day. The waste consists of 53% organic, compostable material; and 47% inorganic material, of which around half is recyclable. The unrecovered solid waste is transported to the dumping grounds in Urali devachi.[^cite_note-156]

## Economy



World Trade Center, Pune.

Pune is a well known manufacturing and industrial center of India. With an estimated nominal GDP of Rs. 3,31,478 crores for year 2019–20, Pune District is the third largest contributor to the economy of Maharashtra, after Mumbai and Thane. Pune has the fifth largest metropolitan economy and the sixth highest per capita income in the country.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162] As per the Directorate of Economics and Statistics (Government of Maharashtra), the GDP per capita of Pune District in 2019–20 was Rs. 3,16,848. In 2014–15, the manufacturing sector provided employment to over 500,000 people.[^cite_note-industry-163]

[Shinde Chhatri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinde_Chhatri) in Wanowrie.

Historical attractions include the 8th century rock-cut [Pataleshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pataleshwar) cave temple, the 18th century [Shaniwar Wada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaniwar_Wada), the 19th century [Aga Khan Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aga_Khan_Palace), [Lal Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Mahal) and Sinhagad fort. [Shinde Chhatri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinde_Chhatri), located at Wanowrie, is a memorial dedicated to the Maratha general [Mahadaji Shinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahadaji_Shinde) (Scindia).[^cite_note-186] The old city had many residential buildings with courtyards called [Wada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wada_\(house\)). Landmark architectural works by [Christopher Charles Benninger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Charles_Benninger) surround the city, including the [Mahindra United World College of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahindra_United_World_College_of_India), the Centre for Development Studies and Activities, the YMCA Retreat at Nilshi and the [Samundra Institute of Maritime Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samundra_Institute_of_Maritime_Studies).

### Museums, parks and zoos

Museums in Pune include the [Raja Dinkar Kelkar Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja_Dinkar_Kelkar_Museum), [Mahatma Phule Industrial Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Phule_Museum), Deccan college museum of Maratha history,[^cite_note-188] Dr. Babasaheb Ambedkar Museum, [Joshi's Museum of Miniature Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshi's_Museum_of_Miniature_Railway) and the [Pune Tribal Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Tribal_Museum) Pune also houses Blades of Glory Cricket Museum which is the biggest cricket museum in the world. The College of Military Engineering has an archive and an equipment museum; this includes a rail exhibit with a metre-gauge train. The [Aga Khan Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aga_Khan_Palace), where [Mahatma Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahatma_Gandhi) was interned during the Quit India movement, has a memorial dedicated to his wife, [Kasturba Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasturba_Gandhi) who died during the internment.

For a city of its size, Pune has very few large public parks and gardens. Parks and green spaces in the city include the [Kamala Nehru Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamala_Nehru_Park,_Pune), Sambhaji Park, *Shahu Udyan*, [Peshwe Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwe_Park), [Saras Baug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saras_Baug), Empress Gardens, and [Bund Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bund_Garden,_Pune).[^cite_note-189] The [Pu La Deshpande Udyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pu_La_Deshpande_Garden) is a replica of the [Korakuen Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korakuen_Garden) in [Okayama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okayama), Japan.[^cite_note-PuneOkayamaGarden-190] The [Hanuman hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman_Tekdi), [Vetal hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vetal_Tekdi), and [Taljai Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taljai_Hills) are protected nature reserves on hills within the city limits.



[Rajiv Gandhi Zoological Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi_Zoological_Park)

The [Rajiv Gandhi Zoological Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi_Zoological_Park) is located in Katraj.[^cite_note-RajivGandhiZoologicalPark-191] The zoo, earlier located at Peshwe Park, was merged with the reptile park at Katraj in 1999. [Pune Riverfront](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Riverfront) is under-construction being developed by [Pune Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Municipal_Corporation) along the banks of [Mula-Mutha River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mula-Mutha_River).[^cite_note-192]

### Performing arts



[Ganesh Chaturthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesh_Chaturthi) procession

Both experimental and professional theatre receive extensive patronage from the Marathi community. The [Tilak Smarak Ranga Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilak_Smarak_Ranga_Mandir), [Bal Gandharva Ranga Mandir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal_Gandharva_Ranga_Mandir), Bharat Natya Mandir, [Yashwantrao Chavan Natya Gruha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yashwantrao_Chavan_Natya_Gruha), and [Sudarshan Rangmanch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudarshan_Rangmanch) are prominent theatres in the city.[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195]

Ganesh Kala Krida Rangamanch is the largest indoor theatre in the city, with a [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) of approximately 45,000.[^cite_note-196] The [Sawai Gandharva Sangeet Mahotsav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawai_Gandharva_Sangeet_Mahotsav), one of the most prominent and sought-after [Indian classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_Classical_Music) festivals in India, is held in Pune every year in December. It commemorates the life and achievements of [Sawai Gandharva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawai_Gandharva).[^cite_note-197] The concept of *Diwāḷī Pahāṭ* (lit. [Diwali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diwali) dawn) originated in Pune as a music festival on the morning of the festival of Diwali.[^cite_note-198]

### Festivals

[Ganesh Chaturthi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesh_Chaturthi) is widely and publicly celebrated in Pune. [Lokamanya Bal Gangadhar Tilak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bal_Gangadhar_Tilak) started the public celebration of the festival as a means to circumvent the colonial British government ban on Hindu gatherings through its anti-public assembly legislation in 1892.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200] Pandals with Ganesh idols are erected all across Pune. Many *Ganesh* *mandals* display live or figurine shows called *Dekhava* during the festival. These shows often carry socially relevant messages. Processions of Ganpati are accompanied by *Dhol-Tasha pathaks* (groups who play Dhol-Tasha percussion instruments). Involvement of these *pathaks* has become a cultural identity of Pune with there being over 150 such groups operating in and around Pune. [Jnana Prabodhini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jnana_Prabodhini), a social organisation in Pune is widely accredited for founding the tradition of *Dhol-Tasha* *pathaks*.[^cite_note-201]

### Air

[Pune International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_International_Airport) at [Lohagaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lohagaon) is one of the busiest airports in India. #### Metro

Main article: [Pune Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Metro)



[District Court Pune metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Court_Pune_metro_station)



[SNDT College metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SNDT_College_metro_station)

Pune Metro is a [mass rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system serving the city. The system comprises two operational lines with a combined operational length of 31.25 km (19.42 mi).[^cite_note-\:1-214] Construction for a [third line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_3_\(Pune_Metro\)) is underway, which on completion would increase the combined operational length to 59.1 km.[^cite_note-\:1-214]

The two operation lines are the [Purple Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purple_Line_\(Pune_Metro\)) and the [Aqua Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqua_Line_\(Pune_Metro\)). The Purple Line starts from the [Pimpri Chinchwad Municipal Corporation metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PCMC_Bhavan_metro_station) and terminates at the [Swargate metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swargate_metro_station). The Aqua line starts from the [Vanaz metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanaz_metro_station) and ends at the [Ramwadi metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramwadi_metro_station). Both lines pass through the [District Court Pune metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Court_Pune_metro_station), which as acts as a [cross platform interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-platform_interchange).

The presently under construction [Pink Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pink_Line_\(Pune_Metro\)) will plough from Megapolis Circle in Hinjawadi till the District Court Pune station. It will see a cross platform interchange at [Shivaji Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivaji_Nagar_metro_station_\(Pune\)) (for the Purple Line) and District Court (for both, the Purple and Aqua lines).[^cite_note-215]

Main articles: [Pune Mahanagar Parivahan Mahamandal Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Mahanagar_Parivahan_Mahamandal_Limited) and [Rainbow BRTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_BRTS)

Public buses within the city and its suburbs are operated by [PMPML](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Mahanagar_Parivahan_Mahamandal_Limited) .In January 2019, Pune became the first Indian city to adopt [e-buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_bus) and Bhekrai Nagar became the country's first all electric bus depot. Due to the then ongoing metro construction related work and high incidences of accidents, dedicated bus lanes from Yerawda to Vimannagar were removed in 2023.[^cite_note-218]

[Maharashtra State Road Transport Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_State_Road_Transport_Corporation) runs buses from stations in Wakdewadi, [Pune station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Station_Bus_Stand) and [Swargate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swargate) to all major cities and towns in Maharashtra and neighbouring states. Once known as the "[cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle) city of India", Pune has experienced a rapid growth in the number of [motorised two wheelers](19876.md) replacing the bicycle.[^cite_note-225] In 2005 the city was reported to have one million two wheelers. The report also stated that the increase in vehicular and industrial activity had led to a 10-fold increase in particulate pollution in some areas of the city.[^cite_note-226] In 2018 the number of vehicles in the city has exceeded its population with 3.62 million total vehicles, 2.70 million being two wheelers.[^cite_note-227] In the fiscal year 2017–18 alone 300,000 new vehicles were registered in the city, two-thirds of them two wheelers.[^cite_note-228]

A revival of cycling in Pune with 130 kilometres (81 mi) of cycle tracks built was attempted as a part of the [BRT system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) under the [Jawaharlal Nehru National Urban Renewal Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru_National_Urban_Renewal_Mission) in 2004. However, a 2011 report revealed that only 88 kilometres (55 mi) of tracks were actually built and most were unusable at the time of the report.[^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230] Under the [Smart Cities Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Cities_Mission), app based [cycle sharing schemes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle-sharing_system) have been launched in the city since late 2017.[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232][^cite_note-233] The PMC has devised the Pune Cycle Plan with 470 kilometres (290 mi) of cycle tracks planned.[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236] Cycles are also seen as a possible way of improving [last mile connectivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Last_mile_\(transportation\)) for the metro system.[^cite_note-237]

## Education

Main article: [List of educational institutions in Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_educational_institutions_in_Pune)



Main building of Pune University

Pune has over a hundred educational institutes and more than nine deemed universities apart from the [Savitribai Phule Pune University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savitribai_Phule_Pune_University) (SPPU; formerly University of Pune), which is the largest university in the country based on total number of affiliated colleges.[^cite_note-Pune100Educational9Universities-238] Higher education institutes attract international students mainly from the Middle Eastern countries such as Iran, and United Arab Emirates, and also African countries such as Ethiopia and Kenya.[^cite_note-239] Pune is the largest centre for Japanese learning in India.[^cite_note-240] Other languages taught in the city include German, which is taught at the [Goethe-Institut](470336.md), and French, which is taught at [Alliance Française](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_Fran%C3%A7aise). Several colleges in Pune have [student exchange programmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_exchange_program) with colleges in Europe.[^cite_note-241]

### Primary and secondary education

Main article: [List of schools in Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Pune)

The PMC runs 297 primary schools and 30 secondary and higher secondary schools.[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-\:8-243] While it is mandatory for the PMC to provide primary education under state law, secondary education is an optional duty.[^cite_note-\:8-243][^cite_note-244][^cite_note-245] In the rural and suburban areas of the PMR, public primary schools are run by the Pune [Zilla Parishad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Councils_of_India). Private schools are run by education trusts and are required to undergo mandatory inspection by the concerned authorities. Private schools are eligible for financial aid from the state government.[^cite_note-246] Public schools are affiliated to the [Maharashtra State Board of Secondary and Higher Secondary Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_State_Board_of_Secondary_and_Higher_Secondary_Education) (State Board). The language of instruction in public schools is primarily Marathi, although the PMC also runs [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu), English and [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada) [medium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_of_instruction) schools.[^cite_note-\:8-243][^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248] Along with these languages, private schools also offer instruction in Hindi and [Gujarati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujarati_language).[^cite_note-249] Private schools vary in their choice of curriculum and may follow the State Board or one of the two central boards of education, the [CBSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Secondary_Education) or [CISCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_for_the_Indian_School_Certificate_Examinations).[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251]

[Jnana Prabodhini Prashala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jnana_Prabodhini_Prashala), located in Sadashiv Peth, is the first school for [intellectually gifted and talented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gifted_education) students in India.[^cite_note-252] Additionally, it counts with one of the 18 United World Colleges across the globe, having UWC Mahindra College in the Mulshi Valley.

## Media

A number of Marathi-language newspapers from the British era continued publishing decades after independence. These included [*Kesari*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kesari_\(Marathi_newspaper\)), *[Tarun Bharat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarun_Bharat)*, *Prabhat* and *[Sakal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakal)*.[^cite_note-267] *Sakal* has remained the most popular Marathi daily.[^cite_note-268][^cite_note-269] *Kesari* is now only published as an online newspaper. The Mumbai-based *[Maharashtra Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Times)*, *[Loksatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loksatta)* and *[Lokmat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokmat)* have all introduced Pune based editions in the last fifteen years. The Mumbai-based popular English newspaper the *[Indian Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Express)* has a Pune edition. Its rival the *[Times of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Times_of_India)* introduced a tabloid called *[Pune Mirror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Mirror)* in 2008. *[Mid-Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Day)*, *[Daily News and Analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_News_and_Analysis)* and *[Sakaal Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakaal_Times)* are other local English newspapers. The English-language newspaper *The Hindu* has launched a Pune edition covering local as well as national news. Another English-language online news website [Puneri Pages](https://puneripages.in/) was launched, covering local and national news.[^cite_note-270]

The government owned [All India Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Radio) (AIR) has been broadcasting from Pune since 1953.[^cite_note-BARUAH2017-271] Savitribai Phule Pune University broadcasts programmes focusing on its different departments and student welfare schemes on its own [FM radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM_radio) channel called *Vidyavani*.[^cite_note-272] A number of commercial FM channels are also received in the city.[^cite_note-273] The city receives almost all of the [television channels in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_India) including [broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcasting), [cable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television) and [direct-to-home TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct-to-home_television_in_India).

## Sports

Main article: [Sports in Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Pune)



[Maharashtra Cricket Association Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Cricket_Association_Stadium)

Badminton in its modern form originated in Pune.[^cite_note-274] The game of badminton was also known as Poona or Poonah after the then British garrison town of Poona where it was particularly popular and where the first rules for the game were drawn up in 1873. (Games employing shuttlecocks have been played for centuries across Eurasia, but the modern game of badminton developed in the mid-19th century among the British as a variant of the earlier game of battledore and shuttlecock. "Battledore" was [an older term for "racquet"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_badminton).)[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-276]



Badminton Arena in [Balewadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shree_Shiv_Chhatrapati_Sports_Complex).

Women's tennis [ITF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Tennis_Federation) \$25K tournament held at [Deccan Gymkhana club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Gymkhana_Ground)'s tennis courts in 2021. It also hosted men's ITF \$15K event. [ATP 250 Maharashtra Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Open) was held at [Balewadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balewadi) till 2022. It was India's biggest professional tennis championship and only ATP event of India, where top professional tennis players participated.[^cite_note-277][^cite_note-278]

Popular games and sports in Pune include [cricket](25675557.md), athletics, basketball, badminton, [field hockey](10886.md), football, tennis, *[kabaddi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaddi)*, [paragliding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paragliding), *[kho-kho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kho-kho)*, [wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling), [rowing](25736.md), and [chess](5134.md). The [Chhatrapati Shivaji Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balewadi_Sports_Complex) in Balewadi is the venue for wrestling and other traditional sports. The [Royal Connaught Boat Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Connaught_Boat_Club) is one of several boating clubs on the Mula-Mutha river. Pune has basketball courts at the Deccan Gymkhana and at Fergusson College.[^cite_note-279] Pune Skatepark is a skateboarding park built in Sahakarnagar, consisting of an eight-foot bowl in a 3,000 square foot flatground.[^cite_note-280] Other prominent sporting institutions in Pune include the [Nehru Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nehru_Stadium,_Pune), the PYC Hindu Gymkhana, the Poona Golf Club and the Poona Cricket Club. The PYC has a long history of excellence in cricket. It is one of the oldest clubs in India and has produced many great cricketers, including D. B. Deodhar, Vijay Hazare and C. K. Naid.[^cite_note-281]



Closing ceremony of the [2008 Commonwealth Youth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Commonwealth_Youth_Games) hosted in Pune.



People in Pune celebrating after India won the [2011 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Cricket_World_Cup)

The [Neharu Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nehru_Stadium,_Pune) has hosted a game of the [1996 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Cricket_World_Cup) and five games of the [2023 Cricket World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Cricket_World_Cup) including one of India against Bangladesh.

The [Pune International Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_International_Marathon) is an annual [marathon](49132.md) conducted in Pune. The [National Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Games_of_India) of 1994 and the [2008 Commonwealth Youth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Commonwealth_Youth_Games) were held in the city at the Balewadi Stadium. The Deccan Gymkhana has hosted [Davis Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davis_Cup) matches on several occasions. The 37,000 [seating capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seating_capacity) [Maharashtra Cricket Association Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Cricket_Association_Stadium) has hosted international cricket – [T20s](770690.md), [One Day Internationals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Day_International), and a [test match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_cricket).[^cite_note-282] The National Education Foundation organises [Enduro3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enduro3), a cross country adventure race in Pune. It is a two- or three-day event with activities including cycling, trekking, river-crossing and rifle shooting.[^cite_note-283] [Pune Race Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Race_Course) was built in 1830 on 118.5 acres (0.480 km<sup>2</sup>) of land and is managed by the [Royal Western India Turf Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Western_India_Turf_Club). The course has two training tracks and two racing surfaces. The racing season is from July to October and includes the Pune Derby, the RWITC Invitational, the Independence Cup and the Southern Command Cup.[^cite_note-284] The city hosted the [2009 FIVB Men's Junior World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FIVB_Men's_Junior_World_Championship).

The [Maharashtra cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_cricket_team) and [state's women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_women's_cricket_team) owned by [Maharashtra Cricket Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Cricket_Association) represents the state in domestic cricket competitions such as the [Ranji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranji_Trophy), [Vijay Hazare Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijay_Hazare_Trophy) and women's domestic cricket respectively, is based in Pune (headquarter at [Gahunje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gahunje) village). Established in 2014, FC Pune City became the only professional football club in India to have teams which participated at all levels of professional football; Senior Team ([ISL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Super_League)), U-18 Team ([Elite league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_League_\(India\))), U- 16 Team, U-14 Team and the Women's Team.[^cite_note-289] The city is home to the [Pune Peshwas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Peshwas), runners-up in the 2015 [UBA Pro Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBA_Pro_Basketball_League) season. Pune also has an [American football](18951490.md) [franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franchise_\(sports\)), called the [Pune Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Marathas_\(American_football\)), which began playing in the inaugural season of the [Elite Football League of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elite_Football_League_of_India) in 2011 and which plays at the Balewadi Stadium.[^cite_note-290][^cite_note-291]
