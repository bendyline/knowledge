**Accra** ([/əˈkrɑː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is the capital and largest city of [Ghana](12067.md), located on the southern coast at the [Gulf of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Guinea), which is part of the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md).[^cite_note-\:6-2][^cite_note-3] As of the 2021 [census](6889.md), the [Accra Metropolitan District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_Metropolitan_District), 20.4 km<sup>2</sup> (7.9 sq mi), had a population of 284,124, and the larger [Greater Accra Region](2227696.md), 3,245 km<sup>2</sup> (1,253 sq mi), had a population of 5.46 million.[^cite_note-2021_census-1] The name "Accra" sometimes refers to the territory of the Accra Metropolitan District as it existed before 2008, when it covered 199.4 km<sup>2</sup> (77.0 sq mi).[^cite_note-land_area-4] This territory has since been split into 13 [local government districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Ghana): 12 independent municipal districts (total area: 179.0 km<sup>2</sup>) and the reduced Accra Metropolitan District (20.4 km<sup>2</sup>), which is the only district within the capital to be granted city status.[^cite_note-5] This territory of 199.4 km<sup>2</sup> contained 1,782,150 inhabitants at the 2021 census,[^cite_note-6] and serves as the capital of Ghana, while the district under the jurisdiction of the Accra Metropolitan Assembly proper (20.4 km<sup>2</sup>) is distinguished from the rest of the capital as the "City of Accra".[^cite_note-aba_background-info-7]

Formed from the merger of separate settlements around British [Fort James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_James_\(Ghana\)), Dutch [Fort Crêvecoeur (Ussher Fort)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ussher_Fort), and Danish [Fort Christiansborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Christiansborg) as [Jamestown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown/Usshertown,_Accra), Usshertown, and [Christiansborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osu,_Accra), respectively, Accra served as the capital of the British [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(British_colony\)) between 1877 and 1957 and has since transitioned into a more modern metropolis. The capital's architecture reflects this history, ranging from 19th-century [colonial architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_architecture) to modern [skyscrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyscrapers) and apartment blocks.[^cite_note-8]

Accra is the [Greater Accra Region](2227696.md)'s economic and administrative hub, and serves as the anchor of the larger [Greater Accra Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Accra_Metropolitan_Area) (GAMA),[^cite_note-9] which is inhabited by about 4 million people, making it the thirteenth-largest metropolitan area in Africa. In 2020, the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network) [think tank](37101.md) designated Accra as a "Gamma −" level [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city), indicating a growing level of international influence and connectedness.[^cite_note-10]

## Etymology

The word *Accra* is derived from the [Akan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akan_language) word *Nkran* meaning "ants", a reference to anthills seen in the countryside around Accra.[^cite_note-11] The name specifically refers to [soldier ants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_ant), and was applied to the town and people by the [Twi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twi) speakers.[^cite_note-\:0-12][^cite_note-13]

The name of Accra in the local [Ga language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga_language) is *Ga* or *Gaga*, the same name as that of the [Ga people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ga-Adangbe_people) and a [cognate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognate) with *Nkran*. The word is sometimes rendered with the nasalised vowels as *Gã* or *Gãgã*. Historian [Carl Christian Reindorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Christian_Reindorf) confirmed this etymology, proposing a link between the martial qualities and migratory behaviour of the local ants and those of the Ga people. The link between the [ethnonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnonym) and ants was explicitly reflected in the recognition of anthills as sacred places. Sometimes ringed by sacred fences (*aklabatsa*), the tall red mounds dotting Accra's hinterland were seen as microcosms of human community and as nodal points between the known world and the world of the dead.[^cite_note-\:0-12]

The Gas used the reference to the invasive species of dark-red swarming ants to connote the military prowess of the Gas and their ancient conquest of [Guang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guang_languages) speakers residing in the Accra Plains.[^cite_note-14]

The name *Ga* is a cognate of the name *Akan*, one of the words in which \[g] corresponds to \[k] in Akan.[^cite_note-15] *Ga* also gave its name to the [Ga districts](2227696.md#Districts) surrounding Accra.[^cite_note-\:1-16]

The spelling *Accra* was given to *Nkran* by [Europeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europeans).[^cite_note-\:1-16] An earlier spelling used by the [Danes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_colonial_empire) was *Akra.*[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Accra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Accra).



A contemporary drawing of the [Danish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danish_overseas_colonies) [Fort Christiansborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osu_Castle) (later is Osu Castle) in what later is Accra. The outpost to the right is *Fort Prøvestenen*.

### Accra Kingdom

By the 15th century, the kingdom of Accra ruled the area from the capital at [Ayawaso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayawaso_West).[^cite_note-19]

Initially, Accra was not the most prominent trading centre; the trading hubs were the ports at [Ada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ada,_Ghana) and [Prampram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prampram), along with the inland centres of [Dodowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodowa) and [Akuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akuse).[^cite_note-Traveltill-20] A Portuguese fort was destroyed by the local inhabitants in 1576; trade afterwards was conducted on the beaches.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilks195730-21] The Dutch later built the nearby [Fort Crèvecœur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ussher_Fort) while the British and the Swedes built James Fort and Christiansborg castles, respectively, in the 17th century.[^cite_note-22]

By 1646 the kingdom was a regional power, bolstered by European trade. It depended on the goodwill of the rising [Akwamu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akwamu_Kingdom), which controlled the trade routes in the interior. A dispute in 1646 escalated to war when Accra invaded Larteh.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilks195730-21] At the same time, Accra was weakened by a civil war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilks195732-23]

### Akwamu conquest of Greater Accra

Further information: [Akwamu Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akwamu_Empire) and [Old Akyem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Akyem)

In 1677 [Akwamuhene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akwamuhene) Ansa Sasraku, using the [circumcision](8718425.md) of a visiting Akwamu prince as pretext, attacked Accra. He sacked Ayawaso and beheaded the king, [Okai Koi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okai_Koi).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilks195732-23] The king's son, [Ofori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ofori_\(king\)), retreated to 'Small Accra', the town that had grown up under the walls of [Fort Crèvecœur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ussher_Fort).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilks195733-24] Ansa Sasraku's attempts to finish off the Accrans were defeated by the guns of the Danish [Fort Christianborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Christiansborg).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilks195734-25] Ofori and his people survived for years, until the Akwamu fomented a rebellion amongst the Danish garrison, and the fort was turned over to the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people). Ansa Sasraku returned in 1681, burning Osu town and Small Accra and chasing Ofori to exile in [Fetu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fetu_Kingdom).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilks195736-26] As Akwamu continued to expand, strips of land east and west were added to the Accra province.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilks195744-27]

### British power increases

Britain gradually acquired the interests of all other countries beginning in 1851, when Denmark sold [Christiansborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Christiansborg) (which they had acquired from the Swedes) and their other forts to the British. The [Netherlands](21148.md) was the last to sell out, in 1871. In 1873, after decades of tension between the British and [Ashantis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashanti_people), the British captured [Kumasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumasi), destroying portions of the city. The British then captured Accra in 1874, and in 1877, at the end of the second [Anglo-Asante War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Asante_Wars), Accra replaced [Cape Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Coast) as the capital of the British [Gold Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_Coast_\(British_colony\)). This decision was made because Accra had a drier climate relative to Cape Coast. Until this time, the settlement of Accra was confined between Ussher Fort to the east and the [Korle Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korle_Lagoon) to the west.[^cite_note-28]

As the newly established Gold Coast's administrative functions were moved to Accra (1877), an influx of British colonial administrators and European settlers grew around the Christiansborg (what later is [Osu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osu,_Accra), Ministries, Ridge, Labone, and [Cantonments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonments,_Accra)) began, and the city began to expand to accommodate the new residents. Victoriaborg was formed in the 19th century as an exclusively European residential neighbourhood, located to the east of the city limits of the time. The boundaries of Accra were further stretched in 1908, after a bubonic plague epidemic.[^cite_note-\:7-29] This expansion entailed the creation of a native-only neighbourhood, intended to accommodate members of the native population as a means of relieving congestion problems in the overcrowded city centre. [Adabraka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adabraka) was thus established to the north of the city.[^cite_note-30]

In 1918, the [Grand Orange Lodge of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Orange_Lodge_of_Ghana) was established in Accra to oversee and govern the local Ghanaian Orange Lodges. The Ghanaian [Orange Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Order) focus on [evangelism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelism), positive values and youth development.[^cite_note-31] They have [Orange parades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_parades) in the city, such as the [12th of July parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_of_July_Parade).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]



A main street of central Accra sometime between 1885 and 1908

By the 1920s, transportation between Accra and the inland cities producing exportable commodities was enhanced. This allowed cocoa to become Accra's largest export and allowed commerce to overtake government as the primary element in the city.[^cite_note-34]

Among the achievements of Guggisberg was the building of a bridge across the [Korle Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korle_Lagoon) in 1923, which increased settlement at Korle Bu, Korle Gonno and Chokor, to the west of the lagoon. Guggisberg also oversaw the building of a hospital (Korle-Bu) and secondary school (Achimota).[^cite_note-\:7-29]

### After World War II



[Jamestown Lighthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown_Lighthouse) in [Jamestown/Usshertown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamestown/Usshertown,_Accra)

In 1948, Ghana remained a colony of Great Britain following [World War II](32927.md). The chief of Osu Alata, Nii Kobina Bonney III, had set up a boycott of European goods across the country due to the rise of prices for essential commodities.[^cite_note-35] At the same time, veterans of the war were fighting for their benefits and promised pay.[^cite_note-36]



Black Star Square

After [Kwame Nkrumah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwame_Nkrumah) became Ghana's first [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Ghana) in 1957, he created his own plan for Accra's development.[^cite_note-37] Nkrumah proposed creating spaces to inspire pride and nationalism in his people and people throughout Africa.[^cite_note-38]

The [Parliament](3166296.md), [Supreme Court of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Ghana), [Black Star Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Star_Square) and the [Bank of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_of_Ghana) are located in Accra.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] The city is a transportation hub, home to [Accra International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_International_Airport), and railway links to [Tema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tema), [Sekondi-Takoradi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sekondi-Takoradi) and [Kumasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumasi). Accra has become a location for national and international business conferences, such as the BarCamp Ghana series, organised by GhanaThink Foundation.[^cite_note-42]

## Administration

The administration of Accra occurs at two levels. Strategic initiatives, such as the urban transportation project, are coordinated between district authorities.[^cite_note-49]

## Cityscape

Main article: [Neighbourhoods of Accra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighbourhoods_of_Accra)

Geographically, the areas west of Ring Road West, extending as far west as the saltponds and south of Graphic Road, are considered Accra West.[^cite_note-50]

## Demographics

The period between 1960 and 1970 saw more rapid industrialization and expansion in Accra's manufacturing and commercial sectors. This contributed to higher [rural-urban migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rural-urban_migration) to the city, and consequently a higher population growth rate.[^cite_note-51]

56% of the population of Accra is under 24 years of age. 51% of the population are females, and the remaining 49% males. This gives a males-to-females ratio of 1\:1.04. The greater number of females is a reflection of the nationwide trend, where the estimated ratio of males to females is 1\:1.03.[^cite_note-52]

## Economy

In 2008, the [World Bank](36752.md) estimated that Accra's economy constituted around [US\$](18717338.md)3 billion of Ghana's total gross domestic product (GDP).[^cite_note-53]



Accra Central, Accra, Ghana

Accra is a centre for manufacturing, marketing, finance, insurance, and transportation. Its financial sector incorporates a central bank, nine commercial banks (with 81 branches), four development banks (with 19 branches), four merchant banks (with seven branches), three discount houses, one home finance mortgage bank, [building societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_societies), [Ghana Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_Stock_Exchange), [foreign exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_exchange_market) bureaus, finance houses, insurance companies, insurance brokerage firms, two savings and loans companies, and real estate developers, with industrial sites and residential developments.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] The road network in the Accra Metropolitan Area totals 1,117 kilometres (694 mi) in length.

There are over 50,506 identified residential properties in Accra, and about 4,054 commercial/industrial/mixed properties, with a total rateable value of GH¢13,849,014.[^cite_note-ghanadistricts1-56]



Ecobank Ghana Limited

The sectors of Accra's economy consist of the primary, secondary (manufacturing, electricity, gas, water, construction) and tertiary sectors (supermarkets, shopping malls, hotel, restaurant, transportation, storage, communication, financial intermediation, real estate service, public administration, education, health and other social services). The tertiary service sector is the city's largest, employing about 531,670 people. The second-largest, the secondary sector, employs 22.34% of the labour force, or around 183,934 people. 12.2% of the city's workforce are reportedly unemployed, totalling around 114,198 people.[^cite_note-ghanadistricts1-56]

## Education

### Pre-school

Pre-school comprises nursery and kindergarten. In 2001, there were 7,923 children (3,893 girls and 4,030 boys) in [pre-schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-school) in Accra. In 2010, the enrolment rate at Pre-school was 98%.[^cite_note-UNICEF_at_a_glance:_Ghana_–_Statistics-57]

### Primary school

Primary school enrolment of girls is higher than that of boys.[^cite_note-UNICEF_at_a_glance:_Ghana_–_Statistics-57] In 2010, the enrollment rate at primary school level was 95%.[^cite_note-UNICEF_at_a_glance:_Ghana_–_Statistics-57]

### Junior high school (JHS)

In the 2001/2002 academic year, 61,080 pupils had enrolled in Accra, representing 57.17% of the 129,467 school-age 12–to-14-year-olds. In 2010, the enrolment rate at Junior high school level was 95%.[^cite_note-UNICEF_at_a_glance:_Ghana_–_Statistics-57] The ratio of girls is higher at this level.[^cite_note-UNICEF_at_a_glance:_Ghana_–_Statistics-57]

### Senior high school (SHS)



[Achimota School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achimota_School)

The transition rate between junior high and senior high school increased from 30% in 1990 to 50% in 2001. The number of students grew by 23,102 between 1990 and 2005, an increase of about 2,310 a year, since 2010 the senior high school enrolment rate has been 95%.[^cite_note-UNICEF_at_a_glance:_Ghana_–_Statistics-57]

[Ghana International School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghana_International_School) (GIS), a private non-profit [A-Level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-Level) school founded in 1955 for children from ages 3–18, is located in Accra's Cantonments. Abelemkpe is the home of [Lincoln Community School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Community_School), a private, non-profit [International Baccalaureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Baccalaureate) (IB) school for students aged 3–18, established in 1968.[^cite_note-58]

A number of public secondary schools lie on the outskirts of Accra: [Achimota School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achimota_School), also referred to as "Motown", which was founded in 1924 and opened in 1927;[^cite_note-59] the [Accra Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_Academy), known as "Accra Aca"; the [Presbyterian Boys' Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian_Boys'_Secondary_School) in [Legon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legon), also known as "Presec"; Wesley Grammar School – WESS-G Dansoman, [St. Mary's Senior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Mary's_Senior_High_School_\(Ghana\)), also referred to as "Merriez", is a girls' boarding school in Ghana; [St. Thomas Aquinas Senior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Thomas_Aquinas_Senior_High_School), also known as "Quinas"; [Accra High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_High_School) also known as "Ahisco"; [West Africa Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Africa_Secondary_School), also known as "WASS"; the [Accra Girls' Senior High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_Girls_Senior_High_School), also known as "Agiss"; [Kaneshie Secondary Technical School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaneshie_Secondary_Technical_School?action=edit\&redlink=1) ("[Kateco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kateco?action=edit\&redlink=1)"); the Armed Forces Secondary Technical; and St. John's [Grammar School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammar_School); among others.

## Environment

### Water

As a growing city, Accra has both a water supply and scarcity challenge.[^cite_note-\:5-61] Water access uses a patchwork of different delivery processes.[^cite_note-\:5-61]

### Pollution

Accra as a growing city, with vehicles and other urban pollution sources, has an increasing amount of air pollution and [plastic pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastic_pollution). Accra's urban ecosystem has been a site of dumping by international waste networks, with the [Agbogbloshie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agbogbloshie) suburb known for its [e-waste recycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-waste_recycling) sites, and the [Kantamanto Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantamanto_Market) as handling an overwhelming flow of used [fast fashion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fast_fashion) from other parts of the world.[^cite_note-marieclaire.com.au-62] Both sites are sources of pollution and trash for other parts of the urban ecosystem.[^cite_note-marieclaire.com.au-62][^cite_note-63]

Air pollution varies seasonally, and the average measure PM<sub>2.5</sub> average concentration of 49.5 μg/m<sup>3</sup>.[^cite_note-64] Live monitoring of air pollution is done at three sites in the city of Accra.[^cite_note-65] A 2021 review by the [Environmental Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Protection_Agency_\(Ghana\)) in Ghana found that the levels were exceeding national and international standards for health.[^cite_note-66]

Plastic management is also an increasing challenge.[^cite_note-67] Use of plastic water bottles [water sachets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_sachets) and other packaging for food safety, and use of plastic during events like [COVID-19](63030231.md) have increased plastic pollution.[^cite_note-\:3-68] The beaches around Accra have plastic pollution, and surrounding fisheries are impacted by plastics.[^cite_note-\:4-69] Non-profit organizations have been formed to collect the plastic and an increasingly growing network of recyclers and informal waste recovery networks.[^cite_note-\:4-69][^cite_note-\:3-68][^cite_note-70] Activists and researchers largely attribute the issue to upstream use of plastics.[^cite_note-\:4-69]

## Decongestion exercises

There's been decongestion exercises carried out by successive mayors in the city to help solve the hawking menace, flood issues and ensure road safety in the city.[^cite_note-71]

Growing population and construction within the city has been resulting in a reduction in [Urban green space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_green_space).[^cite_note-72] A 2021 study of low income communities in the city found increasing concern and desire among these communities for urban green spaces for recreation and health.[^cite_note-73] A 2018 study of greenspaces in the city, found the ones that do exist are important for mammal biodiversity in the city.[^cite_note-74]

## Transportation

[Accra International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_International_Airport) (KIA)



KIA International Terminal Building



Terminal 3 Departure Hall

Accra is on the Atlantic coast and has not served as a port city since the 1950s. Instead, the Port of [Tema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tema), about 29 km to the east along the Atlantic coast, was developed for deepwater shipping to and from Ghana as it can accommodate larger ships; the port opened in 1961.[^cite_note-75]

### Rapid transit

Accra is connected by railway line to [Kumasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumasi) and [Takoradi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takoradi).[^cite_note-76] There is a suburban railway line from [Accra Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_Central_Station) to [Tema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tema).[^cite_note-77]

### Aviation

Accra is served by [Accra International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_International_Airport), which has civil and military uses.[^cite_note-78] Located 6 miles (9.7 kilometres) from downtown Accra, the airport handles all of the city's [scheduled passenger services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scheduled_air_transport). There are plans to build a second airport to relieve the aviation pressure on the Accra International Airport. This new airport will be located at Prampram in Accra and will be constructed by China Airport Civil Construction. This plan was still under review in 2020, as additional runways at Kotoka are in consideration as an alternative to a new international airport.[^cite_note-79]

### Taxis



Taxis on Accra's High Street

Accra has a [taxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab) network and [taxi ranks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_rank), and most taxis lack a meter system, so price negotiation is required between the passenger and driver. Metered taxis operate in the city, and tend to be more expensive. Taxis in Ghana are painted in two colours: the four bumpers [fenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fender_\(vehicle\)) are yellow/orange, and the rest of the car is in a colour of the operator's choice. These taxis usually function on a shared ride model, where several passengers share one vehicle and the fare is calculated based on the distance traveled. More modern taxi services such as [Uber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uber), [Taxify](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxify), and Yango have joined the market, providing a more advanced and comfortable alternative. Some taxi drivers might charge extra for luggage or late-night trips while some also offer fixed prices and allow for advance bookings, offering a more reliable and secure option.[^cite_note-80]

### Tro tros and buses

Further information: [Tro tro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tro_tro) and [Metro Mass Transit Company Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Mass_Transit_Company_Limited)

*[Tro tros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tro_tro)* (minibuses or cargo vans modified for passenger transport) is the third biggest, and second most efficient and cost-effective way of getting around the city. The buses are typically minibuses or vans. Some models used as tro tros are Nissan Urvan 15-seaters and Mercedes Benz Sprinter or D 309 vans. Trotros are typically decorated with decals such as flags of countries or (local and international) soccer teams, popular sayings, proverbs (in English or local languages) or Bible verses. Trotros pick up and offload passengers at designated bus stops along their regular routes. Tro tros will also usually offload at undesignated locations along the route, by passenger request. Most routes commence and terminate at urban transport terminals such as the Neoplan station or the [Achimota Transport Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achimota_Transport_Terminal).[^cite_note-81]

## Culture



The [Black Star Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Star_Square) in Accra

[Tourism in Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Ghana) accounted for 1,087,000 international tourist arrivals in 2011.[^cite_note-Ghana_To_Earn_US\$8.3_Billion_From_Tourism_By_2027-82] Accra is the Greater Accra region's tourist hub, sporting hotels, monuments, museums and nightclubs. The city has three [five-star hotels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-star_hotel): the Labadi Beach Hotel, the La Palm Royal Beach Hotel and the [Movenpick Ambassador Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B6venpick_Ambassador_Hotel_Accra). The Golden Tulip Hotel and Novotel Accra, located in Accra's central business district, are ranked four stars. There are three-star hotels, including the Hotel Wangara, Hotel Shangri-La and Erata Hotel, and budget hotels. The [Accra International Conference Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accra_International_Conference_Centre) and other meeting facilities provide venues for conference tourism.[^cite_note-ouring_Ghana_–_Greater_Accra_Region-83]

In 1994, Emmanuel Aboki Essien, a high-up member within the [Grand Orange Lodge of Ghana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Orange_Lodge_of_Ghana), in Accra, was elected President of the [Imperial Orange Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Orange_Council), the governing body of the worldwide [Orange Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Order). He was the first African to hold this position, raising the profile of African Orange lodges within the [fraternity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraternity).[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] Accra hold [orange walks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_walk) to celebrate historical events, such as the [Battle of the Boyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Boyne).[^cite_note-86]

Ghana's film industry is growing. Its first documentary addressing the impacts of climate change premiered in Accra on 14 October 2022.[^cite_note-87]
