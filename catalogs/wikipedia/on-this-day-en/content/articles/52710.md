**Brazzaville** (French pronunciation: [\[bʁazavil\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)) is the [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_the_Republic_of_the_Congo) of the [Republic of the Congo](19599929.md). Administratively, it is a [department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_the_Republic_of_the_Congo) and a [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_the_Republic_of_the_Congo).[^cite_note-5] Constituting the financial and administrative centre of the country, it is located on the north side of the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River), opposite [Kinshasa](45773.md), the capital city of the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) (DR Congo).

The population of the capital is estimated to exceed 2.1 million residents, comprising more than a third of the national populace. Some 40% are employed in non-agricultural professions. During [World War II](32927.md), Brazzaville served as the *de facto* capital of [Free France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_France) between 1940 and 1942.

In 2013, Brazzaville was designated a [City of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Music_\(UNESCO\)) by [UNESCO](21786641.md); since then it has also been a member of the [Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network).[^cite_note-6]

## Toponymy

The prefix "Brazza" comes from the surname of the Italian-French explorer [Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Savorgnan_de_Brazza), who worked on exploration expeditions for [France](5843419.md) and is credited with founding the town.[^cite_note-Cybriwsky_2013-7]

The place name Brazzaville unintentionally has the literal meaning of 'city of the armed wing'.[^cite_note-Cybriwsky_2013-7] The surname Brazza refers to the village of [Brazzacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazzacco), in the commune of [Moruzzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moruzzo), [Italy](14532.md), whose name derives from the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *bracchium*, meaning 'armed wing'.[^cite_note-8]

In the [Kongo language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongo_language) it has the names or variants of *Ntamo*, *Ntambo*, *Kintamo*, *Kintambo*, *Tandala*, and *Mavula*, and in the [Teke languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teke_languages) *M'fa*, *Mfaa*, *Mfa*, and *Mfoa*.[^cite_note-Cybriwsky_2013-7][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## Geography



[Kinshasa](45773.md) seen from Brazzaville. The two capitals are separated by the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River).



View of Brazzaville from space

Brazzaville covers a large area to the north of the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River), just below the [Pool Malebo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Malebo). [Mbamu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbamu), a large island within the Pool, is part of the Republic of Congo's territory.

Brazzaville is 506 km (314 mi) inland from the Atlantic Ocean and approximately 474 km (295 mi) south of the [equator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equator). Around the city are large plains. The town is relatively flat, and situated at an altitude of 317 m (1,040 ft). Downriver the Congo has numerous rapids, known as Livingston Falls, preventing navigation upriver to this point from its mouth at the Atlantic.

[Kinshasa](45773.md), the capital of the Democratic Republic of the Congo, is located on the southern bank of the Congo, directly across from Brazzaville. To distinguish between the two African countries that have "Congo" in their names, the Republic of the Congo is sometimes called Congo-Brazzaville, as opposed to Congo-Kinshasa. Kinshasa is more than five times larger than Brazzaville in population. This is the only place in the world where two national [capital cities](181337.md) developed on opposite banks of a river, within sight of each other.[^cite_note-Guardian1-13]

In March 2018, the "Brazzaville Declaration" was signed to promote better management and conservation of the [Cuvette Centrale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuvette_Centrale), a region in [Congo Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_Basin) and primarily in DRC. It is the world's largest tropical [peatland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peatland), made up of swamp forests. Conservation of this area is important for the survival of megafauna, and also critical to the world's climate. Burning the peat would release too much carbon and raise the Earth's temperature. The declaration to save peatlands as the world's largest terrestrial organic carbon stock was signed by [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md), the [Republic of the Congo](19599929.md), and [Indonesia](14579.md), which also has peatlands.[^cite_note-14]

## History

See also: [Timeline of Brazzaville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Brazzaville)



Brazzaville Market in 1905



Brazzaville railway station in 1941

In precolonial times, the location of the modern-day city of Brazzaville was inhabited by two trading centres, [Mfwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mfwa?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Mpila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpila?action=edit\&redlink=1), which were part of the [Tio Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tio_Kingdom) (the [Eastern Teke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Teke_language)).[^cite_note-\:7-15]<sup>: 247–55 </sup>

Brazzaville was founded by the [French colonial empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire) upon an existing indigenous [Bateke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teke_people) settlement called Ncuna, during the [Scramble for Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramble_for_Africa) when European nations established spheres of influence on the continent.[^cite_note-Scramble1-16] The Italian-born explorer [Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Savorgnan_de_Brazza),[^cite_note-EB1911-17] who was granted French citizenship in 1874, officially founded the settlement on 10 September 1880; it commemorates his name.[^cite_note-Cybriwsky_2013-7][^cite_note-Scramble1-16]

The Tio king, [Iloo I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iloo_I), signed a treaty of protection with Brazza, which subjugated his lands to the [French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire).[^cite_note-Scramble1-16] From October 1880 until May 1882, a small squad of troops led by Senegalese Sergeant [Malamine Camara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malamine_Camara) occupied the site, in order to prevent the land from falling into Belgian hands. Their forces were active on the south side of the river, where King Leopold II ruled the Belgian Congo for a period as a private holding. The first large-scale building work of the city began four years later, as the French competed with [Léopoldville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%A9opoldville) (now [Kinshasa](45773.md)) which Belgian colonists were developing on the south side of the river.[^cite_note-Scramble1-16]

The [Berlin Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Conference) of 1884 placed French control over this area on an official footing. The city became the capital of the [French Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Congo) in 1904.[^cite_note-Encyc1-18] It continued as capital when [French Equatorial Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Equatorial_Africa) was founded in 1910, as a federation of French colonial states: it included [Gabon](12027.md), the [Central African Republic](5478.md), and [Chad](5488.md) until 1960.[^cite_note-Encyc1-18] From 1910 to 1915 the major municipal buildings were constructed, including a courthouse and headquarters for the Banque de l'AEF and Institut Pasteur.[^cite_note-YoulouDianzinga2006-19]

In 1934, the [Congo–Ocean Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo%E2%80%93Ocean_Railway) opened, linking Brazzaville with the Atlantic port of [Pointe-Noire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointe-Noire) and bypassing the rapids on the Congo River. Construction of the railway resulted in the deaths of more than 17,000 Africans, and the people revolted against the French in 1928.[^cite_note-20]

During World War II, Brazzaville and the rest of [French Equatorial Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Equatorial_Africa) remained beyond the control of [Vichy France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France), which served the Nazi occupation. The city served as the capital of [Free France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_France) from 1940 to 1943.[^cite_note-YoulouDianzinga2006-19] In 1944, Brazzaville hosted a meeting of the [French resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_resistance) forces and representatives of France's African colonies. The resulting [Brazzaville Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazzaville_Conference_of_1944) represented an attempt to redefine the relationship between France and its African colonies.[^cite_note-Encyc1-18]

Until the 1960s, the city was divided into European (the centre of the city) and [African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_of_Africa) sections ([Poto-Poto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poto-Poto), Bacongo, and Makélékélé). In 1980, it became a "[commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_the_Republic_of_the_Congo)" separated from the surrounding [Pool Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Department) and divided into nine "[arrondissements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrondissements)" along the French model of administration.

Since the late 20th century, the city has frequently been a staging ground for wars, including internal conflicts between rebel and government forces. It has been a base of conflicts between forces of the Republic of the Congo, the [Democratic Republic of the Congo](76762.md) (DRC), and [Angola](701.md). During the 1990s, civil wars resulted in thousands of civilian deaths here and forced hundreds of thousands of refugees to flee the city.

More recently thousands of people leaving the DRC have made their way to Brazzaville; the [local United Nations force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MONUSCO) and the DRC government have accused the city of deporting thousands of these refugees.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

In April 2016 fighting occurred between police and local militia units, with at least 18 people killed.[^cite_note-Guardian2-23]

As of the 2023 census, the city had a population of 2.15 million.[^cite_note-24]

The National Institute of Statistics for 2014 is 1.73 million. [Kinshasa](45773.md), DRC, had more than 10 million inhabitants in 2014.[^cite_note-Kinshasa-25]

Together with Kinshasa, the combined [conurbation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conurbation) of Kinshasa-Brazzaville has about 12 million inhabitants. Significant political and infrastructure challenges prevent the two cities from functioning with any meaningful connection.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

Since the mid-19th century, the two cities have been rivals in trade, sports and power.[^cite_note-Guardian1-13] There have been proposals to connect the two capitals by a [Brazzaville–Kinshasa Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazzaville%E2%80%93Kinshasa_Bridge). In 2018, with relative peace re-established in the region, the [African Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Development_Bank) and [Africa50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa50) signed a deal with both governments to develop the project.[^cite_note-28]

Brazzaville, like Pointe-Noire, is a [commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communes_of_the_Republic_of_the_Congo) (municipality) contained within a [department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_the_Republic_of_the_Congo) of the same name.[^cite_note-laws_12-14-29] It is governed by a municipal council and a departmental council. The mayor is the president of the municipal council.[^cite_note-30]

The department of Brazzaville includes the area of the commune and, since 2011, the new district of [Ile Mbamou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ile_Mbamou).[^cite_note-laws_12-14-29] In October 2024, the department absorbed the commune of [Kintélé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kint%C3%A9l%C3%A9), which was previously part of the department of [Pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pool_Department).[^cite_note-32]

## Economy



Nabemba Tower

The location of Brazzaville near the pool of the Congo River enabled it to grow as an industrial, trading and port settlement. It was connected through trade by ships and boats traveling upriver to inland areas, which produced raw materials from the beginning of the colonial period.[^cite_note-Encyc1-18] Construction of the railway connecting to [Pointe-Noire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointe-Noire) increased the ability of city businessmen to get their products to the port for export. Industries present in Brazzaville include [machine shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machining), [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile), [tanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanning_\(leather\)), and [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing). As a key port on the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River), Brazzaville still takes deliveries of [raw materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raw_material), such as [rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_rubber), [wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood), and [agricultural products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture). From here they are generally sent onward to [Pointe-Noire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointe-Noire) for export.

Many companies, government organizations and [NGOs](46539.md) have regional offices in the capital city, where they can work with government officials. The [World Health Organization](33583.md) has its regional office for Africa located in Brazzaville.[^cite_note-33] Companies headquartered in Brazzaville include the country's flag carrier [Equatorial Congo Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Congo_Airlines)[^cite_note-34] and the mobile operator [Warid Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warid_Congo).[^cite_note-35]

## Culture

[Roger Erell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Erell), a highly regarded architect, designed a house in the city for [Charles de Gaulle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle) when he was the leader of [Free France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_France) here. Other buildings include the Pierre Savorgnan de Brazza Mausoleum, the [Nabemba Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabemba_Tower), and the Congressional Palace (Brazzaville).

The [Marien Ngouabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marien_Ngouabi) Mausoleum, [Brazzaville Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazzaville_Zoo), and the Poto-Poto School of Painting are also destinations for visitors and city residents.

## Places of worship



The Sacred Heart Cathedral in 1926

Many Congolese converted to Catholicism during the French colonial period. [Christian](5211.md) churches are most prevalent in the city, where the Roman Catholic Church has an [archdiocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Brazzaville). Since then, churches have been established by new immigrants and by local adoption of evangelical Protestantism. Examples include the [Basilica of Sainte-Anne-du-Congo in Brazzaville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Sainte-Anne-du-Congo_in_Brazzaville), Greek Orthodox Archdiocese of Brazzaville and Gabon ([Patriarchate of Alexandria and All Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church_of_Alexandria)), [Evangelical Church of Congo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Church_of_Congo) ([World Communion of Reformed Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Communion_of_Reformed_Churches)), and [Assemblies of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assemblies_of_God).[^cite_note-36]

## Climate

Brazzaville features a [tropical wet and dry climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wet_and_dry_climate). Its wet season, which runs from October to May, is longer than its dry season, which covers the remaining months. Brazzaville's driest months, July and August, on average have no significant precipitation. Since Brazzaville is south of the equator, its dry season begins at around its "winter" solstice, which is the month of June. The city has relatively consistent temperatures throughout the course of the year.

## Transport



[Maya-Maya Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya-Maya_Airport)



A railroad in Brazzaville

The city is home to [Maya-Maya Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya-Maya_Airport), which lies in the centre of the city and which has regular flights to [Pointe-Noire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointe-Noire) as well as international destinations in Africa, Europe and the Middle East. A flight operates twice weekly between Brazzaville and Kinshasa, but the flight time is only five minutes.[^cite_note-Guardian1-13]

The [Congo-Ocean Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo-Ocean_Railway) has a station in the city and in 2014 was operating the [*La Gazelle*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Gazelle_train) train service every other day to [Pointe-Noire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointe-Noire) and intermediate destinations.[^cite_note-39]



Road transport in Brazzaville

The city is an important [river port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_port), with [ferries](50771.md) sailing to [Kinshasa](45773.md) and to [Bangui](56617.md) via [Impfondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impfondo).[^cite_note-Guardian1-13] Ferries and fast private boats serve as the primary means of connection between Kinshasa and Brazzaville.[^cite_note-Guardian1-13] The [Livingstone Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livingstone_Falls) lie on the outskirts of the city, where the [Djoué River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djou%C3%A9_River) meets the Congo, rendering river transport to the coast impossible, qualifying the railway as a [portage railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portage_railway).

Although there is no organised public transport system, privately owned buses are available in the capital.[^cite_note-40]

Taxis are available on every street and are easily recognized, being painted with a green body and white top, and the fare for a short trip is CF700. About twenty percent of the vehicles in Brazzaville are taxis. There are also collective taxis that drive certain routes and charge CF150.

[A road-rail bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazzaville%E2%80%93Kinshasa_Bridge) is proposed to connect Brazzaville with Kinshasa. The rail gauge on both sides is the same at 1067mm.[^cite_note-41]
