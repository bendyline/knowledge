The **Northern Territory** (abbreviated as **NT**; known formally as the **Northern Territory of Australia**[^cite_note-9] and informally as **the Territory**)[^cite_note-Territory-1][^cite_note-10] is an [Australian internal territory](4689096.md) in the central and central-northern regions of [Australia](4689264.md). The Northern Territory shares its borders with [Western Australia](33613.md) to the west ([129th meridian east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/129th_meridian_east)), [South Australia](26716.md) to the south ([26th parallel south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26th_parallel_south)), and [Queensland](59051.md) to the east ([138th meridian east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/138th_meridian_east)). To the north, the Northern Territory looks out to the [Timor Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timor_Sea), the [Arafura Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arafura_Sea), and the [Gulf of Carpentaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Carpentaria), including [Western New Guinea](1432822.md) and various other islands of the [Indonesian archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_archipelago).

The NT covers 1,347,791 square kilometres (520,385 sq mi),[^cite_note-11] making it the third-largest Australian federal division, and [the 11th-largest country subdivision in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_country_subdivisions_by_area). It is sparsely populated, with a population of only 260,400[^cite_note-ABSPop-4] as of March 2025 – fewer than half the population of [Tasmania](29944.md).[^cite_note-ABSPop-4] The largest population centre is the capital city of [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory), having about 52.6% of the Territory's population. The largest inland settlement is [Alice Springs](183935.md) with a population of about 25,000 people.

The archaeological history of the Northern Territory may have begun more than 60,000 years ago when [humans](682482.md) first settled this region of the [Sahul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahul) Continent. From at least the 18th century, [Makassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makassar_people) traders began [a relationship with the indigenous people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makassan_contact_with_Australia) of the Northern Territory around the trading of [trepang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_cucumber_\(food\)). The coast of the Territory was first seen by Europeans in the 17th century.[^cite_note-12] The British were the first Europeans to attempt to settle the coastal regions. After three failed attempts to establish a settlement (1824–1828, 1838–1849, and 1864–1866), success was achieved in 1869 with the establishment of a settlement at [Port Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory).

The economy is based largely on mining and petroleum, which during 2018–2019 contributed 23% of the [gross state product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_state_product), or \$5.68 billion, accounting for 92.4% of exports.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

The Territory's population is concentrated in coastal regions and along the [Stuart Highway](1026714.md). Besides the capital of Darwin, the major settlements are (in order of size) [Palmerston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmerston,_Northern_Territory), [Alice Springs](183935.md), [Katherine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine,_Northern_Territory), [Nhulunbuy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nhulunbuy) and [Tennant Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennant_Creek). Residents of the Northern Territory are often known as "Territorians".[^cite_note-15]

## History

Main article: [History of the Northern Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Northern_Territory)

### Pre-colonial

Further information: [Prehistory of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory_of_Australia), [History of Indigenous Australians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Indigenous_Australians), and [Makassan contact with Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makassan_contact_with_Australia)

Humans have lived in the present area of the Northern Territory since at least 48,400 to 68,700 years ago.[^cite_note-Clarkson_Jacobs_Marwick_Fullagar_2017_pp._306–310-16]

From the 17th or 18th century AD onwards, traders from [Sulawesi](28740.md) established extensive seasonal trade links with the [Indigenous](12598742.md) peoples of [Kimberley region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberley_\(Western_Australia\)), the modern-day Northern Territory, and [Arnhem Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem_Land).[^cite_note-Macknight-2011-17] They collected *trepang* ([sea cucumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_cucumber)) for Chinese markets and introduced several goods and technologies to the Indigenous peoples.[^cite_note-Macknight-2011-17] There is significant evidence of contact with [Makassan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makassar_people) fishers in examples of [Indigenous Australian rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_Australian_rock_art) and [bark painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bark_painting) of northern Australia, with the Makassan *[perahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perahu)* a prominent feature.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETaçonMayFallonTravers2010-18][^cite_note-19]

### Colonial administration

With the coming of the British, there were four early attempts to settle the harsh environment of the northern coast, of which three failed in starvation and despair. The land now occupied by the Northern Territory was part of colonial [New South Wales](21654.md) from 1825 to 1863. For a brief time from February to December 1846, it was part of the short-lived colony of [North Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Australia). The Northern Territory was part of South Australia from 1863 to 1911. Under its administration, the [Overland Telegraph Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Overland_Telegraph_Line) was constructed between 1870 and 1872.[^cite_note-20]

From its establishment in 1869, the [Port Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Darwin) was the major supply point for the Territory for many decades.[^cite_note-21]

[A railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmerston_and_Pine_Creek_Railway) was built between [Palmerston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmerston,_Northern_Territory) and [Pine Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Creek,_Northern_Territory) between 1883 and 1889 as part of the [North Australia Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Australia_Railway). The economic pattern of cattle raising and mining was established so that by 1911 there were 513,000 cattle. [Victoria River Downs Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_River_Downs_Station), 686 kilometres (426 mi) west of Darwin,[^cite_note-22] was at one time the largest [cattle station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle_station) in the world.Gold was found at Grove Hill in 1872 and at [Pine Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine_Creek,_Northern_Territory) (in 1871), Brocks Creek, Burundi, and copper was found at [Daly River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daly_River,_Northern_Territory).[^cite_note-23]

### Federal territory

On 1 January 1911, a decade after [Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federation_of_Australia), the Northern Territory was separated from South Australia, alongside the [Australian Capital Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Capital_Territory) from NSW, and transferred to federal control. [Alfred Deakin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Deakin) opined at this time *"\[t]o me the question has been not so much commercial as national, first, second, third and last. Either we must accomplish the peopling of the northern territory or submit to its transfer to some other nation."*[^cite_note-24]



The Letters Patent annexing the Northern Territory to South Australia, 1863.

In late 1912 there was growing sentiment that the name "Northern Territory" was unsatisfactory.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] The names "Kingsland" (after King [George V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_V) and to correspond with [Queensland](59051.md)), "Centralia" and "Territoria" were proposed with Kingsland becoming the preferred choice in 1913. However, the name change never went ahead.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

Between 1927 and 1931, the Northern Territory was divided into [North Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Australia_\(territory\)) and [Central Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Australia_\(territory\)) at the [20th parallel south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_parallel_south). Soon after this time, parts of the Northern Territory were considered in the [Kimberley Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberley_Plan) as a possible site for the establishment of a [Jewish Homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_for_the_Jewish_people), understandably considered the "[Unpromised Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promised_Land)".[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

In the early 20th century, the southern part of the Territory was considered the "last frontier" of Australian settlement, where 'sympathetic whites' hoped that Aboriginal traditions would continue to be practised.[^cite_note-O'Brien-31] Conflicts arose due to the resource scarcity and the fragility of the cattle industry and the area was rife with Indigenous 'bush bandits' who speared cattle for food for want of employment by ranchers. This was exacerbated by a drought between 1925 and 1929 that led to the deaths of 85 per cent of the children at the [Hermannsburg Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermannsburg,_Northern_Territory) in Central Australia. In the meantime, white attitudes towards Aboriginal people were paternalistic, torn between the desire to help them in times of hunger and the fear of "pauperizing" them and reducing their incentives to work.[^cite_note-O'Brien-31]

In the 1928 [Coniston massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coniston_massacre), punitive expeditions were carried out by white colonists led by [Northern Territory Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_Police) constable [William George Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_George_Murray) in response to the murder of a [dingo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dingo) hunter, resulting in the deaths of dozens to hundreds of people of the [Warlpiri](1002978.md), [Anmatyerre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anmatyerre), and [Kaytetye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaytetye_people) groups.[^cite_note-Bradley-32] This was one of many massacres of Aboriginal people in the region.[^cite_note-33]

#### World War II

Main article: [Japanese air raids on Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_air_raids_on_Australia)

During World War II, most of the Top End was placed under military government. This is the only time since Federation that part of an Australian state or territory has been under military control. After the war, control for the entire area was handed back to the Commonwealth. The [Bombing of Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Darwin) occurred on 19 February 1942. It was the largest single attack ever mounted by a foreign power on Australia. Evidence of Darwin's World War II history is found at a variety of preserved sites in and around the city, including ammunition bunkers, airstrips, oil tunnels and museums. The port was damaged in the 1942 Japanese air raids. It was subsequently restored.[^cite_note-34]

#### Post-World War II

In the late 1960s improved roads in adjoining States linking with the territory, port delays and rapid economic development led to uncertainty in port and regional infrastructure development. As a result of the Commission of Enquiry established by the Administrator,[^cite_note-35] port working arrangements were changed, berth investment deferred and a port masterplan prepared.[^cite_note-36] Extension of rail transport was then not considered because of low freight volumes.

[Aboriginal Australians](2912594.md) in the territory struggled for rights to land and fair wages. In 1963, the Yolngu people of [Arnhem Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem_Land) submitted two petitions, known as the [Yirrkala bark petitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yirrkala_bark_petitions), to the Australian Parliament to protest their land being taken for mining [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite) without prior consultation.[^cite_note-founding-37][^cite_note-38] Another important event in this struggle was the [strike and walk off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurindji_strike) by the [Gurindji people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurindji_people) at [Wave Hill Cattle Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalkaringi) in 1966. The Northern Territory Council for Aboriginal Rights (NTCAR) supported the strikers and provided publicity.[^cite_note-ntcar-39]

In February 1973, the Federal government of [Gough Whitlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gough_Whitlam#Prime_Minister) set up [the Woodward Royal Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Land_Rights_Commission), to enquire into how land rights might be achieved in the Northern Territory. [Justice Woodward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Woodward_\(judge\))'s first report in July 1973 recommended that a [Central Land Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Land_Council) and a [Northern Land Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Land_Council) be established to present to him the views of Aboriginal people. A Land Rights Bill was drafted, and the *[Aboriginal Land Rights (Northern Territory) Act 1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Land_Rights_Act)* was passed by the [Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Fraser) government in December 1976 and began operation in January 1977.[^cite_note-40] The Northern Territory Council for Aboriginal Rights was disestablished in 1976.[^cite_note-ntcar-39]

In 1974, from Christmas Eve to Christmas Day, Darwin was devastated by tropical [Cyclone Tracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Tracy). Cyclone Tracy killed 66 people, caused A\$837 million in damage, approximately A\$8.38 billion as of 2024,[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] and destroyed more than 70 per cent of Darwin's buildings, including 80 per cent of houses. Tracy left more than 41,000 out of the 47,000 inhabitants of the city homeless. The city was rebuilt with much-improved construction codes and is a modern, landscaped metropolis today.[^cite_note-43]

### Self-government

In 1978, the Territory was granted self-government by the *[Northern Territory (Self-Government) Act 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_\(Self-Government\)_Act_1978)*, with a Legislative Assembly headed by a [Chief Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_the_Northern_Territory).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] The [Administrator of the Northern Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_the_Northern_Territory) is another important position in the government. They act as the King's representative in the Territory and a part of their role is appointing the Chief Minister.[^cite_note-46] The Territory publishes official notices in its own *[Government Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_of_Australia_Government_Gazette)*.[^cite_note-47] In the 1980s, conservation-oriented areas in the Northern Territory such as [Kakadu National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakadu_National_Park) and [Uluru-Kata Tjuta National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uluru-Kata_Tjuta_National_Park) were inscribed on the [UNESCO World Heritage List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_World_Heritage_List) for their mix of natural heritage and Aboriginal culture.[^cite_note-48]

In 1995–96, the Northern Territory was briefly one of the few places in the world with [legal voluntary euthanasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euthanasia_in_Australia#Northern_Territory), until the Federal Parliament overturned [the legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rights_of_the_Terminally_Ill_Act_1995).[^cite_note-49] Before the over-riding legislation was enacted, four people used the law supported by Dr [Philip Nitschke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Nitschke).[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52]

## Geography

The northern coast of Australia is on the left, with [Melville Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melville_Island_\(Northern_Territory\)) in the lower right.

[^cite_note-53]

There are many very small settlements scattered across the Territory. The larger population centres are located on the single paved road that links Darwin to southern Australia, the [Stuart Highway](1026714.md), known to locals simply as "the track".

The Territory is home to many spectacular natural rock formations, including [Uluru / Ayers Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uluru) and [Kata Tjuta / The Olgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kata_Tjuta) in the Central Australian region and [Kakadu National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakadu_National_Park) in the north. Each of these sites are sacred to the local Aboriginal people and which have become major International tourist attractions and recognised [UNESCO World Heritage sites](44940.md).[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

The northern portion of the Territory is principally [tropical savannas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_grasslands,_savannas,_and_shrublands), composed of several distinct [ecoregions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ecoregions_in_Australia) – [Arnhem Land tropical savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem_Land_tropical_savanna), [Carpentaria tropical savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpentaria_tropical_savanna), [Kimberley tropical savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimberley_tropical_savanna), [Victoria Plains tropical savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Plains_tropical_savanna), and [Mitchell Grass Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitchell_Grass_Downs). The southern portion of the Territory is covered in [deserts and xeric shrublands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deserts_and_xeric_shrublands), including the [Great Sandy-Tanami desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Sandy-Tanami_desert), [Simpson Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpson_Desert), and [Central Ranges xeric scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Ranges_xeric_scrub).

There are extensive river systems in the Territory. These rivers include: the [Alligator Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alligator_Rivers),[^cite_note-56] [Daly River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daly_River_\(Northern_Territory\)), [Finke River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finke_River), [McArthur River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McArthur_River), [Roper River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roper_River),[^cite_note-57] [Todd River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_River) and [Victoria River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_River_\(Northern_Territory\)). The Hay River is a river south-west of Alice Springs, with the Marshall River, Arthur Creek, Camel Creek and Bore Creek flowing into it.[^cite_note-58]

The Northern Territory has two distinctive climate zones.

The northern end, including Darwin, has a tropical climate with high humidity and two seasons, the wet (October to April) and dry season (May to September). During the dry season nearly every day is warm and sunny, and afternoon humidity averages around 30%. There is very little rainfall between May and September. In the coolest months of June and July, the daily minimum temperature may dip as low as 14 °C (57 °F), but very rarely lower, and frost has never been recorded.

The [wet season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_monsoon) is associated with [tropical cyclones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclones) and [monsoon](57630.md) rains. The majority of rainfall occurs between December and March (the southern hemisphere summer), when [thunderstorms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunderstorms) are common and afternoon relative humidity averages over 70% during the wettest months. On average more than 1,570 mm (62 in) of rain falls in the north. Rainfall is highest in north-west coastal areas, where rainfall averages from 1,800 to 2,100 mm (71 to 83 in).

The central region is the [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert) centre of the country, which includes Alice Springs and [Uluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uluru) (Ayers Rock), and is [semi-arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid) with little rain usually falling during the hottest months from October to March. Seasons are more distinct in central Australia, with very hot summers and cool winters. Frost is recorded a few times a year. The region receives less than 250 mm (9.8 in) of rain per year.

The highest temperature recorded in the territory was 48.3 °C (118.9 °F) at [Finke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aputula) on 1 and 2 January 1960. The lowest temperature was −7.5 °C (18.5 °F) at Alice Springs on 17 July 1976.[^cite_note-59]

## Governance



The [Parliament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_House,_Darwin) building in [Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory)

### Parliament

Main articles: [Northern Territory Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_Parliament), [Northern Territory Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_Legislative_Assembly), and [Proposed Northern Territory statehood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_Northern_Territory_statehood)

The Northern Territory Parliament is one of the three [unicameral](204304.md) parliaments in the country. Based on the [Westminster System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westminster_System), it consists of the [Northern Territory Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_Legislative_Assembly) which was created in 1974, replacing the [Northern Territory Legislative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_Legislative_Council). It also produces the *[Northern Territory of Australia Government Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_of_Australia_Government_Gazette)*.

The Northern Territory Legislative Council was the partly elected governing body from 1947 until its replacement by the fully elected Northern Territory Legislative Assembly in 1974. The total enrolment for the 1947 election was 4,443. The Northern Territory was split into five electorates: Darwin, Alice Springs, Tennant Creek, Batchelor, and Stuart.While this assembly exercises powers similar to those of the parliaments of the states of Australia, it does so by legislated [devolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution) of powers from the [Commonwealth Government](1222540.md), rather than by any constitutional right. As such, the Commonwealth Government retains the right to legislate for the territory, including the power to override legislation passed by the Legislative Assembly. The [Monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Australia) is represented by the [Administrator of the Northern Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_the_Northern_Territory), who performs a role similar to that of a [state governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governors_of_the_Australian_states).

Twenty-five members of the Legislative Assembly are elected to four-year terms from single-member [electorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district).[^cite_note-61]

Numerous times since self government was granted there has been [agitation for full statehood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proposed_Northern_Territory_statehood) within the region.[^cite_note-62] A [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Northern_Territory_referendum) of voters in the Northern Territory was held on the issue in 1998, which resulted in a 'no' vote.[^cite_note-63] This was a shock to both the Northern Territory and Commonwealth governments, as opinion polls showed most Territorians supported statehood. But under the Australian Constitution, the federal government may set the terms of entry to full statehood. The Northern Territory was offered three senators, rather than the twelve guaranteed to original states (because of the difference in populations, equal numbers of Senate seats would mean a Territorian's vote for a senator would have been worth more than 30 such votes in [New South Wales](21654.md) or [Victoria](4689460.md)). Alongside what was cited as an arrogant approach adopted by then chief minister [Shane Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shane_Stone), it is believed that most Territorians, regardless of their general views on statehood, were reluctant to adopt the particular offer that was made.[^cite_note-64]

### Chief minister and cabinet

Main article: [Northern Territory Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_Government)

The [chief minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_the_Northern_Territory) is the head of government of a self-governing territory (the head of a state government is a *[premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier)*). The chief minister is appointed by the [administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_the_Northern_Territory), who in normal circumstances appoints the leader of whichever party holds the majority of seats in the Northern Territory Legislative Assembly. The current chief minister is [Lia Finocchiaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lia_Finocchiaro) of the [Country Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Liberal_Party). The CLP defeated Territory Labor to win office on 24 August 2024.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

### Administrator

The Northern Territory became self-governing on 1 July 1978 under its own [administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrator_of_the_Northern_Territory) appointed by the [Governor-General of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_Australia). The federal government, not the NT government, advises the governor-general on the appointment of the administrator, but by convention consults first with the Territory government. The current administrator is [David Connolly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Connolly_\(cattleman\)) who commenced his term on 27 February 2026.[^cite_note-68]

### Federal government



Children wave Australian flags during an [Anzac Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anzac_Day) parade in Palmerston.

The Northern Territory is represented in the [federal parliament](302298.md) by two members in the House of Representatives and two members in the Senate. As of May 2022, resulting from the [2022 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Australian_federal_election), [Marion Scrymgour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Scrymgour) from the [Australian Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party) (ALP) in [Lingiari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Lingiari) and [Luke Gosling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Gosling) from the [Australian Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party) (ALP) in [Solomon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_of_Solomon) serve in the House of Representatives, and [Malarndirri McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malarndirri_McCarthy) from the ALP and [Jacinta Nampijinpa Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacinta_Nampijinpa_Price) from the [Country Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Liberal_Party) serve in the Senate.

### Local government

The Northern Territory is divided into [seventeen local government areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_areas_of_the_Northern_Territory): two cities, three municipalities, nine regions, and three shires. Shire, city and town councils are responsible for functions delegated by the Northern Territory parliament, such as road infrastructure and waste management. Council revenue comes mostly from property taxes and government grants.[^cite_note-69]

Aboriginal Australians own about 49% of the Northern Territory's land.

[Aboriginal land councils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_land_council) in the Northern Territory are groups of Aboriginal landowners, set up under the *[Aboriginal Land Rights Act 1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aboriginal_Land_Rights_Act_1976)*.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71]

### Political parties

The two historically dominant political parties in the Northern Territory are the conservative [Country Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_Liberal_Party) which governed the Territory from 1974 to 2001, from 2012 to 2016 and since 2024, and the social-democratic [Australian Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Labor_Party_\(Northern_Territory_Branch\)) which governed the Territory from 2001 to 2012 and from 2016 to 2024. Minor parties that are also active in the NT include the [Northern Territory Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_Greens), the [Shooters and Fishers Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shooters_and_Fishers_Party) and various others. It is common for [independent politicians](2172378.md) to win elections.[^cite_note-72]

The CLP's rule was once so tight, that a former minister once said the CLP had a "'rightful inheritance of being the party that runs this place'".[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

The [2024 Northern Territory general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Northern_Territory_general_election) saw the [NT Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NT_Greens) win a seat in the Legislative Assembly, with [Kat McNamara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kat_McNamara) defeating former Chief Minister [Natasha Fyles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natasha_Fyles) in the seat of [Nightcliff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_division_of_Nightcliff); this marked the first time the Greens entered the Legislative Assembly in the history of the Northern Territory.[^cite_note-75]

As fewer parties and candidates contest Northern Territory general elections than they do Australian federal elections in the Northern Territory, the CLP, Labor and independents usually have a higher vote share at territory elections than at federal elections in the Northern Territory due to the absence of right-wing minor parties such as [Pauline Hanson's One Nation](83013.md) and the fact that the Greens do not run in every seat at territory elections.

The population of the Northern Territory at the 2011 Australian census was 211,945,[^cite_note-2011census-76] a 10 per cent increase from the 2006 census. The [Australian Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Bureau_of_Statistics) estimated a June 2015 resident population of 244,300, taking into account residents overseas or interstate. The territory's population represents 1% of the total population of Australia.[^cite_note-abs-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-ads-79]



[Darwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin,_Northern_Territory) is the capital and largest city in the Northern Territory

The Northern Territory's population is the youngest in Australia and has the largest proportion (23.2%) under 15 years of age and the smallest proportion (5.7%) aged 65 and over. The [median age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_age) of residents of the Northern Territory is 31 years, six years younger than the national median age.[^cite_note-2011census-76]



[Alice Springs](183935.md)

Indigenous Australians make up 30.3% of population[^cite_note-80] and own some 49% of the land. The life expectancy of Aboriginal Australians is well below that of non-Indigenous Australians in the Northern Territory, a fact that is mirrored elsewhere in Australia. ABS statistics suggest that Indigenous Australians die about 11 years earlier than the average non-Indigenous Australian.

There are Aboriginal communities in many parts of the territory, the largest ones being the [Pitjantjatjara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitjantjatjara_people) near [Uluru / Ayers Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uluru), the [Arrernte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrernte_people) near Alice Springs, the [Luritja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luritja) between those two, the [Warlpiri](1002978.md) further north, and the [Yolngu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yolngu) in eastern [Arnhem Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem_Land).

In 2019, 147,255[^cite_note-81] people lived in Darwin, an overwhelming majority of the Territory's population. Despite this, the Northern Territory is the least urbanised jurisdiction in the Commonwealth (followed by [Tasmania](29944.md)).[^cite_note-\:1-82]

### Languages

At the 2021 census, 57.3% of the population spoke only [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_English) at home. The other languages most commonly spoken at home were [Kriol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Kriol) (2.2%), [Djambarrpuyngu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhuwal_language) (1.7%), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) (1.4%) and [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language) (1.3%).[^cite_note-census2021-97][^cite_note-quickstats.censusdata.abs.gov.au-89][^cite_note-censusdata.abs.gov.au-90]

There are more than 100 [Aboriginal languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Aboriginal_languages) and dialects spoken in the Northern Territory,[^cite_note-98] in addition to English which is most common in cities such as Darwin or Alice Springs. Major indigenous languages spoken in the Northern Territory include [Murrinh-patha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrinh-patha_language) and Ngangikurrungurr in the northwest around [Wadeye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wadeye), [Warlpiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warlpiri_language) and [Warumungu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warumungu_language) in the centre around [Tennant Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennant_Creek), [Arrernte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Arrernte_language) around [Alice Springs](183935.md), and [Pintupi-Luritja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pintupi_dialect) to the south east.[^cite_note-\:2-99]

[Pitjantjatjara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitjantjatjara_language) is spoken in the south near [Uluru / Ayers Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uluru), [Yolngu Matha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yolngu_Matha) to the far north in [Arnhem Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem_Land) (where the dialect Djambarrpuyngu of [Dhuwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhuwal_language) is considered a lingua franca), and [Burarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burarra_language), [Maung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maung_language), [Iwaidja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iwaidja_language) and [Kunwinjku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bininj_Kunwok_language) in the centre north and on [Croker Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croker_Island) and the [Goulburn Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goulburn_Islands). [Tiwi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiwi_language) is spoken on [Melville Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melville_Island_\(Australia\)) and [Bathurst Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bathurst_Island_\(Northern_Territory\)).[^cite_note-\:2-99] Literature in many of these languages is available in the [Living Archive of Aboriginal Languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Archive_of_Aboriginal_Languages).[^cite_note-100]

In 1971, Christianity was followed by 70.2% of the population and it has been declining since. The percentage of people who identified as having no religious affiliation has increased from 17.9% in 1971, to 38.5% in 2021.[^cite_note-census2021-97]

In 2021, [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism) was the territory's largest non-Christian religion (2.7%), followed by [Buddhism](3267529.md) (2.1%) and [Islam](6037917.md) (1.4%).[^cite_note-census2021-97][^cite_note-101]

## Education



[Charles Darwin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin_University)

### Primary and secondary

A Northern Territory school education consists of six years of primary schooling, including one transition year, three years of middle schooling, and three years of secondary schooling. In the beginning of 2007, the Northern Territory introduced Middle School for Years 7–9 and High School for Years 10–12. In 2026, the territory is transitioning middle schools into high schools in order to align with the rest of the country. Northern Territory children generally begin school at age five. On completing secondary school, students earn the [Northern Territory Certificate of Education and Training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_Certificate_of_Education_and_Training) (NTCET). Students who successfully complete their secondary education also receive a tertiary entrance ranking, or ATAR score, to determine university admittance.

Northern Territory schools are either publicly or privately funded. Public schools, also known as state or government schools, are funded and run directly by the Department of Education.[^cite_note-102] Private fee-paying schools include schools run by the Catholic Church and independent schools, some elite ones similar to English [public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_school_\(UK\)). Some Northern Territory Independent schools are affiliated with [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant), [Lutheran](23371382.md), [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican), [Greek Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox) or [Seventh-day Adventist Churches](28632.md), but include non-church schools and an Indigenous school.

In 2009, the Northern Territory had 151 public schools, 15 Catholic schools and 21 independent schools. 39,492 students were enrolled in schools around the territory with 29,175 in public schools, and 9,882 in independent schools. The Northern Territory has about 4,000 full-time teachers.[^cite_note-103]

### Tertiary

The Northern Territory has [one university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Australia) which opened in 1989 under the name of the [Northern Territory University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_University).[^cite_note-104] Now renamed as the [Charles Darwin University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Darwin_University), it had about 19,000 students enrolled: about 5,500 higher education students and about 13,500 students on vocational education and training (VET) courses. The first tertiary institution in the territory was the [Batchelor Institute of Indigenous Tertiary Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batchelor_Institute_of_Indigenous_Tertiary_Education), which was established in the mid-1960s.[^cite_note-105]

### Libraries and archives

[Library & Archives NT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_&_Archives_NT) is the Northern Territory's public research library and archival organisation and it supports the development of library services across the Territory by supporting public libraries.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] Library & Archives NT was formed in 2019 following the merge of the Northern Territory Archives Service and the Northern Territory Library.[^cite_note-108] The [National Archives of Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Archives_of_Australia) also have a repository in Darwin, this is collocated with the Library & Archives NT repository at the Northern Territory Archives Centre.[^cite_note-109]

## Economy

See also: [Australian economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_economy)

### Mining



[Ranger Uranium Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranger_Uranium_Mine) in the [Kakadu National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakadu_National_Park).

The Northern Territory's economy is largely driven by [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_in_the_Northern_Territory), which is concentrated on energy producing [minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral), petroleum and energy. In 2011, mining and resources contributed around \$2.5 billion to the [gross state product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_regional_domestic_product) and employed over 4,600 people. Mining accounted for 14.9% of the gross state product in 2014–15 compared to just 7% nationally.[^cite_note-110]

In 2016, largely due to the effect of major infrastructure projects and mine expansions, construction overtook mining as the largest single industry in the territory. Construction, mining and manufacturing, and government and community services, combine to account for about half of the territory's gross state product (GSP), compared to about a third of national gross domestic product (GDP).[^cite_note-Economy-Overview-111]

The economy has grown from a value of \$15 billion in 2004–05 to over \$22 billion in 2014–15. In 2012–13 the territory economy expanded by 5.6%, over twice the level of national growth. In 2014–15 it grew by 10.5%, four times the national growth rate.[^cite_note-Economy-Overview-111]

Between 2003 and 2006 the gross state product had risen from \$8.67 billion to \$11.476 billion, an increase of 32.4%. During the three years to 2006–2007 the Northern Territory gross state product grew by an average annual rate of 5.5%. Gross state product per capita in the Northern Territory (\$72,496) is higher than any Australian state or territory and is also higher than the gross domestic product per capita for Australia (\$54,606).

The Northern Territory's exports were up 12.9% or \$681 million in 2012–13. The largest contributor to the territory's exports was: mineral fuels (largely LNG), crude materials (mainly mineral ores) and food and live animals, primarily live cattle. In 2014, the main international markets for territory exports are Japan, China, Indonesia, the United States and Korea.[^cite_note-112]

In 2007, imports to the Northern Territory totalled \$2,887.8 million which consisted of mainly machinery and equipment manufacturing (58.4%) and petroleum, coal, chemical and associated product manufacturing (17.0%).[^cite_note-ABS_Economics-113]

In 2007, the principal mining operations were bauxite at Gove Peninsula, where the production is estimated to increase 52.1% to \$254 million in 2007–08, manganese at Groote Eylandt, production is estimated to increase 10.5% to \$1.1 billion which will be helped by the newly developed mines include Bootu Creek and Frances Creek, gold which is estimated to increase 21.7 per cent to \$672 million at the Union Reefs plant and uranium at Ranger Uranium Mine.[^cite_note-114]

### Tourism

Tourism is an important economic driver for the territory and a significant industry in regional areas.[^cite_note-economicprofile.com.au-115] Iconic destinations such as [Uluru / Ayers Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uluru) and [Kakadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakadu_National_Park) make the Northern Territory a popular destination for domestic and international travellers. Diverse [landscapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscapes), [waterfalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfalls), wide open spaces, [aboriginal culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Aboriginal_culture) and wild and untamed [wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife) provides the opportunity for visitors to immerse themselves in the natural wonder that the Northern Territory offers. In 2015, the territory received a total of about 1.6 million domestic and international visitors contributing an estimated \$2.0 billion to the local economy. Holiday visitors made up the majority of total visitation (about 792,000 visitors).[^cite_note-116]

Tourism has strong links to other sectors in the economy including accommodation and food services, retail trade, recreation and culture, and transport.[^cite_note-economicprofile.com.au-115][Wide Open Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide_Open_Space_\(festival\)) is an annual festival of music, arts and culture[^cite_note-hist-117] that takes place over three days at the Ross River Resort in the [McDonnell Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McDonnell_Ranges),[^cite_note-events-118] around 80 km (50 mi) east of Alice Springs,[^cite_note-119] over three days in April/May.[^cite_note-events-118]

### Other industries

In 2021, the Northern Territory announced that it will undertake a project which will benefit its [marine industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_industry), including the development of a new Marine Industry Park near Darwin.[^cite_note-120]

## Transport

See also: [Transportation in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Australia)



The [Lasseter Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasseter_Highway) connects [Uluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uluru) (Ayers Rock) to the [Stuart Highway](1026714.md).



[The Ghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ghan).

The Northern Territory is the most sparsely populated state or territory in Australia.[^cite_note-\:1-82]

The NT has a connected network of sealed roads, including two national highways, linking with adjoining states and connecting the major territory population centres, and other important centres such as Uluru (Ayers Rock), [Kakadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kakadu_Highway) and Litchfield National Parks. The Stuart Highway, once known as "The Track", runs north to south, connecting Darwin and [Alice Springs](183935.md) to [Adelaide](1148.md). Some of the sealed roads are single-lane [bitumen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitumen). Many unsealed (dirt) roads connect the more remote settlements.

The fatigue resulting from long-distance driving and the hazards inherent in dirt roads, wildlife, water crossings and wild weather have led the Northern Territory Government to pursue road safety campaigns in English and several Aboriginal languages. Persuading people to drive at the right speed for the road conditions has been a key goal.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]

As of 2021, the Northern Territory's road vehicle speed limit in built-up areas was 60 kilometres per hour, unless the town had gazetted a lower default speed limit: many had chosen 50 km/h or lower. Outside most built-up areas the default speed limit was 110 km/h unless a speed limit sign stated otherwise. Reflecting the nature of the topography and very low population density, some sections of the Arnhem, Barkly, Stuart and Victoria highways had a maximum speed of 130 km/h.[^cite_note-123]

In 2004, a [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge) railway was opened between Alice Springs and Darwin, completing the [Adelaide–Darwin rail corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelaide%E2%80%93Darwin_rail_corridor) and bringing to fruition the dream of a transcontinental railway initiated in 1878 with the [Central Australia Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Australia_Railway) from the south and the [North Australia Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Australia_Railway) from the north – but with a gap of more than 800 km (500 mi) between Alice Springs and [Birdum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birdum,_Northern_Territory) still to be bridged when the antique 1067 mm (3 ft 6 in) narrow-gauge railway was closed in 1976.[^cite_note-Fuller-124] The line carries fast freight trains and one passenger train: [The Ghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Ghan) experiential tourism train that runs between Darwin and Adelaide, stopping in the NT at [Katherine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine,_Northern_Territory), [Tennant Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennant_Creek), [Alice Springs](183935.md) and [Kulgera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulgera,_Northern_Territory).

[Darwin International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darwin_International_Airport) is the major domestic and international airport for the territory. Several smaller airports are also scattered throughout the territory and are served by smaller airlines, including [Alice Springs Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_Springs_Airport), [Ayers Rock Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayers_Rock_Airport), [Katherine Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAAF_Base_Tindal) and [Tennant Creek Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennant_Creek_Airport).[^cite_note-125]

## Media

### Print

The Northern Territory has only one daily tabloid newspaper, [News Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Corporation_\(1980%E2%80%932013\))'s *[Northern Territory News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_News),* or *NT News*. *[The Sunday Territorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Territory_News)* is the sister paper to the *NT News* and is the only dedicated Sunday tabloid newspaper in the Northern Territory.

The *[Centralian Advocate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centralian_Advocate)* is circulated around the [Alice Springs](183935.md) region twice a week. There are also five weekly community newspapers. The territory receives the national daily, *[The Australian](396952.md)*, while *[The Sydney Morning Herald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sydney_Morning_Herald), [The Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Age)* and [*The* *Guardian Weekly*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian_Weekly) are also available in Darwin. Katherine's paper is the *[Katherine Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katherine_Times?action=edit\&redlink=1)*.

There is an LGBT community publication, *QNews Magazine*,[^cite_note-126] which is published in Darwin and Alice Springs.
