A **monolith** is a geological feature consisting of a single massive [stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_\(geology\)) or rock, such as some [mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain). [Erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) usually exposes the geological formations, which are often made of very hard and solid [igneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_rock) or [metamorphic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metamorphic_rock). Some monoliths are [volcanic plugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_plug), solidified lava filling the vent of an extinct [volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano).

In architecture, the term has considerable overlap with [megalith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalith), which is normally used for [prehistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistory), and may be used in the contexts of [rock-cut architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock-cut_architecture) that remains attached to solid rock, as in [monolithic church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monolithic_church), or for exceptionally large stones such as [obelisks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obelisk), statues, [monolithic columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monolithic_column) or large [architraves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architrave), that may have been moved a considerable distance after quarrying. It may also be used of large [glacial erratics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glacial_erratic) moved by natural forces.

The word derives, via the [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *monolithus*, from the [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) word [μονόλιθος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%BC%CE%BF%CE%BD%CF%8C%CE%BB%CE%B9%CE%B8%CE%BF%CF%82#Ancient_Greek) (*monólithos*), from [μόνος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%BC%CF%8C%CE%BD%CE%BF%CF%82#Ancient_Greek) (*mónos*) meaning "one" or "single" and [λίθος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%BB%CE%AF%CE%B8%CE%BF%CF%82#Ancient_Greek) (*líthos*) meaning "stone".
