The **Iran national football team**[^cite_note-9] have represented [Iran](14653.md) in men's international [football](10568.md) since their first international match in 1941. It is administered by the [Football Federation Islamic Republic of Iran](1262861.md) (FFIRI), the governing body of [football in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Iran), which is affiliated with [AFC](715249.md) and comes under the global jurisdiction of [FIFA](11049.md).

Regionally, Iran has won four [WAFF Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WAFF_Championship) and one [CAFA Nations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAFA_Nations_Cup). At the continental level, the country has won three [AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup) championships ([1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_AFC_Asian_Cup), [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_AFC_Asian_Cup), and [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_AFC_Asian_Cup)), three [Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_Asian_Games) ([1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1974_Asian_Games), [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1990_Asian_Games), and [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1998_Asian_Games)), and the [AFC–OFC Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC%E2%80%93OFC_Challenge_Cup) (2003).

Iran's best performance at the global level was reaching the quarterfinals at the [1976 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1976_Summer_Olympics). The team has appeared at 12 qualifiers of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) and qualified seven times ([1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup), [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup), [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), [2014](656933.md), [2018](13327177.md), [2022](17742072.md) and [2026](36581929.md)); Iran has never progressed beyond the group stages, but has won three matches: against the [United States](582488.md) in 1998, [Morocco](1024865.md) in 2018, and [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team) in 2022.

## History

### Early years



The first Iran football team that traveled to [Baku](4566.md), [USSR](746.md) in 1926

The [Iranian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Football_Federation) was founded in 1920.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] In 1926, [Tehran XI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_XI) (selected players from [Tehran Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehran_Club), [Toofan](https://fa.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D8%A8%D8%A7%D8%B4%DA%AF%D8%A7%D9%87%20%D9%81%D9%88%D8%AA%D8%A8%D8%A7%D9%84%20%D8%AA%D9%88%D9%81%D8%A7%D9%86%20%D8%AA%D9%87%D8%B1%D8%A7%D9%86), and [Armenian Sports Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Sports_Club)) traveled across the border to [Baku](4566.md), Soviet Union; this was the first away football match for an Iranian team. This Tehran Select team is the predecessor of Iran's national football team.

The first match that *Team Melli* played was on 23 August 1941, away at [Kabul](16826.md) in a 1–0 win against [British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_men's_national_football_team) while Iran's first [FIFA](11049.md) international match was on 25 August 1941, away at [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_national_football_team).[^cite_note-Iran:_Fixtures_and_Results-6]

Iran along with its Golden Generation of players such as [Parviz Ghelichkhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parviz_Ghelichkhani), [Ali Jabbari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Jabbari), [Ali Parvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Parvin), [Nasser Hejazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasser_Hejazi), [Gholamhossein Mazloumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gholamhossein_Mazloumi), and [Homayoun Behzadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homayoun_Behzadi) Won Three [AFC Asian Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Asian_Cup) in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_AFC_Asian_Cup), [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_AFC_Asian_Cup), and [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_AFC_Asian_Cup), the latter being their Most Recent Asian Cup Win. In [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_FIFA_World_Cup), Iran made its first Appearance in the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) after defeating [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_men's_National_soccer_team?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Tehran](57654.md). They lost two of three group stage matches against the [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team) and [Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peru_national_football_team),[^cite_note-12] and only earned one point by drawing[^cite_note-13] [Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_national_football_team), in a match that saw [Iraj Danaeifard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraj_Danaeifard) cancel out an own goal scored by [Andranik Eskandarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andranik_Eskandarian) for a score of 1–1.[^cite_note-autogenerated2-14]

### After the Iranian Revolution

After the [Iranian Revolution](347268.md), football was somewhat neglected. During the [1980s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_national_football_team_results_%E2%80%93_1980s), the Iranian national team did not feature in World Cup competitions due to the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) (1980–88) and domestic football embraced the inevitable effects of conflict. The national team withdrew from the Asian qualifiers for the [1982 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup) and refused to participate in the qualifiers for the [1986 FIFA World Cup](157231.md) because of having to play on neutral ground. The war and political upheavals left Iran without major club competitions until 1989 when the [Qods League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_Qods_League) was established. A year later, the Qods League was renamed the [Azadegan League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadegan_League). Despite failing to qualify for both the [1990](157232.md) and [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup) World Cups, it was said that during this period, a number of quality players burst onto the Iranian football scene laying the foundation for third place in the [1996 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_AFC_Asian_Cup) (victories in that tournament included a 3–0 victory against [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) and a 6–2 victory against [South Korea](1018627.md)) and their second stab at World Cup glory in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-15]

### 1998–2006

Further information: [1998 FIFA World Cup qualification (AFC–OFC play-off)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC%E2%80%93OFC_play-off\)) and [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup)

On 29 November 1997, Iran qualified for the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) after eliminating [Australia](4792249.md) in a close playoff series. After being tied 3–3 on aggregate, Iran advanced due to the [away goals rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Away_goals_rule); Iran held Australia to a 1–1 draw at home, and a 2–2 draw in [Melbourne](17306237.md).[^cite_note-16]

At their first game at the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) against Yugoslavia, Iran lost 1–0 to a free kick by [Siniša Mihajlović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sini%C5%A1a_Mihajlovi%C4%87).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-15] Iran recorded their first World Cup victory in the second game[^cite_note-autogenerated2-14] beating and eliminating the [United States](582488.md) 2–1 with [Hamid Estili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Estili) and [Mehdi Mahdavikia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Mahdavikia) scoring goals for Iran. The [Iran-US World Cup match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_v_Iran_\(1998_FIFA_World_Cup\)) was preheated with certain excitement because of each country's political stance after the [Iranian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_revolution) and the [Iran hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_hostage_crisis). However, in an act of defiance against all forms of hatred or politics in sports, both sides presented one another with gifts and flowers and took ceremonial pictures before the match kickoff.[^cite_note-17] Iran played against [Germany](250204.md) in the third game, losing 2–0 courtesy of goals from [Oliver Bierhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Bierhoff) and [Jürgen Klinsmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BCrgen_Klinsmann), finishing third in the group with 3 points.[^cite_note-18]

Iran finished first in the group stage of the [2000 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_AFC_Asian_Cup) but lost to [South Korea](1018627.md) in the quarter-finals. They failed to qualify for [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), held in Asia for the first time, after an aggregate defeat to the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team), losing 2–0 in [Dublin](8504.md) and winning 1–0 in [Tehran](57654.md). The elimination saw manager [Miroslav Blažević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miroslav_Bla%C5%BEevi%C4%87) step down from the top spot to be replaced by his assistant [Branko Ivanković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branko_Ivankovi%C4%87) who stepped up from assistant coach.

After qualifying to the [2004 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_AFC_Asian_Cup), Iran was drawn with [Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thailand_national_football_team), [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team) and [Japan](993546.md) in the tournament. Iran finished second in the group. In the quarter-final clash against [South Korea](1018627.md), Iran won 4–3. They then lost to host [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_football_team) on penalty kicks, and won against [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team) 4–2 to finish third place in the tournament.



Iran scores against [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team) at the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup).

On 8 June 2005, Iran and [Japan](993546.md) became the first countries other than hosts [Germany](250204.md) to qualify for the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup). The match between the two countries in Tehran, played on 24 March 2005, was the highest attended qualifying match among all confederations. The match ended with five fans dead and several others injured as they left the [Azadi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Stadium) at the end of the match.[^cite_note-19]

Iran started their 2006 FIFA World Cup appearance in Germany with a match against North America's [Mexico](808402.md) in [Group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_D). Being 1–1 at half-time, defensive mistakes led to a Mexican 3–1 win, with goals from [Omar Bravo](1271580.md) and [Sinha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinha_\(footballer\)). [Yahya Golmohammadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahya_Golmohammadi) scored the only Iranian goal. Team Melli played against [Portugal](576328.md) in the second game, losing 2–0. The goals were scored by [Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deco) and a penalty from [Cristiano Ronaldo](623737.md). Iran were eliminated from the competition before their third and final game against [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angola_national_football_team), a 1–1 draw.

### Temporary suspension

In November 2006, Iran was suspended by [FIFA](11049.md) from all participation in international football on the grounds of governmental interference in the national football association.[^cite_note-20] The ban lasted less than a month[^cite_note-21] and as a dispensation was given to allow the [Iran under-23 team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_national_under-23_football_team) to participate in the [football competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_2006_Asian_Games) of the [2006 Asian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Asian_Games),[^cite_note-22] fixtures were unaffected.

### 2007–2014

Main article: [2007 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup)

The [IRIFF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IRIFF) appointed [Amir Ghalenoei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amir_Ghalenoei) as manager of Iran on 17 July 2006 to succeed [Branko Ivanković](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Branko_Ivankovi%C4%87). After finishing first in the [2007 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_AFC_Asian_Cup) qualifying round two points ahead of [South Korea](1018627.md), Iran placed first in the group stage of the final tournament in Malaysia, eventually losing to their qualifying opponents in a penalty shoot-out in the quarterfinals. His contract was not renewed and Team Melli was left with a caretaker manager for several months.

Then-leading international goalscorer [Ali Daei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Daei) was chosen to become the new manager after Spanish coach [Javier Clemente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Clemente) had been closer to signing on as Iran's national team manager, when talks collapsed when he refused to live full-time in the country.[^cite_note-23] Iran was placed with [Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuwait_national_football_team), [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syria_national_football_team) and [United Arab Emirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team) in the third round of [2010 FIFA World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)). Ali Daei resigned from his position as the Iranian national coach on 29 March 2009,[^cite_note-24] replaced by [Afshin Ghotbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afshin_Ghotbi). Iran failed to qualify for the 2010 World Cup after finishing fourth overall in its group.[^cite_note-25]

During the final game of the [2010 FIFA World Cup qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round) against [South Korea](1018627.md) in [Seoul](19159283.md) on 17 June 2009, seven members of the team wore green wristbands in support of the [Iranian Green Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Green_Movement) during the [2009 Iranian election protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Iranian_election_protests). Initial rumors and false reports were that all seven players were banned for life by the [Iranian Football Federation](1262861.md), however, state-run media claimed that all seven had "retired".[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-theguardian-27] On 24 June 2009, FIFA wrote to Iran's Football Federation asking for clarification on the situation. The Iranian Football Federation replied that no disciplinary action has been taken against any player.[^cite_note-28]

[Afshin Ghotbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afshin_Ghotbi) renewed his contract until the end of [2011 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup) and the team qualified for the tournament with 13 points as group winners. During the final [qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round) match against [South Korea](1018627.md), several Iranian players started the match wearing green [armbands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armband) or [wristbands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wristband), a symbol of [protest at the outcome of the Iranian presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Iranian_election_protests).[^cite_note-29] Most removed them at half-time. The newspaper *Iran* reported that [Ali Karimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Karimi), [Mehdi Mahdavikia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Mahdavikia), [Hosein Kaebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hosein_Kaebi) and [Vahid Hashemian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vahid_Hashemian) had received life bans from the [Iranian FA](1262861.md) for the gesture.[^cite_note-30] However, the Iranian FA denied this claim in a response to FIFA's inquiry saying that "the comments in foreign media are nothing but lies and a mischievous act."[^cite_note-31] Head coach [Afshin Ghotbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afshin_Ghotbi) also confirmed that it was a rumour and the Iranian FA "has not taken any official stand on this issue."[^cite_note-32]

[Afshin Ghotbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afshin_Ghotbi) qualified Iran for the 2011 Asian Cup and finished second in the [2010 WAFF Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_WAFF_Championship) just a few months before the Asian Cup. Iran were able to gain all nine possible points in the group stage of the Asian Cup but after an extra time goal from South Korea, Iran were yet again eliminated from the quarter-finals.

On 4 April 2011, former [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid) manager [Carlos Queiroz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Queiroz) agreed to a two-and-a-half-year deal to coach the Iranian national team until the end of the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) in Brazil.[^cite_note-Goal-33] Under Queiroz, Iran began their [2014 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)) campaign successfully, defeating the [Maldives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maldives_national_football_team) 4–0 in the first leg of their [second round of qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round). After winning 5–0 on aggregate, Iran advanced to the [third round of qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round) where they were drawn with [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_national_football_team), [Qatar](1111350.md) and [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team).



Iran's squad in July 2011 against Maldives. Manager: [Carlos Queiroz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Queiroz). Standing left to right: [Aghili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadi_Aghili), [Hosseini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalal_Hosseini), [Haddadifar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghasem_Haddadifar), [Ansarifard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Ansarifard), [Zare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maziar_Zare), [Pooladi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehrdad_Pooladi); sitting left to right: [Heydari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khosro_Heydari), [Norouzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadi_Norouzi), [Rahmati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Rahmati), [Khalatbari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Reza_Khalatbari_\(footballer,_born_1983\)), [Karimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Karimi).

Iran topped [their group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round#Group_E) by defeating Bahrain 6–0 at home in the [Azadi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Stadium) as well as inviting former [German youth international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_national_youth_football_team) [Ashkan Dejagah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkan_Dejagah) who scored twice on his debut against Qatar. After a 4–1 win at Indonesia, Iran qualified for the final round of direct qualifiers, the [fourth round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round). In the fourth round, Iran played South Korea, [Qatar](1111350.md), [Uzbekistan](1032413.md) and [Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_national_football_team) in [their group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_fourth_round#Group_A). Queiroz made new [foreign-based](9573713.md) additions to his squad, adding players such as [Reza Ghoochannejhad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Ghoochannejhad).

Iran started the fourth round of Asian qualifiers with a 1–0 win in Uzbekistan. *Team Melli* then drew Qatar and lost in Lebanon before defeating South Korea at the Azadi on 16 October with a goal from captain [Javad Nekounam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javad_Nekounam). After a 1–0 loss in Tehran against Uzbekistan, Iran defeated Qatar 1–0 in [Doha](26214389.md) and Lebanon 4–0 at home. In their last qualification match, Iran defeated South Korea 1–0 in [Ulsan Munsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_Munsu_Football_Stadium) with a goal from Ghoochannejhad, clinching their ticket to Brazil as group winners with 16 points. Thus, Iran became the third team that Queiroz has managed to qualify for the World Cup, having reached the [2002 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup) with [South Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Africa_national_football_team) and the [2010 edition](168079.md) with [Portugal](576328.md), leading the latter to a knockout stage finish. Iran continued their winning streak, securing [qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_qualification) to the [2015 Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup) months later as well.[^cite_note-34]



Iran v [Argentina](454699.md) at the [2014 World Cup](656933.md) in Brazil

Since Queiroz's role as manager of the Iranian national team, he has introduced players from the [Iranian diaspora](9573713.md) to the national squad. These players include [German-Iranians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranians_in_Germany) [Daniel Davari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Davari) and [Ashkan Dejagah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkan_Dejagah), [Dutch-Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranians_in_the_Netherlands) [Reza Ghoochannejhad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Ghoochannejhad), [Swedish-Iranians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Iranians) [Omid Nazari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omid_Nazari) and [Saman Ghoddos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saman_Ghoddos), and [Iranian-American](2234641.md) [Steven Beitashour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Beitashour) among others.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-guardianmagath-36]

Iran competed in [Group F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_F) alongside [Argentina](454699.md), [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) and [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md). Prior to the tournament, they founded the [Central Asian Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Asian_Football_Association).[^cite_note-37]

In the opening match of the tournament on 16 June, Iran drew [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) 0–0 making it their first clean sheet in the World Cup.[^cite_note-38] In their next match, Iran was [defeated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2014_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies#Argentina_vs_Iran) by [Argentina](454699.md) 1–0 with a late goal from [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) and received praise after holding Argentina for 90 minutes while creating some attacking opportunities of their own.[^cite_note-39] Iran was eliminated from the tournament in their next game, a 3–1 defeat to [Bosnia and Herzegovina](1008974.md). Iran's lone goal was scored by [Reza Ghoochannejhad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Ghoochannejhad).[^cite_note-40] After the tournament, Queiroz declared he would resign as manager of Iran but later switched and extended his contract until the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md).[^cite_note-41]

### Late 2010s

Iran qualified for the [2015 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup) as [group winners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_qualification#Group_B) where Team Melli were the highest [ranked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings) [seed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seed_\(sports\)).[^cite_note-42] Iran faced [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team), [Qatar](1111350.md) and the [UAE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates_national_football_team) in [Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_Group_C). With the second highest number of fans in the tournament after hosts [Australia](4792249.md), the Iranians defeated Bahrain 2–0 with limited preparations.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] A defensive-minded Iran then defeated Qatar 1–0 thanks to a [Sardar Azmoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Azmoun) goal before defeating UAE by the same scoreline to reach the top of their group.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] In the [quarter-finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage#Iran_vs_Iraq) Iran faced [Iraq](1039652.md) who they had beaten weeks prior in a [friendly match](977090.md#International_football). Having received a controversial [red card](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_card_\(sport\)) in the first half, Iran competed with ten men, managing to score goals late in [extra time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtime_\(association_football\)) to draw the match 3–3. In the ensuing [penalty shootout](511302.md), Iran lost 7–6.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]



Iran's squad against [Portugal](576328.md) in [Mordovia Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordovia_Arena) at the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md)

Iran began their [2018 FIFA World Cup qualification campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)) with [friendly matches](977090.md#International_football) against [Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_national_football_team) and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_football_team) in March 2015.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Queiroz resigned from his managerial post thereafter due to disagreements with the [Iranian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Football_Federation).[^cite_note-52] On 14 April 2015, Iran were drawn with [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team), [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_men's_national_football_team), [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmenistan_national_football_team) and [Guam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam_national_football_team) in the [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round#Group_D) of qualifiers.[^cite_note-53] On 26 April, Queiroz announced that he would continue as the manager of Iran for their 2018 World Cup campaign. Iran became the second team to qualify for the [2018 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_World_Cup)[^cite_note-54] after a 2–0 win at home over [Uzbekistan](1032413.md) on 12 June 2017.[^cite_note-55] They also clinched first place in their qualification [group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round#Group_A) after South Korea's defeat to [Qatar](1111350.md).[^cite_note-56]

Iran won the first match against [Morocco](1024865.md) after [Aziz Bouhaddouz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aziz_Bouhaddouz) scoring an own goal.[^cite_note-57] They then lost against [Spain](609825.md) with a goal scored by [Diego Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Costa), with [video assistant referees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_assistant_referee) denying an Iranian equalizer.[^cite_note-58] The third match against [Portugal](576328.md) ended in a draw after a [penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_\(association_football\)) scored by [Karim Ansarifard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karim_Ansarifard) in the stoppage time, while [Cristiano Ronaldo](623737.md) missed a penalty when Iran was trailing, with [Alireza Beiranvand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alireza_Beiranvand) making a decisive save[^cite_note-59] and because Morocco could only manage a 2–2 draw to Spain, Iran were eliminated.[^cite_note-60] Iran's four points is the most received in any World Cup appearance.

Having qualified for the [2019 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup) earlier, Iran was drawn into [group D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_AFC_Asian_Cup_Group_D), where they shared fate with [Iraq](1039652.md), [Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnam_national_football_team) and [Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yemen_national_football_team). Iran opened their tournament against Yemen, nearly conceding a goal early but after 10 minutes, Iran regained the ground and proved more dominant in certain aspects, thrashing Yemen 5–0.[^cite_note-61] A 2–0 win over the Vietnamese side gave Iran direct qualification to the knockout stage.[^cite_note-62] Iran ended their group stage with a goalless draw over neighbor Iraq and took first place.[^cite_note-63] After the group stage, Iran encountered [Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oman_national_football_team), with a defending mistake almost costing Iran's chances but [Ahmed Mubarak Al-Mahaijri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Mubarak_Al-Mahaijri)'s penalty was saved by [Alireza Beiranvand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alireza_Beiranvand). ### Early 2020s

Main articles: [2022 FIFA World Cup qualification (AFC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)), [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md), and [2022 FIFA World Cup controversies § Iranian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies#Iranian_protests)



Iran's squad against [England](9904.md) at the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md)

Iran was the highest-ranked team to be seeded in the [2022 FIFA World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC\)), being drawn into the [second round](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_second_round) where they would deal with two Arab countries, Iraq and [Bahrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_national_football_team), along with [Cambodia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodia_national_football_team) and [Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Kong_national_football_team). Iran, under new Belgian manager [Marc Wilmots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marc_Wilmots), began with a 2–0 away win over Hong Kong. After the [death of Sahar Khodayari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Sahar_Khodayari), the 14–0 win over Cambodia allowed select Iranian women to enter the stadium for the first time since the [1979 Islamic Revolution](347268.md).[^cite_note-reuters-40-yrs-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] Their next away encounters against Bahrain and Iraq went on to be consecutive defeats which Iran lost 0–1 and 1–2, respectively.[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] Following two consecutive draws between Iraq and Bahrain, Iran was left with the possibility of being eliminated from the World Cup outright in the second round, leading to the sacking of Marc Wilmots.[^cite_note-72]

The [COVID-19 pandemic in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Iran) forced the remaining fixtures to be played without spectators in June 2021, forcing Iran to play their remaining games in Bahrain under new coach [Dragan Skočić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragan_Sko%C4%8Di%C4%87); but with Bahrain losing its home support as an advantage, and Iraq assured a place in the [final phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round), Iran was able to stage a comeback, occupying first place and, together with Iraq, progressed to the final phase.[^cite_note-73]

Iran became the thirteenth team to qualify for the [2022 FIFA World Cup](17742072.md) after a 1–0 win at home over Iraq on 27 January 2022. In March 2022, Iranian women were again banned from entering the stadium for a World Cup qualifier.[^cite_note-74] Iran's World Cup preparation friendly match against [Canada](723688.md) at [BC Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Place) in June 2022 was cancelled by the [Canadian Soccer Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Soccer_Association) amid opposition and mounting criticism, namely in regards to the Iranian government's role in shooting down [Ukraine International Airlines Flight 752](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_International_Airlines_Flight_752).[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

By the start of the World Cup in Qatar, Iran was the highest ranked team from Asia. In October 2022, calls were made to ban the Iranian national team from the World Cup for the Iranian government's [blocking of Iranian women from their stadiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_segregation_in_Iran#Sport_events), their supplying of weapons to Russia during the [invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), and the treatment towards protesters during the [Mahsa Amini protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahsa_Amini_protests).[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] However, Iran started its World Cup campaign with a nightmare, losing 2–6 to [England](9904.md) in the opening game as Iran suffered the worst ever loss in its FIFA World Cup history.[^cite_note-80] The Iranian team notably made headlines in their opening match against England after refusing to sing the [national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Anthem_of_the_Islamic_Republic_of_Iran) in solidarity with the [Mahsa Amini protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahsa_Amini_protests), with some Iranian supporters cheering against their own team or [boycotting their team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies#Iranian_protests) amidst the ongoing protests as they felt the team was representing the government.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

In the following match against [Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_national_football_team), amidst the boos and whistles from some of the Iranian supporters during the playing of the national anthem, the Iranian players were filmed singing the national anthem before defeating Wales 2–0 for their first-ever win over a European nation at the World Cup, with some protestors having their pre-revolutionary [Lion and Sun](8702401.md) [flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Iran) and [Women, Life, Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman,_Life,_Freedom) banners snatched from them by pro-government fans and stadium security at the [Ahmad bin Ali Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_bin_Ali_Stadium).[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] Protesters were harassed by government supporters with some protesters being detained by Qatari police, while stadium security confirmed they were given orders to confiscate anything but the flag of the Islamic Republic of Iran.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] Documents obtained by [Iran International](58555280.md) showed Iran was coordinating secret efforts with Qatar to control who attends the World Cup and restrict any signs of dissent.[^cite_note-91]

Ahead of Iran's final group stage match against the [United States](582488.md), the Iranian players were allegedly called in to a meeting with members of the [IRGC](1285472.md) and were threatened with violence and torture for their families if they did not sing the national anthem or joined the protests against the Iranian regime.[^cite_note-92] During the match, the Iranian players sang the national anthem again before losing to the United States 1–0 for the first time in their history and thereby being knocked out of the tournament, being the only team to finish third in the group with three points instead of four.[^cite_note-93] Many Iranians celebrated the defeat and one Iranian man was killed by security forces in [Bandar-e Anzali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Anzali) after honking his car horn in celebration.[^cite_note-94] Another Iranian fan was also arrested by Qatari police after he was wearing a shirt with the [Woman, Life, Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woman,_Life,_Freedom) slogan.[^cite_note-95]

During the FIFA World Cup as well as the [2023 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_AFC_Asian_Cup), the Iranian government reportedly sent paid members of the [Basij](2892082.md) and [IRGC](1285472.md) to cheer for the team in the stands.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] Iran were eventually knocked out in the semi-final of the Asian Cup by hosts [Qatar](1111350.md).[^cite_note-99] After the game, Iranian officials reportedly arrested ten teenagers in the city of [Javanrud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanrud) for celebrating the team's elimination from the tournament.[^cite_note-100]

#### 2026 FIFA World Cup Qualification

Iran qualified for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) on 25 March 2025 following a 2–2 home draw against [Uzbekistan](1032413.md) in the [third round of AFC qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_AFC_third_round).[^cite_note-101]

#### World Cup Draw and Pride Match Controversy

They were placed in pot 2 for the [World Cup draw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_FIFA_World_Cup_seeding) and were subsequently drawn into [Group G](81660197.md) alongside [Belgium](679716.md), [Egypt](1022953.md), and [New Zealand](1023035.md).[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103]

Prior to the draw, a group stage match scheduled to be played in Seattle was designated as a "[Pride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pride_\(LGBTQ_culture\)) Match" by the City of [Seattle](11388236.md) to coincide with the city's [annual Pride celebrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seattle_Pride) and to promote [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) inclusion.[^cite_note-104] Following the draw, the fixture was confirmed to be [the match between Iran and Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt_vs_Iran_at_the_2026_FIFA_World_Cup). Both Iran and Egypt are countries where homosexuality is criminalized under domestic law, with [Iran in particular having imposed capital punishment in certain cases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBTQ_history_in_Iran).[^cite_note-105] The [Iranian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Football_Federation) and the [Egyptian Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Football_Association) objected to the match's designation; however, [FIFA](11049.md) and local organizers confirmed that associated LGBT events would proceed, and that [rainbow flags](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_flag_\(LGBTQ\)) would be permitted inside the stadium.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

#### 2025–26 Iranian Protests and Impact on Football

In January 2026, multiple Iranian footballers and athletes were killed by the Iranian regime during the [2026 Iran massacres](82036668.md) amid the [2025–2026 Iranian protests](81976180.md), including former [Tractor Sazi F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tractor_Sazi_F.C.) midfielder [Mojtaba Tarshiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mojtaba_Tarshiz), prompting former Iran national team captain [Masoud Shojaei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masoud_Shojaei) to criticise FIFA for its silence over the killing of Iranian athletes during protests, while then-captain [Mehdi Taremi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Taremi) expressed solidarity with the Iranian people.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110]

Following the massacres, activists called on FIFA to ban the Iranian national team from the 2026 FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] [Ali Karimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Karimi), a former footballer for the Iran national team, along with a coalition of prominent Iranians, wrote an open letter to FIFA and all its member associations, calling on FIFA president [Gianni Infantino](9286117.md) to speak up on the protest deaths.[^cite_note-113] Following the protests and ensuing crackdown, the [Spanish Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation) reportedly pulled out of a planned friendly match with Iran.[^cite_note-114]

Amid the [Iran war](82533582.md), [Sardar Azmoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sardar_Azmoun) was reportedly expelled from the national team after posting a picture of himself with the ruler of the UAE on social media.[^cite_note-115] On 30 April 2026, [protests](82164645.md) were held outside the 2026 [FIFA Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Congress) in [Vancouver](32706.md), calling on FIFA to ban the Iranian team and stating that it represents and recruits players from the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) instead of the people of Iran.[^cite_note-116]

On 19 May, it was reported that FIFA would again [ban](83181783.md#Protests_and_massacres_in_Iran) the pre-revolutionary [Lion and Sun flag](82020140.md) from World Cup stadiums, [similar to previous World Cups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_2022_FIFA_World_Cup_controversies#Iranian_protests).[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] On 25 May, President [Claudia Sheinbaum](57813311.md) announced that Mexico will host the team for the World Cup after Trump administration refused to host the team in the United States during [its war against Iran](82533582.md).[^cite_note-119]

#### US visa disputes

According to reports, several members of the support staff for the Iranian national football team were denied entry visas to the United States, leading to a diplomatic dispute just days before the team was scheduled to compete in the 2026 FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-120] The Iranian embassy in [Turkey](11125639.md) issued a statement claiming that visas were refused for "a large part of the management and executive staff, technical advisors, and others who are an integral part of any national football team," arguing that the United States government was "effectively depriving the Iranian national team of its right to play in the World Cup under normal conditions and without undue pressure and stress."

Among those reportedly excluded from receiving visas was [Mehdi Taj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Taj), the head of the Football Federation Islamic Republic of Iran (FFIRI). The [Tasnim news agency](42800472.md) reported that three additional FFIRI officials—executive director Mehdi Kharati, secretary general Hedayat Mombini, and media director Mohsen Motamedkia—had also not received visas, and were expected to travel with the team to Mexico while efforts to secure them continued. In response, the [FFIRI](1262861.md) issued a statement asserting that the conduct of the United States, as a co-host of the tournament, "contradicts international sporting rules," and that "the US government, continuing its hostile actions against the Iranian national team, has taken an unsportsmanlike and purely political decision to deny visas to key management and administrative members of the Iranian national football team." The federation also called on FIFA, as the world governing body of football, to pursue and finalize visa arrangements for the team's managerial, executive, technical, and support staff.[^cite_note-121]

Meanwhile, Iran's ambassador to Mexico, Abolfazl Pasandideh, told reporters that the squad had been informed that under the terms of the visas issued, the team must enter and leave United States territory on the same day as its matches, adding: "We can enter in the morning and we must leave the same day."[^cite_note-122]

### 2026 World Cup

Despite the visa dispute and training in Mexico, the Iranian national team flew to the US for its first two games in the tournament. In the first game Iran came twice from behind to draw 2-2 against New Zealand, with R. Rezaeian M. Mohebi scoring for Iran.[^cite_note-123] Mohammad Mohebi's goal celebration sparked controversy after he appeared to mimic a finger-gun gesture while celebrating. The match was already politically charged, with pre-revolutionary Iranian flags visible in the crowd despite FIFA's restrictions. The incident added another layer of debate, as social media users widely discussed the meaning and appropriateness of the celebration. Mohebi later denied any political intent, stating that it was simply a celebratory gesture and nothing more.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] In its second game Iran drew 0-0 against Belgium.[^cite_note-126] It was reported that the team left a handwritten note in the dressing room of [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) after the game calling for peace: "may peace, respect and friendship prevail among all nations".[^cite_note-127] They left a similar message in the dressing room of [Lumen Field](206812.md) after their third and final game of the tournament against Egypt which ended 1-1.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129] Despite not qualifying for the knockout round, this was the first time Iran finished an iteration of the World Cup without a loss and became the only undefeated team to not advance to the knockout stage.[^cite_note-\:0-130][^cite_note-\:1-131][^cite_note-\:2-132]

### Nicknames

The Iranian national team has received several nicknames by supporters and media. The most common one used is *Team Melli* (meaning "National Team" in [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language)). Although the Iranian supporters have popularized *Team Melli*,[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-Veo_2014-134] other nicknames for the team include *Shiran e Iran*, ("[Lions of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_lion)"),[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136][^cite_note-varzesh11-137] and "Princes of Persia"[^cite_note-espnfc-138][^cite_note-irandaily-139] (used since [AFC Asian Cup 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_AFC_Asian_Cup)).[^cite_note-140] Iran's slogan for the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) was *Honour of Persia*, selected in an internet poll held by [FIFA](11049.md).[^cite_note-honourofpersia-141][^cite_note-fifa.slogans2014-142] A more recently used nickname, due to the presence of the [Asiatic cheetah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatic_cheetah) on the 2014 World Cup jersey, is *Yuzpalangan* which means "The Cheetahs";[^cite_note-PersianFootball.com-143] the team mascot "Yupa" is also a cheetah,[^cite_note-144] although this has been seen as a government propaganda ploy to wane people away from the lion nickname due to the pre-revolutionary [Lion and Sun](8702401.md).[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146]

### Rivalries

Main articles: [Iran–Iraq football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_football_rivalry) and [Iran–Saudi Arabia football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Saudi_Arabia_football_rivalry)

Iran and [Iraq](1039652.md) are neighboring rivals.[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] According to the *[Malay Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Mail)*, "Emotions are always high when Iran and Iraq meet on the football pitch". In the contemporary era, especially during the reign of [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein), the two countries had worsened relations and fought the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) for 8 years.[^cite_note-ESPN-154][^cite_note-CNN-155][^cite_note-Iran_Daily-156][^cite_note-157][^cite_note-BBC-158] In 2001, for the first time in decades, an Iran-Iraq match was not held at a neutral venue.[^cite_note-Iran_Daily-156] The rivalry was escalated after Iraq knocked Iran out of the [2015 AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup) in controversial circumstances.[^cite_note-ABC-159][^cite_note-160] Iran leads the series with 18 wins, 7 draws and 6 losses.

Iran and [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_national_football_team) are ideological rivals.[^cite_note-FIFA-161] The game was ranked eighth in *[Goal.com](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal.com)'s* 2010 list of "Football's 10 Greatest International Rivalries".[^cite_note-Goal.com-162] and ninth in *[Bleacher Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleacher_Report)'s* 2014 list of "International Football's 10 Most Politically-Charged Football Rivalries"[^cite_note-Bleacher_Report-163] All of their matches have been competitive; none of them were [friendlies](977090.md). The first match was played on 24 August 1975, with Iran defeating Saudi Arabia 3–0. Iran leads the series with 5 wins, 6 draws and 4 losses.

### Stadiums

Main article: [Iran national football team records § Home record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_national_football_team_records#Home_record)



[Azadi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Stadium), 1991

Since 1972, Iran's national stadium has been [Tehran](57654.md)'s [Azadi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadi_Stadium) with a nominal capacity of 78,116 spectators.[^cite_note-164] Azadi Stadium is the [28th largest association football stadium in the world, seventh in Asia and first in West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_association_football_stadia_by_capacity). A record was set in Azadi for the [1998 FIFA World Cup qualifier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_\(AFC%E2%80%93OFC_play-off\)) against [Australia](4792249.md) with over 128,000 in attendance.[^cite_note-fifa97-165] Since [1979](347268.md), the government [restricts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_revolution) [Iranian women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_Iran) from entering the stadiums.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167] However, FIFA condemned the move and wrote to Iranian Football Federation in June 2019. The federation in their letter gave deadline to uplift the restrictions and let women enter the stadiums. On 25 August 2019, deputy Sports Minister Jamshid Taghizadeh stated: "Women can go to Tehran's Azadi stadium to watch the match between Iran's national team and Cambodia in October for the Qatar World Cup qualifier."[^cite_note-168] After the [death of Sahar Khodayari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Sahar_Khodayari), select Iranian women were allowed to attend the match against Cambodia in October 2019.[^cite_note-reuters-40-yrs-67] However, in March 2022, Iranian women were again blocked from entering the stadium for a FIFA World Cup qualifier.[^cite_note-169]

From 1942 to 1972, [Amjadieh Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amjadieh_Stadium) was Iran's national stadium. Other stadiums that Iran has played international games at are [Bagh Shomal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takhti_Stadium_\(Tabriz\)) and [Yadegar Emam Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yadegar-e-Emam_Stadium_\(Tabriz\)) ([Tabriz](439791.md)), [Takhti Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takhti_Stadium_\(Tehran\)) ([Tehran](57654.md)), [Enghelab Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enghelab_Stadium) ([Karaj](978698.md)), [Hafezieh Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafezieh_Stadium) ([Shiraz](39622.md)), [Takhti Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takhti_Stadium_\(Mashhad\)) and [Imam Reza Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imam_Reza_Stadium) ([Mashhad](406512.md)).

The [Iran National Football Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_National_Football_Camp) is the team's training ground.
