# Terrorism in the Philippines



Wanted poster in [Lapu-Lapu City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapu-Lapu_City) displaying the photos and names of local terrorist suspects.

[Terrorism](30636.md) is a major [social issue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_issue) in the [Philippines](23440.md) linked to the [Moro conflict](4602307.md) and the [communist rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_armed_conflicts_in_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-1] The country ranks in 23rd place on the [Global Terrorism Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Terrorism_Index)'s 2026 list of countries most affected by terrorism.[^cite_note-2]

## Overview

Since the late 1960s, terrorism has become a major problem in the Philippines.[^cite_note-3] These terrorist attacks are often carried out by several [Jihadist](7826589.md) and militants groups with different ideologies and motives.[^cite_note-4] Such acts of terrorism include [bombings](47628.md), [domestic terrorism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_terrorism), [kidnapping](169376.md), [drug trafficking](218449.md), [extortion](162449.md), [mass murder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_murder), [assassination](2963.md) and other types of attack. Terrorist groups like [Abu Sayyaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Sayyaf), the [Bangsamoro Islamic Freedom Fighters](39963003.md) and [Jemaah Islamiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jemaah_Islamiyah) mostly operate in the Southern Philippines.[^cite_note-5] The [New People's Army](1262096.md) maintains a wider range of operations across the country and launches attacks albeit on a lesser intensity than other rebel movements.

Following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), the Philippines became a new front on the American-led [war on terror](13425800.md)[^cite_note-6] During [George W. Bush](3414021.md)’s presidency, the U.S sent foreign aid and American troops in Mindanao to assist in counterinsurgency operations as part of [Operation Enduring Freedom in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom_%E2%80%93_Philippines). The [Siege of Marawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Marawi) ended in the deaths of several terrorist leaders and reduction of [Islamic State](9087364.md) presence in the Philippines.

## Legal aspects

According to Philippine laws, terrorism is a crime under the [Human Security Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Security_Act) of 2007 which describes such acts as causing "widespread and extraordinary fear and panic among the populace". The first group to be officially listed as a terrorist organization under the law was the [Abu Sayyaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Sayyaf) on September 10, 2015, by the [Basilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilan_province) provincial court.[^cite_note-ph_asg-11] The law's weaknesses was cited by Senator [Panfilo Lacson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panfilo_Lacson), who called the law a "dead letter law" for being "severely underutilized".[^cite_note-12]

On 3 July 2020, President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) signed into law Republic Act 11479, better known as the [Anti-Terrorism Act of 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Terrorism_Act_of_2020). This replaced the Human Security Act and revised and expanded the definition of terror offenses.[^cite_note-Amnesty-13] Rights groups criticized the law, calling it "draconian", and filed a case on its constitutionality to the [Supreme Court](1335534.md); the Court upheld the law on 26 April 2022[^cite_note-14] but struck down provisions allowing the anti-terror council (ATC) to designate a person or a group as terrorists based on a request by another country[^cite_note-15] and a vague definition of terrorist acts that was deemed too "overbroad and violative of freedom of expression."[^cite_note-16]

Through the ATC, the Philippine government has designated individuals and organizations linked to the group as "terrorists".[^cite_note-17] On 21 September 2022, a petition filed by the Philippine government in 2018 to declare the [Communist Party of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Philippines) (CPP) and its armed wing, the [New People's Army](1262096.md) (NPA) as terrorist organizations citing the Human Security Act was denied by the Manila [Regional Trial Court](36842278.md), which ruled that the groups were not organized for terrorism and their resort to armed guerrilla warfare was only a means to achieve their purpose. It also cited the absence of a law that banned membership in communist groups.[^cite_note-18]

### Challenges to allegations of violating the anti-terror law

This section is an excerpt from [Anti-Terrorism Act of 2020 § Challenges to allegations of violating the anti-terror law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Terrorism_Act_of_2020#Challenges_to_allegations_of_violating_the_anti-terror_law).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Anti-Terrorism_Act_of_2020\&action=edit)]

In 2021, the Olongapo Regional Trial Court acquitted Japer Gurung and Junior Ramos, Indigenous Aeta residents of Zamboanga, in the first known anti-terror law case, ruling that the accusations were the result of mistaken identities.[^cite_note-19]

In November 2023, the prosecutor's office in Santa Rosa City in Laguna rejected a complaint against human rights worker Hailey Pecayo and other activists over the alleged violation of the anti-terror law.[^cite_note-20] In the same month, a prosecutor in Antipolo, Rizal, dismissed anti-terror law charges against human rights activists Kenneth Rementilla and Jasmine Rubia.[^cite_note-21]

In November 2023, Windel Bolinget and three other Indigenous rights workers of the Cordillera People's Alliance filed a case before the Baguio Regional Trial Court challenging their designation as terrorists by the Anti-Terrorism Council.[^cite_note-22]

## Recent trends

Since January 2000 [radical Islamist groups](566821.md) and Islamist separatist forces have carried out over 40 major bombings against civilian targets, mostly in the southern regions of the country around [Mindanao](20546.md) and the [Sulu archipelago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_archipelago).[^cite_note-hrwintro-23] Numerous bombings have also been carried out in and around [Metro Manila](72340.md), several hundred kilometres from the conflict in the southern regions, due to its political importance. From 2000 to 2007 attacks killed nearly 400 civilians and injured well over 1500 more,[^cite_note-tnyt-24] more casualties than caused by bombings and other attacks in [Indonesia](9751508.md), Morocco, Spain, Turkey, or [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorism_in_Britain) during the same period.[^cite_note-hrwintro-23]

[Public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) and other gathering places, such as [street markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_market), have been targets, however large-scale abductions and shootings have also been carried out by these groups, predominantly by the [Abu Sayyaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Sayyaf) and the Rajah Solaiman Movement.[^cite_note-tnyt-24]

Under the [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) administration in June 2023, the Anti-Terrorism Council (ATC) designated four leaders of the Cordillera Peoples Alliance (CPA)[^cite_note-25] as terrorists; Windel Bolinget, Jennifer Awingan, Sarah Abellon-Alikes, and Steve Tauli were accused of being members of the CPP-NPA Ilocos Cordillera Regional White Area Committee.[^cite_note-26] A month later, the ATC suspended Congressman for the [3rd district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Oriental's_3rd_congressional_district) of [Negros Occidental](266813.md), [Arnolfo Teves Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnolfo_Teves_Jr.); his brother, [Pryde Henry Teves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pryde_Henry_Teves); and eleven other associates as terrorists acting under what it called the "Teves Terrorist Group", in connection with the [assassination of Negros Oriental Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Roel_Degamo) [Roel Degamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roel_Degamo) in March. This was the first time the council designated an elected official as a terrorist.[^cite_note-27]
