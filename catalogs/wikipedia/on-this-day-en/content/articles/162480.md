**Baguio** ([UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_English): [/ˈbæɡioʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*BAG-ee-oh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English): [/ˈbɑːɡioʊ, ˌbɑːɡiˈoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*BAH-ghee-oh, -⁠OH*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), Tagalog: [\[ˈbaɡjo\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Tagalog)), officially the **City of Baguio** ([Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_language): *Siudad ne Bagiw*; [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language): *Siudad ti Baguio*; [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Lungsod ng Baguio*), is a [highly urbanized city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Legal_classification) in the [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region), [Philippines](23440.md). According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 368,426 people.[^cite_note-7]

It is known as the "Summer Capital of the Philippines", owing to the city's cool climate relative to the lowlands. With an approximate elevation of 1,500 meters (4,900 feet) above mean sea level, Baguio belongs to the [Luzon tropical pine forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luzon_tropical_pine_forests) [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion); the climate is conducive for the growth of mossy plants, orchids and pine trees, to which it attributes its other moniker as the "City of Pines".[^cite_note-8]

Baguio was established as a [hill station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_station) by the [United States](3434750.md) in 1900 at the site of an [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_people) village known as *Kafagway*. It was the United States' only hill station in Asia.[^cite_note-9]

Baguio is classified as a [highly urbanized city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_the_Philippines#Classification) (HUC). It is the largest city in [Benguet](274018.md), serving as the provincial capital from 1901 to 1916,[^cite_note-BaguioMidlandCourier3-10] but has since been administered independently from the province following its conversion into a chartered city. Baguio is geographically located within the province of [Benguet](274018.md) by the [Philippine Statistics Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Statistics_Authority) for its geographical and statistical purposes only. The city is the center of business, commerce, and education in northern Luzon, as well as the most populous and seat of government of the [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region).[^cite_note-baguio.gov.ph-11]

As of 2025 the City of Baguio has an estimated population of approximately 407,000 residents. This figure reflects a steady annual growth rate of around 1.75% from the previous year. The population has been gradually increasing over the past decade, with notable growth from 366,358 in 2020 to 392,000 in 2023. The city is also part of the larger [Baguio Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Baguio), which includes surrounding municipalities and has a combined population of about 451,844 as of 2024.

## Etymology

Baguio was called Kafagway by indigenous peoples. The name Baguio originated in the [American period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898%E2%80%931946\)) and is derived from the [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_language) word bagiw ([moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss)), which was then Hispanicized as Baguio.[^cite_note-12] A [demonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonym) for natives of the city, Ibagiw, is also derived from it. It is also the name for the city's annual arts festival.[^cite_note-cgov-13][^cite_note-Pungayan-14]

## History

### Ibaloi town of Kafagway

Baguio used to be a vast mountain zone with lush highland forests, teeming with various wildlife such as the indigenous deer, [cloud rats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_rat), [Philippine eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_eagle), [Philippine warty pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_warty_pig), and numerous species of flora. The area was a hunting ground of the indigenous peoples, notably the [Ibalois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_people) and other [Igorot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot_people) ethnic groups. When the Spanish arrived in the Philippines, the area was never fully subjugated by Spain due to the intensive defense tactics of the indigenous Igorots of the [Cordilleras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Luzon\)).[^cite_note-PhilStudies2-15]

Igorot oral history states the [Benguet](274018.md) upper class, *baknang*, was founded between 1565 and the early 1600s, by the marriage of a gold trader, Amkidit, and a Kankanaey maiden [gold panning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gold_panning) in Acupan. Their son, Baruy, discovered a gold deposit in the area, which he developed with hired workers and slaves.[^cite_note-PhilStudies2-15]

In 1755, the [Augustinian](144980.md) [Fray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friar) Pedro de Vivar established a mission in Tonglo (Tongdo) outside Baguio. Before he was driven out the following year, this *rancheria* included 220 people, including several *baknang* families. The Spanish tried to regain the mission in 1759, but were ambushed. This prompted Governor General [Pedro Manuel de Arandía Santisteban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Manuel_de_Arand%C3%ADa_Santisteban) to send Don Manuel Arza de Urrutia on a punitive expedition, which resulted in the mission being burned to the ground.[^cite_note-PhilStudies2-15]<sup>: 477–478 </sup>

### Spanish rule

During [Spanish rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1521%E2%80%931898\)) in 1846, the Spaniards established a command post or a *_comandancia_* in the nearby town of [La Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Trinidad,_Benguet), and organized Benguet into 31 *[rancherías](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancher%C3%ADa)*, one of which was Kafagway, a wide grassy area where the present [Burnham Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnham_Park_\(Baguio\)) is situated. Kafagway was then a minor *rancheria* consisting of only about 20 houses; most of the lands in Kafagway were owned by a prominent Ibaloi, [Mateo Cariño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mateo_Cari%C3%B1o), who served as its chieftain.[^cite_note-NordisNet-BaguioHistory2-16] The Spanish *presidencia*, which was located at Bag-iw in the vicinity of Guisad Valley was later moved to Cariño's house where the current city hall stands. *Bag-iw* was the Ibaloi toponym of the town, an Ibaloi term for "moss" which was historically abundant in the area. This name was spelled by the Spaniards as *Baguio*.[^cite_note-BaguioMidlandCourier3-10][^cite_note-DILG-CAR2-17]

### First Philippine Republic

During the [Philippine Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Revolution) in July 1899, Filipino revolutionary forces under [Pedro Paterno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Paterno) liberated La Trinidad from the Spaniards and took over the government, proclaiming Benguet as a province of the new [Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Philippine_Republic). Baguio was converted into a "town", with Mateo Cariño being the *presidente* (mayor).[^cite_note-BaguioMidlandCourier3-10][^cite_note-DILG-CAR2-17]

### American rule



Baguio city plan map made by [Daniel Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Burnham), circa 1905



Summer offices of the Philippine Insular Government in Baguio in 1909



Aerial view of Baguio, 1937

When the United States occupied the Philippines after the [Spanish–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish%E2%80%93American_War), Baguio was selected to become the [summer capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_capital) of the then [Philippine Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insular_Government_of_the_Philippine_Islands). American zoologist [Dean Conant Worcester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Conant_Worcester) headed an expedition in 1900 after convincing U.S. Secretary of State [Elihu Root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elihu_Root) to order an expedition to a cool place in the northern mountains of the Philippines.[^cite_note-18] [Governor-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-General_of_the_Philippines) [William Taft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Taft), on his first visit in 1901, noted the "air as bracing as [Adirondacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adirondack_Mountains) or [Murray Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Malbaie)..."[^cite_note-Kane2-19]<sup>: 317–319 </sup> On November 11, 1901, the American colonial government expropriated lands in Baguio owned by the Ibaloi people, who were forced to sell their lands.[^cite_note-20]

In 1903, [Filipinos](2298105.md), [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_people) and [Chinese](372983.md) workers were hired to build [Kennon Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennon_Road), the first road directly connecting Baguio with the lowlands of [La Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Union) and [Pangasinan](1691800.md). Before this, the only road to [Benguet](274018.md) was [Naguilian Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naguilian_Road), and it was largely a horse trail at higher elevations. [Camp John Hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_John_Hay) was established in October 1903, after [President](24113.md) [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) signed an [executive order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order_\(United_States\)) setting aside land in [Benguet](274018.md) for a military reservation for the [United States Army](32087.md) to rest and recuperate from the lowland heat.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] It was named after Roosevelt's [Secretary of State](32293.md), [John Milton Hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Milton_Hay).

[The Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mansion_\(Baguio\)), built in 1908, served as the official residence of the American Governor-General during the summer to escape Manila's heat. The Mansion was designed by architect [William E. Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_E._Parsons) based on preliminary plans by architect [Daniel Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Burnham).[^cite_note-23]

Burnham, one of the earliest successful modern [city planners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planner), designed the mountain retreat following the tenets of the [City Beautiful movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Beautiful_movement). In 1904, the rest of the city was planned out by Burnham. On September 1, 1909, Baguio was declared as a chartered city and nicknamed the "Summer Capital of the Philippines".[^cite_note-BaguioGovPH-About2-24]

The succeeding period saw further developments of and in Baguio with the construction of [Wright Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Park_\(Baguio\)) in honor of Governor-General [Luke Edward Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Edward_Wright), [Burnham Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnham_Park_\(Baguio\)) in honor of Burnham, [Governor Pack Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_Pack_Road), and [Session Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Session_Road).[^cite_note-25]

### World War II

Main article: [Battle of Baguio (1945)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baguio_\(1945\))



The ruins of an office building along [Session Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Session_Road), destroyed by artillery fire during the [Battle of Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baguio_\(1945\)). The ruins were demolished for new development in 2021.

Prior to [World War II](32927.md), Baguio was the summer capital of the [Commonwealth of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_the_Philippines), and the home of the [Philippine Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Military_Academy).[^cite_note-26] As such, it was very important in military and political terms. Philippine President [Manuel Quezon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Quezon) was even in Baguio when the war began.

On December 8, 1941, 17 Japanese bombers attacked [Camp John Hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_John_Hay),[^cite_note-People2-27]<sup>: 291 </sup>as part of the first Japanese air raid on Luzon.[^cite_note-WW2Bolido-28] Baguio was declared an [open city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_city) in December 27.[^cite_note-29]

Following the [Japanese invasion of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippines_campaign_\(1941%E2%80%931942\)) in 1941, the [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army) used [Camp John Hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_John_Hay), an American installation in Baguio, as a military base.[^cite_note-30] The nearby [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) base, [Camp Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Holmes_Internment_Camp), was used as an [internment camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_camp) for about 500 civilian enemy aliens, mostly Americans, between April 1942 and December 1944.[^cite_note-Morton-2018-31][^cite_note-32]



General Yamashita (center, on the near side of the table) at the surrender ceremony at [Camp John Hay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_John_Hay) on September 3, 1945.

President [José P. Laurel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_P._Laurel) of the [Second Philippine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Philippine_Republic), a [puppet state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppet_state) established in 1943, departed the city on March 22 and reached [Taiwan](25734.md) eight days later, on March 30.[^cite_note-SMCOrg-33] The remainder of the Second Republic government, along with Japanese civilians, were ordered to evacuate Baguio on March 30. General [Tomoyuki Yamashita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomoyuki_Yamashita) and his staff then relocated to [Bambang, Nueva Vizcaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambang,_Nueva_Vizcaya).[^cite_note-Zeiler2-34]

By late March 1945, Baguio was within range of the American and Filipino military [artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_artillery).[^cite_note-SMCOrg-33] Between March 4 and 10, United States [Fifth Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Air_Force) planes dropped 933 tons of bombs and 1,185 gallons of [napalm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napalm) on Baguio, reducing much of the city to rubble.[^cite_note-35] A major offensive to capture Baguio did not occur until April 1945, when the [USAFIP-NL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Forces_in_the_Philippines_%E2%80%93_Northern_Luzon)'s 1st Battalion of the 66th Infantry, attached with the United States Army's [37th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/37th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), the USAFIP-NL's 2nd Battalion of 66th Infantry, attached with the US [33rd Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33rd_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)), and the USAFIP-NL's 3rd Battalion of the 66th Infantry, converged on Baguio. By April 27, 1945, the city was liberated and the joint force proceeded to liberate the La Trinidad valley.[^cite_note-People2-27]

In September 1945, the Japanese forces in the Philippines, headed by General Yamashita and Vice Admiral Okochi, formally surrendered at [Camp John Hay's American Residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hay_Air_Station#The_American_Residence) in the presence of [lieutenant generals](201916.md) [Arthur Percival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Percival) and [Jonathan Wainwright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_M._Wainwright_\(general\)).[^cite_note-multiref2-36]

### Post-World War II recovery

With the end of World War II, Baguio recovered quickly, earning a significant reputation as a tourism venue and earning significantly from tourism even though it ceased to be the official "Summer Capital of the Philippines" in 1976.[^cite_note-BaguioGovPH-History-37]

### During the Marcos dictatorship

The beginning months of the 1970s marked a period of turmoil and change in the Philippines,[^cite_note-Robles2016-38] as well as in Baguio.[^cite_note-Panaglagip-39][^cite_note-BantayogProfileMaitaGomez-40] During his [bid to be the first Philippine president to be re-elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos%E2%80%99_1969_reelection_campaign) for a second term, Ferdinand Marcos launched an unprecedented number of public works projects. This caused[^cite_note-Balbosas1992-41][^cite_note-Balisacan\&Hill2003-42] the Philippine economy took a sudden downwards turn known as the [1969 Philippine balance of payments crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Philippine_balance_of_payments_crisis), which in turn led to a period of economic difficulty and social unrest.[^cite_note-Cororaton1997-43][^cite_note-Kessler1989-44] <sup>: "43" </sup>[^cite_note-Celoza1997-45][^cite_note-46] With only a year left in his last constitutionally allowed term as president Ferdinand Marcos placed the Philippines under [Martial Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_Law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos) in September 1972 and thus retained the position for fourteen more years.[^cite_note-Kasaysayan9ch10-47] This period in Philippine history is remembered for the Marcos administration's record of [human rights abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship),[^cite_note-McCoy199909202-48][^cite_note-Abinales\&Amoroso20052-49] particularly targeting political opponents, student activists, journalists, religious workers, farmers, and others who fought against the Marcos dictatorship.[^cite_note-Rappler-50]

During this time, Baguio City jail was partially adapted to become a detention center for "[political detainees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_detainees_under_the_Marcos_dictatorship)" who were jailed because the administration saw them as threats, and who were often held without being formally charged, which is why they were classified as "detainees", not "prisoners."[^cite_note-binubura-51][^cite_note-RocamoraBook-52] Among the prominent prisoners held at the Baguio City jail were 1967 Miss Philippines–World [Maita Gomez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maita_Gomez) who had spoken against the government,[^cite_note-BantayogProfileMaitaGomez-40] and Bulletin Today journalist Isidoro Chammag who had angered Marcos' soldiers by covering the 1983 [Beew Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beew_Massacre) in [Tubo, Abra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tubo,_Abra).[^cite_note-Panaglagip-39]<sup>: 197–198 </sup> Camp Henry T. Allen, the original site of the Philippine Military Academy near the Baguio City Hall, was also designated as a detention center during this time.[^cite_note-Panaglagip-39]<sup>: 142 </sup> Many of these political detainees could not afford representation, so the Baguio Chapter of the [Free Legal Assistance Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Legal_Assistance_Group), headed by Human Rights Lawyer [Arthur Galace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Galace), was kept busy defending them throughout the Martial law era.[^cite_note-BantayogProfileArthurGalace-53]

### During the 1986 People Power Revolution

Main article: [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution)

In the wake of the [Snap Presidential elections of 1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snap_Presidential_elections_of_1986), antidictatorship organizers were based largely in the Azotea Building midway up Session Road, and in Cafe Amapola further up Session, at its intersection with Governor Pack Road. Because the United States' Armed Forces Radio and Television Network station at Camp John Hay was transmitting news from Manila, they learned early on that the [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_revolution) had begun in Manila. Deciding that their locations were too unsafe, they encamped in the courtyard of the [Baguio Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_Cathedral), which was located on higher ground.[^cite_note-PeoplePowerBaguioRappler-54] They were later joined by Lt. [Benjamin Magalong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Magalong), of the [Philippine Constabulary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Constabulary) detachment in Buguias, Benguet,[^cite_note-PeoplePowerBaguioHerald-55] who had defected from the government, gone to the nearby Central Police Station in Baguio, and disarmed its personnel to prevent any untoward incidents while Baguio residents continued to gather at the cathedral to protest the [abuses of the Marcos administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_abuses_of_the_Marcos_dictatorship).[^cite_note-PeoplePowerBaguioHerald-55] The Baguio Cathedral, and Session Road adjacent to it, thus became the center of the [People Power revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_revolution) in Baguio - paralleling similar protests in [Cebu](285868.md), [Davao](489401.md), [Bacolod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacolod), [Manila](184334.md), and other major Philippine cities, eventually leading to the ouster of President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) on February 25, 1986.[^cite_note-PeoplePowerBaguioRappler-54]

### Creation of the Cordillera Administrative Region

On July 15, 1987, President [Corazon Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corazon_Aquino) issued *[Executive Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order_\(Philippines\)) 220* which created the Cordillera Administrative Region,[^cite_note-CountrySTAT-56][^cite_note-DeptAgri-57] and made the highly urbanized city of Baguio its seat of government.[^cite_note-baguio.gov.ph-11] [Various attempts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_autonomy_movement) at legally turning the Cordillera Administrative Region into an [autonomous region](191145.md) have been pursued, but failed to gather enough public support in two separate autonomy [plebiscites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plebiscite).[^cite_note-ABS-CBNNews-58]

### 1990 Luzon Earthquake and aftermath

The [1990 Luzon earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Luzon_earthquake) ([M<sub>s</sub>](6207163.md#Ms) = 7.7) destroyed some parts of Baguio and the surrounding province of Benguet on the afternoon of July 16, 1990.[^cite_note-Phivolcs-1990Earthquake2-59] A significant number of buildings and infrastructure were damaged, including the Hyatt Terraces Plaza, Nevada Hotel, Baguio Park Hotel, FRB Hotel and Baguio Hilltop Hotel; major highways were temporarily blocked due to landslides and pavement breakup; and a number of houses were leveled or severely shaken with numerous casualties.[^cite_note-60] Some of the fallen buildings were built on or near fault lines; local architects later admitted structural building codes should have been followed more religiously, particularly regarding concrete and rebar standards, and "soft stories." Baguio has been rebuilt with aid from the national government and international donors such as [Japan](15573.md), [Singapore](27318.md) and the [United States](3434750.md).

## Geography



Pine trees near [UP Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UP_Baguio)

Baguio is a highland city nestled within the [Cordillera Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Central_\(Luzon\)) mountain range in northern [Luzon](69375.md). While the downtown core ranges from 1,400 to 1,500 meters above sea level, elevations within Baguio's administrative boundaries extend from about 900 meters (along the [Bued River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bued_River)) to almost 1,700 meters (5,600 ft) (around the Busol watershed). [Baguio City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_City_Hall) sits at 1,465 meters (4,806 ft) above sea level. Mountain peaks on the outskirts of Baguio reach over 2,200 m (7,200 ft) above sea level, the highest being [Mount Santo Tomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Santo_Tomas) in [Tuba](2137478.md).[^cite_note-61]

Enclosed by the province of [Benguet](274018.md),[^cite_note-BaguioGovPH-About2-24] the city covers a small area of 57.5 square kilometers (22.2 sq mi). Most of the developed part of the city is built on the uneven, hilly terrain of the northern section. When [Daniel Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Burnham) drew plans for the city, he made the City Hall a reference point where the city limits extend 8.2 kilometers (5.1 mi) from east to west and 7.2 km (4.5 mi) from north to south.[^cite_note-BaguioGovPH-About2-24]

#### Proposed merger of barangays

A proposed merging of the city's 129 barangays had not been implemented since its inception in 2000. Several local officials stressed that many of the city's barangays did not comply with the minimum requirements in the [Local Government Code of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_Government_Code_of_the_Philippines) that a highly urbanized city must have a certified population of least 5,000 inhabitants. According to Mayor Mauricio Domogan, in the past, benefits granted to local governments were based on the number of existing barangays; this led former local officials to create as many barangays as possible in the city in order to acquire additional benefits from the national government. The proposed merger, which will reduce the barangays from 130 to about 40 to 50 by merging adjacent ones, is believed to solve several issues concerning barangay boundary disputes, seemingly biased [allocation of funds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_Revenue_Allotment) for larger barangays in relation to barangays with lesser area and population, as well as the inadequate [honorarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorarium) of barangay officials.[^cite_note-BaguioGovPh-BrgyMerger2-62][^cite_note-SunStarPh-BrgyMerger2-63][^cite_note-Midland-BrgyMerger2-64]

Owing to its generally high altitude (mostly above 1300 meters), the city is known for its mild climate and unusually low temperatures for the Philippines: typically about 7 to 10 °C (13 to 18 °F) cooler than lowland areas near sea level.[^cite_note-BaguioGovPH-About2-24][^cite_note-asiatravel.com2-69] Daily temperatures range from 15 to 23 °C (59 to 73 °F), on average, with the lowest temperatures occurring between November and February. The lowest recorded temperature was 6.3 °C (43.3 °F) on January 18, 1961; in contrast, the all-time high of 30.4 °C (86.7 °F) was recorded on March 15, 1988,[^cite_note-PAGASA-BaguioStation-70] during the 1988 [El Niño](317311.md) season.[^cite_note-Inq200812172-71] The temperature seldom exceeds 26 °C (78.8 °F) even during the warmest part of the year.

#### Precipitation



Fog in Baguio as viewed from [Mount Cabuyao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Cabuyao)

Like many other cities with a monsoon-influenced climate, Baguio receives noticeably less [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) during its dry season. However, the city has an extraordinary amount of precipitation during the rainy season from June to October.[^cite_note-BaguioGovPH-About2-24] The city averages over 3,914 mm (154 in) of rainfall annually, the highest in the country.[^cite_note-72]

## Environment

### Pollution



[Smog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smog) from vehicles in Bonifacio Street in 2018

Baguio suffers from [air pollution](10934212.md) and is one of the cities with the dirtiest air in the Philippines, according to a 2014 [WHO](33583.md) report;[^cite_note-73] a slight improvement in the city's air quality was cited in 2017 by the [DENR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Environment_and_Natural_Resources)'s Environmental Management Bureau. In a 2018 WHO report, the city was listed as having the most polluted air among 8 other local cities ([Cebu](285868.md), [Dagupan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagupan), [Davao](489401.md), [Manila](184334.md), San Carlos, [Urdaneta](2069163.md) and [Zamboanga](25167165.md)).[^cite_note-74] Eco-vehicles and [Euro 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_4) compliant vehicles have been tested to see whether or not they are suited for the city's steep slopes in line with efforts to modernize its [Public Utility Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transit) (PUVs).[^cite_note-75] PUVs, specifically [jeepneys](884965.md), have been pushed for its modernization, in line with [President Duterte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_Duterte)'s [PUV modernization initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeepney_modernization_program).[^cite_note-76]

Another problem that plagues Baguio is its garbage and waste disposal. The city has been dumping its garbage in a [landfill](211285.md) in [Urdaneta](2069163.md) City, but rising costs are putting a strain on the city's budget.[^cite_note-77] In early 2018, the city government started using its [garbage transfer station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transfer_station_\(waste_management\)) in the city outskirts near [Marcos Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aspiras-Palispis_Highway), drawing protests from residents of the nearby town of [Tuba](2137478.md), who cited the facility poses health hazards to their communities.[^cite_note-78] As of 2019, the [Philippine National Oil Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_National_Oil_Company) has offered to test a [waste-to-energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste-to-energy) technology as a possible solution to its garbage woes.[^cite_note-79] Baguio's [waste water treatment plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_water_treatment_plant) is also eyed for an expansion as it has been unable to fully cater to the city's needs; wastewater which the plant could not accommodate were dumped in the [Balili River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balili_River), leading to its high [coliform levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coliform_index)—even higher than that of [Manila Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bay)'s.[^cite_note-80]

## Landscape

Baguio is also a planned city. American Architect and Urban Planner [Daniel Burnham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Burnham) was commissioned to design the new capital; his design for the city was based on the [City Beautiful movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Beautiful_movement),[^cite_note-81] which features broad streets and avenues radiating out from rectangles.

During the Second World War, Baguio was razed to the ground during the Japanese forces' invasion and the subsequent shelling by American forces during the liberation.[^cite_note-Locsin-2013-82] After the liberation, rebuilding began and most of the historical buildings were thoroughly reconstructed. However, some of the historic buildings from the 19th century that had been preserved in reasonably reconstructible form were nonetheless eradicated or otherwise left to deteriorate. The [1990 Luzon earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Luzon_earthquake) further devastated Baguio's old buildings, which include 28 collapsed buildings such as hotels, factories, government and university buildings, and many private homes and establishments.[^cite_note-83]

Baguio's current landscape is mostly of contemporary architecture.

### Architecture



Porta Vaga Mall along Session Road

Baguio's contemporary architecture is largely of American build, since Americans were the ones to establish a station here. A few examples include those built at Teacher's Camp and Camp John Hay, previous American installations in the city as well as the current Baguio City Hall. Some buildings are also influenced by Spanish building concepts, such as *Porta Vaga Mall* and *La Azotea*. One of the more modern buildings in the city is [SM City Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_City_Baguio), established in 2003.[^cite_note-84]

Moves by various groups with the goal to preserve these buildings have been made.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] As a historic building, the Baguio City Hall has faced opposition to renovation of its grounds, since that may be against laws on national cultural heritage sites;[^cite_note-87] the renovations, however, continued as there has been no documentation supporting the City Hall as a national heritage site.[^cite_note-88] The construction of the City Hall park was finished in May 2019 as was said to define the city's new moniker as a creative center for crafts and folk arts.[^cite_note-89]

The original inhabitants of Baguio are the [Ibaloi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_people) (natively pronounced as "Ivadoi"). When the Americans established the city in the early 1900s, early settlers in the city included members of other [Igorot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot) tribes (*Igudut* in [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_language)), the lowlander [Ilocanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_people) (*Iduko*), Americans (*Merikano*), and *[mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Mestizos)*. A significant number of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Chinese) (both [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_people) and [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoklo_people)) and [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_in_the_Philippines) laborers were also hired to build [Kennon Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennon_Road), many of whom later settled in the city.[^cite_note-Pungayan-14]

Baguio is an [overpopulated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overpopulated) city, having a population density of more than 6,000 people per square kilometer.[^cite_note-SunStar-CarryingCapacity-95] Originally planned by Daniel Burnham to accommodate 25,000 to 30,000 people,[^cite_note-SunStar-CarryingCapacity-95] the city's population has swelled to 366,358 persons as of May 2020.[^cite_note-PSA20–14-96] The city has a very young age structure as 65.5 percent of its total population is below thirty years old. Females comprise 51.3 percent of the population as against 48.7 percent for males. The household population comprises 98 percent of the total population, or 245,000 persons. With an average of 4.6 members per household, a total of 53,261 households are gleaned. During the peak of the annual tourist influx, particularly during the [Lenten period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lent), [transients](17625621.md) triple the population.[^cite_note-BaguioGovPH-About2-24]

### Crime

Crime in Baguio is concentrated in theft and vehicular accidents. Crime in the city is also directly related to its changing demographics and unique criminal justice system. The [illegal drug trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illegal_drug_trade_in_the_Philippines) is also a problem of the city as 24 of its 129 [barangays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) are considered as drug affected as of December 2017.[^cite_note-Llanes-97]

In 2018, Baguio was listed as one of the safest cities both in the [ASEAN](106539.md) region, ranking sixth with a crime index of 40.57 and safety index of 59.43. The Baguio City Police Office also has the highest crime solution efficiency nationwide of 84%, compared to the national 77% and the region's 70%.[^cite_note-Llanes-97] In May 2019 BCPO also reported a drop of 27% in crimes, from 1,150 in 2018 to 834 in 2019. The BCPO was awarded as the country's best city police station in 2018.[^cite_note-98]

### Religion



The [Cathedral of Our Lady of the Atonement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_Cathedral), the seat of the [Roman Catholic Diocese of Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Baguio)

Baguio is a predominantly [Christian](8276776.md) city, as of 2015; [Roman Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_the_Philippines) at 74% (254,716), [Evangelicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicals) ([Philippine Council of Evangelical Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Council_of_Evangelical_Churches)) at 7% (24,236), [National Council of Churches in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_of_Churches_in_the_Philippines) at 5% (17,968), and [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md) at 4% (13,897).[^cite_note-PSAGovPH-2015-Baguio-99]

As of 2015, [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_the_Philippines) comprise 1% (3,269) of the city's total population.[^cite_note-PSAGovPH-2015-Baguio-99] The largest mosque in the area is *Masjid Al-Maarif*, which is a centre of Islamic studies in the Philippines.[^cite_note-100] The city also has smaller numbers of [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhism_in_the_Philippines) and [atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism_in_the_Philippines), along with members of other faiths.[^cite_note-PSAGovPH-2015-Baguio-99]

As a melting pot of different peoples and cultures in the [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region), numerous investments and business opportunities are lured to Baguio.[^cite_note-BaguioGovPH-About2-24] Baguio has a large retail industry, with shoppers coming to the city to take advantage of the diversity of competitively priced commercial products on sale.[^cite_note-110] The city is also popular with bargain hunters;[^cite_note-111] some of the most popular bargaining areas include Baguio Market and [Maharlika Livelihood Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharlika_Livelihood_Center). The city is home to numerous shopping centers and malls catering to increasing commercial and tourist activity;[^cite_note-Padawangi-2018-112] these include: [SM City Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SM_City_Baguio), Baguio Center Mall, Abanao Square, and Tiong San.[^cite_note-113]

Various food and retail businesses run by local residents proliferate, forming a key part of Baguio's cultural landscape. Several retail outlets and dining outlets are situated along Bonifacio Street, [Session Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Session_Road), [Teacher's Camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teacher's_Camp), [Mines View Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mines_View_Park) and Baguio Fastfood Center near the market.



[Maharlika Livelihood Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharlika_Livelihood_Center)

The areas of [Session Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Session_Road), Harrison Road, Magsaysay Avenue and Abanao Street, comprise the city's trade center, where commercial and business structures such as cinemas, hotels, restaurants, department stores, and shopping centers are concentrated. The City Market offers a wide array of locally sourced goods and products, usually from [Benguet](274018.md) province,[^cite_note-NordisNet2-114][^cite_note-CNNPhil2-115] which includes colorful woven fabrics and hand-strung beads to primitive wood carvings, cut flowers,[^cite_note-NordisNet2-114] strawberries and "Baguio" vegetables. The term 'Baguio vegetables' often denotes vegetable types that thrive in the cooler growing climate. Strawberries and [string beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/String_beans)—referred to as *Baguio beans* across the Philippines—are shipped to major urban markets across the archipelago.

Another key source of income for Baguio is its position as the economic hub of the Cordillera Administrative Region.[^cite_note-116] The economy of the city has benefited from the vibrant mining industry in several towns of Benguet.[^cite_note-117] Many agricultural goods produced in Benguet pass through Baguio for processing, sale or further distribution to the lowlands.[^cite_note-118]

### Industrial

Baguio is one of the Philippines' most profitable and best investment areas.[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120]

A [Philippine Economic Zone Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Economic_Zone_Authority) (PEZA)-accredited business and industrial park called the [Baguio City Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_City_Economic_Zone) (BCEZ) is located in the southern part of the city between [Camp John Hay Country Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_John_Hay) and [Philippine Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Military_Academy) in Barangay Loakan. Firms located in the BCEZ mostly produce and export knitted clothing, transistors, small components for vehicles, electronics and computer parts. Notable firms include [Texas Instruments Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas_Instruments), which is the second largest exporter in the country.[^cite_note-121] Other companies headquartered within the economic zone include [Baguio Ayalaland Technohub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_Ayalaland_Technohub?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-122] [Moog Philippines, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moog,_Inc),[^cite_note-123] [Linde Philippines, Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linde_AG), [LTX Philippines Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LTX), and [Sitel Philippines, Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitel).[^cite_note-124]

### Outsourcing



A building hosting a BPO in Baguio

Outsourcing contributes to the city's economy and employment. [Sitel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitel), whose main office is located in the [Baguio City Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_City_Economic_Zone), is the largest [BPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_process_outsourcing) company in the city with four sites established within the BCEZ. There are also multiple BPOs present in the city with numerous PEZA-accredited private economic zones established to cater to this industry. The Ayala Technohub located in Camp John Hay hosts [Concentrix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentrix) and [InterContinental Hotels Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterContinental_Hotels_Group) alongside other commercial establishments.[^cite_note-125] Teleperformance Baguio and Thoughtfocus is established at the SM Cyberzone Building (also known as SM Fiesta Strip) located in front of Sunshine Park,[^cite_note-126] while other call centers downtown are Optimum Transsource, Sterling Global and Global Translogic. Tech-Synergy operates a large transcription and back office operation near Wright park.

In recent years, there has been a surge of ESL ([English as a Second Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_as_a_Second_Language)) Tutorial Schools throughout Baguio that caters to students from other countries and also provide online services. This industry however has been hit hard by the COVID-19 pandemic with foreign students returning to their home countries.[^cite_note-127]

## Culture

### Arts and museums



Laperal White House

The city became a haven for many Filipino artists in the 1970s–1990s. Drawn by the cool climate and low cost of living, artists such as [Ben Cabrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Cabrera) (now a National Artist) and filmmaker Butch Perez relocated to the city. At the same time, locals such as mixed-media artist [Santiago Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santi_Bose) and filmmaker [Kidlat Tahimik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidlat_Tahimik) were also establishing work in the city. Even today, artists like painters and sculptors from all over the country are drawn to the Baguio Arts Festival which is held annually.[^cite_note-asiatravel.com2-69] The city houses several museums, such as the Baguio Museum, Museo Kordilyera, Emilio F. Aguinaldo Museum, and the SLU Museum of Arts and Cultures. The Mansion House located at Wright Park was also re-opened to the public as a presidential museum.[^cite_note-128]

Baguio has been included in [UNESCO](21786641.md)'s [Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network) due to craft and folk art traditions of the city particularly ranging on expressions to wood carving, silver craft, traditional weaving and tattooing.[^cite_note-129] Baguio is the first city in the Philippines to be part of the inter-city network which aims to promote the creative industries as well as integrate culture in sustainable urban development.[^cite_note-130]

### Languages

The [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Philippines) commonly spoken in Baguio are [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language), [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language), and [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_English). Languages like [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_language), [Kankanaey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankanaey_language), [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language), [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese),[^cite_note-\:0-131] [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Hokkien),[^cite_note-\:0-131] [Japanese](15606.md),[^cite_note-Pungayan-14] [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language), [Kapampangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapampangan_language), [Cebuano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cebuano_language), [Hiligaynon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiligaynon_language), [Maranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maranao_language), [Maguindanaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_language) and [Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tausug_language) are also spoken to varying degrees by their respective ethnic communities within the city.[^cite_note-PSAGovPH-2000Census-Baguio-132][^cite_note-133]

### Media

In 1939, when the country's [literacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy) was below 50%, the city already had a weekly paper. [Manila Bulletin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Bulletin)'s predecessor, The Manila Daily Bulletin, published a [weekly supplement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekly_newspaper), *Baguio Bulletin*.[^cite_note-134]

Local and regional newspapers circulating in Baguio include the *Baguio Midland Courier*, *Baguio Herald Express*, *Zigzag Weekly*, and *Baguio Chronicle*.[^cite_note-135]

*[Baguio Midland Courier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_Midland_Courier)* was the largest newspaper publication around Baguio and [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region). It started out as a four-page tabloid on April 28, 1947, by [Sinai Hamada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinai_Hamada) and siblings Oseo and Cecilia. Sinai was its first and longest-lasting editor-in-chief while his older brother Oseo managed its business operations.[^cite_note-136] Circa 1963, it distributed 7,500 copies. It launched its [website](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Website) in its 60th anniversary.[^cite_note-137] The Baguio Midland Courier announced its closure effective July 22, 2024 and it issued its final edition on July 21, 2024.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139] Baguio Midland Courier's publisher, Hamada Printers & Publishers Corp., also ceased the operation of their social media pages and website on October 31, 2024. The publisher donated archives of Baguio Midland Courier issues to the Baguio City Library and Benguet Provincial Library for use by the public.[^cite_note-140]

### Festivals and holidays



[Panagbenga Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagbenga_Festival)

The annual flower festival, called the [Panagbenga Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagbenga_Festival), is held every February; it was created to highlight the city's flowers and cool temperature and as a way to rise up from the devastation of the [1990 Luzon earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Luzon_earthquake). The festival includes floats covered mostly with flowers;[^cite_note-141] it also includes street dancing, presented by dancers clad in flower-inspired costumes inspired by the *Bendian*, an [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_people) dance of celebration. The indigenous people were initially wary with government-led tourism due to a perceived threat that the government would interfere with or change their communities' rituals.[^cite_note-142]

The annual Gong Festival is celebrated on the 29th of October. Baguio Gong Day coincides with the [National Indigenous Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Philippines)’ Thanksgiving Day. The festivity includes the [Gaddang people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaddang_people)'s courtship dances palakis and the takik and the symbolic beating of the seven gongs to attain peace, unity and cultural heritage preservation.[^cite_note-143]

Baguio celebrates its city charter anniversary every September 1, which has been declared as a special non-working holiday in 1989 through Republic Act 6710.[^cite_note-144]

## Tourism

See also: [List of Cultural Properties of the Philippines in the Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Cultural_Properties_of_the_Philippines_in_the_Cordillera_Administrative_Region)



[Burnham Park Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnham_Park_\(Philippines\))

Tourism is one of Baguio's main industries due to its cool climate and history. The city is one of the country's top tourist destinations. During the year end holidays some people from the lowlands prefer spending their vacation in Baguio, to experience cold temperatures they rarely have in their home provinces. Also, during the summer, especially during [Holy Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_the_Philippines), tourists from all over the country flock to the city. During this time, the total number of people in the city doubles.[^cite_note-145] To accommodate visitors, there are more than 80 hotels and inns available, as well as numerous transient houses set up by the locals.[^cite_note-146] Local festivities such as the [Panagbenga Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panagbenga_Festival) also attract both local and foreign tourists.[^cite_note-147] In early 2026, a rare bloom of [sakura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_blossom) (Japanese cherry blossoms) occurred at the Baguio Country Club in the Philippines, marking the first significant flowering of the trees in the country in seven years. The bloom attracted scores of local visitors and tourists alike. This event illustrated how climatic conditions in higher-elevation areas warmer Asian climates can occasionally support temperate flora typically associated with East Asia.[^cite_note-148]

Baguio is the lone Philippine destination in the 2011 [TripAdvisor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TripAdvisor) Traveller's Choice Destinations Awards (Asia category) with the city being among the top 25 destinations in [Asia](689.md).[^cite_note-149] [Burnham Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnham_Park_\(Philippines\)), [Mines View Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mines_View_Park), [Wright Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wright_Park_\(Baguio\)), [The Mansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mansion_\(Baguio\)), and Botanical Garden are among the popular tourist sites in Baguio.[^cite_note-150]

## Government

### Local government

Further information: [Sangguniang Panlungsod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Panlungsod) and [Mayor of Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Baguio)



Baguio City Hall

As a highly urbanized city with its own charter, it is not subject to the jurisdiction of Benguet province, of which it was formerly a part.

The City of Baguio is led by its own mayor and vice mayor. The vice mayor leads the [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_City_Council), composed of 12 elected councilors and 3 *ex-officio* members (the ABC President, SK President, and IPMR of the city). The City Government holds office at the Baguio City Hall.[^cite_note-151]

The 129 barangays are led by their own captains assisted by a 7-man barangay council.[^cite_note-152]

These officials are term-limited by up to 3 consecutive terms, with each term lasting for 3 years.

## Sports



Baguio Athletic Bowl

Baguio has hosted several sporting events, even those of international standing. The [Baguio Athletic Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_Athletic_Bowl) within the grounds of [Burnham Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnham_Park_\(Philippines\)) is one of Baguio's primary sporting venues. Baguio hosted the [1978 World Chess Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Championship_1978) match between [Anatoly Karpov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoly_Karpov) and [Viktor Korchnoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Korchnoi), building the Baguio Convention Center for that purpose. The city is a participant in the CARAA games or the Cordillera Administrative Region Athletic Association, hosting it last in 2016 and 2017. The winners of the said event will eventually represent the region in the annual [Palarong Pambansa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palarong_Pambansa) games, which is also sponsored by the [Department of Education](5464548.md).[^cite_note-160] As of 2019, the city is still the overall champion with 205 gold, 110 silver and 79 bronze medals.[^cite_note-\:02-161][^cite_note-162]

In recent years, Baguio has been racking up titles and medals in the field of Mixed Martial Arts led by [Team Lakay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Lakay_Wushu).[^cite_note-163][^cite_note-164]

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

#### Air



[Loakan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loakan_Airport) runway in the outskirts of the city

[Loakan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loakan_Airport) is the lone airport serving the general area of Baguio. The airport is classified as a trunkline airport, or a major commercial domestic airport, by the [Civil Aviation Authority of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Aviation_Authority_of_the_Philippines). Major commercial operations to Baguio however ceased after [Philippine Airlines](296889.md) closed their Baguio route in 1998. There were attempts to reopen Baguio routes throughout the following two decades by different airline operators such as [Asian Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Spirit) and [Sky Pasada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_Pasada) but none were able to sustain continuous operation.[^cite_note-165] Commercial flights resumed in 2022 with Philippine Airlines (operated by [PAL Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAL_Express)) operating Baguio–Cebu flights and vice versa but operations were halted by Philippine Airlines in July 2024 because of poor passenger traffic.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167]

The airport is located south of the city center. Due to the limited length of the runway, being only 1,802 m (5,912 ft) long, it is restricted to commuter size aircraft. The airport is used primarily by helicopters, turbo-prop and piston engine aircraft, although on rare occasions light business jets (LBJ) have flown into the airport.

#### Land



[Session Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Session_Road), one of Baguio's primary roads



[Jeepney](884965.md) terminal in downtown Baguio



Flyover in Baguio

[Jeepneys](884965.md) and [taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxicab) are the main means of public transportation in the city. The government's push for jeepney modernization has led to an increase of modern EURO 4 compliant PUVs plying Baguio's center. The rollout however for full jeepney modernization had been hampered by the COVID-19 pandemic. There are several bus lines linking Baguio with [Manila](184334.md), [Bontoc](2118046.md), [Mariveles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariveles,_Bataan), [Olongapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olongapo), [Cabanatuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabanatuan), and provinces such as [Pangasinan](1691800.md), [Pampanga](255602.md), [Bataan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bataan),[^cite_note-168] [Nueva Ecija](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Ecija), [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)), [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite), [La Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Union), [Nueva Vizcaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Vizcaya) and those in the Ilocos regions. Notable bus companies that operate the Baguio to Manila routes are [Victory Liner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Liner), [Genesis Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genesis_Transport) and its premium bus line JoyBus, and [Solid North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_Solid_North_Transit_Inc.).

From Metro Manila, Baguio is accessible via [NLEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NLEX) (from Bulacan to Pampanga), [SCTEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SCTEX) (Pampanga to Tarlac) and [TPLEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TPLEX) (from Tarlac to La Union). The newest road that connects the city to the lowlands is [Asin Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asin_Road) (also known as *Asin-San Pascual-Tubao, La Union Road*).[^cite_note-Midland-AsinTubaoRd-Opens2-171] All these roads traverse the municipality of [Tuba, Benguet](2137478.md).

##### HoHo Bus

In an effort to address traffic congestion and the lack of car parking at tourist spots in the city, Baguio operates an experimental free bus shuttle service called the **Hop On, Hop Off (HoHo) Tourist Bus**. The bus service has an estimated waiting interval of 30 minutes and is operational from 8\:30 AM to 6\:20 PM daily.[^cite_note-HoHoBusDec2022-180] The operations of the HoHo bus was eventually suspended at the end of the year.

### Water and electricity

Most of the water supply of the city is provided for by the Baguio Water District, founded in 1975 as the successor to the now-defunct Department of Public Services. It currently operates 60 deep wells to cater to its more than 300,000 consumers. It currently serves 122 out of the 129 barangays in the city and some parts of [Tuba, Benguet](2137478.md).[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-\:12-185] In recent years, the BWD has expressed concern over the city's depleting water supply, due in part to private wells dug by private individuals and companies.[^cite_note-\:12-185]

Electric services are provided by [Benguet Electric Cooperative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguet_Electric_Cooperative) (BENECO), the sole electric power distributor in Benguet. In 2012, a bill was filled in the House of Representatives seeking the creation of the Baguio Electric Cooperative or BAELCO, an entity to provide for the city's own electricity needs, separate from BENECO. Its creation has been met with opposition by various groups citing the need of a feasibility study on the separation. The creation of a separate electric franchise for Baguio would also infringe on BENECO's existing franchise that mandated BENECO to provide electricity for both Baguio and Benguet which would create legal implications if it was to be amended.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188]

The city is also the only local government unit to own and operate its own renewable energy plant. Originally constructed in the 1920s, the Asin Mini Hydropower Plants 1, 2 and 3 located in Tuba, Benguet came under the city's possession after the lapse of the 25-year lease agreement with the Aboitiz-owned Hydroelectric Development Corporation (HEDCOR). The plant acted as both a power source and another income-generating asset of the city as BENECO was also its main client. The power plant however was forced to cease operation in 2015 when the Energy Regulatory Commission issued a [cease and desist order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cease_and_desist_order) to the city due to the lack of a certificate of compliance from the city. The city currently has plans to rehabilitate the power plants so that they could resume operations once again.[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190]

## Healthcare

See also: [List of hospitals in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_the_Philippines)



[BGHMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_General_Hospital_and_Medical_Center), Baguio's sole government hospital

Baguio's healthcare is mainly provided by various private corporations. Private hospitals operating in the city are the Baguio Medical Center, BCU-Santo Niño de Jesus Medical Center Foundation, Parkway Medical Center, Notre Dame de Chartres Hospital, Pines City Doctors' Hospital and Saint Louis University's Hospital of Sacred Heart. The BCU-Santo Niño de Jesus Medical Center Foundation ceased operations in 2009 due to financial reasons but was reopened during the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) with the local government refurbishing it as an isolation center for COVID-19 patients.[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] It was eventually idled at the end of the COVID-19 pandemic.

In early 2019, several groups were eyeing to establish a hospital in Barangay Scout Barrio, but was met with opposition; as of March 2019, the project has been shelved.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196]

The Baguio City Health Services Office is the office responsible for the health care programs provided by the city government, operating 16 health centers and 15 satellite clinics. Baguio hosts the [Baguio General Hospital and Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_General_Hospital_and_Medical_Center) or BGHMC, a tertiary state-owned hospital administered and operated by the [Department of Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Health_\(Philippines\)). It is the sole government hospital in the city and the largest government tertiary hospital in the [Cordillera Administrative Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region).[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198]

## Education



Baguio Central School

Education is a major contributor to the economy of Baguio. Considered as the "Educational Center of the North", the city has a transient student population who migrate to the city to attend tertiary education.[^cite_note-Daly-2016-199]

Baguio is the center of education in the Northern Philippines due to high performances in various professional licensure exams as well as adherence to high educational quality standards, housing some of the prestigious and largest universities in Northern Luzon.[^cite_note-200]

### Elementary and Secondary schools



Baguio City High School

The Baguio Schools Division Office governs all educational institutions within the municipality. It oversees the management and operations of all private and public, from primary to secondary schools.[^cite_note-201] There are three schools district offices namely: District I, District II, and District III.

The city has 45 public elementary schools and 21 public secondary schools. Most of its secondary private schools are divisions of the private universities of the city. The Philippine Science High School - CAR campus was established in the city in 2009 and is located in Irisan. In 2016, the city government established in Irisan the Baguio City Science High School to create a unified science high school campus.[^cite_note-202]

Baguio Central School, the country's first [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_school) [primary school](25058.md) was opened on September 2, 1899. It was once a [one-room school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-room_school) built upon [Mateo Cariño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mateo_Cari%C3%B1o)'s 2 [hectare](24919513.md) lot and [Ibaloy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_people) house he donated to the local government. Its roof was made of [reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_\(plant\)) and [cogongrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperata_cylindrica) with [hewn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hewing) wood walls and floor. Its first students were only 25 [Igorot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igorot) boys, including Dr. Jose Cariño and Maximo Carantes, under a [United States Armed Forces](32212.md) teacher, Mr. Patrick. The pupils used [slate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slate_\(writing\)) and chalks to write. In 1901, female students were enrolled. World War II heavily damaged the school which was renovated by the Department of Education in 2004. The Heritage conservation Society declared it as a [heritage school](34759496.md).[^cite_note-203] On May 24, 2024, the City Planning, Development, and Sustainability Office stopped the P41.2-million reconstruction project on BCS’ right wing portions of the [Gabaldon School Buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabaldon_School_Buildings). Under the Cultural Mapping Book, BCS is a “significant tangible and immovable cultural heritage property, and the biggest elementary school in the city, with about 70 classrooms, 3,300 pupils and 83 teaching staff” which ought to be restored to its 1949 state.[^cite_note-204]

### Higher educational institutions



[Saint Louis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Louis_University_\(Philippines\))



[Philippine Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Military_Academy)

The city houses eight major institutions of higher education. Baguio's first private school, Easter College, was set up in 1906 by the Rt. Rev. Charles Brent, who was a bishop of the Episcopalian Church.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206]

The [University of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines), the country's premier state university, was established as an extension campus in [Vigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigan), Ilocos Sur before transferring to Baguio in 1938.[^cite_note-207] In 1961, it became a branch campus of [UP Diliman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines_Diliman), before finally becoming [UP Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines_Baguio) in 2002.[^cite_note-208]

[Saint Louis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Louis_University_\(Philippines\)) is the largest university in the city and in the north of Manila, catering to over 30,000 students;[^cite_note-209] it was founded by Belgian [CICM missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CICM_missionaries) in 1911[^cite_note-210] initially as a one-storey school for boys. It became a college in the 1950s before becoming a university in 1963. It currently has four campuses spread across the city.

[Baguio Central University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_Central_University) was founded in 1945 as the Centro Academy by the Fernandez family.[^cite_note-211] The following year, another educational institution, the [University of the Cordilleras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Cordilleras) was established as the Baguio Colleges before becoming the Baguio Colleges Foundation; it became a full-fledged university in 2003.[^cite_note-212] The [University of Baguio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Baguio) was established as the Baguio Technical and Commercial Institute in 1948 by the Bautista family; it was upgraded to university status in 1969.[^cite_note-213]

Mainly a nursing and medical school, Pines City Colleges was founded in 1969 as the Pines City Doctors Hospital School of Nursing, three years after the opening of Pines City Doctors' Hospital in 1966.

The [Philippine Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Military_Academy), the country's military school, was originally founded in the Walled City of [Intramuros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intramuros) in 1907 before relocating to Baguio in 1908.[^cite_note-214]
