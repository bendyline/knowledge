The **Czech Republic**,[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] also known as **Czechia**[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] and historically known as **Bohemia**,[^cite_note-23] is a [landlocked country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked_country) in [Central Europe](5188.md). The country is bordered by [Austria](26964606.md) to the south, [Germany](11867.md) to the west, [Poland](22936.md) to the northeast, and [Slovakia](26830.md) to the southeast.[^cite_note-24] The Czech Republic has a hilly landscape that covers an area of 78,871 square kilometers (30,452 sq mi) with a mostly temperate [continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) and [oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate). The capital and largest city is [Prague](23844.md); other major cities and urban areas include [Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno), [Ostrava](464078.md), [Plzeň](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plze%C5%88) and [Liberec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberec).

The [Duchy of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bohemia) was founded in the 9th century under [Great Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Moravia). It was formally recognized as an [Imperial Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Estate) of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in 1002 and became [a kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bohemia) in 1198.[^cite_note-The_Path-25][^cite_note-26] Following the [Battle of Mohács](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Moh%C3%A1cs) in 1526, all the [Lands of the Bohemian Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lands_of_the_Bohemian_Crown) were gradually integrated into the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy). Nearly a hundred years later, the [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) [Bohemian Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Revolt) led to the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War). After the [Battle of White Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_White_Mountain), the Habsburgs consolidated their rule. With the dissolution of the Holy Roman Empire in 1806, the Crown lands became part of the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire).

During the 19th century, the [Czech lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_lands) underwent significant industrialization. Following the collapse of [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) after [World War I](4764461.md), most of the region became part of the [First Czechoslovak Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Czechoslovak_Republic) in 1918.[^cite_note-27] [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) was the only country in Central and Eastern Europe to remain a [parliamentary democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) during the entirety of the [interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period).[^cite_note-Ash0-28] After the [Munich Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Agreement) in 1938, [Nazi Germany](21212.md) systematically [took control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Czechoslovakia_\(1938%E2%80%931945\)) over the Czech lands. Czechoslovakia was restored in 1945 as the [Third Czechoslovak Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Czechoslovak_Republic) and three years later became an [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) communist state following a [coup d'état in 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Czechoslovak_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). Attempts to liberalize the government and economy were suppressed by a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)-led invasion of the country during the [Prague Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring) in 1968. In November 1989, the [Velvet Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Revolution) ended communist rule in the country and restored democracy. On 31 December 1992, [Czechoslovakia was peacefully dissolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_Czechoslovakia), with its constituent states becoming the independent states of the Czech Republic and Slovakia.

The Czech Republic is a [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state) [parliamentary republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_republic) and [developed country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) with an [advanced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country#IMF_advanced_economies), [high-income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-income_economy) [social market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_market_economy). It is a [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state) with a [European social model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_social_model), [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_the_Czech_Republic) and [free-tuition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_education) [university education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Czech_Republic#Tertiary_education). It ranks 32nd in the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index). The Czech Republic is a member of the [United Nations](31769.md), [NATO](21133.md), the [European Union](9317.md), the [OECD](33853117.md), the [OSCE](22796.md), the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) and the [Visegrád Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visegr%C3%A1d_Group).

## Etymology

Main article: [Name of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_the_Czech_Republic)

The traditional English name "[Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemia)" derives from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) *Boiohaemum*, which means "home of the [Boii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boii_\(Bohemia\))" (a [Gallic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauls) tribe). The current English name ultimately comes from the Czech word *Čech*.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-American_Heritage_Dictionary-30][^cite_note-OxfordDictionary-31] The name comes from the [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) tribe ([Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language): *Češi, Čechové*) and—according to legend—their leader [Čech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lech,_Czech,_and_Rus) who brought them to Bohemia to settle on [Říp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%98%C3%ADp), a 461-metre high hill. The etymology of the word *Čech* is uncertain. According to the most common derivation, it can be traced back to the [Proto-Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Slavic_language) root \**čel-*, meaning 'member of the people; kinsman'. Thus, it is [cognate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cognate_\(linguistics\)) to the Czech word *[člověk](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%C4%8Dlov%C4%9Bk#Czech)* 'person'.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-Cmejrkova2010-33][^cite_note-Dickins2011-34][^cite_note-Rejzek2012-35]

The country has been traditionally divided into three lands, namely Bohemia (*Čechy*) in the west, [Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravia) (*Morava*) in the east, and [Czech Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Silesia) (*Slezsko*; the smaller, south-eastern part of historical [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia), most of which is located within modern Poland) in the northeast.[^cite_note-36] Known as the *lands of the Bohemian Crown* since the 14th century, a number of other names for the country have been used, including *Czech/Bohemian lands*, *Bohemian Crown*, *Czechia*,[^cite_note-37] and the *lands of the Crown of [Saint Wenceslaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia)*. When the country regained its independence after the dissolution of the [Austro-Hungarian empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) in 1918, the name *Czechoslovakia* was coined to reflect the union of the Czech and Slovak nations within one country.[^cite_note-Thompson2012-38]

After Czechoslovakia dissolved on 31 December 1992, *Česko* was adopted as the Czech short name for the new state, and the [Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Czech_Republic\)) recommended *Czechia* for the English-language equivalent.[^cite_note-39] This form was not widely adopted at the time, leading to the long name *Czech Republic* being used in English in nearly all circumstances. The Czech government [directed use of *Czechia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_the_Czech_Republic#Adoption_of_Czechia) as the official English name in 2016.[^cite_note-40] Czechia has been listed by the [United Nations](31769.md)[^cite_note-41] and is used by other organizations such as the [European Union](9317.md),[^cite_note-42] [NATO](21133.md),[^cite_note-43] the [CIA](5183633.md),[^cite_note-44] [Google Maps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Maps),[^cite_note-45] and the [European Broadcasting Union](10380.md).[^cite_note-46] In 2022, the American *[AP Stylebook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AP_Stylebook)* states "both \[Czechia and the Czech Republic] are acceptable. The shorter name Czechia is preferred by the Czech government. If using Czechia, clarify in the story that the country is more widely known in English as the Czech Republic."[^cite_note-47]

Archaeological evidence indicates that the area now known as the Czech Republic has been inhabited since the [Paleolithic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic). Notably, the [Venus of Dolní Věstonice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venus_of_Doln%C3%AD_V%C4%9Bstonice), a ceramic figurine dated to approximately 29,000–25,000 BC, was discovered in this region.[^cite_note-50] This artifact is considered the oldest known ceramic figurine in the world.[^cite_note-51]

In the [classical era](252905.md), as a result of the 3rd century BC [Celtic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celts) migrations, Bohemia became associated with the Boii.[^cite_note-Rankin2002-52] The Boii founded an [oppidum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oppidum) near the site of modern [Prague](23844.md).[^cite_note-(Firm)1997-53] In the 1st century AD, the Germanic tribes of the [Marcomanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcomanni) and [Quadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quadi) settled there.[^cite_note-Salvia2007-54]

[Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) from the [Black Sea](3386.md)–[Carpathian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_Mountains) region settled in the area (their migration was pushed by an invasion of peoples from [Siberia](29639.md) and Eastern Europe into their area:[^cite_note-Agnew2004-55] [Huns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huns), [Avars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_Avars), [Bulgars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulgars) and [Magyars](17106358.md)).[^cite_note-HahnNadel2014-56] In the 6th century, the Huns had moved westwards into Bohemia, Moravia, and some of present-day Austria and Germany.[^cite_note-HahnNadel2014-56]

During the 7th century, the [Frankish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) merchant [Samo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samo), supporting the Slavs fighting against nearby settled Avars,[^cite_note-BartlŠkvarna2002-57] became the ruler of the first documented Slavic state in Central Europe, Samo's Empire.[^cite_note-58] The principality of [Great Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Moravia), controlled by [Moymir dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moymirid_dynasty), arose in the 8th century.[^cite_note-Champion2005-59] It reached its zenith in the 9th century during the reign of [Svatopluk I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svatopluk_I_of_Moravia), holding off the influence of the Franks. Great Moravia was Christianized, with a role being played by the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) mission of [Cyril and Methodius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_and_Methodius).[^cite_note-60] They codified the [Old Church Slavonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic) language, the first literary and liturgical language of the Slavs, and the [Glagolitic script](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glagolitic_script).[^cite_note-BenedettoDuke2008-61]

### Bohemia

Main article: [Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemia)



The [Crown of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lands_of_the_Bohemian_Crown) within the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) (1600). The Czech lands were part of the Empire in 1002–1806, and Prague was the imperial seat in 1346–1437 and 1583–1611.

The principality of the [Bohemians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemians_\(tribe\)) was represented by many dukes and finally emerged as a centralized [Duchy of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bohemia) in the late 9th century when it was unified by the [Přemyslid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C5%99emyslid_dynasty). From 1002 until 1806, Bohemia was an [Imperial Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Estate) of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire).[^cite_note-PánekTůma2019-62]

In 1212, [Přemysl Ottokar I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottokar_I_of_Bohemia) extracted the [Golden Bull of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Bull_of_Sicily) from the emperor, confirming Ottokar and his descendants' royal status; the Duchy of Bohemia was raised to a kingdom.[^cite_note-PánekTůma2019b-63] [German immigrants](152735.md) settled in the Bohemian periphery in the 13th century.[^cite_note-PánekTůma2019c-64] The [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) in the [invasion of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Europe) carried their raids into Moravia but were defensively defeated at [Olomouc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olomouc).[^cite_note-65]

After a series of dynastic wars, the [House of Luxembourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Luxembourg) gained the Bohemian throne.[^cite_note-66] Efforts for a [reform of the church in Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Reformation) started already in the late 14th century. [Jan Hus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Hus)' followers seceded from some practices of the [Roman Church](606848.md) and in the [Hussite Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussite_Wars) (1419–1434) defeated five crusades organized against them by [Emperor Sigismund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismund,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). On 21 December 1421, [Jan Žižka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_%C5%BDi%C5%BEka), a successful military commander and mercenary, led his group of forces in the [Battle of Kutná Hora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kutn%C3%A1_Hora), resulting in a victory for the Hussites. He is honoured to this day as a national hero. During the next two centuries, 90% of the population in Bohemia and Moravia were considered [Hussites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussites). The pacifist thinker [Petr Chelčický](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Chel%C4%8Dick%C3%BD) inspired the movement of the [Moravian Brethren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_of_the_Brethren_\(Czech_Republic\)) (by the middle of the 15th century) that completely separated from the Roman Catholic Church.[^cite_note-67]

Painting of battle between mounted knights

Battle between [Hussites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussites) and [crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) during the [Hussite Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussite_Wars); Jena Codex, 15th century

After 1526, Bohemia came increasingly under [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) control as the Habsburgs became first the elected and then in 1627 the hereditary rulers of Bohemia. Between 1583 and 1611 Prague was the official seat of Holy Roman Emperor [Rudolf II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) and his court.

The [Defenestration of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenestrations_of_Prague) and subsequent revolt against the Habsburgs in 1618 marked the start of the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War). In 1620, the rebellion in Bohemia was crushed at the [Battle of White Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_White_Mountain), and the ties between Bohemia and the Habsburgs' hereditary lands in Austria were strengthened. The leaders of the [Bohemian Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Revolt) were [executed in 1621](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Square_execution). The nobility and the middle class Protestants had to either convert to Catholicism or leave the country.[^cite_note-68]

The following era of 1620 to the late 18th century became known as the "Dark Age". During the Thirty Years' War, the population of the [Czech lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_lands) declined by a third through the expulsion of Czech Protestants as well as war, disease and [famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famines_in_the_Czech_lands).[^cite_note-69] The Habsburgs prohibited all Christian confessions other than Catholicism.[^cite_note-70] The flowering of [Baroque culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Baroque_architecture) shows the ambiguity of this historical period. [Ottoman Turks](22278.md) and [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate) invaded Moravia in 1663.[^cite_note-71] In 1679–1680 the Czech lands faced the [Great Plague of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Plague_of_Vienna) and an uprising of serfs.[^cite_note-72] There were peasant uprisings influenced by famine.[^cite_note-73] [Serfdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serfdom) was abolished between 1781 and 1848. Several battles of the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) took place in the Czech lands.



The 1618 [Defenestration of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defenestrations_of_Prague) marked the beginning of the [Bohemian Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Revolt) against the [Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg) and therefore the first phase of the [Thirty Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty_Years'_War).

The end of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) in 1806 led to degradation of the political status of Bohemia which lost its position of an [electorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-elector) of the Holy Roman Empire as well as its own political representation in the [Imperial Diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Diet_\(Holy_Roman_Empire\)).[^cite_note-74] Bohemian lands became part of the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire). During the 18th and 19th century the [Czech National Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_National_Revival) began its rise, with the purpose to revive [Czech language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language), culture, and national identity. The [Revolution of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848_in_the_Austrian_Empire) in Prague, striving for liberal reforms and autonomy of the Bohemian Crown within the Austrian Empire, was suppressed.[^cite_note-auto-75]

It seemed that some concessions would be made also to Bohemia, but in the end, Emperor [Franz Joseph I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Joseph_I) affected a compromise with Hungary only. The [Austro-Hungarian Compromise of 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Compromise_of_1867) and the never-realized coronation of Franz Joseph as king of Bohemia led to profound disappointment among prominent Czech politicians.[^cite_note-auto-75] The Bohemian Crown lands became part of the so-called [Cisleithania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisleithania).

The Czech Social Democratic and progressive politicians started the fight for universal suffrage. The first elections under universal male suffrage were held in 1907.[^cite_note-76]

### Czechoslovakia

Main article: [History of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Czechoslovakia)



The [First Czechoslovak Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Czechoslovak_Republic) comprised 27% of the population of the former Austria-Hungary and nearly 80% of the industry.

[^cite_note-Stephen_J._Lee._Page_107.-77]

In 1918, during the collapse of the Habsburg monarchy at the end of World War I, the independent republic of [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), which joined the winning [Allied powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I), was created, with [Tomáš Garrigue Masaryk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Garrigue_Masaryk) in the lead.[^cite_note-78] This new country incorporated the [Bohemian Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Crown).[^cite_note-79]

The [First Czechoslovak Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Czechoslovak_Republic) comprised only 27% of the population of the former Austria-Hungary but nearly 80% of the industry, which enabled it to successfully compete with Western industrial states.[^cite_note-Stephen_J._Lee._Page_107.-77] In 1929 compared to 1913, the gross domestic product increased by 52% and industrial production by 41%. In 1938 Czechoslovakia was 10th in world industrial production.[^cite_note-80] Czechoslovakia was the only country in Central and Eastern Europe to remain a liberal democracy throughout the entire interwar period.[^cite_note-81] Although the First Czechoslovak Republic was a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state), it provided certain rights to its minorities, the largest being [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans_in_Czechoslovakia_\(1918%E2%80%931938\)) (23.6% in 1921), [Hungarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarians_in_Slovakia) (5.6%) and [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusyns_and_Ukrainians_in_Czechoslovakia_\(1918%E2%80%931938\)) (3.5%).[^cite_note-82]



Czechoslovak fighter pilots of [No. 310 Squadron RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._310_\(Czechoslovak\)_Squadron_RAF) during the [Battle of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Britain) in 1940

Western Czechoslovakia was [occupied by Nazi Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Czechoslovakia_\(1938%E2%80%931945\)) in 1938, which placed most of the region into the [Protectorate of Bohemia and Moravia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protectorate_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia). The protectorate was proclaimed part of the Third Reich, and the president and prime minister were subordinated to Nazi Germany's *[Reichsprotektor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protector_\(title\))*. [One Nazi concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresienstadt_Ghetto) was located within the Czech territory at [Terezín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terez%C3%ADn), north of Prague. The vast majority of the protectorate's Jews were murdered in [Nazi-run concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_concentration_camps). The Nazi *[Generalplan Ost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generalplan_Ost)* called for the extermination, expulsion, [Germanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanization) or enslavement of most or all Czechs for the purpose of providing more [living space for the German people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebensraum).[^cite_note-83] There was [Czechoslovak resistance to Nazi occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resistance_in_the_Protectorate_of_Bohemia_and_Moravia) as well as [reprisals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_war_crimes) against the Czechoslovaks for their anti-Nazi resistance. The German occupation ended on 9 May 1945, with the arrival of the Soviet and American armies and the [Prague uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_uprising).[^cite_note-84] Most German speakers were forcibly [expelled from the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Germans_from_Czechoslovakia), first as a result of local acts of violence and then under the aegis of an "organized transfer" confirmed by the Soviet Union, the United States, and Great Britain at the [Potsdam Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Conference).[^cite_note-85]



Prague during the 1968 [Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact_invasion_of_Czechoslovakia)

In the [1946 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_Czechoslovak_parliamentary_election), the [Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Czechoslovakia) gained 38%[^cite_note-86] of the votes and became the largest party in the Czechoslovak parliament, formed a coalition with other parties, and consolidated power. A Soviet-backed [coup d'état in 1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Czechoslovak_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) formed a single-party government. For the [next 41 years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Czechoslovakia_\(1948%E2%80%931989\)), the Czechoslovak communist state conformed to [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) economic and political features.[^cite_note-87] Perforating this tendency, the brief [Prague Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring) political liberalization was stopped by the 1968 [Warsaw Pact invasion of Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact_invasion_of_Czechoslovakia), which contributed to the [split on authoritarianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tankie) in the [international communist movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_communism) and the rise of [Eurocommunism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocommunism) in the West, while in Czechoslovakia, a repressive [period of normalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normalization_\(Czechoslovakia\)) ensued.[^cite_note-88]

[Václav Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel), one of the most important figures in Czech history during the 20th century—leader of the [Velvet Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Revolution), the last president of [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) and the first president of the Czech Republic

In November 1989, Czechoslovakia again became a liberal democracy through the [Velvet Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_Revolution). However, Slovak national aspirations strengthened ([Hyphen War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyphen_War)), and on 31 December 1992, the [country peacefully split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_Czechoslovakia) into the independent countries of the Czech Republic and [Slovakia](26830.md). Both countries went through economic reforms and privatizations, with the intention of creating a [market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_economy), as they had been trying to do since 1990 when Czechs and Slovaks still shared the common state. This process was largely successful; in 2006 the Czech Republic was recognized by the World Bank as a "developed country",[^cite_note-worldbank06-89] and in 2009 the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) ranked it as a nation of "Very High Human Development".[^cite_note-hdi09-90]

From 1991, the Czech Republic, originally as part of Czechoslovakia and since 1993 in its own right, has been a member of the [Visegrád Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visegr%C3%A1d_Group) and from 1995, the [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development). The Czech Republic joined [NATO](21133.md) in 1999, the [European Union](9317.md) in 2004, and the [Schengen Area](16565139.md) in 2007.[^cite_note-91]

Until 2017, either the centre-left [Czech Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democracy_\(Czech_Republic\)) or the centre-right [Civic Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Democratic_Party) led the government. In October 2017, the populist movement [ANO 2011](40808475.md), led by [Andrej Babiš](40378134.md), won [the elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Czech_parliamentary_election) with three times more votes than its closest rival, the Civic Democrats.[^cite_note-92] In December 2017, Czech President [Miloš Zeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Zeman) appointed Babiš as prime minister.[^cite_note-93] In the [2021 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Czech_parliamentary_election), ANO 2011 was narrowly defeated, and [Petr Fiala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Fiala) became prime minister.[^cite_note-94] He formed a government coalition of the alliance [Spolu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spolu) (Civic Democratic Party, [KDU-ČSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KDU-%C4%8CSL) and [TOP 09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TOP_09)) and the alliance of [Pirates and Mayors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirates_and_Mayors). In January 2023, retired general [Petr Pavel](42272533.md) won the [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Czech_presidential_election), succeeding Zeman.[^cite_note-95]

In February 2022, Russian forces began a large-scale [war against Ukraine](79400268.md). The Czech Republic took in half a million [Ukrainian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%932023_Ukrainian_refugee_crisis), the largest number per capita in the world.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] In December 2025, Babiš returned to office as prime minister,[^cite_note-98] following his populist ANO party's win in [October's parliamentary election](65703300.md).[^cite_note-99]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_the_Czech_Republic)



Topographic map



[Giant Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Mountains) in winter

The Czech Republic lies mostly between latitudes [48°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/48th_parallel_north) and [51° N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/51st_parallel_north) and longitudes [12°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_meridian_east) and [19° E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_meridian_east). Bohemia, to the west, consists of a basin drained by the [Elbe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbe) ([Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language): *Labe*) and the [Vltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vltava) rivers, surrounded by mostly low mountains, such as the [Giant Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Mountains) range ([Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language): *Krkonoše*) of the [Sudetes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudetes). The highest point in the country, [Sněžka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sn%C4%9B%C5%BEka) at 1,603 m (5,259 ft), is located here. Moravia in the east is hilly. It is drained mainly by the [Morava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morava_\(river\)) River, but it also contains the source of the [Oder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oder) River ([Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language): *Odra*). The three historical lands correspond with the river basins of the Elbe and the Vltava for Bohemia; the Morava for Moravia; and the Oder river basin for Czech Silesia.

Water from the Czech Republic flows to three different seas: the [North Sea](21179.md), [Baltic Sea](3335.md), and [Black Sea](3386.md). The Czech Republic also leases the [Moldauhafen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldauhafen), a 30,000-square-meter (7.4-acre) [lot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lot_\(real_estate\)) in the middle of the [Hamburg](13467.md) Docks, which was awarded to Czechoslovakia by Article 363 of the [Treaty of Versailles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Versailles), to allow the landlocked country a place where goods transported down river could be transferred to seagoing ships. The territory reverts to Germany in 2028.

### Climate



Köppen climate classification types of the Czech Republic using the −3 °C isotherm:&#x20;

&#x20; [Humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate)

&#x20; [Oceanic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate)

&#x20; [Subarctic climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subarctic_climate)

The climate is temperate, situated in the transition zone between the [oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) and [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) types, with warm summers and cold, cloudy and snowy winters. The temperature difference between summer and winter is the result of its landlocked geographical position.[^cite_note-100]

Temperatures vary depending on the elevation. In general, at higher altitudes, the temperatures decrease and precipitation increases. The coldest temperature ever measured was −42.2 °C (−44.0 °F) in [Litvínovice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litv%C3%ADnovice) near [České Budějovice](149384.md) in 1929, and the hottest measured was 40.4 °C (104.7 °F) in [Dobřichovice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dob%C5%99ichovice) in 2012.[^cite_note-101] The wettest area is found around [Bílý Potok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%ADl%C3%BD_Potok_\(Liberec_District\)) in [Jizera Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jizera_Mountains), and the driest region is the [Louny District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louny_District) to the northwest of Prague. Another factor is the distribution of the mountains. At the highest peak of [Sněžka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sn%C4%9B%C5%BEka) (1,603 m or 5,259 ft), the average temperature is −0.4 °C (31 °F), whereas in the lowlands of the [South Moravian Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Moravian_Region), the average temperature is as high as 10 °C (50 °F). Prague has a similar average temperature, although this is influenced by urban factors.

The coldest month is usually January, followed by February and December. During these months, there is snow in the mountains and sometimes in the cities and lowlands. During March, April, and May, the temperature usually increases, especially during April, when the temperature and weather tends to vary during the day. Spring is also characterized by higher water levels in the rivers, due to melting snow with occasional flooding. The warmest month of the year is July, followed by August and June. On average, summer temperatures are about 20–30 °C (36–54 °F) higher than during winter. Summer is also characterized by rain and storms. Autumn generally begins in September, which is still warm and dry. During October, temperatures usually fall below 15 °C (59 °F) or 10 °C (50 °F) and deciduous trees begin to shed their leaves. By the end of November, temperatures usually range around the freezing point.

Most rain falls during the summer. Sporadic rainfall is throughout the year (in Prague, the average number of days per month experiencing at least 0.1 mm (0.0039 in) of rain varies from 12 in September and October to 16 in November), but concentrated rainfall (days with more than 10 mm (0.39 in) per day) are more frequent in the months of May to August (average around two such days per month).[^cite_note-102] Severe thunderstorms, producing damaging straight-line winds, hail, and occasional tornadoes occur, especially during the summer period.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

### Biodiversity and conservation

See also: [Protected areas of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_areas_of_the_Czech_Republic) and [Fauna of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna_of_the_Czech_Republic)

[Phytogeographically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phytogeography), the Czech Republic belongs to the Central European province of the [Circumboreal Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circumboreal_Region), within the [Boreal kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boreal_kingdom). According to the [World Wide Fund for Nature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Fund_for_Nature), the territory can be subdivided into four ecoregions: the [Western European broadleaf forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_European_broadleaf_forests), [Central European mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_mixed_forests), [Pannonian mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannonian_mixed_forests), and [Carpathian montane conifer forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpathian_montane_conifer_forests).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-105]

As of 2020, the Czech Republic ranks as the 21st most environmentally conscious country in the world in [Environmental Performance Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Performance_Index).[^cite_note-106] It had a 2018 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 1.71/10, ranking it 160th globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-FLII-Supplementary-107] The Czech Republic has four national parks ([Šumava National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0umava_National_Park), [Krkonoše National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krkono%C5%A1e_National_Park), [České Švýcarsko National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cesk%C3%A9_%C5%A0v%C3%BDcarsko_National_Park), [Podyjí National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podyj%C3%AD_National_Park)) and 25 protected landscape areas. Fauna includes a wide variety of animal species. Some species (especially endangered ones) are bred in reserves. Among the rare animals are, for example, eagles, ospreys, bustards, and storks.[^cite_note-108]

The Czech Republic is a pluralist multi-party [parliamentary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_democracy) [representative democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy). The [Parliament](3991200.md) (*Parlament České republiky*) is bicameral, with the [Chamber of Deputies](3991120.md) ([Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language): *Poslanecká sněmovna*, 200 members) and the [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_the_Czech_Republic) ([Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language): *Senát*, 81 members).[^cite_note-109] The [members of the Chamber of Deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament#Czech_Republic) are elected for a four-year term by [proportional representation](23298.md), with a 5% [election threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_threshold). There are 14 voting districts, identical to the country's administrative regions. The Chamber of Deputies, the successor to the [Czech National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_National_Council), has the powers and responsibilities of the now defunct federal parliament of the former Czechoslovakia. The members of the Senate are elected in single-seat [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district) by two-round [runoff voting](25932.md) for a six-year term, with one-third elected every even year in the autumn. This arrangement is modeled on the [U.S. Senate](24909346.md), but each constituency is roughly the same size and the voting system used is a two-round runoff.

The president is a formal head of state with limited and specific powers, who appoints the prime minister, as well the other members of the cabinet on a proposal by the prime minister. From 1993 until 2012, the [President of the Czech Republic](410288.md) was selected by a joint session of the parliament for a five-year term, with no more than two consecutive terms ([Václav Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel) and [Václav Klaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Klaus) were both elected twice). Since 2013, the president has been elected directly.[^cite_note-110] Some commentators have argued that, with the introduction of direct election of the President, the Czech Republic has moved away from the parliamentary system and towards a [semi-presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-presidential_system) one.[^cite_note-111] The [Government](14380629.md)'s exercise of executive power derives from the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Czech_Republic). The members of the government are the [Prime Minister](410283.md), Deputy prime ministers and other ministers. The Government is responsible to the [Chamber of Deputies](3991120.md).[^cite_note-112] The Prime Minister is the head of government and wields powers such as the right to set the agenda for most foreign and domestic policy and choose government ministers.[^cite_note-113]

### Law

Main article: [Law of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_of_the_Czech_Republic)

See also: [Gun law in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gun_law_in_the_Czech_Republic), [Judiciary of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary_of_the_Czech_Republic), [Law enforcement in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_the_Czech_Republic), and [Self-defence (Czech Republic)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-defence_\(Czech_Republic\))



Interior of the [Constitutional Court of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_the_Czech_Republic) in [Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno)

The Czech Republic is a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state),[^cite_note-CabadaWaisová2011-114] with a [civil law system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_law_\(legal_system\)) based on the continental type, rooted in Germanic legal culture. The basis of the legal system is the [Constitution of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Czech_Republic) adopted in 1993.[^cite_note-Kuklík2015-115] The [Penal Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_code) is effective from 2010. The civil code became effective in 2014. The court system includes district, county, and supreme courts and is divided into civil, criminal, and administrative branches.&#x20;

The [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_the_Czech_Republic) consists of 15 constitutional judges and oversees violations of the constitution by either the legislature or by the [government](14380629.md).[^cite_note-Kuklík2015-115] The [Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_the_Czech_Republic) is formed of 67 judges and is the court of highest appeal for most legal cases. The [Supreme Administrative Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Administrative_Court_of_the_Czech_Republic) decides on issues of procedural and administrative propriety. It also has jurisdiction over certain political matters, such as the formation and closure of political parties, jurisdictional boundaries between government entities, and the eligibility of persons to stand for public office.[^cite_note-Kuklík2015-115] The Supreme Court and the Supreme Administrative Court are both based in [Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno), as is the Supreme Public Prosecutor's Office.[^cite_note-Kuklík2015-115]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_the_Czech_Republic)

See also: [Ministry of Foreign Affairs of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_the_Czech_Republic)



Visa-free entry countries for Czech citizens in green, EU in blue (see [citizenship of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_of_the_European_Union))

The Czech Republic has ranked as [one of the safest or most peaceful countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Peace_Index) for the past few decades.[^cite_note-116] It is a member of the United Nations, the [European Union](9317.md), [NATO](21133.md), [OECD](33853117.md), [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe) and is an observer to the [Organization of American States](22788.md).[^cite_note-117]

The Czech passport is [restricted by visas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visa_requirements_for_Czech_citizens). According to the 2018 [Henley & Partners Visa Restrictions Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henley_&_Partners_Visa_Restrictions_Index), citizens have visa-free access to 173 countries, which ranks them 7th globally.[^cite_note-118] The [World Tourism Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Tourism_Organization) ranks the Czech passport 24th.[^cite_note-119] The US [Visa Waiver Program](421825.md) applies to Czech nationals.



[Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Czech_Republic\)) in [Prague](23844.md)

The [prime minister](410283.md) and [minister of foreign affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_the_Czech_Republic) have primary roles in setting foreign policy, although the [president](410288.md) also has influence and represents the country abroad. Membership in the European Union and NATO is central to foreign policy. The [Office for Foreign Relations and Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_for_Foreign_Relations_and_Information) serves as the foreign intelligence agency responsible for espionage and foreign policy briefings, as well as protection of embassies abroad.

The Czech Republic has ties with Slovakia, Poland and Hungary as a member of the [Visegrád Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visegr%C3%A1d_Group),[^cite_note-120] as well as with Germany,[^cite_note-121] Israel,[^cite_note-122] the United States[^cite_note-123] and the European Union and its members. After 2020, relations with Asian democratic states, such as Taiwan, are being strengthened.[^cite_note-124] Conversely, the Czech Republic has long had bad relations with Russia; from 2021, the Czech Republic appears on Russia's official list of enemy countries.[^cite_note-125] The Czech Republic also has problematic relations with China.

Czech officials have supported dissenters in Belarus, Moldova, Myanmar and Cuba.[^cite_note-democrats-126] Notable Czech diplomats of the past include [Jaroslav Lev of Rožmitál](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaroslav_Lev_of_Ro%C5%BEmit%C3%A1l), [Humprecht Jan Czernin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humprecht_Jan_Czernin), Count [Philip Kinsky of Wchinitz and Tettau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_Kinsky_of_Wchinitz_and_Tettau), [Wenzel Anton, Prince of Kaunitz-Rietberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenzel_Anton,_Prince_of_Kaunitz-Rietberg), Prince [Karl Philipp Schwarzenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Philipp,_Prince_of_Schwarzenberg), [Alois Lexa von Aehrenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alois_Lexa_von_Aehrenthal), [Ottokar Czernin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottokar_Czernin), [Edvard Beneš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edvard_Bene%C5%A1), [Jan Masaryk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Masaryk), [Jiří Hájek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_H%C3%A1jek), [Jiří Dienstbier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Dienstbier), [Michael Žantovský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_%C5%BDantovsk%C3%BD), [Petr Kolář](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Kol%C3%A1%C5%99), [Alexandr Vondra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandr_Vondra), Prince [Karel Schwarzenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Schwarzenberg) and [Petr Pavel](42272533.md).

### Military

See also: [Designated Reserves (Czech Republic)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designated_Reserves_\(Czech_Republic\))



General Staff of the [Army of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Czech_Republic) in Prague

The [Czech Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Armed_Forces) consist of the [Czech Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Land_Forces), the [Czech Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Air_Force) and of specialized support units. The armed forces are managed by the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(Czech_Republic\)). The president is commander-in-chief of the armed forces. In 2004 the army became a fully professional organization, and compulsory military service was abolished. Defence spending is approximately 1.28% of the GDP (2021).[^cite_note-127] The armed forces are charged with protecting the Czech Republic and its allies, promoting global security interests, and contributing to NATO.

As a member of NATO, the military participated in the [Resolute Support Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resolute_Support_Mission) and [Kosovo Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Force) operations. The Czech Air Force served in the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states) and [Iceland](14531.md).[^cite_note-128] The main equipment of the Czech military includes [JAS 39 Gripen](87577.md) multi-role fighters, [Aero L-159 Alca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_L-159_Alca) combat aircraft, [AH-1Z Viper](10805384.md) attack helicopters, armored vehicles ([Pandur II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandur_II), [BVP-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-2)) and tanks ([T-72M4CZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72M4CZ) and [Leopard 2A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard_2A4)).

### Human rights

See also: [LGBT rights in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_the_Czech_Republic)

[Human rights in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_the_Czech_Republic) are guaranteed by the [Charter of Fundamental Rights and Freedoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_of_Fundamental_Rights_and_Freedoms) and international treaties on human rights. Conducting [same-sex marriage](92656.md) is not legal.[^cite_note-133]

### Administrative divisions

See also: [Obec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obec)

The Czech Republic is divided into [thirteen regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_the_Czech_Republic) ([Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language): [*kraje*, singular *kraj*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/en\:kraj#Czech)) and the capital city of Prague. Every region has its own elected regional assembly and a regional governor. In Prague, the assembly and presidential powers are executed by the city council and the mayor. The 76 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_the_Czech_Republic) (*okresy*, singular *[okres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okres)*) including three "statutory cities" (without Prague, which had special status) lost most of their importance in 1999 in an administrative reform; they remain as territorial divisions and seats of various branches of state administration.[^cite_note-134]

The smallest administrative units are [*obce* (municipalities)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obec). As of 2021, there are 6,254 municipalities. Cities and towns are also municipalities. Prague is both a region and a municipality.

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_the_Czech_Republic)



Real GDP per capita in the Czech Republic from 1973 to 2018



The Czech Republic is part of the [European Single Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Single_Market) and the [Schengen Area](16565139.md) (dark and light blue on the map above), but uses its own currency, the [Czech koruna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_koruna).

The Czech Republic has a [developed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMF_advanced_economy_list),[^cite_note-135] [high-income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank_high-income_economy)[^cite_note-136] [export-oriented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_nation) [social market economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_market_economy) based in services, manufacturing and innovation, that maintains a [welfare state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_state) and the [European social model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_social_model).[^cite_note-An_Ideal-Typical_Perspective-137] The Czech Republic participates in the [European Single Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Single_Market) as a member of the European Union and is therefore a part of the [economy of the European Union](1598533.md) but uses its own currency, the [Czech koruna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_koruna), instead of the [euro](9472.md). It has a per capita GDP rate that is 91% of the EU average[^cite_note-138] and is a member of the [OECD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_for_Economic_Co-operation_and_Development). [Monetary policy](297032.md) is conducted by the [Czech National Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_National_Bank), whose independence is guaranteed by the constitution. The Czech Republic ranks 12th in the [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme) [inequality-adjusted human development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_inequality-adjusted_HDI) and 24th in [World Bank Human Capital Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Bank_Human_Capital_Index). It is described by *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* as "one of Europe's most flourishing economies".[^cite_note-139]

As of 2023, the [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita) at [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) is \$51,329[^cite_note-140] and \$29,856 at [nominal value](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_versus_nominal_value_\(economics\)).[^cite_note-IMFWEOCZ201901-141] According to Allianz A.G., in 2024 the country was a mean wealth country, ranking 24th in net financial assets.[^cite_note-142] The country experienced a 4.5% [GDP growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_growth) in 2017.[^cite_note-143] The 2016 unemployment rate was the lowest in the EU at 2.4%,[^cite_note-Eurostat_Unemployment_2016-144] and the 2016 poverty rate was the second lowest of OECD members.[^cite_note-FT_19_October_2016-145] It ranked 32nd in the 2025 [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index),[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] 29th in the [Global Competitiveness Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Competitiveness_Report),[^cite_note-GCR_2018/19-148] and 25th in the [Global Enabling Trade Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Enabling_Trade_Report).[^cite_note-149]

The diverse economy [ranks 7th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_economic_complexity) in the 2016 [Economic Complexity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Complexity_Index).[^cite_note-Atlas_of_Economic_Complexity-150] The industrial sector accounts for 37.5% of the economy, while services account for 60% and agriculture for 2.5%.[^cite_note-151] The largest trading partner for both export and import is Germany and the EU in general. Dividends worth CZK 270 billion were paid to the foreign owners of Czech companies in 2017, which has become a political issue.[^cite_note-152]

### Industry

See also: [Automotive industry in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_industry_in_the_Czech_Republic)



[Škoda Octavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0koda_Octavia) RS iV

In 2018, the [largest companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_companies_of_the_Czech_Republic#Largest_by_revenue) by revenue were: automobile manufacturer [Škoda Auto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0koda_Auto), utility company [ČEZ Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CEZ_Group), conglomerate [Agrofert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrofert), energy trading company [EPH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energetick%C3%BD_a_pr%C5%AFmyslov%C3%BD_holding), oil processing company [Unipetrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unipetrol), electronics manufacturer [Foxconn CZ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxconn_CZ) and steel producer [Moravia Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C5%99inec_Iron_and_Steel_Works).[^cite_note-153] Other transportation companies include: [Škoda Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0koda_Transportation) (tramways, trolleybuses, metro), [Tatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatra_\(company\)) (heavy trucks, the second oldest car maker in the world), [Avia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avia_Motors) (medium trucks), [Karosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karosa) and [SOR Libchavy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SOR_Libchavy) (buses), [Aero Vodochody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aero_Vodochody) (military aircraft), [Let Kunovice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let_Kunovice) (civil aircraft), [Zetor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zetor) (tractors), [Jawa Moto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawa_Moto) (motorcycles) and [Čezeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cesk%C3%A1_zbrojovka_Strakonice) (electric scooters).

Škoda Transportation is the fourth largest [tram](30733.md) producer in the world; nearly one third of all trams in the world come from Czech factories.[^cite_note-154] The Czech Republic is also the world's largest [vinyl records](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phonograph_record) manufacturer, with [GZ Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GZ_Media) producing about 6 million pieces annually in [Loděnice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lod%C4%9Bnice_\(Beroun_District\)).[^cite_note-155] Food industry companies include [Agrofert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrofert), [Kofola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofola) and [Hamé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ham%C3%A9_\(company\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).&#x20;

Defense has also been a traditionally important part of industry, with Czechoslovakia having been the world's largest exporter of arms in 1934 and 1935.[^cite_note-Euro_platinum-156] In 2025, the largest defense companies included the [Czechoslovak Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Group) (worth € 31,3 billion), [STV Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/STV_Group_\(defence_company\)) (worth € 1,13 billion), [Colt CZ Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colt_CZ_Group) (worth € 1,09 billion) and [Omnipol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omnipol) (worth € 0,58 billion).[^cite_note-Euro_platinum-156]

### Energy

Main article: [Energy in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_in_the_Czech_Republic)



[Dukovany Nuclear Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukovany_Nuclear_Power_Station)

Production of electricity exceeds consumption by about 10 [TWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilowatt_hour) per year, the excess being exported. Nuclear power presently provides about 30 percent of the total power needs, and its share is projected to increase to 40 percent. In 2005, 65.4 percent of electricity was produced by steam and combustion power plants (mostly coal); 30 percent by nuclear plants; and 4.6 percent came from renewable sources, including hydropower. The largest power resource is [Temelín Nuclear Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temel%C3%ADn_Nuclear_Power_Station), with another nuclear power plant in [Dukovany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dukovany_Nuclear_Power_Station).

The Czech Republic is reducing its dependence on highly polluting low-grade [brown coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lignite) as a source of energy. Natural gas is purchased from Norwegian companies and as liquefied gas from the Netherlands and Belgium. In the past, three-quarters of gas supplies came from Russia, but after the start of the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine, the government gradually stopped these supplies.[^cite_note-157] Gas consumption (approx. 100 TWh in 2003–2005) is almost double electricity consumption. South Moravia has small [oil and gas deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_and_gas_deposits_in_the_Czech_Republic).

### Transport infrastructure

Main article: [Transport in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_the_Czech_Republic)



[Václav Havel Airport Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel_Airport_Prague)

As of 2020, the road network is 55,768.3 kilometers (34,652.82 mi) long, out of which 1,276.4 km (793.1 mi) are motorways.[^cite_note-158] The Czech Republic has one of the [densest rail networks in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_rail_transport_network_size). As of 2020, the country has 9,542 kilometers (5,929 mi) of lines. Of that number, 3,236 km (2,011 mi) is electrified, 7,503 km (4,662 mi) are single-line tracks and 2,040 km (1,270 mi) are double and multiple-line tracks.[^cite_note-159] The length of tracks is 15,360 km (9,540 mi), out of which 6,917 km (4,298 mi) is electrified.[^cite_note-160]

[České dráhy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cesk%C3%A9_dr%C3%A1hy) (lit. 'Czech railways') is the main railway operator in the country, with about 170 million passengers carried annually.[^cite_note-161] Maximum speed is limited to 160 km/h (99 mph).

[Václav Havel Airport Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel_Airport_Prague) is the main international airport. In 2019, it handled 17.8 million passengers.[^cite_note-162] There are [91 airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_the_Czech_Republic), six of which provide international air services. The public international airports are in [Brno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brno-Tu%C5%99any_Airport), [Karlovy Vary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovy_Vary_Airport), [Mnichovo Hradiště](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mnichovo_Hradi%C5%A1t%C4%9B_Airport?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Mošnov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo%C5%A1_Jan%C3%A1%C4%8Dek_Airport_Ostrava) (near [Ostrava](464078.md)), [Pardubice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pardubice_Airport) and Prague.[^cite_note-163] The non-public international airports capable of handling airliners are in [Kunovice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kunovice_Airport) and [Vodochody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodochody_Airport).[^cite_note-164]

### Communications and IT

Main article: [Internet in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_in_the_Czech_Republic)



The main office of the antivirus group [Avast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avast) in [Prague](23844.md)

In 2006, Český Telecom was acquired by globalized company (Spain-owned) Telefónica group and adopted the new name [Telefónica O2 Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telef%C3%B3nica_O2_Czech_Republic). As of 2017, VDSL and ADSL2+ are offered in variants, with download speeds of up to 50 Mbit/s and upload speeds of up to 5 Mbit/s. Cable internet is gaining more popularity with its higher download speeds ranging from 50 Mbit/s to 1 Gbit/s.

Two [computer security](7398.md) companies, [Avast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avast) and [AVG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AVG_Technologies), were founded in the Czech Republic. In 2016, Avast led by [Pavel Baudiš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Baudi%C5%A1) bought rival AVG for [US\$](18717338.md)1.3 billion, together at the time, these companies had a user base of about 400 million people and 40% of the consumer market outside of China.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166] Avast is the leading provider of antivirus software, with a 20.5% market share.[^cite_note-167]

### Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_the_Czech_Republic)



Medieval castle [Karlštejn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl%C5%A1tejn)

&#x20;Prague is the fifth most visited city in Europe.[^cite_note-Bremner_2015-168] In 2001, the total earnings from tourism reached 118 billion [CZK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_koruna), making up 5.5% of [GNP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Measures_of_national_income_and_output) and 9% of its overall export earnings. The industry employs more than 110,000 people – over 1% of the population.[^cite_note-169] Guidebooks and tourists reporting overcharging by taxi drivers and pickpocketing problems talk mainly about Prague, though the situation has improved recently.[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-indy-171] Prague's mayor, [Pavel Bém](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_B%C3%A9m), worked to improve this reputation by cracking down on petty crime[^cite_note-indy-171] and, aside from these problems, Prague is a "safe" city.[^cite_note-172] The crime rate is described by the United States State department as "low".[^cite_note-173]



[Český Krumlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cesk%C3%BD_Krumlov)

The Czech Republic boasts [17 UNESCO World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_the_Czech_Republic), 3 of them being transnational. As of 2024, further 13 sites are on the tentative list.[^cite_note-174] Architectural heritage is an object of interest to visitors – it includes [castles and châteaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lists_of_castles_by_country) from different historical epochs, namely [Karlštejn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl%C5%A1tejn) Castle, [Český Krumlov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cesk%C3%BD_Krumlov) and the [Lednice–Valtice Cultural Landscape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lednice%E2%80%93Valtice_Cultural_Landscape). There are [12 cathedrals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cathedrals_in_the_Czech_Republic) and 15 churches elevated to the rank of [basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_basilica) by the pope, as well as many monasteries.&#x20;

Away from the towns, areas such as [Bohemian Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Paradise), [Bohemian Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_Forest) and the [Giant Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_Mountains) attract visitors seeking outdoor pursuits. The country is also known for its [various museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_the_Czech_Republic), puppetry and marionette exhibitions that take part within larger puppet festivals, and beer festivals.[^cite_note-175] [Aquapalace Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquapalace_Prague) in [Čestlice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8Cestlice) is the largest water park in the country.

### Science



Chemist [Jaroslav Heyrovský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaroslav_Heyrovsk%C3%BD), Nobel Prize winner

The Czech lands have a long and well-documented history of scientific innovation.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] Today, the Czech Republic has a developed scientific community supported by the government,[^cite_note-178] industry,[^cite_note-179] and leading [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Universities).[^cite_note-180] Czech scientists are embedded members of the global scientific community.[^cite_note-181] They contribute annually to multiple international academic journals and collaborate with their colleagues across boundaries and fields.[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185] The Czech Republic was ranked 24th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2020 and 2021, up from 26th in 2019.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187][^cite_note-188]

Historically, the Czech lands, especially Prague, have been the seat of scientific discovery going back to early modern times, including [Tycho Brahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tycho_Brahe), [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus), and [Johannes Kepler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johannes_Kepler). In 1784 the scientific community was first formally organized under the charter of the [Royal Czech Society of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Czech_Society_of_Sciences). Currently, this organization is known as the [Czech Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Academy_of_Sciences).[^cite_note-189] Similarly, the Czech lands have a well-established history of scientists,[^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191] including Nobel laureates biochemists [Gerty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerty_Cori) and [Carl Ferdinand Cori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Ferdinand_Cori), chemists [Jaroslav Heyrovský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaroslav_Heyrovsk%C3%BD) (inventor of [polarography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polarography), [electroanalytical chemistry](2408.md) and recipient of the Nobel Prize) and [Otto Wichterle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Wichterle) ([chemists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemist) responsible for the invention of the modern [contact lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_lens) and silon-[synthetic fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_fiber)), physicists [Ernst Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Mach) (physicist and critic of Newton's theories of space and time, foreshadowing Einstein's theory of relativity) and [Peter Grünberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Gr%C3%BCnberg), physiologist [Jan Evangelista Purkyně](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Evangelista_Purkyn%C4%9B) and chemist [Antonín Holý](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton%C3%ADn_Hol%C3%BD) (scientist and chemist, in 2009 was involved in creation of the most effective drug in the treatment of AIDS),[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] [Sigmund Freud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigmund_Freud), the founder of psychoanalysis, was born in [Příbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C5%99%C3%ADbor),[^cite_note-194] [Gregor Mendel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Mendel), the founder of genetics, was born in [Hynčice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyn%C4%8Dice_\(Vra%C5%BEn%C3%A9\)) and spent most of his life in Brno,[^cite_note-195] logician and mathematician [Kurt Gödel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_G%C3%B6del) was born in Brno.



Eli Beamlines Science Center with the most powerful laser in the world in [Dolní Břežany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doln%C3%AD_B%C5%99e%C5%BEany)

Other famous scientists include: [Stanislav Brebera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanislav_Brebera) (inventor of the plastic explosive [Semtex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semtex)),[^cite_note-196] [Václav Prokop Diviš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Prokop_Divi%C5%A1) (inventor of the first grounded [lightning rod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning_rod)), [Jakub Husník](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_Husn%C3%ADk) (improved the process of [photolithography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photolithography)), [Jan Janský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Jansk%C3%BD) (serologist and neurologist, discovered the [ABO blood groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABO_blood_groups)), [Karel Klíč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Kl%C3%AD%C4%8D) (painter and photographer, inventor of the [photogravure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photogravure)), [František Křižík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franti%C5%A1ek_K%C5%99i%C5%BE%C3%ADk) (electrical engineer, inventor of the [arc lamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc_lamp)), [Jan Marek Marci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Marek_Marci) (mathematician, physicist and imperial physician, one of the founders of [spectroscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscopy)),[^cite_note-197] [Vladimír Remek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladim%C3%ADr_Remek) (the first person outside of the Soviet Union and the United States to go into space), [Jakub Kryštof Rad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakub_Kry%C5%A1tof_Rad) (inventor of sugar cubes), [Josef Ressel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Ressel) (inventor of the screw propeller and modern compass).[^cite_note-science1-198]

Historically, most scientific research was recorded in Latin, but from the 18th century onwards increasingly in German and Czech, archived in libraries supported and managed by religious groups and other denominations, as evidenced by historical locations of international renown and heritage such as the [Strahov Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strahov_Monastery) and the [Clementinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clementinum) in [Prague](23844.md). Increasingly, scientists publish their work and that of their history in English.[^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200]

Current important scientific institutions include the [Academy of Sciences of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Academy_of_Sciences), the [CEITEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Institute_of_Technology) Institute in Brno and the [HiLASE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiLASE?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Eli Beamlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eli_Beamlines?action=edit\&redlink=1) centers with the most powerful laser in the world in [Dolní Břežany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doln%C3%AD_B%C5%99e%C5%BEany). Prague is the seat of the administrative center of the GSA Agency operating the European [navigation system Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_\(satellite_navigation_system\)) and the [European Union Agency for the Space Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Agency_for_the_Space_Programme).

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_the_Czech_Republic)

The [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) in 2020 was estimated at 1.71 children per woman, which is below the replacement rate of 2.1.[^cite_note-202] The population has an average age of 43.3 years.[^cite_note-203] The life expectancy in 2021 was estimated to be 79.5 years (76.55 years male, 82.61 years female).[^cite_note-cia.gov-204] In 2024, there were 121,823 immigrants to and 84,978 emigrants from the Czech Republic, for a net migration of 36,845, of whom 17 thousand were Ukrainian and 4.7 thousand were Slovak.[^cite_note-205] [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people_in_the_Czech_Republic) immigrants began settling in the country during the Communist period, when they were invited as guest workers by the Czechoslovak government.[^cite_note-206] In 2009, there were about 70,000 Vietnamese in the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-207] Most have decided to stay in the country permanently.[^cite_note-208]

According to results of the 2021 census, the majority of the inhabitants are [Czechs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs) (57.3%), followed by [Moravians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravians_\(ethnic_group\)) (3.4%), [Slovaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovaks) (0.9%), [Ukrainians](145144.md) (0.7%), [Viets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people) (0.3%), [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) (0.3%), [Russians](59510.md) (0.2%), [Silesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesians) (0.1%) and [Germans](152735.md) (0.1%). Another 4.0% declared a combination of two nationalities (3.6% combination of Czech and other nationality). As the 'nationality' was an optional item, a number of people left this field blank (31.6%).[^cite_note-census2021-9] According to some estimates, there are about 250,000 [Romani people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romani_people) in the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210] The [Polish minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_minority_in_the_Czech_Republic) resides mainly in the [Trans-Olza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Olza) region.[^cite_note-211]

There were 658,564 foreigners residing in the country in 2021,[^cite_note-212] according to the [Czech Statistical Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Statistical_Office), with the largest groups being [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainians_in_the_Czech_Republic) (22%), [Slovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovaks_in_the_Czech_Republic) (22%), [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people_in_the_Czech_Republic) (12%), [Russian](59510.md) (7%) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germans_in_the_Czech_Republic) (4%). Most of the foreign population lives in Prague (37.3%) and Central Bohemia Region (13.2%).[^cite_note-213]

About 75%[^cite_note-221] to 79%[^cite_note-Czech_Statistical_Office_27_February_2014-222] of residents do not declare having any religion or faith in surveys, and the proportion of convinced [atheists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism) (30%) is the third highest in the world behind those of China (47%) and Japan (31%).[^cite_note-Global_Index_of_Religion_and_Atheism-223] The Czech people have been historically characterized as "tolerant and even indifferent towards religion".[^cite_note-224] The religious identity of the country has changed drastically since the first half of the 20th century, when more than 90% of Czechs were Christians.[^cite_note-225]



[Saint Wenceslaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia) is the patron saint of the Czech lands

[Christianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization) took place in the 9th and 10th centuries. After the Bohemian Reformation, most Czechs became followers of Jan Hus, [Petr Chelčický](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Chel%C4%8Dick%C3%BD) and other regional [Protestant Reformers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Reformers). [Taborites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taborites) and [Utraquists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utraquists) were [Hussite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussite) groups. Towards the end of the [Hussite Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussite_Wars), the Utraquists changed sides and allied with the Roman Catholic Church. Following the joint Utraquist—Roman Catholic victory, Utraquism was accepted as a distinct form of Christianity to be practiced in Bohemia by the Roman Catholic Church while all remaining Hussite groups were prohibited. After the [Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation), some Bohemians went with the [teachings of Martin Luther](23371382.md), especially [Sudeten Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudeten_Germans).&#x20;

In the wake of the Reformation, Utraquist Hussites took a renewed increasingly [anti-Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Catholic) stance, while some of the defeated Hussite factions were revived. After the Habsburgs regained control of Bohemia, the whole population was forcibly converted to Roman Catholicism—even the Utraquist Hussites. Going forward, Czechs have become more wary and pessimistic of religion as such. A history of resistance to the Roman Catholic Church followed. It suffered a schism with the neo-Hussite [Czechoslovak Hussite Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Hussite_Church) in 1920, lost the bulk of its adherents during the Communist era and continues to lose in the modern, ongoing [secularization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularization). Protestantism never recovered after the [Counter-Reformation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-Reformation) was introduced by the [Austrian Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy) in 1620.&#x20;

Prior to the Holocaust, the Czech Republic had a sizable Jewish community of around 100,000. There are many historically important and culturally relevant synagogues in the Czech Republic such as Europe's oldest active synagogue, the [Old New Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_New_Synagogue), and the second largest synagogue in Europe, the [Great Synagogue (Plzeň)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Synagogue_\(Plze%C5%88\)). The Holocaust decimated Czech Jewry, and the Jewish population as of 2021 is 3,900.[^cite_note-226]

According to the 2011 census, 34% of the population stated they had no religion, 10.3% were Roman Catholic, 0.8% were [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) (0.5% [Czech Brethren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Church_of_Czech_Brethren) and 0.4% [Hussite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_Hussite_Church)),[^cite_note-227] and 9% followed other forms of religion both denominational or not (of which 863 people answered they are [pagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neopaganism)). 45% of the population did not answer the question about religion.[^cite_note-Czech_Statistical_Office-220] From 1991 to 2001 and further to 2011 the adherence to Roman Catholicism decreased from 39% to 27% and then to 10%; Protestantism similarly declined from 3.7% to 2% and then to 0.8%.[^cite_note-228] The Muslim population is estimated to be 20,000 representing 0.2% of the population.[^cite_note-229] The proportion of religious believers varies significantly across the country, from 55% in [Zlín Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zl%C3%ADn_Region) to 16% in [Ústí nad Labem Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Ast%C3%AD_nad_Labem_Region).[^cite_note-230]

### Languages



Dialects of Czech, [Moravian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_dialects), [Lach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lach_dialects), and [Cieszyn Silesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cieszyn_Silesian) spoken in the Czech Republic. In the border areas, where German was formerly spoken, mixed Czech dialects are now spoken.

The official language is [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_language), a [West Slavic language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Slavic_language) traditionally divided into four principal dialect groups that shares a very high [mutual intelligibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutual_intelligibility) with the [Slovak language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_language).[^cite_note-231][^cite_note-232] Although the constitution does not specify an official language of the republic, Czech is spoken as sole mother tongue by 92% to 98% of the population depending on the survey, making the Czech Republic one of the most linguistically homogeneous countries in Europe.[^cite_note-233]

The government recognizes [14 national minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minority_languages_of_the_Czech_Republic), whose respective languages are protected.[^cite_note-234] [Until 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_Germans_from_Czechoslovakia), German was the mother tongue of over 30% of the people.[^cite_note-235] Today, only [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language) is geographically concentrated enough to result in significant language infrastructure, including bilingual place names in the [Cieszyn Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cieszyn_Silesia) region.[^cite_note-236]

Among foreign languages, English has drastically increased in popularity since 1990 and is studied by over 98% of public school students. German is the principal second foreign language and studied by about 50% of students, a value that has remained stable over the past decades.[^cite_note-237][^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239] Russian has been significantly declining in popularity since 1990 and is now studied by less than 20% of students, while French and Spanish have gradually increased in popularity to about 6-8% each.[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241] The 2024 [Eurobarometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurobarometer) poll reported 41% feeling proficient in English, 12% in German, and 6.5% in Russian.[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243]

### Education and health care



[Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) is the oldest university in [Central Europe](5188.md)

[Education in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_the_Czech_Republic) is compulsory for nine years, and citizens have access to a [free-tuition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_education) [university education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education_in_the_Czech_Republic), while the average number of years of education is 13.1.[^cite_note-Sign_In-244] Additionally, the Czech Republic has a "relatively equal" educational system in comparison with other countries in Europe.[^cite_note-Sign_In-244] Founded in 1348, [Charles University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_University) was the first university in Central Europe. Other major universities are [Masaryk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masaryk_University), [Czech Technical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Technical_University_in_Prague), [Palacký University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palack%C3%BD_University,_Olomouc), [Academy of Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_of_Performing_Arts_in_Prague) and [University of Economics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Economics,_Prague).

The [Programme for International Student Assessment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Programme_for_International_Student_Assessment), coordinated by the OECD, currently ranks the education system as the 15th most successful in the world, higher than the OECD average.[^cite_note-245] The UN [Education Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_Index) ranked the Czech Republic 10th as of 2013.[^cite_note-246]

[Healthcare in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_the_Czech_Republic) is similar in quality to that of other developed nations. The [universal health care](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_health_care) system is based on a [compulsory insurance model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance_mandate), with [fee-for-service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fee-for-service) care funded by mandatory employment-related insurance plans.[^cite_note-primary-247] According to the 2016 [Euro health consumer index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro_health_consumer_index), a comparison of [healthcare in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Europe), the Czech healthcare is 13th.[^cite_note-EHCI_2016-248]

[Theodoric of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodoric_of_Prague) was a painter in the Gothic era who decorated the castle [Karlštejn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl%C5%A1tejn). In the Baroque era, there were painters [Wenceslaus Hollar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslaus_Hollar), [Jan Kupecký](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Kupeck%C3%BD), [Karel Škréta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_%C5%A0kr%C3%A9ta), [Anton Raphael Mengs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Raphael_Mengs) and [Petr Brandl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Brandl) and sculptors [Matthias Braun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_Braun) and [Ferdinand Brokoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Brokoff).

In the first half of the 19th century, [Josef Mánes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_M%C3%A1nes) joined the romantic movement. In the second half the so-called "National Theatre generation" rose to prominence: sculptor [Josef Václav Myslbek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_V%C3%A1clav_Myslbek) and painters [Mikoláš Aleš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikol%C3%A1%C5%A1_Ale%C5%A1), [Václav Brožík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Bro%C5%BE%C3%ADk), [Vojtěch Hynais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vojt%C4%9Bch_Hynais) and [Julius Mařák](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Ma%C5%99%C3%A1k). At the end of the century came [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau), with [Alfons Mucha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alphonse_Mucha) becoming its main representative. He is known for his Art Nouveau posters and a cycle of 20 large canvases named [the Slav Epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Slav_Epic), which depicts the history of Czechs and other Slavs. As of 2012, it can be seen in the Veletržní Palace of the [National Gallery in Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_in_Prague), which manages the largest collection of art in the Czech Republic. [Max Švabinský](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_%C5%A0vabinsk%C3%BD) was another Art Nouveau painter.

The 20th century brought an [avant-garde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avant-garde) revolution, represented in the Czech lands mainly by expressionists and cubists: [Josef Čapek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_%C4%8Capek), [Emil Filla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Filla), [Bohumil Kubišta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohumil_Kubi%C5%A1ta) or [Jan Zrzavý](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Zrzav%C3%BD). Surrealism emerged particularly through the work of [Toyen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyen), [Josef Šíma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_%C5%A0%C3%ADma) and [Karel Teige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Teige). In the world, however, the most well-known Czech avant-garde artist might be [František Kupka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franti%C5%A1ek_Kupka), a pioneer of abstract painting. Illustrators and cartoonists to gain fame in the first half of the 20th century include [Josef Lada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Lada), [Zdeněk Burian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zden%C4%9Bk_Burian) or [Emil Orlík](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emil_Orl%C3%ADk). Art photography became a new field represented by [František Drtikol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franti%C5%A1ek_Drtikol), [Josef Sudek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Sudek), later [Jan Saudek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Saudek) and [Josef Koudelka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josef_Koudelka).

The Czech Republic is also known for its individually made, mouth-blown, and decorated [Bohemian glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_glass).

### Architecture

Main article: [Czech architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_architecture)



[Historic center of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_\(Prague\))

The earliest preserved stone buildings in Bohemia and Moravia date back to the time of the [Christianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization) in the 9th and 10th centuries. Since the Middle Ages, the Czech lands have been using the same architectural styles as most of [Western](33800.md) and Central Europe. The oldest still standing churches were built in the [Romanesque style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture). During the 13th century, it was replaced by the [Gothic style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Gothic_architecture). In the 14th century, Emperor Charles IV invited architects from France and Germany, [Matthias of Arras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthias_of_Arras) and [Peter Parler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Parler), to his court in Prague. During the Middle Ages, some fortified castles were built by the king and aristocracy, as well as some monasteries.

The [Renaissance style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture) penetrated the Bohemian Crown in the late 15th century when the older Gothic style started to be mixed with Renaissance elements. An example of pure Renaissance architecture in Bohemia is the [Queen Anne's Summer Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Anne's_Summer_Palace), which was situated in the garden of [Prague Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Castle). Evidence of the general reception of the Renaissance in Bohemia, involving an influx of Italian architects, can be found in spacious chateaus with arcade courtyards and geometrically arranged gardens.[^cite_note-architecture-249] Emphasis was placed on comfort, and buildings that were built for entertainment purposes also appeared.[^cite_note-czecharchitecture-250]

In the 17th century, the Baroque style spread throughout the Crown of Bohemia.[^cite_note-kotalik-251]

In the 18th century, Bohemia produced an architectural peculiarity – the *Baroque Gothic style*, a synthesis of the Gothic and Baroque styles.[^cite_note-architecture-249]



Czech artists developed a [distinct cubist style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Cubism) in architecture and applied arts. It later evolved into national Czechoslovak style, *[rondocubism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rondocubism)*.

During the 19th century stands the [revival architectural styles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revival_architecture). Some churches were restored to their presumed medieval appearance and there were constructed buildings in the [Neo-Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_Revival_architecture), [Neo-Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_Revival_architecture) and [Neo-Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Revival_architecture) styles. At the turn of the 19th and 20th centuries, the new art style appeared in the Czech lands – [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau).

Bohemia contributed an unusual style to the world's architectural heritage when Czech architects attempted to transpose the [Cubism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubism) of painting and sculpture into architecture.

Between World Wars I and II, [Functionalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functionalism_\(architecture\)), with its sober, progressive forms, took over as the main architectural style.[^cite_note-architecture-249]

After World War II and the Communist coup in 1948, art in Czechoslovakia became Soviet-influenced. The Czechoslovak avant-garde artistic movement is known as the *[Brussels style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brussels_style?action=edit\&redlink=1) came up* in the time of political liberalization of Czechoslovakia in the 1960s. [Brutalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brutalism) dominated in the 1970s and 1980s.

The Czech Republic is not shying away from the more modern trends of international architecture, an example is the [Dancing House (Tančící dům)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tan%C4%8D%C3%ADc%C3%AD_d%C5%AFm) in Prague, [Golden Angel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Angel) in Prague or Congress Centre in Zlín.[^cite_note-architecture-249]

### Literature

Main article: [Czech literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_literature)



[Franz Kafka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Kafka)

The literature from the area of today's Czech Republic was mostly written in Czech, but also in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) and German or even [Old Church Slavonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Church_Slavonic). [Franz Kafka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Kafka), although a competent user of Czech, wrote in his mother tongue, German.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-253] His works include *[The Trial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Trial)* and *[The Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Castle_\(novel\))*.

In the second half of the 13th century, the royal court in Prague became one of the centers of German [Minnesang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesang) and courtly literature. The Czech German-language literature can be seen in the first half of the 20th century.

[Bible translations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bible_translations_into_Czech) played a role in the development of Czech literature. The oldest Czech translation of the [Psalms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psalms) originated in the late 13th century and the first complete Czech translation of the Bible was finished around 1360. The first complete printed Czech Bible was published in 1488. The first complete Czech Bible translation from the original languages was published between 1579 and 1593. The [Codex Gigas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codex_Gigas) from the 12th century is the largest extant medieval manuscript in the world.[^cite_note-254]



[Jaroslav Seifert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaroslav_Seifert), winner of the Nobel Prize for Literature

Czech-language literature can be divided into several periods: the Middle Ages; the Hussite period; the [Renaissance humanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_humanism); the Baroque period; the Enlightenment and Czech reawakening in the first half of the 19th century, modern literature in the second half of the 19th century; the avant-garde of the interwar period; the years under Communism; and the Czech Republic.

The antiwar comedy novel *[The Good Soldier Švejk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Soldier_%C5%A0vejk)* is the most translated Czech book in history.

The international literary award the [Franz Kafka Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Kafka_Prize) is awarded in the Czech Republic.[^cite_note-255]

The Czech Republic has the densest network of [libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_libraries_in_the_Czech_Republic) in Europe.[^cite_note-256]

Czech literature and culture played a role on at least two occasions when Czechs lived under oppression and political activity was suppressed. On both of these occasions, in the early 19th century and then again in the 1960s, the Czechs used their cultural and literary effort to strive for political freedom, establishing a confident, politically aware nation.[^cite_note-257]

### Music

Main article: [Music of the Czech Lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_the_Czech_Lands)

See also: [Moravian traditional music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moravian_traditional_music)



[Antonín Dvořák](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton%C3%ADn_Dvo%C5%99%C3%A1k)

The musical tradition of the Czech lands arose from the first church hymns, whose first evidence is suggested at the break of the 10th and 11th centuries. Some pieces of Czech music include two chorales, which in their time performed the function of anthems: "[Lord, Have Mercy on Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospodine_pomiluj_ny)" and the [hymn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hymn) "Saint Wenceslaus" or "[Saint Wenceslaus Chorale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Wenceslas_Chorale)".[^cite_note-258] The authorship of the anthem "Lord, Have Mercy on Us" is ascribed by some historians to [Saint Adalbert of Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Adalbert_of_Prague) (sv.Vojtěch), bishop of [Prague](23844.md), living between 956 and 997.[^cite_note-259]

The wealth of musical culture lies in the classical music tradition during all historical periods, especially in the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_music), [Classicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_\(music\)), Romantic, [modern classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism_\(music\)) music and in the [traditional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_music) folk music of Bohemia, Moravia and [Silesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silesia). Since the early era of artificial music, Czech musicians and composers have been influenced the folk music of the region and dance.

Czech music can be considered to have been contributed strongly to the development of music in both the European and worldwide contexts, having several times co-determined or even determined a newly arriving era in musical art,[^cite_note-260] above all of the [Classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_period_\(music\)) era, as well as by original attitudes in [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_\(music\)), [Romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romantic_\(music\)) and [modern classical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_classical) music. Czech music burst onto the world stage in the romantic era beginning with the works of [Bedřich Smetana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bed%C5%99ich_Smetana) who is often considered the 'father of Czech music'. Some Czech musical works are *[The Bartered Bride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bartered_Bride)* (Smetana), *[New World Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World_Symphony)* (Dvořák), and *[Sinfonietta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinfonietta_\(Jan%C3%A1%C4%8Dek\))* and *[Jenůfa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jen%C5%AFfa)* by [Janáček](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo%C5%A1_Jan%C3%A1%C4%8Dek).

A music festival in the country is [Prague Spring International Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_Spring_International_Music_Festival) of classical music, a permanent showcase for performing artists, symphony orchestras and chamber music ensembles of the world.

### Theatre

Main article: [Theatre of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_of_the_Czech_Republic)

Portrait

Portrait

The [National Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Theatre_\(Prague\)) (left) and the [Estates Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estates_Theatre) (right)

The roots of Czech theatre can be found in the Middle Ages, especially in the cultural life of the [Gothic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_period). In the 19th century, the theatre played a role in the national awakening movement and later, in the 20th century, it became a part of modern European theatre art. The original Czech cultural phenomenon came into being at the end of the 1950s. This project called [Laterna magika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laterna_magika), resulting in productions that combined theater, dance, and film in a poetic manner, considered the first [multimedia art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia_art) project in an international context.

One drama is [Karel Čapek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_%C4%8Capek)'s play *[R.U.R.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R.U.R.)*, which introduced the word "[robot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robot)".[^cite_note-261]

The country has a tradition of [puppet theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppetry). In 2016, [Czech and Slovak Puppetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puppetry#Czech_Republic_and_Slovakia) was included on the [UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage Lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_Intangible_Cultural_Heritage_Lists).[^cite_note-262]

### Film

Main article: [Cinema of the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_the_Czech_Republic)



[Hermína Týrlová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herm%C3%ADna_T%C3%BDrlov%C3%A1), animator and film director

The tradition of Czech cinematography started in the second half of the 1890s. Peaks of the production in the era of silent movies include the historical drama *The Builder of the Temple* and the social and erotic drama *Erotikon* directed by [Gustav Machatý](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Machat%C3%BD).[^cite_note-263] The early Czech [sound film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_film) era was productive, above all in mainstream genres, with the comedies of [Martin Frič](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Fri%C4%8D) or [Karel Lamač](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Lama%C4%8D). There were dramatic movies sought internationally.

[Hermína Týrlová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herm%C3%ADna_T%C3%BDrlov%C3%A1) was a prominent Czech animator, screenwriter, and film director. She was often called the mother of Czech animation. Over the course of her career, she produced over 60 animated children's short films using puppets and the technique of stop motion animation.

Before the German occupation, in 1933, filmmaker and animator [Irena Dodalová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irena_Dodalov%C3%A1?action=edit\&redlink=1) established the first Czech animation studio "IRE Film" with her husband Karel Dodal.[^cite_note-264]

After the period of Nazi occupation and early communist official dramaturgy of socialist realism in movies at the turn of the 1940s and 1950s with fewer exceptions such as *[Krakatit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krakatit)* or *Men without wings* (awarded by [Palme d'Or](612946.md) in 1946), an era of the Czech film began with animated films, performed in anglophone countries under the name "The Fabulous World of Jules Verne" from 1958, which combined acted drama with animation, and [Jiří Trnka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji%C5%99%C3%AD_Trnka), the founder of the modern puppet film.[^cite_note-265] This began a tradition of animated films (*[Mole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mole_\(Zden%C4%9Bk_Miler_character\))* etc.).



Oscar-winning director [Miloš Forman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milo%C5%A1_Forman)

In the 1960s, the hallmark of [Czechoslovak New Wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovak_New_Wave)'s films were improvised [dialogues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialogue), [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_comedy) and [absurd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absurdity) humor and the occupation of non-actors. Directors are trying to preserve natural atmosphere without refinement and artificial arrangement of scenes. A personality of the 1960s and the beginning of the 1970s with original manuscript and psychological impact is [František Vláčil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franti%C5%A1ek_Vl%C3%A1%C4%8Dil). Another international author is [Jan Švankmajer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_%C5%A0vankmajer), a filmmaker and artist whose work spans several media. He is a self-labeled [surrealist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrealism) known for animations and features.[^cite_note-266]

The [Barrandov Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrandov_Studios) in Prague are the largest film studios with film locations in the country.[^cite_note-267] Filmmakers have come to [Prague](23844.md) to shoot scenery no longer found in Berlin, Paris and Vienna. The city of [Karlovy Vary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovy_Vary) was used as a location for the 2006 James Bond film Casino Royale.[^cite_note-268]

The [Czech Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Lion) is the highest Czech award for film achievement. [Karlovy Vary International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovy_Vary_International_Film_Festival) is one of the film festivals that have been given competitive status by the [FIAPF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIAPF). Other film festivals held in the country include [Febiofest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Febiofest), [Jihlava International Documentary Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihlava_International_Documentary_Film_Festival), [One World Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_World_Film_Festival), [Zlín Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zl%C3%ADn_Film_Festival) and [Fresh Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_Film_Festival).

### Media

Main article: [Mass media in the Czech Republic](56712569.md)



Headquarters of [Czech Television](1910176.md)

Czech journalists and media enjoy a degree of [freedom](105323.md). There are restrictions against writing in support of [Nazism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism), racism or violating [Czech law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_law). The Czech press was ranked as the 40th most free press in the World Press Freedom Index by [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md) in 2021.[^cite_note-269] [Radio Free Europe/Radio Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Free_Europe/Radio_Liberty) has its headquarters in Prague.

[Czech Television](1910176.md) is the country's national public television broadcaster. It operates a number of channels, including [ČT1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CT1), [ČT2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CT2), and the 24-hour news channel [ČT24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CT24), as well as the news website ct24.cz.[^cite_note-270] As of 2020, it is the most watched broadcaster, followed by the private [TV Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Nova_\(Czech_Republic\)) and [Prima TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prima_televize). However, TV Nova features the most watched main [news program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_program) and [prime time](24973.md) program.[^cite_note-271] Other public media services include the [Czech Radio](3100526.md) and the [Czech News Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_News_Agency).

The best-selling daily national newspapers in 2020/21 are [Blesk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blesk) (average 703,000 daily readers), [Mladá fronta DNES](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mlad%C3%A1_fronta_DNES) (average 461,000 daily readers), [Právo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pr%C3%A1vo) (average 182,000 daily readers), [Lidové noviny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidov%C3%A9_noviny) (average 163,000 daily readers) and [Hospodářské noviny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospod%C3%A1%C5%99sk%C3%A9_noviny) (average 162,000 daily readers).[^cite_note-272]

Most Czechs (87%[^cite_note-273]) read their news online,[^cite_note-274] with [Seznam.cz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seznam.cz), [iDNES.cz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDNES.cz), [Novinky.cz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novinky.cz), [iPrima.cz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prima_televize) and [Seznam Zprávy.cz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seznam_Zpr%C3%A1vy) being the most visited as of 2021.[^cite_note-275]

A mug of [Pilsner Urquell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilsner_Urquell), the first [pilsner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilsner) type of [pale lager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pale_lager) beer, brewed since 1842

Czech cuisine is marked by an emphasis on meat dishes with pork, beef, and chicken. Goose, duck, rabbit, and venison are served. Fish is less common, with the occasional exception of fresh [trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trout) and [carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carp), which is served at Christmas.[^cite_note-276][^cite_note-277] One popular Czech menu item is *smažený vepřový řízek* (fried breaded pork filet), served with boiled potatoes.[^cite_note-278]

There is a variety of local sausages, wurst, pâtés, and smoked and cured meats. Czech desserts include a variety of whipped cream, chocolate, and fruit pastries and tarts, crêpes, creme desserts and cheese, poppy-seed-filled and other types of traditional cakes such as *[buchty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buchteln)*, *[koláče](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolach_\(cake\))* and [*štrúdl*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_strudel).[^cite_note-279]

[Czech beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_in_the_Czech_Republic) has a history extending more than a millennium; the earliest known brewery existed in 993. Today, the Czech Republic has the highest [beer consumption per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_beer_consumption_per_capita) in the world. The [pilsner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilsner) style beer (pils) originated in [Plzeň](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plze%C5%88), where the world's first blond lager [Pilsner Urquell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilsner_Urquell) is still produced. It has served as the inspiration for more than two-thirds of the beer produced in the world today. The city of [České Budějovice](149384.md) has similarly lent its name to its beer, known as [Budweiser Budvar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budweiser_Budvar).

The South Moravian region has been producing [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_wine) since the Middle Ages; about 94% of [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyard) in the Czech Republic are Moravian. Aside from beer, [slivovitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slivovitz) and wine, the Czech Republic also produces two [liquors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquor), [Fernet Stock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernet_Stock) and [Becherovka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Becherovka). [Kofola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofola) is a non-alcoholic domestic [cola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cola) soft drink which competes with [Coca-Cola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coca-Cola) and [Pepsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepsi).

### Sport

Main article: [Sport in the Czech Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_the_Czech_Republic)



Hockey player [Jaromír Jágr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jarom%C3%ADr_J%C3%A1gr) has the second-most [points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_\(ice_hockey\)) in NHL history, after [Wayne Gretzky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Gretzky).

[^cite_note-280]

The most watched and most attended sport in the Czech Republic are [football](10568.md) and [ice hockey](14790.md).[^cite_note-281] The most watched sporting events are the [Ice hockey at the Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_hockey_at_the_Olympic_Games) and the [Ice Hockey World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Hockey_World_Championships).[^cite_note-282][^cite_note-283] The most popular sports in the Czech Republic according to the size of the membership base of sports clubs are: football, [tennis](29773.md), ice hockey, [volleyball](32558.md), [floorball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floorball), [golf](19568112.md), [ball hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ball_hockey), [athletics](43719.md), [basketball](3921.md) and [skiing](28478.md).[^cite_note-284]

The country has won 15 gold medals in the [Summer Olympics](27566.md) and nine in the [Winter Games](34069.md). (See [Olympic history](3729318.md).) The [Czech ice hockey team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Republic_men's_national_ice_hockey_team) won the gold medal at the [1998 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Winter_Olympics) and has won (along with the [Czechoslovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_men's_national_ice_hockey_team) team) thirteen gold medals at the [World Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHWC), including three straight from [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Men's_World_Ice_Hockey_Championships) to [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Men's_World_Ice_Hockey_Championships).[^cite_note-285]

The [Škoda Motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0koda_Motorsport) is engaged in [competition racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsport) since 1901 and has gained a number of titles with various vehicles around the world. [MTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTX_\(automobile\)) automobile company was formerly engaged in the manufacture of [racing](1022.md) and [formula](1618495.md) cars since 1969.[^cite_note-286]

[Hiking](147313.md) is a popular sport. The word for 'tourist' in Czech, *turista*, also means 'trekker' or 'hiker'. For hikers, thanks to the more than 120-year-old tradition, there is the [Czech Hiking Markers System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_Hiking_Markers_System) of [trail blazing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trail_blazing), that has been adopted by countries worldwide. There is a network of around 40,000 km of marked short- and long-distance trails crossing the whole country and all the Czech mountains.[^cite_note-287][^cite_note-288]
