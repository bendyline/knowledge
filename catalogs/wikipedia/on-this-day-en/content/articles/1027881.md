**PJSC Lukoil** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): ПAO Лукойл) is a Russian [multinational](214491.md) [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_industry) corporation headquartered in [Moscow](19004.md), specializing in the business of extraction, production, transport, and sale of [petroleum](23195.md), [natural gas](22131.md), [petroleum products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_product), and [electricity](9550.md).&#x20;

It was formed in 1991 when three state-run companies from western Siberia merged. The original companies were named after the towns in [Khanty–Mansi Autonomous Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanty%E2%80%93Mansi_Autonomous_Okrug) where each was based: Langepasneftegaz, Urayneftegaz, and Kogalymneftegaz. Its new name is the combination of the acronym LUK (initials of the oil-producing cities of [Langepas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langepas), [Uray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uray), [Kogalym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kogalym)) and the [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) word [oil](23195.md).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-\:5-4]

Lukoil is the [third largest company in Russia](268429.md) after [Rosneft](1313566.md) and [Gazprom](320489.md), and the country's largest non-state enterprise in terms of revenue, with [₽](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_ruble)4,744 billion in 2018.[^cite_note-rbc20180112-5][^cite_note-6] In the 2020 [*Forbes* Global 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes_Global_2000), Lukoil was ranked as the 99th-largest public company in the world. Internationally, it is one of the world's largest crude oil producers. In 2019, the company produced 87.488 million metric tons of oil (1.639 million barrels per day) and 35.046 billion cubic meters of natural gas.[^cite_note-2012_production-7] In 2021, it had operations and subsidiaries in more than 30 countries around the world, but as of 2025, that was down to 14 countries.[^cite_note-reuters-8] The company's revenue amounted to 2.9 trillion rubles in 2022, and reached 3 trillion rubles in 2024.[^cite_note-9]

## History

### Origins



[Vagit Alekperov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vagit_Alekperov), [CEO](52234.md) and founder of Lukoil

"[Langepas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langepas), [Uray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uray), and [Kogalym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kogalym)" oil (Lukoil) was established by the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) Council of Ministers Decree No. 18 on 25 November 1991, as a state-owned enterprise. In the new company, three oil production companies, Langepasneftegaz, Uraineftegaz, and Kogalymneftegaz, processing company Permnefteorgsintez, and the [Volgograd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volgograd_Refinery) and [Novosibirsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novosibirsk) refineries, were merged (the latter soon came under the control of the authorities of [Bashkortostan](735530.md)).[^cite_note-fundinguniverse-10]

The central figure in the company's founding was the Soviet deputy minister of oil production [Vagit Alekperov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vagit_Alekperov).[^cite_note-fundinguniverse-10] He came to believe the only way Russians could compete against Western companies was to copy their business model. That meant vertically integrating the three branches of the industry—exploration, refining, and distribution—that were strictly separate under the old Soviet system.[^cite_note-kingofcrude-11]

On 5 April 1993, Lukoil transformed itself from a state-owned enterprise to a [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privately_held_company) [open joint-stock company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_joint-stock_company) based on Presidential Decree No. 1403 of 17 November 1992.

### 1990s

In 1994, Lukoil became the first company to begin offering shares of stock on the new [Russian Trading System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Trading_System).[^cite_note-fundinguniverse-10] The [Filanovsky oil field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filanovsky_oil_field) was discovered and operated by Lukoil.[^cite_note-12]

In 1995, Lukoil controlled the stakes of nine oil-producing, marketing and service enterprises in [Western Siberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_regions_of_Russia), the [Urals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ural_Federal_District), and [Volgograd Oblast](478748.md) to abide by Government Decree No. 861 of 1 September 1995.[^cite_note-\:0-13] In that same year, a 5% stake of Lukoil was sold by the state with a minimum excess of the starting price in an auction.[^cite_note-\:6-14] In November 1995, Lukoil filed with the [U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Securities_and_Exchange_Commission) to issue [American depositary receipts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_depositary_receipt) on Western stock markets. This allowed United States investors, for the first time, to purchase shares in a Russian company.[^cite_note-15]

In 1997, Lukoil signed a contract with the [Iraqi](26215470.md) Ministry of Oil for the development and production of the second stage of the [West Qurna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Qurna_Field)-2 oil field. After [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein)'s regime was overthrown, the project was suspended and later terminated.[^cite_note-\:1-16]

In 1999, Lukoil acquired numerous enterprises such as the [Odesa Oil Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_Oil_Refinery) in [Ukraine](31750.md), the [Burgas Oil Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukoil_Neftohim_Burgas) in [Bulgaria](3415.md), and KomiTEK.[^cite_note-\:0-13]

### 2000s



A Lukoil gas station in [New York](8210131.md), United States

In 2000, Lukoil acquired the distribution and marketing operations of American oil company [Getty Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Getty_Oil). This resulted in the control of a network of [gas stations](61260.md) in the United States, as well as the first time Lukoil entered the American oil market.[^cite_note-\:0-13]

In September 2004, [ConocoPhillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ConocoPhillips) purchased a 7.6% stake in Lukoil for about \$2 billion. According to some commentators, the sale of this deal was planned before in a personal meeting between [Russian President](239646.md) [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) and ConocoPhillips' [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_\(corporate_title\)) and [CEO](52234.md), [James Mulva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Mulva). After the auction, Lukoil and ConocoPhillips announced the creation of a strategic alliance. Later, the American company increased its stake to 20% in Lukoil and sold to the Russian company part of its network of gas stations in the [United States](3434750.md) and [Western Europe](33800.md). The two oil companies also agreed to jointly develop an oil and gas field in the northern [Timan-Pechora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timan-Pechora) area of Russia ([Komi Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komi_Republic)) and intended to secure the rights to develop the [West Qurna Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Qurna_Field) in Iraq, one of the country's largest.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

Uzbekistan's deputy prime minister [Ergash Shaismatov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ergash_Shaismatov) announced on 30 August 2006 that the [Uzbek government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_government) and an international consortium consisting of state-run [Uzbekneftegaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbekneftegaz), Lukoil Overseas, [Petronas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petronas), [Korea National Oil Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_National_Oil_Corporation), and [China National Petroleum Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Petroleum_Corporation) signed a production sharing agreement to explore and develop oil and gas fields in the [Aral Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aral_Sea), stating "The Aral Sea is largely unknown, but it holds a lot of promise in terms of finding oil and gas. There is risk of course but we believe in the success of this unique project".[^cite_note-GAS-19] In December 2006, Lukoil announced the acquisition of 376 [filling stations](61260.md) in six European countries: [Belgium](3343.md), the [Czech Republic](5321.md), [Finland](10577.md), [Hungary](13275.md), [Poland](22936.md), and [Slovakia](26830.md), from ConocoPhillips.[^cite_note-20]

In 2007, Lukoil established a [joint-venture](538782.md) with Gazprom and in 2008, established a joint-venture as well with Italian oil company [Erg S.p.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erg_\(company\))[^cite_note-\:0-13] In 2009, Lukoil and [Norwegian](21241.md) oil company [Statoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statoil) won a [tender offer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tender_offer) for the development of the [West Qurna Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Qurna_Field) in Iraq. However, in early 2012, Statoil withdrew from the project, resulting in Lukoil consolidating a 75% stake in the development of the oil field.[^cite_note-\:0-13][^cite_note-\:1-16]

### 2010s

From 2010 to February 2011, [ConocoPhillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ConocoPhillips) sold its whole 20% stake in Lukoil due to its difficult financial situation.[^cite_note-bbc280710-21][^cite_note-22]

In September 2012, Lukoil created a shared service centre in the [Czech Republic](5321.md) to provide accounting services to its subsidiaries in [Belgium](3343.md), [Poland](22936.md), and [Bulgaria](3415.md).[^cite_note-23] In December 2012, Lukoil bought the Imilor field for [₽](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_ruble)50.8 billion in the [Khanty-Mansi Autonomous Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khanty-Mansi_Autonomous_Okrug) to [explore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon_exploration) and develop the [hydrocarbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) deposits located there.[^cite_note-24]

In February 2013, Lukoil sold the [Odesa Oil Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odesa_Oil_Refinery) to the [Ukrainian](31750.md) "East European Fuel and Energy Company" (VETEK). For Lukoil, the oil refinery was unprofitable even before production was stopped as early as October 2010, and it finally closed in the summer of 2013.[^cite_note-25] In April 2013, Lukoil agreed to buy [Hess Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hess_Corporation)'s Russian unit for \$2.05 billion.[^cite_note-26]

In 2014, the company faced a sharp decline in retail sales in Ukraine by 42%, caused by [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_military_intervention_in_Ukraine_\(2014%E2%80%93present\)). As a result, the management of Lukoil agreed to sell 100% of its subsidiary Lukoil Ukraine to the [Austrian](26964606.md) company [AMIC Energy Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amic_Energy), which was announced at the end of July 2014.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

In 2014, Lukoil sold its service stations in the [Czech Republic](5321.md), [Slovakia](26830.md), and [Hungary](13275.md).[^cite_note-29]

In 2015, it sold its service stations in [Estonia](28222445.md) and [Ukraine](31750.md), and in 2016, it sold its service stations in [Latvia](17514.md), [Lithuania](17675.md), [Poland](22936.md), and [Cyprus](5593.md).[^cite_note-rc150317-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

### 2020s

In March 2022, Lukoil's market stock price dropped 95 percent, as a result of [international sanctions during the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_2022_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-33]

On 21 April 2022, Lukoil issued a statement saying that president [Vagit Alekperov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vagit_Alekperov) had stepped down and resigned from the board of directors after 29 years.[^cite_note-34]

The Norwegian state-owned oil company [Equinor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equinor) exited the last of their joint ventures in Russia by withdrawing the joint venture with Lukoil and exiting the [Kharyaga project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharyaga_field) on 2 September 2022.[^cite_note-ak\&m20220902-35]

In 2023, Lukoil stated they were planning to double the production of oil from the Iraq project, [West Qurna-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Qurna_field), to 800,000 bpd.[^cite_note-36] By November 2025, they had achieved only 60% of the goal, at 480,000 barrels per day; still making it approximately nine percent of Iraq's total oil output.[^cite_note-reuters20251110-37]

Lukoil was involved in schemes of illegal party financing and [money laundering](19390.md) by the group of [Moldovan](19260.md) [pro-Russian](2541825.md) fugitive [oligarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_oligarch) [Ilan Shor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilan_Shor), as reported on a 16 September 2025 Moldovan newspaper *Deschide.MD* article, citing sources from Moldova's State Fiscal Service (SFS).[^cite_note-38]

In November 2025, [Gunvor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centalion) made a \$22 billion offer to acquire Lukoil’s international assets, including oil refineries in Romania and Bulgaria, petrol stations in Europe and the United States, and oil and gas operations in the Middle East, Central Asia, and Africa. The proposed purchase came after U.S. sanctions were imposed on Lukoil and other Russian oil companies. In November 2025, Gunvor withdrew its bid after the [U.S. Treasury](53667.md) announced that it would block the deal, describing the company as a “Kremlin’s puppet” and stating that no licence would be issued while Russia’s invasion of Ukraine continued. Gunvor rejected the allegations as “fundamentally misinformed and false,” emphasizing that it had sold its Russian assets more than a decade earlier and had publicly condemned the invasion.[^cite_note-39]

On 4 November 2025, Lukoil declared *[force majeure](709128.md)* at the [West Qurna-2 oil field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Qurna_field) in Iraq, which Lukoil owned a 75% stake of, due to the impact of Western—especially U.S. and British—[sanctions](43449324.md), and may exit the field entirely. Iraq immediately halted all cash and crude oil payments to Lukoil. The field had been producing around 480,000 barrels per day, approximately nine percent of Iraq's total oil output.[^cite_note-reuters20251110-37]

## Operations

### Oil and gas production

#### Hydrocarbon reserves

The company's [proved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proven_reserves) [hydrocarbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) reserves as of 1 January 2011, amounted to 17.255 billion [barrels of oil equivalent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrel_of_oil_equivalent), including 13.319 billion barrels of [petroleum](23195.md) and 0.67 trillion cubic meters of [natural gas](22131.md). In terms of proved oil reserves, Lukoil, according to its own information, was the sixth-largest [private](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privately_held_company) oil company in the world at the time.[^cite_note-зап_2011-40][^cite_note-41]

In addition, probable hydrocarbon reserves as of 1 January 2011 were 8.46 billion barrels of oil equivalent (including oil, 6.47 billion barrels of petroleum, and 0.34 trillion cubic meters of natural gas). Possible reserves were 3.17 billion barrels of oil equivalent (including 2.78 billion barrels of petroleum and 65.7 billion cubic meters of natural gas).[^cite_note-зап_2011-40]

Lukoil refinery in [Perm](389777.md)

### Petrochemistry



The [Lukoil Neftochim Burgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukoil_Neftohim_Burgas) [refinery](195137.md) in [Burgas](86758.md), [Bulgaria](3415.md)

The subsidiary company Lukoil-Neftekhim specializes in [petrochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrochemistry), and operates the Stavrolen ([Budyonnovsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budyonnovsk)), Saratovorgsintez, and the Karpatneftekhim ([Kalush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalush,_Ukraine), [Ukraine](31750.md)) petrochemical plants. Petrochemical facilities are also part of the Neftochim Burgas Combine in Bulgaria. "Lukoil" is the largest producer of [alkene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkene) and [acrylonitrile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrylonitrile) in Eastern Europe. Together with [Sibur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibur), Lukoil-Neftekhim owns a controlling stake in the Polief plant.

### Transportation

Transportation of oil produced by Lukoil in Russia is carried out for the most part by the pipelines of [Transneft](5707669.md), as well as by rail and water transport. ## Environmental record

According to Lukoil, their numbers for 2007 showed a 7.8% decrease in the volume of pollutant effects and a 3.8% decrease in the area of contaminated lands compared to 2006. These numbers came after an appeal from EMERCON, the [Ministry of the Russian Federation for Civil Defense, Emergencies and Natural Disaster Recovery](1568895.md), which proposed that Lukoil participate in the development of monitoring, prevention, and emergency recovery systems.[^cite_note-71]

In an effort to increase productivity, Lukoil organized a contract to commence oil pumping operations in the Azerbaijani sector of the Caspian Sea. It conducted an Environmental Impact Assessment of the drill site to facilitate a second exploration drill. This block, D-222, was the largest prospective structure in the north-east section of the Caspian Sea as of 2008.[^cite_note-72] The key issue was to assess how much damage the oil block would inflict on local fish populations. Taking into account the depth of the operation, around 700 meters, the amount of harm was projected to be minimal, with the majority of the damaged marine life being plankton and [benthos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benthos). A rescue and salvage ship would be stationed there to mitigate the environmental effects on the area. Lukoil would develop contingency plans for oil spills and implement an [environmental monitoring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_monitoring) system.[^cite_note-73]

At the same time, Lukoil faces criticism from various environmental organizations. In particular, the company's oil production in the [Baltic Sea](3335.md) near [Kaliningrad Oblast](17311.md) was criticized as it is 22 kilometers away from the [Curonian Spit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curonian_Spit), a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-74]

According to several critics, Lukoil has inherited the worst environmental standards of the Soviet oil industry, failing to take sufficient responsibility for minimizing pollution in its oil fields and refineries.[^cite_note-75]

Lukoil has been ranked as among the 14th best of 92 oil, gas, and mining companies on indigenous rights and resource extraction in the Arctic.[^cite_note-76]

In the [Arctic Environmental Responsibility Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Environmental_Responsibility_Index) (AERI), Lukoil is ranked no. 37 out of 120 oil, gas, and mining companies involved in resource extraction north of the Arctic Circle.[^cite_note-77]

## Controversies

### Environmental incidents

On one of the storage ponds at JSC "Lukoil-Volgograd-neftepererabotka" between 25 July and 8 August 1996, oil sludge was ignited due to the improper conduct of welding operations. The surface layer of oil products was formed during the last two decades, and a similar ignition in this area was already noted in 1972. As a result of the 1996 fire, approximately 50,000 tons of oil products were burned, as the soil at this site was already saturated with volatile fractions. Where the fire was first lit, the concentration of [carbon monoxide](488743.md) exceeded the permissible standards by almost 28 times, [nitrogen dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_dioxide#Toxicity) tripled, [hydrogen sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_sulfide#Toxicity) and [phenol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenol#Toxicity) more than 1.5 times. In the residential areas of the Krasnoarmeysky district of [Volgograd](32585.md), located 7 km from the fire, as well as in the nearby settlements - B. and M. Chapurnik, Dubov Gully, Chervlen, Tingut - the content of combustion products in the air also exceeded the maximum permissible concentration. In the liquidation of this major technogenic emergency with severe environmental consequences, the divisions of the [Ministry of Emergency Situations of Russia](1568895.md) took part.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

In the autumn of 2003, the Russian Emergencies Ministry revealed the oil spill as a result of the depressurization of the interfacial oil pipeline belonging to the TPP Lukoil-Usinskneftegaz on the territory of the [Komi Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komi_Republic) near the city of [Usinsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usinsk). The area of oil pollution in one case was about 1.8 thousand m<sup>2</sup>, in the second - 377 m<sup>2</sup>.[^cite_note-80]

On 25 January 2011, at about 10\:00 (local time), as a result of oil leakage in the engine room of LGPZ (CCI "Langepasneftegaz"), there was a fire. More than 50 firefighters extinguished the fire. The plant suspended its work.

On 20 April 2012, at the Trebs field, jointly developed by Lukoil and Bashneft, an accident occurred that caused significant damage to the natural environment. Over the course of a day, oil continued to flow from the reopened well, resulting in large-scale contamination of the surrounding territory. According to the press service of the administration of the [Nenets Autonomous Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nenets_Autonomous_Okrug), the contamination area exceeded 5 thousand square meters, the volume of spilled oil, according to Bashneft, was 600 tonnes (in independent sources, numbers were up to 2.2 thousand tonnes).[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83]

On 11 May 2021, a leakage was identified in a pipeline connected to Lukoil's Oshkoye field. The spill was estimated at 100 tons of oil, yet environmentalists argued that 100 tons is an underestimate. The spill had infiltrated the [Kolva river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolva_\(Perm_Krai\)) and traveled upstream, affecting the river habitats. Russia's Northwest Komi Republic declared an emergency. The damage was estimated at \$4.1 billion.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

### Ukrainian investigation

In January 2015, the [Security Service of Ukraine](709222.md) announced an investigation into whether Lukoil had financed separatists in [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas).[^cite_note-MT150116-86]

### Antitrust law violations

In November 2009, the [Federal Antimonopoly Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Antimonopoly_Service) of Russia (FAS) imposed a record fine of [₽](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_ruble)6.54 billion on the company for violating antitrust legislation. The fine was imposed for the abuse of the company's dominant position in the wholesale market of [petroleum products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroleum_product) in the first half of 2009, expressed in "the seizure of goods from circulation" and the creation of "discriminatory conditions for the sale of petroleum products to individual counterparties". As FAS has calculated, these actions led to an increase in prices in the wholesale markets of motor [gasoline](23639.md), [diesel fuel](81761.md), and aviation [kerosene](16992.md) in the first half of 2009.[^cite_note-87]

### Cambridge Analytica

In March 2018, the data firm [Cambridge Analytica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambridge_Analytica), tied to the 2016 Trump Campaign, was accused of discussing "political targeting" of American voters with representatives of Lukoil.[^cite_note-88] "Cambridge Analytica sought to identify mental and emotional characteristics in certain subsets of the American population and worked to exploit them by designing them to activate some of the worst vulnerabilities in people, such as [neuroticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuroticism), [paranoia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paranoia) and [racial biases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_bias)," whistleblower [Christopher Wylie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Wylie) told the [Senate Judiciary Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Senate_Committee_on_the_Judiciary) in 2018.[^cite_note-89] With Lukoil, the consulting firm shared election disruption strategies, which included videos and posters intended to demoralise and alarm voters.[^cite_note-\:3-90] Lukoil is on the Sectoral Sanctions Identifications list, has been linked to Russian influence in the past, and [CEO Vagit Alekperov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vagit_Alekperov), a former oil minister, had made statements suggesting that he considers helping Russia to be a strong political ambition.[^cite_note-\:3-90][^cite_note-\:4-91]

### VP driving accident

On 25 February 2010, Lukoil's vice president [Anatoliy Barkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatoliy_Barkov?action=edit\&redlink=1) crashed his [Mercedes S500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes-Benz_S-Class) into a [Citroën C3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citro%C3%ABn_C3) car with doctor Olga Alexandrina and famous [obstetrician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obstetrician) Vera M. Sidelnikova inside; both women died in the collision. The [General Administration for Traffic Safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Administration_for_Traffic_Safety) blamed the driver of the Citroën. Still, it was suggested that the administration was covering up the fact that the real culprit of the accident was the driver of the Mercedes, who, according to eyewitnesses, had driven around a traffic jam and entered the oncoming lane. There is also speculation that the driver of the Mercedes was the vice president of Lukoil himself. A few days after the accident, the Head of the State Traffic Safety Inspectorate of Moscow issued a notice of misconduct to the commander of the Traffic Police battalion who registered the accident on [Leninsky Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leninsky_Avenue,_Moscow) because he had prematurely called the driver of the Citroën, Olga Alexandrin, the culprit of the accident. The incident sparked a public reaction, particularly a boycott of the company's gas stations. Blogger Andrei Bocharov announced a mock advertisement of Lukoil based on this accident, and rapper [Noize MC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noize_MC) wrote the song "Mercedes S666 (Make Way for the Chariot)".[^cite_note-newizv122439-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97][^cite_note-Глава_ГИБДД_Москвы_наказал_гаишника,_оформлявшего_ДТП_на_Ленинском-98][^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

### Bulgaria

In July 2011, Lukoil had a conflict with [Bulgarian](3415.md) authorities. According to the latter, the company's Bourgas refinery did not have timely installed meters for the manufactured fuel (used to determine the amount of excises paid), which, according to officials, Lukoil allegedly underpaid about €250 million to the country's budget. As a result, the company lost its license and was shut down, but in early August 2011, the plant was relaunched.[^cite_note-101]

In 2023, Bulgaria took the decision to force Russian energy companies out of the country, and increased the tax rate to 60% on the [Lukoil Neftohim Burgas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukoil_Neftohim_Burgas) profits, hoping the plant would be sold.[^cite_note-102] Bulgaria also banned the export of refined Russian oil from 1 January 2024 and the import of Russian crude oil from 1 March 2024.[^cite_note-103]

### Romania

In October 2023, Romania invalidated the petroleum products wholesale trading licence that had been held by the Swiss-registered subsidiary of Lukoil, Litasco SA, which supplies crude oil to and distributes refined oil from the [Petrotel Lukoil Refinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrotel_Lukoil_Refinery). Apart from a tax dispute, Romania claims the refinery does not disclose the source of its crude oil, the amount it processes, or where the refined oil is distributed.[^cite_note-104]

### Sanctions

On 11 September 2014, [US President Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) said that the United States would join the [EU](9317.md) in imposing tougher sanctions on Russia's [financial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_services), [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) and [defence](608296.md) sectors, following the escalation of [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md).[^cite_note-105] The U.S. added Lukoil to the Sectoral Sanctions Identifications list on 12 September 2014.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-\:4-91]

The effect of sanctions in 2022 against shipped Russian oil has had a detrimental effect on the [Priolo Gargallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priolo_Gargallo) ISAB refinery in Sicily, with an inability to bring in oil from Russia and related cash flow finance problems.[^cite_note-107] This has resulted in the refinery being put under Italian trusteeship with a buyer being sought.[^cite_note-108]

New Zealand has sanctioned Lukoil.[^cite_note-109] On 22 October 2025, the U.S. imposed further sanctions on Lukoil.[^cite_note-110]
