**Gelderland** ([/ˈɡɛldərlənd/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*GHEL-dər-lənd*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key), Dutch: [\[ˈɣɛldərlɑnt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Dutch) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/90/Nl-Gelderland.ogg/Nl-Gelderland.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/9/90/Nl-Gelderland.ogg/Nl-Gelderland.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), also known as **Guelders** ([/ˈɡɛldərz/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*GHEL-dərz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key))[^cite_note-6] in English, is a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_the_Netherlands) of the [Netherlands](21148.md), located in the centre-east of the country. With a total area of 5,136 km<sup>2</sup> (1,983 sq mi) of which 176 km<sup>2</sup> (68 sq mi) is water, it is the largest province of the Netherlands by land area, and second by total area.[^cite_note-CBS_key_figs-7] Gelderland shares borders with six other provinces ([Flevoland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flevoland), [Limburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limburg_\(Netherlands\)), [North Brabant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Brabant), [Overijssel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overijssel), [South Holland](28687.md) and [Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utrecht_\(province\))) and the [German](11867.md) state of [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md).

The capital is [Arnhem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem) (pop. 159,265[^cite_note-8]); however, [Nijmegen](21713.md) (pop. 176,731) and [Apeldoorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apeldoorn) (pop. 162,445) are both larger municipalities. Other major regional centres in Gelderland are [Ede](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ede,_Netherlands), [Doetinchem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doetinchem), [Zutphen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zutphen), [Harderwijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harderwijk), [Tiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiel), [Wageningen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wageningen), [Zevenaar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zevenaar), and [Winterswijk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterswijk). Gelderland had a population of about 2,134,000 as of January 2023.[^cite_note-opendata.cbs.nl-3] It contains the Netherlands's largest [forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest) region (the [Veluwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veluwe)), the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) and other major rivers, and a significant amount of [orchards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchard) in the south ([Betuwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betuwe)).

## History

Historically, the province dates from states of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) and takes its name from the nearby German city of [Geldern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geldern). According to the [Wichard saga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sagas_of_Veluwe), the Lords of Pont fought and killed a [dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon) in 878 AD, and named the town they founded after the death-rattle of the dragon: "*Gelre!*"[^cite_note-9]

The County of [Guelders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelders) arose out of the [Frankish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franks) [pagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagus) [Hamaland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamaland) in the 11th century around castles near [Roermond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roermond) and [Geldern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geldern). The counts of Gelre acquired the [Betuwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betuwe) and [Veluwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veluwe) regions and, through marriage, the [County of Zutphen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Zutphen). Thus the counts of Guelders laid the foundation for a territorial power that, through control of the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine), [Waal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waal_\(river\)), [Meuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meuse) and [IJssel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IJssel) rivers, was to play an important role in the later [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages). The geographical position of their territory dictated the external policy of the counts during the following centuries; they were committed to the interests of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) and to expansion south and west.

Further enlarged by the acquisition of the imperial city of Nijmegen in the 13th century, the countship was raised to a duchy in 1339 by the Holy Roman Emperor [Louis IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). After 1379, the duchy was ruled from [Jülich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J%C3%BClich) and by the counts of [Egmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egmond_aan_den_Hoef) and [Cleves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kleve). The duchy resisted [Burgundian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Burgundy) domination, but [William, Duke of Jülich-Cleves-Berg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William,_Duke_of_J%C3%BClich-Cleves-Berg) was forced to cede it to [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) in 1543, after which it formed part of the Burgundian-Habsburg hereditary lands.[^cite_note-Britannica-10]

The duchy revolted with the rest of the Netherlands against [Philip II of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Spain) and joined the [Union of Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Utrecht) (1579). After the deposition of Philip II, its sovereignty was vested in the [States of Gelderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Gelderland), and the [princes of Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince_of_Orange) were [stadtholders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadtholder). In 1672, the province was temporarily occupied by [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV) and, in 1713, the southeastern part, including the ducal capital of Geldern, fell to [Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussia). Part of the [Batavian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavian_Republic) (1795–1806), of [Louis Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Bonaparte)'s [Kingdom of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Holland) (1806–10), and of the [French Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_French_Empire) (1810–13), Gelderland became a province of the Kingdom of the Netherlands in 1815.[^cite_note-Britannica-10]

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the area saw heavy fighting between [Allied Paratroopers, British XXX Corps and the German II SS Panzer Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Market_Garden), at the [Battle of Arnhem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Arnhem).

## Geography

Gelderland can roughly be divided into four geographical regions: the [Veluwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veluwe) in the north, the Rivierenland including the [Betuwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betuwe) in the southwest, the [Achterhoek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achterhoek) ('back corner') or Graafschap (which originally means [earldom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earldom) or county) in the east and the city-region of Arnhem and Nijmegen in the centre-south.

## Economy

The [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the region was €78.3 billion in 2018, accounting for 10.1% of the Netherlands' economic output. GDP per capita adjusted for purchasing power was €33,000 or 110% of the [EU27](9317.md) average in the same year.[^cite_note-12]

## Culture

In Gelderland there are many museums, like the [Netherlands Open Air Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Open_Air_Museum) and [Museum Arnhem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_Arnhem) in Arnhem, [Valkhof Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkhof_Museum) in Nijmegen, the [Het Loo Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Het_Loo_Palace) in [Apeldoorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apeldoorn) and in [Otterlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otterlo) the [Kröller-Müller Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kr%C3%B6ller-M%C3%BCller_Museum). There are several large theatres in Gelderland such as the Stadsschouwburg in Nijmegen, Stadstheater in Arnhem and Orpheus (theater) in Apeldoorn. Some cities are also equipped with large concert halls like MUSIS (formerly: Musis sacrum) in Arnhem and [Concertgebouw de Vereeniging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concertgebouw_de_Vereeniging) in Nijmegen. The more widely-known [popular music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_music) venues are Luxor Live in Arnhem, Doornroosje in Nijmegen, Gigant in Apeldoorn, and the [GelreDome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GelreDome) stadium in Arnhem. Every year the municipality [Renkum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renkum) and [Overbetuwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overbetuwe) receive a large amount of tourist visiting because of the [Battle of Arnhem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Arnhem). Often historical locations are visited, like the [John Frost Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Frost_Bridge) and the [Arnhem Oosterbeek War Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnhem_Oosterbeek_War_Cemetery) in [Oosterbeek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oosterbeek).

## Religion

Religion in Gelderland (2015)

[^cite_note-religieus-13]

1. Not religious (46.3%)

2. [Protestant Church in the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Church_in_the_Netherlands) (23.2%)

3. [Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) (21.6%)

4. Other (5.30%)

5. [Islam](6037917.md) (3.60%)

In 2015, 23.2% of the population belonged to the [Protestant Church in the Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant_Church_in_the_Netherlands), while 21.6% were [Roman Catholic](606848.md), 3.6% were [Muslim](6037917.md), and 5.3% adhered to other churches or faiths. Close to half of the population (46.3%) identified as non-religious.
