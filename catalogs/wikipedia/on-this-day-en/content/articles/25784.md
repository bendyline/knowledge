**Renewable energy** (also called **green energy**) is [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) made from [renewable natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_resource) that are replenished on a [human timescale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_lifetime). The most widely used renewable energy types are [solar energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy), [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), bioenergy/biomass, and [hydropower](14073.md). [Bioenergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioenergy) and [geothermal power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power) are also significant in some countries. Renewable energy installations can be large or small and are suited for both urban and rural areas. Renewable energy is often deployed together with further [electrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrification). This has several benefits: electricity can [move heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_pump) and [vehicles](279350.md) efficiently and is clean at the point of consumption.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] [Variable renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_renewable_energy) sources are those that have a fluctuating nature, such as wind power and solar power. In contrast, *controllable renewable energy* sources include dammed [hydroelectricity](381399.md), [bioenergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioenergy), or [geothermal power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_power).

For an energy to be renewable, it must be an endless source[^cite_note-3]. Nuclear power is therefore not considered renewable, due to its reliance on non-renewable fuel.[^cite_note-4]

Renewable energy systems have rapidly become more efficient and cheaper over the past 40 years.[^cite_note-5] A large majority of newly installed worldwide electricity capacity is now renewable.[^cite_note-6] Renewable energy sources, such as solar and wind power, have seen significant cost reductions over the past decade, making them more competitive with traditional fossil fuels.[^cite_note-7] In some geographic localities, [photovoltaic solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_system) or [onshore wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power) is the cheapest new-build electricity.[^cite_note-IEA_Renewables_2020-8] From 2011 to 2021, renewable energy grew from 20% to 28% of the global electricity supply. Power from the sun and wind accounted for most of this increase, growing from a combined 2% to 10%. Use of [fossil energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_energy) shrank from 68% to 62%.[^cite_note-\:8-9] In 2025, renewables accounted for 34% of global electricity generation, up from 32% in 2024, and are projected to reach over 43% by 2030.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Many countries already have renewables contributing more than 20% of their total energy supply, with some generating over half or even all their electricity from renewable sources.[^cite_note-\:5-12][^cite_note-13]

The main motivation to use renewable energy instead of fossil fuels is to reduce [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md), which cause [climate change](5042951.md). In general, renewable energy sources pollute much less than fossil fuels.[^cite_note-\:2-14] Renewables also cause much less [air pollution](10934212.md) than fossil fuels, improving public health, and are less [noisy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_pollution).[^cite_note-\:2-14] The [International Energy Agency](285292.md) estimates that to achieve [net zero emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_zero_emissions) by 2050, 90% of global electricity will need to be generated by renewables.[^cite_note-15] The current pace of renewable expansion remains far from this required rate globally,[^cite_note-16] including in major economies with high financial capacities such as the G7 and the EU.[^cite_note-17]

The deployment of renewable energy still faces obstacles, especially [fossil fuel subsidies](44334234.md),[^cite_note-18] [lobbying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbying) by incumbent power providers,[^cite_note-19] and local opposition to the use of land for renewable installations.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-\:0-21] Like all mining, the extraction of minerals required for many renewable energy technologies also results in [environmental damage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_effects_of_mining).[^cite_note-22]

Some also consider [nuclear power a renewable power source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_as_sustainable_energy), although this is controversial, as nuclear energy requires mining uranium, a nonrenewable resource.

### Definition

Renewable energy is usually understood as energy harnessed from continuously occurring natural phenomena. The [International Energy Agency](285292.md) defines it as "energy derived from natural processes that are replenished at a faster rate than they are consumed". [Solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy), [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), [hydroelectricity](381399.md), [geothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy) energy, and [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass_\(energy\)) are widely agreed to be the main types of renewable energy.[^cite_note-26] Renewable energy often displaces conventional fuels in four areas: [electricity generation](9540.md), [hot water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_water_heating)/[space heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_heating), [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation), and rural (off-grid) energy services.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEREN21_Renewables_Global_Status_Report2010-27]

Although almost all forms of renewable energy cause much fewer carbon emissions than fossil fuels, the term is not synonymous with [low-carbon energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-carbon_electricity). Some non-renewable sources of energy, such as [nuclear power](22153.md),generate almost no emissions, while some renewable energy sources can be very carbon-intensive, such as the burning of biomass if it is not offset by planting new plants.[^cite_note-\:2-14] Renewable energy is also distinct from [sustainable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy), a more abstract concept that seeks to group energy sources based on their overall permanent impact on future generations of humans. For example, biomass is often associated with unsustainable [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation).[^cite_note-28]

### Role in addressing climate change

As part of the global effort to [limit climate change](2119179.md), most countries have committed to [net zero greenhouse gas emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_zero_emissions).[^cite_note-29] In practice, this means [phasing out fossil fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_phase-out) and replacing them with low-emissions energy sources.[^cite_note-\:2-14] This much needed process, coined as "low-carbon substitutions"[^cite_note-Suzuki_et_al._2023-30] in contrast to other transition processes including energy additions, needs to be accelerated multiple times in order to successfully mitigate climate change.[^cite_note-Suzuki_et_al._2023-30] At the [2023 United Nations Climate Change Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_United_Nations_Climate_Change_Conference), around three-quarters of the world's countries set a goal of tripling renewable energy capacity by 2030.[^cite_note-31] The [European Union](9317.md) aimed in 2022 to generate 40% of its electricity from renewables by the same year,[^cite_note-32] and by 2025, 47% of EU electricity generation was by renewables.[^cite_note-33]

### Other benefits

Main article: [Climate change mitigation § Co-benefits](2119179.md#Co-benefits)

Renewable energy is more evenly distributed around the world than fossil fuels, which are concentrated in a limited number of countries.[^cite_note-34] It also brings health benefits by reducing [air pollution](10934212.md) caused by the burning of fossil fuels. The potential worldwide savings in health care costs have been estimated at trillions of dollars annually.[^cite_note-35]

### Intermittency

Main article: [Variable renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variable_renewable_energy)



Energy from sunlight or other renewable energy is converted to potential energy for storage in devices such as electric batteries. The stored potential energy is later converted to electricity that is added to the power grid, even when the original energy source is not available.



Estimated power demand over a week in May 2012 and May 2020, Germany, showing the variability in solar and wind power both day-to-day and month-to-month

The two most important forms of renewable energy, solar and wind, are *intermittent energy sources*: they are not available constantly, resulting in lower [capacity factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capacity_factor). In contrast, [fossil fuel power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel_power_station), nuclear power plants and hydropower are usually able to produce precisely the amount of energy an [electricity grid](20344155.md) requires at a given time. Solar energy can only be captured during the day, and ideally in cloudless conditions. Wind power generation can vary significantly not only day-to-day, but even month-to-month.[^cite_note-36] This poses a challenge when transitioning away from fossil fuels: energy demand will often be higher or lower than what renewables can provide.[^cite_note-Olauson_16175-37]

In the medium-term, this variability may require keeping some [gas-fired power plants](12438990.md) or other [dispatchable generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispatchable_generation) on standby[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] until there is enough energy storage, [demand response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demand_response), grid improvement, or [base load power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Base_load) from non-intermittent sources. In the long-term, [energy storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_storage) is an important way of dealing with intermittency.[^cite_note-40] Using diversified renewable energy sources and [smart grids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_grid) can also help flatten supply and demand.[^cite_note-41]

[Sector coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sector_coupling) of the power generation sector with other sectors may increase flexibility: for example the transport sector can be coupled by charging electric vehicles and sending electricity from [vehicle to grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle-to-grid).[^cite_note-42] Similarly the industry sector can be coupled by hydrogen produced by electrolysis,[^cite_note-43] and the buildings sector by thermal energy storage for space heating and cooling.[^cite_note-44]

Building overcapacity for wind and solar generation can help ensure sufficient electricity production even during poor weather. In optimal weather, it may be necessary to curtail energy generation if it is not possible to use or store excess electricity.[^cite_note-45]

#### Electrical energy storage

Main articles: [Energy storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_storage) and [Grid energy storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_energy_storage)

Electrical energy storage is a collection of methods used to store electrical energy. Electrical energy is stored during times when production (especially from intermittent sources such as [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), [tidal power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_power), [solar power](13690575.md)) exceeds consumption, and returned to the [grid](20344155.md) when production falls below consumption. [Pumped-storage hydroelectricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumped-storage_hydroelectricity) accounts for more than 85% of all [grid power storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grid_energy_storage).[^cite_note-46] Batteries are increasingly being deployed for storage[^cite_note-47] and grid [ancillary services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancillary_services)[^cite_note-48] and for domestic storage.[^cite_note-49] [Green hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_hydrogen) is a more economical means of long-term renewable energy storage, in terms of [capital expenditures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_expenditure) compared to pumped hydroelectric or batteries.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

#### Energy supply security

Two main renewable energy sources - solar power and wind power - are usually deployed in [distributed generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distributed_generation) architecture, which offers specific benefits and comes with specific risks.[^cite_note-52] Notable risks are associated with centralisation of 90% of the supply chains in a single country (China) in the photovoltaic sector.[^cite_note-53] Mass-scale installation of photovoltaic [power inverters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_inverter) with remote control, [security vulnerabilities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulnerability_\(computer_security\)) and [backdoors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_shell) results in cyberattacks that can disable generation from millions of physically decentralised panels, resulting in disappearance of hundreds of gigawatts of installed power from the grid in one moment.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Similar attacks have targeted wind power farms through vulnerabilities in their remote control and monitoring systems.[^cite_note-56] The European [NIS2 directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyber-security_regulation) partially responds to these challenges by extending the scope of cybersecurity regulations to the energy generation market.[^cite_note-57] Recent analyses indicate that global solar photovoltaic capacity surpassed 1 terawatt in 2024, providing about 6–7% of global electricity supply.[^cite_note-58] Renewable energy infrastructure is also increasingly vulnerable to extreme weather events linked to climate change, such as heat waves, wildfires, severe storms, and flooding. Solar farms can experience reduced output during prolonged heat or smoke conditions, while wind turbines may require shutdowns during high-wind events or face damage from icing or wave action. These climate-related stresses can threaten the continuity of electricity supply in regions with high shares of variable renewables. As a result, governments and grid operators are adopting climate-resilience standards, hardening infrastructure, and developing emergency-response protocols to maintain energy security under more frequent and severe weather conditions.[^cite_note-59]

Solar power produced around 1.3 terrawatt-hours (TWh) worldwide in 2022,[^cite_note-\:5-12] representing 4.6% of the world's electricity. Almost all of this growth has happened since 2010.[^cite_note-65] Solar energy can be harnessed anywhere that receives sunlight; however, the amount of solar energy that can be harnessed for electricity generation is influenced by [weather conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weather), geographic location and time of day.[^cite_note-66]

There are two mainstream ways of harnessing solar energy: [solar thermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_thermal_energy), which converts solar energy into heat; and [photovoltaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaics) (PV), which converts it into electricity.[^cite_note-\:2-14] PV is far more widespread, accounting for around two thirds of the global solar energy capacity as of 2022.[^cite_note-\:4-67] It is also growing at a much faster rate, with 170 GW newly installed capacity in 2021,[^cite_note-68] compared to 25 GW of solar thermal.[^cite_note-\:4-67]

[Passive solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_solar) refers to a range of construction strategies and technologies that aim to optimize the distribution of solar heat in a building. Examples include [solar chimneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_chimney),[^cite_note-\:2-14] orienting a building to the sun, using [construction materials that can store heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_mass), and designing spaces that [naturally circulate air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventilation_\(architecture\)).[^cite_note-69]

From 2020 to 2022, solar technology investments almost doubled from US\$162 billion to US\$308 billion, driven by the sector's increasing maturity and cost reductions, particularly in solar photovoltaic (PV), which accounted for 90% of total investments. China and the United States were the main recipients, collectively making up about half of all solar investments since 2013. Despite reductions in Japan and India due to policy changes and [COVID-19](63030231.md), growth in China, the United States, and a significant increase from Vietnam's feed-in tariff program offset these declines. Globally, the solar sector added 714 gigawatts (GW) of solar PV and [concentrated solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentrated_solar_power) (CSP) capacity between 2013 and 2021, with a notable rise in large-scale solar heating installations in 2021, especially in China, Europe, Turkey, and Mexico.[^cite_note-\:1-70] In 2023, global solar power capacity grew by nearly 30%, driven by falling panel prices and expanded government incentives worldwide.[^cite_note-71]

#### Photovoltaics

Main articles: [Growth of photovoltaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_of_photovoltaics), [Solar power by country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_power_by_country), and [List of photovoltaic power stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_photovoltaic_power_stations)



[Swanson's law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swanson's_law)–stating that solar module prices have dropped about 20% for each doubling of installed capacity—defines the "[learning rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experience_curve_effects)" of [solar photovoltaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaics).

[^cite_note-SolarPVlearningCurve-72]

[^cite_note-73]

A [photovoltaic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_system), consisting of [solar cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_cell) assembled into [panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panel), converts light into electrical [direct current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_current) via the [photoelectric effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photoelectric_effect).[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

PV systems range from small, residential and commercial [rooftop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooftop_solar_power) or [building integrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building-integrated_photovoltaics) installations,[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] to large utility-scale [photovoltaic power station](34779206.md).[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] A household's solar panels can either be used for just that household or, if connected to an electrical grid, can be aggregated with millions of others.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

The first utility-scale solar power plant was built in 1982 in [Hesperia, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hesperia,_California) by [ARCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARCO).[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] The plant was not profitable and was sold eight years later.[^cite_note-87] However, over the following decades, PV cells became significantly more efficient and cheaper.[^cite_note-deutsche-2015-chasm-88] As a result, PV adoption has grown exponentially since 2010.[^cite_note-89] Global capacity increased from 230 GW at the end of 2015 to 890 GW in 2021.[^cite_note-IRENA2018-90] PV grew fastest in China between 2016 and 2021, adding 560 GW, more than all advanced economies combined.[^cite_note-IEA-3-91] In 2025, four of the ten biggest solar power stations were in China, including the biggest, [Talatan Solar Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talatan_Solar_Park).[^cite_note-ie-20251009-92]

[Solar panels are recycled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_cell#Recycling) to reduce [electronic waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_waste) and create a source for materials that would otherwise need to be mined,[^cite_note-93] but such business is still small and work is ongoing to improve and scale-up the process.[^cite_note-techrev-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

#### Solar thermal

Main article: [Solar thermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_thermal_energy)

Unlike photovoltaic cells that convert sunlight directly into electricity, [solar thermal systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_thermal_system) convert it into heat. They use mirrors or lenses to concentrate sunlight onto a receiver, which in turn heats a water reservoir. The heated water can then be used in homes. The advantage of solar thermal is that the heated water can be stored until it is needed, eliminating the need for a separate energy storage system.[^cite_note-97] Solar thermal power can also be converted to electricity by using the steam generated from the heated water to drive a [turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbine) connected to a generator. However, because generating electricity this way is much more expensive than photovoltaic power plants, there are very few in use today.[^cite_note-98]

#### Floatovoltaics

Main article: [Floating solar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floating_solar)

Floatovoltiacs, or floating solar panels, are solar panels floating on bodies of water. There are both positive and negative points to this. Some positive points are increased efficiency and price decrease of water space compared to land space. A negative point is that making floating solar panels could be more expensive.

#### Agrivoltaics

Main article: [Agrivoltaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrivoltaics)

Agrivoltaics is where there is simultaneous use of land for energy production and agriculture. There are again both positive and negative points. A positive viewpoint is there is a better use of land, which leads to lower land costs. A negative viewpoint is it the plants grown underneath would have to be plants that can grow well under shade, such as [Polka Dot Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polka_Dot_Plant), [Pineapple Sage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple_Sage), and [Begonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Begonia).[^cite_note-99] Agrivoltaics not only optimizes land use and reduces costs by enabling dual revenue streams from both energy production and agriculture, but it can also help moderate temperatures beneath the panels, potentially reducing water loss and improving [microclimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microclimate) for crop growth. However, careful design and crop selection are crucial, as the shading effect may limit the types of plants that can thrive, necessitating the use of shade-tolerant species and innovative management practices.[^cite_note-100]

Humans have harnessed wind energy since at least 3500 BC. Until the 20th century, it was primarily used to power ships, windmills and water pumps. Today, the vast majority of wind power is used to generate electricity using wind turbines.[^cite_note-\:2-14] Modern utility-scale wind turbines range from around 600 kW to 9 MW of rated power. The power available from the wind is a function of the cube of the wind speed, so as wind speed increases, power output increases up to the maximum output for the particular turbine.[^cite_note-EWEA-105] Areas where winds are stronger and more constant, such as [offshore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_wind_power) and high-altitude sites, are preferred locations for wind farms.

Wind-generated electricity met nearly 4% of global electricity demand in 2015, with nearly 63 GW of new wind power capacity installed. Wind energy was the leading source of new capacity in Europe, the US and Canada, and the second largest in China. In Denmark, wind energy met more than 40% of its electricity demand while Ireland, Portugal and [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_Spain) each met nearly 20%.[^cite_note-106]

Globally, the long-term technical potential of wind energy is believed to be five times total current global energy production, or 40 times current electricity demand, assuming all practical barriers needed were overcome. This would require wind turbines to be installed over large areas, particularly in areas of higher wind resources, such as offshore, and likely also industrial use of new types of [VAWT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VAWT) turbines in addition to the horizontal axis units currently in use. As offshore wind speeds average \~90% greater than that of land, offshore resources can contribute substantially more energy than land-stationed turbines.[^cite_note-107]

Investments in wind technologies reached US\$161 billion in 2020, with onshore wind dominating at 80% of total investments from 2013 to 2022. Offshore wind investments nearly doubled to US\$41 billion between 2019 and 2020, primarily due to policy incentives in China and expansion in Europe. Global wind capacity increased by 557 GW between 2013 and 2021, with capacity additions increasing by an average of 19% each year.[^cite_note-\:1-70]

Since water is about 800 times [denser than air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Density_of_air), even a slow flowing stream of water, or moderate sea [swell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swell_\(ocean\)), can yield considerable amounts of energy. Water can generate electricity with a [conversion efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_conversion_efficiency) of about 90%, which is the highest rate in renewable energy.[^cite_note-Ang-111] There are many forms of water energy:

- Historically, hydroelectric power came from constructing large hydroelectric dams and reservoirs, which are still popular in [developing countries](78449.md).

  [^cite_note-112]

  &#x20;The [largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_hydroelectric_power_stations) of them are the [Three Gorges Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Gorges_Dam) (2003) in [China](5405.md) and the [Itaipu Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itaipu_Dam) (1984) built by Brazil and Paraguay.

- [Small hydro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small_hydro) systems are hydroelectric power installations that typically produce up to 50 MW of power. They are often used on small rivers or as a low-impact development on larger rivers. China is the largest producer of hydroelectricity in the world and has more than 45,000 small hydro installations.

  [^cite_note-113]

- [Run-of-the-river hydroelectricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run-of-the-river_hydroelectricity) plants derive energy from rivers without the creation of a large [reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir). The water is typically conveyed along the side of the river valley (using channels, pipes or tunnels) until it is high above the valley floor, whereupon it can be allowed to fall through a penstock to drive a turbine. A run-of-river plant may still produce a large amount of electricity, such as the [Chief Joseph Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Joseph_Dam) on the [Columbia River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_River) in the United States.

  [^cite_note-114]

  &#x20;However many run-of-the-river hydro power plants are [micro hydro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro_hydro) or [pico hydro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_hydro) plants.

Much hydropower is flexible, thus complementing wind and solar, as it not intermittent.[^cite_note-115] In 2021, the world renewable hydropower capacity was 1,360 GW.[^cite_note-IEA-3-91] Only a third of the world's estimated hydroelectric potential of 14,000 TWh/year has been developed.[^cite_note-iha2021-116][^cite_note-etp2006-117] New hydropower projects face opposition from local communities due to their large impact, including relocation of communities and flooding of wildlife habitats and farming land.[^cite_note-118] High cost and lead times from permission process, including environmental and risk assessments, with lack of environmental and social acceptance are therefore the primary challenges for new developments.[^cite_note-119] It is popular to repower old dams thereby increasing their efficiency and capacity as well as quicker responsiveness on the grid.[^cite_note-120] Where circumstances permit existing dams such as the [Russell Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_B._Russell_Dam) built in 1985 may be updated with "pump back" facilities for [pumped-storage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumped-storage_hydroelectricity) which is useful for peak loads or to support intermittent wind and solar power. Because [dispatchable power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dispatchable_generation) is more valuable than VRE[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] countries with large hydroelectric developments such as Canada and Norway are spending billions to expand their grids to trade with neighboring countries having limited hydro.[^cite_note-123]

[Biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass_\(energy\)) is biological material derived from living, or recently living organisms. Most commonly, it refers to plants or plant-derived materials. As an energy source, biomass can either be used directly via [combustion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combustion) to produce heat, or converted to a more energy-dense [biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel) like ethanol. Wood is the most significant biomass energy source as of 2012[^cite_note-Scheck2012-127] and is usually sourced from a trees cleared for [silvicultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silviculture) reasons or [fire prevention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_prevention). Municipal wood waste – for instance, construction materials or sawdust – is also often burned for energy.[^cite_note-128] The biggest per-capita producers of wood-based bioenergy are heavily forested countries like Finland, Sweden, Estonia, Austria, and Denmark.[^cite_note-129]

Bioenergy can be environmentally destructive if old-growth forests are cleared to make way for crop production. In particular, demand for palm oil to produce biodiesel has contributed to the deforestation of tropical rainforests in Brazil and Indonesia.[^cite_note-\:10-130] In addition, burning biomass still produces carbon emissions, although much less than fossil fuels (39 grams of CO<sub>2</sub> per megajoule of energy, compared to 75 g/MJ for fossil fuels).[^cite_note-131]

Some [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass_\(energy\)) sources are unsustainable at current rates of exploitation (as of 2017).[^cite_note-CarbonBrief1-132]

#### Biofuel

Main article: [Biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel)

See also: [Ethanol fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fuel), [Sustainable biofuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_biofuel), and [Issues relating to biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issues_relating_to_biofuels)

[Biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel) are primarily used in transportation, providing 3.5% of the world's transport energy demand in 2022,[^cite_note-133] up from 2.7% in 2010.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEREN21_Renewables_Global_Status_Report201113–14-134] [Biojet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biojet) is expected to be important for short-term reduction of carbon dioxide emissions from long-haul flights.[^cite_note-135]



A bus fueled by [biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel)

Aside from wood, the major sources of bioenergy are [bioethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fuel) and [biodiesel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiesel).[^cite_note-\:2-14] Bioethanol is usually produced by fermenting the sugar components of crops like [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) and [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), while biodiesel is mostly made from oils extracted from plants, such as [soybean oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean_oil) and [corn oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_oil).[^cite_note-\:9-136] Most of the crops used to produce bioethanol and biodiesel are grown specifically for this purpose,[^cite_note-137] although used [cooking oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_oil) accounted for 14% of the oil used to produce biodiesel as of 2015.[^cite_note-\:9-136] The biomass used to produce biofuels varies by region. Maize is the major feedstock in the United States, while sugarcane dominates in Brazil.[^cite_note-138] In the European Union, where biodiesel is more common than bioethanol, [rapeseed oil](71016451.md) and [palm oil](57561.md) are the main feedstocks.[^cite_note-139] China, although it produces comparatively much less biofuel, uses mostly corn and wheat.[^cite_note-140] In many countries, biofuels are either subsidized or mandated to be [included in fuel mixtures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_ethanol_fuel_mixtures).[^cite_note-\:10-130]



[Sugarcane plantation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_biofuel#Sugarcane_in_Brazil) to produce [ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethanol_fuel) in Brazil

There are many other sources of bioenergy that are more niche, or not yet viable at large scales. For instance, bioethanol could be [produced from the cellulosic parts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulosic_ethanol) of crops, rather than only the seed as is common today.[^cite_note-141] [Sweet sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_sorghum) may be a promising alternative source of bioethanol, due to its tolerance of a wide range of climates.[^cite_note-142] [Cow dung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cow_dung) can be converted into methane.[^cite_note-143] There is also a great deal of research involving [algal fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algal_fuel), which is attractive because algae is a non-food resource, grows around 20 times faster than most food crops, and can be grown almost anywhere.[^cite_note-ZhuLiHiltunen2018-144]

Geothermal energy is [thermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_energy) (heat) extracted from the [Earth's crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_crust). It originates from several different [sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_internal_heat_budget#Sources_of_heat), of which the most significant is slow [radioactive decay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_decay) of minerals contained in the [Earth's interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_structure_of_Earth),[^cite_note-\:2-14] as well as some leftover heat from the [formation of the Earth](9228.md#Formation).[^cite_note-\:11-149] Some of the heat is generated near the Earth's surface in the crust, but some also flows from deep within the Earth from the [mantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_mantle) and [core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_structure_of_Earth#Core).[^cite_note-\:11-149] Geothermal energy extraction is viable mostly in countries located on [tectonic plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics) edges, where the Earth's hot mantle is more exposed.[^cite_note-\:13-150] As of 2023, the United States has by far the most geothermal capacity (2.7 GW,[^cite_note-\:12-151] or less than 0.2% of the country's total energy capacity[^cite_note-152]), followed by Indonesia and the Philippines. Global capacity in 2022 was 15 GW.[^cite_note-\:12-151]

Geothermal energy can be either used directly to heat homes, as is common in Iceland where almost all of its energy is renewable, or to generate electricity. Iceland is a global leader in renewable energy, relying almost entirely on its abundant geothermal and hydroelectric resources derived from volcanic activity and glaciers.[^cite_note-153] At smaller scales, geothermal power can be generated with [geothermal heat pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_source_heat_pump), which can extract heat from ground temperatures of under 30 °C (86 °F), allowing them to be used at relatively shallow depths of a few meters.[^cite_note-\:13-150] Electricity generation requires large plants and ground temperatures of at least 150 °C (302 °F). In some countries, electricity produced from geothermal energy accounts for a large portion of the total, such as Kenya (43%) and Indonesia (5%).[^cite_note-154]

Technical advances may eventually make geothermal power more widely available. For example, [enhanced geothermal systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_geothermal_system) involve drilling around 10 kilometres (6.2 mi) into the Earth, breaking apart hot rocks and extracting the heat using water. In theory, this type of geothermal energy extraction could be done anywhere on Earth.[^cite_note-\:13-150]

## Emerging technologies

There are also other renewable energy technologies that are still under development, including [enhanced geothermal systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_geothermal_system), [concentrated solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentrated_solar_power), [cellulosic ethanol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cellulosic_ethanol), [piezoelectricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezoelectricity), and [marine energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_energy).[^cite_note-\:7-155][^cite_note-IEA-156] These technologies are not yet widely demonstrated or have limited commercialization. Some may have potential comparable to other renewable energy technologies, but still depend on further breakthroughs from research, development and engineering.[^cite_note-IEA-156]

### Enhanced geothermal systems

Main article: [Enhanced geothermal systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_geothermal_systems)

Enhanced geothermal systems (EGS) are a new type of geothermal power which does not require natural hot water reservoirs or steam to generate power. Most of the underground heat within drilling reach is trapped in solid rocks, not in water.[^cite_note-Duchane-157] EGS technologies use [hydraulic fracturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_fracturing) to break apart these rocks and release the heat they contain, which is then harvested by pumping water into the ground. The process is sometimes known as "hot dry rock" (HDR).[^cite_note-158] Unlike conventional geothermal energy extraction, EGS may be feasible anywhere in the world, depending on the cost of drilling.[^cite_note-159] EGS projects have so far primarily been limited to [demonstration plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonstration_plant), as the technology is capital-intensive due to the high cost of drilling.[^cite_note-160]

### Piezoelectricity

[Piezoelectricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezoelectricity) is the conversion of existing [mechanical stress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stress_\(mechanics\)) or vibration ([classical mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_mechanics)) into an electrical charge without consuming or depleting a fuel source.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162] [Piezotronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezotronics) enables the interaction of piezoelectric and semiconducting behaviors to modulate energy barriers at contact surface, thereby controlling charge carrier transport.[^cite_note-163] Since the introduction of [nanogenerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanogenerator), the efficiency of microscale energy harvesting has improved. For instance, nanogenerators typically consist of piezoelectric [nanowires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanowire); as these wires bend or compress, the applied mechanical stress causes the ions within the material's [crystal lattice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_lattice) to shift their positions. This shift disrupts the nanowire's charge symmetry which causes an instantaneous charge [polarization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polarization_\(physics\)) (separation of positive and negative charges) across the nanowire's ends. Once polarized, electrons are freed from the attached electrode which generates [usable alternating current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternating_current) (AC) electricity that can energize low-power sensors.[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165] [Piezoelectric microelectromechanical systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezoelectric_microelectromechanical_systems) (piezoMEMS), such as [actuators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Actuator) for artificial organs and [pacemakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacemaker) or [micropumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micropump) for drug delivery and [reagent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reagent) transfers, are vital for medical purposes and energy harvesting.[^cite_note-166] Furthermore, specialized components like [piezoelectric resonators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piezoelectric_resonator_\(disambiguation\)) and quartz [crystal oscillators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_oscillator) are used to regulate electrical circuit frequencies.[^cite_note-167]

### Marine energy

Main article: [Marine energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_energy)



Aerial view of [Sihwa Tidal Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sihwa_Lake_Tidal_Power_Station) in [South Korea](27019.md)

Marine energy (also sometimes referred to as ocean energy) is the energy carried by [ocean waves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_wave), [tides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tide), [salinity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salinity), and [ocean temperature differences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_thermal_energy_conversion). Technologies to harness the energy of moving water include [wave power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wave_power), [marine current power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_current_power), and [tidal power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_power). [Reverse electrodialysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reverse_electrodialysis) (RED) is a technology for generating electricity by mixing [fresh water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fresh_water) and salty [sea water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawater) in large power cells.[^cite_note-168] Most marine energy harvesting technologies are still at low [technology readiness levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technology_readiness_level) and not used at large scales. Tidal energy is generally considered the most mature, but has not seen wide deployment.[^cite_note-169] The world's largest tidal power station is on [Sihwa Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sihwa_Lake_Tidal_Power_Station), South Korea,[^cite_note-170] which produces around 550 gigawatt-hours of electricity per year.[^cite_note-171]

### Earth infrared thermal radiation

Earth emits roughly 10<sup>17</sup> W of infrared thermal radiation that flows toward the cold outer space. Solar energy hits the surface and atmosphere of the earth and produces heat. Using various theorized devices like emissive energy harvester (EEH) or thermoradiative diode, this energy flow can be converted into electricity. In theory, this technology can be used during nighttime.[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173]

### Others

#### Algae fuels

Main article: [Algae fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae_fuels)

Producing liquid fuels from oil-rich (fat-rich) varieties of algae is an ongoing research topic. Various microalgae grown in open or closed systems are being tried including some systems that can be set up in brownfield and desert lands.[^cite_note-174]

#### Space-based solar power

Main article: [Space-based solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-based_solar_power)

There have been numerous proposals for [space-based solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-based_solar_power), in which very large satellites with photovoltaic panels would be equipped with [microwave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microwave) transmitters to beam power back to terrestrial receivers. A 2024 study by the [NASA](18426568.md) Office of Science and Technology Policy examined the concept and concluded that with current and near-future technologies it would be economically uncompetitive.[^cite_note-175]

#### Water vapor

Collection of static electricity charges from water droplets on metal surfaces is an experimental technology that would be especially useful in [low-income countries](78449.md) with relative air humidity over 60%.[^cite_note-176]

#### Nuclear energy

[Breeder reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breeder_reactor) could, in principle, depending on the fuel cycle employed, extract almost all of the energy contained in [uranium](31743.md) or [thorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorium), decreasing fuel requirements by a factor of 100 compared to widely used once-through [light water reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_water_reactors), which extract less than 1% of the energy in the actinide metal (uranium or thorium) mined from the earth.[^cite_note-Argonne-177] The high fuel-efficiency of breeder reactors could greatly reduce concerns about fuel supply, energy used in mining, and storage of [radioactive waste](37257.md). With [seawater uranium extraction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawater_uranium_extraction) (currently too expensive to be economical), there is enough fuel for breeder reactors to satisfy the world's energy needs for 5 billion years at 1983's total energy consumption rate, thus making nuclear energy effectively a renewable energy.[^cite_note-sustainablenuclear-178][^cite_note-179] In addition to seawater the average crustal granite rocks contain significant quantities of uranium and thorium with which breeder reactors can supply abundant energy for the remaining lifespan of the sun on the main sequence of stellar evolution.[^cite_note-180]

#### Artificial photosynthesis

Main article: [Artificial photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_photosynthesis)

Artificial photosynthesis uses techniques including [nanotechnology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanotechnology) to store solar electromagnetic energy in chemical bonds by splitting water to produce hydrogen and then using carbon dioxide to make methanol.[^cite_note-181] Researchers in this field strived to design molecular mimics of photosynthesis that use a wider region of the solar spectrum, employ catalytic systems made from abundant, inexpensive materials that are robust, readily repaired, non-toxic, stable in a variety of environmental conditions and perform more efficiently allowing a greater proportion of photon energy to end up in the storage compounds, i.e., carbohydrates (rather than building and sustaining living cells).[^cite_note-faunce-182] However, prominent research faces hurdles, Sun Catalytix a MIT spin-off stopped scaling up their prototype fuel-cell in 2012 because it offers few savings over other ways to make hydrogen from sunlight.[^cite_note-183]

Recent research emphasizes that while artificial photosynthesis shows promise in splitting water to generate hydrogen, its broader significance lies in the ability to produce dense, carbon-based solar fuels suitable for transport applications, such as aviation and long-haul shipping. These fuels, if derived from carbon dioxide and water using sunlight, could close the carbon loop and reduce reliance on fossil-based hydrocarbons. However, realizing this potential requires overcoming major technical hurdles, including the development of efficient, durable catalysts for water oxidation and CO<sub>2</sub> reduction, and careful attention to land use and public perception.[^cite_note-184]

## Technical potential

Global energy consumption in 2019 was approximately 65 petawatt-hours (PWh).[^cite_note-185] The technical potentials for utility-scale solar photovoltaic, concentrated solar power, onshore wind, and offshore wind each exceed 100 PWh/year, and thus each of them is capable of meeting the total demand in theory.[^cite_note-Angliviel_de_La_Beaumelle_Blok_2023_Global_Technical-186]

Solar PV has a technical potential of around 5,800 PWh per year; almost 100 as much as is used annually.[^cite_note-187] The technical potential of wind energy (onshore + offshore) is nearly 900 PWh per year.[^cite_note-188] The theoretical potential of hydropower is about 52 PWh per year.[^cite_note-189] Conventional geothermal could be above 10 PWh/year,[^cite_note-Angliviel_de_La_Beaumelle_Blok_2023_Global_Technical-186] enhanced geothermal systems could be about 4,000 PWh.[^cite_note-190]

The literature assessing the global economic potential of renewables shows that the economic potential is higher than current and near-future electricity demand.[^cite_note-Angliviel_de_La_Beaumelle_Blok_2023_Global_Technical-186] More specifically: Around 60% of the world's solar resource and 15% of its wind resource is already economically competitive compared with local fossil fuel generation.[^cite_note-191]

## Market and industry trends

Main article: [Renewable energy commercialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_commercialization)



Production of technology such as renewable energy sources starts a [positive feedback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positive_feedback) loop to form what has been called a *virtuous* cycle—the opposite of a [vicious cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicious_circle). For example, as more and more solar modules are deployed, prices fall because of the [economies of scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economies_of_scale), allowing the technology to become cost-competitive in new applications that in turn increase demand for more deployment.

[^cite_note-OWID_TechGrowthCycle-192]



Though the share of electricity generation from renewables has increased since 2010, there is substantial variance among major world regions.

[^cite_note-EmberStatisticalReviewFor2025-193]



In 2025, growth in solar, wind and other low-carbon electric power exceeded the overall growth in demand for electricity, reducing reliance on fossil fuels and helping to curb [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md).

[^cite_note-EmberElectricityReview_re2025-194]

Most new renewables are solar, followed by wind then hydro then bioenergy.[^cite_note-195] Investment in renewables, especially solar, tends to be more effective in creating jobs than coal, gas or oil.[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197] Worldwide, renewables employ about 12 million people as of 2020, with solar PV being the technology employing the most at almost 4 million.[^cite_note-198] However, as of February 2024, the world's supply of workforce for solar energy is lagging greatly behind demand as universities worldwide still produce more workforce for fossil fuels than for renewable energy industries.[^cite_note-199]

In 2021, China accounted for almost half of the global increase in renewable electricity.[^cite_note-200] There were 3,146 gigawatts installed in 135 countries, while 156 countries have laws regulating the renewable energy sector.[^cite_note-\:8-9][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEREN21_Renewables_Global_Status_Report2021-201]

The [International Renewable Energy Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Renewable_Energy_Agency) reported that during 2025 renewables accounted for 85.6% of new electricity generation capacity globally, with [solar photovoltaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_photovoltaics) providing nearly three-quarters of the increase. At the end of 2025, renewables constituted 49.4% of global installed electricity generating capacity.[^cite_note-thereg-20260401-202][^cite_note-irena-202603-203]

Globally in 2020 there are over 10 million jobs associated with the renewable energy industries, with solar photovoltaics being the largest renewable employer.[^cite_note-204] The clean energy sectors added about 4.7 million jobs globally between 2019 and 2022, totaling 35 million jobs by 2022.[^cite_note-205]<sup>: 5 </sup>

### Usage by sector or application

Some studies say that a global transition to [100% renewable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100%25_renewable_energy) across all sectors – power, heat, transport and industry – is feasible and economically viable.[^cite_note-Bogdanov-2021-206][^cite_note-Teske-2019-207][^cite_note-208]

One of the efforts to decarbonize transportation is the increased use of [electric vehicles](279350.md) (EVs).[^cite_note-wg3-209] Despite that and the use of [biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel), such as [biojet](17903761.md), less than 4% of transport energy is from renewables.[^cite_note-Ren21b-210] Occasionally [hydrogen fuel cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_fuel_cells) are used for heavy transport.[^cite_note-211] Meanwhile, in the future [electrofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrofuels) may also play a greater role in decarbonizing hard-to-abate sectors like aviation and maritime shipping.[^cite_note-212]

[Solar water heating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_water_heating) makes an important contribution to [renewable heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_heat) in many countries, most notably in China, which now has 70% of the global total (180 GWth). Most of these systems are installed on multi-family apartment buildings[^cite_note-213] and meet a portion of the hot water needs of an estimated 50–60 million households in China. Worldwide, total installed solar water heating systems meet a portion of the water heating needs of over 70 million households.

[Heat pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_pumps) provide both heating and cooling, and also flatten the electric demand curve and are thus an increasing priority.[^cite_note-energy.gov-214] [Renewable thermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_thermal_energy) is also growing rapidly.[^cite_note-215] About 10% of heating and cooling energy is from renewables.[^cite_note-Ren21a-216]

### Demand

In July 2014, the [WWF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Wide_Fund_for_Nature) and the [World Resources Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Resources_Institute) convened a discussion among a number of major US companies who had declared their intention to increase their use of renewable energy. These discussions identified a number of "principles" which companies seeking greater access to renewable energy considered important market deliverables. These principles included choice (between suppliers and between products), cost competitiveness, longer term fixed price supplies, access to third-party financing vehicles, and collaboration.[^cite_note-247]

UK statistics released in September 2020 noted that "the proportion of demand met from renewables varies from a low of 3.4 per cent (for transport, mainly from biofuels) to highs of over 20 per cent for 'other final users', which is largely the service and commercial sectors that consume relatively large quantities of electricity, and industry".[^cite_note-248]

In some locations, individual households can opt to purchase renewable energy through a [consumer green energy program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_green_energy_program).

### Developing countries

This section is an excerpt from [Renewable energy in developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_developing_countries).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Renewable_energy_in_developing_countries\&action=edit)]

[Renewable energy in developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_in_developing_countries) is an increasingly used alternative to [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) energy, as these countries scale up their energy supplies and address [energy poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_poverty). Renewable energy technology was once seen as unaffordable for developing countries.[^cite_note-249] However, since 2015, investment in non-hydro renewable energy has been higher in [developing countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_countries) than in developed countries, and comprised 54% of global renewable energy investment in 2019.[^cite_note-250] The [International Energy Agency](285292.md) forecasts that renewable energy will provide the majority of energy supply growth through 2030 in Africa and Central and South America, and 42% of supply growth in China.[^cite_note-Renewable_energy_in_developing_countries_\:1-251]

Most developing countries have abundant renewable energy resources, including [solar energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_energy), [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power), [geothermal energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy), and [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass), as well as the ability to manufacture the relatively labor-intensive systems that harness these. By developing such energy sources developing countries can reduce their dependence on oil and natural gas, creating energy portfolios that are less vulnerable to price rises. In many circumstances, these investments can be less expensive than fossil fuel energy systems.[^cite_note-Renewable_energy_in_developing_countries_mdg-252]

In Kenya, the [Olkaria V Geothermal Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olkaria_V_Geothermal_Power_Station) is one of the largest in the world.[^cite_note-253] The [Grand Ethiopia Renaissance Dam project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ethiopian_Renaissance_Dam) incorporates wind turbines.[^cite_note-254] Once completed, Morocco's [Ouarzazate Solar Power Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouarzazate_Solar_Power_Station) is projected to provide power to over a million people.[^cite_note-255]

## Policy



Deaths caused as a result of [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) use (areas of rectangles in chart) greatly exceed those resulting from production of renewable energy (rectangles barely visible in chart).

[^cite_note-OWID_SafestEnergy_2021-256]

Policies to support renewable energy have been vital in their expansion. Where Europe dominated in establishing [energy policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy) in the early 2000s, most countries around the world now have some form of energy policy.[^cite_note-IEA-2019-257]

The [International Renewable Energy Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Renewable_Energy_Agency) (IRENA) is an [intergovernmental organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intergovernmental_organization) for promoting the adoption of renewable energy worldwide. It aims to provide concrete policy advice and facilitate [capacity building](3045799.md) and technology transfer. IRENA was formed in 2009, with 75 countries signing the charter of IRENA.[^cite_note-258] As of April 2019, IRENA has 160 member states.[^cite_note-259] The then United Nations Secretary-General [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon) has said that renewable energy can lift the poorest nations to new levels of prosperity.[^cite_note-renewableenergyworld1-260]

The 2015 [Paris Agreement](30242372.md) on climate change motivated many countries to develop or improve renewable energy policies.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEREN21_Renewables_Global_Futures_Report2017-261] In 2017, a total of 121 countries adopted some form of renewable energy policy.[^cite_note-IEA-2019-257] National targets that year existed in 176 countries.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEREN21_Renewables_Global_Futures_Report2017-261] In addition, there is also a wide range of policies at the state/provincial, and local levels.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEREN21_Renewables_Global_Status_Report201113–14-134] Some [public utilities](171136.md) help plan or install [residential energy upgrades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_energy_upgrades_from_public_utilities).

Many national, state and local governments have created [green banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_bank_\(financial_institution\)). A green bank is a quasi-public financial institution that uses public capital to leverage private investment in clean energy technologies.[^cite_note-262] Green banks use a variety of financial tools to bridge market gaps that hinder the deployment of clean energy.

Global and national policies related to renewable energy can be divided based on sectors, such as agriculture, transport, buildings, industry:

*Climate neutrality* ([net zero emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_zero_emissions)) by the year 2050 is the main goal of the [European Green Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Deal).[^cite_note-263] For the European Union to reach their target of climate neutrality, one goal is to decarbonise its energy system by aiming to achieve "net-zero [greenhouse gas emissions](9528025.md) by 2050."[^cite_note-264]

Between 2013 and 2022, the renewable energy sector underwent a significant realignment of investment priorities. Investment in solar and wind energy technologies markedly increased. In contrast, other renewable technologies such as hydropower (including [pumped storage hydropower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumped-storage_hydropower)), [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass_\(energy\)), [biofuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biofuel), [geothermal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy), and [marine energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_energy) experienced a substantial decrease in financial investment. Notably, from 2017 to 2022, investment in these alternative renewable technologies declined by 45%, falling from US\$35 billion to US\$17 billion.[^cite_note-\:1-70]

In 2023, the renewable energy sector experienced a significant surge in investments, particularly in solar and wind technologies, totaling approximately US\$200 billion—a 75% increase from the previous year. The increased investments in 2023 contributed between 1% and 4% to the GDP in key regions including the United States, China, the European Union, and India.[^cite_note-267]

The energy sector receives investments of approximately US\$3 trillion each year, with US\$1.9 trillion directed towards [clean energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy) technologies and infrastructure. To meet the targets set in the [Net Zero Emissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_zero_emissions) (NZE) Scenario by 2035, this investment must increase to US\$5.3 trillion per year.[^cite_note-268]<sup>: 15 </sup>

## Debates

Main articles: [Renewable energy debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_debate), [Green job](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_job), and [Intermittent power source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermittent_power_source)

### Nuclear power proposed as renewable energy



The [Leibstadt Nuclear Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibstadt_Nuclear_Power_Plant) in Switzerland

This section is an excerpt from [Nuclear power as sustainable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_as_sustainable_energy).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Nuclear_power_as_sustainable_energy\&action=edit)]

Whether [nuclear power](22153.md) should be considered a form of [sustainable energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_energy) or renewable energy is an ongoing subject of debate. Legal definitions of renewable energy usually exclude nuclear energy technologies, with the notable exception of the U.S. state of [Utah](31716.md).[^cite_note-Nuclear_power_as_sustainable_energy_Utah_House_Bill_430,_Session_198-269] Dictionary-sourced definitions of renewable [energy technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_technologies) often omit or explicitly exclude mention of nuclear energy sources, with an exception made for the natural nuclear [decay heat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decay_heat) generated [within the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geothermal_energy).[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-Nuclear_power_as_sustainable_energy_renewalt_basics-271]

The most common fuel used in conventional [nuclear fission power stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_station), [uranium-235](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranium-235) is "non-renewable" according to the United States' [Energy Information Administration](1704673.md), the organization, however, is silent on the recycled [MOX fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MOX_fuel).[^cite_note-Nuclear_power_as_sustainable_energy_renewalt_basics-271] The [National Renewable Energy Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Renewable_Energy_Laboratory) does not mention nuclear power in its "energy basics" definition.[^cite_note-272]

In 1987, the [Brundtland Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brundtland_Commission) (WCED) classified fission reactors that produce more [fissile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fissile) [nuclear fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fuel) than they consume ([breeder reactors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breeder_reactor), and if developed, [fusion power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_power)) among conventional [renewable energy sources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renewable_energy_sources), such as [solar power](13690575.md) and [hydropower](14073.md).[^cite_note-Nuclear_power_as_sustainable_energy_Brundtland-273] The monitoring and storage of [radioactive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive) waste products is also required upon the use of other renewable energy sources, such as geothermal energy.[^cite_note-274]

### Geopolitics

See also: [Electrostate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrostate)

Further information: [Russia in the European energy sector](24648652.md)



A concept of a [super grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_grid)

The [geopolitical](163225.md) impact of the growing use of renewable energy is a subject of ongoing debate and research.[^cite_note-275] Many countries wealthy in oil, such as [Qatar](8238258.md), [Russia](25391.md), [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and [Norway](21241.md), are able to exert diplomatic or geopolitical influence as a result of their oil. Most of these countries are expected to be among the geopolitical "losers" of the energy transition, although some, like Norway, are also significant producers and exporters of renewable energy. Fossil fuels and the infrastructure to extract them may, in the long term, become [stranded assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranded_asset).[^cite_note-276] It has been speculated that countries dependent on fossil fuel revenue may one day find it in their interests to quickly [sell off](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_sale) their remaining fossil fuels.[^cite_note-277]

Conversely, nations abundant in renewable resources, and the minerals required for renewables technology, are expected to gain influence.[^cite_note-278][^cite_note-279] In particular, [China](5405.md) has become the world's dominant manufacturer of the technology needed to produce or store renewable energy, especially [solar panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panel), [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine), and [lithium-ion batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery).[^cite_note-280] Nations rich in solar and wind energy could become major energy exporters.[^cite_note-cb1-281] Some may produce and export [green hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_hydrogen),[^cite_note-282][^cite_note-cb1-281] although electricity is projected to be the dominant [energy carrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_carrier) in 2050, accounting for almost 50% of total energy consumption (up from 22% in 2015).[^cite_note-283] Countries with large uninhabited areas such as Australia, China, and many African and Middle Eastern countries have a potential for huge installations of renewable energy. The production of renewable energy technologies requires [rare-earth elements](145440.md) with new supply chains.[^cite_note-284]

Countries in Africa with already weak governments that rely on fossil fuel revenue may face even higher political instability or popular unrest. Analysts consider [Nigeria](21383.md), [Angola](701.md), [Chad](5488.md), [Gabon](12027.md), and [Sudan](27421.md), all countries with a history of [military coups](25914154.md), to be at risk of instability due to dwindling oil income.[^cite_note-285]

A study found that transition from fossil fuels to renewable energy systems reduces risks from mining, trade and political dependence because renewable energy systems don't need fuel – they depend on trade only for the acquisition of materials and components during construction.[^cite_note-286]

In October 2021, European Commissioner for Climate Action [Frans Timmermans](9524458.md) suggested "the best answer" to the [2021 global energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_global_energy_crisis) is "to reduce our reliance on fossil fuels."[^cite_note-Timmermans-287] He said those blaming the [European Green Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Deal) were doing so "for perhaps ideological reasons or sometimes economic reasons in protecting their vested interests."[^cite_note-Timmermans-287] Some critics blamed the [European Union Emissions Trading System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Emissions_Trading_System) (EU ETS) and [closure of nuclear plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_power_phase-out) for contributing to the energy crisis.[^cite_note-288][^cite_note-289][^cite_note-290] European Commission President [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) said that Europe is "too reliant" on [natural gas](22131.md) and too [dependent on natural gas imports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_natural_gas_imports). According to Von der Leyen, "The answer has to do with diversifying our suppliers ... and, crucially, with speeding up the transition to clean energy."[^cite_note-291]

Energy transition can also improve a country's [energy security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_security) and [energy independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_independence), mitigating impacts from fossil fuel markets or geopolitical pressure from oil producers.[^cite_note-292][^cite_note-293] The proliferation of renewable energy could also weaken petrostates' trading power and their global influence. As the world becomes more electrified, renewable technologies will be embedded into the national economies of various markets, especially developing countries, removing the leverage petrostates have on the global energy structure.[^cite_note-294][^cite_note-295][^cite_note-296]

### Metal and mineral extraction

See also: [Environmental footprint of electric cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_footprint_of_electric_cars) and [Rare-earth element § Environmental considerations](145440.md#Environmental_considerations)

The [transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_transition) to renewable energy requires increased extraction of certain metals and minerals. Like all mining, this impacts the environment[^cite_note-297] and can lead to [environmental conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_conflict).[^cite_note-298] For example, lithium mining uses around 65% of the water in the [Salar de Atacama](9980356.md) desert forcing farmers and [llama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llama) herders to abandon their ancestral settlements and creating environment degradation,[^cite_note-299] in several African countries, the green energy transition has created a mining boom, causing deforestation, and threatening already endangered species.[^cite_note-300] Wind power requires large amounts of [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) and [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), as well as smaller amounts of the rarer metal [neodymium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neodymium). Solar power is less resource-intensive, but still requires significant amounts of aluminum. The expansion of electrical grids requires both copper and aluminum. Batteries, which are critical to enable storage of renewable energy, use large quantities of copper, [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel), aluminum and [graphite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphite). Demand for lithium is expected to grow 42-fold from 2020 to 2040. Demand for nickel, [cobalt](24580536.md) and graphite is expected to grow by a factor of about 20–25.[^cite_note-IEA_minerals-301] For each of the most relevant minerals and metals, its mining is dominated by a single country: [copper in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_mining_in_Chile), [nickel in Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel_mining_in_Indonesia), [rare earths in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rare_earth_industry_in_China), [cobalt in the Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining_in_the_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo), and [lithium in Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium_mining_in_Australia). China dominates [processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral_processing) of all of these.[^cite_note-IEA_minerals-301]

Recycling these metals after the devices they are embedded in are spent is essential to create a [circular economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_economy) and ensure renewable energy is sustainable. By 2040, recycled copper, lithium, cobalt, and nickel from spent batteries could reduce combined primary supply requirements for these minerals by around 10%.[^cite_note-IEA_minerals-301]

A controversial approach is [deep sea mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_sea_mining). Minerals can be collected from new sources like [polymetallic nodules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymetallic_nodules) lying on the [seabed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seabed).[^cite_note-302] This would damage local biodiversity,[^cite_note-303] but proponents point out that biomass on resource-rich seabeds is much scarcer than in the mining regions on land, which are often found in vulnerable habitats like rainforests.[^cite_note-304]

Due to co-occurrence of rare-earth and radioactive elements ([thorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorium), [uranium](31743.md) and [radium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radium)), rare-earth mining results in production of low-level [radioactive waste](37257.md).[^cite_note-305]

### Conservation areas

Installations used to produce wind, solar and hydropower are an increasing threat to key conservation areas, with facilities built in areas set aside for nature conservation and other environmentally sensitive areas. They are often much larger than fossil fuel power plants, needing areas of land up to 10 times greater than coal or gas to produce equivalent energy amounts.[^cite_note-306] More than 2000 renewable energy facilities are built, and more are under construction, in areas of environmental importance and threaten the habitats of plant and animal species across the globe. The authors' team emphasized that their work should not be interpreted as anti-renewables because renewable energy is crucial for reducing carbon emissions. The key is ensuring that renewable energy facilities are built in places where they do not damage biodiversity.[^cite_note-307]

In 2020 scientists published a [world map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_map) of areas that contain renewable energy materials as well as estimations of their overlaps with "Key Biodiversity Areas", "Remaining Wilderness" and "[Protected Areas](100406.md)". The authors assessed that careful [strategic planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_planning) is needed.[^cite_note-308][^cite_note-309][^cite_note-310]

### Impact of climate change on renewable energy production

Climate change is making weather patterns less predictable. This can seriously hamper the use of renewable energy. For example, in the year 2023, in Sudan and Namibia, hydropower production dropped by more than half due to drastic reduction in rainfall, in China, India and some regions in Africa unusual weather phenomena reduced the amount of produced wind energy, heatwaves and clouds reduce the effectiveness of solar panels, melting glaciers create problems to hydropower. Nuclear energy is also affected as drought creates water shortage, so nuclear power plants sometimes do not have enough water for cooling.[^cite_note-311]

## History

See also: [History of climate change policy and politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_climate_change_policy_and_politics)

Prior to the development of coal in the mid 19th century, nearly all energy used was renewable. The oldest known use of renewable energy, in the form of traditional [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass_\(energy\)) to [fuel fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_of_fire_by_early_humans), dates from more than a million years ago. The use of biomass for fire did not become commonplace until many hundreds of thousands of years later.[^cite_note-Fire-332] The second oldest usage of renewable energy was probably harnessing the wind in order to drive ships over water. This practice can be traced back some 7000 years, to ships in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) and on the [Nile](21244.md).[^cite_note-333] Geothermal energy from [hot springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_spring) has been used for bathing since [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) times and space heating since ancient Roman times.[^cite_note-334] Moving into the time of recorded history, the primary sources of traditional renewable energy were [human labor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manual_labour), [animal power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_power), [water power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_power), wind, in grain crushing [windmills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windmill), and [firewood](481098.md), a traditional biomass.

In 1885, [Werner Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Siemens), commenting on the discovery of the [photovoltaic effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_effect) in the solid state, wrote:

> In conclusion, I would say that however great the scientific importance of this discovery may be, its practical value will be no less obvious when we reflect that the supply of solar energy is both without limit and without cost, and that it will continue to pour down upon us for countless ages after all the coal deposits of the earth have been exhausted and forgotten.[^cite_note-335]

[Max Weber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Weber) mentioned the end of fossil fuel in the concluding paragraphs of his [Die protestantische Ethik und der Geist des Kapitalismus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_protestantische_Ethik_und_der_Geist_des_Kapitalismus) (The Protestant Ethic and the Spirit of Capitalism), published in 1905.[^cite_note-336] Development of solar engines continued until the outbreak of World War I. The importance of solar energy was recognized in a 1911 *[Scientific American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_American)* article: "in the far distant future, [natural fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) having been exhausted \[solar power] will remain as the only means of existence of the human race".[^cite_note-337]

The theory of [peak oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_oil) was published in 1956.[^cite_note-338] In the 1970s environmentalists promoted the development of renewable energy both as a replacement for the eventual [depletion of oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Depletion_of_oil), as well as for an escape from dependence on oil, and the first electricity-generating [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine) appeared. Solar had long been used for heating and cooling, but solar panels were too costly to build solar farms until 1980.[^cite_note-339]

New government spending, regulation and policies helped the renewables industry weather the [2008 financial crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_financial_crisis) and the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession) better than many other sectors.[^cite_note-obama-340] In 2022, renewables accounted for 30% of global electricity generation, up from 21% in 1985.[^cite_note-\:3-341]
