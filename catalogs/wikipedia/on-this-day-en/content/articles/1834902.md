**Estadio Azteca** (Latin American Spanish: [\[esˈtaðjo asˈteka\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)), officially known as **Estadio Banorte** for sponsorship reasons, is a [football](10568.md) stadium located in [Coyoacán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coyoac%C3%A1n), Mexico City.[^cite_note-10] At an elevation of 2,241 meters (7,352 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Height_above_mean_sea_level)[^cite_note-googleearth-1] it is the official home of [Club América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Am%C3%A9rica) of [Liga MX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_MX) and the [Mexico national team](808402.md). With a capacity of 87,523, it is the largest stadium in Latin America and the [eighth-largest association football stadium in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_association_football_stadiums_by_capacity).

One of the best known football stadiums in the world,[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] often called the "Temple of Football" or the "Cathedral of Football",[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] it was the first to host two [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) finals;[^cite_note-19] [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_squads#Brazil) defeated [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_squads#Italy) 4–1 in [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_final) and [Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_squads#Argentina) beat [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_squads#West_Germany) 3–2 in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_final). It also hosted the [1986 quarter-final match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_v_England_\(1986_FIFA_World_Cup\)) between Argentina and [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_squads#England) in which [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) scored both the "[Hand of God goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_hand_of_God)" and the "[Goal of the Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_2%E2%80%931_England_\(1986_FIFA_World_Cup\)#The_Goal_of_the_Century)".[^cite_note-20]

Both [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9)'s (1970) and Diego Maradona's (1986) national teams won the FIFA World Cup in Estadio Azteca. It also hosted the "[Game of the Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Game_of_the_Century_\(football\))", when Italy defeated West Germany 4–3 in extra time in one of the 1970 semifinal matches. The stadium was the principal venue for the [football tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1968_Summer_Olympics) of the [1968 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Summer_Olympics)[^cite_note-21] and the [1971 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Women's_World_Cup).[^cite_note-22] It also hosted games during the [2026 FIFA World Cup](83423432.md), including the opening game where [South Africa](1018598.md) played against Mexico, making it the only stadium to host three editions of the FIFA World Cup.[^cite_note-23] Additionally, the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL) of the United States features one game of [American football](18951490.md) at the stadium per season as a part of its [International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_International_Series).&#x20;

## History



Spectators outside the Azteca during the 1986 FIFA World Cup

Estadio Azteca was envisioned as a major sports venue during the presidency of [Adolfo López Mateos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolfo_L%C3%B3pez_Mateos); Mexico was awarded the [1968 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Summer_Olympics) in 1963, and the [football final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_at_the_1968_Summer_Olympics) was held there. Designed by architects [Pedro Ramírez Vázquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Ram%C3%ADrez_V%C3%A1zquez) and [Rafael Mijares Alcérreca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Mijares_Alc%C3%A9rreca), the stadium broke ground in 1961 and the inaugural match was between [Club América](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Am%C3%A9rica) and [Torino F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino_F.C.) on 29 May 1966, with a capacity for 107,494 spectators. The first goal was scored by Brazilian [Arlindo Dos Santos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arlindo_dos_Santos_Cruz) and the second one by Brazilian [José Alves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zague_\(footballer,_born_1934\)); later, the Italians tied the game, which ended in a 2–2 draw. Mexican president [Gustavo Díaz Ordaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavo_D%C3%ADaz_Ordaz) made the initial kick and [FIFA](11049.md) president [Sir Stanley Rous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_Stanley_Rous) was the witness.

A modern illumination system was inaugurated on 5 June 1966, with the first night game played between Spanish side [Valencia C.F.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_C.F.) and [Necaxa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Necaxa). The first goal of the match was scored by [Honduran](13394.md) [José Cardona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Cardona) for Valencia. Roberto Martínez, aka Caña Brava, became the first Mexican to score a goal in the stadium after scoring for Necaxa. The result was a 3–1 victory for Valencia. In 1978, the stadium hosted the final of the [Copa Interamericana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Interamericana) between América and [Boca Juniors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boca_Juniors) of [Argentina](18951905.md), and again in 1990 between América and [Club Olimpia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Olimpia) of [Paraguay](23420.md).



Interior of the stadium prior to a match between Club América and Tijuana, 25 September 2011

The venue is where superstars [Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9) ([1970](157227.md)) and [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) ([1986](157231.md)) lifted the [FIFA World Cup Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_Trophy) for the last time. Estadio Azteca has also been used for musical performances throughout its history. [Michael Jackson](14995351.md) (5 sold-out shows in 1993),[^cite_note-24] [Menudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menudo_\(group\)) (in 1983), [U2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2) (in 2006 and 2011), [Luis Miguel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Miguel) (in 2002), [Paul McCartney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCartney) (in 2012 and 2017), [Elton John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elton_John), [Maná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man%C3%A1), [Juan Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Gabriel), [Gloria Estefan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gloria_Estefan), [Jaguares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguares_\(band\)), [Lenny Kravitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenny_Kravitz), [\*Nsync](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/*Nsync), [Hanson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanson_\(band\)), [Ana Gabriel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Gabriel), and [The Three Tenors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Three_Tenors) all have become part of the stadium's main spectacle. The stadium has also been used for political events, including Mexican president [Felipe Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Calder%C3%B3n)'s campaign closure in 2006, as well as religious events, such as Jehovah's Witnesses conventions and the appearance of Pope [John Paul II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Paul_II) in 1999.[^cite_note-25]

In April 2017, it was announced that starting July 2018, [Cruz Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruz_Azul) would relocate to the Azteca on a temporary basis, due to the impending demolition of the [Estadio Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Azul).[^cite_note-ca2018-26] The stadium hosted multiple matches during the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), including the opener. It was the third World Cup for Azteca, which hosted the final match in both [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_final) and [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_final).[^cite_note-27]

### 2015–19 renovation project

The stadium has undergone gradual improvements and renovations, including the replacing of seating within the stadium as well as the installation of electronic advertising boards. In May 2015, modern [Panasonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panasonic) LED panels were installed at the north and south ends of the stadium, replacing the phosphorus panels installed in 1998.[^cite_note-28]



Bench area and side stand

In February 2015, a vast renovation plan was unveiled with the intention that the completion of the project coincide with the stadium's 50th anniversary and with Club América's [centenary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centenary) in 2016, as well as the construction of a commercial hub outside the stadium to be completed some time in 2019. It was reported that [Televisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televisa), owners of the stadium, approved a joint-venture bid from private development firms IQ Real Estate and Alhel. The hub, named "Foro Azteca", would reportedly consist of a mall, office spaces, two hotels, new leisure spaces, and parking spaces for 2,500 cars.[^cite_note-capacity2-9] The planned building of the hub was ultimately abandoned. The renovations to the stadium were planned in two phases: the first saw the demolition of the restaurant and seating at the lower east stand and the construction of a new hospitality area with dining and banqueting spaces, and the second saw the construction of new media boxes and private skyboxes at the upper west stand.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] The renovations to the stadium were completed in November 2016, with the seating capacity ultimately reduced to 81,070.[^cite_note-31]

### 2026 World Cup and renovation works



The stadium was renamed from Azteca (logo above) to Banorte during the renovations

Mexico City was formally announced as a host city for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) in June 2020 when [FIFA](11049.md) announced the host cities for the tournament. Mexico City was one of three host cities in Mexico and one of sixteen host cities overall for the tournament which was staged across the United States, Canada and Mexico.[^cite_note-32] On 4 February 2024, it was revealed that the stadium would host the opening match of both [Mexico](808402.md) and the tournament on 11 June 2026. In total, the stadium hosted five matches: three group stage matches, one Round of 32 match, and one Round of 16 match, hosting four of Mexico's games as winners of Group A.[^cite_note-33]

A major renovation for the tournament, delayed several times due to financing and access issues, began with the stadium's closure in May 2024. The seating capacity was increased from 83,000 to 87,500 spectators, new video screens were installed, and a hybrid turf pitch was laid.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-ElPais-35] The renovation plan included refurbishing the stadium facade, the installation of LED lighting, new locker rooms under the suites, and a tunnel for the players. High-resolution LED screens were to be installed throughout the stadium, and the seats in the stands would be replaced.[^cite_note-36] Special lounges in the lower part of the stadium were also removed to provide more space in the stands.[^cite_note-stadiumworks1-37]

The stadium was planned to be structurally reinforced by building two large areas on the sides that will serve as bars. The roof was planned to be renovated, which has reportedly not been repaired for a long time and suffers from leaking.[^cite_note-stadiumworks1-37] In addition to renovations of the stadium itself, works to the neighborhood were also proposed, however those plans were scaled down after residents of the surrounding area complained about the project.[^cite_note-38] In March 2025, FIFA was accused by [Building and Wood Workers' International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_and_Wood_Workers'_International) labor union of not allowing their members to inspect safety regulations taking place during the renovations.[^cite_note-39]

Estadio Azteca reopened on 28 March 2026 with a friendly between Mexico and [Portugal](576328.md) that ended in a scoreless draw. A spectator died during the pre-match ceremonies after an apparent fall from a box suite.[^cite_note-40] The renovated stadium was criticized by *[The Athletic](54936152.md)* for uneven concrete work, unfinished furnishings, and a chaotic security queue.[^cite_note-41] In May 2026, the stadium was monitored by [NASA](18426568.md) amid concerns about the structural stability of the stadium.[^cite_note-42]







The stadium on 11 June 2026, prior to the opening match of the 2026 FIFA World Cup and inside the stadium during the opening ceremony

On 11 June 2026, the opening match of the 2026 FIFA World Cup was held at the Azteca, in which [Mexico](808402.md) played against [South Africa](1018598.md). The first goal was scored by Mexico's [Julián Quiñones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juli%C3%A1n_Qui%C3%B1ones) in the ninth minute of the match,[^cite_note-43] this being the first time a player from a [CONCACAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF) national team scored the first goal of a FIFA World Cup edition. [^cite_note-quinones_concacaf_first_goal_2026-44] The match ended with Mexico winning by 2–0.&#x20;

That same day, an opening ceremony was held before the match, which included performances by [Maná](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Man%C3%A1), playing "[Oye Mi Amor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oye_Mi_Amor)" with indigenous dancers surrounding the stage. Afterwards, [Danny Ocean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Ocean_\(singer\)) performed "[Partidazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partidazo?action=edit\&redlink=1)" surrounded by female dancers dressed in [china poblana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_poblana) dresses. This was followed by [Los Ángeles Azules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_%C3%81ngeles_Azules) and [Belinda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belinda) performing "[Por Ella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Por_Ella?action=edit\&redlink=1)" surrounded by male dancers dressed in charro outfits and performing a stylized version of the jarabe with the female dancers, followed by [J Balvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J_Balvin) and [Ryan Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Castro) performing a medley of songs including "[Qué Calor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qu%C3%A9_Calor)", "Una a la Vez", and "[I Like It](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I_Like_It)", surrounded by dancers in suits matching the color scheme of the tournament. [Shakira](6479315.md) and [Burna Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burna_Boy) closed the ceremony with a performance of the tournament's official song "[Dai Dai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_Dai)" followed by flairs in the colors of the Mexican flag and a fireworks display. ## Name



Estadio Azteca prior to a kickoff

The name "Azteca" is a tribute to the [Aztec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec) heritage of Mexico City. The stadium is currently owned by Mexican multimedia conglomerate [Televisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televisa). In January 1997, Televisa officially changed the stadium's name to **Estadio Guillermo Cañedo**, in tribute to [Guillermo Cañedo de la Bárcena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Ca%C3%B1edo_de_la_B%C3%A1rcena), a top network executive, former [Mexican Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Football_Federation) president, and a prominent member of the [FIFA](11049.md) executive committee who had died earlier that month.[^cite_note-47] After the change proved to be unpopular with the public,[^cite_note-48] Televisa returned to referring to it solely as Estadio Azteca. The stadium is also referred to by the nickname *"Coloso de Santa Úrsula"* ("Colossus of Santa Ursula"), due to its large structure and [Santa Úrsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_%C3%9Arsula,_Mexico_City) referring to the suburb where the stadium is located.[^cite_note-49]

On 14 March 2025, [Banorte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banorte) bought the [naming rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_rights) to the stadium, which helped fund the renovations for the 2026 FIFA World Cup, and renamed it Estadio Banorte.[^cite_note-50] The name was immediately met with overwhelming negative reception.[^cite_note-51] During the 2026 FIFA World Cup, the stadium was called "**Mexico City Stadium**" (*Estadio Ciudad de México*) in accordance with FIFA's policy on corporate sponsored names.[^cite_note-52]

## Access, transportation, and entrance

It is served by [Estadio Azteca station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Azteca_light_rail_station) on the [Xochimilco Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xochimilco_Light_Rail) line. This line is an extension of the [Mexico City Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_City_Metro) system which begins at [Tasqueña light rail station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasque%C3%B1a_light_rail_station) station—connecting with the metro at the [Tasqueña metro station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasque%C3%B1a_metro_station)—and ends in the [Xochimilco light rail station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xochimilco_light_rail_station).[^cite_note-53] A [bikeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bikeway), named [Ciclovía La Gran Tenochtitlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciclov%C3%ADa_La_Gran_Tenochtitl%C3%A1n), services the area.[^cite_note-54] A nearby [transportation hub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_hub), [CETRAM Huipulco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centro_de_transferencia_modal) ([es](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/CETRAM%20Huipulco)), offers local city bus routes.[^cite_note-55] Tickets are available up until kick-off times from the ticket office which is located at the front of the stadium, located towards the exit ramps from the Azteca station. Prices start from as little as [MXN\$](147253.md)100 (about US\$5 as of 2016), and could cost up to MXN\$500 (about US\$26 as of 2016) for more high-profile matches.[^cite_note-56]

## Notable events

### FIFA World Cups







[Pelé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pel%C3%A9) in 1970 (top) and [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) in 1986 (middle), celebrate winning the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) at the stadium, and the opening match of the 2026 FIFA World Cup (bottom)

&#x20;\
&#x20;Estadio Azteca has hosted the FIFA World Cup on three occasions, hosting a total of 24 FIFA World Cup matches overall. The stadium hosted ten matches during the [1970 FIFA World Cup](157227.md) including the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_FIFA_World_Cup_final). Sixteen years later the stadium hosted nine matches during the [1986 FIFA World Cup](157231.md), including the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FIFA_World_Cup_final) which was the second [FIFA World Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup_final) to be played at the stadium. Estadio Azteca hosted five matches during the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) (including the [opening ceremony](83423432.md)) which made the stadium the only one to have hosted the tournament on three occasions.

### American football

See also: [American Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bowl), [Fútbol Americano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%BAtbol_Americano), and [NFL International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_International_Series)

- On 15 August 1994, Estadio Azteca hosted a preseason [American Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bowl) game between the [Houston Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Houston_Oilers_season) and [Dallas Cowboys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Dallas_Cowboys_season) which still holds the record for the highest attendance at any [NFL](21211.md) game, with 112,376 in attendance.

  [^cite_note-NFLAttendanceRecord-59]

  &#x20;The Houston Oilers won the game 6–0.

- On 2 October 2005, the first international regular-season game in the history of the NFL was played in the stadium between the [San Francisco 49ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_San_Francisco_49ers_season) and the [Arizona Cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Arizona_Cardinals_season). The game was a 31–14 victory for the Cardinals. It set the record of the largest crowd to attend a regular-season NFL game with 103,467, but this record would be broken in 2009.

  [^cite_note-NFLAttendanceRecord-59]

- On 21 November 2016, the [Oakland Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Oakland_Raiders_season) hosted a home game along with the [Houston Texans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Houston_Texans_season) as part of the [NFL International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_International_Series) in the first game dubbed **NFL Mexico Game**. It was the first *[Monday Night Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monday_Night_Football)* game played outside the United States.

  [^cite_note-60]

  &#x20;The game saw a sell-out crowd of 76,743 in a renovated Estadio Azteca.

  [^cite_note-61]

- On 19 November 2017, the [Raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Oakland_Raiders_season) hosted the [Patriots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_New_England_Patriots_season) at the stadium.

  [^cite_note-shook20171119-62]

- The [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs) were scheduled to play the [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) at the stadium on [19 November 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Kansas_City_Chiefs%E2%80%93Los_Angeles_Rams_game).

  [^cite_note-63]

  &#x20;However, due to poor field conditions brought on by recent events, as well as rain, the NFL cancelled the event and moved it to the Rams' home stadium at the time, [Los Angeles Memorial Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Memorial_Coliseum).

  [^cite_note-64]

  &#x20;The Rams won the relocated game 54–51.

- The [Kansas City Chiefs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City_Chiefs) played the [Los Angeles Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Chargers) on 18 November 2019, with the Chargers designated as the "home" team.
