**Brandon Joseph Herrera** (born November 20, 1995), also known as **The AK Guy**, is an American [YouTuber](57313961.md), firearms manufacturer, and political candidate. He gained prominence through his YouTube channel, launched in 2014, which features firearms testing, historical weapon demonstrations, and commentary on gun policy and politics. Herrera is a member of the [Republican Party](32070.md).&#x20;

In [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Texas), Herrera ran to represent [Texas's 23rd congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas's_23rd_congressional_district) in the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md), challenging incumbent [Tony Gonzales](65756758.md) in the Republican primary. He lost by approximately 1 percentage point in a runoff. He is running again for the seat in [2026](78639082.md#District_23), forcing a runoff and becoming the nominee after Gonzales withdrew his candidacy.

## Early life

Brandon Herrera was raised in [Fayetteville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayetteville,_North_Carolina), North Carolina in a military family.[^cite_note-Butler-2026-1] He first became interested in firearms as a teenager in middle school.[^cite_note-Butler-2026-1] In 2008, at age 12, Herrera became a member of the [Sons of Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_Confederate_Veterans) chapter in [Fayetteville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayetteville,_North_Carolina).[^cite_note-2] Herrera attended [Campbell University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbell_University), where he studied [pre-law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-law), before dropping out to run his firearms manufacturing business.[^cite_note-expressnews-harris-3]

## Career

### YouTube

Started in 2014, Herrera's YouTube channel focuses on testing and reviewing firearms.[^cite_note-texas-tribune-choi-4] Subjects of his videos include tests of antique and military weapons, such as the [AK-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47) and [RPG-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RPG-7); discussions about gun laws and politics; and a series recreating the assassinations of public figures, including [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy), [Martin Luther King Jr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr) and [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln).[^cite_note-expressnews-harris-3] His videos often feature guests who have served in the military; Herrera himself is not a military veteran.[^cite_note-wapost-svitek-5] He moved to Texas in 2020.[^cite_note-Butler-2026-1]

On October 16, 2022, Herrera collaborated with [Kyle Rittenhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyle_Rittenhouse) in Rittenhouse's first ever YouTube video after Rittenhouse launched his own channel dedicated to guns and Second Amendment rights.[^cite_note-6]

In 2024, Herrera released a rifle he and his team engineered called the AK-50, an [AK-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47) pattern rifle chambered in [.50 BMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.50_BMG) in order to blend the durability and power of the two separately.[^cite_note-7] The gun was later added to the video game *[Escape from Tarkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escape_from_Tarkov)*.[^cite_note-8]

In the aftermath of the [Annunciation Catholic Church shooting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annunciation_Catholic_Church_shooting), an alleged [manifesto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manifesto) by the perpetrator referenced Herrera, stating the two had met at [SHOT Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHOT_Show).[^cite_note-9] Herrera harshly condemned the shooting and perpetrator, stating that he and his friends who attended SHOT show have "zero recollection of ever meeting this creep."[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] The [National Shooting Sports Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Shooting_Sports_Foundation), the organizers of SHOT show, released a statement in which they detailed there is no record or evidence the shooter ever attended any year of the event.[^cite_note-12]

### Politics

On March 19, 2024, Herrera spoke outside of the [Colorado State Capitol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_State_Capitol) to around 400 people and later testified inside to the [Colorado Legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Legislature) against a proposed [assault weapons ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_weapons_legislation_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

Herrera has described himself as [libertarian-leaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarianism_in_the_United_States), often emphasizing [gun rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_keep_and_bear_arms) and [limited government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_government), but has generally aligned himself with [right-wing populists](8354382.md) such as [Jim Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Jordan) and [Matt Gaetz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Gaetz). He also has appealed to [anti-establishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-establishment) voters in the primaries.[^cite_note-houston-chronicle-15][^cite_note-expressnews-harris-3]

In August 2023, Herrera, a [Republican](32070.md), announced he would run for the [U.S. House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._House) to represent [Texas's 23rd congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Texas's_23rd_congressional_district) in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Texas), citing a frustration in incumbent [Tony Gonzales](65756758.md)' votes and gun policy.[^cite_note-ksat-spriester-16] In the primary, Herrera received 25 percent of the vote, while Gonzales received 45 percent; as no candidate received more than 50 percent, a [runoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runoff_election) was held between the two, where Herrera lost by 354 votes.[^cite_note-wapost-svitek-5][^cite_note-ksat-spriester-16][^cite_note-17] Herrera called for a recount, saying, "I don't expect the results to change, but I feel I owe it to my volunteers, voters, and supporters to leave no stone unturned."[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

Several political action committees from outside of Texas donated millions of dollars to Gonzales' campaign against Herrera. The pro-[Israel](9282173.md) [lobbying group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobbying_in_the_United_States) [AIPAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIPAC) ran an ad campaign accusing Herrera of joking about veteran suicide and having "glorified Nazis and mocked the Holocaust" in his YouTube videos.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] A *[Politico](9172097.md)* article cited this ad campaign as an example of AIPAC strategically attacking certain candidates while supporting its endorsed ones, without mentioning Israel.[^cite_note-22] After the primary, [U.S. Term Limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Term_Limits), a [congressional term limits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Term_limits_in_the_United_States) advocacy group, tapped Herrera to be their Texas chair.[^cite_note-23] Herrera criticized AIPAC after it spent over US\$1 million against him, calling it "Israel first bullshit" and saying, "I'm anti Israel buying American elections."[^cite_note-Benson-2026-24]

#### 2026 congressional election

Main article: [2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Texas § District 23](78639082.md#District_23)

Herrera again announced his candidacy for the Republican nomination in Texas' 23rd Congressional district in 2026. In November 2025, Herrera announced his campaign had raised \$1.1 million dollars for the primary, with over 33,000 small dollar donors contributing alongside his own PACs.[^cite_note-25] He solicited campaign donations through his YouTube channel and ran raffles for collector's items.[^cite_note-Butler-2026-1] His campaign slogan was "[Let's Go Brandon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Let's_Go_Brandon)".[^cite_note-Butler-2026-1] On February 18, 2026, Herrera called on Gonzales to exit the race and resign over an [alleged affair with a former staffer](65756758.md#Affair_with_political_aide) who committed [suicide](16831059.md) in November after another former Gonzales staffer backed the rumors.[^cite_note-26]

During the March 3, 2026, primary, Herrera forced Gonzales into a second runoff, this time coming in first with 43% to Gonzales' 41%.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] Gonzales suspended his campaign two days after the primary, leaving Herrera the presumptive nominee.[^cite_note-29]

## Commentary and criticism

Herrera's content contains what he has described as "[dark humor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_humor)".[^cite_note-expressnews-harris-3] He co-hosts the podcast *Unsubscribe* (with three military veterans) that was described by the *[Houston Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Chronicle)* as displaying "offensive commentary or repeats racist stereotypes", such as an episode where he joked he was essentially a [military veteran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veteran) because he "often \[thought] about putting a gun in \[his] mouth".[^cite_note-houston-chronicle-15] [Tony Gonzales](65756758.md) criticized Herrera, saying that there was a "special place in hell for scum and villainy who mock veteran suicide".[^cite_note-texas-tribune-choi-4]

Herrera has posted several YouTube videos that have featured [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_Party) imagery and included jokes about Nazis and the [Holocaust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust).[^cite_note-Lapin-2026-30] He has boasted about his 1939 edition of *[Mein Kampf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mein_Kampf)*, while calling Adolf Hitler a "schizophrenic", whose ideas were "all over the fucking place", while also stating his ownership of the book doesn’t mean he agrees with the ideas it holds. [^cite_note-Benson-2026-24][^cite_note-Lapin-2026-30][^cite_note-\:1-31][^cite_note-\:2-32] In a 2020 video, Herrera played the songs "[Erika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erika_\(song\))" and "[Panzerlied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panzerlied)" (Tank Song), with other Third Reich-related songs in the background while covering firearms that were used by the Nazis; Herrera subsequently "liked" numerous comments praising his use of the Nazi marching songs in the video. In a 2022 video, he referred to the [MP 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MP_40), a submachine gun developed by Nazi Germany, as "Hitler's street sweeper" and the "original [ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_ghettos_in_Europe) blaster".[^cite_note-\:1-31] The video includes a montage of Herrera and an associate firing the weapon and [goose stepping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goose_step) to "Erika".[^cite_note-jewish-insider-33] Another video, covering historical weapons in the [Rhodesian Bush War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodesian_Bush_War), featured the [flag of Rhodesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Rhodesia), a symbol used by [white nationalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_nationalism).[^cite_note-34] Herrera responded to criticism, saying, "This should be obvious, but I am not, nor have I ever been a [neo-Nazi](54361.md)."[^cite_note-wapost-svitek-5] The [Republican Jewish Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Jewish_Coalition) stated its opposition to Herrera.[^cite_note-Benson-2026-24][^cite_note-Lapin-2026-30]

Herrera is a member of the [Sons of Confederate Veterans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_Confederate_Veterans), a [neo-Confederate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-Confederate) organization that promotes the [Lost Cause](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_Cause_of_the_Confederacy).[^cite_note-35] He appeared in welcome videos produced by the organization, where he referred to the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) as the "[War of Northern Aggression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_Northern_Aggression)" and the "War for Southern Independence", and advertised the group's "annual [Yankee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee) shoot", where members used antique rifles to shoot at "posters of \[their] favorite Yankees". Herrera's support for the Sons of Confederate Veterans organization was stated in the videos citing the need to preserve history, "because those who do not learn from history are doomed to repeat it."[^cite_note-jewish-insider-33][^cite_note-36]

Herrera has referred to children as "cum trophies" while criticising the political opinions of [Shannon Watts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannon_Watts).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] In 2023, he said:

> The fact that you kept your cum trophy doesn't make you any less wrong about the dumb shit that you say. Being a mother doesn't make your opinion any more or less valid. Just because you let somebody [creampie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creampie_\(sexual_act\)) you doesn't mean I have to give a shit about your political opinions.

When these comments were resurfaced in 2026 during Herrera's race for Congress, Herrera's campaign responded to a request for comment from an *[Austin American-Statesman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austin_American-Statesman)* journalist about the sexual references by sending her a picture of a [cream pie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cream_pie).[^cite_note-39]

Herrera described his 2023 video "recreating" the [assassination of Martin Luther King Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Martin_Luther_King_Jr.) as an attempt to combine "a pretty somber and serious topic" with "dark, edgy humor."[^cite_note-40] However, a reporter at [MeidasTouch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MeidasTouch) speculated that the attempt "may not sit well with voters," pointing out that "At the end of his video, Herrera shot the MLK target dummy at close proximity, execution style, laughed, then commented, 'this is so fucking wrong.'"[^cite_note-\:2-32]
