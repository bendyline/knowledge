The **2025** **Japan Series** (日本シリーズ, *Nippon Shirīzu*; branded as the **SMBC Nippon Series 2025** for sponsorship reasons) was the championship series of [Nippon Professional Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Professional_Baseball)'s (NPB) [2025 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Nippon_Professional_Baseball_season). The 76th edition of the [Japan Series](379669.md), it was a [best-of-seven playoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best-of-seven_playoff) series played between the [Central League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_League) (CL) [Climax Series champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Central_League_Climax_Series) [Hanshin Tigers](490998.md) and the [Pacific League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_League) (PL) [Climax Series champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Pacific_League_Climax_Series) [Fukuoka SoftBank Hawks](318122.md). The Hawks defeated the Tigers in five games.

SoftBank returned to the Japan Series for the second consecutive year after losing the [previous year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Japan_Series) to the CL's third-seeded team. This was their 22nd overall appearance, a new PL record. For Hanshin, this was their eighth Japan Series overall appearance, with their last coming in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Japan_Series), a series that they won to secure their first championship in 38 years. The Hawks won all three previous meetings between the two teams in the championship round, with the last time being [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Japan_Series). As per NPB tradition, home field advantage was awarded to the Pacific League team, as the Central League team held advantage the previous year.&#x20;

Hanshin clinched their first CL championship in two years on September 7, the earliest pennant win in CL history. [Kyuji Fujikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyuji_Fujikawa), former Tiger and [Major League](38776.md) player, led the team to the pennant in his first year as [manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manager_\(baseball\)). They finished the season 13 games ahead of the second-place [Yokohama DeNA BayStars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama_DeNA_BayStars), and as winners of the CL, they advanced directly to the final stage of the CL [Climax Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climax_Series) where they hosted the BayStars. The Tigers began the best-of-six series with a one-win advantage and swept the next three games against DeNA to advance. SoftBank clinched their second straight PL championship late in the season after a close race with the [Hokkaido Nippon-Ham Fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaido_Nippon-Ham_Fighters). Injuries to key players contributed to a slow start and forced manager [Hiroki Kokubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroki_Kokubo) to dip into the team's depth early. They bounced back after April and played well in the final five months of the season, including an [interleague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interleague_play_\(NPB\)) title. SoftBank moved directly to the final stage of the PL Climax Series with an automatic one-win advantage over the Fighters and won the first two games to make it 3–0. However, Nippon-Ham came back to win the next three games and tie the series at three games apiece. The Hawks went on to win Game 6 to advance to the Japan Series.

In a reflection of the [2014 Japan Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Japan_Series) between the Tigers and Hawks, in which the Tigers won Game 1, but lost Games 2–5 to allow the Hawks to win the Japan Series, Game 1 was once again won by the Tigers, their first ever Japan Series win in Fukuoka in eight games. However, [Ukyo Shuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukyo_Shuto)'s record-breaking five-hit game contributed to SoftBank's 14 total hits that powered the Hawks to a 10–1 victory in Game 2 to tie the series. The next two games were tight contests ultimately won by SoftBank, in part thanks to [home runs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_run) hit by [Hotaka Yamakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotaka_Yamakawa) in both. With home runs in Games 2, 3, and 4, Yamakawa tied the record for most consecutive Japan Series games with a home run. The Hawks clinched their 12th Japan Series championship at [Koshien Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koshien_Stadium) in Game 5, doing so in extra innings for their first Japan Series victory in five years and their eighth title since 2011. SoftBank's Yamakawa was named the [Japan Series Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Series_Most_Valuable_Player).

## Background

Further information: [2025 Central League Climax Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Central_League_Climax_Series) and [2025 Pacific League Climax Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Pacific_League_Climax_Series)

In their 90th anniversary season, the [Hanshin Tigers](490998.md) clinched their first [Central League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_League) (CL) championship in two years and seventh overall on September 7, the earliest pennant win in CL history.[^cite_note-NHK_tigers_clinch-1][^cite_note-sankei_tigers_clinch-2] The previous record was September 8, set by the [Yomiuri Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yomiuri_Giants) in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Nippon_Professional_Baseball_season).[^cite_note-sankei_tigers_clinch-2] [Kyuji Fujikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyuji_Fujikawa), former Tigers [closer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Closer_\(baseball\)) and [Major League](38776.md) player, led the team to the pennant in his first year as [manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manager_\(baseball\)).[^cite_note-tigers_clinch_kyodo-3] At the time the championship was decided, the Tigers were 17 [games ahead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Games_behind) of the second-place team, and all of the other CL teams had losing records.[^cite_note-NHK_tigers_clinch-1] Hanshin was the top team in the Central League throughout the season.[^cite_note-japan_forward_tigers_clinch-4] The team struggled during [interleague play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interleague_play_\(NPB\)) in June, losing seven straight games, their biggest losing streak of the season.[^cite_note-NHK_tigers_clinch-1] Strong pitching and good offense powered Hanshin's success throughout the season. They were league best in runs scored and runs allowed at the time of clinching.[^cite_note-japan_forward_tigers_clinch-4] By season's end, [Teruaki Sato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teruaki_Sato) finished with 40 [home runs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_run) and 102 [runs batted in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Run_batted_in) (RBIs), both NPB bests for the year and making him a likely PL [Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Professional_Baseball_Most_Valuable_Player_Award) (MVP) candidate. Teammates [Shota Morishita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shota_Morishita) had the second-most home runs in the CL with 23 and [Koji Chikamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koji_Chikamoto) [stole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolen_base) the most bases in the league for the fourth straight year. As for pitching, [starter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starting_pitcher) [Hiroto Saiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroto_Saiki) finished the season with a CL-best 1.55 [earned run average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earned_run_average) (ERA) and [Shoki Murakami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoki_Murakami) accumulated the most wins and [strikeouts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strikeout).[^cite_note-end_of_season_awards_JT-5] [Daichi Ishii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daichi_Ishii) also pitched 50 straight scoreless innings in relief, a [Nippon Professional Baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Professional_Baseball) (NPB) record.[^cite_note-kyodo_s2_g1-6]

Hanshin finished the season 13 games ahead of the second-place [Yokohama DeNA BayStars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yokohama_DeNA_BayStars).[^cite_note-standings-7] As winners of the Central League, they advanced directly to the final stage of the [CL Climax Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Central_League_Climax_Series) where they hosted the BayStars, eventual winner of the first stage. The Tigers began the best-of-six series with a one-win advantage and swept the next three games against DeNA to advance to the Japan Series.[^cite_note-kyodo_s2_g3-8]

The [Fukuoka SoftBank Hawks](318122.md) clinched their second straight [Pacific League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_League) (PL) championship and 21st overall on September 27 with only four games remaining in the season.[^cite_note-hawks_clinch_jn-9] Unlike [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Nippon_Professional_Baseball_season) in which they occupied first place for the majority of the season, the Hawks' [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Nippon_Professional_Baseball_season) campaign started poorly.[^cite_note-hawks_clinch_kyodo-10] The team fell as low as seven games under .500 by early May and found itself last in the PL, six games behind first.[^cite_note-otto-11] Injuries to key players [Yuki Yanagita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Yanagita), [Kensuke Kondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kensuke_Kondo), [Ryoya Kurihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryoya_Kurihara), [Ukyo Shuto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukyo_Shuto), and [Kenta Imamiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenta_Imamiya) contributed to SoftBank's slow start and forced manager [Hiroki Kokubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroki_Kokubo) to dip into the team's depth early to give new players an opportunity to cover for the injured veterans. After April, the team bounced back and won at least 14 games in each of the next five months.[^cite_note-hawks_clinch_JT-12] They won interleague play in June,[^cite_note-interleague-13] first moved into the top spot in the PL in late July,[^cite_note-otto-11] and secured first place for good in early August.[^cite_note-hawks_clinch_jn-9] Outfielder [Tatsuru Yanagimachi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatsuru_Yanagimachi) won the interleague MVP award and contended for the PL batting title all year, ultimately coming in second to teammate [Taisei Makihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taisei_Makihara) who finished with a .304 [batting average](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batting_average).[^cite_note-hawks_clinch_kyodo-10][^cite_note-end_of_season_awards_JT-5] Both are contenders for PL MVP.[^cite_note-hawks_clinch_kyodo-10] Hawks' pitching also excelled throughout the season. Three of their starting pitchers accumulated at least 140 innings pitched and ERAs of 2.78 or lower, four had at least 12 wins, and [Liván Moinelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liv%C3%A1n_Moinelo) led NPB with a 1.46 ERA. [Relief pitcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relief_pitcher) [Kazuki Sugiyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuki_Sugiyama) also led the league in [saves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_\(baseball\)).[^cite_note-hawks_clinch_JT-12] Ukyo Shuto stole the most bases for the third straight season.[^cite_note-end_of_season_awards_JT-5]

As winners of the Pacific League, SoftBank advanced directly to the final stage of the [PL Climax Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Pacific_League_Climax_Series). There they played the second-place [Hokkaido Nippon-Ham Fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkaido_Nippon-Ham_Fighters), the eventual first stage winner. The Hawks were granted the automatic one-win advantage and won the first two games of the series to make it 3–0. However, the Fighters came back to win the next three games and tie the series at three games apiece.[^cite_note-kyodo_s2_g6-14] It was the first time in NPB Climax Series history that a team forced a winner-take-all game after falling behind 0–3 in a series.[^cite_note-15] SoftBank went on to win the final game 2–1 and advanced to the Japan Series for the second straight year.[^cite_note-kyodo_s2_g6-14]

## Series notes

2025 Japan Series managers



[Hiroki Kokubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiroki_Kokubo), SoftBank



[Kyuji Fujikawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyuji_Fujikawa), Hanshin

SoftBank is considered to be a current NPB [dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynasty_\(sports\)), winning seven of the ten Japan Series from 2011 to 2020.[^cite_note-16] This is their second consecutive Japan Series appearance, however, the team lost in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Japan_Series) to the Yokohama DeNA BayStars, the third-seeded CL team. The Hawks won the first two games in that series but then lost the next four games to lose the championship to a team that was considered to be a heavy underdog. This series is also their 22nd Japan Series appearance, surpassing the [Saitama Seibu Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Seibu_Lions) as the PL team with the most championship appearances.[^cite_note-17] Hanshin's last appearance in the Japan Series was two years ago in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Japan_Series), a series that they won to secure their first Japan Series championship in 38 years and only their second overall.[^cite_note-japan_times_preview-18] This series is their eighth time appearing in the championship round.[^cite_note-19]

The two teams have met in the championship series three times. In their first meeting, the [1964 Japan Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_Japan_Series), the then Osaka-based Hawks defeated the Tigers in seven games. They again beat the Tigers in seven games during their second meeting in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Japan_Series).[^cite_note-20] The last time the two teams met in the Series was in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Japan_Series), when the Hawks defeated the Tigers a third time, four games to one.[^cite_note-japan_times_preview-18] The Tigers had lost all seven road games in the two Series played against the Hawks since their move to Fukuoka, a occurrence dubbed the "curse of [Hakata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakata-ku,_Fukuoka)" by Japanese media.[^cite_note-21] In their only regular season meeting during interleague play in June this season, SoftBank won the series 2–1.[^cite_note-standings-7]

Home field advantage for the Japan Series alternates between the Pacific and Central League representatives every year. For 2025, it was the PL's turn to hold the advantage, so home field was awarded to the Hawks. The [designated hitter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Designated_hitter) rule was only in effect for games one and two, played at [Mizuho PayPay Dome Fukuoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizuho_PayPay_Dome_Fukuoka), home of the Hawks, as the CL has not adopted the rule.[^cite_note-rules-22] [Sumitomo Mitsui Banking Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumitomo_Mitsui_Banking_Corporation) (SMBC) sponsored the event for the twelfth consecutive year and held the [naming rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naming_rights) for the series, with its full title being "SMBC Nippon Series 2025".[^cite_note-23]

Before the start of Game 1, [sopranist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sopranist) [Tomotaka Okamoto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomotaka_Okamoto_\(singer\)) sang the [Japanese national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_national_anthem), and former Hawks manager [Kimiyasu Kudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimiyasu_Kudo), who led the team to five championships, threw out the [ceremonial first pitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_first_pitch).[^cite_note-29] Of the 18 starting players for Game 1, none were foreign-born. It was the first time in 25 years that there were no foreign-born starting players in a Japan Series game.[^cite_note-30] Shoki Murakami started for the Tigers, while [Kohei Arihara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kohei_Arihara) started for the Hawks. Back for the Japan Series after missing the entirety of the Climax Series due to pain on his left side, Kensuke Kondo began the game's scoring in the bottom of the first inning when he hit an RBI [single](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_\(baseball\)) for the Hawks. In the top of the sixth inning, the Tigers scored two runs after Koji Chikamoto hit a single and recorded a stolen base, followed by a [bunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunt_\(baseball\)) single by [Takumu Nakano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takumu_Nakano), moving Chikamoto to third base, and Nakano stealing second base. Shota Morishita then tied the game with an RBI [ground out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_out_\(baseball\)) and Teruaki Sato gave the Tigers the lead with an RBI [double](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_\(baseball\)). Murakami allowed one run in seven [innings pitched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innings_pitched). Though SoftBank had two baserunners in the ninth inning after a Yuki Yanagita single and Ukyo Shuto reached base on a [catcher's interference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catcher's_interference) call, Daichi Ishii ended the inning without allowing a run.[^cite_note-game1-31] It was Ishii's 12th consecutive scoreless postseason game since 2021, and it earned him his first save in the Japan Series.[^cite_note-32] The win was Hanshin's first in Fukuoka in the Japan Series in eight tries.[^cite_note-33] For SoftBank, it was the first time they lost Game 1 of a Japan Series since 2014.[^cite_note-34]

## Aftermath

The Hawks' series win was their first championship title in five years and twelfth overall, putting them within one championship of the [Saitama Seibu Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saitama_Seibu_Lions) for the most Japan Series championships by a Pacific League team.[^cite_note-44] After Game 5, Hotaka Yamakawa was named the [Japan Series Most Valuable Player](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Series_Most_Valuable_Player).[^cite_note-awards-45] He batted 5-for-13 with three home runs, seven RBIs, and six walks for the series.[^cite_note-japan_times_g5-43] His three home runs came in three consecutive games, (Games 2, 3, and 4) tying the Japan Series record.[^cite_note-46] Teruaki Sato batted 7-for-19 with five RBIs and won the Fighting Spirit Award, given to the best player on the losing team.[^cite_note-japan_times_g5-43] Sato recorded his RBIs across all five games, tying a Japan Series record for most consecutive games with an RBI, and the only player to record one in every game played in the series.[^cite_note-47] SoftBank's Yuki Yanagita, Kazuki Sugiyama, and Koya Fujii were all named as recipients of the Outstanding Player Award. Hawks' captain Yanagita was also voted the SMBC Everyone's Cheering Award winner by fans.[^cite_note-awards-45]
