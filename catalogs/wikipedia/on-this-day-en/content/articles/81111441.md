On September 4, 2025, a series of widespread demonstrations began in the [Philippines](23440.md), with the largest initial protests occurring on September 11 at [Rizal Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Park) and [Liwasang Bonifacio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwasang_Bonifacio) in [Manila](184334.md), and at the [People Power Monument](31029934.md), [Camp Aguinaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Aguinaldo) Gate 4, and [EDSA Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Shrine) along [EDSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA) in [Quezon City](284223.md), both within [Metro Manila](72340.md).[^cite_note-22] They involve several anti-corruption protests stemming from the ongoing [flood control projects controversy](80804811.md) that largely involved the [Department of Public Works and Highways](4701882.md) (DPWH) and the [legislative branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Philippines) of the Philippine government. According to reports, the demonstrations focus on allegations of massive irregularities in [flood control programmes](20913655.md), with some [₱](227672.md)1.9 trillion ([US\$](18717338.md)33 billion) spent over the past 15 years, more than half of which was allegedly lost to corruption.[^cite_note-auto-23] Several cities and municipalities across different provinces also held local demonstrations.

The September 11 protest in Rizal Park ("[Baha sa Luneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baha_sa_Luneta)") was organised by various sectoral groups, including activists and students, while the simultaneous protest at the People Power Monument and EDSA Shrine is known as the "[Trillion Peso March](81111441.md)", organised mainly by church groups, civil society organisations, student organisations, labour unions, and political coalitions.[^cite_note-whattoknow-5][^cite_note-TheGuardian-24] Although protests were characterised as peaceful throughout the country, an isolated riot by masked demonstrators occurred along streets near [Malacañang Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaca%C3%B1ang_Palace) in Manila, injuring several protestors, bystanders, and policemen, and resulting in two casualties: one from a stabbing attack, and another from a stray bullet.

Protests have continued as of February 2026. The "Trillion Peso March Part 2" and a "Baha Sa Luneta 2" were held on November 30, 2025, while a third reiteration of the aforementioned protests was conducted on February 25, 2026, timed with the 40th anniversary of the [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution).

Inter-religious leaders have also announced the formation of White Ribbon Movement (WRM) which will lead an anti-corruption rally on June 28, 2026 at the EDSA People Power Monument.[^cite_note-25]

## Background

Main article: [Flood control projects scandal in the Philippines](80804811.md)

Since 2024, a series of allegations of corruption, mismanagement, and irregularities in government-funded [flood management](20913655.md) projects have been occurring in the [Philippines](23440.md) under the [administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Bongbong_Marcos) of President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md). The controversy centres on billions of [pesos](227672.md) allocated for flood management initiatives, reports of ["ghost" projects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%22ghost%22_project),[^cite_note-ghost-27] substandard construction, and the alleged cornering of contracts by a small group of favoured contractors.

In his fourth [State of the Nation Address](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Nation_Address_\(Philippines\)) (SONA) in [July 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_State_of_the_Nation_Address_\(Philippines\)), President Marcos reported that his administration had implemented over 5,500 flood control projects and announced plans for at least ten large-scale projects amounting to more than [₱](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Peso)500 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)10.15 billion) over the next 13 years.[^cite_note-28] The [Department of Public Works and Highways](4701882.md) (DPWH) justified these projects as urgent measures to mitigate flooding, particularly in Metro Manila and Central Luzon, but admitted that poor waste management practices were aggravating the flooding problem.[^cite_note-29] Marcos later vetoed ₱16.7 billion ([US\$](18717338.md)339.09 million) worth of flood control projects included in the 2025 national budget, citing redundancy and improper project placement.[^cite_note-30]

On September 1, 2025, [Sarah Discaya](80879220.md) attended a [Senate Blue Ribbon Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Blue_Ribbon_Committee) hearing and revealed that her [nine construction firms](80879220.md#Business_careers) have sometimes been involved in bidding for the same government flood control projects simultaneously. Initially, Discaya denied the allegation, but [Senate President pro tempore](8750570.md) and committee vice-chairperson [Jinggoy Estrada](2395334.md) pressed for data from the DPWH, to which Discaya admitted.[^cite_note-31]

## Timeline of events

### September 11 protests

Calls for the September 11 demonstrations were first announced by church leaders and civil society groups in early September 2025. Organisers made use of social media platforms and networks of parishes, universities, and labour unions to circulate information and mobilise participants. Practical guidelines for attendees were also shared online, advising protestors to bring water, snacks, umbrellas, and power banks while avoiding items that could be misconstrued as weapons.[^cite_note-auto-23]

#### Baha sa Luneta

The main programme began at 9\:00 a.m. at [Rizal Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Park) (Luneta) in [Manila](184334.md), dubbed as "Baha sa Luneta: Aksyon na Laban sa Korapsyon" ('Flood at Luneta: Action Against Corruption') or the "Billion People March". Around the morning, large numbers of protestors began to converge, and by mid-day, estimates placed the crowd at about 50,000 people. Demonstrators carried banners and chanted anti-corruption slogans, calling for transparency and accountability from government officials.[^cite_note-AlJazeera-32] It was followed by a march to the [Liwasang Bonifacio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwasang_Bonifacio) in Manila, dubbed as "Save the Philippines", and parallel rallies were held in [Baguio](162480.md), [Bohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol), [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro), [Cebu](160278.md), and [Negros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Island), with [overseas Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Filipino) communities staging solidarity actions.[^cite_note-33]

#### Trillion Peso March

Occurring simultaneously, the Trillion Peso March was centred at the [People Power Monument](31029934.md) and [EDSA Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Shrine) in [Quezon City](284223.md). Comedian and television host [Vice Ganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Ganda) addressed the rally, urging President Marcos to take action against government officials accused of corruption and expressing support for reinstating the [death penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_penalty_in_the_Philippines) for those involved in questionable flood control projects. Other public figures, including [Ogie Alcasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogie_Alcasid), [Iza Calzado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iza_Calzado), [Anne Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Curtis), and [Catriona Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catriona_Gray), were also present.[^cite_note-34] Former [Ilocos Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Sur) governor [Chavit Singson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavit_Singson) also attended but was booed by protestors who called him a "plunderer".[^cite_note-35]

The protest at the People Power Monument saw the band [Ben\&Ben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben\&Ben) perform their single "[Kapangyarihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapangyarihan_\(song\))" ('Power'). In the afternoon, protests at the monument and White Plains Avenue spilled over onto EDSA, leading to the closure of all northbound lanes. The [Metropolitan Manila Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Manila_Development_Authority) redirected traffic towards [Ortigas Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigas_Avenue), and the [Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Transportation_\(Philippines\)) temporarily opened the [EDSA Busway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Carousel) to all vehicles. The northbound lanes were reopened around 6\:00 p.m.[^cite_note-MMDA-EDSA-Closure-36]

#### Mendiola clashes and vandalism

In the afternoon of September 11, masked youth groups numbering roughly 400 joined the Baha sa Luneta protest. Some moved toward [Ayala Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayala_Bridge) and later [Mendiola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendiola_Street), where confrontations escalated around 5\:30 p.m. Police reported that demonstrators hurled rocks, bottles, and improvised incendiary devices, including [Molotov cocktails](20922.md), while attempting to breach police barricades.[^cite_note-37]

A trailer truck and several motorcycles, including some belonging to the PNP, were set on fire by demonstrators. The PNP accused rioters of stealing firearms from officers.[^cite_note-inq-38] A branch of [Hotel Sogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Sogo) along [Recto Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recto_Avenue) was also ransacked.[^cite_note-39] By late evening, police announced that at least 72 individuals had been arrested in Manila due to violent acts, and at least 39 to 40 police officers were injured.[^cite_note-40] Authorities emphasised that the majority of participants in Luneta remained peaceful.

#### Digital activism

During the September 21 demonstrations, virtual rallies on online game platforms such as *[Roblox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roblox)* were organised by Filipino users.[^cite_note-41] Additionally, the websites of 19 government agencies were defaced by hackers in protest against state corruption.[^cite_note-42] Many [Pinoy pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinoy_pop) and OPM artists expressed support through social media, including [SB19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SB19), [Bini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bini_\(group\)), [G22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G22_\(Filipino_girl_group\)), [Kaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaia_\(group\)), and [Katseye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katseye) member Sofia Laforteza.[^cite_note-43]

### October to November 2025 protests

The Trillion Peso March Movement was launched on October 1, 2025, with another protest planned for November 30, 2025, coinciding with [Bonifacio Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonifacio_Day).[^cite_note-44] Weekly protests were planned in the lead up to the November 30 demonstrations, with the first of these weekly protests held on October 21, 2025, at the EDSA Shrine.[^cite_note-45]

### February 2026 protests

Third major reiterations of the Baha sa Luneta and Trillion Peso March were conducted on February 25, 2026, explicitly timed with the 40th anniversary of the [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution).

## Groups involved and demands

### Civil society

On September 11, 2025, Baha sa Luneta was convened by various student networks and activist coalitions such as Taumbayan Ayaw sa Magnanakaw at Abusado Network Alliance/TAMA NA (a broad anti-corruption alliance), Panatang Luntian Coalition (youth environmentalists), and student councils from various universities.[^cite_note-whattoknow-5] Many groups that co-organized the Baha sa Luneta later formed the Kilusang Bayan Kontra Kurakot (KBKK)[^cite_note-46]

The Church Leaders Council for National Transformation led the Trillion Peso March,[^cite_note-seeks-47] along with Simbahan at Komunidad Laban sa Katiwalian (SIKLAB), Alyansa ng Nagkakaisang Mamamayan (ANIM), and Taumbayan Ayaw sa Magnanakaw at Abusado Network Alliance (TAMA NA).[^cite_note-48] Organisers stressed that the protests were not intended to call for the removal of any specific political leader but instead aimed at institutional reforms and accountability.[^cite_note-abc-49]

Protest organisers at the Baha sa Luneta and Trillion Peso March protests listed several demands, including:[^cite_note-gma1-50][^cite_note-Ataleoftwo-51]

- the arrest of individuals implicated in the alleged anomalous flood control projects

- the recovery of alleged ill-gotten wealth, including luxury goods and vehicles

- the public release of government officials' [Statements of Assets, Liabilities, and Net Worth (SALNs)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statement_of_Assets,_Liabilities,_and_Net_Worth), along with the lifting of bank secrecy restrictions

- the enactment of stronger anti-corruption measures, including granting subpoena and contempt powers to independent investigative bodies

- the inclusion of representatives from civil society, people's organisations, and anti-corruption groups in the [Independent Commission for Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Commission_for_Infrastructure) (ICI)

- the granting of contempt powers to the ICI to compel the participation of government officials in its investigation

- the recovery of stolen public funds and their reallocation toward health, education, housing, and compensation for flood victims.

On November 16, 2025, the Roundtable for Inclusive Development (RFID)—composed of lawyers, businessmen, church and education leaders, and former public officials—called for transparency following recent calamities exacerbated by the alleged misuse of public funds. They called for an Independent People's Commission, a ban on [political dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_families_of_the_Philippines), budget transparency, a [Freedom of Information Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_information_laws_by_country#Philippines), and a comprehensive review of the [party-list system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party-list_representation_in_the_House_of_Representatives_of_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-Business-52]

### Left-leaning parties and coalitions

Progressive parties including [Laban ng Masa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laban_ng_Masa) (including [Partido Lakas ng Masa (PLM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partido_Lakas_ng_Masa), [Bukluran ng Manggagawang Pilipino (BMP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukluran_ng_Manggagawang_Pilipino), and Samahan ng Progresibong Kabataan (SPARK)) and national democratic parties affiliated with [Bagong Alyansang Makabayan (BAYAN)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagong_Alyansang_Makabayan) and [Makabayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makabayan) participated at Baha sa Luneta.[^cite_note-gma7-53] BAYAN also led protest actions in regions across the Philippines and by OFWs globally, including in [Utrecht](50333.md).[^cite_note-espanol-54]

The broad goals of the progressive and national-democratic organisations include the resignation of both President Marcos and Vice President Duterte and the formation of a civilian-led People's Transition Council (PTC).[^cite_note-lunetaprotesters-55] The PLM posited that the PTC should hold corrupt officials accountable, establish safeguards against corruption, and hold fair elections, but without running in the elections themselves.[^cite_note-ptcam-56]

Groups such as 1Sambayan, Liberal Party of the Philippines, and Akbayan Partylist joined the Trillion Peso March. [^cite_note-faithleaders-57]

[Cardinal](6221.md) [Pablo Virgilio David](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Virgilio_David) distanced himself from Baha sa Luneta 2.0 due to its demands for Marcos and Duterte to resign, drawing pushback from protest organisers who warned his statement might negatively impact the opposition's unity leading into the [2028 Philippine presidential election](78758443.md).[^cite_note-nanlumo-58]

### Communists

Far-left groups affiliated with the [Communist Party of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Philippines) and the [National Democratic Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Front_of_the_Philippines), such as [Kabataang Makabayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabataang_Makabayan), also organised protests.[^cite_note-thecatalyst-59]

### Diehard Duterte supporters

On September 21, 2025, Duterte supporters gathered near Gate 4 of [Camp Aguinaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Aguinaldo) at 7\:00 a.m. and had a brief scuffle with other protestors attending the Trillion Peso March.[^cite_note-PSMangaluz-11] They called for President Marcos's resignation in addition to their pronouncements against corruption. A Catholic nun was ganged up on by Duterte supporters at [Liwasang Bonifacio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwasang_Bonifacio) after stating Marcos was "only a victim of dark forces".[^cite_note-60]

In [Davao City](489401.md), pro-Duterte groups roasted 100 cows as an "offering to the heavens" against corruption in a prayer rally organised by Vice President Duterte. The sacrificial slaughtering was condemned by the [Philippine Animal Welfare Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Animal_Welfare_Society).[^cite_note-100cows-61][^cite_note-PAWS-62] It was observed that Duterte loyalists shared the calls for the resignation of Marcos, but drew the line at removing Vice President Duterte.[^cite_note-63]

### Pro-Marcos counterprotesters

Supporters of President Marcos launched [counterprotests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counter-protest) in response to the demonstrations against the administration.[^cite_note-civicgroups-64] Groups such as the Filipinos Do Not Yield Movement called for accountability for everyone involved in the flood control projects scandal.[^cite_note-65]

### Iglesia ni Cristo

Main article: [Rally for Transparency and a Better Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rally_for_Transparency_and_a_Better_Democracy)

The Rally for Transparency and a Better Democracy is a protest led by the [Iglesia ni Cristo (INC)](288343.md) which began on November 16, 2025. Around 650,000 people gathered according to the [Manila Police District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Police_District).[^cite_note-66] While open to non-members, INC spokesman Edwil Zabala said the rally was not meant to "interfere in politics" but to demand accountability for the flood control projects scandal.[^cite_note-auto1-67]

Senator [Rodante Marcoleta](68240498.md), an INC member, spoke at the rally.[^cite_note-68] Senator [Imee Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imee_Marcos) was in attendance and alleged that her brother, President Bongbong Marcos, First Lady [Liza Araneta Marcos](70769981.md), and their children used drugs.[^cite_note-69] Zabala later clarified that Senator Imee Marcos's speech was outside the protest's scope of calling for transparency.[^cite_note-70]

## Government response

The [Philippine National Police](1313055.md) (PNP) placed Metro Manila under full alert in the week leading up to the September 11 rallies, suspending leave for officers and preparing road closures near [Rizal Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Park) and [EDSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA). Authorities stated that no specific security threats had been identified as of mid-September 2025.[^cite_note-71]

President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) expressed support for the protests, saying he "might be out in the streets" as well if he "were not president". He added that he shared the public's frustration and encouraged people to demand accountability while maintaining peaceful demonstrations.[^cite_note-abc-49] [Department of Public Works and Highways](4701882.md) Secretary [Vince Dizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Dizon) fully supported the anti-corruption demonstrations, noting that protestors should avoid violence.[^cite_note-72] Conversely, [Malacañang Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaca%C3%B1ang_Palace) Press Officer [Claire Castro](77955986.md) warned that certain actors "may try to exploit this legitimate cause for their own personal interests and to undermine the government."[^cite_note-73]

Defence Secretary [Gilbert Teodoro](21940341.md) and [Armed Forces](23448.md) chief of staff [Romeo Brawner Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romeo_Brawner_Jr.) responded to calls for the military to withdraw support from President Marcos by issuing a joint statement affirming the armed forces' non-partisanship and professionalism. They emphasised that the 160,000-member institution follows the [Constitution](19554336.md) and operates under the chain of command.[^cite_note-abc-49]

### September 4–10

Protesters converged at the headquarters of [Curlee and Sarah Discaya](80879220.md)'s St. Gerrard Construction General Contractor and Development Corporation in [Pasig](359702.md). The protesters vandalized the headquarter's gate.[^cite_note-74] According to [Jonila Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonila_Castro), one of the protesters, "We chose to come here to one of the companies of the so-called 'Flood Control Queen and King,' the Discayas, to show and bring the people's anger against thieves."[^cite_note-z-75] In front of the DPWH main office along [Bonifacio Drive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonifacio_Drive), protesters, including those from youth group [Anakbayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anakbayan) threw rotten vegetables and smelly water at the gate, which the [Manila Police District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manila_Police_District) [Philippine National Police](1313055.md) (PNP) tried to defend using their shields.[^cite_note-z-75]

In Cebu, regional leaders from AMA Sugbu [Kilusang Mayo Uno](5308660.md) (KMU), [Bayan Muna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayan_Muna) Cebu, and [Bagong Alyansang Makabayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagong_Alyansang_Makabayan) (Bayan) Central Visayas threw rotten tomatoes at the office of DPWH Region VII office in [Cebu City](285868.md) decrying corruption.[^cite_note-\:4-76] A *Takbo Laban sa Korapsyon* (Run Against Corruption) [fun run](8836052.md) was done at the [University of the Philippines Diliman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines_Diliman) (UP Diliman) as a protest against corruption where more than 1,000 people participated. They condemned President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) as the original [nepo baby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepo_baby). Organized by the [UP Diliman University Student Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UP_Diliman_University_Student_Council) (UPD USC), Chairperson Joaquin "Waks" Buenaflor said that they organized the event to unite the people against corruption especially amidst the flood control projects controversy.[^cite_note-1000katao-6]

### September 12



A pre-September 21 Black Friday Protest held by [UP Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UP_Manila) chapters of Anakbayan and LFS.

A "Black Friday" [walkout](2016860.md) was held by thousands of faculty, students and staff of the [University of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines), particularly in the [UP Diliman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines_Diliman) and in the [University of the Philippines Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines_Manila). Over 3,000 from UP Diliman students, faculty, staff and community joined the protests, making it the largest mass gathering action since the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Metro_Manila) in the university.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] Series of walkouts were staged by student governments and national-democratic mass organizations such as [League of Filipino Students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Filipino_Students) (LFS) also among various campuses.[^cite_note-79]

### September 6

On September 6, several groups marched from the [EDSA Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Shrine) in [Ortigas Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigas_Center) up to the [People Power Monument](31029934.md) on [EDSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA) to protest the alleged corruption in nationwide flood control projects.[^cite_note-80]

### September 7

Over 500 people took part in the Marikina City leg of Takbo Laban sa Korapsyon to protest corruption in government infrastructure projects. [Sarah Elago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Elago) of [Gabriela Women's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Women's_Party) joined the run. On the same day, 150 cyclists from Youth Advocates for Climate Action Philippines and other groups joined the #KontrakTOUR Bike Protest Against Corruption from Quezon City Hall to Luneta Park in Manila.[^cite_note-81]

### September 8

Students from [Bulacan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulacan_State_University) staged a campus "walkout" on September 8 to protest alleged corruption in government projects.[^cite_note-82] The activity was joined by around 300 participants, including students, faculty members, youth organizations, and representatives from marginalized sections.[^cite_note-83]

Transport group Pagkakaisa ng mga Samahan ng Tsuper at Operator Nationwide ([Piston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PISTON_\(transport_group\))) staged a nationwide strike to protest corruption, explaining how floods result in a loss of livelihood and additional expenses for jeepney drivers and their families.[^cite_note-84]

### September 9

Over 7,000 students joined in the [UP Los Baños](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UP_Los_Ba%C3%B1os) walkout on September 19.[^cite_note-85] This was considered as the biggest protest inside the university in history. Before the protest, local colleges held their separate protests before merging.[^cite_note-86]

### September 10

Local residents of [Hagonoy, Bulacan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hagonoy,_Bulacan), held a protest on 3.9-foot-high (1.2 m) high tide water that engulfed many areas in the town and in the towns plaza to protest the failed flood control projects in the town.[^cite_note-87]

In [Baguio City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguio_City), a city-wide walkout was staged by students of [Saint Louis University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Louis_University_\(Philippines\)) where it was participated by local chapters of Anakbayan, [Bahaghari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahaghari_Philippines), Innabuyog-[Gabriela Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Youth?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Kabataan Partylist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabataan_Partylist), and [National Union of Students of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Union_of_Students_of_the_Philippines) (NUSP). Students wore black and chanted "*Marcos singilin, Duterte panagutin!*" (Charge Marcos, hold Duterte accountable!). They said that "accountability must extend to President Bongbong Marcos, as he himself signed the budget that enabled these corrupt schemes; he bears primary responsibility." The protest was staged as a build-up for the September 21 protests.[^cite_note-88]

### September 11



Students of University of the Philippines going to Luneta on the morning of September 21.



[BAYAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagong_Alyansang_Makabayan) and [Makabayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makabayan) leaders near Mendiola.

A series of mass demonstrations around the country were held on Sunday, September 21. The date of the rally, September 11, coincides with the anniversary of the [imposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_No._1081) of [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos) by President [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) in 1972, a symbolic choice for groups emphasizing democratic rights and government accountability.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-\:1-90][^cite_note-91]

Ahead of the rallies, the [Philippine National Police](1313055.md) placed Metro Manila under full alert, suspending leave for officers and preparing road closures near [Rizal Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Park), [EDSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA), and other strategic sites.[^cite_note-92] Authorities stated that no specific security threats had been identified as of mid-September 2025.[^cite_note-93] [Malacañang Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaca%C3%B1ang_Palace) Press Officer [Claire Castro](77955986.md) warned that certain actors "may try to exploit this legitimate cause for their own personal interests and to undermine the government."[^cite_note-94]

The largest protests were held in [Metro Manila](72340.md): the [Baha sa Luneta](#Baha_sa_Luneta) in [Rizal Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Park), and the [Trillion Peso March](#Trillion_Peso_March) in the [EDSA Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Shrine). Organizers described the two rallies as complementary and encouraged participation in either or both protests.[^cite_note-Ataleoftwo-51] Both events have avoided calling for the removal of President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) and Vice President [Sara Duterte](42504861.md), welcoming all groups opposed to corruption regardless of political affiliation.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] Bayan chairperson [Teodoro Casiño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teodoro_Casi%C3%B1o) warned against "vested interests" calling for the removal of Marcos and Duterte, while [Akbayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbayan) representative [Perci Cendaña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perci_Cenda%C3%B1a) noted that Marcos' resignation may benefit Duterte.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] In response, [Anakbayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anakbayan) National Chairperson Mhing Gomez said that there must be no compromises—more than the removal of both Marcos and Duterte, it is the system that must be changed.[^cite_note-99]

Several government officials have expressed support for the protests. President Marcos expressed his support for the protests, stating that he understands their anger and would likely join them if he were not president.[^cite_note-TheGuardian-24] DPWH Secretary [Vince Dizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Dizon) expressed his full support for rallies that condemn government corruption, responding to ongoing anti-corruption demonstrations.[^cite_note-100] Vice President Duterte, who flew to visit [OFW communities in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipinos_in_Japan), also voiced her support for the protests, calling on the government to listen.[^cite_note-101] Defense Secretary [Gilbert Teodoro](21940341.md) and [Armed Forces](23448.md) chief of staff [Romeo Brawner Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romeo_Brawner_Jr.) issued a joint statement affirming the armed forces' non-partisanship and professionalism, emphasizing that the 160,000-member institution follows the [Constitution](19554336.md) and operates under the chain of command, after a rally speaker urged the military to end its loyalty to Marcos and called for a non-violent "[people power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_power)" movement.[^cite_note-abc-49]

During the rallies, Duterte supporters who were gathered near Gate 4 of [Camp Aguinaldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Aguinaldo) had a brief scuffle with other protesters attending the Trillion Peso March.[^cite_note-PSMangaluz-11][^cite_note-INQCabalza-102] They had been calling for President Marcos's resignation in addition to their general pronouncements against corruption.[^cite_note-PSMangaluz-11] Among those who attended the anti-Marcos rally were retired military officers led by [Romeo Poquiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romeo_Poquiz), a former general in the [Air Force](27918720.md).[^cite_note-INQCabalza-102] In later weeks, Armed Forces Chief of Staff [Romeo Brawner Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romeo_Brawner_Jr.) admitted to being visited by Poquiz and seven other retired military officers at Camp Aguinaldo in the evening before September 21, with them calling for the military's withdrawal of support for president Marcos and initiating a "soft coup" in order to solve corruption.[^cite_note-INQCabalza-102][^cite_note-SCMPRobles-103] Brawner denied their proposal, citing the military's allegiance to the constitution.[^cite_note-INQCabalza-102][^cite_note-SCMPRobles-103] The administration has since deemed their actions seditious and treasonous, although president Marcos was noted to have been unbothered by the retired generals' actions.[^cite_note-INQCabalza-102]

#### Luneta Park

Rizal Park became one of the primary venues for the September 11 mass demonstrations. The activities around Luneta were collectively referred to as "Baha sa Luneta" (transl. "Flood in Luneta"),[^cite_note-104] a name first adopted by civic groups such as Artikulo Onse: Citizens' War Against Corruption and [Sanlakas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanlakas), who had also referred to the planned event as the "Billion Peso March".[^cite_note-105]

Protesters gathered at the Luneta Park on Sunday in a program led by the group Taumbayan Ayaw sa Magnanakaw at Abusado Network Alliance (transl. "People Against Thieves and Abusers Network Alliance"), or TAMA NA.[^cite_note-gma7-53] Students from the [University of the Philippines Manila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines_Manila) marched to the Luneta rally.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-faithleaders-57] The main program began at 9\:00 a.m. at [Rizal Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Park) (Luneta) in [Manila](184334.md). The [pedestrian crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedestrian_crossing) in front of the [Rizal Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Monument) drew thousands of participants, including first-time demonstrators as well as representatives from various sectoral and church groups.[^cite_note-108]

About 49,000 people were in the park by 10 a.m., according to Manila Public Information Office.[^cite_note-109] The number of protesters swelled to about 100,000.[^cite_note-\:5-110]

Civic organizations that participated included the [Gabriela Women's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriela_Women's_Party), Health Alliance for Democracy, and Karapatan.[^cite_note-gma7-53] Among the performers and personalities at the rally were [drag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drag_queen) performers [Pura Luka Vega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pura_Luka_Vega), Gabriela She Lang, and Maria Cristina, who led the Harong Queens in a performance. [Miss Philippines Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miss_Philippines_Earth) [Joy Barcoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joy_Barcoma) also took part, heading a group of volunteers in post-rally clean-up efforts. Comedian [Jun Sabayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jun_Sabayton) and rapper artists Supafly, Morobeats, and Vitrum also participated.[^cite_note-111] Actresses [Angel Aquino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angel_Aquino), [Andrea Brillantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrea_Brillantes), [Maris Racal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maris_Racal), [Jodi Sta. Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jodi_Sta._Maria), and [Tessie Tomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tessie_Tomas) were also spotted at the rally,[^cite_note-112] as was indie-rock band [Lola Amour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lola_Amour).[^cite_note-113] Student performers from the University of the Philippines staged a performance covered in mud. UP Repertory Company manager Seah Antalan stated that the act was meant to "reclaim the narrative of the people", contrasting it with displays of privilege.[^cite_note-114] Some protesters waved the [Straw Hat Pirates' Jolly Roger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straw_Hat_Pirates'_Jolly_Roger) from the [manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manga) series *[One Piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece)*, a symbol of rebellion against corruption and injustice similar to flags seen in demonstrations in [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2025_Indonesian_protests) and [Nepal](81013780.md) weeks earlier.[^cite_note-115]

#### EDSA Shrine

Led by Tindig Pilipinas, Catholic Educational Association of the Philippines, Clergy for Good Governance, and other religious groups, protesters gathered at the [EDSA Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Shrine) to conduct the Trillion Peso March demonstration.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] The number of protesters were about 70,000.[^cite_note-\:5-110]

Comedian and television host [Vice Ganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Ganda) addressed the rally at the People Power Monument, urging President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) to take action against government officials accused of corruption and expressing support for reinstating the [death penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_penalty_in_the_Philippines) for those allegedly involved in questionable flood control projects. Other public figures, including [Ogie Alcasid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ogie_Alcasid), [Iza Calzado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iza_Calzado), [Anne Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Curtis), and [Catriona Gray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catriona_Gray), were also present at the event.[^cite_note-118] Former [Ilocos Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocos_Sur) governor [Chavit Singson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavit_Singson) also attended, but was booed by protesters who called him a "plunderer".[^cite_note-119] The band [Ben\&Ben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben\&Ben) performed their single "[Kapangyarihan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapangyarihan_\(song\))" (lit. 'Power'), a song about accountability.[^cite_note-120]

Some [Pinoy pop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinoy_pop) and OPM artists did not participate in the rally but expressed support through social media. [SB19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SB19) members [Josh Cullen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josh_Cullen_\(musician\)) and [Pablo Nase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_\(Filipino_musician\)) posted a video of themselves singing a portion of Ben\&Ben's "Kapangyarihan". The [girl group](25555420.md) [Bini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bini_\(group\)) released a statement on [X](9988187.md), with individual members also posting on their own accounts. Other groups such as [G22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G22_\(Filipino_girl_group\)) and [Kaia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaia_\(group\)) referenced their song "Tanga" (transl. Fool). Filipino singer-songwriter [Maki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maki_\(singer\)) posted in solidarity, while [Katseye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katseye) member Sofia Laforteza shared on Instagram her call for "a future built on truth, integrity, and hope".[^cite_note-121] Other artists who either participated in the rally or spoke online were R\&B artist [Denise Julia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denise_Julia), pop-rock band [Cup of Joe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cup_of_Joe_\(band\)) (which jokingly referred their song "[Multo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multo_\(song\))" to the said scandal, but also spread their message on social media while performing at a gig in [Iligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iligan)), bassist Coey Ballesteros of the band [Hey June!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hey_June!) (who personally attended the rally while seeing rallyists' placards using a reference to the band's hit single "[Lasik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasik_\(song\))"), and alt-rock band [Over October](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Over_October).[^cite_note-musiciansrappler-122]

In the afternoon, the protests at the People Power Monument and White Plains Avenue spilled over onto EDSA, leading to the closure of all northbound lanes including the service road. In response, the [Metropolitan Manila Development Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Manila_Development_Authority) redirected all [Ortigas Interchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigas_Interchange) service road traffic towards [Ortigas Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ortigas_Avenue), while the [Department of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Transportation_\(Philippines\)) temporarily opened the [EDSA Busway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Carousel) to all types of vehicles coming from the northbound flyover to Santolan.[^cite_note-MMDA-EDSA-Closure-36][^cite_note-DOTr-EDSA-Busway-123] The northbound lanes of EDSA were made open to traffic again around 6 p.m. [PST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Standard_Time).[^cite_note-QC-EDSA-Open-124]

#### Morayta

The September Twenty-One People's Movement Against Corruption, a coalition of pro-administration civic and reform-oriented groups gathered at Morayta at 4\:00 PM, calling for Marcos and the [Independent Commission for Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_Commission_for_Infrastructure) to end corruption.[^cite_note-civicgroups-64]

#### Other cities around the country

The rally lasted for about two hours.[^cite_note-127] A separate rally was held by pro-Duterte protesters at [Plaza Independencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Independencia_\(Cebu_City\)), calling for President Marcos' resignation.[^cite_note-128]

In [Davao City](489401.md), pro-Duterte groups roasted 100 cows as an "offering to the heavens" against corruption in a prayer rally organized by Vice President Duterte.[^cite_note-100cows-61] The sacrificial slaughtering was condemned by the [Philippine Animal Welfare Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Animal_Welfare_Society).[^cite_note-PAWS-62]

Similar protests were also held in [Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legazpi,_Albay),[^cite_note-129] [Laoag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laoag), [Tuguegarao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuguegarao), [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_Pampanga), [Olongapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olongapo), [Baguio](162480.md), [Pangasinan](1691800.md), [Dumaguete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumaguete), [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro),[^cite_note-130] and [Butuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuan).[^cite_note-131]

In Baguio City, more than 5,000 protested composed of youth, church, and workers' groups. Those who spoke included [Joanna Cariño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joanna_Cari%C3%B1o) of the [Cordillera People's Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_People's_Alliance?action=edit\&redlink=1) and public health worker Beth Solang who called out the government amidst unfinished healthcare facilities, understaffed healthcare systems, and underpaid healthcare workers.[^cite_note-132]

#### Violent demonstrations along Mendiola Street, Recto Avenue and Ayala Boulevard



Protesters wave the [Straw Hats' Jolly Roger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straw_Hats'_Jolly_Roger) flag in [Manila](184334.md), September 11, 2025



Two masked rioters near Ayala bridge in Manila, September 21, 2025

While protests nationwide were relatively peaceful, violent demonstrations occurred along [Mendiola Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendiola_Street), [Recto Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recto_Avenue) and [Ayala Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayala_Boulevard?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Manila](184334.md).[^cite_note-921_protest_casualties-18][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-ProspectsBallaran-134] Police reported that protesters in Mendiola threw improvised incendiary devices, including [Molotov cocktails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molotov_cocktails), which injured at least 39 officers.[^cite_note-135] Alongside these confrontations, media also reported scattered incidents of property damage such as burning tires and barricades during face-offs between security forces and demonstrators.[^cite_note-\:1-90] Demonstrators engaged in violent confrontations with police and ignited a trailer truck on [Ayala Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayala_Bridge) in Manila, also setting fire to several motorcycles including some belonging to the PNP.[^cite_note-136] A group of protesters in masks violently demonstrated along Recto Avenue by setting fires in the middle of the street, hurling stones, human waste, and objects at police officers, and attacking several nearby establishments including ransacking a [Sogo Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sogo_Hotel) branch.[^cite_note-137] The PNP accused rioters of stealing firearms from police officers.[^cite_note-138] Reports vary on testimonies regarding small explosions heard during confrontations between the authorities and rallyists in Mendiola.[^cite_note-PCO-139][^cite_note-140] The SWAT team eventually resorted to sporadically opening fire, resulting in the death of Eric Saber.[^cite_note-Bulatlat-19][^cite_note-Bulatlatverify-141] Some of them even helped the Sogo Hotel employees beat up a minor. None of the initial police officers assigned to crowd control held firearms,[^cite_note-MM-142] while a [SWAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SWAT) team later arrived carrying firearms and was seen using tear gas by eyewitnesses.[^cite_note-143][^cite_note-PSLaqui-144]

Supporters of [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) were reported to have attempted a march from their protest venue in [Liwasang Bonifacio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwasang_Bonifacio) to Mendiola, intending to move their rally to nearby [Malacañang Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaca%C3%B1ang_Palace) and call for President Marcos' resignation, but halted their march due to the rioters along Recto.[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147]

Seventeen individuals were arrested for throwing rocks at riot police and igniting barricade tires, as reported by local authorities. Less than an hour later, police deployed a water cannon against another group of masked protesters. Some officers retaliated by throwing rocks back at the demonstrators. Manila Mayor [Isko Moreno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isko_Moreno) stated that police officers were injured during the confrontations and are currently receiving medical care.[^cite_note-\:1-90] Photojournalists who documented the event also reported beatings and harassment of rallyists and bystanders committed by the police;[^cite_note-PSLaqui-144] photojournalist Zedrich Xylak Madrid himself was hit by police officers, while attempts were made by officers in preventing Reuters photojournalist Liza Marie David from taking photographs.[^cite_note-ProspectsBallaran-134]

&#x20;Mark Chustin Serbo, a 15-year-old protester from [Taguig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taguig) who participated in the violent demonstration, was fatally stabbed by a 52-year-old watch technician after attempting to set fire to a police motorcycle near the latter's shop; he was initially reported to have been the lone casualty during the incident, having been declared dead on arrival upon being brought to the [Jose R. Reyes Memorial Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_R._Reyes_Memorial_Medical_Center) (JRRMMC).[^cite_note-INQClores-148][^cite_note-ABS-CBNCruzat-149][^cite_note-AMGudaAng-150] Serbo's parents had no prior knowledge of his participation in the Manila rally, only being told by Serbo that he was going out for a Sunday afternoon with friends.[^cite_note-ABS-CBNCruzat-149] The technician later surrendered to Manila police and apologized, claiming to have panicked and blanked out in committing the attack amidst the violence.[^cite_note-INQClores-148]



Activists and concerned citizens condemned the police and offered a memorial to Eric Saber, a by-stander construction worker, was said to have been shot and killed by the police on the aftermath of Baha sa Luneta September 11 anti-corruption protests.

Eric Q. Saber, a 35-year-old construction worker from [Paracale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paracale), [Camarines Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camarines_Norte), was going home from work in [Pasay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasay) when he was fatally shot during the riots.[^cite_note-Bulatlat-19][^cite_note-AMGudaAng-150] According to *[Bulatlat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulatlat)* and *AlterMidya*, he had been crossing Recto Avenue to ride a jeepney when SWAT officers fired shots in his direction, resulting in a stray bullet hitting his neck;[^cite_note-Bulatlat-19][^cite_note-AMGudaAng-150] a spinal injury caused by the bullet wound was later confirmed by forensic pathologist [Raquel Fortun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raquel_Fortun).[^cite_note-INQdeVilla-151] Saber was soon brought to the JRRMMC, but died two days later from pneumonia as a result of his gunshot wound.[^cite_note-AMGudaAng-150][^cite_note-INQdeVilla-151]

According to the police, 224 individuals were arrested during a protest against corruption, which resulted in 131 police officers and 57 civilians[^cite_note-921_protest_casualties-18] getting injured.[^cite_note-\:2-152] The MPD reported arrests at various locations: 51 at [Ayala Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayala_Bridge) (38 adults, 13 minors), 21 at Mendiola (14 adults, 7 minors), and 41 at Recto (13 adults, 28 minors). Among the injured police, eight had lacerations, with nine hospitalized for observation and one possibly needing admission for a nasal fracture, according to police.[^cite_note-gmanetwork1-153][^cite_note-\:0-21] Among the injured civilians, 48 were hospitized to Jose Reyes Memorial Medical Center.[^cite_note-PCO-139] According to the National Union of People's Lawyers (NUPL), police tortured detainees at the police tent at Mendiola and at the MPD headquarters.[^cite_note-154] The NUPL also said that police made several detainees beat up other detainees, while some detainees were being forced to give false confessions.[^cite_note-\:7-155]

The violent demonstrations were estimated to have resulted in [₱](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Peso)10.4 million ([US\$](18717338.md)211,167.51) in damage to both public infrastructure and private property.[^cite_note-156] The International Coalition for Human Rights in the Philippines described the mass arrests and violent dispersal as human rights violations.[^cite_note-157]

Karapatan stated on September 22 that detainees were not given food and water, with many of them injured and had no place to lie down. Police allegedly took bites out of hamburgers that lawyers brought for the children in detention. Karapatan said that police officers could be held liable under the Philippine Anti-Torture Act.[^cite_note-\:7-155] Some people were detained without charges for longer than the 36 hours that Philippine laws allow for arrests without warrants.[^cite_note-\:8-158]

A report by human rights group [Amnesty International](18947898.md) stated that police used "unnecessary and excessive force" and are guilty of torture and other human rights violations. A 20-year old protester testified that he was assaulted by plainclothes officers and later punched, kicked and beaten by police with truncheons until he passed out. He was again beaten in an ambulance until reporters arrived. Three teenage brothers who did not take part in the protest were beaten by police with truncheons and then accused of assaulting police.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-\:8-158]

Public Interest Law Center and Sentro para sa Tunay na Repormang Agraryo said that those arrested had bruises, fractures, and other signs of torture, and that some minors were coerced to beat other minors. Injured protesters who were arrested were not given medical treatment.[^cite_note-160]

The police subsequently issued subpoenas to student leaders as part of its investigation on the Mendiola protests, where police used sonic weapons, tear gas, and water cannons against protesters and passersby. Student council leaders and university officials condemned the police for issuing subpoenas and harassing students.[^cite_note-161] The NUPL said the subpoenas aim to threaten people to prevent them from exercising their right to free expression, peaceful assembly and redress of grievances".[^cite_note-162]

There were journalists who were assaulted by police or prevented from covering the September 11 protests. The Foreign Correspondents Association of the Philippines and the Presidential Task Force on Media Security protested these attacks against journalists.[^cite_note-163] [Commission on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commission_on_Human_Rights_\(Philippines\)) said it will probe reports of harassment against journalists and the use of police force on protesters.[^cite_note-164]

Virtual rallies on online game platforms such as *[Roblox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roblox)* were also organized by Filipino users.[^cite_note-165] The websites of 19 government agencies were defaced by hackers during the protests.[^cite_note-166]

Journalist [Manuel Mogato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Mogato) remarked that the violence along the areas was an isolated incident among the peaceful nationwide demonstrations, noting that both protesters and police authorities were markedly more restrained than during the dictatorial presidency of [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) in the 1970s and 1980s.[^cite_note-MM-142] [University of the Philippines Diliman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_Philippines_Diliman) journalism professor Danilo Arao did not just consider the Sep 21 rallies as culminating activities but also a time for all citizens, not just journalists, to carry out their patriotic duty to "fact check everything."[^cite_note-167]

In October 2025, human rights advocates and families of those arrested organized Alyansa laban sa Korapsyon at Brutalidad ng Pulis (AKAB) to support victims of [police brutality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_brutality) and to protest government corruption.[^cite_note-168]

A rally by PUP students, headed by SAMASA PUP and Anakbayan ang other national-democratic mass organizations was held by the gate of the university. They decried the General Apporiations Bill (GAB) or the proposed budget for fiscal year 2026. They stressed the unfinished and horrible conditions of facilities in the university. The students burned a large ₱500-bill bearing the faces of alleged corrupt politicians like President Marcos Jr. and Vice President Duterte.[^cite_note-293] The ₱500-bill was in response to the statement of [Department of Trade and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Trade_and_Industry_\(Philippines\)) [Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_Trade_and_Industry_\(Philippines\)) [Cristina Roque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristina_Aldeguer-Roque) that ₱500 is enough for a [Noche Buena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Eve) feast.[^cite_note-294]

Revolutionary groups, including Kabataang Makabayan, launched a 'lightning rally' along Rizal Avenue denouncing corruption and fascism on December 21. According to these groups, the national democratic revolution will end the corruption of the rotten system. A placard claimed how the AFP and PNP are the protectors of the corrupt, while the [New People's Army](1262096.md) protects the oppressed masses.[^cite_note-thecatalyst-59] On December 29, BAYAN and Makabayan-affiliated groups staged an anti-corruption protest outside of the House of Representatives on the last day of Congress session.

On [Rizal Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Day), KBKK and allied personalities led a [wreath laying ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreath_Laying_Ceremony) at the [Rizal Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Monument), [Rizal Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rizal_Park) in honor of the hero. The group posed with calls calling corruption a *social cancer*, an idea used by [José Rizal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Rizal) in his novel [Noli Me Tángere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noli_Me_T%C3%A1ngere_\(novel\)), to describe the social ills during his time.[^cite_note-295]

### January



KMP anti-corruption forum and mobilization, January 22.

On January 5, 2026, Lasallians Against Corruption conducted an anti-corruption protest inside DLSU-Manila.[^cite_note-296] Another protests occurred on January 15, organized by youth activists, including students of PUP, in front of the Department of Justice (DOJ) against corruption and the sedition case filed by the DOJ against PUP campus journalist Jacob Baluyot, among other accused, in relation to the September 21 protest at Mendiola. During the afternoon, PUP students also held a local mobilization inside the school.[^cite_note-297][^cite_note-298] On the following day, students of Ateneo de Manila University held a "Salubong 2026" rally to signal the first day of classes where they condemned corruption and called for the accountability and resignation of both President Marcos Jr. and Vice President Sara Duterte.[^cite_note-299]

BAYAN and Makabayan affiliated organizations filed an [impeachment](15328.md) complaint against President Marcos Jr due to allegations regarding corruption. One major content of the complaint was regarding the creation of the “BBM parametric formula,” a method that allocated the budget of public works towards legislative districts, a factor on the grounds on the '[betrayal of public trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betrayal_of_public_trust)'. The effort was signed by representatives of different organizations. The complaint was filed by the three Makabayan Representatives Tinio, Elago, and Co. The attempt was not fulfilled due to absence of House Secretary General Cheloy Garafil. The impeachment complaint was refiled on January 26. A mass mobilization was made in concurrence outside the House of Representatives on January 22.[^cite_note-300][^cite_note-301] During the afternoon, KMU held a forum and protest along Commission on Human Rights office condemning said corruption under President Marcos Jr. and Vice President Duterte.

Aldrin Kitsune, Secretary-General of Kalayaan Kontra Korapsyon dared the Marcos Jr. administration to jail and prosecute corrupt officials instead. This is in response to the [sedition](324091.md) case filed by the DOJ against Kitsune and other participants of the September 21 mobilization at Mendiola.[^cite_note-302] The group held a mobilization protest, together with students of [De La Salle–College of Saint Benilde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_La_Salle%E2%80%93College_of_Saint_Benilde) outside DOJ on January 26.[^cite_note-303]

### February



One Katipunan protest.

A ‘One Katipunan’ protest, involving student leaders and councils from schools along [Katipunan Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katipunan_Avenue), UP Diliman, Ateneo de Manila University, and Miriam College was conducted on February 20. Calling for the ouster of both Marcos and Duterte, it served as an anti-corruption protest prelude towards the 40th anniversary of the EDSA People Power headed by students, faculty, and other concerned citizens.

#### February 25



Baha Sa EDSA, February 25, 2026.

The 40th anniversary of the [EDSA People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_People_Power_Revolution) was marked by two major anti-corruption protests along the historic thoroughfare: church groups and civil society organizations affiliated with the liberal opposition mounted the third iteration of the [Trillion Peso March](81111441.md) along White Plains Avenue near the [People Power Monument](31029934.md), while various progressive groups and mass organizations led a separate mobilization dubbed *Baha Sa EDSA* (Flood in EDSA), held in the intersection of EDSA and Ortigas Avenue, near the [EDSA Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Shrine).

Despite initial talks for a joint and unified program, negotiations between organizers of the two protests reportedly fell through over disagreements on demands — especially regarding calls for the resignation and impeachment of both President Marcos and Vice President Duterte, as well as the establishment a People's Transition Council.[^cite_note-304] Only the Trillion Peso March was granted a permit by the Quezon City government, and tensions arose as law enforcers confiscated the truck stage to be used in the Baha Sa EDSA mobilization and attempted to block protesters from marching to the EDSA Shrine. Two activists were arbitrarily and forcefully arrested for allegedly “assaulting police officers,” which was condemned by progressive and rights groups such as BAYAN, Amnesty International, PAHRA, and SPARK.

### June

#### "White Ribbon" rally

On June 28, an anti-corruption protest organized by religious organizations and civil society groups, dubbed as the "White Ribbon March", was held along [EDSA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA) in [Quezon City](284223.md),[^cite_note-w628ft-312] calling on the government to address issues, mainly [the ongoing flood control controversy](80804811.md) and [the upcoming impeachment](82338655.md) of vice president [Sara Duterte](42504861.md).[^cite_note-w628ac-313][^cite_note-w628g-314]

The rally started with a [mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Mass) at the [EDSA Shrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Shrine),[^cite_note-w628ft-312][^cite_note-w628mb-315] presided over by [Kidapawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Kidapawan) Bishop [Jose Colin Bagaforo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jose_Colin_Bagaforo).[^cite_note-w628ac-313][^cite_note-w628g1-316] Thereafter, the participants, carrying white ribbons,[^cite_note-w628ft-312] marched towards the [People Power Monument](31029934.md)[^cite_note-w628mb-315] where the program, started at 2\:30 p.m., involved speeches from speakers.[^cite_note-w628ac-313]

The event, being an interfaith activity,[^cite_note-w628ft-312][^cite_note-w628mb-315] was joined by religious sectors including Catholics, Muslims, Protestants, Born Again Christians,[^cite_note-w628ft-312][^cite_note-w628mb-315] and the [Aglipayan Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aglipayan_Church).[^cite_note-w628g1-316] The [Iglesia ni Cristo](288343.md) (INC) was said invited to take part in the march.[^cite_note-w628g1-316] The protest was likewise attended by militant groups.[^cite_note-w628g1-316] Among those joined the event were [Makabayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makabayan) president [Liza Maza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liza_Maza), former legislators [Teddy Casiño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Casi%C3%B1o) and [France Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_Castro), and [Kontra Daya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kontra_Daya) convenor Danilo Arao.[^cite_note-w628ft-312] Meanwhile, at the march along EDSA, [SAGIP Partylist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAGIP_Partylist) representative [Paolo Marcoleta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paolo_Marcoleta) and former [Anakalusugan](69959027.md) representative [Mike Defensor](9457128.md), along with [Duterte supporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diehard_Duterte_Supporters) dressed in white, were seen. Marcoleta was reported going to the event only to drop off food; while Defensor had announced through [Facebook](7529378.md) on his presence.[^cite_note-w628g-314]

The [Quezon City Police District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quezon_City_Police_District) estimated the crowd at the People Power Monument at around 3,600,[^cite_note-w628mb-315] particularly at 3\:30 p.m.[^cite_note-w628ft-312]

#### June–July INC "We Are One" protests

In mid-2026, a three-day rally was organized by the INC in support of its member, senator [Rodante Marcoleta](68240498.md) (Paolo's father),[^cite_note-7226ac-317][^cite_note-7326ps-318] amidst announcement from the [Ombudsman](1866220.md) on an impending [plunder](209811.md) and indirect [bribery](260979.md) complaints against him before the [Sandiganbayan](8563865.md) over allegedly undeclared campaign contributions in [the 2025 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Philippine_Senate_election).[^cite_note-7126pdi-319]

The protest began at around 3 a.m. of June 30[^cite_note-6iq7k-320] with a surprise gathering[^cite_note-7326r-321] near the People Power Monument in Quezon City,[^cite_note-6iq7k-320] held without a permit from the local government, prompting a road closure[^cite_note-322] as portions of EDSA and White Plains were occupied.[^cite_note-7226pdi-323] Buses carrying church members later blocked the northbound path of EDSA including the [Carousel bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDSA_Carousel) lane;[^cite_note-324] and an attempt by the police to remove them led to a brief confrontation, injuring three police officers.[^cite_note-7126pdi-319] Four individuals were arrested but later released after a brief detention.[^cite_note-7226pdi-323] Until the following day, massive crowd around parts of EDSA continued to cause major traffic jams.[^cite_note-7326r-321][^cite_note-7326ps-318]

The crowd at the EDSA rallies, particularly near the People Power Monument, reached 15,000 by 9 p.m.[^cite_note-7326r-321] Meanwhile, simultaneous rallies at the EDSA Shrine and the [Liwasang Bonifacio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwasang_Bonifacio) in [Manila](184334.md) ended early.[^cite_note-63026i-325] Marcoleta appeared on the second day of the EDSA rally;[^cite_note-326] while several political allies likewise joined including Defensor who is also Marcoleta's alleged co-accused, senators [Alan Peter Cayetano](10220741.md) and [Imee Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imee_Marcos), Batangas representative [Leandro Leviste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandro_Leviste), and former Ilocos Sur governor [Chavit Singson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavit_Singson).[^cite_note-327][^cite_note-328]

The June 30 protest was allowed to continue until the Quezon City government granted a permit for the second day along White Plains Avenue.[^cite_note-7126pdi-319] Further continuation of the rally for July 2 was later granted but was revoked shortly after.[^cite_note-7226pdi-323] The main protest was then moved to Liwasang Bonifacio,[^cite_note-7226ac-317][^cite_note-7326ps-318] which was attended by senator [Robin Padilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Padilla) and former Presidential Spokesperson [Salvador Panelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Panelo).[^cite_note-7226ac-317] The crowd reportedly peaked at around 8,200 people by 8 p.m.[^cite_note-7226ac-317][^cite_note-7326ps-318] In a video message aired on [Net 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_25) after the program, INC spokesperson Edwil Zabala said that the rally had delivered its message, before formally announcing the end of their rally.[^cite_note-7326ps-318]

On July 3, the Ombudsman filed before the Sandiganbayan cases against Marcoleta, Defensor, and two businessmen, charging them with plunder and violating a decree that prohibits the providing of gifts to officials.[^cite_note-7326r-321][^cite_note-329] On July 6, the four were separately arrested upon orders from the Sandiganbayan.[^cite_note-330][^cite_note-331][^cite_note-332]
