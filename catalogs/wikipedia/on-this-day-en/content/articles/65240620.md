**Alpine Racing Limited**[^cite_note-13] ([/ˈælpin/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)), currently racing as **BWT Alpine Formula One Team** for sponsorship reasons,[^cite_note-14] is the name under which the [Enstone-based Formula One team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Enstone) has been competing since the start of the [2021 Formula One World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Formula_One_World_Championship).[^cite_note-15] Formerly named [Renault F1 Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_in_Formula_One) and owned by the French automotive company [Groupe Renault](162292.md) as well as [Renault–Nissan–Mitsubishi Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault%E2%80%93Nissan%E2%80%93Mitsubishi_Alliance), the team was rebranded for 2021 to promote Renault's sports car brand, [Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobiles_Alpine), and continued to serve as Renault's [works team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factory-backed),[^cite_note-Alpine-16] a position the team kept until Renault pulled out of Formula One (as an engine manufacturer) at the end of 2025.[^cite_note-17]

The team uses [Mercedes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercedes_AMG_High_Performance_Powertrains) engines, having previously done so as [Lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_F1_Team) in 2015. The team is based in [Enstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enstone), Oxfordshire, England and competes with a [French licence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_One_constructors#Team's_nationality).[^cite_note-18] The team fields [Pierre Gasly](40843850.md) and [Franco Colapinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Colapinto) as its full-time drivers. In its current form as Alpine, the team's sole win was achieved by [Esteban Ocon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteban_Ocon) at the [2021 Hungarian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Hungarian_Grand_Prix), and its first pole position was achieved by Gasly at the [2026 Italian Grand Prix](81006624.md).

## Background

### Origins of the team

Further information: [Toleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toleman), [Benetton Formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benetton_Formula), [Lotus F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_F1), and [Renault in Formula One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_in_Formula_One)

The team has a long history, first competing in Formula One in [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_Formula_One_World_Championship) as [Toleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toleman), when the team was based in [Witney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Witney), England.[^cite_note-the_race_20200906-19] In [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Formula_One_World_Championship), following its purchase by [Benetton Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benetton_Group), it was renamed and competed as [Benetton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benetton_Formula). As Benetton, it won the [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Formula_One_World_Championship) Constructors' Championship and its driver, [Michael Schumacher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Schumacher), won two Drivers' Championships in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Formula_One_World_Championship) and [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Formula_One_World_Championship).[^cite_note-bbc_679468-20] Prior to the [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Formula_One_World_Championship) season it moved to its current location in [Enstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enstone), UK.[^cite_note-pf_20140902-21]

By the [2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Formula_One_World_Championship) season, [Renault](162292.md) had purchased the team (for the first time), and by the [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Formula_One_World_Championship) season its name was changed to [Renault F1 Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_F1_Team), and it was racing as Renault.[^cite_note-gpt_2002-22] Renault won the Constructors' Championship in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Formula_One_World_Championship) and [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Formula_One_World_Championship) and its driver, [Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso) won the Drivers' Championships in the same two years.[^cite_note-f1_20200526-23] In [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Formula_One_World_Championship), [Lotus Cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_Cars) came on board as a sponsor, and the team's name changed to [Lotus Renault GP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_Renault_GP), though still racing as just "Renault" for that season.[^cite_note-f1n_20101208-24] By 2012, [Genii Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genii_Capital) had a majority stake in the team, and from 2012 until 2015 the team's name was [Lotus F1 Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_F1), after its branding partner, and it raced as "Lotus".

At the end of 2015, Renault had taken over the team for a second time, renaming it to [Renault Sport Formula One Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Sport_Formula_One_Team).[^cite_note-as_20160202-25][^cite_note-ms_201604-26] The team raced as "Renault" again, from [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Formula_One_World_Championship), and continued as such until the end of the [2020 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Formula_One_World_Championship).[^cite_note-F1Announcement-27] When discussing the history of the organisation as a whole rather than those of specific constructors it has operated, the colloquialism "*[Team Enstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Enstone)*" is generally used.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] The team operates in a 17,000 m<sup>2</sup> (180,000 sq ft) facility on a 17-acre (6.9 ha) site in Enstone.[^cite_note-31] By May 2023, Alpine had approximately 1,000 personnel in Enstone and 350 in [Viry-Châtillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viry-Ch%C3%A2tillon).[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

#### Early Formula One involvement

The involvement of the sportscar manufacturer [Automobiles Alpine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobiles_Alpine) in Formula One can be traced back to [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Formula_One_season), when the Alpine A350 Grand Prix car was built, powered by a [Gordini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordini) V8 engine. However, after initial testing with Mauro Bianchi at [Circuit Zandvoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Zandvoort), the project was ended when it was found that the engine produced around 300 horsepower (220 kW) compared to the [Cosworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosworth) V8 engines' 400.[^cite_note-34] In [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Formula_One_season), the company produced the Alpine A500 prototype to test a 1.5 L V6 turbo engine for the Renault factory team which would eventually début in [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Formula_One_season).[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

In September 2020, Groupe Renault announced their intention to use "Alpine" as their works team's new name going forward to promote the Alpine brand, and thus the team became known as the "Alpine F1 Team" whilst retiring the "Renault F1 Team" moniker after five years.[^cite_note-F1Announcement-27]

In June 2023, a group of investors, including actor [Ryan Reynolds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Reynolds), agreed to a deal to pay €200 million for a 24% stake in the Alpine Formula One team.[^cite_note-39] More investors joined the group later in 2023, including [NFL](21211.md) players [Patrick Mahomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Mahomes), [Travis Kelce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Kelce), golfer [Rory McIlroy](3949844.md), former heavyweight champion boxer [Anthony Joshua](33343240.md) and [footballers](10568.md) [Trent Alexander-Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_Alexander-Arnold) and [Juan Mata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Mata).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

## Ownership

In June 2023, Renault Group sold a 24% stake in Alpine Racing Ltd to a consortium led by Otro Capital, [RedBird Capital Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RedBird_Capital_Partners) and Maximum Effort Investments for €200 million.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Additional minority investors joined the group later in 2023, including NFL players [Patrick Mahomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Mahomes) and [Travis Kelce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travis_Kelce), golfer [Rory McIlroy](3949844.md), footballers [Trent Alexander-Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_Alexander-Arnold) and [Juan Mata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Mata), and former heavyweight boxing champion [Anthony Joshua](33343240.md).[^cite_note-44]

In 2025, Renault further adjusted Alpine Racing Ltd's ownership structure by selling an additional minority stake to new financial partners, reducing its shareholding to approximately 60%. The expanded investor group built on the earlier 2023 transaction and was intended to strengthen Alpine's commercial growth strategy and increase long-term Formula One investment capacity.[^cite_note-45] In 2026, it was reported that Otro Capital was looking to sell its stake in Alpine.[^cite_note-46]

## Racing history

### Rebranded Renault works team (2021–2025)

#### 2021 season



[Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso) driving the [A521](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_A521) at the [2021 British Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_British_Grand_Prix)

For 2021 Alpine signed two-time [World Champion](3437295.md) [Fernando Alonso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Alonso) to replace an outgoing [Daniel Ricciardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Ricciardo).[^cite_note-47] This would mark Alonso's third spell driving for [Team Enstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Enstone) having driven for them from [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Formula_One_World_Championship) to [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Formula_One_World_Championship) and again from [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Formula_One_World_Championship) to [2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Formula_One_World_Championship) and won two drivers championships with the team in 2005 and 2006 when the team used the Renault name.[^cite_note-48] [Esteban Ocon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteban_Ocon) was retained from the [2020 Renault team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_in_Formula_One).[^cite_note-49] The Alpine car used [Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_in_Formula_One) engines.[^cite_note-F1Announcement-27] Renault team boss, [Cyril Abiteboul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyril_Abiteboul), announced he would leave as Renault transitioned to Alpine.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Abiteboul was replaced by [Davide Brivio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Brivio_\(motorsport\)), who previously worked for [Suzuki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzuki_MotoGP) in [MotoGP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MotoGP).[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

Alpine's first race ended with Alonso being forced to retire, after debris caused his car to overheat.[^cite_note-54] Ocon was hit by [Aston Martin](63473867.md) driver, [Sebastian Vettel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Vettel).[^cite_note-55] Despite a disappointing start, Alpine scored in the next fifteen races,[^cite_note-56] including a victory for Ocon at the [2021 Hungarian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Hungarian_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-57]

It marked the first victory for a [French driver](37178186.md) driving for a [French team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Formula_One_constructors#Team's_nationality) since [Olivier Panis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivier_Panis)' triumph at the [1996 Monaco Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Monaco_Grand_Prix) driving for the [Ligier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89quipe_Ligier) team as well as the first victory for a French driver driving a French car powered by a [French engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_French_Formula_One_engine_manufacturers) since [Alain Prost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Prost)'s triumph at the [1983 Austrian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Austrian_Grand_Prix) driving a [Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_in_Formula_One) car.[^cite_note-FOM_Hungarian_GP_Facts_and_Stats_2021-58] Alonso also scored a podium in the [Qatar Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Qatar_Grand_Prix),[^cite_note-59] after qualifying fifth but starting third due to [Max Verstappen](41758713.md) and [Valtteri Bottas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valtteri_Bottas) gaining grid penalties.

#### 2022 season



[Esteban Ocon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteban_Ocon) driving the [A522](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_A522) at the [2022 Austrian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Austrian_Grand_Prix)

In January 2022, both team principal, [Marcin Budkowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcin_Budkowski),[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] and non-executive director, [Alain Prost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Prost), left their roles.[^cite_note-62] [Otmar Szafnauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otmar_Szafnauer), formerly of [Aston Martin F1 Team](63473867.md), was announced as the new team principal in the same month.[^cite_note-63] Former deputy secretary-general for sport at the [FIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA), Bruno Famin, has been recruited as executive director of Alpine at Viry-Châtillon, responsible for power-unit development.[^cite_note-64] Famin had also previously led [Peugeot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peugeot) to three consecutive [Dakar Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakar_Rally) victories as head of its sporting division from 2016 to 2018, and a [Le Mans 24 Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) triumph in 2009 as technical head of its endurance program.[^cite_note-65] [Oscar Piastri](59449712.md) replaced [Daniil Kvyat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniil_Kvyat) as test driver.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] In February 2022, [BWT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWT_\(company\)) became the title sponsor of the team, in a deal aimed at sustainability drive.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

Alonso qualified in second for the [Canadian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Canadian_Grand_Prix), only behind Verstappen.[^cite_note-70] This was his best qualifying position since the [2012 German Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_German_Grand_Prix),[^cite_note-71] though he suffered an issue in the race and finished in ninth.

Even though the team would fail to finish on the podium during the campaign, consistent points finishes from both drivers resulted in the French marque finishing a best ever (to this day) fourth in the Constructors’ standings.[^cite_note-72]

#### 2023 season



[Pierre Gasly](40843850.md) at the [2023 Austrian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Austrian_Grand_Prix)

Alonso moved to [Aston Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Martin_F1) for the [2023 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Formula_One_World_Championship) because he wanted a multi-year contract extension, and Alpine was only willing to give him one more year in F1.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] Alpine announced that Piastri would be replacing Alonso;[^cite_note-75] but Piastri immediately denied he had a contract to race with the team.[^cite_note-76] Team principal Szafnauer criticised Piastri, saying that Piastri should show more loyalty to Alpine,[^cite_note-77] and claimed that Piastri was thankful when told about his F1 promotion prior to Alpine's announcement.[^cite_note-78] In September 2022, a hearing of the [Contract Recognition Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA_Contract_Recognition_Board) determined that Alpine did not have a valid contract with Piastri[^cite_note-79] and was free to race with rival team [McLaren](20994.md) in 2023.[^cite_note-80] During the [2022 Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Japanese_Grand_Prix) weekend the team confirmed that [Pierre Gasly](40843850.md) has signed a multiple-year contract with them starting in 2023.[^cite_note-81]

In July 2023, [Laurent Rossi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurent_Rossi) was replaced by Philippe Krief as CEO.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83] During the [Belgian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Belgian_Grand_Prix) weekend (which incidentally would also see Gasly claim a P3 podium in the Sprint race), Alpine announced that Szafnauer and sporting director [Alan Permane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Permane) would leave the team after the race. Chief technical officer Pat Fry would also leave the team at the end of the year to join [Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Williams_Grand_Prix_Engineering).[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

Alpine finished the season sixth in the Constructors' Championship, while Gasly and Ocon were 11th and 12th, respectively, in the drivers' standings. Despite the two-place drop in position compared to the previous year, the squad still did manage to take a pair of solid podiums (one P3 apiece for Ocon and Gasly in Monaco and Zandvoort respectively). The team reportedly lost about half a second per lap on average because the Renault power unit was lagging behind its rivals.[^cite_note-\:0-86] Without this deficit, the team could have had performance similar to that of the [Mercedes](25098794.md) team.[^cite_note-\:0-86]

#### 2024 season

Alpine retained the driver pairing of Gasly and Ocon for the [2024 season](72504121.md). At the [Bahrain Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Bahrain_Grand_Prix), the team locked out the bottom of the grid in qualifying and finished the race in 17th and 18th.[^cite_note-Starting_grid-87][^cite_note-88] After the race, it was announced that Alpine's technical director [Matt Harman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Harman) and head of aerodynamics [Dirk de Beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirk_de_Beer) had left the team,[^cite_note-89] with the team moving to a structure of three technical directors, with Joe Burnell overseeing engineering, David Wheater for aerodynamics and [Ciaron Pilbeam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciaron_Pilbeam) for performance.[^cite_note-Technical_Directors-7] A few days later, it was reported that [Bob Bell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Bell_\(motorsport\)) will leave his advisory role at Alpine to reunite with Fernando Alonso at Aston Martin in an operational role.[^cite_note-90] On 2 May, [David Sanchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Sanchez_\(engineer\)), who had left McLaren a month earlier, was signed as executive technical director.[^cite_note-91] In May 2024, Alpine announced the controversial return of [Flavio Briatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavio_Briatore) to the team as an executive advisor. He previously worked at [Team Enstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Enstone) as team principal from 2000 to 2009, having previously resigned due to a [race-fixing scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_Formula_One_crash_controversy).[^cite_note-92]



Gasly at the [2024 Dutch Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Dutch_Grand_Prix)

After finishing out of the top ten four races later, Alpine scored their first points at the [Miami Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Miami_Grand_Prix) where Ocon finished tenth.[^cite_note-93] At the [Monaco Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Monaco_Grand_Prix), Ocon collided with Gasly during an overtake attempt in the first lap, resulting in the former's retirement from damage. Ocon was issued a five-place grid penalty at the [Canadian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Canadian_Grand_Prix)[^cite_note-94] and later apologised for his role in the incident. Team principal [Bruno Famin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Famin) commented that there would be "appropriate consequences".[^cite_note-95] A week later, Alpine announced that Ocon will leave the team at the end of the season following his contract running out.[^cite_note-96] During the [Belgian Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Belgian_Grand_Prix) weekend, Alpine announced that team principal Bruno Famin would leave his role as team principal. Famin is set to move to [Renault](162292.md)'s mainline motorsports divisions.[^cite_note-97] Famin's position was taken by [Oliver Oakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Oakes) from the [Dutch Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Dutch_Grand_Prix) onwards.[^cite_note-98] Alpine scored a double podium at the [2024 São Paulo Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_S%C3%A3o_Paulo_Grand_Prix) with Ocon finishing second and Gasly third behind only race winner and defending drivers champion [Max Verstappen](41758713.md) of Red Bull Racing, a result which moved Alpine from ninth to sixth in the Constructors' Championship ahead of the Haas, RB and Williams teams with three rounds remaining. This marked ["Team Enstone's"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Enstone) first double podium under the Alpine name and their first double double under any name since the [2013 Korean Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Korean_Grand_Prix) when the team was known as [Lotus F1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotus_F1).[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100]

The day after the [Qatar Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Qatar_Grand_Prix)—where Gasly finished 5th and Ocon DNFed due to a crash on the first lap—it was announced that Ocon would be released from the team early, prior to his scheduled move to [Haas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haas_F1_Team) in 2025. In his place, [Jack Doohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Doohan), who was already signed for the team in 2025, made an early debut for the last round of the season at the [2024 Abu Dhabi Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Abu_Dhabi_Grand_Prix).[^cite_note-101]

#### 2025 season

For [2025](72768531.md), Gasly was retained by the team for what will be Alpine's last season with a works Renault power unit. He was paired by [Jack Doohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Doohan), who has served as the team’s reserve driver in previous seasons was promoted to a full time race drive replacing [Esteban Ocon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteban_Ocon) who joined [Haas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haas_F1_Team).[^cite_note-102] Four drivers were selected to replace Doohan as test and reserve driver for the 2025 season, including [Paul Aron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Aron),[^cite_note-103] [Franco Colapinto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco_Colapinto),[^cite_note-104] [Ryō Hirakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ry%C5%8D_Hirakawa),[^cite_note-105] and [Kush Maini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kush_Maini).[^cite_note-106] Hirakawa left for Haas shortly after the [Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Japanese_Grand_Prix). [BP](18998720.md) and [Castrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castrol) left Alpine after eight years to supply fuels and lubricants to the impending [Audi entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audi_in_Formula_One), so [Eni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eni) and [Valvoline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valvoline) joined the team as official suppliers.



[Jack Doohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Doohan) at the [2025 Japanese Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Japanese_Grand_Prix)

The 2025 season started poorly for Alpine, with the team sitting bottom of the Constructors' Championship in 10th place after the first three rounds in [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Australian_Grand_Prix), [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Chinese_Grand_Prix) and [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Japanese_Grand_Prix), being the only team not to score a point in any of those three rounds.[^cite_note-107] Following a poor Melbourne weekend for the team, Doohan was involved in numerous wheel to wheel incidents with other drivers during the China weekend in both the Sprint and the Grand Prix which saw him pick up four penalty points on his FIA Super Licence after only two Grands Prix.[^cite_note-108] To make matters worse, Gasly was disqualified from the main Grand Prix result due to his [A525](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpine_A525) chassis being underweight. The Ferrari of Charles Leclerc was disqualified for the same infringement at the same race.[^cite_note-109] At the following round at the Japanese Grand Prix, having had to vacate his car to [Ryō Hirakawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ry%C5%8D_Hirakawa) for the first practice session that weekend, Doohan returned to the cockpit for the second practice session only for him to crash heavily after failing to close his car's DRS system before going through the first turn.[^cite_note-110] Doohan started and finished the race in Japan in 19th, while Gasly started 11th and finished 13th in the same event.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] The Japanese round also laid bare the continued deficencies of the Alpine's Renault power unit once more, with data suggesting the Alpines losing at best 0.6 seconds in the second sector at the lap at the Suzuka Circuit (which features two long straights), mostly because of the power deficit of their Renault engines.[^cite_note-113]

Following the [Miami Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Miami_Grand_Prix), team principal Oliver Oakes resigned, with Executive Advisor [Flavio Briatore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavio_Briatore) becoming the *de facto* team principal and racing director Dave Greenwood assuming Oakes' formal duties.[^cite_note-\:5-114] Doohan was also confirmed to have lost his Alpine seat to Colapinto on a "rotating seat" basis, with the latter scheduled to debut at the subsequent [Emilia Romagna Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Emilia_Romagna_Grand_Prix) and race for Alpine for five races. Doohan would be demoted to reserve driver in Colapinto's place.[^cite_note-115]

Even after bringing in Colapinto, Alpine's struggles continued throughout the season; Colapinto suffered a big crash in qualifying in Imola, and neither he nor Gasly was able to score points. Gasly suffered an early retirement in [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Monaco_Grand_Prix) after hitting old [AlphaTauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_AlphaTauri) teammate [Yuki Tsunoda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuki_Tsunoda), but managed to score a point in the subsequent [Spanish Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Spanish_Grand_Prix). The team recorded three further finishes in the points in [Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_British_Grand_Prix), [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Belgian_Grand_Prix), and [São Paulo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_S%C3%A3o_Paulo_Grand_Prix). However a pointless weekend at the [penultimate round in Qatar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Qatar_Grand_Prix) guaranteed that the team would finish bottom of the constructors' championship in their final year with a works engine, 46 points behind 9th placed Sauber with a maximum of 43 constructor points available at [the season finale in Abu Dhabi](77743503.md). Alpine in 2025 scored a record high number of points for a team finishing last in the constructors' championship.[^cite_note-116]

### Mercedes customer team (2026–present)

#### 2026 season

On 30 September 2024, owing to lack of strong results with its power unit during the V6 turbo-hybrid era since it began in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Formula_One_World_Championship), [Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renault_in_Formula_One) announced it would be ending its engine programme following the conclusion of the [2025](72768531.md) championship and would not be making engines for the new [2026](71681684.md) regulations.[^cite_note-117] This means Alpine lost its status as a works team and become a customer team of [Mercedes](25098794.md), whilst still being majority owned by Groupe Renault.[^cite_note-118] Alpine utilised Mercedes engines and gearboxes from the 2026 season onwards.[^cite_note-119]

For 2026, Alpine retained Gasly,[^cite_note-Formula_1-8] with Colapinto being signed for a full-time drive. After just 3 races, the Mercedes gearbox helped both drivers gain points, thus being a bigger improvement than 2025 so far.

This includes numerous points finishes, a short-lived podium for Gasly in Monaco (taken away months later due to the reinstatement of two five-second penalties he earned), and a shock first career pole position for him and his team at Monza. All of this has culminated in the squad currently sitting in a respectable P6 in the Constructors’ Championship.[^cite_note-120]

#### 2027 season

On 27 May 2026, it was announced that [Gucci](844136.md) would replace [BWT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BWT_\(company\)) as the title sponsor from [2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_Formula_One_World_Championship) onwards, competing as Gucci Racing Alpine Formula One Team.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122]
