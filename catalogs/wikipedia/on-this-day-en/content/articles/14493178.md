# Quasi-star

This article is about stars containing an internal black hole. For extremely luminous [active galactic nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_galactic_nuclei), see [quasars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quasars).



An artist's impression of a quasi-star, showing a black hole core residing in the massive photospheric envelope of the object

A **quasi-star**[^cite_note-czerny-1] or **quasistar**[^cite_note-formation-2] (**QS**), also called a **black hole star**,[^cite_note-begelman2025-3] is a [hypothetical type](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypothetical_star) of extremely massive and [luminous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminosity) [star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star) that may have existed early in the [history of the universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_universe). Unlike modern stars, which are powered by [nuclear fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion) in their cores, a quasi-star's [energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy) would come from material falling into a [black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_hole) at its core.[^cite_note-newsci-4] The formation of such an object would have resulted from the [core](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_core) of a large [supermassive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermassive_star) [protostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protostar) collapsing into a [stellar-mass black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stellar_black_hole), where the outer layers of the protostar are massive enough to absorb the resulting burst of energy without being blown away or falling into the black hole, as occurs in a [supernova](27680.md) event. As such they would resemble [red giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_giant) to an external observer, but enormously scaled-up and, as they were powered by an accreting central black hole, having luminosities comparable to a [Seyfert galaxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seyfert_galaxy)'s nucleus.[^cite_note-formation-2][^cite_note-volonteri-5]

Quasi-stars were first hypothesized in 2006;[^cite_note-formation-2] although a confirmed observation has not yet been made, potential sightings of these objects have been reported by the [James Webb Space Telescope](434221.md) since its launch,[^cite_note-reddots-6] with [little red dots](78148157.md) (LRDs), such as one called [The Cliff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cliff_\(astronomical_object\)) and [MoM-BH\*-1](83959037.md), as possible examples and candidates.[^cite_note-PhysOrg2025-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-naidu-9] The study of quasi-stars would provide valuable insight into the early [universe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universe), [galaxy formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galaxy_formation_and_evolution), and the behavior of black holes, namely because they are considered as possible progenitors of the modern [supermassive black holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermassive_black_hole) that formed soon after the [Big Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Bang).[^cite_note-formation-2]

## Structure

Although different models exist for the structure of a quasi-star, it is generally thought to consist of a spinning black hole consuming gas as fast as angular momentum losses will allow, surrounded by a star-like envelope of gas (which contains the bulk of the star's mass) held back by radiation pressure generated by the infalling gas.[^cite_note-dotan-10][^cite_note-ball-11][^cite_note-formation-2] This overlying envelope is highly convective and supported by [radiation pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_pressure). According to recent models, it may also become adiabatic during a quasi-star's late stage,[^cite_note-dotan-10][^cite_note-coughlin-12] with an inner saturated-convection region forming by that time, thereby conforming to a convection-dominated [accretion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accretion_\(astrophysics\)) flow around the black hole.[^cite_note-coughlin-12] The quasi-star would also have a porous atmosphere around the convective envelope, where convection becomes inefficient.[^cite_note-dotan-10] In a 2011 model for the structure of a [super-Eddington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddington_luminosity) quasi-star with masses over a million M<sub>☉</sub> where it would suffer substantial mass loss due to its extreme luminosity, its strong stellar wind appears as optically thick surface and hence appears as a cooler extended pseudo-[photosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosphere) above the porous atmosphere, where the [optical density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optical_density) of the wind drops to near zero, typically measured at a particular [Rosseland opacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opacity_\(optics\)#Planck_and_Rosseland_opacities) value such as 2⁄3.[^cite_note-dotan-10] Photon-tired winds – stellar winds intense enough that the wind luminosity is equivalent to the total available stellar luminosity – appear between both.[^cite_note-dotan-10][^cite_note-photon_tiring_limit-13]

A thin pre-galactic disk also surrounds the quasi-star, in which the star feeds it at rates from 2×10<sup>−3</sup> to a few of tens of M<sub>☉</sub> per year.[^cite_note-ball-11][^cite_note-dotan-10] The black hole within the core would rapidly accrete the surrounding gas from the envelope through a convectively or advectively dominated thick [accretion disk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accretion_disk) or a quasi-spherical accretion,[^cite_note-dotan-10] with at least 10<sup>−4</sup> to several M<sub>☉</sub> per year,[^cite_note-ball-11][^cite_note-czerny-1] possibly up to a highly super-Eddington accretion rate.[^cite_note-formation-2][^cite_note-dotan-10] The accretion rate onto the black hole energetically sustains the gaseous envelope,[^cite_note-coughlin-12] but its limit is often set by the [Eddington limit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eddington_limit) of the entire object, which is initially much larger than that of the black hole alone. The excess energy is carried away by convection.[^cite_note-ball-11] Analogous to [active galactic nuclei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_galactic_nucleus) and [gamma-ray bursts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamma-ray_bursts), the rotation of the quasi-star alongside the poloidal magnetic fields in the black hole or disk magnetosphere (or both) may mediate the production of a [relativistic jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysical_jet), and they may be transported from the outer regions to the center only thanks to thick accretion flows.[^cite_note-czerny-1] This would produce gamma rays in the reconfinement shocks formed within one-hundredth to one times the quasi-star's radius.[^cite_note-czerny-1]

[Hawking stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawking_star) are a theoretical type of star proposed by Stephen Hawking. They contain a [primordial black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primordial_black_hole) and were also formed in the early universe.[^cite_note-14] They differ from black hole stars in that the black hole is microscopic.

## Formation and properties



Size comparison of a hypothetical quasi-star to some of the [largest known stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_stars).

Formation of quasi-stars could only happen early in the development of the universe before [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) and [helium](13256.md) were contaminated by heavier elements; thus, they may have been very massive [Population III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_III) stars.[^cite_note-ball-11] A quasi-star would have resulted from the core of a [protostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protostar) of at least 1,000 [solar masses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_mass) (2.0×10<sup>33</sup> kg) collapsing into a black hole, where the outer layers of the protostar are massive enough to absorb the resulting [supernova](27680.md) without being blown away.[^cite_note-ball-11] Quasi-stars may have also formed from [dark matter halos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dark_matter_halo) drawing in enormous amounts of gas via gravity, which can produce [supermassive stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermassive_star) with tens of thousands of solar masses.[^cite_note-zeroing_in-15][^cite_note-cooking_up-16] In either case, quasi-stars would have ballooned to enormous sizes, proportional to      M  ∗   1  /  2     {\displaystyle M\_{\*}^{1/2}}  {\displaystyle M\_{\*}^{1/2}} and around 8,200 solar radii (38 AU) at 1000 M<sub>☉</sub>, dwarfing the [largest known modern stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_known_stars) and approaching the [Solar System](26903.md) in size.[^cite_note-spf01-17][^cite_note-reddots-6] They are predicted to have had surface temperatures higher than 4,000 K (3,730 °C). In the limit where the black hole is a small fraction of the overall mass, surface temperature scales as      M  BH   − 2  /  5    M  ∗   7  /  20     {\displaystyle M\_{\text{BH}}^{-2/5}M\_{\*}^{7/20}}  {\displaystyle M\_{\text{BH}}^{-2/5}M\_{\*}^{7/20}}.[^cite_note-bra08-18] At these temperatures, each one would be about as [luminous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luminosity) as a small galaxy.[^cite_note-newsci-4]

Once the black hole had formed at the core of a new quasi-star, it would continue generating a large amount of [radiant energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiant_energy) from the infall of stellar material. This constant outburst of energy would counteract the force of [gravity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity), creating an equilibrium similar to the one that supports modern fusion-based stars.[^cite_note-bra08-18] Quasi-stars would have had a short maximum lifespan, approximately 7 to 10 million years,[^cite_note-spf01-17] during which the core black hole would have grown to about 1,000–10,000 solar masses (2×10<sup>33</sup>–2×10<sup>34</sup> kg).[^cite_note-newsci-4][^cite_note-bra08-18]

As a quasi-star cooled over time, its outer envelope would become transparent, until further cooling to a limiting temperature of 4,000 K (3,730 °C).[^cite_note-bra08-18] This would mark the end of the quasi-star's life since there is no [hydrostatic equilibrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrostatic_equilibrium) at or below this limiting temperature.[^cite_note-bra08-18] It would then dissipate without a [supernova](27680.md), leaving behind an [intermediate-mass black hole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate-mass_black_hole),[^cite_note-bra08-18] although an extremely powerful supernova around 1×10<sup>48</sup> J may be observable if the initial mass was close to 55000 M<sub>☉</sub>.[^cite_note-spf01-17] These intermediate-mass black holes are theorized as the progenitors of modern [supermassive black holes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermassive_black_hole), and would help explain how supermassive black holes formed so early in the history of the universe.

## Candidates

### MoM-BH\*-1

[Rohan Naidu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohan_Naidu?action=edit\&redlink=1) et al. have claimed that the object [MoM-BH\*-1](83959037.md), formed 660 million years after the Big Bang and observed by the James Webb Space Telescope, is a black hole star.[^cite_note-black_hole_star-19] The light produced by the object is almost entirely at the red end of the spectrum, an effect attributed to light scattering from gas.[^cite_note-naidu-9] The model proposed for MoM-BH\*-1 may also explain other [little red dots](78148157.md) detected by the telescope.[^cite_note-20] At least 300 little red dots have been identified in Ultra Deep surveys, all of which may be quasi-stars.[^cite_note-little_red_dots-21]
