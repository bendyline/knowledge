**Bologna**[^cite_note-4] ([Bolognese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolognese_dialect): *Bulåggna*)[^cite_note-5] is the capital and largest city of the [Emilia-Romagna](162715.md) region in [northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy). It is the [seventh most populous city in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Italy), with 391,473 inhabitants from 150 different nationalities.[^cite_note-population-2][^cite_note-6] Its [metropolitan province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Bologna) is home to more than 1 million people as of 2026.[^cite_note-population-2] Bologna is most famous for being the home to the [oldest university in continuous operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_universities_in_continuous_operation),[^cite_note-Top_Universities-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-Hunt_Janin_2008-10][^cite_note-Ridder-Symoens_1992-11] the [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna), established in AD 1088.

The city has been an important urban center for centuries, first under the [Etruscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization) (who called it *Felsina*), then under the Celts as *Bona*, later under the Romans (*Bonōnia*), then again in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), as a free municipality and later *[signoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signoria)*, when it was among the [largest European cities by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_European_cities_in_history). Famous for its towers, churches and lengthy [porticoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porticoes_of_Bologna), Bologna has a well-preserved historical centre, thanks to a careful restoration and [conservation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Historical_conservation) policy which began at the end of the 1970s.[^cite_note-12] In 2000, it was declared [European capital of culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_capital_of_culture)[^cite_note-13] and in 2006, a [UNESCO](21786641.md) "City of Music" and part of the [Creative Cities Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Cities_Network).[^cite_note-14] In 2021, UNESCO recognized the lengthy [porticoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porticoes_of_Bologna) of the city as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-unesco-15][^cite_note-16]

Bologna is an important agricultural, industrial, financial and transport hub, where many large mechanical, electronic and food companies have their headquarters, and is also home to one of the largest permanent trade fairs in Europe. In 2022, *[Il Sole 24 Ore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Il_Sole_24_Ore)* named Bologna the best city in Italy for overall quality of life,[^cite_note-17] although it dropped to 4th place in 2025.[^cite_note-18] Bologna intends to become [carbon neutral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_neutral) by 2040 and raise female employment rates, focusing on sustainable and equitable urban development.[^cite_note-Bank-19] The city is also increasing its investment in sustainability as part of a 2022–2024 program that integrates gender perspectives into urban planning, with an emphasis on sustainable mobility, public infrastructure, and green spaces.[^cite_note-Bank-19]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Bologna).

### Antiquity and Middle Ages



The [*Due Torri*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Towers,_Bologna)

Traces of human habitation in the area of Bologna go back to the 3rd millennium BCE, with significant settlements from about the 9th century BCE ([Villanovan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villanovan_culture)). The influence of [Etruscan civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etruscan_civilization) reached the area in the 7th to 6th centuries, and the Etruscan city of *Felsina* was established at the site of Bologna by the end of the 6th century. By the 4th century BCE, the site was occupied by the [Gaulish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauls) [Boii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boii_\(Cisalpine_Gaul\)), and it became a Roman colony and *[municipium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipium)* with the name of *Bonōnia* in 196 BCE.[^cite_note-20] During the waning years of the Western Roman Empire Bologna was repeatedly sacked by the [Goths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goths). It is in this period that legendary Bishop [Petronius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petronius), according to ancient chronicles, rebuilt the ruined town and founded the [basilica of Saint Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Stefano,_Bologna).[^cite_note-21] Petronius is still revered as the patron saint of Bologna.

In 727–728, the city was sacked and captured by the [Lombards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) under [King Liutprand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Liutprand), becoming part of that kingdom. These Germanic conquerors built an important new quarter, called *addizione longobarda* (Italian meaning 'Longobard addition') near the complex of St. Stephen.[^cite_note-22] In the last quarter of the 8th century, [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne), at the request of [Pope Adrian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Adrian_I), invaded the Lombard Kingdom, causing its eventual demise. Occupied by Frankish troops in 774 on behalf of the papacy, Bologna remained under imperial authority and prospered as a frontier [mark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/March_\(territory\)) of the [Carolingian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolingian_Empire).[^cite_note-Kleinhenz-23]

Bologna was the center of a revived study of law, including the scholar [Irnerius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irnerius)[^cite_note-24] (c. 1050 – after 1125) and his famous students, the [Four Doctors of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Doctors_of_Bologna). Bologna boasts of having one of the oldest universities in the western world ([University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna)), with a continuous operation since 1088, and the first university in the sense of a higher-learning and degree-awarding institute.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-Hunt_Janin_2008-10][^cite_note-Ridder-Symoens_1992-11] The university originated as a centre for the study of [medieval Roman law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Roman_law) under major [glossators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossator), including Irnerius. It numbered [Dante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante), [Boccaccio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boccaccio) and [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch) among its students.[^cite_note-unibo-27] The medical school was especially renowned.[^cite_note-28] By 1200, Bologna was a thriving commercial and artisanal centre of about 10,000 people.[^cite_note-29]



Porta Maggiore, one of the twelve medieval city gates of Bologna



Depiction of a 14th-century fight between the [Guelf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelf) and [Ghibelline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghibelline) factions in Bologna, from the *Croniche* of Giovanni Sercambi of Lucca

After the death of [Matilda of Tuscany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilda_of_Tuscany) in 1115, Bologna obtained substantial concessions from Emperor [Henry V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor). However, when [Frederick Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa) subsequently attempted to strike down the deal, Bologna joined the [Lombard League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_League), which then defeated the imperial armies at the [Battle of Legnano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Legnano) and established an effective autonomy at the [Peace of Constance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Constance) in 1183. Subsequently, the town began to expand rapidly and became one of the main commercial trade centres of northern Italy thanks to a system of canals that allowed barges and ships to come and go.[^cite_note-30]

During a campaign to support the imperial cities of [Modena](62418.md) and [Cremona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cremona) against Bologna, [Frederick II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor)'s son, King [Enzo of Sardinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_of_Sardinia), was defeated and captured on 26 May 1249 at the [Battle of Fossalta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Fossalta). Although the emperor demanded his release, Enzo was thenceforth kept a knightly prisoner in Bologna, in a palace that came to be named [Palazzo Re Enzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Re_Enzo) after him. Every attempt to escape or to rescue him failed, and he died after more than 22 years in captivity.[^cite_note-31] After the death of his half-brothers [Conrad IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conrad_IV) in 1254, [Frederick of Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_of_Antioch) in 1256 and [Manfred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manfred,_King_of_Sicily) in 1266, as well as the execution of his nephew [Conradin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conradin) in 1268, he was the last of the [Hohenstaufen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hohenstaufen) heirs.

During the late 13th-century, Bologna was affected by political instability when the most prominent families incessantly fought for the control of the town. The free commune was severely weakened by decades of infighting, allowing the pope to impose the rule of his envoy Cardinal [Bertrand du Pouget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertrand_du_Pouget) in 1327. Du Pouget was eventually ousted by a popular rebellion and Bologna became a *[signoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signoria)* under Taddeo Pepoli in 1334.[^cite_note-Partner-32] By the arrival of the [Black Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Death) in 1348, Bologna had 40,000 to 50,000 inhabitants, reduced to just 20,000 to 25,000 after the plague.[^cite_note-33]

In 1350, Bologna was conquered by archbishop [Giovanni Visconti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Visconti_\(archbishop_of_Milan\)), the new lord of Milan. But following a rebellion by the town's governor, a renegade member of the Visconti family, Bologna was recovered by the papacy in 1363 by Cardinal [Gil Álvarez Carrillo de Albornoz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gil_%C3%81lvarez_Carrillo_de_Albornoz) after a long negotiation involving a huge indemnity paid to [Bernabò Visconti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernab%C3%B2_Visconti), Giovanni's heir, who died in 1354.[^cite_note-Partner-32] In 1376, Bologna again revolted against Papal rule and joined Florence in the unsuccessful [War of the Eight Saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Eight_Saints). However, extreme infighting inside the Holy See after the [Western Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Schism) prevented the papacy from restoring its domination over Bologna, so it remained relatively independent for some decades as an oligarchic republic. In 1401, [Giovanni I Bentivoglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_I_Bentivoglio) took power in a coup with the support of Milan, but, having turned his back on them and allied with Florence, the Milanese marched on Bologna and had Giovanni killed the following year. In 1442, [Hannibal I Bentivoglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annibale_I_Bentivoglio), Giovanni's nephew, recovered Bologna from the Milanese, only to be assassinated in a conspiracy plotted by [Pope Eugene IV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Eugene_IV) three years later. But the *signoria* of the [Bentivoglio family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentivoglio_family) was then firmly established, and the power passed to his cousin [Sante Bentivoglio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sante_Bentivoglio), who ruled until 1462, followed by [Giovanni II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_II_Bentivoglio). Giovanni II managed to resist the expansionist designs of [Cesare Borgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesare_Borgia) for some time, but on 7 October 1506, [Pope Julius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Julius_II) issued a bull deposing and [excommunicating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excommunicating) Bentivoglio and placing the city under [interdict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interdict). When the papal troops, along with a contingent sent by [Louis XII of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XII_of_France), marched against Bologna, Bentivoglio and his family fled. Julius II entered the city triumphantly on 10 November.

### Early modern



Bologna in 1640

The period of papal rule over Bologna (1506–1796) has been generally evaluated by historians as one of severe decline. However, this was not evident in the 1500s, which were marked by some major developments in Bologna. In 1530, [Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) [Charles V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_V,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) was [crowned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_the_Holy_Roman_Emperor) in Bologna, the last to be crowned by the pope. In 1564, the Piazza del Nettuno and the [Palazzo dei Banchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_dei_Banchi) were built, along with the [Archiginnasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archiginnasio), the main building of the university. The period of Papal rule saw also the construction of many churches and other religious establishments, and the restoration of older ones. At this time, Bologna had ninety-six convents, more than any other Italian city. Painters working in Bologna during this period established the [Bolognese School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolognese_School) which includes [Annibale Carracci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annibale_Carracci), [Domenichino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenichino), [Guercino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guercino), and others of European fame.[^cite_note-Raimond_Van_Marle_1924_pp_394-481-34]



Engraving of the city of Bologna from [Leandro Alberti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandro_Alberti)'s *History of Bologna*, 1590, showing the two surviving towers and several others

It was only towards the end of the 16th century that severe signs of decline began to manifest. A series of plagues in the late 16th to early 17th century reduced the population of the city from some 72,000 in the mid-16th century to about 47,000 by 1630. During the [1629–1631 Italian plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1629%E2%80%931631_Italian_plague) alone, Bologna lost up to a third of its population.[^cite_note-35] In the mid-17th century, the population stabilized at roughly 60,000, slowly increasing to some 70,000 by the mid-18th century. The economy of Bologna started to show signs of severe decline as the global centres of trade shifted towards the Atlantic. The traditional silk industry was in a critical state.[^cite_note-36] The university was losing students, who once came from all over Europe, because of the illiberal attitudes of the Church towards culture (especially after the [trial of Galileo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_affair)).[^cite_note-37] Bologna continued to suffer a progressive deindustrialisation also in the 18th century.[^cite_note-38]

In the mid-1700s, [Pope Benedict XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XIV), a Bolognese, tried to reverse the decline of the city with a series of reforms intended to stimulate the economy and promote the arts. However, these reforms achieved only mixed results. The pope's efforts to stimulate the decaying textile industry had little success, while he was more successful in reforming the tax system, liberalising trade[^cite_note-39] and relaxing the oppressive system of censorship.[^cite_note-40]

The economic and demographic decline of Bologna became even more noticeable starting in the second half of the 18th century. In 1790, the city had 72,000 inhabitants, ranking as the second largest in the Papal States; however, this figure had remained unchanged for decades.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-\:01-41]

During this period, Papal economic policies included heavy customs duties and concessions of monopolies to single manufacturers.[^cite_note-\:01-41]

### Modern history



Piazza del Nettuno in 1855, looking towards Piazza Maggiore

[Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) entered Bologna on 19 June 1796. Napoleon briefly reinstated the ancient mode of government, giving power to the [Senate of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Bologna), which however had to swear fealty to the short-lived [Cispadane Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cispadane_Republic), created as a [client state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Client_state) of the [French First Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_First_Republic) at the congress of Reggio (27 December 1796 – 9 January 1797) but succeeded by the [Cisalpine Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisalpine_Republic) on 9 July 1797, later by the [Italian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Republic_\(Napoleonic\)) and finally the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(Napoleonic\)). After the fall of Napoleon, the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna) of 1815 restored Bologna to the [Papal States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_States). Papal rule was contested in the [uprisings of 1831](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1830). The insurrected provinces planned to unite as the *[Province Italiane Unite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_Italiane_Unite)* with Bologna as the capital. [Pope Gregory XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Gregory_XVI) asked for [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) help against the rebels. [Metternich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metternich) warned French king [Louis Philippe I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Philippe_I) against intervention in Italian affairs, and in the spring of 1831, Austrian forces marched across the Italian peninsula, defeating the rebellion by 26 April.

By the mid-1840s, unemployment levels were very high and traditional industries continued to languish or disappear; Bologna became a city of economic disparity with the top 10 percent of the population living off rent, another 20 percent exercising professions or commerce and 70 percent working in low-paid, often insecure manual jobs. The Papal census of 1841 reported 10,000 permanent beggars and another 30,000 (out of a total population of 70,000) who lived in poverty.[^cite_note-42] In the [revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848) the Austrian garrisons which controlled the city on behalf of the pope were temporarily expelled, but eventually came back and crushed the revolutionaries.

Papal rule finally ended in the aftermath of [Second War of Italian Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_War_of_Italian_Independence), when the French and Piedmontese troops expelled the Austrians from Italian lands, on 11 and 12 March 1860, Bologna voted to join the new [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy). In the last decades of the 19th century, Bologna once again thrived economically and socially. In 1863 Naples was linked to Rome by railway, and the following year Bologna to Florence.[^cite_note-43] Bolognese moderate agrarian elites, that supported liberal insurgencies against the papacy and were admirers of the British political system and of free trade, envisioned a unified national state that would open a bigger market for the massive agricultural production of the Emilian plains.[^cite_note-44] Indeed, Bologna gave Italy one of its first prime ministers, [Marco Minghetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Minghetti).

After [World War I](4764461.md), Bologna was heavily involved in the [Biennio Rosso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biennio_Rosso) socialist uprisings. As a consequence, the traditionally moderate elites of the city turned their back on the progressive faction and gave their support to the rising [Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Fascism) movement of [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini).[^cite_note-45] [Dino Grandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_Grandi), a high-ranking Fascist party official and Ministry of Foreign Affairs, remembered for being an Anglophile, was from Bologna. During the [interwar years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_years), Bologna developed into an important manufacturing centre for food processing, agricultural machinery and metalworking. The Fascist regime poured in massive investments, for example with the setting up of a giant tobacco manufacturing plant in 1937.[^cite_note-46]

#### World War II



Sappers of the 136 Indian Railway Maintenance Company repair some of the extensive damage to the railyards in 1945.

Bologna suffered extensive damage during [World War II](32927.md). The strategic importance of the city as an industrial and railway hub connecting northern and central Italy made it [a target for the Allied forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Bologna_in_World_War_II). On 24 July 1943, a massive aerial bombardment destroyed a significant part of the historic city centre and killed about 200 people. The main railway station and adjoining areas were severely hit, and 44% of the buildings in the centre were listed as having been destroyed or severely damaged. The city was heavily bombed again on 25 September. The raids, which this time were not confined to the city centre, left 2,481 people dead and 2,000 injured.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] By the end of the war, 43% of all buildings in Bologna had been destroyed or damaged.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

After the [armistice of 1943](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Cassibile), the city became a key centre of the [Italian resistance movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_resistance_movement). On 7 November 1944, a pitched battle around [Porta Lame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Lame,_Bologna), waged by partisans of the 7th Brigade of the *Gruppi d'Azione Patriottica* against [Fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Social_Republic) and Nazi occupation forces, did not succeed in triggering a general uprising, despite being one of the largest resistance-led urban conflicts in the European theatre.[^cite_note-51] Resistance forces entered Bologna on the morning of 21 April 1945. By this time, the Germans had already largely left the city in the face of the Allied advance, spearheaded by Polish forces advancing from the east during the [Battle of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bologna) which had been fought since 9 April. First to arrive in the centre was the 87th Infantry Regiment of the Friuli Combat Group under general [Arturo Scattini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arturo_Scattini), who entered the centre from *Porta Maggiore* to the south. Since the soldiers were dressed in British outfits, they were initially thought to be part of the allied forces; when the local inhabitants heard the soldiers were speaking Italian, they poured out onto the streets to celebrate.

#### Cold War period



Aftermath of the 1980 terrorist bombing

In the post-war years, Bologna became a thriving industrial centre as well as a political stronghold of the [Italian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Communist_Party). Between 1945 and 1999, the city was helmed by an uninterrupted succession of [mayors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Bologna) from the PCI and its successors, the [Democratic Party of the Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_of_the_Left) and [Democrats of the Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democrats_of_the_Left), the first of whom was [Giuseppe Dozza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Dozza). At the end of the 1960s the city authorities, worried by massive [gentrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentrification) and suburbanisation, asked Japanese [starchitect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starchitect) [Kenzo Tange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenzo_Tange) to sketch a master plan for a [new town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_town) north of Bologna; however, the project that came out in 1970 was evaluated as too ambitious and expensive.[^cite_note-52] Eventually the city council, in spite of vetoing Tange's master plan, decided to keep his project for a [new exhibition centre and business district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiera_District).[^cite_note-53] At the end of 1978 the construction of a tower block and several diverse buildings and structures started.[^cite_note-54] In 1985 the headquarters of the regional government of [Emilia-Romagna](162715.md) moved in the new district.[^cite_note-55]

In 1977, Bologna was the scene of [rioting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_1977#The_riots) linked to the [Movement of 1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movement_of_1977), a spontaneous political movement of the time. The police shooting of a far-left activist, [Francesco Lorusso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Lorusso), sparked two days of street clashes. On 2 August 1980, at the height of the "[years of lead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Years_of_Lead_\(Italy\))", a terrorist bomb was set off in the central railway station of Bologna killing 85 people and wounding 200, an event which is known in Italy as the [Bologna massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_massacre). In 1995, members of the [neo-fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neo-fascist) group [Nuclei Armati Rivoluzionari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclei_Armati_Rivoluzionari) were convicted for carrying out the attack, while [Licio Gelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Licio_Gelli)—Grand Master of the underground Freemason lodge [Propaganda Due](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propaganda_Due) (P2)—was convicted for hampering the investigation, together with three agents of the secret military intelligence service [SISMI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SISMI) (including [Francesco Pazienza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Pazienza) and [Pietro Musumeci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Musumeci)). Commemorations take place in Bologna on 2 August each year, culminating in a concert in the main square.

#### 21st century

In 1999, the long tradition of left-wing mayors was interrupted by the victory of independent centre-right candidate [Giorgio Guazzaloca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Guazzaloca). However, Bologna reverted to form in 2004 when [Sergio Cofferati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Cofferati), a former trade union leader, unseated Guazzaloca. The next centre-left mayor, [Flavio Delbono](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavio_Delbono), elected in June 2009, resigned in January 2010 after being involved in a corruption scandal. After a 15-month period in which the city was administered under [Anna Maria Cancellieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anna_Maria_Cancellieri) (as a state-appointed [prefect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prefect#Modern_sub-national_administration)), [Virginio Merola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virginio_Merola) was elected as mayor, leading a left-wing coalition comprising the [Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Italy\)), [Left Ecology Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Ecology_Freedom) and [Italy of Values](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_of_Values).[^cite_note-56] In [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Bologna_municipal_election), Merola was confirmed mayor, defeating the conservative candidate, [Lucia Borgonzoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucia_Borgonzoni). In 2021, after ten years of Merola's mayorship, one of his closest allies, [Matteo Lepore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Lepore), [was elected mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Bologna_municipal_election) with 61.9% of votes, becoming the most voted mayor of Bologna since the introduction of the direct elections in 1995.[^cite_note-ansa.it-57]

## Geography



Aerial photograph of Bologna (north facing on the right)

Bologna is situated on the edge of the [Po Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Plain) at the foot of the [Apennine Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apennine_Mountains), at the meeting of the [Reno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reno_\(river\)) and [Savena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savena) river valleys. As Bologna's two main watercourses flow directly to the sea, the town lies outside of the drainage basin of the [River Po](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Po). The [province of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Bologna) stretches from the western edge of the Po Plain on the border with [Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara) to the Tuscan-Emilian Apennines. The centre of the town is 54 metres (177 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level) (while elevation within the municipality ranges from 29 metres (95 ft) in the suburb of Corticella to 300 metres (980 ft) in Sabbiuno and the Colle della Guardia). The province of Bologna stretches from the Po Plain into the Apennines; the highest point in the province is the peak of Corno alle Scale (in [Lizzano in Belvedere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lizzano_in_Belvedere)) at 1,945 metres (6,381 ft) above sea level.

## Government

See also: [Mayor of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Bologna)

### Municipal government

The legislative body of the [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comune) is the City Council (*Consiglio Comunale*), which is composed by 48 councillors elected every five years with a corrected proportional system (granting the majority to the list or alliance of lists which receives more votes), contextually to the mayoral elections. The executive body is the City Committee (*Giunta Comunale*), composed by 12 [assessors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assessor_\(Italy\)), that is nominated and presided over by a directly elected [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Bologna). The current mayor of Bologna is Matteo Lepore ([PD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_\(Italy\))), elected on 4 October 2021 with 61.9% of the votes.[^cite_note-ansa.it-57]

The municipality of Bologna is subdivided into six administrative boroughs (*quartieri*), down from the former nine before the 2015 administrative reform. Each borough is governed by a council (*Consiglio*) and a president, elected contextually to the city mayor. The urban organization is governed by the Italian Constitution (art. 114). The boroughs have the power to advise the mayor with nonbinding opinions on a large spectrum of topics (environment, construction, public health, local markets) and exercise the functions delegated to them by the City Council; in addition, they are supplied with an autonomous founding to finance local activities.

### Provincial and regional government



Fiera District, seat of the regional government of Emilia-Romagna

Bologna is the capital of the eponymous metropolitan city and of [Emilia-Romagna](162715.md), one of the twenty [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) of Italy. While the province of Bologna has a population of 1,007,644,[^cite_note-69] making it the twelfth most populated province of Italy, Emilia-Romagna ranks as the sixth most populated region of Italy, with about 4.5 million inhabitants, more than 7% of the national total. The seat of the regional government is [Fiera District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiera_District), a tower complex designed by Japanese architect [Kenzo Tange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenzo_Tange) in 1985.

According to the last governmental dispositions concerning administrative reorganisation, the urban area of Bologna is one of the 15 [metropolitan municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_cities_of_Italy) (*città metropolitane*), new administrative bodies fully operative since 1 January 2015.[^cite_note-70] The new Metro municipalities, giving large urban areas the administrative powers of a province, are conceived for improving the performance of local administrations and to slash local spending by better co-ordinating the municipalities in providing basic services (including transport, school and social programs) and environment protection.[^cite_note-Ferri-71] In this policy framework, the mayor of Bologna is designated to exercise the functions of a metropolitan mayor (*sindaco metropolitano*), presiding over a Metropolitan Council formed by 18 mayors of municipalities within the Metro municipality.

The Metropolitan City of Bologna is headed by the metropolitan mayor (*sindaco metropolitano*) and by the Metropolitan Council (*Consiglio metropolitano*). Since 11 October 2021 [Matteo Lepore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matteo_Lepore), as mayor of the capital city, has been the mayor of the Metropolitan City.

The [porticoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porticoes_of_Bologna) of Bologna are a symbol of the city.

Until the late 19th century, when a large-scale urban renewal project was undertaken, Bologna was one of the few remaining large walled cities in Europe; to this day and despite having suffered considerable bombing damage in 1944, Bologna's 142 hectares (350 acres) historic centre is Europe's second largest,[^cite_note-72] containing an immense wealth of important medieval, renaissance, and baroque artistic monuments.

Bologna developed along the [Via Emilia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Emilia) as an Etruscan and later Roman colony; the Via Emilia still runs straight through the city under the changing names of Strada Maggiore, Rizzoli, Ugo Bassi, and San Felice. Due to its Roman heritage, the central streets of Bologna, today largely pedestrianized, follow the grid pattern of the Roman settlement. The original Roman ramparts were supplanted by a high medieval system of fortifications, remains of which are still visible, and finally by a third and final set of ramparts built in the 13th century, of which numerous sections survive. No more than twenty medieval defensive towers remain out of up to 180 that were built in the 12th and 13th centuries before the arrival of unified civic government. The most famous of the [towers of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Towers_of_Bologna) are the central *Due Torri* (*Asinelli* and *Garisenda*), whose iconic leaning forms provide a popular symbol of the town.[^cite_note-73]

The cityscape is further enriched by its elegant and extensive [porticoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porticoes_of_Bologna), for which the city is famous. In total, there are some 38 kilometres (24 miles) of [porticoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portico) in the city's historical centre[^cite_note-unesco-15] (over 45 km (28 mi) in the city proper), which make it possible to walk for long distances sheltered from the elements.

The Portico di San Luca is possibly the world's longest.[^cite_note-74] It connects [Porta Saragozza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porta_Saragozza) (one of the twelve gates of the ancient walls built in the Middle Ages, which circled a 7.5 km (4.7 mi) part of the city) with the [Sanctuary of the Madonna di San Luca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madonna_di_San_Luca,_Bologna), a church begun in 1723 on the site of an 11th century edifice which had already been enlarged in the 14th century, prominently located on a hill (289 metres (948 feet)) overlooking the town, which is one of Bologna's main landmarks. The winding 666 vault arcades, almost four kilometres (3,796 m or 12,454 ft) long, effectively links *San Luca*, as the church is commonly called, to the city centre. Its porticos provide shelter for the traditional procession which every year since 1433 has carried a Byzantine icon of the Madonna with Child attributed to [Luke the Evangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_the_Evangelist) down to the [Bologna Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_Cathedral) during the [Feast of the Ascension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_the_Ascension).[^cite_note-unesco-15]

In 2021, the porticoes were named as a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-unesco-15]



View from Via Pescherie Vecchie of San Petronio Basilica showing the different building materials used at different times in its construction

[San Petronio Basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Petronio_Basilica), built between 1388 and 1479 (but still unfinished), is the tenth-largest church in the world by volume, 132 metres long and 66 metres wide, while the vault reaches 45 metres inside and 51 metres in the facade. With its volume of 258,000 m<sup>3</sup>, it is the largest (Gothic or otherwise) church built of bricks of the world.[^cite_note-75] The Basilica of [Saint Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Stefano,_Bologna) and its sanctuary are among the oldest structures in Bologna, having been built starting from the 8th century, according to the tradition on the site of an ancient temple dedicated to Egyptian goddess [Isis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isis). The [Basilica of Saint Dominic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_San_Domenico) is an example of Romanic architecture from the 13th century, enriched by the monumental tombs of great Bolognese [glossators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossator) Rolandino de'Passeggeri and Egidio Foscherari. Basilicas of [St Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francesco,_Bologna), [Santa Maria dei Servi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Maria_dei_Servi,_Bologna) and [San Giacomo Maggiore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Giacomo_Maggiore) are other magnificent examples of 14th century architecture, the latter also featuring Renaissance artworks such as the [Bentivoglio Altarpiece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bentivoglio_Altarpiece) by [Lorenzo Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Costa). Finally, the Church of [San Michele in Bosco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Michele_in_Bosco) is a 15th century religious complex located on a hill not far from the city's historical center.



View from the top of the Basilica di San Petronio: the dome of Santuario di Santa Maria della Vita dominates the foreground; the *Asinelli* (higher) and *Garisenda* towers (*Due Torri*) are seen on the right.

## Culture



The [International museum and library of music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_museum_and_library_of_music) displays ancient musical instruments and unique musical scores from the 16th to the 20th centuries.

Over the centuries, Bologna has acquired many nicknames: "the fat" (*la grassa*) refers to its cuisine, in which the most famous specialities are prepared using rich meats (especially pork), egg pasta and dairy products, such as butter and Parmesan. Another nickname that has been given to the city is "the red" (*la rossa*), which was originally used as a reference to the colour of the buildings in the city centre, has later become connected with the communist ideology supported by the majority of the population, in particular after World War II: until the election of a centre-right mayor in 1999, the city was renowned as a bastion of the [Italian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Communist_Party).[^cite_note-76] The centre-left regained power again in the 2004 mayoral elections, with the election of [Sergio Cofferati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Cofferati). It was one of the first European cities to experiment with the concept of [free public transport](11163017.md).[^cite_note-77] Bologna has also two other nicknames: the first one, "the towered" (*la turrita*) refers to the high number of medieval towers that can be found in the city, even if today only 24 towers are still standing. The second one, "the learned" (*la dotta*) is a reference to its university.

### University

Bologna's [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna) was founded in 1088 and it is considered the oldest university in the world. According to the QS University Rankings, Bologna university is the 3rd-ranked Italian university and the 138th-ranked in the world.

The large number of students coming from all over Italy and the world (there are several campuses of foreign universities in Bologna, including Johns Hopkins University, Dickinson College, Indiana University, Brown University, the University of California and more) has a considerable effect on everyday life. While it contributes to livening up the city centre (an area in which the average age of the residents is very high) and it also helps to promote cultural initiatives, on the other hand, it creates public order and waste management problems that stem from the lively nightlife of the university district.

### Entertainment and performing arts



Façade of Arena del Sole theatre

The city of Bologna became a [UNESCO](21786641.md) City of Music on 26 May 2006. According to UNESCO, "As the first Italian city to be appointed to the Network, Bologna has demonstrated a rich musical tradition that is continuing to evolve as a vibrant factor of contemporary life and creation. It has also shown a strong commitment to promoting music as an important vehicle for inclusion in the fight against racism and in an effort to encourage economic and social development. Fostering a wide range of genres from classical to electronic, jazz, folk and opera, Bologna offers its citizens a musical vitality that deeply infiltrates the city's professional, academic, social and cultural facets."[^cite_note-78]

The theatre was a popular form of entertainment in Bologna until the 16th century. The first public theatre was the Teatro alla Scala, active since 1547 in [Palazzo del Podestà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_del_Podest%C3%A0,_Bologna). An important figure of Italian Bolognese theatre was [Alfredo Testoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfredo_Testoni), the playwright and author of *[Cardinal Lambertini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_Lambertini_\(play\))*, which has had great theatrical success since 1905, repeated on screen by the Bolognese actor [Gino Cervi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gino_Cervi). In 1998, the City of Bologna initiated the project "Bologna dei Teatri" ('Bologna of the Theatres'), an association of the major theatrical facilities in the city. This is a circuit of theatres which offer diverse theatrical opportunities, ranging from Bolognese dialect to contemporary dance, but with a communications strategy and promoting unity. Specifically, the shows on the bill in various theatres participating in the project are advertised weekly through a single poster. Bologna's opera house is the [Teatro Comunale di Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_Comunale_di_Bologna). The [Orchestra Mozart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchestra_Mozart), whose music director was [Claudio Abbado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Abbado) until his death in 2014, was created in 2004.

Bologna hosts a number of international music, art, dance and film festivals, including Angelica,[^cite_note-79] *Bologna* and *Contemporanea* (festivals on contemporary music),[^cite_note-80] Bolognafestival (international classical music festival),[^cite_note-81] Bologna Jazz Festival,[^cite_note-82] Biografilm Festival (devoted to biographical movies),[^cite_note-83] BilBolBul (a comics festival),[^cite_note-84] Danza Urbana (a street contemporary dance festival),[^cite_note-85] [F.I.S.Co](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F.I.S.Co.) (festival on contemporary art, now merged into [Live Arts Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Arts_Week)), Future Film Festival (animation and special effects),[^cite_note-86] *Il Cinema Ritrovato* (film festival about rare and forgotten movies),[^cite_note-87] [Live Arts Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_Arts_Week), Gender Bender (festival on gender identity, sexual orientation, and body representation),[^cite_note-88] Homework festival (electronic music festival),[^cite_note-89] Human Rights Film Festival,[^cite_note-90] Some Prefer Cake (lesbian film festival),[^cite_note-91] [Zecchino d'Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zecchino_d'Oro) (a children's song contest).

### Cuisine



*Tagliatelle al ragù*, as served in Bologna

Bologna is renowned for its culinary tradition. It is the home of the famous [Bolognese sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolognese_sauce), a meat-based pasta sauce. In Italy, it is called [ragù](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rag%C3%B9) and is substantially different from the variety found worldwide. In Bologna, the sauce is served primarily with [tagliatelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagliatelle), and serving it with [spaghetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaghetti) is considered odd.[^cite_note-92]

Situated in the fertile [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley), the rich local cuisine depends heavily on meats and cheeses. As in all of Emilia-Romagna, the production of [cured pork meats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cured_pork_tenderloin) such as [prosciutto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prosciutto), [mortadella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mortadella) and *[salumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salumi)* is an important part of the local food industry.[^cite_note-93] Well-regarded nearby vineyards include [Pignoletto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pignoletto?action=edit\&redlink=1) dei Colli Bolognesi, [Lambrusco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lambrusco) di Modena and [Sangiovese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangiovese) di Romagna.[^cite_note-94] Tagliatelle with ragù, [tortellini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortellini) served in broth, [lasagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasagna) and mortadella Bologna are among the local specialties.[^cite_note-95]

Traditional Bolognese desserts are often linked to holidays, such as *fave dei morti*, multi-coloured [almond paste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond_paste) cookies made for [All Saints' Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Saints'_Day), jam-filled raviole cookies that are served on [Saint Joseph's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph's_Day), and carnival sweets known as *sfrappole*, a light and delicate fried pastry topped with powder sugar, *certosino* or *panspeziale* ('carthusian' or 'apothecary-cake'), a spicy cake served on Christmas. *Torta di riso*, a custard-like cake made of almonds, rice and amaretto, is made throughout the year,[^cite_note-96] as well as [zuppa inglese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zuppa_inglese).

## Economy



[Unipol Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unipol_Tower), at 127 m (417 ft), is the city's tallest building.

In terms of total GDP, the [Metropolitan City of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Bologna) generated a value of about €35 billion (\$40.6 billion) in 2017, equivalent to €34,251 (\$40,165) per capita, the third highest figure among Italian provinces (after [Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Milan) and [Bolzano/Bozen](169611.md)).[^cite_note-97]

The economy of Bologna is characterized by a flourishing industrial sector, traditionally centered on the transformation of agricultural and zootechnical products ([Eridania](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eridania), [Granarolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granarolo_\(company\)), [Segafredo Zanetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segafredo_Zanetti), [Conserve Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conserve_Italia?action=edit\&redlink=1)), machinery ([Coesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coesia?action=edit\&redlink=1), [IMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMA_\(company\)), [Sacmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacmi)), construction equipment ([Maccaferri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maccaferri)); energy ([Hera Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hera_Group)), automotive ([Ducati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ducati), [Lamborghini](18271.md)), footwear, textile, engineering, chemical, printing and publishing ([Cappelli](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappelli%20\(editore\)), [il Mulino](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/il%20Mulino), [Monrif Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monrif_Group?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Zanichelli](https://it.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanichelli)).

In particular, Bologna is considered the centre of the so-called "packaging valley", an area well known for its high concentration of firms specialised in the manufacturing of automatic [packaging machines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packaging_machine) ([Coesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coesia?action=edit\&redlink=1), [IMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMA_\(company\))).[^cite_note-98] Furthermore, Bologna is well known for its dense network of [cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooperative), a feature that dates back to the social struggles of farmers and workers in the 1800s and that today produces up to a third of its GDP[^cite_note-99] and occupies 265,000 people in the Emilia-Romagna region.[^cite_note-100]

## Transport



A [Trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) of the [urban trolleybus network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Bologna) managed by [TPER](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TPER), photographed in Via Saffi

The [Bologna Guglielmo Marconi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_Guglielmo_Marconi_Airport) is the [seventh busiest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_busiest_airports_in_Europe) Italian airport for passenger traffic (11 million passengers in 2025).

[Bologna Centrale railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_Centrale_railway_station) is one of Italy's most important train hubs thanks to the city's strategic location as a crossroad between north–south and east–west routes. It serves 58 million passengers annually.[^cite_note-gs1-101] The city hosts several minor railway stations (see [List of railway stations in Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_railway_stations_in_Bologna)).

Bologna San Donato [classification yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classification_yard), with 33 railway tracks, used to be the largest freight hub in Italy by size and traffic.[^cite_note-102] Since 2018, it has been repurposed as the [Bologna San Donato railway test circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_San_Donato_railway_test_circuit).[^cite_note-I_treni_434-103]

The city is also served by a large network of public bus lines, including [trolleybus lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybuses_in_Bologna), operated since 2012 by [Trasporto Passeggeri Emilia-Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trasporto_Passeggeri_Emilia-Romagna) (TPER).

As of May 2023, the first line of the new [Bologna tramway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_tramway) is under construction.[^cite_note-avviolavori-104] Overall, a four-line tramway network is planned.[^cite_note-105]

The large [commuter rail](6286.md) service centred on Bologna is branded as the [Bologna metropolitan railway service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_metropolitan_railway_service).

### Public transport statistics

The average length of time people spend commuting with public transit in Bologna, for example to and from work, on a weekday is 53 min. 9% of public transit riders ride for more than 2 hours every day. The average length of time people wait at a stop or station for public transit is 12 min, while 16% of riders wait for over 20 minutes on average every day. The average distance people usually ride in a single trip with public transit is 5.4 km (3.4 mi), while 7% travel for over 12 km (7.5 mi) in a single direction.[^cite_note-106]

## Education



[Bologna University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_University), established in AD 1088, is the [world's oldest university in continuous operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_universities_in_continuous_operation).

The [University of Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Bologna), conventionally said to have been founded in 1088 by [glossators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glossator) [Irnerius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irnerius) and [Pepo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepo_\(jurist\)),[^cite_note-107] is the [oldest university in continuous operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_universities_in_continuous_operation), and the first university in the sense of a higher-learning and degree-awarding institute, as the word *universitas* was coined at its foundation,[^cite_note-Top_Universities-7][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-Hunt_Janin_2008-10][^cite_note-Ridder-Symoens_1992-11] as well as one of the leading academic institutions in Italy and Europe.[^cite_note-109] It was an important centre of European intellectual life during the Middle Ages, attracting scholars from Italy and throughout Europe.[^cite_note-110] The Studium, as it was originally known, began as a loosely organized teaching system with each master collecting fees from students on an individual basis. The location of the early university was thus spread throughout the city, with various colleges being founded to support students of a specific nationality.



[Anatomical theatre of the Archiginnasio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomical_theatre_of_the_Archiginnasio), dating from 1637

In the Napoleonic era, the headquarters of the university were moved to their present location on [Via Zamboni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Zamboni?action=edit\&redlink=1), in the northeastern sector of the city centre. Today, the university's 11 schools, 33 departments, and 93 libraries are spread across the city and include four subsidiary campuses in nearby [Cesena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cesena), [Forlì](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forl%C3%AC), [Ravenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravenna), and [Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimini). Noteworthy students present at the university in centuries past included [Dante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dante), [Petrarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrarch), [Thomas Becket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Becket), [Pope Nicholas V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Nicholas_V), [Erasmus of Rotterdam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erasmus_of_Rotterdam), [Peter Martyr Vermigli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Martyr_Vermigli), and [Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernicus). [Laura Bassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Bassi), appointed in 1732, became the first woman to officially teach at a university in Europe. In more recent history, [Luigi Galvani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Galvani), the discoverer of [bioelectromagnetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bioelectromagnetics), and [Guglielmo Marconi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guglielmo_Marconi), the pioneer of radio technology, also worked at the university. The University of Bologna remains one of the most respected and dynamic post-secondary educational institutions in Italy. To this day, Bologna is still very much a university town, with over 80,000 enrolled students in 2015. This community includes a great number of Erasmus, Socrates, and overseas students.[^cite_note-111] The university's [botanical garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botanical_garden), the [Orto Botanico dell'Università di Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orto_Botanico_dell'Universit%C3%A0_di_Bologna), was established in 1568; it is the fourth oldest in Europe.

[Johns Hopkins University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johns_Hopkins_University) maintains its [Bologna Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_Center) in the city, which hosts one of the overseas campuses of the School of Advanced International Studies (SAIS). SAIS Bologna was founded in 1955 as the first campus of a US post-graduate school to open in Europe.[^cite_note-112] It was inspired by Marshall Plan efforts to build a cultural bridge between America and Europe.[^cite_note-113] Today, the Bologna Center also hosts the *Associazione italo-americana "Luciano Finelli"*, which supports cross-cultural awareness and exchange between Italy and the United States.[^cite_note-114]

The Eastern College Consortium (E.C.Co.), comprising [Vassar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassar_College), [Wellesley College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wellesley_College), and [Wesleyan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wesleyan_University), offers a study abroad program in the city in association with the University of Bologna. [Dickinson College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dickinson_College), [Indiana University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_University), [Brown University](4157.md), and the [University of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California) also have campuses or antennas in the city.

In addition, Bologna hosts a music school, [Conservatorio Giovanni Battista Martini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatorio_Giovanni_Battista_Martini), established in 1804, and an art school, [Accademia di Belle Arti di Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accademia_di_Belle_Arti_di_Bologna), founded in 1802. Both institutions were born as part of the reforms introduced by Napoleon Bonaparte.

## Sport



The PalaDozza, Bologna's historic basketball arena

In Bologna, unlike the vast majority of Italian cities, [basketball](3921.md) is the most followed and practiced sport. In fact, the sporting nickname for Bologna is *Basket City* in reference to the successes of the town's two rival historic basketball clubs, [Virtus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtus_Bologna) and [Fortitudo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortitudo_Bologna).[^cite_note-Wolff2003-115] Of the two, the former has won, among others, 17 [Italian basketball championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A_\(Basketball\)), two [EuroLeagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroLeague), one [EuroCup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroCup_Basketball) and one [FIBA Saporta Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Saporta_Cup), making them one of the most successful European basketball clubs; the latter has won two league titles, in 2000 and 2005. The Italian Basketball League, which operates both [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lega_Basket_Serie_A) and [Serie A2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A2_\(basketball\)), has its headquarters in Bologna. There are two indoor arenas in the city: [PalaDozza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PalaDozza) is the oldest, with a capacity of 5,570 seats, while [Virtus Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virtus_Arena) is a temporary venue with a capacity of 9,980 seats. A third arena with a capacity of 11,000 seats, the [Unipol Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unipol_Arena), is located in the neighbouring town of [Casalecchio di Reno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casalecchio_di_Reno).



The 32,000-capacity Stadio Renato Dall'Ara is the home of Bologna FC 1909.

[Football](10568.md) also has a strong tradition in Bologna. The city's main club, [Bologna FC 1909](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_FC_1909), have won seven Italian league championships (the latest in [1963–64](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963%E2%80%9364_Serie_A)), which makes them the sixth most successful team in the history of the league; in addition, they have won three [Coppa Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coppa_Italia) titles, the latest of which came in the [2024–2025 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Coppa_Italia). In their 1930s heyday Bologna FC was known as "*Lo squadrone che tremare il mondo fa*" ('The Team that Shakes the World'). The club play at the 38,000-capacity [Stadio Renato Dall'Ara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_Renato_Dall'Ara), which has hosted the Italian national team in both football and [rugby union](25405.md), as well as the [San Marino national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marino_national_football_team). It was also a venue at the [1990 FIFA World Cup](157232.md).

[Rugby union](25405.md) is also present in the city: Rugby Bologna 1928 was one of the first rugby union clubs established in Italy and was the first club affiliated to the [Italian rugby union federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Rugby_Federation).[^cite_note-116] As of 2026 it is Italy's oldest rugby union club still in operation. The club took part in the top tier of the Italian championship for the first 25 years of their history, never winning the title but finishing as runners-up several times; they returned to the [top division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A_Elite_\(rugby_union\)) in the late 1990s and faced serious financial problems which led to their relegation and near disappearance.

[Gianni Falchi Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianni_Falchi_Stadium) is a [baseball stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_stadium) located in Bologna.[^cite_note-auto-58][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] It is home to the home games of [Fortitudo Baseball Bologna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortitudo_Baseball_Bologna), in the [Italian Baseball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Baseball_League).[^cite_note-auto-58][^cite_note-119]
