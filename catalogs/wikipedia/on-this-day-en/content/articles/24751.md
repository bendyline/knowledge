**Punjab**[^cite_note-10] is a [province](446017.md) of [Pakistan](23235.md). With a population of over 127 million, it is the [most populous Pakistani province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Pakistan) and the [second most populous subnational polity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first-level_administrative_divisions_by_population) in the world. Located in the [central-eastern region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Pakistan) of the country, it has the [largest economy](#Economy), contributing the most to [national GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Pakistan) in Pakistan. [Lahore](125315.md) is the capital and largest city of the province. Other major cities include [Faisalabad](401880.md), [Rawalpindi](343524.md), [Gujranwala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujranwala) and [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan).

It is bordered by the Pakistani provinces of [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) to the north-west, [Balochistan](4687.md) to the south-west and [Sindh](28972.md) to the south, as well as [Islamabad Capital Territory](15294.md) to the north-west and [Azad Kashmir](2745.md) to the north. It shares an [international border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India-Pakistan_border) with the [Indian](14533.md) states of [Rajasthan](26291.md) and [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India) to the east and [Indian-administered Kashmir](61449812.md) to the north-east.&#x20;

The province forms the bulk of the transnational [Punjab region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab), [partitioned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_Punjab) in 1947 between Pakistan and India.[^cite_note-\:5-11] The most fertile province of the country, Punjab is considered the [breadbasket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breadbasket) of the nation, in addition to being the most industrialised.[^cite_note-auto-12] It is one of the more urbanised regions in [South Asia](21566765.md), with approximately 40 per cent of its population being urban.[^cite_note-13]

[Punjabi Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Muslims) form majority of the province.[^cite_note-punjab20172-14] [Their culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_culture) has been strongly influenced by [Islamic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_culture) and [Sufism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism), with a number of Sufi shrines located across the province.[^cite_note-\:10-15][^cite_note-EI3-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-\:11-18] Punjab hosts several UNESCO [World Heritage Sites](44940.md), including the [Shalimar Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalimar_Gardens,_Lahore), the [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort), the archaeological excavations at [Taxila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxila), and the [Rohtas Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohtas_Fort), among others.[^cite_note-\:14-19]

## Etymology

The name "Punjab" consists of two parts (پنج, *panj*, 'five' and آب, *āb*, 'water'), from [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) which are cognates of the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) words (पञ्‍च, *pañca*, 'five' and अप्, *áp*, 'water').[^cite_note-EoS-20][^cite_note-\:15-21] The word *pañj-āb* is thus [calque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calque) of Indo-Aryan *pañca-áp* and means "The Land of Five Waters", referring to the rivers [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum_River), [Chenab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chenab_River), [Ravi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_River), [Sutlej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutlej), and [Beas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beas_River).[^cite_note-22] All are [tributaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) of the [Indus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River), Sutlej being the longest.[^cite_note-23] References to a land of five rivers are found in the *[Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata)*, in which one of the regions is named as *Panchanada* ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language): पञ्चनद, [romanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Sanskrit): *pañca-nada*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'five rivers').[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The ancient [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_people) referred to the region as *Pentapotamía* ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Πενταποταμία, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'land of five rivers').[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-Khalid-28] Earlier, Punjab was also known as *[Sapta Sindhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapta_Sindhu)* in the [Rigveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda) and *[Hapta Hendu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avestan_geography#Vendidad_references)* in the [Avesta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avestan_geography), translating into "The Land of Seven Rivers"; the other two being Indus and [Kabul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabul_river) which are included in the greater Punjab region.[^cite_note-29]

The 14th-century author [Ayn al-Mulk Mahru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayn_al-Mulk_Mahru) referred to the region as the [*wilayat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilaya) of Punjab (ولایت وسیعۀ پنجاب, 'vast realm of Punjab') in *Insha-i-Mahru*;[^cite_note-30] Punjab finds mention in the [travelogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Travels_of_Ibn_Battuta) of [Ibn Battuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Battuta) and in the *Tarikh-i-Wassaf* of [Ilkhanid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhanate) historian [Wassaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wassaf) as well.[^cite_note-31] The current name gained currency during the [Mughal period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire).[^cite_note-32] A term used in the Punjabi language for the Pakistani province is *Lehnda Punjab* while Indian Punjab is known as *Chardha Punjab*.[^cite_note-33]

## History

Main article: [History of Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Punjab)

### Ancient period

The earliest evidence of human habitation in Punjab is traced to the [Soan Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soon_Valley) of the [Pothohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothohar), between the Indus and the Jhelum rivers, where [Soanian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soanian_culture) developed between 774,000 BCE and 11,700 BCE. This period goes back to the first interglacial period in the [Ice age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_age), from which remnants of stone and flint tools have been found.[^cite_note-murray-34] The Punjab region was the site of one of the earliest [cradles of civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cradles_of_civilisation), the Bronze Age [Harrapan civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrapan_civilisation) that flourished from about 3000 BCE and declined rapidly 1,000 years later, followed by the [Indo-Aryan migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Aryan_migrations) that overran the region in waves between 1500 and 500 BCE.[^cite_note-\:9-35] The migrating Indo-Aryan tribes gave rise to the Iron Age [Vedic civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_civilisation), which lasted till 500 BCE. During this period, the [Rigveda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rigveda) was composed in Punjab,[^cite_note-36] laying the foundation of [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism). Frequent intertribal wars in the post-Vedic period stimulated the growth of larger groupings ruled by chieftains and kings, who ruled local kingdoms known as [Mahajanapadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanapadas).[^cite_note-\:9-35] Achaemenid emperor [Darius the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darius_the_Great) crossed the Indus in 518 BCE and annexed the land up to the river Jhelum.[^cite_note-37] [Taxila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxila) in [Gandhara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandhara) was the site of one of the oldest education centre of South Asia and was probably capital of the Achaemenid province of [Hindush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindush).[^cite_note-\:1-38][^cite_note-39]



[Dharmarajika Stupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmarajika_Stupa), a 3rd-century BCE Buddhist [world heritage site](44940.md) at [Taxila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxila)

One of the early kings in Punjab was [Porus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porus), who fought in the [Battle of the Hydaspes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Hydaspes) against [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great).[^cite_note-\:6-40] The battle is thought to have resulted in a decisive [Greek](42056.md) victory; however, A. B. Bosworth warns against an uncritical reading of Greek sources who were obviously exaggerative.[^cite_note-\:6-40] Porus refused to surrender and wandered about atop an elephant, until he was wounded and his force routed.[^cite_note-\:6-40] When asked by Alexander how he wished to be treated, Porus replied "Treat me as a king would treat another king". Despite the apparently one-sided results, Alexander was impressed by Porus and chose to reinstate him.[^cite_note-\:7-41][^cite_note-42] The battle is historically significant because it resulted in the syncretism of ancient Greek political and cultural influences on the Indian subcontinent, yielding works such as Greco-Buddhist art, which continued to have an impact for the ensuing centuries. In south-central Punjab, the [Mallians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallian_people), together with nearby tribes, gathered a large army to face the Greek army. During the siege of the Mallian citadel, Alexander leaped into its inner area, where he was wounded by an arrow that had penetrated his lung, leaving him severely injured. The city was conquered after a fierce battle.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

The region was then ceded by [Seleucus I Nicator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucus_I_Nicator) to the [Maurya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) in 302 BCE. [Indo-Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Greeks) became established in the region after campaigns of [Greco-Bactrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Bactrian) [Demetrius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demetrius_I_of_Bactria) in 180 BCE; their greatest ruler, [Menander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menander_I) had [Sagala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagala) (present-day [Sialkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot)) as capital.[^cite_note-Hazel_2013-45][^cite_note-Ahir_1971-46] Menander is noted for becoming a patron and converting to [Greco-Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Buddhism).[^cite_note-47] Migrating central Asian tribes of [Saka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Scythian_Kingdom) became entrenched in Punjab under [Maues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maues) but were soon replaced by [Pahlavas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Parthian_kingdom) led by [Gondophares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gondophares), last of whom was the [Apraca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apracharajas) king [Sases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sases). By the middle of the first century CE, [Kushans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushans) had expanded into the region and would go on to rule in Punjab for the next three hundred years, until their kingdom was destroyed in the [Huns' invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huna_people), most notable of whom was Alchon Hun [Mihirakula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihirakula) (r. 515–542).

### Medieval period



[Katas Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katas_Raj_Temples), a large complex consisting of Buddhist stupas, [havelis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havelis) and over a dozen Hindu temples, earliest of which date to the 6th century CE.

[^cite_note-\:12-48]

The Buddhist monk [Xuanzang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xuanzang) visited Punjab in the 7th century CE and described a large kingdom called [Takka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taank_Kingdom), which according to him covered territory between Indus and Sutlej and had capital at Sialkot. Following the [Muslim conquests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquests_in_the_Indian_subcontinent) in the beginning of the 8th century, Arab armies of the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) advanced into Indus Valley, introducing [Islam](6037917.md) in Punjab. Their decline was followed by formation of various Arab-ruled principalities, notably the [Emirate of Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Multan), in the 9th century, coinciding with the rise of the [Odi Shahi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odi_Shahi_dynasty) of Gandhara in the northern Punjab and [Kabulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabulistan).[^cite_note-\:92-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

**Ghaznavids and Ghurids**

Punjab was annexed by the [Ghaznavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavid_dynasty) in the 11th century. [Peshawar was conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Peshawar_\(1001\)) by [Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni) after defeating maharaja [Jayapala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayapala) of Punjab in 1001.[^cite_note-52] The ruler of Multan, Abul Fateh Daud was [defeated in 1010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavid_conquest_of_Multan).[^cite_note-53] [Nandana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandana_Fort), the last Shahi capital, [fell in 1014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nandana) and Punjab became part of expanding Ghaznavid Empire. Lahore became secondary, and after 1163, sole capital of the Ghaznavids. They gradually declined as a power until the Ghurids [conquered Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Lahore_\(1186\)) under [Muhammad of Ghor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_of_Ghor) in 1186, deposing the last Ghaznavid king [Khusrau Malik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khusrau_Malik).[^cite_note-54]

After the assassination of Muhammad of Ghor at [Dhamiak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhamiak) in 1206, the Ghurid state fragmented and was replaced by the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate) in the Indo-Gangetic plains.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAmjad1989\[\[Category\:Wikipedia_articles_needing_page_number_citations_from_September_2022\]\]<sup_class="noprint_Inline-Template_"_style="white-space\:nowrap;">\[<i>\[\[Wikipedia\:Citing_sources|<span_title="This_citation_requires_a_reference_to_the_specific_page_or_range_of_pages_in_which_the_material_appears. (September_2022)">page needed</span>\]\]</i>\]</sup>-55] The Delhi Sultanate ruled Punjab for the next several centuries, led by five unrelated dynasties.

**Delhi Sultanate**

The first ruler of Delhi Sultanate was [Qutb ud-Din Aibak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qutb_ud-Din_Aibak), a [mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_dynasty_\(Delhi\)), who died in Lahore while playing [polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo) in 1210. Following his death his successor [Iltutmish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iltutmish) transferred capital from Lahore to Delhi. The relocation of regional centre was necessitated by the rise of [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) in the west. Mongols under [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan) first invaded Punjab in 1221 while chasing the retreating armies of Khwarezmian prince [Jalal al-Din Mangburni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalal_al-Din_Mangburni); Genghis Khan annihilated the Khwarezmians at the [Battle of the Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Indus) besides modern [Attock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attock). Mongols plundered [Punjab plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Plain) but returned due to the hot climate of Punjab.[^cite_note-56]

Throughout the 13th-century, Punjab bore the brunt of [numerous Mongol invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasions_of_India), and the towns of Lahore, Multan and [Dipalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipalpur) were repeatedly sacked. Mamluk sultan [Balban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balban) led several campaigns against them. [Alauddin Khilji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alauddin_Khilji) and his generals [Zafar Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zafar_Khan_\(Delhi_Sultanate\)), [Nusrat Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nusrat_Khan_Jalesari), [Ulugh Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulugh_Khan) and [Malik Kafur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Kafur) inflicted a series of defeats over them and large-scale raids by Mongol khanates stopped. [Ghiyath al-Din Tughlaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghiyath_al-Din_Tughluq), the former governor of Multan and Dipalpur, founded the Tughlaq dynasty in 1320. Earlier, he had served as the governor of Multan and had fought twenty-eight battles against Mongols from his base at [Dipalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipalpur), preserving Punjab and Sind from their advances. The mother of Ghiyath al-Din was from Punjab, as was the mother of [Firuz Shah Tughlaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firuz_Shah_Tughlaq), who became the emperor in 1351.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESingh2019223–225-57]



The 14th-century [mausoleum of Shah Rukn Alam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Shah_Rukn-e-Alam) features buttresses in each of its 8 corners, and incorporates elements of Tughluq military architecture.

[^cite_note-hill-58]

The Tughlaq dynasty declined towards the end of 14th century. In 1398 Delhi Sultanate faced a devastating invasion by [Emir Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir_Timur), who [massacred the citizens of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sack_of_Delhi_\(1398\)) and Multan. Following his return, [Khizr Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khizr_Khan) established the [Sayyid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid_dynasty), the fourth dynasty of the Delhi Sultanate, with four rulers ruling from 1414 to 1451 for 37 years.[^cite_note-mrpislam-59] Variously described as a [Sayyid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayyid)[^cite_note-60] or a [Khokhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khokhar) chief,[^cite_note-\:02-61][^cite_note-62] Khizr Khan initially ruled as the Timurid vassal of Multan, and conquered Delhi in 1414.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] During the reign of his successor [Mubarak Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubarak_Shah_II), large parts of Punjab were conquered by Punjabi chieftain [Jasrath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasrat) and Mubarak Shah spent his reign fighting against him.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-Iqtidar_Alam_Khan_2008_103-69][^cite_note-70] Sayyids ruled the sultanate until they were displaced by the [Lodi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodi_dynasty) in 1451,[^cite_note-71] when the last of the Sayyids, [Ala ud-Din](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alam_Shah), voluntarily abdicated the throne of the Delhi Sultanate in favour of [Bahlul Khan Lodi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahlul_Khan_Lodi).[^cite_note-72] [Guru Nanak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Nanak), the founder of [Sikhism](27964.md), was born in the town of [Nankana Sahib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nankana_Sahib) in 1470.[^cite_note-Macauliffe-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

**Langah Sultanate**

In 1445, [Rai Sahra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_Sahra_Langah), chief of [Langah tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langah_\(clan\)),[^cite_note-Ahmed_1984_pp._428–434-76][^cite_note-Mubārak_Blochmann_1891_p.321-77][^cite_note-Lambrick_p.212-78] established the [Langah Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langah_Sultanate) in Multan. The sultanate included southern and central Punjab. A large number of Baloch settlers arrived and the towns of Dera Ghazi Khan and Dera Ismail Khan were founded.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERoseberry19874-79]

### Modern period

#### Mughal Empire

The Mughal emperor [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur) defeated the Lodis in 1526, establishing [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire). During Mughal period Punjab region was divided into two provinces: [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subah_of_Multan) and [Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subah_of_Lahore).[^cite_note-lally-80][^cite_note-Wahi_2013_p.3-81] They were created as one of the original twelve [subahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subah) of the empire under the administrative reforms carried out by [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar) in 1580.[^cite_note-lally-80][^cite_note-Wahi_2013_p.3-81] By the 16th century, Punjabi Muslims were the majority in the region and an elaborate network of [mosques](19894.md) and [mausoleums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleums_of_Multan) marked the landscape.[^cite_note-oxford22-82] Some of them chose to enter [Mughal bureaucracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansabdar) and rose to the highest ranks, notably the grand vizier [Sa'adullah Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadullah_Khan_\(Mughal_Empire\)) and the military general [Shahbaz Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahbaz_Khan_Kamboh).[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] The period was also marked by the initial flourishing of [Punjabi literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_literature). A number of scholars and poets including [Shah Hussain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Hussain), [Sultan Bahu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan_Bahu), [Abdullah Lahori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Lahori), [Bulleh Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulleh_Shah), [Abdul Hakim Sialkoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hakim_Sialkoti), [Mita Chenabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mita_Chenabi) and [Waris Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waris_Shah) wrote their works in the Mughal period.



A painting of [Wazir Khan Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Khan_Mosque) in 1856. It was founded by [Wazir Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Khan_\(Lahore\)), a Punjabi viceroy of [Shah Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jahan), in 1634 and shows regional architectural influences.

[^cite_note-\:13-86]

[^cite_note-87]

The Mughal Empire ruled the region until it was severely weakened in the eighteenth century.[^cite_note-\:9-35] After the death of the last viceroy, [Adina Arain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adina_Beg) in 1758, large parts of Punjab were incorporated into [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire).[^cite_note-\:9-35] The decline of Mughals resulted in the rise of Sikh principalities, called [misls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misl), as well as several Punjabi Muslim polities, notably those of [Chattha clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chattha_\(clan\)),[^cite_note-\:4-88][^cite_note-\:0-89][^cite_note-\:2-90][^cite_note-91] [Gakhar clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakhars) and [Sial clan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sial_dynasty).[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-\:3-93][^cite_note-94] The 18th century was marked by constant warfare between these principalities and by several foreign invasions.&#x20;

[Ranjit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjit_Singh), born in [Gujranwala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujranwala) in the ruling family of one such principality, established the [Sikh Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire) which ruled Punjab from 1799 until the British annexed it in 1849 following the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Sikh_War) and [Second Anglo-Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Sikh_War) wars.[^cite_note-\:4-88]

#### British rule



Punjab region under [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) on a world map

Most of the Punjab formed a province of British India, though a number of [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) retained local rulers who recognised British authority.[^cite_note-\:93-95] In the [Revolt of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolt_of_1857), a [Kharal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharal) sardar, [Rai Ahmad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_Ahmad_Khan_Kharal), led a rebellion against the British Raj in the [Bar region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_region), as did the [Dhund tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhund_\(tribe\)) in [Murree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murree). However, most Punjabis were divided with regards to their allegiance and many did not feel loyalty towards the Mughal emperor in Delhi. The British recruited a number of Sikh, Pathan and Muslim troops to quell the revolt. The Punjab with its rich farmlands became one of the most important colonial assets.[^cite_note-\:9-35] Lahore was a noted center of learning and culture, and [Rawalpindi](343524.md) became an important military installation.[^cite_note-\:9-35]

A large number of Punjabis were recruited in the British Indian Army during [World War I](4764461.md), even though the Punjab remained a source of anti-colonial activities.[^cite_note-96] Disturbances in the region increased as the war continued.[^cite_note-\:9-35] At the end of the war, high casualty rates, heavy taxation, inflation, and a widespread influenza epidemic disrupted Punjabi society.[^cite_note-\:9-35] In 1919 a British officer ordered his troops to fire on a crowd of demonstrators, mostly Sikhs in Amritsar. The [Jallianwala massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jallianwala_Bagh_massacre) fuelled the [Indian independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement).[^cite_note-\:9-35] Nationalists declared the independence of India from Lahore in 1930 but were quickly suppressed.[^cite_note-\:9-35] Various social and independence movements and organisations originated from or had prominent influence in Punjab against the British during first half of 20th century, such as [Silk Letter movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silk_Letter_Movement), [Ghadar movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadar_Movement), [Khaksar movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaksar_movement), and [Majlis-e Ahrar-e Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis-e_Ahrar-e_Islam).



[Darbar palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darbar_Mahal) of the [Nawabs of Bahawalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahawalpur_\(princely_state\)). Established in the 18th century, Bahawalpur was the largest of the princely states in the [Punjab States Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_States_Agency).

When the Second World War broke out, nationalism in British India had already divided into religious movements.[^cite_note-\:9-35] Many Sikhs and other minorities supported the Hindus, who promised a secular multicultural and multireligious society, and [Muslim leaders in Lahore passed a resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Resolution) to work for a [Muslim state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Pakistan) (later called Pakistan), making the Punjab region a center of growing conflict between Indian and Pakistani nationalists.[^cite_note-\:9-35] At the end of the war, the British granted separate independence to India and Pakistan, setting off massive communal violence as Muslims fled to Pakistan and Hindu and Sikh Punjabis fled east to India.[^cite_note-\:9-35]

The [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj) had major political, cultural, philosophical, and literary consequences in the Punjab, including the establishment of a new system of education. During the [independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_independence_movement), many Punjabis played a significant role, including [Inayatullah Khan Mashriqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inayatullah_Khan_Mashriqi), [Agha Shorish Kashmiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agha_Shorish_Kashmiri), [Syed Ata Ullah Shah Bukhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syed_Ata_Ullah_Shah_Bukhari), [Madan Lal Dhingra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madan_Lal_Dhingra), [Sukhdev Thapar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhdev_Thapar), [Bhagat Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagat_Singh), [Udham Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udham_Singh), [Kartar Singh Sarabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartar_Singh_Sarabha), [Bhai Parmanand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhai_Parmanand), [Choudhry Rahmat Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Choudhry_Rahmat_Ali), and [Lala Lajpat Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lala_Lajpat_Rai).

#### After independence

At the time of partition in 1947, the province was split into East and West Punjab. [East Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Punjab) (48%) became part of India, while [West Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Punjab) (52%) became part of Pakistan.[^cite_note-97] The Punjab bore the brunt of the [civil unrest](329906.md) following [partition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India), with casualties estimated to be in the millions.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-dcosta2011-99][^cite_note-Silence2000-100][^cite_note-101]

Another major consequence of partition was the sudden shift towards religious homogeneity that occurred in all districts across Punjab owing to the new international border that cut through the province. This rapid demographic shift was primarily due to wide-scale migration but also caused by large-scale [religious cleansing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_persecution#Religious_cleansing) riots which were witnessed across the region at the time. According to historical demographer [Tim Dyson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Dyson), in the eastern regions of Punjab that ultimately became [Indian Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India) following independence, districts that were 66% Hindu in 1941 became 80% Hindu in 1951; those that were 20% Sikh became 50% Sikh in 1951. Conversely, in the western regions of Punjab that ultimately became Pakistani Punjab, all districts became almost exclusively Muslim by 1951.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDyson2018188–189-102]

In 1955, the province of Punjab along with other provinces and princely states was abolished and [West Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Pakistan) was created, however earlier administrative units were restored in 1970. A territory was carved out of Punjab during the period to establish [Islamabad](51153.md), the national capital; in 1960, [Ayub government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayub_Khan) agreed to recognise sole rights of India over the use of water of the three eastern rivers of Punjab under [Indus River Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River_Treaty), causing severe damage to the agricultural sector and water resources in the eastern areas of the province. The [1973 constitution of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Constitution_of_Pakistan) formalised a federal structure in the country. Following the [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Afghanistan) in 1979, the large-scale migration of [Afghan refugees](3657017.md) coupled with the rise of [global jihadism](16203.md) adversely affected the social fabric of the country, including Punjab, effects of which would only get more prominent after the [NATO-led invasion of Afghanistan](19666611.md) in 2001 and increase in terrorism.&#x20;

The [Sharif Chief Ministership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_ministership_of_Shehbaz_Sharif) (2008–2018) was noted for decrease in terrorism, expansion of infrastructure such as [motorways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorways_of_Pakistan) and [Orange Train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Train) projects, and the business friendly policies, but also criticised for the rise in corruption and the perceived unequal spending on a few urban centres in northeastern Punjab. The succeeding [Buzdar Chief Ministership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buzdar_provincial_government) was generally viewed negatively and associated with economic difficulties, inadequate response to the disastrous [floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Pakistan_floods) and the [COVID-associated economic recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic). After [2024 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Pakistani_general_election), [Maryam Nawaz](34181325.md) became the first female chief minister of Punjab, although the elections were widely described as unfair.

## Geography

Punjab is Pakistan's second largest province by area after Balochistan with an area of 205,344 square kilometres (79,284 square miles).[^cite_note-Punjab-103] It occupies 25.8% of the total landmass of Pakistan.[^cite_note-Punjab-103] Punjab province is bordered by Sindh to the south, the province of Balochistan to the southwest, the province of Khyber Pakhtunkhwa to the west, and the Islamabad Capital Territory and Azad Kashmir in the north. Punjab borders Jammu and Kashmir in the north, and the Indian states of Punjab and Rajasthan to the east.

The undivided Punjab region was home to six rivers, of which five flow through Pakistan's Punjab province. From west to east, the rivers are: the [Indus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River), [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum_River), [Chenab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chenab_River), [Ravi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravi_River) and [Sutlej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutlej). The land bounded by two rivers is called [Doab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doab); Punjab province has four doabs i.e [Sind Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindh_Sagar_Doab), [Jech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaj_Doab), [Rachna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachna_Doab) and [Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari_Doab). It is the only province that touches every other province; it also surrounds the [federal enclave](15294.md) of the [national capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_capital) city of Islamabad.[^cite_note-Now_or_Never-104][^cite_note-Ikram1995-105]

### Topography



Punjab features mountainous terrain near the [hill station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_station) of [Murree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murree).



The route from [Dera Ghazi Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dera_Ghazi_Khan) to [Fort Munro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Munro)

Punjab's landscape mostly consists of fertile alluvial plains of the Indus River and its four major tributaries in Pakistan: the Jhelum, Chenab, Ravi, and Sutlej rivers, which traverse Punjab north to south. The landscape is amongst the most heavily irrigated on earth and canals can be found throughout the province. Punjab also includes several mountainous regions, including the [Sulaiman Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulaiman_Mountains) in the southwest part of the province, the [Margalla Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margalla_Hills) in the north near Islamabad, and the [Salt Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Range) which divides the most northerly portion of Punjab, the [Pothohar Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothohar_Plateau), from the rest of the province. In south the elevation reaches 2,311 metres (7,582 ft) at the hill station of Suroh in Dera Ghazi Khan, which is also the highest peak in province.[^cite_note-106] A portion of [Thar desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thar_desert) lies in southern Punjab along the border with Indian state of Rajasthan, known as [Cholistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholistan_Desert). Another semi-arid desert lies in the Sind Sagar Doab called [Thal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thal_Desert).

### Fauna



[Chital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chital) (centre) and [chinkaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinkara) (background) at Lal Suhanra National Park&#x20;

Terrain of Punjab supports a wide range of fauna due to the variety of landscape present in the region, ranging from snowy hills to deserts. The mammal species in the province include [leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard), [barking deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barking_deer), [gray wolf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_wolf), [striped hyena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striped_hyena), [jackal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_jackal), [White-footed fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-footed_fox), [chinkara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinkara), [hog deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_hog_deer), [rhesus macaque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhesus_macaque), and [pangolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_pangolin). The [blackbuck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackbuck), [nilgai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgai) and [chital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chital) have been successfully reintroduced at [Lal Suhanra National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Suhanra_National_Park). [Punjab urial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_urial) is the provincial animal, and is found only in the Salt Range and [Kala Chitta Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kala_Chitta_Range) of northern Punjab. [Sulaiman markhor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulaiman_markhor) is also present in the southwestern Sulaiman Range. Reptilian species include [mugger crocodile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugger_crocodile), [gharial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharial), [monitor lizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monitor_lizard), [Russell's viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell's_viper), [Indian cobra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cobra), [saw-scaled viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saw-scaled_viper), [wolf snake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_snake), John's [sand boa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_boa), and [spiny-tailed lizard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spiny-tailed_lizard). [Indus river dolphin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_river_dolphin) is the best known aquatic mammal endemic to the Indus River Basin in Punjab and Sindh.[^cite_note-107] The avian species include [Himalayan monal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalayan_monal), [Kalij pheasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalij_pheasant), [Koklass pheasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koklass_pheasant), [grey partridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_partridge), [grey francolin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grey_francolin), [common wood pigeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_wood_pigeon), [houbara bustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houbara_bustard), [bulbul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulbul), [common quail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_quail), [pied cuckoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pied_cuckoo), [parakeet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parakeet), [Sind woodpecker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sind_woodpecker), [great Indian bustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Indian_bustard), [black kite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_kite), [Eurasian nightjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_nightjar), [steppe eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steppe_eagle), [shikra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikra), [Eurasian griffon vulture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_griffon_vulture), and [peregrine falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_falcon).[^cite_note-\:03-108]

### Climate



Sunset in Punjab, during summer

Most areas in Punjab experience extreme weather with foggy winters, often accompanied by rain. By mid-February the temperature begins to rise; springtime weather continues until mid-April, when the summer heat sets in. The onset of the southwest [monsoon](57630.md) is anticipated to reach Punjab by May, but since the early 1970s, the weather pattern has been irregular. The spring monsoon has either skipped over the area or has caused it to rain so hard that floods have resulted. June and July are oppressively hot. Although official estimates rarely place the temperature above 46 °C, newspaper sources claim that it reaches 51 °C and regularly carry reports about people who have succumbed to the heat. Heat records were broken in [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan) in June 1993, when the [mercury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperature_measurement) was reported to have risen to 54 °C. In August the oppressive heat is punctuated by the rainy season, referred to as *barsat*, which brings relief in its wake. The hardest part of the summer is then over, but cooler weather does not come until late October.

In early 2007, the province experienced one of the coldest winters in the last 70 years.[^cite_note-109]

Punjab's region temperature ranges from −2° to 45 °C, but can reach 50 °C (122 °F) in summer and can touch down to −10 °C in winter.

Climatically, Punjab has three major seasons:[^cite_note-ReferenceB-110]

- Hot weather (April to early June) when temperature rises as high as 123 °F (51 °C).

- Rainy season (late June to September). Average annual rainfall ranges between 950 and 1300 mm sub-mountain region and 500–800 mm in the plains.

- Cold / Foggy / mild weather (October to March). Temperature goes down as low as 35.6 °F (2.0 °C).

Weather extremes are notable from the hot and barren south to the cool hills of the north. The foothills of the [Himalayas](37966.md) are found in the extreme north as well, and feature a much cooler and wetter climate, with snowfall common at higher altitudes.

### Population

The province is home to over half the population of [Pakistan](23235.md), and is the world's [second-most populous subnational entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first-level_administrative_divisions_by_population), and the most populous outside of [India](14533.md) and [China](5405.md). The capital and largest city is Lahore which has been the capital of the wider Punjab region since 16th-century. Other important cities include [Faisalabad](401880.md), [Rawalpindi](343524.md), [Gujranwala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujranwala), [Sargodha](1738747.md), [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan), [Sialkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot), [Bahawalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahawalpur), [Gujrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujrat_city), [Sheikhupura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikhupura), [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum), [Rahim Yar Khan](2392033.md) and [Sahiwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahiwal).

As per a 2025 *[Dawn News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawn_News)* report, based on data compiled by research organisation [Population Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_Council), [UK Aid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_International_Development) and the [United Nations Population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Population_Division), Pakistan's Punjab had an annual growth rate of 2.53%. According to demographic projections, if the province’s current [total fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_fertility_rate) of 3.4 children per woman remains unchanged, Punjab’s population could more than double to approximately 253 million by 2050.[^cite_note-DawnPop-131]

&#x20;The major native language spoken in Punjab is [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language), representing the largest language spoken in the country. The Punjabi language is spoken in the form of many dialects across the province, including [Majhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majhi_dialect), [Pothwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pahari-Pothwari), [Thali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thali_dialect), [Jhangvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhangvi_dialect), [Dhanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhani_dialect), [Shahpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahpuri_dialect), and [Doabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doabi_dialect). In addition to Punjabi, other closely related languages such as [Saraiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraiki_language) in the south (including [Multani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multani_dialect), [Derawali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derawali_dialect), and [Riasti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riasti_dialect) dialects) and [Hindko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindko) in the northwest (including [Chachhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chachhi_dialect), [Ghebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghebi), and [Awankari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awankari_dialect) dialects) are also spoken widely. Both Saraiki and Hindko have been enumerated separately from Punjabi in the [Pakistani censuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_in_Pakistan) of 1981 and 2017.

## Government and administration

Main article: [Government of Punjab, Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Punjab,_Pakistan)

See also: [Provincial Assembly of the Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_the_Punjab); [Chief Minister of Punjab, Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Punjab,_Pakistan); and [Governor of Punjab, Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Punjab,_Pakistan)



Punjab assembly, Lahore

The Government of Punjab is a provincial government in the federal structure of Pakistan, is based in [Lahore](125315.md), the capital of the Punjab Province. The Chief Minister of Punjab (CM) is elected by the [Provincial Assembly of the Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Assembly_of_the_Punjab) to serve as the head of the provincial government in Punjab, Pakistan. The current Chief Minister is [Maryam Nawaz Sharif](34181325.md), who is also the first ever woman Chief Minister of any province in Pakistan. The Provincial Assembly of the Punjab is a unicameral legislature of elected representatives of the province of Punjab, which is located in Lahore in eastern Pakistan. The Assembly was established under Article 106 of the Constitution of Pakistan as having a total of 371 seats, with 66 seats reserved for women and eight reserved for non-Muslims.

There are 48 departments in Punjab government. Each Department is headed by a Provincial Minister (Politician) and a Provincial Secretary (A civil servant of usually BPS-20 or BPS-21). All Ministers report to the Chief Minister, who is the Chief Executive. All Secretaries report to the Chief Secretary of Punjab, who is usually a BPS-22 Civil Servant. The Chief Secretary in turn, reports to the Chief Minister. In addition to these departments, there are several Autonomous Bodies and Attached Departments that report directly to either the Secretaries or the Chief Secretary.

The province is represented in the [federal parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_Pakistan) through 173, out of 336, seats in [National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Pakistan), the lower house; and 23, out of 96, seats in [Senate](761025.md), the upper house.

## Economy

Further information: [Dadukhel mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadukhel_mine)



GDP by Province

The industrial sector comprising 24 per cent of the province's gross domestic product.[^cite_note-auto-12] Punjab has the [largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Pakistani_provinces_by_GDP) in Pakistan, contributing most to the national GDP. The province's economy has quadrupled since 1972.[^cite_note-152] Its share of Pakistan's GDP was 54.7% in 2000 and 59% as of 2010. It is especially dominant in the service and agriculture sectors of Pakistan's economy. With its contribution ranging from 52.1% to 64.5% in the Service Sector and 56.1% to 61.5% in the agriculture sector. It is also a major manpower contributor because it has the largest pool of professionals and highly skilled (technically trained) manpower in Pakistan. It is also dominant in the manufacturing sector, though the dominance is not as huge, with historical contributions ranging from a low of 44% to a high of 52.6%.[^cite_note-153] In 2007, Punjab achieved a growth rate of 7.8%[^cite_note-154] and during the period 2002–03 to 2007–08, its economy grew at a rate of between 7% and 8% per year.[^cite_note-155] and during 2008–09 grew at 6% against the total GDP growth of Pakistan at 4%.

It is known for its relative prosperity,[^cite_note-tribune.com.pk-156] and has the lowest rate of poverty among all Pakistani provinces.[^cite_note-\:8-157][^cite_note-158] However, a clear divide is present between the northern and southern regions of the province;[^cite_note-tribune.com.pk-156] with northern Punjab being relatively more developed than southern Punjab.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-auto1-160]

Despite the lack of a coastline, Punjab is the most industrialised province of Pakistan;[^cite_note-auto-12] its manufacturing industries produce textiles, sports goods, heavy machinery, electrical appliances, surgical instruments, vehicles, auto parts, metals, sugar mill plants, aircraft, cement, agricultural machinery, bicycles and rickshaws, floor coverings, and processed foods. In 2003, the province manufactured 90% of the paper and paper boards, 71% of the fertilisers, 69% of the sugar and 40% of the cement of Pakistan.[^cite_note-161]



Industrial Zones Punjab, Source:

[^cite_note-162]

Lahore and Gujranwala Divisions have the largest concentration of small light engineering units. The district of Sialkot excels in sports goods, surgical instruments and cutlery goods. Industrial estates are being developed by Punjab government to boost industrialisation in province, [Quaid e Azam Business Park Sheikhupura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaid_e_Azam_Business_Park_Sheikhupura) is one of the industrial areas which is being developed near Sheikhupura on Lahore-Islamabad motorway.[^cite_note-163]

Punjab has the lowest poverty rates in Pakistan, although a divide is present between the northern and southern parts of the province.[^cite_note-tribune.com.pk-156] [Sialkot District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot_District) in the prosperous northern part of the province has a poverty rate of 5.63%,[^cite_note-164] while [Rajanpur District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajanpur_District) in the poorer south has a poverty rate of 60.05%.[^cite_note-auto1-160]

## Culture

Main article: [Punjabi culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_culture)

The culture in Punjab grew out of the settlements along the five rivers, which served as an important route to the [Near East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_East) as early as the ancient [Indus Valley civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_civilisation), dating back to 3000 BCE.[^cite_note-Nayar1-196] [Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) has been the major economic feature of the Punjab and has therefore formed the foundation of Punjabi culture, with one's social status being determined by landownership.[^cite_note-Nayar1-196] Punjab emerged as an important agricultural region, especially following the [Green Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution) during the mid-1960s to the mid-1970s, has been described as the "breadbasket of both India and Pakistan".[^cite_note-Nayar1-196]

### Fairs and festivals

Main article: [Punjabi festivals (Pakistan)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_festivals_\(Pakistan\))

The [Islamic festivals](2035419.md) are typically observed.[^cite_note-Official_Holidays_2016-197][^cite_note-kmc.gos.pk-198] Non-Islamic festivals include [Lohri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lohri), [Basant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basant_\(festival\)) and [Vaisakhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaisakhi), which are usually celebrated as seasonal festivals.[^cite_note-autogenerated5-199] The Islamic festivals are set according to the lunar [Islamic calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_calendar) (Hijri), and the date falls earlier by 10 to 13 days from year to year.[^cite_note-200]

Some Islamic clerics and some politicians have attempted to ban the participation of non-Islamic festivals because of the religious basis,[^cite_note-201] and they being declared [haram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haram) (forbidden in Islam).[^cite_note-202]

## Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Punjab, Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Punjab,_Pakistan)



The [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort), a landmark built during the Mughal era, is a [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md)



[Rohtas Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohtas_Fort), a UNESCO world heritage site, was built upon a hill overlooking the [Pothohar Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothohar_Plateau).



[Derawar Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derawar_Fort), a medieval era fort in the [Cholistan desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholistan_desert)

Tourism in Punjab is regulated by the *Tourism Development Corporation of Punjab*.[^cite_note-203] The province has a number of large cosmopolitan cities, including the provincial capital [Lahore](125315.md). Major visitor attractions there include [Lahore Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Fort) and [Shalimar Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shalimar_Gardens_\(Lahore\)), which are now recognised [World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Heritage_Sites). The [Walled City of Lahore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_City_of_Lahore), [Badshahi Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badshahi_Mosque), [Wazir Khan Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wazir_Khan_Mosque), [Tomb of Jahangir and Nur Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_Jahangir), [Tomb of Asaf Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hasan_Asaf_Khan), [Chauburji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauburji) and other major sites are visited by tourists each year.

[Murree](343524.md) is a famous hill station stop for tourists.[^cite_note-204] The [Pharwala Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pharwala), which was built by an ancient Hindu civilisation, is on the outskirts of the city. The city of [Sheikhupura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikhupura) also has a number of sites from the Mughal Empire, including the World Heritage-listed [Rohtas Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohtas_Fort) near [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum). The [Katasraj temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katasraj_temple) in the city of [Chakwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakwal_District) is a major destination for Hindu devotees. The [Khewra Salt Mines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khewra_Salt_Mines) is one of the oldest mines in South Asia. [Faisalabad](401880.md)'s [clock tower and eight bazaars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clock_Tower,_Faisalabad) were designed to represent the [Union Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Jack).[^cite_note-205]



[Noor Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noor_Mahal), [Bahawalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahawalpur)

The province's southward is arid. [Multan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan) is known for its [mausoleums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleums_of_Multan) of saints and [Sufi pirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pir_\(Sufism\)). The [Multan Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multan_Museum), Multan fort, DHA 360° zoo and Nuagaza tombs are significant attractions in the city. The city of [Bahawalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahawalpur) is located near the [Cholistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholistan_Desert) and [Thar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thar_Desert) deserts. [Derawar Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derawar_Fort) in the [Cholistan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholistan_Desert) is the site for the annual [Cholistan Jeep Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholistan_Jeep_Rally). The city is also near the ancient site of [Uch Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uch_Sharif) which was once a [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate) stronghold. The [Noor Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noor_Mahal), Sadiq Ghar Palace, and Darbar Mall were built during the reign of the [Nawabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawabs). The [Lal Suhanra National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Suhanra_National_Park) is a major zoological garden on the outskirts of the city.[^cite_note-206]

## Social issues

See also: [Punjabi Language Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Language_Movement)



A demonstration by Punjabis at Lahore, Pakistan, demanding to make Punjabi as official language of instruction in schools of the Punjab.

The use of Urdu and English as the near exclusive languages of broadcasting, the public sector, and formal education have led some to fear that the Punjabi language in the province is being relegated to a low-status language and that it is being denied an environment where it can flourish.[^cite_note-207][^cite_note-apnaorg-208][^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210]

In August 2015, the Pakistan Academy of Letters, International Writer's Council (IWC) and World Punjabi Congress (WPC) organised the *Khawaja Farid Conference* and demanded that a Punjabi-language university should be established in [Lahore](125315.md) and that Punjabi language should be declared as the medium of instruction at the primary level.[^cite_note-The_Nation_2011-211][^cite_note-212] In September 2015, a case was filed in [Supreme Court of Pakistan](843857.md) against [Government of Punjab, Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Punjab,_Pakistan) as it did not take any step to implement the Punjabi language in the province.[^cite_note-The_Nation_2015-213][^cite_note-Business_Recorder_2015-214] Additionally, several thousand Punjabis gather in [Lahore](125315.md) every year on [International Mother Language Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Mother_Language_Day).

[Hafiz Saeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafiz_Muhammad_Saeed), chief of Jama'at-ud-Da'wah (JuD), has questioned Pakistan's decision to adopt Urdu as its national language in a country where majority of people speak Punjabi language, citing his interpretation of Islamic doctrine as encouraging education in the mother-tongue.[^cite_note-215] Some of the organisations and activists that demand the promotion of the Punjabi language include:

- Cultural and research institutes: Punjabi Adabi Board, the Khoj Garh Research Centre, Punjabi Prachar, Institute for Peace and Secular Studies, Adbi Sangat, Khaaksaar Tehreek, Saanjh, Maan Boli Research Centre, Punjabi Sangat Pakistan, Punjabi Markaz, Sver International.

- Trade unions and youth groups: Punjabi Writers Forum, National Students Federation, Punjabi Union-Pakistan, Punjabi National Conference, National Youth Forum, Punjabi Writers Forum, National Students Federation, Punjabi Union, Pakistan, and the Punjabi National Conference.

- Notable activists include Tariq Jatala, Farhad Iqbal, Diep Saeeda, Khalil Ojla, Afzal Sahir, Jamil Ahmad Paul, Mazhar Tirmazi, Mushtaq Sufi, Biya Je, Tohid Ahmad Chattha and Bilal Shaker Kahaloon, Nazeer Kahut.

But, in truth, that final word is never, to my knowledge, used by the Indians in proper names compounded in this way; on the other hand, there exist multiple Persian names which end with that word, e.g., *Doab* and *Nilab*. Therefore, it is likely that the name Penjab, which is now found in all geographical books, is of more recent origin and can be attributed to the Muslim kings of India, among whom the Persian language was predominantly in use. That the Indian name Panchanada is ancient and genuine is evident from the fact that it is already seen in the *Ramayana* and *Mahabharata*, the most ancient Indian poems, and that no other exists in addition to it among the Indians; for *Panchála*, which English translations of the *Ramayana* render with Penjab...is the name of another region, entirely distinct from Pentapotamia...."
