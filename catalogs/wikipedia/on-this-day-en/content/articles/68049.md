**Nottinghamshire** ([/ˈnɒtɪŋəmʃər, -ʃɪər/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-CollinsDictionary-4] abbreviated *Notts.*) is a [ceremonial county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceremonial_county) in the [East Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands) of England. The county is bordered by [South Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Yorkshire) to the north-west, [Lincolnshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincolnshire) to the east, [Leicestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicestershire) to the south, and [Derbyshire](71070.md) to the west. The largest settlement is the city of [Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham).

The county has an area of 2,160 km<sup>2</sup> (830 sq mi) and had an estimated population of 1,188,090 in 2024. Nottingham is in the south-west of the county, which is the most densely populated area. Other settlements include [Worksop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worksop) in the north-west, [Newark-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark-on-Trent) in the east, and [Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield) in the west. For [local government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_England) purposes Nottinghamshire comprises a [non-metropolitan county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-metropolitan_county), with seven districts, and the Nottingham [unitary authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authorities_of_England) area. The [East Midlands Combined County Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands_Combined_County_Authority) includes [Nottinghamshire County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottinghamshire_County_Council) and [Nottingham City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_City_Council).

The geography of Nottinghamshire is largely defined by the [River Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent), which forms a wide valley which crosses the county from the south-west to the north-east. North of this, in the centre of the county, is [Sherwood Forest](28739.md), the remnant of a large [ancient woodland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_woodland).

## History

Main article: [History of Nottinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Nottinghamshire)

Nottinghamshire lies on the [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain) [Fosse Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fosse_Way), and there are Roman settlements in the county; for example at [Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield), and forts such as at the [Broxtowe Estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broxtowe_Estate) in [Bilborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilborough). The county was settled by [Angles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angles_\(tribe\)) around the 5th century, and became part of the Kingdom, and later Earldom, of [Mercia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercia). However, there is evidence of [Anglo-Saxon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Saxon) settlement at the Broxtowe Estate, [Oxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxton,_Nottinghamshire), near Nottingham, and [Tuxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuxford), east of [Sherwood Forest](28739.md). The name first occurs in 1016, but until 1568, the county was administratively united with Derbyshire, under a single [Sheriff](89516.md). In [Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans) times, the county developed [malting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malting) and [woollen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woollen) industries.

The village of [Edwinstowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwinstowe) close to [Sherwood Forest](28739.md) took the name from [Edwin of Northumbria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_of_Northumbria), who died in 633 nearby and was provisionally buried in St Mary's Church, [Edwinstowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwinstowe).[^cite_note-5]

[William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror) in 1066 made [Sherwood Forest](28739.md) a [Royal Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Forest) for hunting which was frequently visited by the Mercian and later Kings.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-groves-8]

William's 1086 [Domesday Book](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domesday_Book) identified certain areas in Nottinghamshire being under the land of King [Edward the Confessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_the_Confessor) these included [Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield) and [Sutton in Ashfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton_in_Ashfield), amongst other places including [Skegby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skegby), [Dunham-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunham-on-Trent), [East Drayton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Drayton), [East Markham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Markham), [Farnsfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farnsfield), [Warsop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsop), [Carburton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carburton), [Edwinstowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwinstowe), [Carlton-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton-on-Trent), [Budby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budby), [Perlethorpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perlethorpe) and [Walesby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walesby,_Nottinghamshire).[^cite_note-9]

&#x20;\
&#x20;[King John's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_John's_Palace) ruin near [Kings Clipstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_Clipstone) was a royal residence for [King John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John,_King_of_England), the area being near to [Sherwood Forest](28739.md). [King John's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_John's_Palace) was a place where [William I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Lion) met [Richard I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_I_of_England) to congratulate him on his return from the crusades.[^cite_note-groves-8] [King John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John,_King_of_England) died at [Newark Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_Castle,_Nottinghamshire) in 1216.[^cite_note-10]

During the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), the county held much needed minerals such as [coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal) and [iron ore](145352.md), and had constructed some of the first experimental waggonways in the world; an example of this is the [Wollaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollaton) wagonway of 1603–1616, which transported minerals from [bell pit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_pit) mining areas at [Strelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strelley) and [Bilborough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bilborough), this led to [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal) and [railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway) being constructed in the county, and the [lace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lace) and cotton industries grew. In the 18th and 19th centuries, mechanised deeper [collieries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collieries) opened, and mining became an important economic sector. Although there are no remaining working mines in Nottinghamshire now.



Hand-drawn map of Lincolnshire and Nottinghamshire from 1576.

Until 1610, Nottinghamshire was divided into eight [Wapentakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wapentake). Sometime between 1610 and 1719, they were reduced to six – [Newark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_\(wapentake\)), [Bassetlaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassetlaw_\(wapentake\)), [Thurgarton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurgarton_\(wapentake\)), [Rushcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushcliffe_\(wapentake\)), [Broxtowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broxtowe_\(wapentake\)), and [Bingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingham_\(wapentake\)), some of these names still being used for the modern districts. Oswaldbeck was absorbed in Bassetlaw, of which it forms the North Clay division, and Lythe in Thurgarton.

[Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield) and surrounding areas in Nottinghamshire in the 17th century became the centre of [nonconformism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonconformist_\(Protestantism\)) in separation from the [Church of England](5955.md). In particular [Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield) became the birthplace of the [Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quaker) with [George Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Fox) living in the town.[^cite_note-11]

Nottinghamshire is famous for its involvement with the legend of [Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood). This is also the reason for the numbers of tourists who visit places like [Sherwood Forest](28739.md), [City of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham), and the surrounding villages in Sherwood Forest. To reinforce the Robin Hood connection, the [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham) in 2010 has begun the [Nottingham Caves Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Caves_Survey), with the goal "to increase the tourist potential of these sites". The project "will use a 3D laser scanner to produce a three dimensional record of more than 450 sandstone caves around Nottingham".[^cite_note-12]

Nottinghamshire was mapped first by [Christopher Saxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Saxton) in 1576; the first fully surveyed map of the county was by John Chapman, who produced Chapman's Map of Nottinghamshire in 1774.[^cite_note-13] The map was the earliest printed map at a sufficiently useful scale (one statute mile to one inch) to provide basic information on village layout, and the existence of landscape features such as roads, [milestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milestone), [tollbars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tollbar), parkland, and mills.

Nottinghamshire saw a slight change to its overall boundary in the extreme northern part of the county in 1974, when the villages of [Blaxton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blaxton), [Finningley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finningley) and [Auckley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckley) (part) were merged into the Doncaster in [South Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Yorkshire).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## Physical geography

[Map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special\:Map/9/53.12/-1.004/en)

Interactive map of Nottinghamshire and city/districts

Nottinghamshire, like [Derbyshire](71070.md), and South Yorkshire, sits on extensive coal measures, up to 900 [metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre) (3,000 [feet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foot_\(unit\))) thick, and occurring largely in the north of the county. There is an [oilfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oilfield) near [Eakring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eakring). These are overlaid by [sandstones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandstone) and [limestones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone) in the west, and [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay) in the east.[^cite_note-16] The north of the county is part of the [Humberhead Levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humberhead_Levels) [lacustrine plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lacustrine_plain). The centre and south west of the county, around Sherwood Forest, features undulating hills with ancient [oak](39696.md) woodland. Principal rivers are the [Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent), [Idle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Idle), [Erewash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Erewash), and [Soar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Soar). The Trent, fed by the Soar, Erewash, and Idle, composed of many streams from Sherwood Forest, run through wide and flat valleys, merging at [Misterton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misterton,_Nottinghamshire). A point just north of Newtonwood Lane, on the boundary with Derbyshire is the highest point in Nottinghamshire; at 205 metres (673 feet),[^cite_note-17] while [Silverhill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silverhill,_Nottinghamshire), a [spoil heap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spoil_heap) left by the former Silverhill colliery, a human-made point often cited as the highest, reaches 204 metres (669 feet). The lowest is Peat Carr, east of Blaxton, at [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_level); the Trent is tidal below [Cromwell Lock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cromwell_Lock).[^cite_note-Experiencing_the_Highs_and_Lows-18]

Nottinghamshire is sheltered by the [Pennines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines) to the west, so receives relatively low rainfall at 641 to 740 [millimetres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millimetre) (25 to 29 [inches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inch)) annually.[^cite_note-19] The average temperature of the county is 8.8–10.1 degrees [Celsius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius) (48–50 degrees [Fahrenheit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit)).[^cite_note-20] The county receives between 1321 and 1470 hours of sunshine per year.[^cite_note-21]

### Green belt

Main article: [Nottingham and Derby Green Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_and_Derby_Green_Belt)

Nottinghamshire contains one green belt area, first drawn up from the 1950s. Completely encircling the Nottingham conurbation, it stretches for several miles into the surrounding districts, and extends into Derbyshire.

## Politics

See also: [Nottinghamshire local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottinghamshire_local_elections)

Nottinghamshire, including the city of Nottingham, is represented by eleven [members of parliament](13964.md); nine for the [Labour Party](19279158.md), and two [Reform UK](59757202.md) MPs

Following the [2025 County Council election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Nottinghamshire_County_Council_election), [Nottinghamshire County Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottinghamshire_County_Council) is controlled by Reform. The party went from having a single councillor before the election to winning a majority of the council's seats.[^cite_note-22] The council is currently made up of 41 Reform UK councillors, 18 Conservatives, four Labour and three from local parties.[^cite_note-23] Prior to the 2025 election, the council had been fully or partially controlled by the Conservative Party since 2017. The council had historically been under Labour control; the party held a majority on the council from 1981 to 2009.

Local government is devolved to seven local borough and district councils. [Ashfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashfield_District) is controlled by the [Ashfield Independents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashfield_Independents). [Bassetlaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassetlaw_District), [Gedling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Gedling) and [Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield_District) are Labour-controlled. [Newark and Sherwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_and_Sherwood) is controlled by a coalition of Labour, [Liberal Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)) and independent councillors. [Rushcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Rushcliffe) is Conservative-controlled. [Broxtowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_of_Broxtowe) is run by a minority [Broxtowe Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broxtowe_Alliance) administration. [Nottingham City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_City_Council), which governs the Nottingham unitary authority and is independent of Nottinghamshire County Council, is majority Labour-controlled.

### Future

Main article: [Upcoming structural changes to local government in England § Nottinghamshire and Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upcoming_structural_changes_to_local_government_in_England#Nottinghamshire_and_Nottingham)

In July 2026, the [UK Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Government) announced that it intends to reorganise local government in Nottinghamshire by April 2028. Two [unitary authority areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_authority_areas) will cover the county. One will include Nottingham and its hinterland, an area larger than the current Nottingham district, and other will cover the remainder of the county. Shadow elections to the new unitary authorities will take place in May 2027.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

## Economy and industry

The regional economy was traditionally based on industries such as coal mining in the [Leen Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leen_Valley), and manufacturing. Since the invention of the [knitting frame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knitting_frame) by local [William Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lee_\(inventor\)), the county, in particular [Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham), became synonymous with the lace industry.[^cite_note-27]

In 1998, Nottinghamshire had a [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) per-capita of [£](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_Sterling)12,000, and a total GDP of £12,023 million. This is compared to a per-capita GDP of £11,848 for the [East Midlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands), £12,845 for England, and £12,548 for the United Kingdom. Nottingham had a GDP per-capita of £17,373, North Nottinghamshire £10,176, and South Nottinghamshire £8,448.[^cite_note-28] In October 2005, the United Kingdom had 4.7% unemployment, the East Midlands 4.4%, and the Nottingham commuter belt area 2.4%.[^cite_note-29]

## Education

See also: [List of schools in Nottinghamshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Nottinghamshire)

### Secondary education

The county has [comprehensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comprehensive_school) secondary education with 47 state [secondary schools](554992.md#England_and_Wales), as well as 10 [private schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_schools_in_the_United_Kingdom). The City of Nottingham [local education authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_education_authority) (LEA) has 18 state schools and six independent schools, not including [sixth form colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_form_college).

A total of 9,700 pupils took [GCSEs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GCSE) in the Nottinghamshire LEA in 2007. The best results were from the [West Bridgford School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bridgford_School), closely followed by [Rushcliffe Spencer Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rushcliffe_School) and the Minster School in [Southwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwell,_Nottinghamshire). In Nottingham, the best results came from the [Trinity Catholic School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_School_\(Nottingham\)) and the Fernwood School in [Wollaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollaton).[^cite_note-30]

At [A-level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-level), the highest performing institution was [The Becket School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Becket_School), followed by the West Bridgford School. Some of Nottingham city best results tend to come from [Nottingham High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_High_School), closely followed by the all-female [Nottingham High School for Girls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_High_School_for_Girls), both of which are privately run.

[Worksop College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worksop_College) is another private school near to [Worksop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worksop).

### Higher education

The [University of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Nottingham) is a [Russell Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Group) university, offering one of the broadest selections of courses in the UK. [Nottingham Trent University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Trent_University) is one of the [post-1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Further_and_Higher_Education_Act_1992) [universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Universities) of the UK. Nottingham is home to a campus of the [University of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Law). All three of these institutions combine to make Nottingham one of England's largest student cities. Nottingham Trent University also has an agricultural college near [Southwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwell,_Nottinghamshire), while the University of Nottingham has one at [Sutton Bonington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton_Bonington).

## Culture



National and County cricket player [Harold Larwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Larwood)

Nottinghamshire is home to the [Sherwood Forest](28739.md), known for its association with the legend of [Robin Hood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood).[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

Nottinghamshire contains the ancestral home of the poet [Lord Byron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lord_Byron), [Newstead Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newstead_Abbey), which he sold in 1818. It is now owned by Nottingham City Council, and is open to the public. The acclaimed author [D. H. Lawrence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._H._Lawrence) was from [Eastwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastwood,_Nottinghamshire) in Nottinghamshire. [Toton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toton) was the birthplace and home of English folk singer-songwriter [Anne Briggs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anne_Briggs), well known for her song *Black Waterside*. The north of the county is also noteworthy for its connections with the [Pilgrim Fathers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrim_\(Plymouth_Colony\)). [William Brewster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Brewster_\(Pilgrim\)), for example, came from the village of Scrooby, and was influenced by [Richard Clyfton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Clyfton), who preached at [Babworth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babworth).

Nottinghamshire has international [twinning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town_twinning) arrangements with the province of [Wielkopolska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland_Voivodeship) ([Greater Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Poland)) in western [Poland](22936.md), and with the province's capital city, [Poznań](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozna%C5%84).[^cite_note-33]

In 2002, *[Crocus nudiflorus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crocus_nudiflorus)* (Autumn crocus) was voted by the public as the county flower of Nottinghamshire.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

### Sport

[Nottinghamshire County Cricket Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottinghamshire_County_Cricket_Club) (NCCC) are a [first class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-class_cricket) county [cricket](25675557.md) club who play at [Trent Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trent_Bridge) in [West Bridgford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bridgford). They won the [County Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Championship) in 2010.

The most successful [football](10568.md) team within Nottinghamshire is [Nottingham Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Forest_F.C.), a [Premier League](11250.md) club that won the [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977%E2%80%9378_Football_League_First_Division) [English championship](11250.md), and followed it up with winning the [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_European_Cup_Final) and [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_European_Cup_Final) [European Cup](44220.md) titles although success has not been seen by most of their fans in living memory. [Notts County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notts_County_F.C.) and [Mansfield Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield_Town_F.C.) of [League One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EFL_League_One) are the other professional teams in the county. Notts County are the world's oldest professional football club, being formed in 1862 and are world renowned for giving [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus) their black and white striped kit and opening their new stadium in 2011.

Other notable sporting teams are the [Nottingham Rugby Football Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_R.F.C.), and the [Nottingham Panthers Ice Hockey Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Panthers).

### Settlements and features

The traditional county town, and the largest settlement in the historic and ceremonial county boundaries, is the [City of Nottingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham). The city is now administratively independent, but towns including [Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold,_Nottinghamshire), [Beeston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beeston,_Nottinghamshire), [Carlton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlton,_Nottinghamshire), [Hucknall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hucknall), [Stapleford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stapleford,_Nottinghamshire) and [West Bridgford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bridgford) are still within the administrative county, and Hucknall is where the county council are based (West Bridgford prior to 2025).

There are several [market towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_town) in the county. [Newark-on-Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark-on-Trent) is a bridging point of the [Fosse Way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fosse_Way) and [River Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Trent), but is actually an Anglo-Saxon market town with a now ruined [castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_Castle,_Nottinghamshire). [Mansfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mansfield), the second-largest settlement in the county after Nottingham, sits on the site of a [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Britain) settlement, but grew after the [Norman Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest). [Worksop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worksop), in the north of the county, is also an Anglo-Saxon market town which grew rapidly in the [Industrial Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Revolution), with the arrival of [canals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal) and railways and the discovery of coal. Other market towns include Arnold, [Bingham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bingham,_Nottinghamshire), Hucknall, [Kirkby-in-Ashfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirkby-in-Ashfield), [Tuxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuxford), [Retford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retford) and [Sutton-in-Ashfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sutton-in-Ashfield).

The main railway in the county is the [Midland Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midland_Main_Line), which links London to [Sheffield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield) via Nottingham. The [Robin Hood Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Hood_Line) between Nottingham and Worksop serves several villages in the county. The [East Coast Main Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Coast_Main_Line) from London to [Doncaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doncaster), [Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds), [York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York), [Newcastle upon Tyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_upon_Tyne), and [Scotland](26994.md) serves the eastern Nottinghamshire towns of Newark and Retford.

The [M1 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_motorway) runs through the county, connecting Nottingham to London, Leeds, and Leicester by road. The [A1 road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_road_\(Great_Britain\)) follows for the most part the path of the Great North Road, although in places it diverges from the historic route where towns have been bypassed. Retford was by-passed in 1961, and Newark-on-Trent was by-passed in 1964, and the A1 now runs between Retford and Worksop past the village of [Ranby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranby,_Nottinghamshire). Many historic [coaching inns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaching_inn) can still be seen along the traditional route.

[East Midlands Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Midlands_Airport) is just outside the county in [Leicestershire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leicestershire), while [Doncaster Sheffield Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doncaster_Sheffield_Airport) lies within the historic boundaries of Nottinghamshire. These airports serve the county and several of its neighbours. Together, the airports have services to most major European destinations, and East Midlands Airport now also has services to [North America](21139.md) and the [Caribbean](18956035.md). As well as local bus services throughout the county, Nottingham and its suburbs have a tram system, [Nottingham Express Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Express_Transit).

Nottingham and its surrounding areas form part of the [Nottingham Urban Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Urban_Area) while Bassetlaw is a non-constituent part of the Sheffield City Region.
