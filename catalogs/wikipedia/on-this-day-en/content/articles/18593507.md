**Simi Valley** ([/ˈsiːmiː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b8/En-us-simi_valley.ogg/En-us-simi_valley.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b8/En-us-simi_valley.ogg/En-us-simi_valley.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>; [Chumash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumashan_languages): *Shimiyi*)[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] is a city in [the valley of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Valley_\(valley\)) in southeastern [Ventura County, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California), United States. It is 37 miles (60 km) northwest of [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles), making it part of the [Greater Los Angeles Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles_Area). Simi Valley borders [Thousand Oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Oaks,_California), [Moorpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorpark,_California), and the [Chatsworth](336125.md) neighborhood of [Los Angeles](18110.md). As of the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), Simi Valley had a population of 126,356.[^cite_note-Census2020PLLede-11] The city of Simi Valley is surrounded by the [Santa Susana Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Mountains) and the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills), west of the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley), and northeast of the [Conejo Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conejo_Valley). It grew as a [bedroom community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commuter_town) for the cities in the Los Angeles area and the San Fernando Valley when a freeway was built over the [Santa Susana Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Pass).

The [Ronald Reagan Presidential Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan_Presidential_Library), where [the former president](25433.md) was buried in 2004, is in Simi Valley.[^cite_note-12]

## History

### Chumash/pre-colonial period

Simi Valley's name is derived from the Chumash word Shimiyi, which refers to the stringy, thread-like clouds that typify the [region](21139.md).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The name could have originated from the strands of mist from coastal fog that move into the [Oxnard Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxnard_Plain) and wind their way up the [Calleguas Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calleguas_Creek) and the Arroyo Las Posas into Simi Valley.[^cite_note-16] The origin of the name was preserved because of the work of the anthropologist [John P. Harrington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_P._Harrington), whose brother, Robert E. Harrington lived in Simi Valley. Robert Harrington later explained the name: *"The word Simiji in Indian meant the little white wind clouds so often seen when the wind blows up here and Indians living on the coast, would never venture up here when those wind clouds were in the sky. The word Simiji was constructed by whites to the word Simi. There are other explanations about the name Simi, but this one was given to me by my brother who worked over 40 years for the [Smithsonian Institution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithsonian_Institution) and it seems most plausible to me"*.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

Three Chumash settlements existed in Simi Valley during the Mission period in the late 18th and early 19th century: Shimiyi, Ta'apu (present-day [Tapo Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapo_Canyon)), and Kimishax or Quimicas ([Happy Camp Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Camp_Canyon_Regional_Park) west of [Moorpark College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorpark_College)).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] There are many Chumash cave paintings in the area containing pictographs, including the [Burro Flats Painted Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burro_Flats_Painted_Cave) in the Burro Flats area of the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills), located between the Simi Valley, [West Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Hills,_Los_Angeles), and [Bell Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Canyon,_California). The cave is located on private land owned by NASA. Other areas containing Chumash Native American pictographs in the Simi Hills are by Lake Manor and [Chatsworth](336125.md).[^cite_note-22]

### The Rancho period



[Rancho Simi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Simi) was granted in 1795 to the [Pico family of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_family_of_California). Pictured is the [Simi Adobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Adobe%E2%80%93Strathearn_House).

The first Europeans to visit Simi Valley were members of the Spanish [Portolá expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portol%C3%A1_expedition) (1769–1770), the first European land entry and exploration of the present-day state of [California](5407.md). The expedition traversed the valley on January 13–14, 1770, traveling from [Conejo Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conejo_Valley) to San Fernando Valley. They camped near a native village in the valley on the 14th.[^cite_note-23]

[Rancho Simí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Simi), also known as Rancho San José de Nuestra Señora de Altagracia y Simí,[^cite_note-\:1-24] was a 113,009-acre (457 km<sup>2</sup>) [Spanish land grant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchos_of_California) in eastern [Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California) and western [Los Angeles](79734.md) counties granted in 1795 to Santiago Pico. After Santiago Pico's death in 1815, the Rancho was regranted to Santiago's sons Javier Pico and his two brothers, Patricio Pico and Miguel Pico, members of the prominent [Pico family of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_family_of_California).[^cite_note-\:1-24] [Rancho Simí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Simi) was the earliest Spanish colonial land grant within Ventura and [Santa Barbara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Barbara_County,_California) Counties. The name derives from *Shimiji,* the name of the [Chumash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumash_\(tribe\)) Native American village here before the Spanish.[^cite_note-25] It was the largest Spanish or Mexican land grant given in Ventura County, and one of the largest given in California. The [Simi Adobe-Strathearn House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Adobe-Strathearn_House), later the home of Robert P. Strathearn and family, served as the headquarters of the rancho.

[José de la Guerra y Noriega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_de_la_Guerra_y_Noriega), a Captain of the [Santa Barbara Presidio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidio_of_Santa_Barbara), who had begun to acquire large amounts of land in California to raise cattle, purchased Rancho Simí from the Pico family in 1842. After Jose de la Guerra death in 1858, the sons of Jose de la Guerra continued to operate the ranchos. The end of their prosperity came when several years of drought in the 1860s caused heavy losses. In 1865, the De la Guerras lost the ownership of El Rancho Simí excluding the Rancho Tapo. El Rancho Tapo was part of the original 113,009-acre Rancho Simí grant, but sometime around 1820–1830, the Rancho Tapo came to be thought of as a separate place within Rancho Simí.[^cite_note-\:1-24] The last of the De la Guerras to live in Simí Valley retreated to a 14,400-acre portion of the original rancho that was known as the Tapo Rancho. As late as February 1877, Juan De la Guerra was reported in county newspapers to be preparing to plant walnuts in the Tapo, which appears to be the final mention of their farming in relation to the original Simí grant.

The De la Guerra heirs tried every legal means, but by the 1880s, the Rancho Tapo also slipped from their ownership, as had the rest of the Rancho.[^cite_note-\:1-24]

### The Pioneer period

The Pioneer, or 'American,' period in Simi Valley began with the 96,000-acre purchase of El Rancho Simí by an eastern speculator named [Thomas A. Scott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Alexander_Scott) (1814–1882), who had made his money as an investor in the Pennsylvania Railroad during the Civil War. He was president of the [Pennsylvania Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Railroad), and a partner in Philadelphia and California Petroleum Company. Scouts came to California to purchase lands, and thus Scott acquired El Rancho Simí (1865). His goal was to locate sites for oil, since the first oil well had been developed in Titusville, Pennsylvania just a few years earlier (1859). Within a short time, a 27-year-old man named Thomas Bard was sent west by Scott to manage the California properties. In the late 1880s, Simí Land and Water Company was formed to see to the selling of the huge rancho in ranch-size properties. Some American farmers had begun to lease land in the greater Rancho Simí for farming.[^cite_note-\:1-24][^cite_note-Simi_Valley_Historical_Society-26]

The earliest Anglo American ranchers showed up in Simí Valley in the late 1860s into the 1870s. Charles Emerson Hoar was given the title of "first American farmer" by early Simí historian Janet Scott Cameron. He had purchased the Hummingbird's Nest Ranch in the northeast corner of the Valley, and he leased land from the new owners of the Simí Rancho for raising sheep, already a proven way of making a living.

Much of the Simí Rancho land continued, as in Spanish days, to be used for raising sheep, cattle and grain. Wheat was more likely to prosper here than in the rest of the county, being free of [wheat leaf rust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat_leaf_rust), a fungal disease. Barley soon became the predominant grain crop.

Agriculture and ranching dominated the landscape through the 1950s. Citrus, walnuts and apricots were all grown in Simi Valley. In the early 1960s modern residential development began to take place.

### Modern residential development

Prior to modern residential development, Simí was an agricultural community, with ranch houses dotting the valley. 1957 and 1958 brought the first 'tract' housing developments when the Dennis and Ayhens, Wright Ranch and Valley Vista tracts were built, a rapid increase in residential development took place beginning in 1960. The population which was 4,073 in 1950[^cite_note-27] doubled to 8,110 in 1960.[^cite_note-28] By 1970 the population in Simi is reported by the census as 59,832.

### Four communities prior to modern residential development

The pioneers arrived in the late 1860s – 1870s and ever since, this has been 'The Valley of Simi.' But, not all the communities in the valley were known as 'Simi.' There was the township of Simi (known as 'Simiopolis' for about a six-month period in 1888). In the valley there were also the communities of Santa Susana, Community Center and the Susana Knolls (known first as Mortimer Park) at different points in time.

**Simi** – In late 1887–1888, the incorporation of Simi Land and Water Company came about. El Rancho Simí was divided into ranches and farms by that corporation, and advertised for sale to midwestern and New England states. An investor group, the California Mutual Benefit Colony of Chicago, purchased land and laid out a townsite (located between First and Fifth Streets and from Los Angeles south to Ventura Ave), named it 'Simiopolis' and shipped twelve pre-cut, partially assembled houses from a lumberyard in Chicago via rail to Saticoy, then brought by wagon to Simi. These are known as 'colony houses.' This was the first 'neighborhood' in Simi. Stores sprung up on Los Angeles Ave, and the first Simi School was built in 1890 on Third and California Streets, and was used until Simi Elementary was built in the mid-1920s.

**Santa Susana** – In 1903 the [Santa Susana Train Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Depot#cite_note-2) was built, and the railroad was complete through Simi Valley, except for the tunnel, which was completed in 1904. A small business community grew up near the Santa Susana Train Depot, which was located on the north side of Los Angeles Ave, just east of Tapo Street. Over time residential developments followed and the town of Santa Susana was born. The Depot was moved in 1975 by Rancho Simi Recreation and Park District to its current location off of Kuehner.

**Community Center** – In 1922 L.F. Roussey laid out the small development which became known as Community Center. The driving force behind this development was the need for a High School in Simi Valley, as well as an elementary school in a more central location in the valley. The first graduating class from Simi High School was 1924, Simi Elementary was completed in 1926, The Methodist Church (which is now the Cultural Arts Center) was built in 1924. Numerous houses were built in Community Center in the 1920s and 1930s. The Simi Valley Woman's Club was located there as well (the building which served as the clubhouse for the Woman's Club was moved from the town of Simi). The Woman's Club club house was used by many individuals and organizations as a community meeting place.

**Mortimer Park (the Susana knolls)** – The area that is now the Knolls was a nearly 1,800-acre parcel of land (730 ha) that was purchased by Mr. and Mrs. Lewis T. Mortimer in the early 1920s. They planned on selling the lots for cabins, or vacation homes. The lots were very small (30 x 50 feet), and the Mortimers did not take the mountainous nature of the land into account, so quite often the lots were not buildable. Oftentimes several lots were needed to build structures. In 1944 the Garden Club, an active community organization in the area petitioned the county supervisors to change the name of Mortimer Park to the Susana Knolls.

*The first attempt to incorporate the towns of Simi, the area known as Community Center (93065) and Santa Susana (93063) in 1966 was unsuccessful. The second attempt in 1969 was successful, with residents voting 6,454 to 3,685 in favor of incorporation. 59% of eligible voters turned out for this vote. Susana Knolls is an unincorporated area of the Valley. Voters also voted whether to call this newly incorporated city 'Santa Susana' or 'Simi Valley.' The name Simi Valley garnered 2,000 more votes than Santa Susana.[^cite_note-\:2-29]*

### Other items of historical interest

#### Santa Susana Field Laboratory



1990 aerial view of the [Energy Technology Engineering Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Technology_Engineering_Center) at the Santa Susana Field Laboratory, Simi Hills, Simi Valley

The 2,848 acres (1,153 ha) [Santa Susana Field Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Field_Laboratory) located in the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills), was used for the development of pioneering nuclear reactors and rocket engines beginning in 1948. The site was operated by [Atomics International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomics_International) and [Rocketdyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocketdyne) (originally both divisions of the [North American Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aviation) company). The Rocketdyne division developed a variety of [liquid rocket engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_rocket_engine). Rocket engine tests were frequently heard in Simi Valley. The Atomics International division of North American Aviation designed, built and operated the [Sodium Reactor Experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_Reactor_Experiment), which in 1957 became the first United States commercial [nuclear reactor](22151.md) to supply electricity to a public power system,[^cite_note-30] when it powered the city of [Moorpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorpark,_California) (the government owned [BORAX-III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BORAX_experiments) reactor had previously powered [Arco, Idaho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arco,_Idaho) for around an hour in 1955). The last nuclear reactor operated at SSFL in 1980 and the last rocket engine was produced in 2006. The SSFL has been closed to development and testing. The site is undergoing investigation and removal of the nuclear facilities and cleanup of the soil and groundwater. The Boeing Company, the US DOE, and NASA are responsible for the cleanup.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

In July 1959, the [Sodium Reactor Experiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_Reactor_Experiment) suffered a serious incident when 13 of the reactor's 43 fuel elements partially melted resulting in the controlled release of radioactive gas to the atmosphere. The reactor was repaired and returned to operation in September 1960. The incident at the Sodium Reactor Experiment has been a source of controversy in the community. Technical analysis of the incident intended to support a lawsuit against the current landowner ([The Boeing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Boeing_Company)) asserts the incident caused the much greater release of radioactivity than the accident at [Three Mile Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Mile_Island_accident).[^cite_note-33] Boeing's technical response concludes the monitoring conducted at the time of the incident, shows only the allowable amount of radioactive gasses were released, and a Three Mile Island-scale release was not possible.[^cite_note-34] The case was settled, it is reported, with a large payment by Boeing. In September 2009, The U.S. Department of Energy sponsored a [public workshop](https://web.archive.org/web/20170513135251/http://www.etec.energy.gov/Community_Involvement/Public%20Meetings/SRE_Workshop.html) where three nuclear reactor experts shared their independent analysis of the July 1959 incident.[^cite_note-35]

The Santa Susana Field Laboratory also hosted the [Energy Technology Engineering Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_Technology_Engineering_Center). The center performed the design, development and testing of liquid metal reactor components for the [United States Department of Energy](62866.md) from 1965 until 1998.[^cite_note-36]

The Santa Susana Field Laboratory includes sites identified as historic by the [American Institute of Aeronautics and Astronautics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_of_Aeronautics_and_Astronautics) and by the [American Nuclear Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Nuclear_Society). The [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) listed [Burro Flats Painted Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burro_Flats_Painted_Cave) is located within the Santa Susana Field Laboratory, on a portion of the site owned by the [U.S. Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Government). The drawings within the cave have been termed "the best preserved Indian pictograph in Southern California".

## Geography



Simi Valley aerial from west



[Santa Susana Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Pass) is a [mountain pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_pass) in the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills) connecting the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley) and the [Simi Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Valley).

Simi Valley is a city located in the very southeast corner of [Ventura County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County), bordering the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley) in [Los Angeles County](79734.md), and is a part of the [Greater Los Angeles Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles_Area). The city of Simi Valley primarily consists of the [eponymous valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Valley) itself.[^cite_note-37] The city of Simi Valley borders the [Santa Susana Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Mountains) to the north, the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills) to the east and south, and is adjacent to [Thousand Oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Oaks,_California) to the southwest and [Moorpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorpark,_CA) to the west. Simi Valley is connected to the nearby San Fernando Valley by the [Santa Susana Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Pass) in the extreme east of Simi Valley. The [syncline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syncline)[^cite_note-38] Simi Valley is located in the western part of the region called the [Transverse Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transverse_Ranges).[^cite_note-39] While the Santa Susana Mountains separate the valley from the [Los Padres National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Padres_National_Forest) in the north, the Simi Hills separate it from [Conejo Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conejo_Valley) in the south. In the extreme east is [Rocky Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Peak), one of Santa Susana Mountains' highest peaks, which is a dividing line between [Ventura County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County) to the west and [Los Angeles County](79734.md) to the east. On the other side of the valley, in the extreme west side of Simi Valley is [Mount McCoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_McCoy_\(Simi_Valley\)), which may be most known for its 12 ft. concrete cross that sits at its peak. The physiographical valley is a structural as well as a [topographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topographic) depression.[^cite_note-40] The Simi Valley, just as neighboring [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley), owes its existence and shape to the faulting and folding of the rocks. It is essentially a structural valley and not wholly the work of erosion.[^cite_note-41] It is drained by the [Calleguas Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calleguas_Creek) and also its principal tributary, [Conejo Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conejo_Creek). Both of these originate in the Santa Susana Mountains.

According to the United States Census Bureau, the city has a total area of 42.2 sq mi (109.4 km<sup>2</sup>), comprising 41.5 sq mi (107.4 km<sup>2</sup>) of land and 0.77 sq mi (2.0 km<sup>2</sup>), or 1.81%, of it is water. Simi Valley is located northwest of the Los Angeles neighborhood of [Chatsworth](336125.md) and approximately 30 mi (50 km) from [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles), 380 mi (610 km) south of [San Francisco](49728.md), 160 mi (260 km) north of [San Diego](28504.md), and 350 mi (560 km) south of [Sacramento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sacramento). Commutes to Los Angeles are usually via the [State Route 118](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_118) (Ronald Reagan Freeway) or the Southern California [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)) commuter train, which makes several daily trips from Simi Valley. Simi Valley has a [mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate). Temperate variations between day and night tend to be relatively big. The mean annual temperature is 64.1 degrees (17.8 °C), while the annual precipitation is 18.39 inches (467 mm). The precipitation remains less than one inch for seven months – April until October, – while the precipitation exceeds four inches in the two wettest months – January and February. While the mean temperature is at its lowest at 53.6 degrees (12.0 °C) in December, the mean temperature in July and August exceeds 76 degrees (24 °C).[^cite_note-42]

Simi Valley has been the victim of several natural disasters, including the flood of 1967, the storm of 1983, the 1988 lightning strike, as well as the [1994 Northridge earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Northridge_earthquake) and numerous wildfires.[^cite_note-\:2-29]

### Climate

Simi Valley has a warm and dry climate during summer when mean temperatures tend to be in the 70s °F (low 20s °C). The city's climate cools during winter when mean temperatures tend to be in the 50s °F (low 10s °C). Because of its relatively low elevation, the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills) typically experience rainy, mild winters. Snow is rare in the Simi Hills, even in the highest areas. The warmest month of the year is August with an average maximum temperature of 96 °F (36 °C), while the coldest month of the year is December with an average minimum temperature of 38 °F (3 °C). Temperature variations between night and day tend to be relatively large during summer, with a difference that can reach 38 °F (21 °C), and moderate during winter with an average difference of 29 °F (16 °C).&#x20;

The annual average precipitation in Simi Valley is 17.9 inches (450 mm). Winter months tend to be wetter than summer months. The wettest month of the year is February with an average rainfall of 4.8 inches (120 mm). Simi Valley gets 18 inches (460 mm) of rain per year, while the United States average is 37. Snowfall is 0 inches (0 mm), while the U.S. average is 25 inches (640 mm) of snow per year. The number of days with measurable precipitation is 25. On average, there are 277 sunny days in Simi Valley per year. The July high is approximately 96 °F (36 °C). The January low is 39 °F (4 °C).[^cite_note-43] The record low is 18 degrees Fahrenheit (−8 °C) (recorded in February 1989)[^cite_note-weather.com-44] and the record high is 116 degrees Fahrenheit (47 °C) (recorded in August 1985).[^cite_note-weather.com-44]

### Natural hazards



Wildfire in the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills), 2003. U.S. Air Force [C-130 Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130_Hercules) pilots flew eight [C-130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130) cargo airplanes and dropped 129,600 gallons of retardant on the fire.

An aspect of Simi Valley's location, situated beside the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills), is that it lies in a high-risk area for the wildfires that sweep through Southern California's mountain ranges every few years. Simi Valley is also at risk for earthquakes. The valley is surrounded by [faults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)); the closest ones being the Santa Rosa Fault to the Northwest, the Northridge Hills Fault to the Northeast, and the Chatsworth Fault to the South. In 1994, portions of Simi Valley received significant damage from the [Northridge earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Northridge_earthquake).[^cite_note-46]

#### Wildfires

In autumn 2003, the [Simi Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Fire) burned about 108,000 acres (440 km<sup>2</sup>). A 2005 fire started on September 28 and burned an estimated 7,000 acres (30 km<sup>2</sup>). On September 29, the fire was estimated to be 17,000 acres (70 km<sup>2</sup>). More than 1,000 firefighters worked against the tricky combination of dry brush, low humidity and temperatures in the high 90s °F (high 30s °C) along the line that divides Los Angeles and Ventura counties. The fire was later brought under control and extinguished, without serious injury. Three homes were lost in outlying areas, but none within the city limits.

### Wildlife

The Simi Hills are a [wildlife corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_corridor) linkage from the [Santa Monica Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Mountains) to the [Topatopa Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topatopa_Mountains), [San Gabriel Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gabriel_Mountains), and other [Transverse Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transverse_Ranges) further east.[^cite_note-50] Simi Hills' undeveloped native habitat provides routes that protect larger land wildlife of the Santa Monica Mountains from genetic isolation. Large sections of the Simi Hills are protected by parks and open space preserves.

Before the 1960s, Simi Valley once boasted a strong community of Latino families, many of whom worked for white ranchers. However, the housing boom in the 1960s and 1970s attracted many white Americans leaving urban areas in [Los Angeles](18110.md) and the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley).[^cite_note-61] This turned Simi Valley into a predominately white city, but the percentage of those who identified as non-Hispanic white began to decrease from 86.2% in 1980 to 54% in 2020.

## Arts and culture



The [Ronald Reagan Presidential Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan_Presidential_Library) in Simi Valley

[The Simi Valley Cultural Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Simi_Valley_Cultural_Arts_Center?action=edit\&redlink=1) opened in November, 1995 residing in a former neoclassical-style Methodist Church building that was built in 1924.[^cite_note-74]

The [Ronald Reagan Presidential Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan_Presidential_Library), located in Simi Valley, was visited by almost 400,000 people in 2014.[^cite_note-75] After the [death and state funeral of Ronald Reagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Ronald_Reagan), the former US president was buried at the library in June 2004. The library adjoins a hangar in which the Boeing 707 SAM 27000 (Air Force One), which served presidents Nixon through G.W. Bush, is housed and available for tours. In the pavilion are various automobiles used to transport the president, as well as Marine One, the presidential helicopter.

The Simi Valley Public Library opened in 1916, and moved to its current building on February 24, 1980.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77]

## Government

### Local government

See also: [List of mayors of Simi Valley, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Simi_Valley,_California)

Simi Valley's government uses the "[council-manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council-manager_government)" form of government.[^cite_note-88] This means that the city council is composed of one mayor, elected every two years, and four council members elected for four-year terms. The city council appoints both the city attorney and [city manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_manager), who heads the executive branch of the city government. The city manager appoints the various department heads for the city, and acts as the city clerk and city treasurer.

According to the 2008–2009 Comprehensive Annual Financial Report Fund Financial Statements, the city's various funds had \$89.3 million in Revenues, \$86.3 million in expenditures, \$139.9 million in total assets, \$26.1 million in total liabilities, and \$158.5 million in investments.[^cite_note-89]

Simi Valley was at one time considered a [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_the_United_States) stronghold politically, along with the neighboring city of [Thousand Oaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thousand_Oaks,_California).[^cite_note-Los_Angeles_Times-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] However, in recent elections, both cities have become [left-leaning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left_politics).[^cite_note-108]

In the [state legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Legislature), Simi Valley is in [the 27th senatorial district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California's_27th_senatorial_district), represented by [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Democratic_Party) [Henry Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Stern_\(California_politician\)), and in [the 42nd Assembly district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California's_42nd_State_Assembly_district), represented by [Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Democratic_Party) [Jacqui Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacqui_Irwin).

In the [United States House of Representatives](19468510.md), Simi Valley is in [California's 26th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California's_26th_congressional_district), represented by [Democrat](5043544.md) [Julia Brownley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julia_Brownley). In the [United States Senate](24909346.md), California is represented by [Alex Padilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Padilla)[^cite_note-109] and [Adam Schiff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Schiff).[^cite_note-110]

As of the 2024 General Election results, all of Simi Valley's elected representatives are from the [Democratic Party](5043544.md).[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

In November 2018, [Katie Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Hill) unseated Republican Steve Knight and became the first Democratic woman to represent the district in the House of Representatives. Additionally, in 2022, [Alex Padilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Padilla) became the first Latino elected to the Senate from California.[^cite_note-114]

## Education

Simi Valley is served by the [Simi Valley Unified School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Valley_Unified_School_District).

There are five high schools located in Simi Valley: [Royal High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_High_School_\(California\)), [Grace Brethren High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grace_Brethren_High_School), [Santa Susana High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_High_School), [Simi Valley High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Valley_High_School), and [Apollo High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo_High_School_\(Simi_Valley,_California\)) (a continuation school).

There are three middle schools located in Simi Valley: Hillside Middle School, Valley View Middle School, and Sinaloa Middle School.

Schools of higher education in Simi Valley include [Eternity Bible College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternity_Bible_College) and [American Jewish University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jewish_University).

## Infrastructure

### Transportation



[Simi Valley station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Valley_station)



[Ronald Reagan Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan_Freeway)

The Montalvo Cutoff, a railroad line opened by the [Southern Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Pacific_Transportation_Company) on March 20, 1904, to improve the alignment of its [Coast Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Line_\(California\)), runs east–west through the valley.[^cite_note-115] In 1905, the longest train tunnel in the United States at that time was completed at the east end of Simi Valley. [Tunnel #26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Tunnel) is still being used linking Simi Valley and the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley) through the Santa Susana Mountains.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] The area was originally served by the [Santa Susana Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Depot) which was also opened in 1904 as a combination passenger and freight depot built by the Southern Pacific and located on Los Angeles Avenue near Tapo Street. The station remained in use for the 60 years.

[Simi Valley station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Valley_station) is used by Amtrak and [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)) on the railroad's [Ventura County Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County_Line), and by [Simi Valley Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_small_Southern_California_transit_agencies#Simi_Valley_Transit).

### Public safety

Law enforcement is provided by the [Simi Valley Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Valley_Police_Department), and the [Ventura County Fire Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County_Fire_Department) provides fire protection. [American Medical Response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Medical_Response), in conjunction with Ventura County Fire Department, provide emergency medical services at the [Advanced Life Support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Life_Support) level.

## In popular culture



Actors in a death scene at [Corriganville Movie Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corriganville_Movie_Ranch) in 1963

Due to lying within the [30-mile zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30-mile_zone), Simi Valley has long been a popular [entertainment industry](418334.md) location.[^cite_note-120]

- Simi Valley and the surrounding hills have been the site of several television shows, including the long-running series *[Gunsmoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunsmoke)* and *[M\*A\*S\*H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M*A*S*H_\(TV_series\))*.

- Established in 1937 and opened to the public in 1949, the [Corriganville Movie Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corriganville_Movie_Ranch), established by [Ray "Crash" Corrigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_%22Crash%22_Corrigan), is located at the extreme Eastern end of Simi Valley and was the production site for many movies and television shows. Today the site is open to the public as Corriganville Park, just off the Santa Susana Pass Road.

- The popular 1970s television show *[Little House on the Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_House_on_the_Prairie_\(1974_TV_series\))* utilized an expansive collection of sets constructed throughout the hilly landscapes of [Big Sky Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Sky_Ranch) in the [Tapo Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapo_Canyon) hills north of Simi Valley and [Santa Clarita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Clarita,_California), California. In addition to the Little House itself, the entire set for the town of Walnut Grove was built atop the hills. After finishing his work on the series, [Michael Landon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Landon) blew up the town (which became part of the final movie)

  [^cite_note-121]

  &#x20;but the Little House itself was left intact. In July 2004, the house was destroyed by a devastating California wildfire.

  [^cite_note-122]

- The 1973 film *[The Doberman Gang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Doberman_Gang)* was filmed entirely in Simi Valley, with the actual Bank of A. Levy as the backdrop for the robbery scenes.

- In 1983, [Colleen McCullough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colleen_McCullough)'s TV mini-series *[The Thorn Birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Thorn_Birds_\(miniseries\))* was brought to life in a remote corner of the Simi Valley. [Australia](4689264.md) presented too many hurdles for producers, the least of which was the restriction that only two American actors star in any movie filmed there; the rest had to be Australian. Location scouts went scouring, and Simi Valley, some of which strongly resembles parts of the Australian countryside, was chosen for the famous Cleary ranch and sheep station, Drogheda.

- In the 1984 film *[Bachelor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bachelor_Party_\(1984_film\))* starring [Tom Hanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hanks), the MANN 6 Movie Theater, formerly located within the Sycamore Shopping Center, was used for the movie theater scene.

- The 1986 western comedy film *[Three Amigos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Amigos)* was partially filmed here.

- The [Brandeis-Bardin Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandeis-Bardin_Institute)'s House of the Book is the location of the original [Power Ranger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_Ranger) Power Chamber.

- The 1982 hit horror film *[Poltergeist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltergeist_\(1982_film\))* was filmed on Roxbury Street in Simi Valley. At the time, the homes were new and the land behind the street was free, allowing plenty of access for studio trucks. 4267 Roxbury Street (the Freeling house in the film) suffered substantial earthquake damage in the 1994 [Northridge earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northridge_earthquake).

- For the 1992 movie [*Sneakers*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sneakers_\(1992_film\)) with [Robert Redford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Redford), the Gibraltar Savings building (later Countrywide at 400 Countrywide Way) was transformed to The Playtronics Toy company. The entire front lobby and hallways were redone for the movie and then returned to their original design. Some of the filmings were done from hills across from the building. Many scenes were shot at night with the permission of the residents, due to the lighting required.

- The delivery of the ransom money in *[The Big Lebowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Big_Lebowski)* shows a highway sign naming Simi Valley.

- The video for "[Bullet with Butterfly Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullet_with_Butterfly_Wings)" by [The Smashing Pumpkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Smashing_Pumpkins) was filmed in Simi Valley.

- In the 1995 film *[Species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species_\(film\))*, the character, Dr. Laura Baker, is said to be from Simi Valley.

- In the 2001 comedy *[Joe Dirt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Dirt)*, the character found his long-lost parents in a trailer home park in Simi Valley (On another version of the movie, it was changed to [Yucca Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucca_Valley,_California), California).

- The 1992 children's comedy *[Honey, I Blew Up the Kid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey,_I_Blew_Up_the_Kid)* was filmed in and around 676 Coldbrook Pl.

- In the 1992 film *[Forever Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forever_Young_\(1992_film\))* starring [Mel Gibson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Gibson) the test airstrip scenes and the highway chase scene were filmed on the west end of Simi Valley bordering Moorpark.

- Most of the [2003 film adaptation of *The Cat in the Hat*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cat_in_the_Hat_\(2003_film\)), starring [Mike Myers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Myers_\(actor\)) and [Dakota Fanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dakota_Fanning), was filmed in Simi Valley. The elaborate faux suburb where most of the film takes place was built on vacant land in a hilly area in West Simi Valley.

- Parts of Viva Rock Vegas, the sequel to the original Flintstones movie, was filmed at Rocky Peak.

- The video for "[Hexagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagram_\(song\))," by [Deftones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deftones), was filmed with fans watching the band play the song in an indoor skatepark in Simi Valley.

- The 2003 video game *[Black & Bruised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_&_Bruised)* has a character, Jumping Janet, whose hometown is Simi Valley.

- The skatepark in the 2005 movie *[Bad News Bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_News_Bears)* is in Simi Valley.

- In 2005, the PBS children's television series *[Postcards from Buster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postcards_from_Buster)* featured skateboard culture; interviewing local children and visiting the indoor skateboard park, Skatelab.

- The "Retail Rodeo" scenes from the [Jennifer Aniston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennifer_Aniston) movie *[The Good Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Good_Girl)* were filmed in the Ralph's shopping center on L.A. Ave. The set was constructed inside a vacant retail space.

- Nu-metal band [Limp Bizkit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limp_Bizkit) filmed the music video for the single "[Break Stuff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Break_Stuff)" at Skatelab, a skate park in Simi Valley.

- The 2006 comedy *[The Benchwarmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Benchwarmers)* was filmed on location in Knolls Park and Santa Susana Park, both located in Simi Valley.

- In March 2008, *[G.I. Joe: The Rise of Cobra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G.I._Joe:_The_Rise_of_Cobra)* started filming in the northern hills above Simi Valley, near the *Little House on the Prairie* site.

- The [Patrick Swayze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Swayze) movie [*Three Wishes*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Wishes_\(film\)) used a baseball field in Simi Valley, near the Santa Susana Pass.

- In 2009, the rock band [AFI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFI_\(band\)) filmed their music video for "Beautiful Thieves", the second single from their album *[Crash Love](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crash_Love)*, in a mansion in Simi Valley.

- The 2009 [Hallmark Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallmark_Channel) movie *Always and Forever* was filmed in various locations throughout Simi Valley and [Moorpark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorpark,_California), California.

- In 2009 [Gavin Rossdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gavin_Rossdale) filmed part of his music video "[Forever May You Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forever_May_You_Run)" in an office building on 555 E. Easy Street in Simi Valley.
