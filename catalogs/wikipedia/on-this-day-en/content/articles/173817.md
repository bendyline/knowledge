**Jiangxi**[^cite_note-6] is an inland [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) in [east China](683693.md). Spanning from the banks of the [Yangtze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze) river in the north into hillier areas in the south and east, it borders [Anhui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhui) to the north, [Zhejiang](96602.md) to the northeast, [Fujian](88585.md) to the east, [Guangdong](65109.md) to the south, [Hunan](166409.md) to the west, and [Hubei](83319.md) to the northwest. Major prefecture cities include its capital [Nanchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang), [Ganzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzhou), and [Jiujiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiujiang).

Jiangxi was one of the earliest Communist bases in China. The [Nanchang uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang_uprising) took place in Jiangxi on 1 August 1927, beginning the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War). In 1931, the [Chinese Soviet Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Soviet_Republic)'s [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangxi_Soviet) was established in [Ruijin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruijin), which is sometimes called the "Former Red Capital".[^cite_note-7]

The southern half of Jiangxi is hilly and mountainous, with ranges and valleys interspersed; notable mountains and mountain ranges include [Mount Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lu), the [Jinggang Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinggang_Mountains) and [Mount Sanqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sanqing). The northern half is comparatively lower in altitude. The [Gan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_River) flows through the province.

Although the majority of Jiangxi's population is [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), Jiangxi is linguistically diverse. It is considered the center of [Gan Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_Chinese); [Hakka Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese) is also spoken to some degree. Jiangxi is rich in mineral resources, leading the provinces of China in deposits of [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), [tungsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten), [gold](12240.md), [silver](27119.md), [uranium](31743.md), [thorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorium), [tantalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalum), [niobium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niobium) and [lithium](17561.md).[^cite_note-8]

## History

Main article: [History of Jiangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jiangxi)

Jiangxi is centered on the [Gan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_River) valley, which historically provided the main north–south transport route of south China. The corridor along the Gan River is one of the few easily traveled routes through the otherwise mountainous and rugged terrain of the south-eastern mountains. This open corridor was the primary route for trade and communication between the [North China Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_China_Plain) and the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River) valley in the north and the territory of modern [Guangdong](65109.md) province in the south. As a result, Jiangxi has been strategically important throughout much of China's history.

Jiangxi was outside the sphere of influence of early Chinese civilization during the [Shang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty) (16th to 11th centuries BC). It is likely that peoples collectively known as the [Baiyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiyue) inhabited the region. During the [Spring and Autumn period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_period), the northern part of modern Jiangxi formed the western frontier of the state of [Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_\(state\)). After Wu was conquered by the [state of Yue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yue_\(state\)) (a power based in modern northern [Zhejiang](96602.md)) in 473 BC, the state of [Chu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_\(state\)) (based in modern [Hubei](83319.md)) took over northern Jiangxi and there may have been some Yue influence in the south. Chu subjugated Yue in 333 BC. In 223 BC, when [Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_\(state\)) conquered Chu, a majority of the Jiangxi area was recorded to be put under Jiujiang Commandery situated in [Shouchun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shou_County) (壽春).[^cite_note-9] However the commandery was ineffective and ended shortly when Qin falls.

[Yuzhang Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuzhang_Commandery?action=edit\&redlink=1) (豫章, Gan: Ì-zong) was established in Jiangxi at the beginning of the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty), possibly before the death of [Xiang Yu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_Yu) in 202 BC, and it was also the first commandery set up by Chinese dynasty in Jiangxi. It was named after the Yuzhang River (豫章江, Gan: Ì-zong Kong), the original name of Gan River. "Gan" has become the abbreviation of the province. In 201, eight counties were added to the original seven of Qin, and three more were established in later years. Throughout most of the Han dynasty the commandery's eighteen counties covered most of the modern province of Jiangxi. The county seats of Nanchang, Gan, Yudu, Luling among others were located at the sites of modern major cities. Other counties, however, have been moved or abolished in later centuries.

Under the reign of [Emperor Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Wu_of_Han) of the [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty), Yuzhang Commandery was assigned to [Yangzhou Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangzhou_Province?action=edit\&redlink=1), as part of a trend to establish provinces (*[zhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhou_\(political_division\))*) all across China. In 291 AD, during the [Western Jin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Jin_dynasty), Jiangxi became its own *Zhou* called Jiangzhou (江州, Gan: Kong-chiu). During the [Southern and Northern Dynasties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_and_Northern_Dynasties), Jiangxi was under the control of the southern dynasties, and the number of *zhou* slowly grew.

During the [Sui dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_dynasty), there were seven [commanderies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandery_\(China\)) and twenty-four counties in Jiangxi. During the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty), another commandery and fourteen counties were added. Commanderies were then abolished, becoming *zhou* (henceforth translated as "prefectures" rather than "provinces").

[Circuits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_\(administrative_division\)) were established during the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) as a new top-level administrative division. At first Jiangxi was part of the [Jiangnan Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangnan_Circuit) (lit. "Circuit south of the Yangtze"). In 733, this circuit was divided into western and eastern halves. Jiangxi was found in the western half, which was called [Jiangnanxi Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangnanxi_Circuit) (lit. "Western circuits south of the Yangtze"). This is the source of the modern name "Jiangxi".

The [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) collapsed in 907, heralding the division of the [Five Dynasties and Ten Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Dynasties_and_Ten_Kingdoms) period. Jiangxi first belonged to [Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_\(Ten_Kingdoms\)) (吳, Gan: Ng), then to [Southern Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Tang) (南唐, Gan: Nām-thóng). Both states were based in modern-day [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing), further down the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River).

After the ten Chinese kingdoms, Jiangnanxi Circuit was reestablished with nine prefectures and four army districts (with sixty-eight districts). Jiangxi was later divided into thirteen different circuits, and Jiangxi Province was established for the first time. This province also included the majority of modern [Guangdong](65109.md). Jiangxi acquired (more or less) its modern borders.

After the fall of the Qing regime, Jiangxi became one of the earliest bases for the [Communists](7175.md) and many peasants were recruited to join the growing people's revolution. The [Nanchang Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang_Uprising) took place in Jiangxi on 1 August 1927, during the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War). Later the Communist leadership hid in the mountains of southern and western Jiangxi, hiding from the Kuomintang's attempts to eradicate them. In 1931, the [Chinese Soviet Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Soviet_Republic)'s government was established in [Ruijin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruijin), which is sometimes called the "Former Red Capital" (红色故都, Gan: Fūng-set Kū-tu), or just the "Red Capital". In 1935, after complete encirclement by the Nationalist forces, the Communists broke through and began the [Long March](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March) to [Yan'an](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yan'an).

From 1930 to 1934, the National Government carried out five military campaigns against the Jiangxi Soviet area. Its brutal two-party battles and cleansing (including the internal cleansing of the Red Army and the cleaning of the post-war government) caused a large number of deaths or escapes, causing the population of Jiangxi to drop by 40%, until only 13.8 million people were left in 1936.[^cite_note-10]

In 1936, after the opening of the [Yuehan Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou%E2%80%93Hankou_railway) in Hunan, Jiangxi lost its important position regarding north–south traffic. In 1937, the east-west Zhegan Railway was opened to traffic, which changed the original traffic patterns in Jiangxi to a large extent. The Jiujiang Port (九江港) began to decline in importance.

Following the [Doolittle Raid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doolittle_Raid) during [World War II](32927.md), most of the B-25 American crews that came down in China eventually made it to safety with the help of Jiangxi locals. The Gan who helped them, however, paid dearly for sheltering the Americans. The [Imperial Japanese Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Army) began the [Zhejiang-Jiangxi Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhejiang-Jiangxi_Campaign) to intimidate the Chinese from helping downed American airmen. At least 10,000 civilians died in Jiangxi while searching for Doolittle's men.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

Jiangxi came under the full control of the CCP upon the establishment of the People's Republic of China in 1949. The Republican provincial government was evacuated to [Taichung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taichung) in [Taiwan Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_\(Republic_of_China_province\)) before dissolving itself that same year.[^cite_note-13]

Mountains surround Jiangxi on three sides, with the [Mufu Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mufu_Mountains), [Jiuling Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuling_Mountains), and [Luoxiao Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoxiao_Mountains) on the west; [Huaiyu Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huaiyu_Mountains) and [Wuyi Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuyi_Mountains) on the east; and the [Jiulian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiulian_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1) (九连山) and [Dayu Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayu_Mountains) in the south. The southern half of the province is hilly with ranges and valleys interspersed; while the northern half is flatter and lower in altitude. The highest point in Jiangxi is [Mount Huanggang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Huanggang) (黄岗山) in the Wuyi Mountains, on the border with [Fujian](88585.md). It has an altitude of 2,157 metres (7,077 ft).

The [Gan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_River) dominates the province, flowing through the entire length of the province from south to north. It enters [Lake Poyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Poyang) in the north, the largest freshwater lake of China; that lake in turn empties into the [Yangtze River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River), which forms part of the northern border of Jiangxi. Important [reservoirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir_\(water\)) include the [Zhelin Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhelin_Reservoir) in the northwest of the province on the [Xiushui River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiushui_River), and the [Wan'an Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wan'an_Reservoir?action=edit\&redlink=1) in the upper section of the Gan.

Jiangxi has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa* under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)), with short, cool, damp winters, and very hot, humid summers. Average temperatures are about 3 to 9 °C (37 to 48 °F) in January and 27 to 30 °C (81 to 86 °F) in July. Annual precipitation is 1,200 to 1,900 millimetres (47 to 75 in), much of it falling in the heavy rains occurring in late spring and summer.

[Nanchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang), the provincial capital and the most densely populated city, is one of the largest Chinese [metropolises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis). Nanchang is the hub of [Jiangxi civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Jiangxi) throughout its history, which plays a leading role in the commercial, intellectual and industrial and political fields.[^cite_note-14] [Ganzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzhou) is the largest subdivision of Jiangxi.

## Politics

Main articles: [Politics of Jiangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Jiangxi) and [List of provincial leaders of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provincial_leaders_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China)

The politics of Jiangxi is structured in a dual party-government system like all other governing institutions in [mainland China](55079.md).

The [Governor of Jiangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Jiangxi) is the highest-ranking official in the People's Government of Jiangxi. However, in the province's dual party-government governing system, the Governor has less power than the Jiangxi [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) Provincial [Committee Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Communist_Party_Committee_Secretary), colloquially termed the "Jiangxi CCP Party Chief".

## Economy

Jiangxi was a major recipient of China's investment in industrial capacity during the [Third Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Front_\(China\)) campaign.[^cite_note-\:6-28]<sup>: 298 </sup>

[Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) is the dominant crop in Jiangxi. Cash crops commonly grown include [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) and [rapeseed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapeseed). Jiangxi is the leading [producer of kumquats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumquat_production_in_China) in China, particularly in [Suichuan County](23044903.md).[^cite_note-huibu1988-29]

[Mining-related industries](58230808.md) are a major part of Jiangxi's economy.[^cite_note-\:1-30]<sup>: 23 </sup>Jiangxi is rich in mineral resources, leading the provinces of China in deposits of [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper), [tungsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten), [gold](12240.md), [silver](27119.md), [uranium](31743.md), [thorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thorium), [tantalum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tantalum), [niobium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niobium), among others. Noted centers of mining include [Dexing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dexing,_Jiangxi) (copper) and [Dayu County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayu_County) (tungsten).

It is located in extreme proximity to some of the richest provinces of China ([Guangdong](65109.md), [Zhejiang](96602.md), [Fujian](88585.md)), which are sometimes blamed for taking away talent and capital from Jiangxi.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-31]

Jiangxi has the lowest wages and third lowest property prices in all of China.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-31] As of 2016 Jiangxi's nominal GDP was CNY 1.84 trillion or US\$276.48 billion, and a per capita of CNY 40,400 or US\$6,082.[^cite_note-SNA2008-32]

### Economic and technological development zones

- Nanchang Export Processing Zone

Nanchang National Export Expressing Zone is located in Nanchang Hi-Tech Industrial Development Zone, it was approved by the State Council on May 8, 2006, and passed the national acceptance inspection on Sep 7th, 2007. It has a planning area of 1 km<sup>2</sup> (0.39 sq mi) and now has built 0.31 km<sup>2</sup> (0.12 sq mi). It enjoys simple and convenient customs clearances, and special preferential policies both for Nanchang National Export Expressing Zone and NCHDZ.[^cite_note-34]

- Nanchang National High-tech Industrial Development Zone

Nanchang National High-tech Industrial Development Zone (NCHDZ for short hereafter) is the only national grade high-tech zoned in Jiangxi, it was established in Mar. 1991. The zone covers an area of 231 km<sup>2</sup> (89 sq mi), in which 32 km<sup>2</sup> (12 sq mi) have been completed. NCHDZ possesses unique nature condition and sound industry foundation of accepting electronics industry. NCHDZ has brought 25% industrial added value and 50% industrial benefit and tax to Nanchang city by using only 0.4% land area.[^cite_note-35]

## Demographics



She ethnic townships in Jiangxi

The population of Jiangxi according [2020 Chinese census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_census) is 45.18 million.[^cite_note-Seventh-1] 99.73% of that is [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), predominantly [Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan-speaking_people) and [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_people). [Ganzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzhou), Jiangxi's largest city, has an especially large number of Hakka. Ethnic minorities include the [She people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_people).

Jiangxi and [Henan](166408.md) both have the most unbalanced gender ratios of all Chinese provinces. Based on a 2009 *[British Medical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Medical_Journal)* study, the ratio is over 140 boys for every 100 girls in the 1–4 age group.[^cite_note-40]

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Jiangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Jiangxi)



Porcelain workshop in Jingdezhen

Jiangxi is the main area of concentration of the [Gan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_Chinese) varieties of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language), spoken over most of the northern two-thirds of the province. Examples include the [Nanchang dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang_dialect), [Yichun dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yichun_dialect) and [Ji'an dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ji'an_dialect?action=edit\&redlink=1). The southern one-third of the province speaks [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese). There are also [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese), [Huizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huizhou_Chinese), and [Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Chinese) dialects spoken along the northern border.

[Ganju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gan_opera?action=edit\&redlink=1) (赣剧, Gan Opera) is the type of [Chinese opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_opera) performed in Jiangxi.

Although little known outside of the province, [Jiangxi cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangxi_cuisine) is rich and distinctive. Flavors are some of the strongest in China, with heavy use of [chili peppers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chili_pepper) and especially [pickled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickling) and [fermented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermentation_\(food\)) products.

[Jingdezhen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingdezhen) is widely regarded as the producer of the best [porcelain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcelain) in China,[^cite_note-55] it is known as the "porcelain capital" of China.

Jiangxi also was a historical center of [Chan Buddhism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chan_Buddhism).

Prominent examples of [Hakka architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_architecture) can be found in Jiangxi.

## Transportation

As of January 2015, Jiangxi had two [Yangtze River crossings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangtze_River_bridges_and_tunnels), both in Jiujiang.

### Rail

The [Beijing–Kowloon Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beijing%E2%80%93Kowloon_Railway) and [Shanghai–Kunming Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai%E2%80%93Kunming_Railway) crisscross the province and intersect at Nanchang, which also has a [high-speed rail link to Jiujiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang%E2%80%93Jiujiang_Intercity_Railway). In addition, Jiangxi is connected by rail to Anhui Province via the [Anhui–Jiangxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhui%E2%80%93Jiangxi_Railway) and [Tongling–Jiujiang Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongling%E2%80%93Jiujiang_Railway); to Hubei via the [Wuhan–Jiujiang Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuhan%E2%80%93Jiujiang_Railway); and to Fujian via the [Yingtan–Xiamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yingtan%E2%80%93Xiamen_Railway), [Hengfeng–Nanping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hengfeng%E2%80%93Nanping_Railway), [Ganzhou–Longyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganzhou%E2%80%93Longyan_Railway) and [Xiangtang–Putian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangtang%E2%80%93Putian_Railway).

The mountain peaks of Mount Lu National Park

There are several famous mountains in Jiangxi Province, including [Mount Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lu) in [Jiujiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiujiang), [Jinggang Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinggang_Mountains) at the border of Jiangxi and Hunan, and [Mount Sanqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sanqing) in Yushan county.

Near the northern port city of [Jiujiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiujiang) lies the well-known resort area of [Mount Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lu). Also near the city is the [Donglin Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donglin_Temple_\(Jiujiang\)), an important [Buddhist](3267529.md) temple in china.

Near the small city of [Yingtan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yingtan) is the resort area of [Longhushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longhushan), which purports to be the birthplace of [Taoism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism) and hence has great symbolic value to Taoists. The region has many temples, cave complexes, mountains and villages.

The [Mount Lu National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Lu_National_Park) has been a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md) since 1996.

[Kuling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuling,_Jiujiang) located on the top of [Mount Lu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Lu) is a summer resort developed by European in the 19th century. There were 3000 European living in [Kuling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuling,_Jiujiang), [Lushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lushan_City) and [Jiujiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiujiang) in summer time in 1920s.

In 2007, Jiangxi (specifically the [Zhelin Reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhelin_Reservoir), located in [Jiujiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiujiang)) was the filming location for the fifteenth series of the American TV show *[Survivor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survivor:_China)*.

The mountainous terrain and large forest coverage of Jiangxi has made it historically one of the more wild places of central China. [South China tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China_tiger) have been seen as recently as fifteen or twenty years ago and projects are underway to document evidence of existing tigers, if there are any. Several mountain areas along the northern border with Hunan and Hubei are potential sites for "wilderness" preserves specifically for protecting or even reintroducing tigers.

Other wildlife, though not plentiful, are more numerous in Jiangxi than in many other developed areas of China. Numerous species of birds are common, especially around the marshes of Lake Poyang in the north. Though protected, mammals such as [muntjac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muntjac), wild boar, civet cats, and [pangolins](51124.md), are still common enough that they'll even occasionally be seen in markets for sale as game meat, or possibly even in a forest.

The late [Paleocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleocene) [mesonychid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesonychidae), *[Jiangxia chaotoensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangxia_chaotoensis)* was found in the province, and named after it.
