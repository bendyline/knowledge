The **Nigerian bandit conflict** is an ongoing conflict between the country's [federal government](21387.md) and various gangs and ethnic [militias](20622.md). Starting in 2011, the insecurity remaining from the [conflict between the Fulani and Hausa](54964174.md) ethnic groups quickly allowed other criminal and [jihadist](7826589.md) elements to form in the region.

## Origins

The origins of the [bandit](658200.md) conflict can be traced back to [herder–farmer](54964174.md) conflicts that plague Nigeria. Environmental decline and the scarcity of water and arable land led to communities competing viciously for those limited resources. Unemployment, [large-scale poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_in_Nigeria), and weak local government have allowed for a steady stream of desperate people turning to criminal activity to earn a living. Large forested areas allow for concealment and the formation of camps deep in the forest. Unequipped police and military personnel are unable to reach these areas.[^cite_note-35]

## Escalation

Continued insecurity, [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification) and [jihadist](7826589.md) influence have allowed for a rise in attacks to take place. Large-scale weapons smuggling has allowed criminal gangs access to heavy weapons, increasing the deadliness of attacks which has affected the lives and loss of property worth billions of dollars, and the presence of these groups has driven away foreign investment. Underequipped local and federal forces, coupled with the harsh terrain, make offensive actions into the forest dangerous and susceptible to ambushes and attacks. Continued government inability to effectively deal with the problem has allowed the insecurity to spread and grow in ferocity.[^cite_note-36]

## Kidnapping

Bandits have been known to ride into villages on motorcycles to loot and kidnap the inhabitants, killing anyone who resists. Kidnapping is a very profitable venture in northern Nigeria and is increasingly spreading to some parts of southern Nigeria. Between 2011 and 2020, Nigerians paid at least 18 billion [naira](178686.md) to free family members and friends.[^cite_note-voanews20210228-13][^cite_note-37]

## Arms trade



Nigerian bandits with heavy weapons and RPGs, February 2021

[Illegal arms](42210.md) are prevalent in northwest Nigeria. Bandit gangs control [gold mines](16897370.md#Gold) and use the gold to purchase arms from internal and international arms dealers.[^cite_note-38] There are an estimated 60,000 illegal weapons in circulation in northwest Nigeria.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] The border of northern Nigeria is undefended, with only 1,950 personnel to police the whole border, making it easy for smuggling across the border.[^cite_note-Azeez_2021-41]

## Belligerents



Nigerian bandits inside their base, February 2021

In [Zamfara state](2227664.md) alone, there are (as of 2021) over 30,000 bandits and 100 camps.[^cite_note-42]

### Ali Kachalla

Ali Kawaje, better known by his alias Ali Kachalla,[^cite_note-vanguard20231212-43] was a bandit leader in his early 30s who was born in a small town called Madada near Dansadau. Kachalla controlled a bandit group of about 200 in the Kuyambana Forest. His main base of operations consisted of several huts along the Goron Dutse River, about 25 km south of Dansadau. Kachalla's gang directly controls the villages of Dandalla, Madada and Gobirawa Kwacha, from where he launched attacks on Dansadau and other neighboring communities.&#x20;

Kachalla's gang has carried out numerous attacks, most notably the downing of a [Nigerian Air Force](538869.md) [Alpha Jet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dassault/Dornier_Alpha_Jet) on 18 July 2021[^cite_note-44] and the destruction of a [Mowag Piranha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mowag_Piranha) armored personnel carrier in Dansadau on 23 July 2021.[^cite_note-45] Kachalla's gang has suffered defeats, most notably losing 30 men in a battle with an [Ansaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansaru) cell.[^cite_note-ReferenceC-17]

Kachalla was killed on 11 December 2023. Before his death, Kachalla and his fighters attacked a detachment of security operatives in Magorno, [Munya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munya,_Nigeria). The air component of Nigeria's Operation *Whirl Punch* was notified shortly after, and its aircraft scrambled to protect troops under attack. Aircraft spotted Kachalla and his men withdraw from the area on a convoy of 11 motorcycles. The aircraft trailed them to their hideout near Kopa Hills, and airstrikes were authorized, resulting in the deaths of Kachalla and several of his men.[^cite_note-vanguard20231212-43]

### Dogo Giɗe

Abubakar Abdullahi, known as [Dogo Giɗe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogo_Gi%C9%97e), is the leader of a bandit group near Dansadau. He is from the [Maru local government](24894504.md) and is in his 40s, married with children. He is known for killing bandit leader Buharin Daji and 24 of Daji's gang members by luring Daji to a peace meeting. He also killed a rival bandit leader named Damina who had attacked villages under Giɗe's control.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] Giɗe is believed to have formed criminal ties with [Boko Haram](23769768.md) in 2019.[^cite_note-premiumtimesng.com-49] He is also believed to have the financial capacity and connections to procure weapons.[^cite_note-50] He is believed to have masterminded many kidnapping raids, one of which involved the kidnapping of scores of students from the Federal Government College Yauri in [Kebbi State](2227643.md) in June 2021.[^cite_note-premiumtimesng.com-49]

### Kachalla Halilu Sububu Seno

Kachalla Halilu Sububu Seno is the leader of a [Fulani](102383.md) bandit group. He commands over 1,000 bandits in the Sububu Forest across [Zamfara State](2227664.md)[^cite_note-dailytrust.com-51] and has connections to bandit groups across Mali, Senegal, Burkina Faso, Cameroon and the Central African Republic. In 2019 he signed a peace treaty with officals in [Shinkafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkafi) but has shifted his activities elsewhere.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

His men are known for launching attacks and kidnapping of villagers and travellers in [Sabon Birni](24895511.md), [Rabah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rabah) and [Isa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isa,_Nigeria) in [Sokoto State](2227660.md), and the influence reaches as far as [Katsina State](2227638.md).[^cite_note-dailytrust.com-51] Halilu has become one of Nigeria's most dreaded bandits, having built a formidable militia of young men and stockpiling weapons.[^cite_note-dailytrust.com-51] On 13 September 2024 the [Nigerian Army](14943749.md) confirmed several gang members during a military operation in Mayanchi, [Maru](24894504.md). Government troops recovered two [rocket propelled grenades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_propelled_grenade) and many automatic rifles and ammunition.[^cite_note-54]

### Kachalla Turji

Kachalla Turji, also known as Gudda Turji, is the leader of a bandit group that operates along Sokoto Road, raiding towns, villages and settlements in the area. On 17 July 2021, Turji's main base was raided by security personnel, where they arrested his father. Turji then attacked the villages of Kurya, Keta, Kware, Badarawa, Marisuwa and Maberaya, killing 42, abducting 150 and burning 338 houses.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] He was originally from Shinkafi.[^cite_note-58]

### Dan Karami

Dan Karami was the leader of a bandit gang, reportedly second-in-command to an Islamic State-linked leader,[^cite_note-\:0-59] that operated around [Safana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safana), [Dan Musa](24869404.md), and [Batsari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batsari). Karami's group was responsible for [kidnapping 300 students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankara_kidnapping) from a secondary boarding school in [Kankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankara). On 23 January 2021, Karami was injured during a clash with a rival group headed by Mani Na Saleh Mai Dan Doki over the control of guns, ammunition and stolen cattle. The clash took place at [Illela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illela,_Niger) village and killed 20 of Karami's bandits and nine civilians.[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61]

He was killed in a joint military operation in north-west Nigeria on 12 April 2025, along with 100 of his suspected followers. Karami had been hiding in the Munumu Forest, where several criminal hideouts were also destroyed. His death followed an attack in which bandits kidnapped 43 villagers and killed 4 in Maigora, [Katsina State](2227638.md), earlier that week. Although the Nigerian Air Force previously claimed to have killed him in 2022, his death was confirmed in this operation as a significant blow to banditry in the region.[^cite_note-\:0-59]

### Adamu Aliero Yankuzo

Adamu Aliero Yankuzo is the leader of a bandit group that operates in the forested regions of Katsina and Zamfara states. He controls a bandit group numbering about 2,000. Yankuzo was born in Yankuzo village. On 16 June 2020, Yankuzo was declared wanted by the Katsina State Police Command for five million [Nigerian naira](178686.md). Yankuzo's gang has carried out a number of attacks, including the killing 52 people in Kadisau village in revenge for the arrest of his son on 9 June 2020.[^cite_note-auto1-20] He was declared wanted after his gang members confessed to the kidnapping of innocent villagers, killing of women and rustling of more than hundreds of cattle.[^cite_note-62]

### Bello Turji

Bello Turji Kachalla is the leader of a bandits and kidnappers gang that operates in [Zamfara](2227664.md) and [Sokoto States](2227660.md).[^cite_note-ReferenceA-63] He is known to have imposed levies on many villages and appointed leaders in two of the eastern Sokoto villages. He is notorious and ruthless as shown by him refusing to accept ransom after he kidnapped the father of the Zamafara House of Assembly speaker which eventually led to the elderly man's death.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-63] He was said to have little of Western education and did not even complete Islamic education. According to lecturer Dr. Murtala, Turji's group members are from an influential family, some of whom include Umaru Nagona, Mallam Ina Manara, and Bello Kagara.[^cite_note-BBC_News_Pidgin-64]

### Jihadist groups

[ISWAP](46529902.md) and [Boko Haram](23769768.md) have both claimed to have carried out attacks in northwest Nigeria, and some bandit groups have claimed to have formed alliances with the jihadist groups.[^cite_note-65] In a phone call intercepted by [American intelligence](501244.md) in October 2021, an unnamed jihadist group and a bandit group discussed kidnapping operations and negotiations between the groups.[^cite_note-66] Boko Haram is also believed to have sent specialized personnel, including bomb makers and military advisors, as well as military equipment to the Kaduna state to train and equip their bandit groups' allies.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68]

[Lakurawa](78327630.md) is active in northern Nigeria. Initially organized as a vigiliante militia to fight against bandits, the group became increasingly radical as well as oppressive. By 2023, it had effectively become another bandit group as well as aligned itself with [Islamism](15012.md) and possibly even the Islamic State.[^cite_note-reuters-7][^cite_note-69]

As of April 2025, Borno State Governor [Babagana Zulum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babagana_Zulum) reported that Boko Haram's renewed offensives and kidnappings signaled that the region was losing ground to insurgents. Boko Haram and ISWAP's actions led to significant civilian casualties and displacement, especially in north-eastern Nigeria.[^cite_note-70]

[Ansaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansaru), a jihadist group linked with [al-Qaeda](1921.md), is believed to have been operating in Kaduna State. It is believed they enter Nigeria through the porosity of the Niger and Benin borders with Nigeria.[^cite_note-vanguardngr.com-71] After going silent in 2013, Ansaru began attacking Nigerian military and police personnel and infrastructure,[^cite_note-72] including an ambush of a Nigerian military convoy on 15 January 2020.[^cite_note-73] Most of these terrorist and bandit groups have turned some forests in northern Nigeria to their operational base.[^cite_note-vanguardngr.com-71] In March 2020, Kaduna State Governor [Nasir El-Rufai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasir_El-Rufai) stated that there will be no negotiation or pardon for bandits and terrorist groups in the state.[^cite_note-vanguardngr.com-71]

## Refugees

At least 247,000 people have been displaced and 120 villages have been razed in continuing bandit activity in northwest Nigeria.[^cite_note-auto2-74][^cite_note-Blueprint_Newspapers_Limited_2020-75] At least 77,000 of the displaced have been forced into Niger's [Maradi Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maradi_Region), where cross-border raids and attacks continue. At least 11,320 refugees have been successfully relocated.[^cite_note-76]

## Southern Kaduna crisis



World Press Conference organized by the [Atyap Community Development Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atyap_Community_Development_Association) (ACDA) on May 1, 2023

Between 2011 and 2023, [mass killings occurred in Southern Kaduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killings_in_Southern_Kaduna) against indigenous peoples perpetred by Fulani bandits and extremists for [land grabbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_grabbing), [islamization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamization) and [Fulanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fulani_extremism), killing 1,500.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] Between 2011 and 2016, the governor said 204 people lost their lives while the Catholic Church in Kaduna State said 808 people were killed by the armed bandits.[^cite_note-79] In January 2020, the NGO [Christian Solidarity International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Solidarity_International) released a warning over a possible genocide against Nigerian Christians and referenced the attacks on Christian villages in [Southern Kaduna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Kaduna) as an example of rising violence.[^cite_note-80]

## Counteroffensives



Map of Nigerian bandit conflict

### Nigerian government operations

#### Operation Harbin Kunama

On 8 July 2016, President [Muhammadu Buhari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammadu_Buhari) announced that the Nigerian military would launch a military operation code-named Operation Harbin Kunama. The operation was carried out by the 223 armored battalion of the 1 Mechanised Division[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82] and targeted bandit groups in the Dansadau Forest. In the days before the announcement, convoys carried new military equipment into the Zamfara State, including tanks and [armoured fighting vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_fighting_vehicle).[^cite_note-83]

#### Operation Sharan Daji

In early 2016, Operation Sharan Daji was launched to combat bandits in the northwest. The operation was conducted by 31 Artillery Brigade and 2 Battalion of the first 1 Mechanised Division. By March, 35 bandits were killed, 36 guns were seized, 6,009 cattle were recovered, 49 bandit camps were destroyed and 38 bandits were captured.[^cite_note-84] In 2019, the Nigerian Army confirmed the killing of four bandits under Operation Sharan Daji.[^cite_note-85]

#### Operation Accord

On 5 June 2020, Operation Accord was launched as a joint task force of vigilantes and troops of the 312 Artillery Regiment. An air and ground offensive killed more than 70 bandits.[^cite_note-86] The operation led to the destruction of multiple bandit camps, including a camp belonging to Ansaru.[^cite_note-87]

#### October 2023 airstrikes

On 13 October 2023, Nigerian fighter jets carried out airstrikes on a gathering of bandits in Zamfara State. At least 100 bandits were killed and over 200 injured.[^cite_note-88]

#### December 2023 accidental airstrike

Main article: [Tudun Biri drone strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tudun_Biri_drone_strike)

On 3 December 2023, a [drone strike](38212231.md) was carried out by the Nigerian Armed Forces on Tudun Biri, Kaduna State. Targeting what they thought was a group of bandits, the army mistakenly hit a village, killing at least 88 civilians.

### Conflict between Ansaru and bandits

The first reported clash between Ansaru and bandit groups occurred on 28 September 2021, in [Birnin Gwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birnin_Gwari), killing 4 bandits of Ali Kachalla’s gang as well as 1 Ansaru member. The clashes had started when Ansaru demanded that the bandits stop abductions and invasions of other communities. Further engagements occurred during the week, inflicting huge losses to Kachalla’s gang, with Ansaru claiming to have killed 30 bandits.[^cite_note-89]

On 13 July 2022, a convoy of nearly 200 bandits on motorcycles battled with Ansaru in Damari, a small town in Kazage Ward, Birnin Gwari. The clashes lasted for approximately an hour, causing damage to several buildings and vehicles in the town, as well as the death of 2 civilians by fleeing bandits. Ansaru eventually repelled the attack, with locals hailing them for their defense of the community.[^cite_note-90] Despite Ansaru's victory, the bandits returned to Damari on 25 July unopposed, killing 3 people in the town. The bandits returned on 26 July, looting local shops and kidnapping 13 people.[^cite_note-91] Ansaru had seemingly lost control of the town, allowing bandits to move unchallenged and kidnap more than 50 people over the next few days, leading to a large exodus.[^cite_note-92]

A clash between Ansaru and bandits occurred in Damari on 21 November 2022, with Ansaru killing 5 of the bandits before retreating from the town.[^cite_note-93] On 14 December 2023, Birnin Gwari’s local bandits ambushed a group of Ansaru militants in the town, killing 5 of them. The bandits then overran Kuyello on 17 December as Ansaru retreated into nearby forests. Ansaru then battled the bandits in the forest, reportedly killing many.[^cite_note-94]

Until 2024, Dogo Giɗe’s gang and the jihadi group Ansaru had ties and an alliance against the government.[^cite_note-95] Ansaru started a battle against Giɗe over control of a gold mine in Kuyello on 1 April. Giɗe's group reportedly suffered numerous losses during the battle, including that of close associates, family members and wanted bandits Mudi and Murtala.[^cite_note-96]<https://www.theguardian.com/world/2026/jun/01/nigeria-bandits-crisis-batsari-katsina-state>

### Conflict between Boko Haram and Dogo Giɗe

Before 2023, [Boko Haram](23769768.md)-[Sadiku’s faction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiku_\(Boko_Haram\)) and [Dogo Giɗe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogo_Gi%C9%97e)’s bandit group had cooperated in several attacks and hijacks, such as the [Abuja–Kaduna train attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abuja%E2%80%93Kaduna_train_attack) in 2022.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98] In 2023 the faction broke ties with Gide, resulting in violent clashes. A former associate of Sadiku said that Sadiku had been "arrogant" and demanded respect from the bandits, leading to the fallout.[^cite_note-Barnett2026-99] In January 2025, Gide's men claimed the killing of 20 fighters from Sadiku's faction.[^cite_note-100] Despite this, the [Institute for Security Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Security_Studies) reported in July that Bakura was possibly mediating a deal between the two groups.[^cite_note-Adebayo080226-101]
