The 26 **cantons of Switzerland**[^cite_note-1] are the [member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federated_state) of the [Swiss Confederation](26748.md). The nucleus of the Swiss Confederacy in the form of the first three confederate allies used to be referred to as the *[Waldstätte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldst%C3%A4tte)*. Two important periods in the development of the [Old Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy) are summarized by the terms *[Acht Orte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight_Cantons)* ('Eight Cantons'; from 1353 to 1481) and *[Dreizehn Orte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Cantons)* ('Thirteen Cantons', from 1513 to 1798).[^cite_note-chrono-2]

Each canton of the [Old Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy), formerly also *Ort* ('lieu/locality', from before 1450), or *Stand* ('estate', from c. 1550), was a fully [sovereign state](28791.md) with its own border controls, army, and currency from at least the [Treaty of Westphalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Westphalia) (1648) until the establishment of the Swiss federal state in 1848, with a brief period of centralised government during the [Helvetic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_Republic) (1798–1803). The term *Kanton* has been widely used since the 19th century.[^cite_note-HLS-3]

The number of cantons was increased to 19 with the [Act of Mediation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediation_\(Switzerland\)) (1803), with the recognition of former subject territories as full cantons. The [Federal Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Treaty) of 1815 increased the number to 22 due to the accession of former [associates of the Old Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy#Associates). The [canton of Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Jura) acceded as the 23rd canton with its [secession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_separatism) from [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bern) in 1979.[^cite_note-4] The official number of cantons was increased to 26 in the [federal constitution of 1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Constitution), which designated former half-cantons as cantons.

The areas of the cantons vary from 37 km<sup>2</sup> (15 sq. mi.) ([Basel-Stadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Stadt)) to 7,105 km<sup>2</sup> (2743 sq. mi.) ([Grisons](169850.md)); the populations (as of 2018) range from 16,000 ([Appenzell Innerrhoden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzell_Innerrhoden)) to 1.5 million ([Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Z%C3%BCrich)).

## Terminology

The term *[canton](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/canton#English)*, now also used as the English term for [administrative subdivisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_\(country_subdivision\)) of other countries, originates in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_French) usage in the late 15th century (recorded in Fribourg in 1467),[^cite_note-5] from a word for "edge, corner", at the time the literal translation of [Early Modern High German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Modern_High_German) *Ort*.[^cite_note-6] After 1490, *canton* was increasingly used in French and Italian documents to refer to the members of the Swiss Confederacy.[^cite_note-HLS-3] English use of *[canton](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/canton)* in reference to the Swiss Confederacy (as opposed to the [heraldic sense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_\(heraldry\))) dates to the early 17th century.[^cite_note-7]

In the Old Swiss Confederacy, the term *[Ort](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/Ort#German)* (plural: *Orte*) was in use from the early 15th century as a generic term for the member cantons.[^cite_note-HLS-3] The founding cantons specifically were also known as *[Waldstätte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waldst%C3%A4tte)* 'forest settlements' (singular: *Waldstatt*). The formulaic *Stette und Waldstette* for the members of the early confederacy is recorded in the mid-14th century, used interchangeably with *Stett und Lender* ('cities and lands', 'city cantons and rural cantons') until the late 15th century.[^cite_note-Stette-8] *Ort* was increasingly replaced by *Stand* (plural: *Stände*) '[estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\))' about 1550, a term taken to imply liberty and sovereignty. Abolished in the Helvetic Republic, the term 'Stand' was revived in 1815 and remains in use today.[^cite_note-HLS-3][^cite_note-9]

The French term *canton* adopted into German [after 1648](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Westphalia), and then only in occasional use until the early 19th century: prominent usage of *Ort* and *Stand* gradually disappeared in German-speaking Switzerland from the time of the [Helvetic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_Republic). Only with the [Act of Mediation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Mediation) of 1803 did German *Kanton* become an official designation, retained in the [Swiss Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Constitution) of 1848.[^cite_note-HLS-3] [^cite_note-10]

The term *Stand* ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *état*, [Italian](14708.md): *stato*) remains in synonymous usage and is reflected in the name of the upper chamber of the Swiss Parliament, the [Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_States_\(Switzerland\)) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Ständerat*, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Conseil des États*, [Italian](14708.md): *Consiglio degli Stati*, [Romansh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language): *Cussegl dals Stadis*).

&#x20;In the modern era, since Neuchâtel ceased to be a principality in 1848, all Swiss cantons can be considered to have a [republican form of government](12229.md#Republics). Some cantons formally describe themselves as [republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) in their constitutions. This applies to the Romance-speaking cantons in particular: [Geneva](292834.md) (formally *République et canton de Genève*, 'Republic and canton of Geneva'), [Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Jura), [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Neuch%C3%A2tel), [Valais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Valais),[^cite_note-11] [Vaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Vaud)[^cite_note-12] and [Ticino](84147.md).[^cite_note-13]

## History

Further information: [Flags and coats of arms of cantons of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flags_and_coats_of_arms_of_cantons_of_Switzerland)



The "Thirteen-Canton Confederation" of the [Old Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy) (1513–1798)

In the 16th century, the [Old Swiss Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Swiss_Confederacy) was composed of 13 sovereign confederate allies (the [Thirteen Cantons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Cantons); [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Die Dreizehn Alten Orte*), and there were two different kinds: five rural states ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Länder*) – [Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri), [Schwyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Schwyz) (which became [eponymous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_Switzerland) of the confederacy), [Unterwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unterwalden), [Glarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Glarus), [Appenzell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Appenzell) – and eight urban states ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Städte*) – [Zürich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Z%C3%BCrich), [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bern), [Luzern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Luzern), [Zug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Zug), [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Basel), [Fribourg](292842.md), [Solothurn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Solothurn), [Schaffhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Schaffhausen).

Though they were technically part of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire), they had become *de facto* independent when the Swiss [defeated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Dornach) [Emperor Maximilian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Maximilian_I) in 1499 in Dornach.[^cite_note-14]

In the early modern period, the individual confederate allies came to be seen as [republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic); while the six traditional allies had a tradition of [direct democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_democracy) in the form of the *[Landsgemeinde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landsgemeinde)*, the urban states operated via [representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_\(politics\)) in city councils, de facto [oligarchic](22315.md) systems dominated by families of the [patriciate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrician_\(post-Roman_Europe\)).[^cite_note-16]

The old system was abandoned with the formation of the [Helvetic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetic_Republic) following the [French invasion of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_invasion_of_Switzerland) in 1798. The cantons of the Helvetic Republic had merely the status of an [administrative subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_\(country_subdivision\)) with no sovereignty. The Helvetic Republic collapsed within five years, and cantonal sovereignty was restored with the [Act of Mediation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Mediation) of 1803. The status of Switzerland as a federation of states was restored, at the time including 19 cantons (the six accessions to the early modern Thirteen Cantons being composed of former associates and subject territories: [St. Gallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_St._Gallen), [Grisons](169850.md), [Aargau](2467.md), [Thurgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thurgau), [Ticino](84147.md), [Vaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaud)). Three additional western cantons, [Valais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Valais), [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Neuch%C3%A2tel) and [Geneva](292834.md), acceded in 1815.

The process of "Restoration", completed by 1830, returned most of the former feudal rights to the cantonal [patriciates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrician_\(post-Roman_Europe\)), leading to rebellions among the rural population. The [Radicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_and_radicalism_in_Switzerland) embodied these democratic forces calling for a new federal constitution. This tension, paired with [religious issues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Switzerland) ("Jesuit question") escalated into armed conflict in the 1840s, with the brief [Sonderbund War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonderbund_War). The victory of the Liberal-Radicals resulted in the formation of [Switzerland as a federal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_as_a_federal_state) in 1848. The cantons retained far-reaching sovereignty but were no longer allowed to maintain individual standing armies or international relations. As the [revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848) in Western Europe had failed elsewhere, Switzerland during the later 19th century (and with the exception of the [French Third Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic), until the end of [World War I](4764461.md)) found itself as an isolated democratic republic, surrounded by the restored monarchies of [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Empire), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy), [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) and [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Confederation).

## Constitutions and powers

See also: [List of cantonal executives of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cantonal_executives_of_Switzerland) and [List of cantonal legislatures of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cantonal_legislatures_of_Switzerland)



The 22 cantonal coats of arms (all but [Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Jura), with the half-cantons represented jointly) in [stained glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standesscheibe) set in the dome of the [Federal Palace of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Palace_of_Switzerland) (c. 1900)

The [Swiss Federal Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Constitution)[^cite_note-17] declares the cantons to be sovereign to the extent that their sovereignty is not limited by federal law.[^cite_note-HDS_Modern-18] Areas specifically reserved to the Confederation include the armed forces, currency, postal service, telecommunications, immigration and emigration, the granting of asylum, foreign relations with sovereign states, civil and criminal law, weights and measures, and customs duties.

Each canton has its own [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution), [legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature), [executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)), [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Switzerland) and [courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary).[^cite_note-HDS_Modern-18] Similar to the Confederation, a [directorial system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorial_system) of government is followed by the cantons.

The cantonal legislatures are [unicameral](204304.md) [parliaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament), with their size varying between 58 and 200 seats. A few legislatures also involve or did involve general popular assemblies known as *[Landsgemeinden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landsgemeinde)*; the use of this form of legislature has declined: at present, it exists only in the cantons of [Appenzell Innerrhoden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzell_Innerrhoden) and [Glarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Glarus). The cantonal executives consist of either five or seven members, depending on the canton.[^cite_note-19] For the names of the institutions, see the [list of cantonal executives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cantonal_executives_of_Switzerland) and [list of cantonal legislatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cantonal_legislatures_of_Switzerland).

The cantons retain all powers and competencies not delegated to the Confederation by the federal constitution or law: most significantly the cantons are responsible for [healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_care), [welfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_\(financial_aid\)), law enforcement, [public education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_education), and retain the power of [taxation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxation_in_Switzerland). Each canton defines its official language(s). Cantons may conclude [treaties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty) not only with other cantons but also with foreign states (respectively Articles 48 and 56 of the Federal Constitution).

The cantonal constitutions determine the internal organisation of the canton, including the degree of autonomy accorded to the [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Switzerland), which varies but almost always includes the power to levy taxes and pass municipal laws; some municipalities have their own police forces.

As at the federal level, all cantons provide for some form of [direct democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_democracy). Citizens may demand a popular vote to amend the cantonal constitution or laws or to veto laws or spending bills passed by the parliament. Other than in the instances of general popular assemblies in Appenzell Innerrhoden and Glarus, democratic rights are exercised by secret ballot. The [right of foreigners to vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_of_foreigners_to_vote_in_Switzerland) varies by canton, as does whether Swiss citizens living abroad (and registered to vote in a canton) can take part in cantonal voting.

[Swiss citizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_nationality_law) are citizens of a particular municipality (the [place of origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Place_of_origin)) and the canton in which that municipality is part. Cantons, therefore, have a role in and set requirements for the granting of citizenship (naturalisation), though the process is typically undertaken at a municipal level and is subject to federal law.

Switzerland has only one federal public holiday (1 August); public holidays otherwise [vary from canton to canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Switzerland).

The two-letter abbreviations for Swiss cantons are widely used, e.g. on [car license plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle_registration_plates_of_Switzerland). They are also used in the [ISO 3166-2 codes of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3166-2\:CH) with the prefix "CH-" (*[Confœderatio Helvetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conf%C5%93deratio_Helvetica)* — Helvetian Confederation — *[Helvetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetia#Name_of_Switzerland)* having been the ancient Roman name of the region). *CH-SZ*, for example, is used for the [canton of Schwyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Schwyz).

## Half-cantons

Six of the 26 cantons are traditionally, but no longer officially, called "half-cantons" ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Halbkanton*, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *demi-canton*, [Italian](14708.md): *semicantone*, [Romansh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romansh_language): *mez-chantun*). In two instances (Basel and Appenzell) this was a consequence of a historic division, whilst in the case of Unterwalden a historic mutual association, resulting in three pairs of half-cantons. The other 20 cantons were, and in some instances still are[^cite_note-43]—though only in a context where it is needed to distinguish them from any half-cantons—typically termed "full" cantons in English.[^cite_note-44]

The first article of the 1848 and 1874 constitutions constituted the Confederation as the union of "twenty-two sovereign cantons", referring to the half-cantons as "[Unterwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unterwalden) (*ob und nid dem Wald* \['[above](317636.md) and [beneath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nidwalden) the woods'])", "Basel (*Stadt und Landschaft* \['city and country'])" and "Appenzell (*beider Rhoden* \['both Rhoden'])".[^cite_note-45] The 1874 constitution was amended to list 23 cantons with the accession of the [Canton of Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Jura) in 1978.

The historic half-cantons, and their pairings, are still recognizable in the first article of the [Swiss Federal Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Constitution) of 1999 by being joined to their other "half" with the conjunction "and":

> The People and the cantons of Zurich, Bern, Lucerne, Uri, Schwyz, [Obwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Obwalden) and [Nidwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Nidwalden), Glarus, Zug, Fribourg, Solothurn, [Basel-Stadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Stadt) and [Basel-Landschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Landschaft), Schaffhausen, [Appenzell Ausserrhoden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzell_Ausserrhoden) and [Appenzell Innerrhoden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appenzell_Innerrhoden), St. Gallen, Graubünden, Aargau, Thurgau, Ticino, Vaud, Valais, Neuchâtel, Geneva, and Jura form the Swiss Confederation.

— Article 1 of the Federal Constitution of the Swiss Confederation[^cite_note-46]

The 1999 constitutional revision retained the traditional distinction, on the request of the six cantonal governments, as a way to mark the historic association of the half-cantons to each other.[^cite_note-47] While the older constitutions referred to these states as "half-cantons", a term that remains in popular use, the 1999 revision and official terminology since then use the appellation "cantons with half of a cantonal vote".[^cite_note-48]

The [1⁄2, 1 and 2 francs coins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coins_of_the_Swiss_franc) as minted since 1874 represent the number of cantons by 22 stars surrounding the figure of [Helvetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helvetia) on the obverse. The design of the coins was altered to show 23 stars, including Jura, beginning with the 1983 batch. The design has remained unchanged since, and does not reflect the official number of "26 cantons" introduced in 1999.[^cite_note-49]



Caricature of the division of [Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Basel), 1833

The reasons for the existence of the three pairs of half-cantons are varied:

- [Unterwalden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unterwalden) never consisted of a single unified jurisdiction. Originally, Obwalden, Nidwalden, and the [Abbey of Engelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbey_of_Engelberg) formed distinct communities. The collective term *Unterwalden* remains in use, however, for the area that partook in the creation of the original Swiss confederation in 1291 with [Uri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Uri) and [Schwyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Schwyz). The [Federal Charter of 1291](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Charter_of_1291) called for representatives from each of the three "areas".

  [^cite_note-50]

  [^cite_note-51]

- The historical [canton of Appenzell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Appenzell) divided itself into "inner" and "outer" halves as a consequence of the [Reformation in Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reformation_in_Switzerland) in 1597:

  [^cite_note-52]

  &#x20;[Appenzell Innerrhoden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Appenzell_Innerrhoden) ([Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism)) and [Appenzell Ausserrhoden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Appenzell_Ausserrhoden) ([Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism)).

- The historical [canton of Basel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Basel) was divided in 1833 after the Basel countryside (which became the canton of [Basel-Landschaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basel-Landschaft)) declared its independence from the city of Basel (which became the [canton of Basel-Stadt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Basel-Stadt)), following a period of protest and armed conflict about the under-representation of the more populous countryside in the canton's political system.

With their original circumstances of partition now a historical matter, the half-cantons are since 1848 equal to the other cantons in all but two respects:[^cite_note-53]

- They elect only one member of the [Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Council_of_States) instead of two (Cst. art. 150 par. 2). This means there are a total of 46 seats in the council.

- In [popular referendums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voting_in_Switzerland#Constitutional_referendums_\(Popular_initiatives\)) about constitutional amendments, which require for adoption a national popular majority as well as the assent of a majority of the cantons (**Ständemehr* / *majorité des cantons**), the result of the half-cantons' popular vote counts only one half of that of the other cantons (Cst. arts. 140, 142).

  [^cite_note-54]

  &#x20;This means that for purposes of a constitutional referendum, at least 12 out of a total of 23 cantonal popular votes must support the amendment.

  [^cite_note-55]

Between 1831 and 1833 the canton of Schwyz was divided into half-cantons: (Inner) Schwyz and the break-away [Outer Schwyz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Schwyz); in this instance, the half-cantons were forced by the Confederation to settle their disputes and reunite.

In the 20th century, some [Jura separatists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jura_separatism) suggested a new canton of Jura to be divided into half-cantons of North Jura and South Jura.[^cite_note-56] Instead, North Jura became the (full) [canton of Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Jura) while South Jura remains in the canton of Bern as the [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_the_canton_of_Bern) of [Bernese Jura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernese_Jura).

## Admission of new cantons

Main article: [Territorial evolution of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_Switzerland)

The enlargement of Switzerland by way of the admission of new cantons ended in 1815. The latest formal attempt considered by Switzerland was [in 1919](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1919_Vorarlberg_annexation_referendum) from [Vorarlberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorarlberg) but subsequently rejected. A few representatives submitted in 2010 a parliamentary motion to consider enlargement although it was widely seen as anti-EU rhetoric rather than a serious proposal.[^cite_note-Tages-Anzeiger_11_June_2010-58] The motion was eventually dropped and not even examined by the parliament.[^cite_note-The_Federal_Assembly_—_The_Swiss_Parliament-59]
