The **Royal Moroccan Football Federation** (abbr. **FRMF**; [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الجامعة الملكية المغربية لكرة القدم; [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Fédération Royale Marocaine de Football*) is the governing body for [association football](10568.md) in [Morocco](19291.md). Founded in 1956, it joined [FIFA](11049.md) in 1960 and became a member of [CAF](715265.md) the same year. The federation administers the national football league system and oversees its top division, the [Botola Pro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Botola_Pro). It is also responsible for the Morocco national football teams across all age categories and disciplines. Headquartered in Rabat, the FRMF is a member of the [UAFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Arab_Football_Associations) and the [UNAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_North_African_Football_Federations).[^cite_note-2]

## History

### Africa Cup of Nations

On 29 January 2011, the CAF Board decided that Morocco would host the [2015 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), while the 2017 edition would be held in South Africa.[^cite_note-3]

In October 2014, the [government of Morocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Morocco) requested a postponement of the tournament due to the [Ebola virus epidemic in West Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebola_virus_epidemic_in_West_Africa).[^cite_note-4] After the matter was discussed at the executive committee meeting on 2 November 2014, CAF decided to keep the date of the tournament, while also asking for a clarification from the Royal Moroccan Football Federation of whether they still wish to host the tournament.[^cite_note-5] On 8 November, Morocco failed to meet this deadline to confirm it would host the tournament.[^cite_note-6] Three days later CAF confirmed that Morocco would not host the tournament and a new host would be chosen from a list of countries which have expressed interest. Morocco, who had previously qualified as hosts, were disqualified from participation at the tournament.[^cite_note-caf-7][^cite_note-bbc-8]

On 1 October 2022, [Morocco](19291.md) announced its willingness to host the [2025 Africa Cup of Nations](57061770.md), after it was stripped from [Guinea](12176.md).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] On 27 September 2023, [CAF](715265.md) announced that Morocco will host the 35th edition of the TotalEnergies CAF Africa Cup of Nations 2025.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### Women's Africa Cup of Nations

On 15 January 2021, Morocco was announced as hosts for the [2022 Women's Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Women's_Africa_Cup_of_Nations). This is the first time a North African Arab country has hosted the Women's Africa Cup of Nations.[^cite_note-13] After a successful event, Morocco was given the right to host the [2024 Women's Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Women's_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

### FIFA World Cup Bids

In 1994, [Morocco](19291.md), [United States](3434750.md) and [Brazil](3383.md) bade to host the [1994 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-16] The [United States](3434750.md) eventually won the bid with 10 votes, Morocco in second place with 7 votes and Brazil with 2.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Morocco was set to bid on the upcoming [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup). It ended with 12–7 vote for [France](5843419.md) allowing [France](5843419.md) to be host of the 16th edition of the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

In 2006, [Morocco](19291.md) made their third bid to host the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md). [Germany](11867.md) was successful in winning the vote to host the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup).[^cite_note-22] Morocco continued its attempt to host the [next FIFA World Cup edition](168079.md) but failed in doing so.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] [South Africa](17416221.md) won the bid making it the first [African country](5334607.md) to host the World Cup.[^cite_note-25] On 6 June 2015, [The Daily Telegraph](23797577.md) reported that [Morocco](19291.md) had actually won the vote, but [South Africa](17416221.md) was awarded the tournament instead.[^cite_note-26]

#### 2026 FIFA World Cup

Main articles: [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md) and [Morocco 2026 FIFA World Cup bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco_2026_FIFA_World_Cup_bid)

On 11 August 2017, Morocco was set for submission of an intention to bid, and on that day, the Royal Moroccan Football Federation announced that it would submit a bid for the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-27] In March 2018, the Morocco 2026 bid committee stated their plan to spend \$16 billion on preparing for the tournament, including building new transportation infrastructure, 21 new hospitals, a large number of new hotels and leisure facilities and building and/or renovating new stadiums.[^cite_note-28]

#### 2030 FIFA World Cup

Main articles: [2030 FIFA World Cup](39367087.md) and [Morocco–Portugal–Spain 2030 FIFA World Cup bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morocco%E2%80%93Portugal%E2%80%93Spain_2030_FIFA_World_Cup_bid)

The national football association of Morocco bid to host the [2030 FIFA World Cup](39367087.md).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] On 15 June 2018, the bid was led by the Royal Moroccan Football Federation, who officially confirmed it.[^cite_note-algerie-direct.net-32]

On 25 July 2018, Royal Moroccan Football Federation president [Fouzi Lekjaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fouzi_Lekjaa), confirmed Morocco would apply for the 2030 World Cup bid.[^cite_note-33] On 10 March 2023, the [Royal Spanish Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation) and the [Portuguese Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Football_Federation) announced they were studying the possibility of adding Morocco to their [Iberian Bid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain%E2%80%93Portugal%E2%80%93Morocco_2030_FIFA_World_Cup_bid) to host the 2030 World Cup, replacing [Ukraine](31750.md).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

On 4 October 2023, the [FIFA Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Executive_Committee) unanimously accepted the Morocco–Spain–Portugal bid as a candidate to host the 2030 World Cup.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] King [Mohammed VI](226113.md) appointed Lekjaa as the chairman of the 2030 World Cup committee.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]
