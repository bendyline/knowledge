**Reuters** ([/ˈrɔɪtərz/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/8d/En-Reuters.oga/En-Reuters.oga.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/8d/En-Reuters.oga/En-Reuters.oga.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*ROY-tərz*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a British [news agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_agency) wholly owned by [Thomson Reuters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_Reuters), a multinational information [conglomerate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conglomerate_\(company\)).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] It employs around 2,500 journalists and 600 [photojournalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photojournalist) in 200 locations and 165 countries worldwide writing in 16 languages.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Reuters is one of the largest news agencies in the world.[^cite_note-britannica-two-8][^cite_note-\:5-9]

Reuters does not have a single headquarters. The leadership team is split between multiple locations. Paul Bascobert, the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_\(corporate_title\)) of Reuters News, is based in [New York](645042.md), while the editor-in-chief, Alessandra Galloni, is based in Reuters' UK editorial headquarters in [Canary Wharf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Wharf), [London](17867.md).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The agency was established in London in 1851 by the German-born British [baron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiherr) [Paul Reuter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Reuter). The [Thomson Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_Corporation) of Canada acquired the agency in a 2008 corporate merger, resulting in the formation of the Thomson Reuters Corporation.[^cite_note-\:5-9]

In December 2024, Reuters was ranked as the 27th most visited news site in the world, with over 105 million monthly readers.[^cite_note-12]

## History

### 19th century



[Paul Reuter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Reuter), the founder of Reuters (photographed by [Nadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadar), c. 1865)

Paul Julius Reuter worked at a book-publishing firm in [Berlin](3354.md) and was involved in distributing radical pamphlets at the beginning of the [revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848). These publications brought much attention to Reuter, who in 1850 developed a prototype news service in [Aachen](1520.md) using [homing pigeons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homing_pigeon) and [electric telegraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_telegraph) from 1851 on, in order to transmit messages between [Brussels](3708.md) and Aachen,[^cite_note-13] in what today is Aachen's Reuters House.

Reuter moved to [London](17867.md) in 1851 and established a [news wire agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_agency) at the [London Royal Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Exchange,_London). Headquartered in London, Reuter's company initially covered commercial news, serving banks, brokerage houses, and business firms.[^cite_note-britannica-r-14] The first newspaper client to subscribe was the London *[Morning Advertiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morning_Advertiser)* in 1858, and more began to subscribe soon after.[^cite_note-britannica-r-14][^cite_note-morning-ad-15] According to the *[Encyclopædia Britannica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encyclop%C3%A6dia_Britannica)*: "the value of Reuters to newspapers lay not only in the financial news it provided but in its ability to be the first to report on stories of international importance."[^cite_note-britannica-r-14] It was the first to report [Abraham Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Lincoln)'s assassination in [Europe](9239.md), for instance, in 1865.[^cite_note-britannica-r-14][^cite_note-guardian-16]

In 1865, Reuter incorporated his private business, under the name Reuter's Telegram Company Limited; Reuter was appointed managing director of the company.[^cite_note-funding-universe-17]

In 1870 the press agencies French [Havas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Havas) (founded in 1835), British Reuter's (founded in 1851) and German Wolff (founded in 1849) signed an agreement (known as the Ring Combination) that set 'reserved territories' for the three agencies. Each agency made its own separate contracts with national agencies or other subscribers within its territory. In practice, Reuters, who came up with the idea, tended to dominate the Ring Combination. Its influence was greatest because its reserved territories were larger or of greater news importance than most others. It also had more staff and [stringers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stringer_\(journalism\)) throughout the world and thus contributed more original news to the pool. British control of cable lines made London itself an unrivalled centre for world news, further enhanced by Britain's wide-ranging commercial, financial and imperial activities.[^cite_note-18]

In 1872, Reuter's expanded into the [Far East](11672.md), followed by South America in 1874. Both expansions were made possible by advances in overland telegraphs and undersea cables.[^cite_note-guardian-16] In 1878, Reuter retired as managing director, and was succeeded by his eldest son, [Herbert de Reuter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_de_Reuter).[^cite_note-funding-universe-17] In 1883, Reuter's began transmitting messages electrically to London newspapers.[^cite_note-guardian-16]

### 20th century



Roderick Jones, general manager 1915–1941

Reuter's son Herbert de Reuter continued as general manager until his death by suicide in 1915. The company returned to private ownership in 1916, when all shares were purchased by [Roderick Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roderick_Jones_\(1877%E2%80%931962\)) and Mark Napier; they renamed the company "Reuters Limited" and dropped the apostrophe.[^cite_note-funding-universe-17] In 1923, Reuters began using radio to transmit news internationally, a pioneering act.[^cite_note-guardian-16] In 1925, the [Press Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Association) (PA) of [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) acquired a majority interest in Reuters, and full ownership some years later.[^cite_note-britannica-r-14] The company moved to a new headquarters at [85 Fleet Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/85_Fleet_Street) in the 1930s.[^cite_note-19]

During the world wars, *[The Manchester Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Manchester_Guardian)* reported that Reuters: "came under pressure from the British government to serve national interests. In 1941, Reuters deflected the pressure by restructuring itself as a private company."[^cite_note-guardian-16] In 1941, the PA sold half of Reuters to the [Newspaper Proprietors' Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newspaper_Proprietors'_Association), and co-ownership was expanded in 1947 to associations that represented daily newspapers in New Zealand and Australia.[^cite_note-britannica-r-14] The new owners formed the Reuters Trust. The Reuters Trust Principles were put in place to maintain the company's independence.[^cite_note-aboutus-20] At that point, Reuters had become "one of the world's major news agencies, supplying both text and images to newspapers, other news agencies, and radio and television broadcasters."[^cite_note-britannica-r-14] Also at that point, it directly, or through national news agencies, provided service to most countries, reaching virtually all the world's leading newspapers and many thousands of smaller ones, according to *Britannica*.[^cite_note-britannica-r-14]

In 1961, Reuters scooped news of the erection of the [Berlin Wall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_Wall).[^cite_note-read-21] Reuters was one of the first news agencies to transmit financial data over oceans via computers in the 1960s.[^cite_note-britannica-r-14] In 1973, Reuters "began making computer-terminal displays of foreign-exchange rates available to clients."[^cite_note-britannica-r-14] In 1981, Reuters began supporting electronic transactions on its computer network and afterwards developed a number of electronic brokerage and trading services.[^cite_note-britannica-r-14] Reuters was floated as a public company in 1984,[^cite_note-read-21] when Reuters Trust was listed on [stock exchanges](28168.md)[^cite_note-guardian-16] including the [London Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Stock_Exchange) (LSE) and [NASDAQ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASDAQ).[^cite_note-britannica-r-14] Reuters later published the first story of the Berlin Wall being breached in 1989.[^cite_note-read-21]

Reuters was the dominant news service on the [Internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) in the 1990s. It earned this position by developing a partnership with [ClariNet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ClariNet) and [PointCast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PointCast), two early Internet-based news providers.[^cite_note-22]

### 21st century

Reuters' share price grew during the [dotcom boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dotcom_boom), then fell after the banking troubles in 2001.[^cite_note-guardian-16] In 2002, *Britannica* wrote that most news throughout the world came from three major agencies: the [Associated Press](18935732.md), Reuters, and [Agence France-Presse](471981.md).[^cite_note-britannica-two-8] The company moved to [30 South Colonnade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_South_Colonnade) in [Canary Wharf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canary_Wharf) in 2005.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

Until 2008, the Reuters news agency formed part of an independent company, [Reuters Group plc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reuters_Group). Reuters was acquired by the Canadian [Thomson Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_Corporation) in 2008, forming Thomson Reuters.[^cite_note-britannica-r-14] In 2009, Thomson Reuters withdrew from the LSE and the NASDAQ, instead listing its shares on the [Toronto Stock Exchange](205584.md) (TSX) and the [New York Stock Exchange](21560.md) (NYSE).[^cite_note-britannica-r-14] The last surviving member of the Reuters family founders, [Marguerite, Baroness de Reuter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marguerite,_Baroness_de_Reuter), died at age 96 on 25 January 2009.[^cite_note-abc-26] The parent company Thomson Reuters is headquartered in [Toronto](64646.md), and provides financial information to clients while also maintaining its traditional news-agency business.[^cite_note-britannica-r-14]

In 2012, Thomson Reuters appointed Jim Smith as CEO.[^cite_note-aboutus-20] In July 2016, Thomson Reuters agreed to sell its intellectual property and science operation for \$3.55 billion to private equity firms.[^cite_note-bloomberg-cut-27] In October 2016, Thomson Reuters announced expansions and relocations to [Toronto](64646.md).[^cite_note-bloomberg-cut-27] As part of cuts and restructuring, in November 2016, Thomson Reuters Corp. eliminated 2,000 jobs worldwide out of its estimated 50,000 employees.[^cite_note-bloomberg-cut-27] On 15 March 2020, Steve Hasker was appointed president and CEO.[^cite_note-28]

In 2020, the photography staff of Reuters won the [Pulitzer Prize for Breaking News Photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Breaking_News_Photography) for wide-ranging and illuminating coverage of the [2019–2020 Hong Kong protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932020_Hong_Kong_protests).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

In April 2021, Reuters announced that its website would go behind a [paywall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paywall), following rivals who have done the same.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-NYT-2021apr15-32]

In March 2024, [Gannett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gannett), the largest newspaper publisher in the United States, signed an agreement with Reuters to use the wire service's global content after cancelling its contract with the [Associated Press](18935732.md).[^cite_note-33]

In 2024, Reuters staff won the [Pulitzer Prize for National Reporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_National_Reporting) for their work on [Elon Musk](909036.md) and misconduct at his businesses, including [SpaceX](832774.md), [Tesla](5533631.md), and [Neuralink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuralink), as well as the [Pulitzer Prize for Breaking News Photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_Breaking_News_Photography) for coverage of the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-34] As of 2025, Reuters has won a total of 13 Pulitzer Prizes since 2008.[^cite_note-35]

In April 2026, an agreement was reached between Reuters and the [National Basketball Association](22093.md) to distribute video highlights and official league content worldwide, with the aim of strengthening its media presence and international monetization.[^cite_note-36]

## Journalists

Reuters employs some 2,500 journalists and 600 [photojournalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photojournalist)[^cite_note-37] in about 200 locations worldwide.[^cite_note-\:1-38][^cite_note-\:0-39][^cite_note-\:5-9] Reuters journalists use the *Standards and Values* as a guide for fair presentation and disclosure of relevant interests, to "maintain the values of integrity and freedom upon which their reputation for reliability, accuracy, speed and exclusivity relies."[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

In May 2000, [Kurt Schork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Schork), an American [reporter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reporter), was killed in an [ambush](1905.md) while on assignment in [Sierra Leone](27308.md). In April and August 2003, news cameramen [Taras Protsyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Protsyuk) and [Mazen Dana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazen_Dana) were killed in separate incidents by U.S. troops in [Iraq](7515928.md). In July 2007, [Namir Noor-Eldeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namir_Noor-Eldeen) and [Saeed Chmagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saeed_Chmagh) were killed when they were struck by fire from a U.S. military Apache helicopter in Baghdad.[^cite_note-Bumiller0405-42][^cite_note-43] During 2004, [cameramen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameramen) [Adlan Khasanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adlan_Khasanov) was killed by [Chechen](6095.md) separatists, and Dhia Najim was killed in Iraq. In April 2008, cameraman [Fadel Shana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fadel_Shana) was killed in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md) after being hit by an Israeli [tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank).[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] On 27 August 2025, cameraman [Hussam al-Masri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussam_al-Masri) was killed at [Nasser Hospital](43293381.md) in the Gaza Strip by an Israeli air strike.[^cite_note-46]

While covering China's [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution) in [Peking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peking) in the late 1960s for Reuters, journalist [Anthony Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Grey) was detained by the Chinese government in response to the jailing of several Chinese journalists by the colonial British government of [Hong Kong](13404.md).[^cite_note-47] He was released after being imprisoned for 27 months from 1967 to 1969 and was awarded an [OBE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_British_Empire) by the British Government. After his release, he went on to become a best-selling historical novelist.[^cite_note-48]

In May 2016, the Ukrainian website [Myrotvorets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrotvorets) published the names and personal data of 4,508 journalists, including Reuters reporters, and other media staff from all over the world, who were accredited by the self-proclaimed authorities in the [separatist](286527.md)-controlled regions of eastern [Ukraine](31750.md).[^cite_note-49]

In 2018, two Reuters journalists were convicted in [Myanmar](19457.md) of obtaining state secrets while investigating a massacre in a [Rohingya](1918632.md) village.[^cite_note-50] The arrest and convictions were widely condemned as an attack on [press freedom](105323.md). The journalists, Wa Lone and Kyaw Soe Oo, received several awards, including the Foreign Press Association Media Award and the [Pulitzer Prize for International Reporting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulitzer_Prize_for_International_Reporting), and were named as part of the [Time Person of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_Person_of_the_Year) for 2018 along with other persecuted journalists.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] After 511 days in prison, Wa Lone and Kyaw Soe Oo were freed on 7 May 2019 after receiving a presidential pardon.[^cite_note-54]

In February 2023, a team of Reuters journalists won the [Selden Ring Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selden_Ring_Award) for their investigation that exposed human-rights abuses by the Nigerian military.[^cite_note-55]

### Accusation of collaboration with the CIA

In 1977, *[Rolling Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolling_Stone)* and *[The New York Times](30680.md)* said that according to information from [CIA](5183633.md) officials, Reuters cooperated with the CIA.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-\:2-68] In response to that, Reuters' then-managing director, Gerald Long, had asked for evidence of the charges, but none was provided, according to Reuters' then-managing editor for North America,[^cite_note-\:2-68] Desmond Maberly.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

### Policy of objective language



Reuters building entrance in [New York City](645042.md)

Reuters has a policy of taking a "value-neutral approach," which extends to not using the word *terrorist* in its stories. The practice attracted criticism following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md).[^cite_note-Moeller-71] Reuters' editorial policy states: "Reuters may refer without attribution to terrorism and counterterrorism in general, but do not refer to specific events as terrorism. Nor does Reuters use the word *terrorist* without attribution to qualify specific individuals, groups or events."[^cite_note-72] In 2004, Reuters asked [CanWest Global Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CanWest_Global_Communications), a Canadian newspaper chain, to remove Reuters' [bylines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bylines), as the chain had edited Reuters articles to insert the word *terrorist*. A spokesman for Reuters stated: "My goal is to protect my reporters and protect our editorial integrity."[^cite_note-73]

### Climate change reporting

In July 2013, David Fogarty, former Reuters [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_warming) correspondent in Asia, resigned after a career of almost 20 years with the company and wrote that "progressively, getting any climate change-themed story published got harder" after comments from then-deputy editor-in-chief [Paul Ingrassia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Ingrassia_\(journalist\)) that he was a "[climate change sceptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_sceptic)." In his comments, Fogarty stated:[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

> By mid-October, I was informed that climate change just wasn't a big story for the present, but that it would be if there was a significant shift in global policy, such as the US introducing an emissions cap-and-trade system. Very soon after that conversation I was told my climate change role was abolished.

Ingrassia, formerly Reuters' managing editor, previously worked for *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* and [Dow Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dow_Jones_&_Company) for 31 years.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] Reuters responded to Fogarty's piece by stating: "Reuters has a number of staff dedicated to covering this story, including a team of specialist reporters at Point Carbon and a columnist. There has been no change in our editorial policy."[^cite_note-79]

Subsequently, climate blogger [Joe Romm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_J._Romm) of *[ThinkProgress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ThinkProgress)* cited a Reuters article on climate as having a "[false balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_balance)" and quoted Stefan Rahmstorf, co-chair of Earth System Analysis at the [Potsdam Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Institute_for_Climate_Impact_Research), saying "\[s]imply, a lot of unrelated climate sceptics nonsense has been added to this Reuters piece. In the words of the late Steve Schneider, this is like adding some nonsense from the [Flat Earth Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Flat_Earth_Research_Society) to a report about the latest generation of telecommunication satellites. It is absurd." Romm suggests: "The fact that the blather was all inserted without attribution suggests it was added at the insistence of an editor."[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

### Photograph controversies

According to [Ynetnews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ynetnews), Reuters was accused of bias against Israel in its coverage of the [2006 Israel–Lebanon conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Israel%E2%80%93Lebanon_conflict) after the wire service [used two doctored photos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adnan_Hajj_photographs_controversy) by a [Lebanese](17771.md) freelance photographer, Adnan Hajj.[^cite_note-82] In August 2006, Reuters announced it had severed all ties with Hajj and said his photographs would be removed from its database.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

In 2010, Reuters was criticised again by *[Haaretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haaretz)* for "anti-Israeli" bias when it cropped the edges of photos, removing commandos' knives held by activists and a naval commando's blood from photographs taken aboard the *Mavi Marmara* during the [2010 Gaza flotilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Gaza_flotilla_raid), a raid that left nine Turkish activists dead. It has been alleged that in two separate photographs, knives held by the activists were cropped out of the versions of the pictures published by Reuters.[^cite_note-Haaretz20100608-85] Reuters said it is standard operating procedure to crop photos at the margins, and replaced the cropped images with the original ones after it was brought to the agency's attention.[^cite_note-Haaretz20100608-85]

### Indian man falsely accused of cybercrime

On 9 June 2020, three Reuters journalists (Jack Stubbs, Raphael Satter and Christopher Bing) incorrectly used the image of an Indian [herbal medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbal_medicine) entrepreneur in an exclusive story titled "Obscure Indian cyber firm spied on politicians, investors worldwide."[^cite_note-86] Indian local media picked up the report, and the man whose image was wrongly used was invited and interrogated for nine hours by Indian police. Reuters admitted to the error, with Satter claiming that it had mistaken the man for the suspected hacker Sumit Gupta because both men share the same business address. A check by local media, however, showed that both men were in different buildings.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-\:3-88] As the report of the inaccurate reporting trickled out to the public, Reuters' senior director of communication Heather Carpenter contacted at least one media outlet to ask them to take down their post.[^cite_note-\:3-88]

### Fernando Henrique Cardoso interview

In March 2015, the Brazilian affiliate of Reuters released an excerpt of an interview with Brazilian ex-president [Fernando Henrique Cardoso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Henrique_Cardoso) about [Operation Car Wash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Car_Wash) ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Operação Lava Jato*), in which several politicians from Brazil were found to have accepted bribes from corporations in exchange for government contracts. After the scandal, the excerpt from Brazil's president Cardoso's interview was released. In the publication, a former [Petrobras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrobras) manager suggested that corruption in the company may date back to Cardoso's presidency. Attached was a comment in parentheses: "*Podemos tirar, se achar melhor*" ("we can take it out if you think it would be better").[^cite_note-89] This confused readers, suggesting that the former president was involved in corruption and that the comment was attributed to him. Reuters later confirmed the error and explained that the comment was from an editor, intended for the journalist who wrote the original English text;[^cite_note-90] it was removed from later publications.[^cite_note-91]

### Funding by the UK Government

In November 2019, the [UK Foreign Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_and_Commonwealth_Office) released archive documents confirming that it had provided funding to Reuters during the 1960s and 1970s so that Reuters could expand its coverage in the [Middle East](19323.md). An agreement was made between the [Information Research Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Research_Department) (IRD) and Reuters for the [UK Treasury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HM_Treasury) to provide £350,000 over four years to fund Reuters' expansion. The UK government had already been funding the [Latin American](18524.md) department of Reuters through a [shell company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_corporation), but that method was discounted for the Middle East operation since the accounting of the shell company looked suspicious.[^cite_note-BBC13012020-92] Instead, the [BBC](19344654.md) was used to fund the project by paying for enhanced subscriptions to the news organisation for which the Treasury would reimburse the BBC at a later date. The IRD acknowledged that this agreement would not give them editorial control over Reuters, although they believed it would give them political influence "from Reuters' willingness to consult and to listen to views expressed on the results of its work."[^cite_note-BBC13012020-92][^cite_note-93]

### Partnership with TASS

On 1 June 2020, Reuters announced that Russian news agency [TASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TASS) had joined its "Reuters Connect" programme, comprising 18 partner agencies at the time.[^cite_note-reuters-tass-2020-94] Two years later, TASS's membership in Reuters Connect came under scrutiny in the wake of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md); *[Politico](9172097.md)* reported that Reuters staff members were "frustrated and embarrassed" that their agency had not suspended its partnership with TASS.[^cite_note-politico-22-95]

On 23 March 2022, Reuters removed TASS from its "content marketplace." Matthew Keen, interim CEO of Reuters, said "we believe making TASS content available on Reuters Connect is not aligned with the Thomson Reuters Trust Principles."[^cite_note-Reuters_removed_TASS-96]

### Fossil fuel advertising

An investigation by *[The Intercept](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Intercept)*, *[The Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nation)*, Drilled, and [DeSmog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DeSmog) found that Reuters is one of the leading media outlets that publishes advertising for the [fossil fuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_fuel) industry.[^cite_note-intercept20231205-97] Some journalists who cover [climate change](5042951.md) for Reuters are concerned that [conflicts of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest) with the companies and industries responsible reduces the credibility of their reporting and causes readers to downplay the [climate crisis](17987598.md).[^cite_note-intercept20231205-97] Reuters later removed a [Saudi Aramco](290527.md)-sponsored podcast on energy.[^cite_note-98]

### Allegations of journalism in India without authorization

In December 2023, the Indian Ministry of Home Affairs revoked the [Overseas Citizenship of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Citizenship_of_India) (OCI) status of a Reuters [cybersecurity](7398.md) journalist, Raphael Satter, alleging unauthorised journalistic activities in India. The ministry claimed the journalist violated regulations requiring OCI cardholders to obtain prior approval for such work. According to [The Sunday Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sunday_Guardian), in a related incident, Satter faced legal scrutiny in [Goa](40010153.md), India, where the journalist allegedly engaged in journalistic activities without permission – for example, contacting individuals in India for interviews during visits, despite claiming the trips were for personal purposes.[^cite_note-99] Reuters supported the journalist's denial of conducting journalism in India, and a legal petition was filed in the [Delhi High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_High_Court) to challenge the OCI cancellation, with a hearing scheduled for May 22, 2025, though specific charges were not detailed.[^cite_note-100]

### Valerie Zink resignation

In August 2025, Valerie Zink, a [photo journalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photojournalism) who had been working with Reuters for 8 years, announced her resignation from the agency after accusing it of perpetuating Israel's claims over its [war in Gaza](74998519.md), at the expense of other journalists including its own reporters.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] She further accused Reuters of publishing Israel's claims that [Anas Al-Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anas_Al-Sharif) (a journalist working in Gaza) was a [Hamas](13913.md) operative. She also stated that five more journalists, including Reuters cameraman [Hossam Al-Masri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussam_al-Masri), were among 20 people killed in an attack on Nasser hospital in the [Gaza Strip](12047.md).[^cite_note-103]

### Reporting of false news on India–U.S. trade negotiations

On 13 July 2026, Reuters published a report concerning negotiations over a proposed India–United States trade agreement. The report was publicly disputed by Indian Commerce and Industry Minister [Piyush Goyal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piyush_Goyal), who described it as "completely false" and stated that his meetings with U.S. Trade Representative [Jamieson Greer](78457418.md) had been constructive, with both sides committed to reaching a balanced agreement for businesses, farmers, and consumers.[^cite_note-104]

The report was also challenged by Sergio Gor, the [United States Ambassador to India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Ambassador_to_India), who stated that "no one has rejected anything" and urged Reuters to "do better".[^cite_note-105]

Subsequent reporting noted that Indian officials said trade negotiations were continuing without major obstacles and that a framework agreement was ready to be signed when both sides considered the timing appropriate.[^cite_note-106]
