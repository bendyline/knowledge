**Ulsan** ([Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language): 울산; pronounced [\[uɭ.s͈an\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Korean)), officially the **Ulsan Metropolitan City**, is [South Korea](27019.md)'s seventh-largest metropolitan city and the eighth-largest city overall, with a population of over 1.1 million inhabitants.[^cite_note-4] It is located in the south-east of the country, neighboring [Busan](68115.md) to the south and facing [Gyeongju](426047.md) to the north.

Ulsan is the industrial powerhouse of South Korea, forming the heart of the Ulsan Industrial District. It has the world's largest [automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) assembly plant, operated by the [Hyundai Motor Company](42257.md);[^cite_note-yonhapnews.co.kr-5] the world's largest [shipyard](213654.md), operated by [Hyundai Heavy Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Heavy_Industries);[^cite_note-ohmynews.com-6] and the world's [third-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oil_refineries#\(World's_largest_refineries\)) [oil refinery](195137.md), owned by [SK Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Energy).[^cite_note-\:2-7] In 2020, Ulsan had a [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita) of \$65,352, the highest of any region in South Korea.

Ulsan is the city that built the first automobile hydrogen charging station in the country in 2009. In addition, the first hydrogen ship charging station in the country was installed in Jangsaengpo Port in 2021. Currently, the [Ulsan Green Hydrogen Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_Green_Hydrogen_Town) project, a hydrogen city that supplies hydrogen by connecting the city and a hydrogen power plant, is in progress.[^cite_note-8] In December 2016, 10 [Hyundai ix35 FCEVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_ix35_FCEV), the first hydrogen fuel cell taxis in the country, entered test operation.[^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-10]

## History

Stone tools found at the [Mugeo-dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mugeo-dong) Ok-hyeon archaeological site indicate that Ulsan was inhabited by humans at least as early as the [Paleolithic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic). Other findings indicate human inhabitation in the [Neolithic Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic). Ulsan also contains a substantial number of settlement remains from the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age).[^cite_note-11] During the [Jinhan confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinhan_confederacy), Ulsan was a site of iron mining and production.[^cite_note-12] In the [Later Silla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Later_Silla) period, Ulsan served as an important port for the economic hub of [Gyeongju](426047.md), and likely saw the import of luxury Persian goods such as silver, glass, and peacock tails.[^cite_note-13]

In 1592, the future port-town of Ulsan was the site of a major battle when warrior monks joined citizen soldiers in resisting the Japanese invasion.[^cite_note-\:0-14] On January 29, 1598, a combined military operation between Korea and China tried to besiege the castle, but failed as reinforcements from Japan came to delay the fighting.[^cite_note-\:0-14] Records from the [Joseon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseon) period show that Ulsan was developed as a shipbuilding site as early as 1642.[^cite_note-15]

Owing to the two World Wars, Ulsan began to grow as an industrial center. During this time it was part of [Keishōnan-dō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keish%C5%8Dnan-d%C5%8D). In 1962, Ulsan was chosen to become a Special Industrial Zone and in the following year, it formally became a city.[^cite_note-16] The [Hyundai Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Group) and [SK Group](1174665.md) began setting up large businesses in Ulsan.[^cite_note-17] [Hyundai Heavy Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Heavy_Industries) established the shipyard in Ulsan and [Hyundai Motor Company](42257.md) started there.[^cite_note-18] Later, [SK Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Energy_\(company\)) developed an oil refinery and petrochemical hub there. Ulsan soon transformed into a modern metropolitan city.

On December 31, 1996, legislation on the promotion of Ulsan to a metropolitan city[^cite_note-19] was declared. Ulsan was promoted to a metropolitan city on July 15, 1997.[^cite_note-20]

## Geography, geology, ecology

Geographically, Ulsan is located at the southeastern tip of the Korean Peninsula and the southern end of the [Taebaek Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taebaek_Mountains). The coastline, classified as a mountainous emergent coastline, exhibits relatively simple development and is adjacent to the [East Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Sea_\(Korea\)).[^cite_note-21] The Taehwa River, which flows through the city into the East Sea, features unique fluvial landforms and has been a central axis for the city's ecological restoration efforts since the early 21st century.[^cite_note-22]

Ulsan is bounded to the south by Busan. Busan is 70 km (43 mi) to the south.

Ulsan has a [monsoon](57630.md)-influenced [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa*, bordering on *Cwa*), with cold but dry winters, and hot, humid summers. Monthly means range from 2.0 °C (35.6 °F) in January to 25.9 °C (78.6 °F) in August, with diurnal temperature ranges generally low. Its location on the Korean Peninsula results in a seasonal lag. The warmest days occur in August and average nearly 30 °C (86 °F). Precipitation is relatively low in the winter months, but there is high rainfall from April to September.

## Economy



[Ulsan Central market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_Central_market) entrance.

&#x20;As the centre of the *Ulsan Industrial District*, the city is the corporate base of the multinational [Hyundai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Group) conglomerate. Up to 1962, Ulsan operated as a fishing port and market center. As part of South Korea's first five-year economic plan, Ulsan became an open port. Additionally, the government designated Ulsan as a Special Industrial District,[^cite_note-Jacobs-30] which encouraged development of major industrial plants and factories: an [oil refinery](195137.md), fertilizer plants, automobile production, and heavy industries were developed here. The [shipbuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipbuilding) port [Bangeojin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangeojin) was annexed by the city in 1962.



Planned industrial development for the Ulsan Industrial Zone 1962.

Hyundai Group founded [Hyundai Heavy Industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Heavy_Industries) in Ulsan in 1973, which effectively turned the city into a company town and drew a large influx of workers into the city.[^cite_note-Kwak-31] The company's importance to the city can be seen in its name's omnipresence, with a highway named after Hyundai's founder, and the hospital, school, theater, as well as many restaurants and department stores bearing the Hyundai name.[^cite_note-32]



Hyundai Heavy Industries shipyard in Ulsan

Amid a global downturn in shipbuilding, Hyundai Heavy Industries sold \$1 billion of assets and laid off large numbers of employees in 2016.[^cite_note-33] The company borrowed money from the state-run [Korea Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Development_Bank) in order to purchase [Daewoo Shipbuilding & Marine Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daewoo_Shipbuilding_&_Marine_Engineering), forming [Korea Shipbuilding & Offshore Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_Shipbuilding_&_Offshore_Engineering?action=edit\&redlink=1), with plans to move corporate headquarters to [Seoul](19159283.md).[^cite_note-34] Some view this downturn as an indicator of South Korea's over-reliance on [chaebols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaebol), and fear that a period of [deindustrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deindustrialization) for Ulsan mirroring the [United States'](3434750.md) [Rust Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rust_Belt) could be on the horizon.[^cite_note-35]

The city has the [world's third largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oil_refineries) [oil refinery](195137.md) with a refining capacity of 840,000 BPD, owned by [SK Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Energy). A slightly smaller refinery with a refining capacity of 669,000 BPD, owned by [S-Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-Oil) (majority owned by [Saudi Aramco](290527.md)), is also in the city.[^cite_note-\:2-7] S-Oil is set to complete the Shaheen Project, one of the world's largest petrochemical facilities with an investment of nine trillion won, by 2026.[^cite_note-36]



Petrochemical complex in Ulsan

Ulsan is the home of the world's largest [automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) assembly plant, with an annual capacity of 1,700,000 units, operated by [Hyundai Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_Motors). The plant started with 50,000 capacity in 1968 and has expanded 30 times to become the top complex in the world with its own export piers with logistics competitiveness.[^cite_note-yonhapnews.co.kr-5] Its integrated design of related functions was inspired by the [Ford River Rouge Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_River_Rouge_Complex) in [Dearborn, Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dearborn,_Michigan).

In November 2011, [SB LiMotive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SB_LiMotive) opened an advanced [lithium-ion battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) production plant in Ulsan. SB LiMotive was a 50-50 consortium of [Samsung SDI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung_SDI) and [Robert Bosch GmbH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Bosch_GmbH).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] In September 2012, Samsung SDI bought out Robert Bosch GmbH's portion of SB LiMotive for \$95 million to gain 100% ownership of the Ulsan production facility.[^cite_note-40] The Ulsan plant is one of Samsung SDI's trio of advanced car-battery production facilities. [^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] In 2026, Samsung SDI announced a 16 trillion won investment plan in its Ulsan plant to mass produce solid state batteries.[^cite_note-43]

The Ulsan Green Hydrogen Town is a [hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen) city being developed as a pilot project. ## Education and Research



UNIST campus

Ulsan is home to one of Korea’s four national institutes of science and technology, the [Ulsan National Institute of Science and Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_National_Institute_of_Science_and_Technology) (UNIST). It is also home to the [University of Ulsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ulsan) and [Ulsan College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_College).

## Transportation

The city transport department plans to build a [light-rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail) line. The bus system shows a specific ETA at most bus stops.[^cite_note-Bus_Stops-45]

[Ulsan Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_Airport), constructed in 1970 and expanded in 1997, has more than 20 flights per day to and from [Seoul](19159283.md)'s [Gimpo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gimpo_International_Airport) and 4 flights per week to and from [Jeju International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeju_International_Airport). In November 2010, Korea's high-speed train network, the [KTX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KTX), was extended to Ulsan.[^cite_note-The_Korea_Times-46] This provides a high-speed link to Seoul, with a running time of just over 2 hours. The new KTX station, [Ulsan Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_Station), is in nearby [Eonyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eonyang), with a series of express buses (5001-5005), as well as some city buses serving the new station.[^cite_note-47] The original city station has been renamed Taehwa River Station.[^cite_note-The_Korea_Times-46]

In December 2016, 10 [Hyundai ix35 FCEVs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyundai_ix35_FCEV), the first hydrogen fuel cell taxis in the country, entered test operation.[^cite_note-\:1-9]

## Sports

The city hosts the [K League 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K_League_1) football club [Ulsan HD FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_HD_FC). After the [2002 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIFA_World_Cup), they relocated from their former stadium in [Jung District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jung_District,_Ulsan), which is now a municipal ground, to the [Munsu Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munsu_Stadium), which hosted several matches during the 2002 World Cup. The club have been crowned champions of Asia twice, winning the [AFC Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Champions_League) in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_AFC_Champions_League) and [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_AFC_Champions_League). Ulsan was home to another football team, [Ulsan Hyundai Mipo Dolphin FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_Hyundai_Mipo_Dolphin_FC), which played in the [Korea National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korea_National_League) until 2016, when it was dissolved. Currently, Ulsan is home to another football team, [Ulsan Citizen FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_Citizen_FC), which plays in the [K3 League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K3_League).



Ulsan Munsu Stadium, home to Ulsan HD

It is also home to the [University of Ulsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Ulsan) and its sports programs. Ulsan also hosts [Korean Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Basketball_League) team [Ulsan Hyundai Mobis Phoebus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_Hyundai_Mobis_Phoebus). Their home ground is [Dongchun Gymnasium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongchun_Gymnasium), which is located in Jung-Gu.[^cite_note-48]

[Lotte Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lotte_Giants), a [KBO League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KBO_League) baseball club in [Busan](68115.md), plays some of their home matches at the [Ulsan Munsu Baseball Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_Munsu_Baseball_Stadium), which they share with the [Ulsan Whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_Whales?action=edit\&redlink=1), an unaffiliated team in the [KBO's official minor leagues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KBO_Futures_League).

## Tourist attractions

In the Yeongnam Alps, there are seven tall mountains ([Gajisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gajisan_\(Ulsan/Gyeongsang\)), [Sinbulsan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinbulsan), Ganwolsan, Cheonhwangsan, Yeongchuksan, Goheonsan, Jaeyaksan) over 1,000 m above sea level. Sinbulsan (MT.) Ridge, where grasses turn silver in autumn, is one of the best sights to see in the [Yeongnam Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeongnam_Alps). Eoksae festival is held every early October in Ganwoljae, which is best known for its colony of silver grass. Korea's largest traditional folk [Onggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onggi) (earthenware) village is Oegosan. The traditional Onggi (earthenware) manufacturing process is carried on here and is open to tourists, including Onggi workshops and kilns. The Ulsan Onggi museum offers a variety of information related to Onggi and displays a variety of this earthenware

As the only [whale museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jangsaengpo_Whale_Museum) in Korea, Jangsaengpo Whale Museum collects, maintains and displays whaling-related artifacts. They have become more rare since 1986, when whaling was internationally prohibited in order to protect the species. The museum provides a variety of information related to whales and marine ecosystems; it is a space for education, research and experience. Whale cruises depart from [Jangsaengpo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jangsaengpo) port.

[Ulsan Grand Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulsan_Grand_Park) is claimed as the best ecology park in a downtown area in Korea, boasting a vast area of 3,640,000 m<sup>2</sup> (39,180,634 sq ft). "Natural, Clean and Comfortable" are the main themes. It sponsors a variety of events and festivals for families. Notably, there is the Rose Festival every June.

Taehwagang (River) National Garden; Simnidaesup (10-ri [bamboo](39029.md) grove), one of the 12 "scenic beauties" of Ulsan, was restored and the river and the bamboo grove are now connected. Visitors can observe wildlife in its natural environment and relax.

Ulsan has beaches (Jinha, Ilsan). [Daewangam Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daewangam_Park) has a hundred-year-old pine forest. [Ganjeolgot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganjeolgot) Cape is the first place to see the sunrise from the Korean peninsula. A sunrise festival is held every New Year's Day.

Ulsan Industry Park has been leading the Pacific Rim industry in the 21st century. Ulsan has [Hyundai Motor Company](42257.md), with the world's largest single-purpose plant; Hyundai Heavy Industries, the biggest heavy industry leader in the world; Hyundai Mipo Shipbuilding, and Petrochemical Park, leaders in Korea's chemistry industry.
