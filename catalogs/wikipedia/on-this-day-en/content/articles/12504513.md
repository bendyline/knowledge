The **Argentina national under-20 [football](10568.md) team** is the representative of [Argentina](18951905.md) in [FIFA](11049.md)-sponsored tournaments that pertain to that age level.

Argentina is the most successful nation in the [FIFA U-20 World Cup](1168189.md), winning the competition a record six times. The team has participated in 18 of the 24 World Championship events, since the [1979 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship), which they won. Argentina has also won five [South American Youth Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_American_Youth_Football_Championship).

## History

### 1979: The first world title



The Argentine team that won the 1979 World Youth Championship

Argentina did not participate in the [first FIFA World Youth Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) in [Tunisia](30188.md), but appeared at the [next edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) held in [Japan](15573.md). The team, coached by [César Luis Menotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9sar_Luis_Menotti) with the help of [Ernesto Duchini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernesto_Duchini) (who had previously chosen the players and worked with them),[^cite_note-eldiez-1] won the tournament, showing a fine style of play consisting of high possession of the ball, diverse passing, dribbling, a solid defense and a powerful offensive line that scored a total of 20 goals in the tournament. [Diego Maradona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Maradona) and [Ramón Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_D%C3%ADaz) were the team's most notable players. The tournament was also the first official championship played by Maradona for the national team. After his frustration of 1978, Maradona made the most of his performances during the tournament, being the best player of the team due to his passing moves, dribbling, accuracy with free kicks and the six goals he scored.

> Before every match, Diego played with the ball, putting it on his neck or his shoulders while the Japanese people couldn't stop applauding him. When I would see this, I would say to myself: "Wow, and the show hasn't even started yet".

— Osvaldo Rinaldi, remembering his teammate Maradona's performance at the [1979 FIFA World Youth Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship)

[^cite_note-eldiez-1]

Argentina debuted in Group B, thrashing [Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesia_national_under-20_football_team) 5–0 in their first match, beating [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslavia_national_under-20_football_team) 1–0 in the second and defeating [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_national_under-20_football_team) in the third match, 4–1. The youth squad finished first in the group with ten goals scored and only one conceded. En route to the final, Argentina hammered [Algeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria_national_under-20_football_team) 5–0, then defeated arch-rival [Uruguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruguay_national_under-20_football_team) 2–0. In [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_FIFA_U-20_World_Cup_Final) against the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_national_under-20_football_team) on 7 September, the team won 3–1, becoming the World Youth Champions for the first time. Ramón Díaz won the Golden Shoe as the topscorer, with eight goals, while Maradona was awarded the Golden Ball as best player of the tournament.[^cite_note-2]

Apart from Maradona and Díaz, other notable players of the team were [Juan Simón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Sim%C3%B3n), Hugo Alves, [Gabriel Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Calder%C3%B3n), [Juan Barbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Barbas) and [Osvaldo Escudero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osvaldo_Escudero). That team is still regarded as one of the best Argentine national squads ever.[^cite_note-3]

### 1981–91: The dark decade



Argentina playing against Brazil in the 1983 World Cup final, where they lost 1–0

Argentina attended the next tournament, hosted by [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_national_under-20_football_team) in [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship). They were defeated 2–1 by hosts Australia, then achieved a draw with [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_national_under-20_football_team) (1–1) and beat [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_under-20_football_team) 1–0. Argentina did not qualify to the next stage, however, earning only three points after three matches played.

The team made a much better performance at the [1983 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) in [Mexico](3966054.md), reaching the final with [Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazil_national_under-20_football_team). On the first round, Argentina thrashed [China PR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_national_under-20_football_team) 5–0, then defeated [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria_national_under-20_football_team) 3–0 and beat [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia_national_under-21_football_team) 2–0. The team finished first in the group with zero goals conceded. In the quarter-finals, Argentina defeated Netherlands 2–1 (after [Marco van Basten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_van_Basten) had opened the scored for the *Oranje*) and Poland 1–0 in the semi-finals. On 19 June 1983, Argentina played the final against Brazil, falling 1–0 at the [Estadio Azteca](1834902.md).[^cite_note-4] The team was coached by [Carlos Pachamé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Pacham%C3%A9), designated by the Senior team coach, [Carlos Bilardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Bilardo), to work with youth players.

Some of the players of that team were goalkeeper [Luis Islas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Islas), defenders [Fabián Basualdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabi%C3%A1n_Basualdo), [Jorge Theiler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Theiler), [Carlos Enrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Enrique); midfielders [Mario Vanemerak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Vanemerak), [Oscar Acosta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Rom%C3%A1n_Acosta) and Roberto Zárate; and forwards [Claudio García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Garc%C3%ADa), [Jorge Luis Gabrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Luis_Gabrich) and [Oscar Dertycia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Dertycia).[^cite_note-5]

Argentina did not qualify for the 1985 and 1987 championships (played in the Soviet Union and Chile respectively), but the team participated in the [tournament held in Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) as one of the three qualified in the South American championship. Argentina was defeated by Spain in the first match. The team recovered winning the second game to [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_national_under-20_football_team) 2–0, but although it lost the last match to [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_national_under-20_football_team), Argentina qualified for the second round. In the knockout stage, the team was beaten 1–0 by Brazil.

For the [1991 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) held in Portugal, Argentina was coached by [Reinaldo Merlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reinaldo_Merlo), who was designated by then-senior coach [Alfio Basile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfio_Basile) as it had been in the precedent era.

Argentina made its worst campaign in youth tournaments, finishing last in their group with only one point earned from three matches. The team lost to Korea united team 1–0 in the first match, then were defeated by hosts Portugal 3–0 in a match where three Argentine players ([Claudio París](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudio_Par%C3%ADs), [Mauricio Pellegrino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Pellegrino) and [Juan Esnáider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Esn%C3%A1ider)) were sent off for their rough play which culminated in a brawl on the pitch between both teams. As a result, [FIFA](11049.md) punished the [Argentina Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_Football_Association) (AFA) with a two-year suspension, as well as a one-year suspension for Esnáider and a two-year suspension for Norberto Recassens (one of the representatives of the AFA), both of whom insulted the referees in their dressing room at the end of the match.[^cite_note-6]

Some of the players that took part of that team were goalkeeper [Leonardo Díaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_D%C3%ADaz_\(footballer\)), defenders [Diego Cocca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Cocca), [Mauricio Pochettino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Pochettino) and Pellegrino; midfielders París, [Walter Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Paz) [Hugo Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Morales_\(footballer\)) and [Christian Bassedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Bassedas); and forwards [Marcelo Delgado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Delgado) and Esnáider.[^cite_note-7]

### Pékerman era: the golden years

Because it was banned, Argentina did not participate in the 1993 World Cup in Australia. The [Argentina Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_Football_Association) (AFA) had opted to name a new coach entirely independent from the senior team coach as had been until then. Selected was [José Pékerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_P%C3%A9kerman), who, despite not having much previous experience, convinced the AFA to hire him.

The good results were immediate: Argentina won the first World Cup contested with Pékerman as coach, held in [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) in [Qatar](8238258.md). In the first stage, Argentina defeated the Netherlands 1–0, then lost to Portugal 1–0 followed by a 4–2 win over Honduras, securing a second-place finish and progression to the quarter-finals, where they thrashed Cameroon by 4–0. In semi-finals, Argentina beat Spain 3–0 then defeated Brazil 2–0 in the final, exacting revenge from the 1983 tournament where the Brazilians won.

Some of its most notable players were [Juan Pablo Sorín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Pablo_Sor%C3%ADn), [Joaquín Irigoytía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joaqu%C3%ADn_Irigoyt%C3%ADa), [Federico Domínguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Dom%C3%ADnguez_\(footballer,_born_1976\)), [Mariano Juan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariano_Juan), [Ariel Ibagaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ariel_Ibagaza), [Leonardo Biagini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Biagini) and [Walter Coyette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Coyette).[^cite_note-8]

Argentina won its third title at the [1997 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship), hosted by [Malaysia](3607937.md). The team defeated Hungary 3–0 and Canada 2–1, but lost to Australia 4–3. Argentina passed to the round of 16 where they defeated England 2–1. In quarter-finals, Argentina eliminated Brazil after winning 2–0, then beat the Republic of Ireland 1–0 in the semi-finals. In the final match, played on 5 July 1997, the squad defeated Uruguay 2–1 to win its third championship. The team also received the FIFA Fair Play Award in recognition to the good behaviour showed on the field.

Argentina showed the talent of notable players such as [Leonardo Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Franco), [Fabián Cubero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabi%C3%A1n_Cubero), [Leandro Cufré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandro_Cufr%C3%A9), [Walter Samuel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Samuel), [Diego Placente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Placente), [Esteban Cambiasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esteban_Cambiasso), [Pablo Aimar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Aimar), [Juan Román Riquelme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Rom%C3%A1n_Riquelme) and [Bernardo Romeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardo_Romeo), many of them with already many matched played in the [Primera División](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n) when the tournament began.[^cite_note-9]

The performance during the [1999 World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) in [Nigeria](21383.md) was not so good. Argentina finished 3rd of 4 in the group, winning over Kazakhstan 1–0 at the debut but with a game lost to Ghana (1-0) and a 0–0 draw with Croatia. On the round of 16, Argentina was largely defeated at the hands Mexico by 4-1 and eliminated from the tournament.

In [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) Argentina hosted its first Youth Championship. The team won its fourth title, the third championship in seven years. Argentina debuted in the [José Amalfitani Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Amalfitani_Stadium) (the venue where the team played all its games in Buenos Aires) defeating Finland 1–0. The next game Argentina thrashed Egypt 7–1 (with three goals by [Javier Saviola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Saviola)), and closed its participation in Group A by smashing Jamaica by 5–1. In the knockout round, Argentina successively eliminated China (2–1), France (3–1) and Paraguay (5–0), winning the tournament with a convincing 3–0 over Ghana at the final, played on 8 July at Vélez Sársfield. Argentina won its fourth youth title unbeaten, scoring 27 goals in 7 matches, conceding 4. River Plate's forward Javier Saviola was awarded with the Golden Shoe (as the topscorer with 11) and Golden Ball as the best player of the tournament. Likewise, Argentina was awarded the FIFA Fair Play Award for the second-straight time. Apart from the multi-awarded Saviola, the national squad had a powerful team with most of its players being experienced playing at the domestic first division, notably [Nicolás Burdisso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Burdisso), [Leonardo Ponzio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonardo_Ponzio), [Julio Arca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Arca), [Leandro Romagnoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leandro_Romagnoli), [Mauro Rosales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauro_Rosales), [Andrés D'Alessandro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_D'Alessandro) and [Maxi Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxi_Rodr%C3%ADguez).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The 2001 championship was the last title won with Pékerman as coach, closing an era that brought back the prestige to Argentine football.

### 2003–07: The success continues

After the departure of Pékerman, former goalkeeper [Hugo Tocalli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Tocalli) was designed to replace him. With Tocalli as coach, Argentina made its debut at [2003 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) defeating Spain 2–1. The team also beat Uzbekistan (by the same score) and Mali (3–1). Argentina finished first and unbeaten the first stage. In the round of 16, the national squad beat Egypt 2–1, then defeated United States 2-1 but Argentina lost to Brazil 1–0 at the semifinals. The team was also defeated by Colombia in the 3rd place match so Argentina finished in the 4th position of the general table. Striker [Fernando Cavenaghi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Cavenaghi) was the top scorer of the tournament with 4 goals.

In 2004, Pékerman was appointed to coach the [Argentina senior team](454699.md) ahead of [2006 FIFA World Cup qualifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification).[^cite_note-12] One year later, Tocalli left the youth team to join Pékerman's coaching staff on the senior squad, so Francisco Ferraro was designated coach,[^cite_note-13] won its fifth title at the [World championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_FIFA_World_Youth_Championship) hosted by the Netherlands. Argentina lost to the United States in the first match, won against Egypt (2–1) and Germany (1–0) to qualify for the next stage. From the round of 16 to the semi-finals, Argentina successively eliminated Colombia (2–1), Spain (3–1) and Brazil (2–1) reaching the finals for the sixth time. On 2 July 2005, Argentina defeated Nigeria 2–1 in the final to win its fifth youth championship. Both goals were scored by [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) from the penalty spot.[^cite_note-14] Messi was the leader of the team and his magical performances resulted in him winning both the top scorer and best player's awards.[^cite_note-15]

In the 2005 team's squad, Lionel Messi was the star player and had already been a La Liga champion with FC Barcelona in the 2004–05 season. Other notable players for Argentina in that year were goalkeeper [Oscar Ustari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Ustari); defenders [Ezequiel Garay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ezequiel_Garay) and [Julio Barroso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julio_Barroso), midfielders [Pablo Zabaleta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Zabaleta), [Fernando Gago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Gago) and [Lucas Biglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucas_Biglia) and forwards [Sergio Agüero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Ag%C3%BCero) and [Neri Cardozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neri_Cardozo).

Two years later, the team won its second straight title at the [2007 FIFA U-20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FIFA_U-20_World_Cup), hosted by Canada. After a 0–0 draw in the debut with the Czech Republic, Argentina smashed Panama 6–0 and defeated North Korea[^cite_note-16] 1–0 to secure qualification to the second round. In the knockout stage, Argentina eliminated Poland (3–1), followed by Mexico (1–0) and Chile (3–0) en route to the final. In the final, played on 22 July, Argentina won its sixth title after defeating the Czechs 3–1. [Sergio Agüero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Ag%C3%BCero), the top scorer of the championship with six goals, was also awarded the Golden Ball as best player. Other notable players on the squad were goalkeeper [Sergio Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Romero), midfielders [Éver Banega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ver_Banega) and [Maxi Moralez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximiliano_Moralez) and forwards [Ángel Di María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Di_Mar%C3%ADa), [Mauro Zárate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauro_Z%C3%A1rate) and [Pablo Piatti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Piatti).

### Since 2009: Struggle

Argentina's successful performances could not be repeated at the following seven U-20 World Cups. The team failed to qualify twice (2009, 2013) and was eliminated from the group stage on two occasions (2015, 2017).
