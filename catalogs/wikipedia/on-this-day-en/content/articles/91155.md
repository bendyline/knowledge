**Everton Football Club** ([/ˈɛvərtən/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is a professional [association football](10568.md) club based in [Liverpool](18081.md), England. The club competes in the [Premier League](11250.md), the top tier of [English football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system).&#x20;

Founded in 1878, the club was a founding member of the [Football League](14419588.md) in 1888, and was a founding member of the Premier League in 1992, one of just three clubs to have been a founding member of both leagues. Everton are one of the oldest and most successful clubs in England, having won fifteen major trophies: nine [football league titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_English_football_champions), five [FA Cups](11237.md) and one [European Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup). Everton won their first League Championship during the [1890–91 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1890%E2%80%9391_Everton_F.C._season). After winning four more League championships and two FA Cups, the club experienced a post–[Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) lull until a revival in the 1960s. A period of sustained success came in the mid-1980s, when Everton won a further two League championships, one FA Cup and the [1985 European Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup_Final). The club's most recent major trophy was the [1995 FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FA_Cup_Final).

Everton is the club with the second-longest continuous presence in English top-flight football, and ranks third in the all-time points rankings.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] As of August 2025, the club have competed in the top division for a [record 123 seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_records_and_statistics_in_England#Top-flight_appearances), more than any other club, having missed only [four top-flight seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Everton_F.C._seasons) ([1930–31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930%E2%80%9331_Football_League), [1951–52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951%E2%80%9352_Everton_F.C._season), [1952–53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952%E2%80%9353_Everton_F.C._season), and [1953–54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953%E2%80%9354_Everton_F.C._season)).[^cite_note-3]

The club's traditional [kit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit_\(association_football\)) is [royal blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_blue) shirts with white shorts and socks. Everton play at the [Hill Dickinson Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_Dickinson_Stadium) as of the [2026–27 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026-27_Everton_F.C._season). Everton had played at their previous home ground [Goodison Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodison_Park) since [1892](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1892%E2%80%9393_Everton_F.C._season), having moved from their original home at [Anfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anfield) following a disagreement with the landowner [John Houlding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Houlding) over their rent. The [club's supporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everton_F.C._supporters) are colloquially known as "Evertonians", the "Toffees" or the "Blues". Everton have a long-standing rivalry with nearby club [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.), with whom they contest the [Merseyside derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merseyside_derby). The club was owned by [Farhad Moshiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farhad_Moshiri), after he bought 49.9% of the club's shares in 2016. As of May 2023, the club's value was \$744 million. The club's revenue and operating income for the [2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Everton_F.C._season) were \$242 million and -\$23 million respectively.[^cite_note-4] In December 2024, American billionaire [Dan Friedkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Friedkin) purchased the club.

## History

Main article: [History of Everton F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Everton_F.C.)



Chart showing the progress of Everton through the [English football league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_league_system) since 1889

Everton was founded as St. Domingo's FC in 1878[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-Gore's_Directory_1878-6] so that members of the congregation of St Domingo Methodist New Connexion Chapel in Breckfield Road North, [Everton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everton,_Liverpool), could play sport year round – [cricket](25675557.md) was played in summer. The club's first game was a 1–0 victory over Everton Church Club.[^cite_note-stdomingos-7] The club was renamed Everton in November 1879 after the local area, as people outside the congregation wished to participate.[^cite_note-stdomingos-7][^cite_note-8]



The Everton team that won the first league title in [1891](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1890%E2%80%9391_Football_League)

The club was a founding member of the [Football League](14419588.md) in 1888–89 and won their first [League Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division) title in the [1890–91 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1890%E2%80%9391_Football_League). The 1890–91 season started in superb form with five straight victories, with [Fred Geary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Geary) scoring in each of the first six matches.[^cite_note-9] By mid-January, Everton had completed all but one of their fixtures and were on 29 points, while [Preston North End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preston_North_End_F.C.) were eleven points adrift with seven games still to play. Everton than had to sit out the next two months as Preston completed their fixture list until they were only two points adrift with one match each left to play. Both teams played their final games of the season on 14 March, with Everton losing 3–2 at [Burnley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnley_F.C.) (Geary scored both Everton goals) and Preston going down 3–0 at [Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunderland_A.F.C.).[^cite_note-Gibbons-10] Everton were thus able to win the Football League Championship for the first time, by a margin of two points with fourteen victories from their 22 league games. Geary had been ever-present, and was the club's top goal-scorer with 21 goals.

Everton reached four [FA Cup](11237.md) finals before the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), losing 1–0 against [Wolverhampton Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolverhampton_Wanderers) at [Fallowfield Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallowfield_Stadium), [Manchester](20206.md) on 25 March [1893](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1893_FA_Cup_Final) and 3–2 against [Aston Villa](42173.md) at [Crystal Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystal_Palace_National_Sports_Centre) on 10 April [1897](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1897_FA_Cup_Final) before winning at their third attempt on 20 April [1906](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1906_FA_Cup_Final) against Newcastle United again at Crystal Palace. Everton then reached their second successive final on 20 April [1907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1907_FA_Cup_Final), however, finished in a 2–1 defeat to [Sheffield Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Wednesday_F.C.).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] [1914–15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1914%E2%80%9315_Football_League) was to be the final season before league football was suspended for the duration of the First World War. Everton won their second league title, one point ahead of [Oldham Athletic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldham_Athletic_A.F.C.), with [Bobby Parker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Parker_\(footballer,_born_1891\)) finishing the season as the leagues' top scorer on 35 goals.[^cite_note-kit-13] The outbreak of the First World War in 1914 interrupted the football programme while Everton were reigning champions, which was something that would again occur in 1939.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

In 1925 the club signed [Dixie Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie_Dean) from [Tranmere Rovers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tranmere_Rovers_F.C.). In [1927–28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1927%E2%80%9328_Football_League), Dean set the record for top-flight league goals in a single season with 60 goals in 39 league games, which is a record that still stands. He helped Everton win their third League Championship that season.[^cite_note-Begining-16] However, Everton were relegated to the [Second Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_Second_Division) two years later during internal turmoil at the club. The club quickly rebounded and were promoted at the first attempt, while scoring a record number of goals in the Second Division. On return to the top flight in [1931–32](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931%E2%80%9332_Football_League), Everton wasted no time in reaffirming their status and won a fourth League Championship at the first opportunity.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Everton also won their second FA Cup in 1933 with a 3–0 win against [Manchester City](165813.md) in the final. The era ended in [1938–39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938%E2%80%9339_Football_League) with a fifth League Championship.[^cite_note-Football_and_the_First_World_War-19][^cite_note-20]

The outbreak of the [Second World War](32927.md) again saw the suspension of league football, and when official competition resumed in 1946, the Everton team had been split up and paled in comparison to the pre-war team. [Tommy Lawton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Lawton) was sold to [Chelsea](7473.md), [Joe Mercer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Mercer) disagreed with the manager [Theo Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theo_Kelly) and was sold to [Arsenal](2174.md), and they tried to sell [T. G. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._G._Jones) to [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.S._Roma). Soon, only [Ted Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Sagar) was left. Under the management of the uninspired and under-financed [Cliff Britton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cliff_Britton), Everton were relegated for the second time in [1950–51](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950%E2%80%9351_Football_League) and did not earn promotion until [1953–54](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1953%E2%80%9354_Football_League), when they finished as the runner-up in their third season in the Second Division. The club has been a top-flight presence ever since.[^cite_note-21]

Everton's second successful era started when [Harry Catterick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Catterick) was made manager in 1961. In [1962–63](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962%E2%80%9363_Football_League), his second season in charge, Everton won the League Championship.[^cite_note-22] In 1966 the club won the FA Cup with a 3–2 win over Sheffield Wednesday.[^cite_note-23] Everton again reached the final in 1968, but this time were unable to overcome [West Bromwich Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich_Albion_F.C.) at [Wembley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wembley_Stadium_\(1923\)).[^cite_note-24] Two seasons later in [1969–70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969%E2%80%9370_Football_League), Everton won the League Championship, finishing nine points clear of nearest rivals [Leeds United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_F.C.).[^cite_note-25] During this period, Everton were the first English club to achieve five consecutive years in European competitions – covering the seasons from 1961–62 to 1966–67.[^cite_note-26]

However, the success did not last; the team finished fourteenth, fifteenth, seventeenth and seventh in the following seasons. Harry Catterick retired, but his successors failed to win any [silverware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trophy) for the remainder of the 1970s despite finishing fourth in [1974–75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_Football_League) under manager Billy Bingham, third in [1977–78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977%E2%80%9378_Football_League) and fourth the following season under manager [Gordon Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Lee_\(footballer\)). Lee was sacked in 1981.[^cite_note-27]



Everton's [FA Cup](11237.md) winning medals from [1906](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1906_FA_Cup_final), [1933](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1933_FA_Cup_final), [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_FA_Cup_final), [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_FA_Cup_final) and [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FA_Cup_final)

[Howard Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Kendall) took over as manager and guided Everton to their most successful era. Domestically, Everton won the FA Cup in 1984 and two League Championships in [1984–85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_Football_League) and [1986–87](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_Football_League). In Europe, the club won their first, and so far only, European trophy by securing the [European Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) in [1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup_final).[^cite_note-modernhistory-28] The European success came after first beating [University College Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_College_Dublin_A.F.C.), [Inter Bratislava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FK_Inter_Bratislava) and [Fortuna Sittard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortuna_Sittard). Then, Everton defeated German giants [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) 3–1 in the semi-finals, despite trailing at half time (in a match voted the greatest in Goodison Park history), and recorded the same scoreline over Austrian club [Rapid Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Rapid_Wien) in the final.[^cite_note-29] Having won both the League and Cup Winners' Cup in 1985, Everton came very close to winning a [treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_\(association_football\)), but lost to [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) in the FA Cup [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_FA_Cup_final).[^cite_note-modernhistory-28] The following season, [1985–86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985%E2%80%9386_Football_League), Everton was the runner-up to Liverpool in both the League and the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_FA_Cup_final), but did recapture the League Championship in 1986–87.

After the [Heysel Stadium disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heysel_Stadium_disaster) and the subsequent ban of all English clubs from continental football, Everton lost the chance to compete for more European trophies. A large proportion of the title-winning side was broken up following the ban. Kendall himself moved to [Athletic Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao) after the 1987 title triumph and was succeeded by assistant [Colin Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Harvey). Harvey took Everton to the [1989 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_FA_Cup_final), but lost 3–2 after extra time to Liverpool.[^cite_note-30]

Everton was a founding member of the [Premier League](11250.md) in 1992, but struggled to find the right manager. Howard Kendall had returned in 1990, but could not repeat his previous success. His successor, [Mike Walker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Walker_\(Welsh_footballer\)), was statistically the least successful Everton manager to date. When former Everton player [Joe Royle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Royle) took over in 1994, the club's form started to improve; his first game in charge was a 2–0 victory over derby rivals Liverpool. Royle dragged Everton clear of relegation and led the club to the FA Cup for the fifth time in their history by defeating Manchester United 1–0 in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FA_Cup_final). The cup triumph was also Everton's passport to the Cup Winners' Cup, their first European campaign in the post-Heysel era. Progress under Royle continued in [1995–96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_FA_Premier_League) as the team climbed to sixth place in the Premiership.[^cite_note-modernhistory-28] A fifteenth-place finish the following season saw Royle resign towards the end of the campaign, and he was temporarily replaced by club captain [Dave Watson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Watson).

Howard Kendall was appointed Everton manager for the third time in 1997, but the appointment proved unsuccessful as Everton finished seventeenth in the Premiership. The club only avoided relegation due to their superior [goal difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_difference) over [Bolton Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolton_Wanderers_F.C.). Former [Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rangers_F.C.) manager [Walter Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Smith) then took over from Kendall in the summer of 1998, but only managed three successive finishes in the bottom half of the table.[^cite_note-modernhistory-28] The Everton board finally ran out of patience with Smith, and he was sacked in March 2002 after an FA Cup exit at [Middlesbrough](298603.md) and with Everton in real danger of relegation.[^cite_note-31] His replacement, [David Moyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Moyes), guided Everton to a safe finish in fifteenth place.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

In [2002–03](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002%E2%80%9303_FA_Premier_League) Everton finished seventh, which was their highest finish since 1996. It was under Moyes' management that [Wayne Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Rooney) broke into the first team before being sold to Manchester United for a club record fee of £28 million in the summer of 2004.[^cite_note-34] A fourth-place finish in [2004–05](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_FA_Premier_League) ensured that Everton qualified for the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) qualifying round. The team failed to make it through to the [Champions League group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_UEFA_Champions_League) and were then eliminated from the [UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_UEFA_Cup). Everton qualified for the [2007–08](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_UEFA_Cup)[^cite_note-35] and [2008–09 UEFA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_UEFA_Cup) competitions, and was the runner-up in the [2009 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FA_Cup_final). During this period, Moyes broke the club record for highest transfer fee paid on four occasions: signing [James Beattie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Beattie_\(footballer\)) for £6 million in January 2005,[^cite_note-aj_and_beattie-36] [Andy Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Johnson_\(footballer,_born_1981\)) for £8.6 million in summer 2006,[^cite_note-aj_and_beattie-36] [Yakubu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakubu_\(footballer\)) for £11.25 million in summer 2007,[^cite_note-37] and [Marouane Fellaini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marouane_Fellaini) for £15 million in September 2008.[^cite_note-38]



Everton squad for the [UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_UEFA_Europa_League) round of 16 against Dynamo Kyiv in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_UEFA_Europa_League)

At the end of the [2012–13 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Everton_F.C._season), Moyes left his position at Everton to take over at Manchester United, and was replaced by [Roberto Martínez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Mart%C3%ADnez).[^cite_note-39] Martínez led Everton to 5th place in the Premier League in his [first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Premier_League) while amassing the club's best points tally in 27 years with 72.[^cite_note-40] The following season, he led Everton to the last 16 of the [2014–15 UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_UEFA_Europa_League), where they were defeated by [Dynamo Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Dynamo_Kyiv),[^cite_note-41] whilst domestically finishing 11th in the Premier League. Everton reached the semi-finals of both the [League Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Football_League_Cup) and the [FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_FA_Cup) in 2015–16, but were defeated in both. After a poor run of form in the Premier League, Martínez was sacked following the penultimate game of the season, with Everton lying in 12th place.[^cite_note-42]

Martínez was replaced in the summer of 2016 by former [Southampton](81576.md) manager [Ronald Koeman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Koeman).[^cite_note-43] In [his first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Premier_League) at the club, he qualified for the Europa League, but a poor start to the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Everton_F.C._season) left Everton in the relegation zone after nine games, and Koeman was sacked on 23 October following a 5–2 home defeat to Arsenal.[^cite_note-44] [Sam Allardyce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Allardyce) succeeded him,[^cite_note-\:0x-45] but he resigned at the end of the season amid fan discontent at his style of play.[^cite_note-46]



[Wayne Rooney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Rooney), who returns to the club after becoming Manchester United's record goalscorer, celebrates for Everton after his goal against Liverpool in the [2017–18 Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Premier_League).

[Marco Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Silva) was named Everton manager in May 2018.[^cite_note-47] In November that year, the club was banned from signing academy football players from their youth clubs for two years.[^cite_note-48] Silva led Everton to finish eighth in his first season in charge, but after a poor start to the following season which left the team in the relegation zone, he was sacked on 5 December 2019.[^cite_note-\:2-49] His last league match was a 5–2 loss to Liverpool at Anfield. Former player and first-team coach [Duncan Ferguson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Ferguson) stepped in as caretaker manager for the next three games before his replacement, [Carlo Ancelotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Ancelotti); Ferguson stayed as assistant manager.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

Ancelotti left the club in June 2021 to rejoin former club [Real Madrid](26413.md) as manager, having led the club to a tenth-place finish in his only full season at the club.[^cite_note-52] Former Liverpool manager [Rafael Benítez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Ben%C3%ADtez) was appointed as his replacement, subsequently becoming only the second person to manage both Liverpool and Everton.[^cite_note-53] He was dismissed in January 2022 following nine losses in his last 13 games in charge at the club,[^cite_note-54] and was replaced by former Chelsea boss [Frank Lampard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Lampard).[^cite_note-55] Lampard was later also dismissed in January 2023 after a poor run of results.[^cite_note-56] Everton narrowly escaped relegation with a 1–0 win over [Bournemouth](451121.md) in their last game of the [2022–23 Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Premier_League) under the management of new boss [Sean Dyche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Dyche).[^cite_note-57]



Everton playing against [Nottingham Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Forest_F.C.) at Goodison Park, 21 April 2024

On 17 November 2023, the club received a ten-point deduction with immediate effect for breaches of the Premier League's profit and sustainability rules. The club had made losses of £124.5 million in the three years up to 2021–22, with the limit being £105 million.[^cite_note-58] It was the biggest points deduction in Premier League history, surpassing the nine taken from [Portsmouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portsmouth_F.C.) in 2010.[^cite_note-59] Everton submitted an appeal to the decision, arguing that the commission was biased and that the punishment was excessive and unfair.[^cite_note-60] On 24 January 2024, Everton were charged with further breaches of Premier League financial rules after "sustaining losses above the permitted thresholds for the assessment period ending season 2022–23".[^cite_note-BBC-15Jan2024-61]

On 9 January 2025, Dyche was relieved of his duties with Everton one point above the relegation zone,[^cite_note-62] and David Moyes returned to the club after almost twelve years away.[^cite_note-63]

## Colours and badge

















Everton's first home colours

Everton's traditional home colours are royal blue shirts, white shorts and white socks. However, during the first decades of their history, Everton had several different kit colours. The team originally played in white and then blue and white stripes, but as new players arriving at the club wore their old team's shirts during matches, confusion soon ensued. It was decided that the shirts would be dyed black, both to save on expenses and to instill a more professional look. However, the kit appeared morbid, so a scarlet sash was added.[^cite_note-kit-13] When the club moved to Goodison Park in 1892, the colours were salmon pink and dark blue striped shirts with dark blue shorts. The club later switched to ruby shirts with blue trim and dark blue shorts. Royal blue jerseys with white shorts were first used in the [1901–02](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1901%E2%80%9302_in_English_football) season.[^cite_note-kit-13] The club played in sky blue in 1906; however, the fans protested, and the colour reverted to royal blue. Occasionally, Everton have played in lighter shades than royal blue (such as in [1930–31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1930%E2%80%9331_in_English_football) and [1997–98](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_in_English_football)).[^cite_note-64] The home kit today is royal blue shirts with white shorts and socks. The club may also wear all blue to avoid any colour clashes. Everton's traditional away colours were white shirts with black shorts, but from 1968 amber shirts and royal blue shorts became common. Various editions appeared throughout the 1970s and 1980s. Black, white, grey, and yellow away shirts have also been used.



Monochrome Everton crest (2000–2013)



[2013–14 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_Everton_F.C._season) crest



The [Everton Lock-Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everton_Lock-Up) has featured on Everton's crest since 1938.

At the end of the [1937–38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1937%E2%80%9338_in_English_football) season, Everton secretary [Theo Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theo_Kelly), who later became the club's first manager, wanted to design a club necktie. It was agreed that the colour be blue, and Kelly was given the task of designing a crest to be featured on the necktie. He worked on it for four months until deciding on a reproduction of [Everton Lock-Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everton_Lock-Up), which stands in the heart of the [Everton district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everton,_Liverpool).[^cite_note-crest-65] The Lock-Up has been linked with the Everton area since its construction in 1787. It was originally used as a [bridewell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_lock-up) to incarcerate mainly drunks and minor criminals and it still stands on Everton Brow. The Lock-Up was accompanied by two laurel wreaths on either side and, according to the [College of Arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_of_Arms) in London, Kelly chose to include the [laurels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laurel_wreath) as they were the sign of winners. The crest was accompanied by the club motto, *"[Nil satis nisi optimum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nil_satis_nisi_optimum)"*, meaning "Nothing but the best is good enough".[^cite_note-crest-65] The ties were first worn by Kelly and the Everton chairman, Mr. E. Green, on the first day of the [1938–39](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1938%E2%80%9339_in_English_football) season.[^cite_note-crest-65]

The club rarely incorporated a badge of any description on their shirts. An interwoven "EFC" design was adopted between 1922 and 1930, before the club reverted to plain royal blue shirts until 1972, when bold "EFC" lettering was added. The crest designed by Kelly was first used on the team's shirts in 1978 and has remained there ever since, while undergoing gradual change to become the version used today.

In May 2013, the club launched a new crest to improve the reproducibility of the design in print and broadcast media, particularly on a small scale.[^cite_note-66] Critics suggested that it was external pressure from sports manufacturer [Nike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nike,_Inc.) that evoked the redesign as the number of colours had been reduced and the radial effect was removed, which made the kit more cost efficient to reproduce.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] The redesign was poorly received by supporters, with a poll on an Everton fan site registering a 91% negative response to the crest.[^cite_note-69] A protest petition reached over 22,000 signatures before the club offered an apology and announced a new crest would be created for the 2014–15 season with an emphasis on fan consultation. Shortly afterwards, the Head of Marketing left the club. The latest crest was revealed by the club on 3 October 2013. After a consultation process with the supporters, three new crests were shortlisted. In the final vote, the new crest was chosen by almost 80% of the supporters that took part[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] and began being used in July 2014.[^cite_note-72]

### Nickname

Everton's most widely recognised nickname is "The Toffees" or "The Toffeemen", which came about after Everton had moved to Goodison. There are several explanations for how this name came to be adopted with the best known being that there was a business in Everton village, between Everton Brow and Brow Side, named Mother Noblett's, which was a [toffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toffee) shop that sold sweets including Everton Mints (a type of [humbug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humbug_\(sweet\))). It was also located opposite the lock-up on which Everton's club crest is based. The Toffee Lady tradition, in which a girl walks around the perimeter of the pitch before the start of a game tossing free Everton Mints into the crowd, symbolises the connection. Another possible reason is that there was a house named *Ye Anciente Everton Toffee House* in nearby Village Street, Everton, run by Ma Bushell. The toffee house was located near the Queen's Head hotel in which early club meetings took place.[^cite_note-73]

Everton have had many other nicknames over the years. When the black kit was worn, the team were nicknamed "The Black Watch" after the [famous army regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Watch).[^cite_note-Begining-16] Since going blue in 1901, the team have been given the simple nickname "The Blues". Everton's attractive style of play led to [Steve Bloomer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Bloomer) calling the team "scientific" in 1928, which is thought to have inspired the nickname "The School of Science".[^cite_note-74] The battling 1995 FA Cup winning side were known collectively as "The Dogs of War". In 2002, when David Moyes arrived as manager, he proclaimed Everton "The People's Club", which has been adopted as a semi-official club nickname.[^cite_note-75]

## Stadiums

### Early grounds

Further information: [Stanley Park, Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Park,_Liverpool) and [Anfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anfield)

A black-and-white portrait photograph of a bearded man in a dark three-piece suit.

&#x20;[George Mahon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Mahon_\(accountant\)) arranged for Everton to move to Goodison Park.

Everton originally played in the southeast corner of [Stanley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Park,_Liverpool). The first official match took place in 1879. In 1882, a man named J. Cruitt donated land at Priory Road which became the club's home, though while the new ground was being prepared the club played at least one game at the ground at Walton Stiles (at the other end of City Road from what would become Goodison Park).[^cite_note-royden-76] Everton's first game there was played in November 1883, a 3-1 victory over Hartford St. John's; however the club's tenure there lasted just one season, before Cruitt gave them notice to quit due to concerns about noise and fan behaviour.[^cite_note-royden-76] In 1884, Everton became tenants at [Anfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anfield), which was owned by John Orrell, a land owner who was a friend of Everton member [John Houlding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Houlding). Orrell lent Anfield to the club in exchange for a small rent. Houlding purchased the land from Orrell in 1885 and effectively became Everton's landlord by charging the club rent, which increased from £100 to £240 a year by 1888 – and was still rising until Everton left the ground in 1892.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-Groom-78] The club regarded the increase in rent as unacceptable.[^cite_note-Groom-78] A further dispute between Houlding and the club's committee led to Houlding attempting to gain full control of the club by registering the company, "Everton F.C. and Athletic Grounds Ltd". Everton left Anfield for a new ground, [Goodison Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodison_Park), where the men's team played until the end of the 2024-25 season. Houlding attempted to take over Everton's name, colours, fixtures and league position, but was denied by [The Football Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_Association). Instead, Houlding formed a new club, [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.).[^cite_note-79]

### Goodison Park

Main article: [Goodison Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodison_Park)



Aerial image of [Goodison Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodison_Park). The stadium was used by Everton from 1892 until 2025.

Goodison Park, the first major football stadium to be built in England, was opened in 1892.[^cite_note-80] Goodison Park has staged more top-flight football games than any other ground in the United Kingdom and was the only English club ground to host a semi-final at the [1966 FIFA World Cup](61629.md). It was also the first English ground to have under-soil heating and the first to have two tiers on all sides. The church grounds of St Luke the Evangelist are adjacent to the corner of the Main Stand and the Howard Kendall Gwladys Street End.[^cite_note-81]

On match days, in a tradition going back to 1962, players walk out to the tune "[Johnny Todd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theme_from_Z-Cars)", played in the arrangement used when it was the theme song for *[Z-Cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z-Cars)*.[^cite_note-82] It is a traditional Liverpool children's song collected in 1890 by [Frank Kidson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Kidson) and tells the story of a sailor betrayed by his lover while away at sea.[^cite_note-83] On two occasions in 1994, the club walked out to different songs. In August 1994, the club played [2 Unlimited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2_Unlimited)'s song "[Get Ready For This](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get_Ready_For_This)". A month later, the club used a reworking of the [Creedence Clearwater Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creedence_Clearwater_Revival) classic "[Bad Moon Rising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bad_Moon_Rising_\(song\))". Both songs were met with complete disapproval by Everton fans.[^cite_note-84]

Starting from the 2025-26 season, Goodison Park will be home to [Everton Women](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everton_F.C._Women), as the men's team moved to the Everton Stadium. This will make it the largest dedicated women's football stadium in the UK.[^cite_note-85]

### Hill Dickinson Stadium

Main article: [Hill Dickinson Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_Dickinson_Stadium)



[Hill Dickinson Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_Dickinson_Stadium) under construction in April 2023

Talks regarding a new Everton stadium began in 1996. The original plan was for a 60,000-seat stadium, followed by a proposal for a 55,000-seat stadium at King's Dock, which was rejected due to funding issues.[^cite_note-86] In 2004, Everton entered talks with Liverpool about sharing a proposed stadium on Stanley Park, but negotiations broke down as Everton could not raise 50% of the costs.[^cite_note-87]

Everton secured planning permission for the new [Everton Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everton_Stadium) stadium in July 2021. Construction began in August 2021, with contractors Laing O'Rourke leading the project. The first phase involved infilling the dock with 500,000 cubic metres of sea-dredged sand and installing 2,500 vertical concrete piles. The stadium will have a capacity of 52,888 and is set to open for the start of the 2025–26 season, replacing Goodison Park, at an estimated cost of £760 million.[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] On 16 May 2025, the stadium's first title sponsor was announced as [Hill Dickinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_Dickinson), renaming the stadium to the Hill Dickinson Stadium.[^cite_note-90]

## Training grounds

Main article: [Finch Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finch_Farm)

From 1966 to 2007, Everton trained at [Bellefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellefield) in the [West Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Derby) area of Liverpool.[^cite_note-91] In 2007, the club moved to the [Finch Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finch_Farm) training complex in [Halewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halewood) after acquiring it the previous year. The first team squad officially moved to the complex on 9 October 2007. The training ground is now used by both the Everton men and women's first team and the [youth academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_system).[^cite_note-92] The training ground features ten full-size grass pitches across three slabs, one of which is a floodlit pitch along with a synthetic pitch with additional floodlights and dedicated training areas for fitness and goalkeeping training, as well as a recreated Goodison Park pitch. Within the training complex, there are changing facilities for senior players and academy players. Facilities include a fitness centre, a synthetic indoor training pitch, a hydrotherapy pool, a spa, a sauna, a physiotherapy room, a media centre and a video lounge, including a video editing suite.[^cite_note-93]

## Support

Main article: [Everton F.C. supporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Everton_F.C._supporters)



Everton supporters at Goodison Park in August 2022

Everton has a large fanbase, with the eighth-highest average attendance in the Premier League in the [2008–09](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_in_English_football) season.[^cite_note-94] The club's supporters are colloquially known as Evertonians or Blues.[^cite_note-\:0-95] The majority of Everton's matchday support comes from the [North West of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_of_England), primarily [Merseyside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merseyside), Cheshire, [West Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Lancashire) and parts of Western [Greater Manchester](79819.md) along with many fans who travel from [North Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Wales), Ireland, and Scotland. Within the city of Liverpool, support for Everton and city rivals Liverpool is not determined by geographical basis with supporters mixed across the city. Everton also has many supporters' clubs worldwide[^cite_note-96] in places such as North America,[^cite_note-97] Singapore,[^cite_note-98] Indonesia,[^cite_note-99] Lebanon, Malaysia,[^cite_note-100] Thailand, India,[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] and Australia.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] [Paul McCartney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_McCartney) is an Everton supporter.[^cite_note-105] The official supporters club is *FOREVERTON*,[^cite_note-106] and there are also several [fanzines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanzine) including *When Skies are Grey* and *Speke from the Harbour*, which are sold around Goodison Park on match days.

Everton regularly take large numbers away from home both domestically and in European fixtures. The club implements a loyalty points scheme offering the first opportunity to purchase away tickets to season ticket holders who have attended the most away matches. Everton often sell out the full allocation in away grounds, and tickets sell particularly well for [North West England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_England) away matches. In October 2009, Everton took 7,000 travelling fans to [Benfica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S.L._Benfica),[^cite_note-107] which was their largest ever away crowd in Europe since the [1985 European Cup Winners' Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup_Final).

### Rivalries

Main article: [Merseyside derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merseyside_derby)



Everton playing against rivals [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) in the [Merseyside derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merseyside_derby), in March 2006

Everton's biggest rivalry is with neighbours [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.), against whom the club contests the [Merseyside derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merseyside_derby). The rivalry stems from an internal dispute between Everton officials and the owners of Anfield, which was then Everton's home ground. The dispute resulted in Everton moving to Goodison Park and the subsequent formation of Liverpool in 1892. Following these events, a fierce rivalry has existed between Everton and Liverpool, albeit one that is generally perceived as more respectful than many other [derbies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_derby) in English football. This was illustrated by a chain of red and blue scarves that were linked between the gates of both grounds across Stanley Park as a tribute to the Liverpool fans killed in the [Hillsborough disaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillsborough_disaster).[^cite_note-108] The derby is usually a sellout fixture and has been known as the "friendly derby" because both sets of fans can often be seen side by side dressed in red and blue inside both [Anfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anfield) and Goodison Park.[^cite_note-109] On the field, matches have tended to be extremely stormy affairs; the derby has had more [red cards](3090014.md) than any other fixture in Premier League history.[^cite_note-110]

The last Merseyside derby at Goodison Park was played on 12 February 2025 and ended in a 2–2 draw.[^cite_note-111]

## Ownership and finances

Everton is a [limited company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limited_company) with the board of directors holding a majority of the shares.[^cite_note-112] The club's most recent accounts, from March 2026, show a net total debt of £159 million, with a turnover of £196.7 million and a loss of £8.6 million.[^cite_note-113] Everton agreed to a long-term loan of £30 million with [Bear Stearns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bear_Stearns) and [Prudential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prudential_plc) in 2002 for a duration of 25 years. The loan was a consolidation of debts at the time as well as a source of capital for new player acquisitions.[^cite_note-114] [Goodison Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodison_Park) is secured as collateral. On 27 February 2016, it was announced that [Farhad Moshiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farhad_Moshiri) would buy a 49.9% stake in the club.[^cite_note-115] On 15 September 2023, Everton announced that Miami-based [777 Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/777_Partners) had signed an agreement with Farhad Moshiri to acquire his full 94.1% stake in the club, pending ratification by the Premier League through the owners' and directors' test.[^cite_note-116] The takeover collapsed as it did not meet its deadline of 1 June 2024.[^cite_note-117] This deal collapsed on 19 July 2024 due to issues in regards to debts to 777 Partners and A-Cap Holdings[^cite_note-118]

On 21 June 2024, Everton announced an exclusivity agreement with the [Friedkin Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Friedkin), owners of [Serie A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serie_A) club [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.S._Roma).[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] On 23 September 2024, Everton and the Friedkin Group released a joint statement that an agreement on the terms of sale had been reached pending approval from the Premier League, the Football Association and the Financial Conduct Authority.[^cite_note-121] On 14 December, Friedkin officially received approval from the Premier League to acquire Everton, and five days later he officially acquired the club.[^cite_note-122]

In July 2025, the club's majority owners, The Friedkin Group, launched Pursuit Sports, a dedicated multi-club management vehicle to oversee their football properties, including Everton and [Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.S._Roma).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] Pursuit Sports is led by CEO Dave Beeston and is intended to provide strategic, data-driven support across the clubs while maintaining their individual identities.[^cite_note-125] In August 2025, Everton announced that they would be the first Premier League football club to commit to a living pension for all staff.[^cite_note-126]

### Financial Fair Play

In March 2023, the Premier League referred Everton to an independent commission to investigate alleged breaches of [Financial Fair Play](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_Fair_Play) from the 2021–2022 season.[^cite_note-139] The investigation concluded in November 2023 that Everton breached Financial Fair Play regulations after reporting a loss of £371.8m over three years, where under Premier League rules, clubs are allowed to lose a maximum of £105m over three years.[^cite_note-140] As a result, Everton were deducted 10 points, the heaviest punishment in Premier League history.[^cite_note-141] At the time, Everton were on 14 points after 12 games, sitting 14th in the league table; the points deduction dropped Everton to 19th, in the Premier League's relegation zone.[^cite_note-142] Everton said they were "both shocked and disappointed by the ruling of the Premier League's Commission" and had communicated their "intention to appeal the decision to the Premier League".[^cite_note-143] Liverpool [MP](3776360.md) [Ian Byrne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ian_Byrne) took the ten-point deduction to the [House of Commons](13828.md) calling for the suspension of any proceedings and sanctions, describing the deduction as "grossly unfair".[^cite_note-144]

The team was launched in the summer of 2007 as part of the club's Community programme and played their home games at the [Greenbank Sports Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenbank_Sports_Academy) in Liverpool's [Mossley Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mossley_Hill) suburb. The team was an amalgam of the [Toxteth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxteth) Tigers community youth programme, which started in 1968. The team quickly became one of the most successful in the league by winning the BBL Cup in 2009 and the play-offs in 2010. However, Everton withdrew funding before the 2010–11 season and the team was re-launched as the Mersey Tigers.[^cite_note-163]

Everton's owners also own [AS Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Roma).

## In popular culture

### Film and television

[Ken Loach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Loach)'s 1969 television film *The Golden Vision* combined improvised drama with documentary footage to tell the story of a group of Everton fans for whom the main purpose of life—following the team—is interrupted by such inconveniences as work and weddings. Everton forward [Alex Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alex_Young_\(footballer,_born_1937\)), whose nickname was also the title of the film, appeared as himself.[^cite_note-Golden_Vision-164]

[Paul Greengrass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Greengrass)'s 1997 television film *[The Fix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fix_\(1997_film\))* dramatised the true story of a match-fixing scandal in which the club's newest player [Tony Kay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Kay) (played by [Jason Isaacs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Isaacs)) is implicated in having helped to throw a match between his previous club [Sheffield Wednesday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Wednesday_F.C.) and [Ipswich Town](352843.md). The majority of the story is set during Everton's 1962–63 League Championship winning season, with manager [Harry Catterick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Catterick) played by [Colin Welland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Welland).[^cite_note-165]

In the 2015 *[Rocky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_\(franchise\))* film *[Creed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creed_\(film\))*, Goodison Park serves as the venue of the climactic fight scene. Footage of the stadium and crowd during a home game against [West Bromwich Albion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Bromwich_Albion_F.C.) was used for the scene. Liverpool-born boxing champion [Tony Bellew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Bellew), a lifelong Everton fan,[^cite_note-166] plays Creed's opponent and wore the Everton badge on his training gear and shorts.[^cite_note-167]

### Music

The club entered the UK singles chart on four occasions under different titles during the 1980s and 1990s, when many clubs each released a song to mark reaching the [FA Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Cup_Final). "The Boys in Blue", released in 1984, peaked at No. 82.[^cite_note-boys-in-blue-168] The following year, the club scored their biggest hit when "Here We Go" peaked at No. 14.[^cite_note-here-we-go-169]

In 1986, Everton released "Everybody's Cheering the Blues", which reached No. 83.[^cite_note-team-1986-170] "All Together Now", a reworking of a song by Liverpool band [The Farm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Farm_\(British_band\)), was released for the [1995 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_FA_Cup_final) and reached No. 27.[^cite_note-Everton-FC-Hits-171] By the time the club reached the [2009 FA Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_FA_Cup_final), the tradition had largely been abandoned by all clubs and no song was released.

In 2020, Everton released the single "Spirit of the Blues", which reached number 64 in the UK charts.[^cite_note-Everton-FC-Hits-171]

### Club captains

Since 1888, 48 players have held the position of club captain for Everton. The club's first captain was [Nick Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Ross_\(footballer,_born_1862\)), who captained Everton during the [1888-89 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1888-89_Football_League). The longest-serving captain is [Peter Farrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Farrell_\(Irish_footballer\)), who was club captain for nine years – from 1948 to 1957. Despite his long tenure, Peter Farrell never won a trophy at Everton. [Kevin Ratcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Ratcliffe), who captained Everton during their most successful period in history, is Everton's most decorated captain. He won seven trophies as captain, including two First Division titles, four Charity Shields, and one Cup Winners' Cup. Everton's most recent captain was [Séamus Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A9amus_Coleman). He had been captain since [Phil Jagielka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Jagielka) left the club in 2019.[^cite_note-173]

### Notable managers

Further information: [List of Everton F.C. managers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Everton_F.C._managers)

The club's current manager is [David Moyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Moyes) who returned for his second spell at the club taking over from [Sean Dyche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Dyche) in January 2025.[^cite_note-178] There have also been four caretaker managers, and before 1939 the team was selected by either the club secretary or by committee. The club's longest-serving manager has been [Harry Catterick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Catterick), who was in charge of the team from 1961 to 1973 for 594 first team matches.[^cite_note-\:1-179] The Everton manager to win the most domestic and international trophies is [Howard Kendall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Kendall), who won two [First Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_League_First_Division) championships, the [1984 FA Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_FA_Cup_Final), the [1985 UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup_Final), and three [FA Charity Shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FA_Community_Shield).[^cite_note-180]

## Records and statistics

Further information: [List of Everton F.C. records and statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Everton_F.C._records_and_statistics)



Goalkeeper [Neville Southall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neville_Southall) made a record 751 first-team appearances for Everton between 1981 and 1997.

[Neville Southall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neville_Southall) holds the record for the most Everton appearances with 751 first-team matches between 1981 and 1997. The late centre half and former captain [Brian Labone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Labone) comes in second with 534 matches. The longest serving player is goalkeeper [Ted Sagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ted_Sagar), who played for 23 years between 1929 and 1953. This tenure covered both sides of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) and included a total of 495 appearances. Southall also previously held the record for the most league clean sheets during a season with 15. However, this record was beaten during the [2008–09 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_Premier_League) by American goalkeeper [Tim Howard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tim_Howard), who ended the season with 17 clean sheets.[^cite_note-181] The club's top goalscorer, with 383 goals in all competitions, is [Dixie Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dixie_Dean); the second-highest goalscorer is [Graeme Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graeme_Sharp) with 159. Dean still holds the [English national record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_football_records) of most goals in a season with 60.[^cite_note-records-182]



Finishing positions in the top flight between 1955 and 2011

The record attendance for an Everton home match is 78,299 against Liverpool on 18 September 1948. Remarkably, there was only one injury at this game, which occurred when Tom Fleetwood was hit on the head by a coin thrown from the crowd whilst he marched around the perimeter and played the [cornet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornet) with St Edward's Orphanage Band. Goodison Park, like all major English football grounds since the recommendations of the [Taylor Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taylor_Report) were implemented, is now an all-seater and only holds just under 40,000, meaning it is unlikely that this attendance record will ever be broken at Goodison.[^cite_note-records-182] Everton's record transfer paid was to Swansea City for the [Icelandic](14531.md) midfielder [Gylfi Sigurðsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gylfi_Sigur%C3%B0sson) for a sum of £45m in 2017.[^cite_note-183] The sale of [Romelu Lukaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romelu_Lukaku) to [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) the same year was for an initial sum of £75m, the largest sum Everton has received for a player and then the largest transfer between two English clubs.[^cite_note-184]

Everton hold the record for the most seasons in England's top tier (Division One/Premier League), with 122 seasons out of 126, as of completion of the 2024–25 season (the club played in Division 2 in 1930–31 and from 1951 to 1954). They are one of six teams to have played in every season of the Premier League since its inception in August 1992 – the others being [Arsenal](2174.md), [Chelsea](7473.md), [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.), Manchester United, and [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.). Everton against Aston Villa is the most played fixture in England's top flight. As of the 2024–25 season, the two founding members of the Football League have played a record 214 league games.[^cite_note-185]
