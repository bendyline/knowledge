# AI data center

An **AI data center** is a specialized [data center](579730.md) facility designed for the computationally intensive tasks of training and running [artificial intelligence](1164.md) (AI) and [machine learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_learning) models. Unlike general-purpose data centers, they are often optimized for the parallel processing demands of AI workloads, typically using hardware such as [AI accelerators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_accelerator) ([GPUs](390214.md), [TPUs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_Processing_Unit)) and high-speed [interconnects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interconnect_\(integrated_circuits\)).

The global push to construct these specialized facilities accelerated dramatically during the [AI boom](73291899.md) of the 2020s. In 2026, major tech companies were estimated to spend \$650 billion on AI data centers.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] Their purchases of [memory chips](1313007.md), particularly [High Bandwidth Memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Bandwidth_Memory), led to a [global memory supply shortage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932026_global_memory_supply_shortage) amid a broader competition for semiconductors, power, and infrastructure.[^cite_note-reuters-ai-frenzy-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] About 70% of global [computer memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computer_memory) production in the 2026 [fiscal year](5752099.md) has been purchased for AI data centers.[^cite_note-7]

AI-focused data centers use around 60 kilowatts of electricity per [server rack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Server_rack), while general-purpose data centers typically use around 10 kilowatts of electricity per rack.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-Leppert-9][^cite_note-10]

By 2026, many U.S. communities had [organized against](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_AI_data_centers) the local construction of AI data centers, which have a large power and water footprint and generate heat, noise, and other pollutants. By July 2026, local community resistance had blocked the construction of AI data centers worth some \$130 billion.[^cite_note-11]

## Operators

As of August 2025, [The Information](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Information_\(website\)) tracked 18 planned or existing AI data centers in the United States, operated by [Amazon Web Services](1691376.md), [CoreWeave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CoreWeave), Crusoe, [Meta](62420226.md), [Microsoft](19001.md)/[OpenAI](48795986.md), [Oracle](22591.md), [Tesla](5533631.md), and [xAI](74330685.md).[^cite_note-12] Other AI data center operators include [Digital Realty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_Realty) and [Alibaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alibaba_Group).[^cite_note-Booth-13] Data centers are also being built in China, India, Europe,[^cite_note-Booth-13] Saudi Arabia,[^cite_note-14] and Canada.[^cite_note-15] *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker)* described CoreWeave as the most prominent AI data center operator in the United States.[^cite_note-16]

Two types of data center providers for machine learning have been noted in [news media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media): hyperscalers and neoclouds. *[The Verge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Verge)* listed large technology companies such as [Google](1092923.md), Meta, Microsoft, Oracle and Amazon as hyperscalers.[^cite_note-Lopatto-17] *[The New York Times](30680.md)* described neoclouds as "a new generation of data center providers". CoreWeave, Nebius, [Nscale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NScale), and Lambda have been described as examples of neoclouds.[^cite_note-Weise-18]

In January 2025, OpenAI, in partnership with Oracle and [Softbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SoftBank_Group), announced the [Stargate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_LLC) project, which as of September 2025 is composed of six built or proposed AI data centers in the United States.[^cite_note-19]

In response to the Stargate project, [Amazon](90451.md) launched in October 2025 an AI data center on 1,200 acres of farmland in Indiana. This data center, known as Project Rainier, is one of the largest AI data centers in the world, with Amazon spending \$11 billion on the project. Rainier is specifically intended for training and running machine learning models from [Anthropic](6206236.md).[^cite_note-20] As of that time, this facility contains seven data centers (out of an estimated 30 planned) and will use 2.2 gigawatts of electricity (equivalent to 1 million households) and millions of gallons of water per year. Computer chips from [Annapurna Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annapurna_Labs) and Anthropic, Trainium 2, were designed for use in such facilities. Amazon pumped millions of gallons of water out of the ground to construct the data center, and as of June 2025, Indiana state officials are investigating whether this [dewatering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewatering) process led to dry wells for local residents.[^cite_note-21]

In November 2025, Anthropic announced a partnership with Fluidstack to develop [artificial intelligence infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_infrastructure) in the United States, including data centers in New York and Texas, worth \$50 billion.[^cite_note-22]

Other AI data center projects include the [Colossus supercomputer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_\(supercomputer\)) from xAI, a Louisiana-based project from Meta, Hyperion, expected to use 5 GW of power, and a second Ohio-based Meta project, Prometheus, with a capacity of 1 GW.[^cite_note-23] A 3,200-acre AI data center, capable of 4.4-4.5 GW of power and located on the decommissioned [Homer City Generating Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homer_City_Generating_Station), is under construction as of 2025, and will use seven 30-acre gas generating stations supplied by [EQT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EQT_Corporation).[^cite_note-Halper-24][^cite_note-25]

As of December 2025, [CRH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CRH_plc) is working on over 100 data centers in the United States.[^cite_note-Berg-26]

In 2025, [ExxonMobil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExxonMobil) and [NextEra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NextEra_Energy) announced plans to build a data center powered by [natural gas](22131.md) and using [carbon capture technology](3679268.md), with 1.2 GW of power capacity. They previously purchased 2,500 acres of land in the [Southeastern United States](816925.md) and plan to market the data center to an artificial intelligence company.[^cite_note-27]

The increased interest in AI data centers has led to several executives from companies in that space becoming billionaires, including CoreWeave, [QTS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quality_Technology_Services), [Nebius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nebius_Group), [Astera Labs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astera_Labs), [Groq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Groq), Fermi (which is connected to former United States Secretary of Energy [Rick Perry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Perry)), [Snowflake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowflake_Inc.) and Cipher Mining.[^cite_note-28]

Several companies involved in [cryptocurrency mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryptocurrency_mining), such as [Bitdeer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bitdeer), CoreWeave,[^cite_note-Nicoletti-29] Cipher Mining, [TeraWulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TeraWulf),[^cite_note-30] IREN, Core Scientific, and CleanSpark[^cite_note-31] have also been involved with AI data centers.

## Finances



Between January and August 2024, Microsoft, Meta, Google and Amazon collectively spent \$125 billion on AI data centers.[^cite_note-32] [Citigroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citigroup) forecasted that \$2.8 trillion would be spent on AI data centers by 2030,[^cite_note-Booth-13] while [McKinsey and Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McKinsey_and_Company) estimated that almost \$7 trillion would be spent globally by that time.[^cite_note-Kerr-33] According to [S\&P Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S\&P_Global), \$61 billion has been spent on the data center market as a whole in 2025, while debt issuance for data centers was \$182 billion during the same year.[^cite_note-34]

Large technology companies have offloaded the financial risks of building AI data centers by setting up [special purpose vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special-purpose_entity) or by contracting with neoclouds. For example, Meta's Hyperion was mostly funded by [Blue Owl Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Owl_Capital), which did so using a bond offering from [PIMCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PIMCO). Those bonds were sold to a number of clients, including [BlackRock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlackRock). Meta did not borrow money itself and instead established a special purpose vehicle from which it would rent the data center.[^cite_note-Weise-18] This deal was structured by [Morgan Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morgan_Stanley) for \$30 billion, the largest known private capital transaction as of 2025.[^cite_note-Nicoletti-29]

Neoclouds such as CoreWeave have gone into debt to buy computer chips from Nvidia for their data centers, and the chips themselves have been used for loan collateral. As of December 2025, CoreWeave took out three GPU-backed loans, collectively worth \$12.4 billion, from private credit firms ([Blackstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackstone_Inc.), [Coatue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coatue_Management), [BlackRock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlackRock), [PIMCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PIMCO)) and from banks ([Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs), [JPMorgan Chase](231001.md), [Wells Fargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wells_Fargo)). Thus, these companies provide an indirect connection between private credit and established banks. Data centers have also established [asset-backed securities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset-backed_securities), and debt for data centers has its own [derivative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derivative_\(finance\)) [financial products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_services).[^cite_note-Lopatto-17]

The [real estate industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate_business), including [asset managers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asset_management), [public companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_company) and [private investors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_investors), has also invested in AI development.[^cite_note-35]

## Energy sourcing

Fueled by growth in artificial intelligence, data centers' demand for power increased in the 2020s.

[^cite_note-Data_center_WashPost_20251225-36]



Share of total electricity demand used by data centers (2025)



[Google Data Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Data_Centers), [The Dalles, Oregon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dalles,_Oregon)

Energy consumption and the environmental impacts it creates are a central issue for data centers.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] Power draw ranges from a few kilowatts (kW) for small server racks to several tens of megawatts (MW) for large facilities. Modern hyperscale data centers can exhibit power densities exceeding 100 times those of conventional office buildings, primarily due to the high concentration of servers and cooling systems required to manage continuous digital workloads.[^cite_note-39] For higher power density facilities, electricity costs are a dominant [operating expense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_expense) and account for over 10% of the [total cost of ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Total_cost_of_ownership) (TCO) of a data center.[^cite_note-40]

As of 2024, data centers in the U.S. are primarily powered by natural gas, which supplies 40% of their electricity (with [renewable energy](25784.md) at 24%, nuclear at about 20%, and coal at about 15%).[^cite_note-Data_center_\:03-41] The *[Associated Press](18935732.md)* reported that electricity for AI data centers in the U.S. would likely come from natural gas or oil, as companies prefer using currently available power plants, which primarily use fossil fuels. Fossil energy is also often cheaper in locations where data centers are developed, and experts believe that energy demands from [generative AI](73291755.md) and data centers would be difficult to fulfill with renewable energy alone. Some companies such as Google, Amazon, and Meta have expressed interest in [nuclear power](22153.md) for their data centers.[^cite_note-Data_center_\:7-42] As of 2020, according to the [IEA](285292.md), solar [photovoltaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaics)-generated electricity is at its lowest cost in history.[^cite_note-43] Other data centers, including xAI's [Colossus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colossus_\(supercomputer\)), OpenAI's [Stargate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_LLC), and Meta's Prometheus use their own off-grid [natural gas plants](12438990.md).[^cite_note-Data_center_\:32-44][^cite_note-Data_center_\:8-45] [Electric vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_vehicle_battery) and [lithium-ion batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) have also been used for powering data centers, including for Colossus.[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-Data_center_\:21-47]

[Power utility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_utility) companies upgrade their infrastructure to handle the demands of new data centers, and the cost of these changes typically falls on residential or smaller commercial consumers.[^cite_note-Data_center_\:03-41][^cite_note-Data_center_\:7-42][^cite_note-Data_center_\:10-48] In 2025, the [Mountain Valley Pipeline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Valley_Pipeline) announced plans to expand its capacity by 25% to meet energy needs for data centers.[^cite_note-Data_center_\:6-49]

In December 2025, the [Federal Energy Regulatory Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Energy_Regulatory_Commission) (FERC) issued a unanimous order allowing data centers in the U.S. to connect directly to [power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_plants).[^cite_note-50] United States Secretary of Energy [Chris Wright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Wright) expressed support for un-retiring coal plants to power AI data centers.[^cite_note-51] Electricity demands from AI data centers have slowed or reversed the retirement of [peaking power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peaking_power_plant) in the U.S.[^cite_note-Data_center_\:22-52] For example, in 2025, [Southern Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Company) announced that energy use from data centers would prevent the company from retiring coal-fired power plants as it had earlier promised.[^cite_note-Data_center_\:6-49] In [Nevada](21216.md), the [Desert Research Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Research_Institute) (DRI) calculated that 35% of the state's energy production could go to data centers by the year 2030 if all projects planned in the state as of 2026 are completed.[^cite_note-Data_center_\:15-53] DRI also stated, in areas where there are clusters of data centers, local consumers may end up paying the extra cost of expanding infrastructure.[^cite_note-Data_center_\:15-53]

## Environmental footprint

See also: [Environmental impact of artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_impact_of_artificial_intelligence)

Average AI data centers have an electricity footprint equivalent to 100,000 households, and use billions of gallons of water for cooling their hardware.[^cite_note-54] In 2025, the [International Energy Agency](285292.md) estimated that the larger AI data centers currently under construction could consume as much electricity as 2 million households.[^cite_note-Leppert-9] A 2024 report from the [United States Department of Energy](62866.md) stated that data centers overall used 17 billion gallons of water per year in the United States, primarily due to "rapid proliferation of AI servers", and that this usage was forecasted to grow to nearly 80 billion gallons by 2028.[^cite_note-Kerr-33] Researchers estimated that AI data centers in the United States would emit 24–44 million metric tons of carbon dioxide and use 731–1,125 million cubic meters of water per year between 2024 and 2030.[^cite_note-55]

Peaking power plants, which have been proposed as a power source for AI data centers, emit [sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) and have historically been located disproportionately near communities of color in the United States.[^cite_note-56]

Reciprocating internal combustion engines, proposed as another power source for a data center, emit [PM 2.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate_matter), [nitrogen oxides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrogen_oxides), and [volatile organic compounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volatile_organic_compound).[^cite_note-57]

## Global memory supply shortage

Main article: [2024–2026 global memory supply shortage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%932026_global_memory_supply_shortage)

The construction of AI data centers during the AI boom starting in 2024 has exhausted the global supply of the computer memory. To meet demand from AI servers, the three major DRAM manufacturers, [Samsung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsung), [SK Hynix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Hynix), and [Micron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micron_Technology), prioritized production of [High Bandwidth Memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Bandwidth_Memory) (HBM) and high-end server DRAM, contributing to a global memory supply shortage and intensifying competition for advanced memory chips.[^cite_note-reuters-ai-frenzy-3] Because HBM consumes far more fabrication capacity than conventional memory—producing a single bit of HBM requires roughly three times the wafer capacity of DDR5.[^cite_note-58]

The resulting price increases and shortages have affected computer memory broadly, including HBM, [DRAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_random-access_memory), and [NAND flash memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAND_flash_memory).[^cite_note-reuters-ai-frenzy-3][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

Under an agreement with Samsung and SK Hynix, the [Stargate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stargate_LLC) project alone is set to receive 900,000 DRAM wafers per month, roughly 40% of total global DRAM production.[^cite_note-61]

## In the United States



Data center infrastructure in the United States (2025)

In the United States, both the [Biden administration](65732174.md) and [second Trump administration](78298108.md) supported the construction of AI data centers. In January 2025, then-president Joe Biden signed an executive order for federal government agencies to support AI data centers on federal sites built by private companies, study their effect on energy prices, and encourage their use of renewable energy.[^cite_note-62] In April 2025, the [United States Department of Energy](62866.md) suggested 16 possible sites, including [Los Alamos National Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alamos_National_Laboratory), [Sandia National Laboratories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandia_National_Laboratories) and [Oak Ridge National Laboratory](38147.md).[^cite_note-63] In its July 2025 AI Action Plan, the second Trump administration supported increased production of AI data centers.[^cite_note-64]

Several US states have incentivized local data center construction.[^cite_note-65] For example, in 2024, lawmakers in Michigan approved tax breaks for data center equipment and construction material.[^cite_note-Larson-66] Some data center companies have also invested or promised to invest in the infrastructure of local communities.[^cite_note-Dellinger-67][^cite_note-68]

In December 2025, [Democratic](5043544.md) senators [Elizabeth Warren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Warren), [Chris Van Hollen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Van_Hollen), and [Richard Blumenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Blumenthal) wrote to seven technology companies (Google, Microsoft, Amazon, Meta, CoreWeave, Digital Realty, and [Equinix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equinix)) that they will investigate the effects of those companies' operations on consumer energy bills, highlighting AI data centers in particular.[^cite_note-69] That same month, 25 Democratic lawmakers wrote to the inspector general of the [United States Department of Commerce](70243.md) over possible [conflict of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest) concerns involving secretary of commerce [Howard Lutnick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Lutnick) and AI data centers. Lutnick met with a co-founder of Fermi, which proposed an AI data center project financed by [Cantor Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantor_Fitzgerald) and [Newmark Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newmark_Group).[^cite_note-70]

AI Infrastructure Coalition (AIIC), an organization led by Brian O. Walsh, [Kyrsten Sinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyrsten_Sinema), and [Garret Graves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garret_Graves), has supported Trump's AI Action Plan,[^cite_note-Allen-71] with Sinema becoming personally involved in a local meeting on an AI data center in Arizona.[^cite_note-Plautz-72] Venture capital firm [Andreessen Horowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreessen_Horowitz), Big Tech companies (Google, Meta, Microsoft), telecommunications companies ([Cisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cisco), [Corning Inc.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corning_Inc.), [Lumen Technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumen_Technologies)), data center providers (Digital Realty, QTS), energy companies ([Duke Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Energy), [Entergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entergy), ExxonMobil, NextEra Energy), and utility companies ([PG\&E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Gas_and_Electric_Company), [Pinnacle West Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinnacle_West_Capital)) are members of AIIC as of November 2025.[^cite_note-Allen-71] AIIC has been supported by [Hogan Lovells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogan_Lovells).[^cite_note-Plautz-72]

According to [Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs) in 2025 an addition half million workers will be needed by 2030 to build the power infrastructure, which is acute because of AI data center build out.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

### Concerns and opposition

Main article: [Opposition to AI data centers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opposition_to_AI_data_centers)

Some analysts have expressed concerns about overbuilding of AI data centers, warning that their infrastructure risks [obsolescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technological_obsolescence) due to changes in demand and technology.[^cite_note-Berg-26] Estimates for the lifespan of the GPUs used to power data centers range from one to eight years.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] By contrast, CPUs in more traditional data centers have a shelf life of about 5–7 years. Machine learning model training causes significant stress on computer chips.[^cite_note-78]



Anti-data-center sentiment in rural [Kansas](16716.md) neighborhood

Advocates have also linked data center construction to the [AI bubble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AI_bubble).[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] [Wisconsin Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wisconsin_Center_for_Investigative_Journalism) expressed concern that data centers could become [stranded assets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranded_assets) in the context of an AI bubble, leaving energy customers to pay for the costs.[^cite_note-81] Journalists have warned that the large number of links between actors and organizations in the AI ecosystem[^cite_note-Lopatto-17] as well as the large number of companies involved[^cite_note-Nicoletti-29] each indicate strong risks for the [global economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_economy) and [financial markets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_market).

A hedge fund founder questioned the economics of AI data centers, concluding that they need about \$1 trillion of revenue for profitability.[^cite_note-82]

In November 2025, the [North American Electric Reliability Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Electric_Reliability_Corporation) warned that building new data centers could negatively affect the [electrical grid](20344155.md) and cause power outages during [extreme weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_weather).[^cite_note-FT-83] The independent monitor of [PJM Interconnection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PJM_Interconnection) warned that its [power grid](20344155.md) cannot support new data centers and supported a federal moratorium on data centers.[^cite_note-Halper-24]

Machine learning developers, when [training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Model_training) [large language models](73248112.md), often use data centers at their full capacity, conflicting with other users (including households) during peak usage, which may lead to [blackouts](296636.md).[^cite_note-FT-83]

Power disruptions in data centers can cause errors in machine learning model calculations ("silent data corruption").[^cite_note-84]

Large technology companies who are building data centers have asked public officials and land owners to sign [non-disclosure agreements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-disclosure_agreement) and have appeared to use [shell companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_corporation).[^cite_note-85] In one case, when a joint OpenAI-Oracle data center was rejected by [Saline Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saline_Township,_Michigan) in Michigan, landowners and developers responded by suing the town.[^cite_note-Larson-66][^cite_note-86] The developers acquired the land from the resulting settlement. The [Michigan Public Service Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_Public_Service_Commission) approved [DTE Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DTE_Energy) to provide power to the data center, part of Stargate. The approval was part of an [ex parte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ex_parte) motion, with no public input.[^cite_note-Dellinger-67]

In 2025, over 230 groups have signed a letter supporting a moratorium on constructing AI data centers in the United States, concerned by impact on the environment and energy bills. Signatories include [Food & Water Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_&_Water_Watch), [Greenpeace](12233.md), [Friends of the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends_of_the_Earth) and [Physicians for Social Responsibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physicians_for_Social_Responsibility).[^cite_note-87] Senator [Bernie Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernie_Sanders) has also supported a moratorium on AI data centers.[^cite_note-88]

Ashley LaMont, of [Honor the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honor_the_Earth), argued that data centers on tribal lands would not help Native Americans obtain [data sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Data_sovereignty).[^cite_note-89]

The [NAACP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAACP) has also expressed opposition to AI data centers.[^cite_note-90]

## In space

See also: [Space-based data center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-based_data_center)

Several tech companies, including [Starcloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starcloud), Google, Nvidia, [Blue Origin](887418.md) and [SpaceX](832774.md), have announced projects for or otherwise expressed interest in building data centers in outer space.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] Google announced Project Suncatcher, which plans to test whether its [Tensor Processing Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensor_Processing_Unit) (TPU) chips (which are specifically designed for machine learning) can function in the environment of space. The company aims to deploy satellites with these chips in a data center by 2027.[^cite_note-95] In November 2025, Starcloud, a startup supported by Nvidia, launched a satellite with an [Nvidia H100 GPU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopper_\(microarchitecture\)) that the company used to deploy and develop a large language model. [CNBC](223441.md) reported that this model, NanoGPT, was the first model trained in space. Starcloud deployed a second model based on Google's [Gemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemma_\(language_model\)).[^cite_note-96]
