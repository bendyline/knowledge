The **2026 Berlin state election** was held on 20 September 2026 to elect the 20th [Abgeordnetenhaus of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abgeordnetenhaus_of_Berlin). The [2026 Mecklenburg-Vorpommern state election](70944924.md) took place on the same day, two weeks after the [2026 Saxony-Anhalt state election](70945225.md).&#x20;

Due to numerous irregularities in the [2021 Berlin state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Berlin_state_election), it was ruled invalid and [repeated in 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Berlin_state_election) in which members were elected only for the remainder of the term. [Kai Wegner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Wegner) has served as [Governing Mayor of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governing_Mayor_of_Berlin) since April 2023, leading the CDU-SPD [Wegner senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wegner_senate), but has received criticism and was not seeking re-election as mayor.

The parties of the outgoing government—the [Christian Democratic Union](60584.md) (CDU) and the [Social Democratic Party](60585.md) (SPD)—as well as the [Greens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alliance_90/The_Greens), lost about a third of their support. Meanwhile, [The Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Die_Linke) and the [Alternative for Germany](38708229.md) (AfD) doubled their share, and the [Sahra Wagenknecht Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahra_Wagenknecht_Alliance) (BSW) failed to secure any seats.

The Left became the largest party in a state for the second time, after the [2019 Thuringian state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Thuringian_state_election). Since the AfD is subject to the [German firewall policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firewall_against_the_far-right_in_Germany), and the CDU also rejects The Left, three other parties need to cooperate to form a coalition. Both the SPD and the Greens have signalled a willingness to join a coalition with The Left.[^cite_note-1] This was the first election in Berlin since [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Berlin_state_election) where the SPD or CDU did not finish first.

## Election date

The Berlin parliament did not decide to dissolve itself. The date of the election for the 20th Abgeordnetenhaus of Berlin was set to 20 September 2026. The Berlin Constitution stipulates that a regular election must occur no earlier than 56 months and no later than 59 months after the start of the Abgeordnetenhaus's legislative period.[^cite_note-2] The 19th Abgeordnetenhaus of Berlin was constituted on November 4, 2021.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4]

## Electoral system

The Abgeordnetenhaus is elected via [mixed-member proportional representation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed-member_proportional_representation). 78 members are elected in [single-member constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-member_constituencies) via [first-past-the-post voting](261709.md). 52 members are then allocated using compensatory proportional representation, distributed based on each party's statewide voteshare. Parties can submit either a single statewide list, or one in each of Berlin's twelve [boroughs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boroughs_and_neighborhoods_of_Berlin). Voters have two votes: the "first vote" for candidates in single-member constituencies, and the "second vote" for [party lists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_list), which are used to fill the proportional seats. The statutory size of the Abgeordnetenhaus is 130 members, but if [overhang seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhang_seat) are present, proportional [leveling seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leveling_seat) will be added to ensure proportionality. An [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) of 5% of valid votes is applied to the Abgeordnetenhaus; parties that fall below this threshold are excluded from the Abgeordnetenhaus – except if they win at least one single-member constituency, in which case they are exempt from said threshold and will be eligible for seats.[^cite_note-Wahlen-5]

## Background



Results of Berlin state elections. Since [turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout) dropped under 70% [in 1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Berlin_state_election), abstentions (*Nichtwähler*) have a much higher share than any party.

In the [2023 Berlin repeat state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Berlin_state_election) with 28% of votes, the opposition [Christian Democratic Union](60584.md) (CDU) grew by over ten [percentage points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percentage_points) and emerged as the largest party by a wide margin, the first time it had done so since the [1999 Berlin state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Berlin_state_election). All three governing parties declined; the SPD suffered its worst result in over a century with 18.4%, and only barely remained ahead of the Greens by a margin of 53 votes. The Left also slipped to 12%. The [Alternative for Germany](38708229.md) (AfD) recorded a small upswing to 9%, while the [Free Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Democratic_Party_\(Germany\)) (FDP) fell to 4.6% and lost all their seats. Overall, the incumbent government retained a reduced majority. The CDU claimed a mandate to govern given its first-place result, while mayor [Franziska Giffey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franziska_Giffey) committed to remaining in government. The Left called for a renewal of the outgoing coalition.[^cite_note-6] After various talks between parties, the SPD and CDU voted at the beginning of March to begin negotiations for a [grand coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_coalition_\(Germany\)). CDU leader [Kai Wegner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Wegner) was approved as mayor on 27 April after three rounds of voting.[^cite_note-Board-7][^cite_note-\:0-8]

## Campaign

### Wegner replacement

Wegner received heavy criticism for his lack of presence and poor communicative response to the 3 January [2026 arson attack on the Berlin power grid](82066081.md), especially after it was revealed he spent part of the day playing tennis with his partner [Katharina Günther-Wünsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katharina_G%C3%BCnther-W%C3%BCnsch) instead of handling the crisis. The scandal caused tension inside the coalition and opposition parties accused him of lying about his whereabouts that day. *[Der Tagesspiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Tagesspiegel)* reported on 7 July, citing the Senate Chancellery, that Wegner had not made any official phone calls related to the blackout until 12\:45 p.m., nearly seven hours after the attack; this contradicted his previous public statement that he first began coordinating a response around 8 a.m. After this, internal anger from his party intensified, and on 10 July Wegner stepped down as the CDU's lead candidate for this election. He refused to resign from office and remained the CDU's constituency candidate in his district of Spandau 5. However, he ruled out serving in the next Senate if the CDU end up in government.[^cite_note-9] On the evening of 10 July, Finance Senator [Stefan Evers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stefan_Evers) was named as Wegner's replacement.[^cite_note-10]

### Police investigation into Krach

On 9 September, the [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover) public prosecutor's office executed three search warrants against properties related to SPD lead candidate [Steffen Krach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steffen_Krach); police searched his apartment in Berlin, as well as SPD offices in Berlin and the [Hanover Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover_Region) government offices. Though the office declined to comment on an ongoing investigation, reporting from *[Der Tagesspiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Tagesspiegel)* indicated it was on suspicion of [bribery](260979.md).[^cite_note-ts-11]

Krach told [Rundfunk Berlin-Brandenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rundfunk_Berlin-Brandenburg) in an interview that evening that he suspected this was the basis of the investigation. Krach, in his capacity of President of the Hanover Region from 2021 through the beginning of 2026, was chair of the supervisory board of its municipal hospitals and oversaw the sale of one of them to a private medical provider. He claimed that one of the two bidders – which he said was the one which lost – made a donation of €9,900 to the Berlin SPD during the process, just under the €10,000 threshold which requires the party to disclose the donor's identity. At this time (December 2025), Krach had been nominated as the Berlin lead candidate but had not yet left his Hanover post. Krach called the implication of accepting a bribe "completely baseless", said he immediately recognized the donation as unacceptable and returned it within 24 hours, and denied ever taking improper donations in a personal or professional capacity.[^cite_note-rbb1-12] As donations are normally handled only by party financial staff, he said he had specially instructed them to alert him to any coming from Hanover to avoid the appearance of impropriety, and later abstained from the board's decisive vote. The prosecutor's office had searched the provider's offices in May 2026 in a relation to an unspecified "corruption offense", and Krach said awareness of a previous investigation into the provider ultimately swayed the board's decision.[^cite_note-ts-11][^cite_note-rbb1-12]

The Berlin state executive of the SPD held a crisis meeting on the afternoon of 9 September and pronounced their full confidence in Krach as lead candidate.[^cite_note-ts-11] Reporting from RBB later brought Krach's timeline into question: it obtained copies of the relevant receipts and communications which showed the donation was made on 29 December 2025 and returned on 9 January 2026. Krach was only told about the donation on 7 January. However, the Berlin party treasurer backed up his claim that he instructed the donation be returned immediately.[^cite_note-rbb1-12][^cite_note-rbb2-13] Krach stated his intention to continue campaigning in a press conference on 10 September, and confirmed he had support from the SPD parliamentary group, state executive, and federal leader and Vice Chancellor [Lars Klingbeil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lars_Klingbeil).[^cite_note-rbb2-13]

In the evening of 10 September, the Hanover public prosecutor's office spokeswoman confirmed to *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel)* that there were three active investigations into Krach: two for *Vorteilsannahme* – "acceptance of benefits", accepting a bribe but without a violation of duty to the public, which is the more severe *Bestechlichkeit* (corruption) – and one for entering into possible anti-competitive agreements in conjunction with the misappropriation of [trade secrets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_secrets). The spokeswoman said the investigations had started on 3 September and the search warrants had been issued on 7 September.[^cite_note-14] In light of this, *Der Tagesspiegel* reported that the municipal supervisory authority of [Lower Saxony](18435.md) – Krach is still technically in office as president of the Hanover region on unpaid leave – was evaluating opening disciplinary proceedings against him.[^cite_note-15]

### Polls and potential coalitions

In the two months before the election, the CDU, AfD, and The Left were all shown as the largest party in at least one poll. Recent election winners were also quite different: SPD had won the invalid [2021 Berlin state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Berlin_state_election), CDU the repeat [2023 Berlin state election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Berlin_state_election), the Greens the [local 2024 European Parliament elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_Germany#Results_by_state), and The Left prevailed in the [local 2025 German federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_German_federal_election#Results_by_state).

Polls in August suggested that five parties are in the 15% to 20% range, making a continuation of the CDU–SPD grand coalition or any other two-party coalition mathematically impossible. They predict a CDU–SPD–Green [Kenya coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-red-green_coalition) could have a majority, but Green lead candidate [Werner Graf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Graf) has ruled out working with the CDU.[^cite_note-16] A Left–Green–SPD coalition is thus the most likely coalition to command a majority. The same coalition, but led by the SPD ([red–red–green coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red%E2%80%93red%E2%80%93green_coalition)), governed Berlin from 2016 through 2023.[^cite_note-17] The AfD is subject to a [firewall policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firewall_against_the_far-right_in_Germany) as all other major parties refuse to work with it, and no other parties are predicted to pass the five-percent election threshold. The federal CDU has also passed a "resolution of incompatibility" prohibiting it or any lower-level party organ from working with The Left.

Left lead candidate [Elif Eralp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elif_Eralp) has publicly stated a [red line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_line_\(phrase\)) condition of entering any coalition is beginning the nationalisation (with reducing compensation) of housing properties owned by large companies, which voters approved by a large margin in the non-binding 2021 [Deutsche Wohnen & Co. enteignen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutsche_Wohnen_&_Co._enteignen) referendum. (Article 15 of the [Basic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law_of_the_Federal_Republic_of_Germany) empowers the government to pass a law allowing it to take over land, natural resources, and means of production for the purposes of [socialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialization_\(economics\)) with equitable compensation to the owner, though it has never been used before.[^cite_note-18]) Krach responded that he would not agree to this condition and accused The Left of "drawing a red line here because they do not even want to govern and instead continue as maximum opposition". For his part, Graf said his party felt bound by the referendum and called on the two red parties to settle their differences and "draw green lines" instead.[^cite_note-19]

## Aftermath

### Spandau 1 result error

On 2 October, while verifying the result, electoral authorities announced there had been an error in the Spandau 1 constituency. Upon a recount the constituency seat went from the CDU to AfD, which as it was no longer an [overhang seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overhang_seat) had the effect of removing four [leveling seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leveling_seat) (one each for SPD and Greens, and two for The Left) and reducing the overall size of the House of Representatives from 158 to 153. The state returning officer told *[Der Tagesspiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Tagesspiegel)* that the result at one polling station was at issue; it had been correctly counted on-site but was incorrectly reported by telephone on election night.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

### Controversies surrounding the Left

After the election, the Left faced criticism about the presence of controversial figures at a victory party. Ibrahim Ibrahim, an alleged representative of the [Popular Front for the Liberation of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_for_the_Liberation_of_Palestine), a Palestinian organization designated as a [terrorist group](63578.md) by the European Union, the United States, and others; Issa Remmo, head of the so-called Remmo clan ([de](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remmo-Clan)) as well as members of the Barbakh clan were spotted at the election party of the Neukölln district branch.[^cite_note-afp-35] Both the Remmo clan and the Barbakh clan are large families of Arab origin attributed to [organized crime](22625.md) in Germany.[^cite_note-36]

[Ferat Koçak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferat_Ko%C3%A7ak), the Left member of the Bundestag for [Berlin-Neukölln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-Neuk%C3%B6lln_\(electoral_district\)), was pictured with Ibrahim Ibrahim at the party. Following the election, Issa Remmo's son, Firas Remmo, posted a screenshot of a chat exchange between himself and Koçak, which suggested a close relationship between the two. Koçak wrote: "Respectful greetings back to your father." He also asked Remmo's son to share a video clip of his speeches in the Bundestag.[^cite_note-timesofisrael-37]

Representatives from SPD and CDU called for an investigation into the incident and warned of antisemitic tendencies within The Left party.[^cite_note-afp-35] Koçak's explanation of not having known who Remmo or Ibrahim were was deemed "unconvincing" by other parties' representatives. The Green's [Andreas Audretsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Audretsch) said: "Issa Remmo is a central figure in organized crime. You don't just bump into him at events". Veteran Die Linke MP [Gregor Gysi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregor_Gysi) said in a parliamentary debate that "obviously… Koçak made a mistake; we are investigating that."[^cite_note-timesofisrael-37]

Following the controversy, Koçak announced he would give up his seat on the Bundestag's Committee on Internal Affairs and stop making public appearances, though he remains a member of the Bundestag. In a public statement, he repeated that "I am neither a criminal nor do I support organized crime" and admitted to making a mistake.[^cite_note-38] The party stated that any further consequences would be discussed internally.[^cite_note-39]

### Government formation

On 25 September, a large majority of The Left's members voted in favor of beginning exploratory talks with the Greens and SPD. The Greens voted in favor earlier in the week and the SPD did as well the following day. In light of the Koçak controversy, Greens federal co-leader [Franziska Brantner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franziska_Brantner) set a condition that her party would not accept a member of The Left as senator of the interior or justice.[^cite_note-40]

Both other parties demanded The Left make a clear commitment against antisemitism, which the party then inserted into the binding agreement (*Mandatsträgervereinbarung*, "mandate-holder's agreement") that elected members must sign as a condition of taking their seat.[^cite_note-41] Before beginning exploratory talks, the three parties had initial meetings beginning on 1 October to obtain further assurances from The Left that it is taking concrete steps to distance itself from antisemitism, racism, and organized crime.[^cite_note-42] After two meetings, the parties stated on 3 October they had agreed on a "common way forward" and would continue working together.[^cite_note-43]
