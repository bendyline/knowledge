**Thomas Anthony** "**Tab**" **Baldwin** [ONZM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Officer_of_the_New_Zealand_Order_of_Merit) (born 16 May 1958) is an American-New Zealand [basketball](3921.md) coach who most recently served as the head of the [Ateneo Blue Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ateneo_Blue_Eagles) of the [University Athletic Association of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Athletic_Association_of_the_Philippines) (UAAP). Baldwin also served as a consultant of [Philippine Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Basketball_Association) club team [TNT Tropang Giga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_Tropang_Giga). In June 2026, Baldwin was placed under investigation by the Philippine government for training methods that involved swimming near tidal waves that [left two athletes dead from drowning](83420041.md). The UAAP imposed a unappealable lifetime ban against Baldwin barring him from coaching in the league and appearing in games.

## Coaching career

### New Zealand

Baldwin was born in [Jacksonville, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville,_Florida) and played for the Bishop Kenny High School under the coaching of his father, who played basketball for Notre Dame in the 1930s.[^cite_note-1] From [Jacksonville Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacksonville_Beach), he went to New Zealand in 1988 to coach the [Otago Nuggets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otago_Nuggets).[^cite_note-doublechange-2][^cite_note-3]

He was promoted with them from the second division to the [NBL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_League_\(New_Zealand\)) and then joined the [Auckland Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auckland_Stars) in 1994.[^cite_note-4] In eight seasons with Auckland, he won five NBL titles (1995, 1996, 1997, 1999 and 2000), and was named NBL Coach of the Year four times (1995, 97, 99 and 2014).

He remains the most victorious coach in the history of the [New Zealand NBL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_League_\(New_Zealand\)). Baldwin's involvement continued with the Stars as a co-owner for another eight years after he left as coach.

In 2001, he took the reins of the New Zealand national team. By winning the [2001 FIBA Oceania championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Oceania_Championship_2001), the [Tall Blacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tall_Blacks) — as New Zealand are known — qualified for the [2002 FIBA World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_FIBA_World_Championship), and even reached the semi-finals; this is the best performance by an Oceania team in the history of the World Championships. Baldwin was appointed an Honorary [Officer of the New Zealand Order of Merit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Officer_of_the_New_Zealand_Order_of_Merit) in the [2003 New Year Honours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_New_Year_Honours_\(New_Zealand\)), for services to basketball.[^cite_note-5] For his achievements as coach of the New Zealand team, Baldwin was awarded the coach of the year at the [Halberg awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halberg_award) — New Zealand's premier sports awards — for both 2001 and 2002.[^cite_note-Halberg2001-6][^cite_note-Halberg2003-7]

In 2023, Baldwin would be named into the New Zealand Basketball Hall of Fame.[^cite_note-8]

### Turkey, Lebanon and Jordan

Baldwin has coached [Banvitspor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banvitspor) in [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Basketball_League), [PAOK Thessaloniki B.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAOK_Thessaloniki_B.C.) in [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A1_Ethniki) and [U Mobitelco Cluj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-BT_Cluj-Napoca) in [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Basketball_Division_A).

On 16 April 2010, he was appointed as [Lebanon national basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_men's_national_basketball_team) head coach.,[^cite_note-9] and on 15 August 2010, Lebanon won the [FIBA Asia Stanković Cup 2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIBA_Asia_Stankovi%C4%87_Cup_2010) with Baldwin as head coach.[^cite_note-10]

In June 2011, Baldwin was hired to coach the [Jordanian national basketball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_national_basketball_team).

### Philippines, Ateneo Blue Eagles



Baldwin as the Philippine national team coach in 2015

On 2014, Baldwin was appointed as [Talk 'N Text Tropang Texters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TNT_Tropang_Giga) assistant coach and consultant. He was let go by the team in 2020.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]

On 23 December 2014, the [Samahang Basketbol ng Pilipinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samahang_Basketbol_ng_Pilipinas) formally announced the appointment of Baldwin as head coach of the Philippine men's national team (popularly known as *Gilas Pilipinas*), replacing [Chot Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chot_Reyes).[^cite_note-baldwin1-14] Baldwin's two-year tenure as coach officially began on 1 January 2015.[^cite_note-baldwin2-15]

On 7 December 2015, [Ateneo de Manila University](330339.md) formally announced the hiring of Baldwin as the head coach of its [collegiate men's varsity basketball team](72594613.md). He will be coaching the Blue Eagles in between the 2016 Olympics men's basketball qualifying tournament and the qualifying rounds for the [2019 FIBA Basketball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_FIBA_Basketball_World_Cup), concentrating on the national team during the UAAP offseason.[^cite_note-16] A few days later, the Ateneo management reconsidered its decision to place Baldwin as the team's head coach following an objection by the Basketball Coaches of the Philippines, and instead was to be appointed as the collegiate team's consultant.[^cite_note-BCAP-17] In 2016, Baldwin was officially named as the head coach and no longer just as a team consultant.[^cite_note-18]

On 18 October 2016, SBP executive director Sonny Barrios declared that [Chot Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chot_Reyes) will return as head coach of Gilas while Baldwin will stay with the national team as the team's consultant, the same coaching setup the national team had during its historic 2013 FIBA Asia Championship and 2014 FIBA World Cup runs.[^cite_note-19]

On 3 December 2017, Baldwin led the [Ateneo Blue Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ateneo_Blue_Eagles) to their ninth [UAAP basketball championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAAP_Basketball_Championship) against the [De La Salle Green Archers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_La_Salle_Green_Archers).[^cite_note-20]

In 2019, he coached the Blue Eagles to a 14–0 sweep of the elimination round of [UAAP Season 82 basketball tournaments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAAP_Season_82_basketball_tournaments), with an average winning margin of 17.4 points per game.[^cite_note-21] On 20 November, Ateneo completed the season sweep and won their third consecutive title with Baldwin by defeating the [UST Growling Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UST_Growling_Tigers), 86–79.[^cite_note-22] In May of the same year, Baldwin was also appointed as programme director for the Philippine youth national team.[^cite_note-23] Under Baldwin, the Blue Eagles have won four championships in the UAAP.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] In May 2023, he was recognised by the Collegiate Press Corps as the UAAP Men's Basketball Coach of the Year for the 2019–20 and 2022–23 seasons.[^cite_note-26]

In June 2021, Baldwin returned to take over as head coach of the Philippines,[^cite_note-27] leading the Philippines to three wins out of three games in the final window of the [2021 FIBA Asia Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_FIBA_Asia_Cup_qualification), completing a sweep for *Gilas*. However, weeks before the first window of the [2023 FIBA Basketball World Cup qualification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_FIBA_Basketball_World_Cup_qualification), Baldwin stepped down from his post as *Gilas* coach, choosing to focus on Ateneo's campaign for the [UAAP Season 84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UAAP_Season_84). He was replaced by [Chot Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chot_Reyes).[^cite_note-28]

#### 2026 Ateneo drowning incident

Main article: [Deaths of Divine Adili and Rene Baterbonia](83420041.md)

On 8 June 2026, [Rene Clert Baterbonia and Divine Adili](83420041.md), two student-athletes of the Ateneo Blue Eagles men's basketball team, died in a drowning incident during a training activity in [Dipaculao](2118651.md), [Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_\(province\)).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Following the tragedy, the team's off-campus training practices under Baldwin faced public scrutiny.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] He was later summoned by the [Department of Labor and Employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Labor_and_Employment_\(Philippines\)) on 11 June to explain his [alien employment permit](14666962.md) and was issued a [show-cause order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Show-cause_order) requiring him to appear before the agency on 15 June 2026.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Instead of appearing, Baldwin and team manager Christopher "Epok" Quimpo alongside the other implicated coaching staff resigned from the school team.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40] The UAAP imposed a unappealable lifetime ban against Baldwin barring him from coaching in the league and appearing in games.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

Baldwin was previously married to Efthymia, originally from Greece. Baldwin married Efthymia in 2011, but has been separated from her since 2020.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]
