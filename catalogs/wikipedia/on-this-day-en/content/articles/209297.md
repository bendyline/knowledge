***The Astrophysical Journal*** (***ApJ***[^cite_note-1]) is a [peer-reviewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peer_review) [scientific journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientific_journal) of [astrophysics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrophysics) and [astronomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomy), established in 1895 by American astronomers [George Ellery Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Ellery_Hale) and [James Edward Keeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Edward_Keeler). The journal discontinued its print edition and became an electronic-only journal in 2015.[^cite_note-2]

Since 1953, ***The Astrophysical Journal Supplement Series*** (***ApJS***) has been published in conjunction with *The Astrophysical Journal*, with generally longer articles to supplement the material in the journal. It publishes six volumes per year, with two 280-page issues per volume.

***The Astrophysical Journal Letters*** (***ApJL***), established in 1967 by [Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subrahmanyan_Chandrasekhar) as Part 2 of *The Astrophysical Journal*, is now a separate journal focusing on the rapid publication of high-impact astronomical research.

The three journals were published by the [University of Chicago Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chicago_Press) for the [American Astronomical Society](268042.md) until, in January 2009, publication was transferred to [IOP Publishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IOP_Publishing),[^cite_note-3] following the move of the society's *[Astronomical Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_Journal)* in 2008. The reason for the changes were given by the society as the increasing financial demands of the University of Chicago Press.[^cite_note-4] Compared to journals in other scientific disciplines, *The Astrophysical Journal* has a larger (> 85%) acceptance rate, which, however, is similar to other journals covering astronomy and astrophysics.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

On January 1, 2022, the AAS Journals, including ApJ, changed to an open access model, with access restrictions and subscription charges removed from previously published papers.[^cite_note-7] Articles accepted after October 11, 2022, will be published under the [Creative Commons license](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creative_Commons_license) CC-BY-SA 4.0. Non-open access articles accepted before that date will be free to access but will still need permission to reuse.[^cite_note-8]

## History

The journal was founded in 1895 by [George Ellery Hale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Ellery_Hale) and [James E. Keeler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_E._Keeler) as *The Astrophysical Journal: An International Review of Spectroscopy and Astronomical Physics*.[^cite_note-9] In addition to the two founding editors, there was an international board of associate editors: [M. A. Cornu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Alfred_Cornu), Paris; [N. C. Dunér](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nils_Christoffer_Dun%C3%A9r), Upsala; [William Huggins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Huggins), London; [P. Tacchini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Tacchini), Rome; [H. C. Vogel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Carl_Vogel), Potsdam, [C. S. Hastings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_S._Hastings), Yale; [A. A. Michelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A._A._Michelson), Chicago; [E. C. Pickering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_C._Pickering), Harvard; [H. A. Rowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._A._Rowland), Johns Hopkins; and [C. A. Young](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Augustus_Young), Princeton.[^cite_note-Hale1895-10] It was intended that the journal would fill the gap between journals in astronomy and physics, providing a venue for publication of articles on astronomical applications of the [spectroscope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectroscope); on laboratory research closely allied to astronomical physics, including [wavelength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wavelength) determinations of metallic and gaseous [spectra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spectrum#Electromagnetic_spectrum) and experiments on [radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electromagnetic_radiation) and [absorption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Absorption_\(electromagnetic_radiation\)); on theories of the Sun, Moon, planets, comets, meteors, and nebulae; and on instrumentation for telescopes and laboratories.[^cite_note-Hale1895-10] The further development of ApJ up to 1995 was outlined by [Helmut Abt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmut_Abt) in an article entitled "Some Statistical Highlights of the *Astrophysical Journal*" in 1995.[^cite_note-11]
