**Kota** ([/ˈkoʊtə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/87/Kota.ogg/Kota.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/87/Kota.ogg/Kota.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), previously known as *Kotah*, is the [third-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_and_towns_in_Rajasthan) city of the western [Indian state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_state) of [Rajasthan](26291.md).[^cite_note-8] It is located about 230 km (143 mi) south of the state capital, [Jaipur](166537.md), on the banks of the [Chambal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambal_River). As of 2024, with a population of over 1.5 million, it is the third most populous city in Rajasthan, after Jaipur and [Jodhpur](983053.md).[^cite_note-9] It is also India's first and world's second traffic signal free city after Bhutan's capital Thimphu. It serves as the administrative headquarters for the [Kota district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_district) and [Kota division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_division). It was founded as a walled city in the 14th century in the erstwhile [Bundi state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundi_State) and became the capital of the [princely state of Kota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_state) in 1625, following the separation of the Bundi and the Kota state.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Kota is renowned for its coaching institutes for [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering) and [medical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical) entrance exams, such as [JEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Entrance_Examination) and [NEET](33246416.md). Each year, over 200,000 students move to Kota to prepare for these competitive exams.[^cite_note-\:1-12]

In addition to several monuments, Kota is known for its palaces and gardens.[^cite_note-KOT-13][^cite_note-KOTT-14] The city was included among 98 Indian cities for the [Smart Cities Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_Cities_Mission) initiated by the [Indian Prime Minister](24452.md) [Narendra Modi](444222.md) in 2015[^cite_note-15] and was listed at 67th place after the results of the first round were released. Afterward, the top 20 cities were further selected for funding in the immediate financial year.[^cite_note-16]

## History

See also: [Kota State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_State)

The history of the city dates back to the 12th century CE, when Rao Deva, a [Chauhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauhan_Dynasty) Rajput chieftain belonging to the [Hada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hada_\(clan\)) clan, conquered the territory and founded [Bundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundi) and [Hadoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadoti). Later, in the early 17th century, during the reign of the Mughal Emperor [Jahangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahangir), the ruler of Bundi – Rao Ratan Singh, gave the smaller principality of Kota to his son, Madho Singh. Since then Kota has been recognized as a hallmark of the Rajput gallantry and culture.[^cite_note-KOTJH-17]

Kota became an independent state in 1631 when Rao Madho Singh, the second son of Rao Ratan of \[Bundi], was made the ruler by the Mughal Emperor [Jahangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahangir).[^cite_note-HKOT-18] Soon, Kota outgrew its parent state as it expanded in area, became wealthier in revenue, and more powerful. Maharao Bhim Singh played a pivotal role in Kota's history, having held a 'Mansab'[^cite_note-HKOT-18] of five thousand and being the first in his dynasty to have the title of [Maharao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharao). Zalim Singh, a diplomat and statesman, emerged as another prominent figure of the state in the 18th century. Although initially being a general of Kota's army, he rose to the regent of the kingdom after the king died, leaving a minor on the throne.[^cite_note-KOTJH-17] He remained a direct administrator of the state. In 1817, a treaty of friendship was signed between him and the British on the condition of carving out part of the existing state for his descendants, resulting in the formation of [Jhalawar State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhalawar_State) in 1838.[^cite_note-KOTJH-17] Kota was not involved in the earlier events of the [Indian Rebellion of 1857](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Rebellion_of_1857). However, when in October 1857, rebels murdered the local British resident and his two sons, British forces responded by storming the city and, after some resistance, capturing it in March 1858.[^cite_note-19]

In the 1940s, social activist [Guru Radha Kishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru_Radha_Kishan) organised trade union activities and campaigned against the colonial government. He left Kota after the local administration learned of the arrest warrant issued against him for his participation in Indian Independence activities.

## Geography



Kishore Sagar Lake

Kota is situated along the banks of the [Chambal River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambal_River) in the southern part of [Rajasthan](26291.md). It is the third-largest [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) of Rajasthan after [Jaipur](166537.md) and [Jodhpur](983053.md). The cartographic coordinates are [25°11′N 75°50′E  /  25.18°N 75.83°E  / 25.18; 75.83](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Kota,_Rajasthan\&params=25.18_N_75.83_E_).[^cite_note-20] It covers an area of 221.36 km<sup>2</sup> (85.47 sq mi).[^cite_note-Kotamc-4][^cite_note-KOTA-Census-2011-5][^cite_note-Statistics_by_Govt._of_Rajasthan-21] It has an average elevation of 271 metres (889 ft). The district is bound on the north and northwest by [Sawai Madhopur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawai_Madhopur), [Tonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tonk_district), and [Bundi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundi) districts. The Chambal River separates these districts from the Kota district, forming the natural boundary.

The city of Kota is situated at the centre of the southeastern region of Rajasthan, a region very widely known as [Hadoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadoti), the land of the [Hadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hada_\(clan\)). Kota lies along the banks of the Chambal river on a high sloping tableland forming a part of the Malwa Plateau. The general slope of the city is towards the north. The comparatively rocky, barren, and elevated land in the southern part of the city descends towards a plain agricultural land in the north. The Mukundara hills run from the southeast to the northwest axis of the town.

Kota has fertile land and greenery with irrigation facilities through canals. The two main canals, referred to as the left main canal (towards Bundi) and the right main canal (towards Baran), originate from the reservoir created by [Kota Barrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Barrage).[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-CVP-23] The tributaries of these canals form a network in the city and surrounding areas of Rajasthan and [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md), and supplements the irrigation of these areas.[^cite_note-CVP-23]

Kota has a semi-arid climate ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BSh*), bordering on a [Monsoon-influenced humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monsoon-influenced_humid_subtropical_climate) (Köppen: Cwa), with high temperatures throughout the year. Summers are long, hot, and dry, starting in late March and lasting till the end of June. The temperatures average above 40 °C in May and June, frequently exceed 45 °C, with temperatures as high as 48.5 °C have also been recorded.[^cite_note-24] The monsoon season follows with comparatively lower temperatures, but higher humidity and frequent, torrential downpours. The monsoons subside in October and temperatures rise again. The brief, mild winter starts in late November and lasts until the last week of February. Temperatures hover between 26.7 °C (max) to 12.0 °C (min). This can be considered the best time to visit Kota because of the intense heat in the summer.[^cite_note-Climate-25]

The average annual rainfall in the [Kota district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_district) is 800.6 mm.[^cite_note-Statistics_by_Govt._of_Rajasthan-21] Most of the rainfall can be attributed to the southwest [monsoon](57630.md), which usually begins around the last week of June and may last till mid-September. Pre-monsoon showers usually occur around the middle of June, with post-monsoon rains occasionally occurring in October. The winter is largely dry, although some rainfall does occur as a result of the [Western Disturbance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Disturbance) passing over the region.[^cite_note-Climate-25]

## Economy

The city is the trade centre for an area in which [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [coriander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coriander), and [oilseeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oilseeds) are grown; industries include cotton and oilseed milling, textile weaving, distilling, dairying, and the manufacture of metal handicrafts.[^cite_note-39] Kota also has an extensive industry of stone-polishing (tiles) of a stone called [Kota Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Stone), used for the floor and walls of residential and business buildings. Over the last 15 years, Kota has emerged as an Education hub of the country as producing excellent results in [IIT-JEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIT-JEE) and medical entrance exams.[^cite_note-KOTAEDU-40][^cite_note-KOTE-41][^cite_note-KOTE1-42]

### Kota educational industry

Kota is recognised as a major center for competitive [test preparation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_preparation), attracting around 150,000 students each year, as reported in 2023. These students come from across the country to enroll in coaching institutes that prepare them for national entrance exams for engineering and medical colleges. The industry generates an estimated \$350–\$450 million annually, with over 300 coaching centers, including the Allen Career Institute, which tutors more than one million students nationwide.[^cite_note-\:0-43]

The coaching industry in Kota originated in the 1980s when [Vinod Kumar Bansal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinod_Kumar_Bansal), a mechanical engineer, began tutoring students after being diagnosed with a degenerative condition that confined him to a wheelchair. His methods, inspired by the [Kumon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumon) method of learning, emphasized systematic problem solving. Bansal's success in helping students gain admission to prestigious institutions like the [Indian Institutes of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institutes_of_Technology) (IIT) drew more students to the city. Over time, other coaching centers were established by former colleagues and associates, eventually transforming Kota into an educational hub.[^cite_note-\:0-43]

### Kota stone



Storage area of Kota Stone

Main article: [Kota Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Stone)

The fine-grained variety of limestone quarried from the Kota district is known as Kota stone, with rich greenish-blue and brown colours. Kota stone is tough, non-water-absorbent, non-slip, and non-porous. The varieties include Kota Blue Natural, Kota Blue Honed, Kota Blue Polished, Kota Blue Cobbles, Kota Brown Natural, and Kota Brown Polished.[^cite_note-47]

### Industries

Kota is one of the industrial hubs in [northern India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_India), with chemical, cement, engineering, and power plants based there. The total number of industrial units in the district in 2010–11 stood at 12908, with 705 registered units.[^cite_note-KOTAIND-48] The district power plants show annual growth of 15–20% due to their strategic locations.[^cite_note-KOTAIND-48]

## Education

### Coaching Institutes

The city is especially known in India as a centre for the preparation of various national-level competitive examinations through which the students seek admissions in various engineering and medical colleges of the country. Often termed as the "Kota Factory", the town contains more than 40 large coaching institutes for aspiring students trying to pass entrance exams for [Indian Institutes of Technology (IIT)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institutes_of_Technology), through the [IIT-JEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Entrance_Examination_%E2%80%93_Advanced), and prominent medical colleges such as the [All India Institutes of Medical Sciences (AIIMS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Institutes_of_Medical_Sciences) of India, using [NEET-UG](33246416.md).

Since 2000, the city has emerged as a popular [coaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaching) destination for preparing for competitive exams as well as for-profit educational services. The education sector of Kota has become one of the major contributors to the city's economy.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53] Kota is popularly referred to as "the coaching capital of India".[^cite_note-KOTAEDU-40][^cite_note-KOTE-41][^cite_note-54] Over 150,000 students from all over the country flock every year towards the city for preparation of various exams such as [IIT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Institutes_of_Technology)-[JEE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Entrance_Examination) and [NEET-UG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Eligibility_and_Entrance_Test) etc.[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] Many hostels and PGs are located in Kota near the vicinity of coaching centres for students. Students live here for 2–3 years and prepare for the exams. The annual turnover of the Kota coaching industry is about ₹1500 crore.[^cite_note-59] The majority of the students here are enrolled in schools, providing the facility of "dummy schooling", which gives students admission without the need to attend it regularly. However, it is an illegal practice.[^cite_note-60] In 2019, [The Viral Fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Viral_Fever) launched a [Web Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Web_series) called [Kota Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Factory) to depict the lives of students who study at Kota.

Kota's emergence as a coaching hub began in 1985 when [Vinod Kumar Bansal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinod_Kumar_Bansal), an engineer, established Bansal Classes that eventually became [Bansal Classes Private Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinod_Kumar_Bansal).[^cite_note-61]

In 2024, Kota has also witnessed a significant decline in the number of students enrolling from 2-2.5 lakh students to just 85000-1 lakh students and revenue from ₹6,500-7,000 crore to ₹3,500 crore which is a decline by 50%, owing to the significant rise in student suicides and stricter rules implemented for coaching institutes by the [Rajasthan Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Rajasthan) and the [Ministry of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Education_\(India\)).[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-The_Hindu-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

## Transport

Kota is well connected by road and rail to all major cities within Rajasthan as well as those located outside the state.[^cite_note-71]

### Roadways

Kota has two major interstate bus terminals, namely, Nayapura Bus Stand at Nayapura and Roadways New Bus Stand at Ramchandrapura. National Highway No.27 (via [Udaipur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udaipur), [Kanpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanpur), [Gorakhpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gorakhpur), [Siliguri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siliguri), [Bongaigaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bongaigaon),[Guwahati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guwahati)) and National highway No.52 (via [Hisar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisar_\(city\)), [Churu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Churu,_Rajasthan), [Sikar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikar), [Jaipur](166537.md), [Indore](365670.md), [Aurangabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangabad), [Solapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solapur), and [Hubli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hubli)) pass through the Kota City.[^cite_note-72] National Highway No.27 is a part of East-West Corridor ([Porbandar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porbandar) - [Silchar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silchar)) and National Highway No.52 connects Punjab to Karnataka ([Sangrur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangrur), [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab)—[Ankola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ankola), [Karnataka](16880.md)). The total road length in Kota district is 2,052 km as of March 2011. There are also three upcoming expressway projects in the form of [Delhi–Mumbai Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Mumbai_Expressway) (Via Kota, Rajasthan, and [Vadodara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vadodara)), [Kota–Hyderabad Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota%E2%80%93Hyderabad_Expressway?action=edit\&redlink=1) (Via [Indore](365670.md)) and [Chambal Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chambal_Expressway).

### Airways

[Kota Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_Airport) is a civil airport serving Kota, Rajasthan, India. Spread over 447 acres, Kota Airport was originally built by the royal family of the princely state of [Kota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadoti) and was taken over by the government in 1951. This Airport Also Known As Rajputana Airport. Originally serviced by [Indian Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Airlines) [Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-3) aircraft and later by [Vayudoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vayudoot) and [Jagson Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagson_Airlines), shutdown of major industries and Kota becoming a major railway junction effected decreased demand for air transport and the withdrawal of the airlines.[^cite_note-ie05-76] Kota Airport has had no scheduled services operating since 1999.[^cite_note-toi11-77] The nearest international airport is [Jaipur International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaipur_International_Airport) situated around 240 km away from Kota.

The development of [Kota greenfield airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kota_greenfield_airport) has been approved 15 kilometres outside of the city.
