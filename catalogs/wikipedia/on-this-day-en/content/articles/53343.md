**Plaid Cymru** (English: [/plaɪd ˈkʌmri/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*plyde KUM-ree*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-23] Welsh: [\[plaid ˈkəmrɨ̞\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Welsh); lit. 'Party of Wales', officially **Plaid Cymru – the Party of Wales**, and often referred to simply as **Plaid**) is a [centre-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre-left) to [left-wing](18499.md) [Welsh nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_nationalist) [political party in Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Wales), that supports [Welsh independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_independence) from the [United Kingdom](31717.md). It campaigns on a platform of [social democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy) and [civic nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_nationalism).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] The party is a supporter of the [European Union](9317.md) and is a member of the [European Free Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Free_Alliance) (EFA).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] The party holds 4 of 32 Welsh seats in the UK [House of Commons](13828.md), 44 of 96 seats in the [Senedd](238480.md),[^cite_note-28] and 200 of 1,234 principal local authority councillors.[^cite_note-bbc_2022_results-29] It is currently leading a minority [government in Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government).[^cite_note-30] Plaid was formed in 1925 under the name *Plaid Genedlaethol Cymru* ('the national party of Wales') and [Gwynfor Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynfor_Evans) won the first Westminster seat for the party at the [1966 Carmarthen by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Carmarthen_by-election).[^cite_note-31]

In [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_National_Assembly_for_Wales_election) (in the first devolved [Welsh Assembly](238480.md) election), Plaid Cymru gained considerable ground in traditionally Labour heartlands.[^cite_note-32] These breakthroughs were part of the intentional aim to win more seats in the [Welsh valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_valleys) and [North East Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_East_Wales).[^cite_note-33] The party have mostly been in opposition in the [Senedd](238480.md). Although under the leadership of [Ieuan Wyn Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ieuan_Wyn_Jones), the party was part of a coalition as a [junior partner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Wales) with [Welsh Labour](6732798.md) between 2007 and 2011.[^cite_note-\:2-34] Wyn Jones became the [deputy First Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_First_Minister_of_Wales) and [Minister for the Economy and Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_the_Economy_and_Transport), alongside other Plaid MSs who also joined the cabinet.&#x20;

After losses in the [2011 Assembly elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_National_Assembly_for_Wales_election) and dropping down to being the third largest party, Wyn Jones stepped down. He was succeeded by [Leanne Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leanne_Wood).[^cite_note-\:3-35] In the [2016 Assembly elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_National_Assembly_for_Wales_election) Wood managed to win her constituency seat of [Rhondda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhondda_\(Senedd_constituency\)) meaning the party gained one seat, and became the official opposition once again, although only for a brief period.[^cite_note-\:4-36] In 2018 following internal pressure and a [leadership contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Plaid_Cymru_leadership_election), [Adam Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Price) defeated Wood and was elected the new leader. Following the [2021 Senedd election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Senedd_election) Plaid formed a [co-operation agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Welsh_Labour%E2%80%93Plaid_Cymru_agreement) with the [Welsh Labour government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government).[^cite_note-\:5-37] In May 2023 Price resigned as leader following the publication of a report which detailed failings by the party to prevent sexual harassment and bullying.[^cite_note-\:6-38] In June 2023 [Rhun ap Iorwerth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhun_ap_Iorwerth) was elected unopposed as leader.[^cite_note-\:7-39] The party won both its target seats in the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election_in_Wales), therefore becoming the second largest party representing Wales in the House of Commons.[^cite_note-40]

&#x20;Plaid Cymru won a plurality in the [2026 Senedd election](68245236.md) with 43 out of 96 seats, and have formed the current [Welsh government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government), with Rhun ap Iorwerth becoming the first Plaid Cymru [First Minister](602076.md).

## Ideology

Plaid Cymru is a centre-left to left-wing party that heavily emphasises its Welsh character, having been described as [regionalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionalism_\(politics\)),[^cite_note-Schrijver2006-41] [civic nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_nationalism),[^cite_note-42] [left-wing nationalist](16066160.md),[^cite_note-43] [Welsh nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_nationalism),[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47] and [pro-independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_independence).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-\:02-49][^cite_note-\:12-50]

Economically, the party gradually adopted a [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) line and welcomed many socialists since its founding, but formalised its goal of "decentralized socialism" only in 1981.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] The party has been described as [democratic socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_socialism)[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-SOCDEMIDE-55] and left-wing,[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] and later as [social democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy)[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-\:02-49][^cite_note-\:12-50] and centre-left,[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] but in recent years the former party leader [Leanne Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leanne_Wood) has described herself as a socialist[^cite_note-61] and the party has gradually been re-conceived as left-wing[^cite_note-left-18] and also [left-wing populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_populism).[^cite_note-\:8-62][^cite_note-63]

The party has been described as [environmentalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmentalism),[^cite_note-64] and opposes new [nuclear power](22153.md) plants[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] and [nuclear weapons](21785.md) (including the [UK's Trident nuclear weapons programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trident_\(UK_nuclear_programme\))).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

In May 2025, the party's leader, [Rhun ap Iorwerth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhun_ap_Iorwerth), stated there was no plan for an independence referendum in the first term of a Plaid Cymru government if they are elected at the 2026 Senedd election, and that talk of a referendum would not take place before 2030. However, the party continues to hold an official position favouring Welsh independence.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] At the party's October 2025 conference, the party's members backed a motion to establish a "standing commission" to develop a [white paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_paper) on Welsh independence. However, leader Rhun ap Iorwerth has stated that the white paper would not be for Plaid's first term of government if they were elected, although the commission may still collect evidence for the white paper during a Plaid first term. A date was not given but it is suggested they may develop plans if Plaid win both the 2026 and 2030 Senedd elections. Ap Iorweth stated they aim to "kick start the national debate on independence", "take the discussion forward" and "call the referendum when the time is right", with members backing a statement that Wales was "on a journey to independence".[^cite_note-BBC_plan_delay-73]

### Beginnings



Plaque commemorating the founding of Plaid Cymru, [Pwllheli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pwllheli)

While both the [Labour](19279158.md) and [Liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_\(UK\)) parties of the early 20th century had accommodated demands for Welsh home rule, no political party existed for the purpose of establishing a Welsh government. *Plaid Genedlaethol Cymru* ([Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) for 'the National Party of Wales') was formed on 5 August 1925, by [Moses Gruffydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Gruffydd?action=edit\&redlink=1), [H. R. Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._R._Jones) and [Lewis Valentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Valentine), members of *Byddin Ymreolwyr Cymru* ([Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) for 'the Home Rule Army of Wales'; lit. 'the Self-Rulers' Army of Wales'); and [Fred Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Jones_\(Welsh_politician\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Saunders Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saunders_Lewis) of *Y Mudiad Cymreig* ([Welsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language) for 'the Welsh Movement') and [D. Edmund Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D._Edmund_Williams?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-Ailgeni-74] Initially, home rule for Wales was not an explicit aim of the new movement; keeping Wales [Welsh-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh-speaking) took primacy, with the aim of making Welsh the only official language of Wales.[^cite_note-Philip_1975-75]

Plaid Genedlaethol Cymru's principles (translated from Welsh), as of 1925, were as followed: "(to) Have a Welsh Wales, That includes;", "To preserve the Welsh language as the only living language of Wales, and to restore it as the official language in public life and administration throughout Wales", "To secure for the Welsh language a central place in the educational system of Wales, from elementary school to university", they outlined that they wanted Wales to be "a self-governing nation" and they would do so by having "Control over Welsh affairs through a national parliament elected by the people", "Representation in the League of Nations and the right to determine its own relationships as a member nation among the nations of the world", "Freedom from English political domination", have "Control over all local resources within Wales".[^cite_note-76]

In the [1929 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1929_United_Kingdom_general_election), the party contested its first parliamentary constituency, [Caernarvonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caernarvonshire_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), polling 609 votes, or 1.6% of the vote for that seat. The party contested few such elections in its early years, partly due to its ambivalence towards Westminster politics. Indeed, the candidate [Lewis Valentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Valentine), the party's first president, offered himself in Caernarvonshire on a platform of demonstrating Welsh people's rejection of English dominion.[^cite_note-McAllister_2001-77]

### 1930s

By 1932, the aims of [self-government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-government) and Welsh representation at the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations) had been added to that of preserving Welsh language and culture. However, this move, and the party's early attempts to develop an economic critique, did not broaden its appeal beyond that of an intellectual and [socially conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socially_conservative) Welsh language pressure group.[^cite_note-78] The alleged sympathy of the party's leading members (including President [Saunders Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saunders_Lewis)) towards Europe's [totalitarian regimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totalitarianism) compromised its early appeal further.[^cite_note-79]

Saunders Lewis, David John Williams and Lewis Valentine set fire to the newly constructed RAF [Penyberth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penyberth) air base on the [Llŷn Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ll%C5%B7n_Peninsula) in [Gwynedd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynedd) in 1936, in protest at its siting in the Welsh-speaking heartland. The leaders' treatment, including the trial judge's dismissal of the use of Welsh and their subsequent imprisonment in [Wormwood Scrubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wormwood_Scrubs_\(HM_Prison\)), led to "The Three" becoming a *[cause célèbre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cause_c%C3%A9l%C3%A8bre)*. This heightened the profile of the party dramatically and its membership had doubled to nearly 2,000 by 1939.[^cite_note-Philip_1975-75][^cite_note-Y_tri-80]

### 1940s



A Plaid Cymru rally in [Machynlleth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machynlleth) in 1949

Penyberth, and Plaid Cymru's neutral stance during the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), prompted concerns within the UK Government that it might be used by Germany to insert spies or carry out other covert operations.[^cite_note-81] In fact, the party adopted a neutral standpoint and urged (with only limited success) [conscientious objection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscientious_objection) to war service.[^cite_note-82]

In 1943, Saunders Lewis contested the University of Wales parliamentary seat at a by-election, gaining 1,330 votes, or 22%. In the [1945 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_United_Kingdom_general_election), with party membership at around 2,500, Plaid Cymru contested seven seats, as many as it had in the preceding 20 years, including constituencies in south Wales for the first time. At this time [Gwynfor Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynfor_Evans) was elected president.[^cite_note-83]

### 1950s



1959 election in Merioneth. Gwynfor Evans, standing, is talking at [Bryncrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryncrug)

Iorwerth Thomas and Gwynfor Evans sitting at a table in front of a BBC microphone, debating.

BBC debate between [Iorwerth Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iorwerth_Thomas) (Rhondda MP - [Labour](19279158.md)) and [Gwynfor Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwynfor_Evans), Plaid Cymru's first MP

Gwynfor Evans's presidency coincided with the maturation of Plaid Cymru (as it now began to refer to itself) into a more recognisable political party. Its share of the vote increased from 0.7% in the [1951 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_United_Kingdom_general_election) to 3.1% in [1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_United_Kingdom_general_election) and 5.2% in [1959](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1959_United_Kingdom_general_election). In the 1959 election, the party contested a majority of Welsh seats for the first time. Proposals to flood the village of [Capel Celyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capel_Celyn) in the [Tryweryn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tryweryn) valley in Gwynedd in 1957 to supply the city of [Liverpool](18081.md) with water played a part in Plaid Cymru's growth. The fact that the parliamentary bill authorising the dam went through without support from any Welsh MPs showed that the MPs' votes in Westminster were not enough to prevent such bills from passing.[^cite_note-84]

### 1960s

Support for the party declined slightly in the early 1960s, particularly as support for the Liberal Party began to stabilise from its long-term decline. In 1962, Saunders Lewis gave a radio talk entitled *[Tynged yr Iaith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tynged_yr_Iaith)* ('the fate of the language') in which he predicted the extinction of the Welsh language unless action was taken. This led to the formation of *[Cymdeithas yr Iaith Gymraeg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cymdeithas_yr_Iaith_Gymraeg)* ('the Welsh Language Society') the same year.[^cite_note-85]

Labour's return to power in 1964 and the creation of the post of [Secretary of State for Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Wales) appeared to represent a continuation of the incremental evolution of a distinctive Welsh polity, following the [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) government's appointment of a Minister of Welsh Affairs in 1951[^cite_note-86] and the establishment of [Cardiff](5882.md) as Wales's capital in 1955.[^cite_note-87]

However, [in 1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Carmarthen_by-election), less than four months after coming in third in the constituency of [Carmarthen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmarthen_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), Gwynfor Evans captured the seat from Labour at a by-election. This was followed by two further by-elections in [Rhondda West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhondda_West_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) in [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Rhondda_West_by-election) and [Caerphilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caerphilly_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Caerphilly_by-election) in which the party achieved massive swings of 30% and 40% respectively, coming within a whisker of victory. The results were caused partly by an anti-Labour backlash. Expectations in coal mining communities that the [Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Wilson) government would halt the long-term decline in their industry had been dashed by a significant downward revision of coal production estimates.[^cite_note-88] However, particularly in Carmarthen, Plaid also successfully depicted Labour's policies as a threat to the viability of small Welsh communities.[^cite_note-89]

### 1970s

In the [1970 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_United_Kingdom_general_election), Plaid Cymru contested every seat in Wales for the first time and its vote share surged from 4.5% in 1966 to 11.5%. Gwynfor Evans lost Carmarthen to Labour, but regained the seat in [October 1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_1974_United_Kingdom_general_election), by which time the party had gained a further two MPs, representing the constituencies of [Caernarfon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caernarfon_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) and [Merionethshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merioneth_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

Plaid Cymru's emergence (along with the [Scottish National Party](28887.md)) prompted the Wilson government to establish the [Kilbrandon Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilbrandon_Commission) on the constitution. The subsequent proposals for a Welsh Assembly were, however, heavily defeated in a [referendum in 1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Welsh_devolution_referendum). Despite Plaid Cymru's ambivalence toward home rule (as opposed to outright independence) the [referendum](51582.md) result led many in the party to question its direction.[^cite_note-McAllister_2001-77]

Plaid campaigned to leave the Common Market in the [1975 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_United_Kingdom_European_Communities_membership_referendum),[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-auto-93] feeling that the EC's regional aid policies would "reconcile places like Wales to their subordinate position".[^cite_note-williams-brexit2-94] Nevertheless, 65% of Welsh voters voted to remain in the EC during a [1975 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_United_Kingdom_European_Communities_membership_referendum).[^cite_note-95] The EC was incorporated into the [European Union](9317.md) (EU) in 1993.[^cite_note-96]

At the [1979 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_United_Kingdom_general_election), the party's vote share declined from 10.8% to 8.1% and Carmarthen was again lost to Labour, although [Caernarfon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caernarfon) and [Merionethshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merionethshire) were held by the party.

### 1980s

Caernarfon MP [Dafydd Wigley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafydd_Wigley) succeeded Gwynfor Evans as president in 1981, inheriting a party whose morale was at an all-time low. In 1981 the party adopted a policy of "community socialism".[^cite_note-97] While the party embarked on a wide-ranging review of its priorities and goals, Gwynfor Evans fought a successful campaign (including the threat of a hunger strike) to oblige the Conservative government to fulfill its promise to establish [S4C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S4C), a Welsh-language television station.[^cite_note-98] In 1984, [Dafydd Elis-Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafydd_Elis-Thomas) was elected president, defeating [Dafydd Iwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafydd_Iwan), a move that saw the party shift to the left. [Ieuan Wyn Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ieuan_Wyn_Jones) (later Plaid Cymru leader) captured [Ynys Môn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ynys_Mon_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) from the Conservatives in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_United_Kingdom_general_election). In 1989 Dafydd Wigley once again assumed the presidency of the party.

In 1986, the party established Undeb Credyd Plaid Cymru Credit Union Limited, a savings and loans [co-operative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-operative) based in [Roath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roath), Cardiff, open to party members.[^cite_note-99] The [credit union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Credit_union) closed in the late 2010s and was deregistered in 2023.[^cite_note-100]

### 1990s

In the [1992 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_United_Kingdom_general_election), the party added a fourth MP, [Cynog Dafis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cynog_Dafis), when he gained [Ceredigion and Pembroke North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceredigion_and_Pembroke_North) from the [Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)). Dafis was endorsed by the local branch of the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wales_Green_Party). The party's vote share recovered to 9.9% at the [1997 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_United_Kingdom_general_election).

In 1997, following the election of a Labour government committed to devolution for Wales, a [further referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Welsh_devolution_referendum) was narrowly won, establishing the [National Assembly for Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_for_Wales). Plaid Cymru became the main opposition to the ruling Labour Party, with 17 seats to Labour's 28. In doing so, it appeared to have broken out of its rural Welsh-speaking heartland, and gained seats in traditionally strong Labour areas in industrial [South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Wales).[^cite_note-101]

Ahead of the [1999 National Assembly for Wales election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_National_Assembly_for_Wales_election), Plaid Cymru dropped its policy of Welsh independence in favour of continued membership in the European Union. These changes in policy were made as it was believed that the electorate in Wales did not view independence as an important issue. It also adopted [social democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy) for its economic policy in an attempt to weaken Labour. These changes in policy have been used to explain the party's subsequent electoral success in Labour's traditional [South East Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_Wales) heartlands.[^cite_note-\:1-102]

### Assembly/Senedd era



Plaid Cymru Group office in the [Senedd building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senedd_building)

#### First National Assembly (1999–2003)

In the [1999 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_National_Assembly_for_Wales_election), Plaid Cymru gained seats in traditional Labour areas such as [Rhondda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhondda_\(National_Assembly_for_Wales_constituency\)), [Islwyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islwyn_\(National_Assembly_for_Wales_constituency\)) and [Llanelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanelli_\(National_Assembly_for_Wales_constituency\)), achieving by far its highest share of the vote in any Wales-wide election until the [2026 Senedd election](68245236.md) While Plaid Cymru regarded itself as the natural beneficiary of devolution, others attributed its performance in large part to the travails of the Labour Party, whose nomination for [Assembly First Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_First_Secretary), [Ron Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Davies_\(British_politician\)), was forced to stand down in an alleged [sex scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_scandal). The ensuing leadership battle, won by [Alun Michael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alun_Michael), did much to damage Labour, and thus aided Plaid Cymru, whose leader was the more popular and higher profile [Dafydd Wigley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafydd_Wigley). The Labour Party's UK national leadership was seen to interfere in the contest and deny the popular [Rhodri Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodri_Morgan) victory.[^cite_note-103] Less than two months later, in [elections to the European parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_European_Parliament_election_in_the_United_Kingdom), Labour support slumped further, and Plaid Cymru came within 2.5% of achieving the largest share of the vote in Wales. Under the new system of [proportional representation](23298.md), the party also gained two MEPs.

Plaid Cymru then developed political problems of its own. Dafydd Wigley resigned, citing health problems but amid rumours of a plot against him.[^cite_note-104] His successor, [Ieuan Wyn Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ieuan_Wyn_Jones), struggled to impose his authority, particularly over controversial remarks made by a councillor, Seimon Glyn.[^cite_note-105] At the same time, Labour leader and First Minister Alun Michael was replaced by Rhodri Morgan.[^cite_note-106]

In the [2001 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_United_Kingdom_general_election), notwithstanding Plaid Cymru recording its highest-ever vote share in a general election, 14.3%, the party lost Wyn Jones's former seat of [Ynys Môn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ynys_M%C3%B4n_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) to [Albert Owen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Owen), although it gained [Carmarthen East and Dinefwr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmarthen_East_and_Dinefwr_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)), where [Adam Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Price) was elected.

#### Second National Assembly (2003–07)

The [Assembly elections of May 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_National_Assembly_for_Wales_election) saw the party's representation drop from 17 to 12, with the seats gained in the 1999 election falling again to Labour and the party's share of the vote declining to 21%. Plaid Cymru narrowly remained the second-largest party in the National Assembly ahead of the Conservatives, Liberal Democrats and [a single independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Marek_Independent_Party).[^cite_note-107]

On 15 September 2003, [folk-singer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_music) and county councillor [Dafydd Iwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafydd_Iwan) was elected as Plaid Cymru's president. Ieuan Wyn Jones, who had resigned from his dual role as president and Assembly group leader following the losses in the 2003 Assembly election, was re-elected in the latter role. [Elfyn Llwyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elfyn_Llwyd) remained the Plaid Cymru leader in the Westminster Parliament. Under Iwan's presidency, the party formally adopted a policy of independence for Wales within Europe at its conference in September 2003.[^cite_note-108] Plaid Cymru had historically supported Welsh independence but dropped this policy ahead of the 1999 devolved election.[^cite_note-\:1-102]

The 2004 local election saw the party lose control of the two South Wales councils it gained in 1999, [Rhondda Cynon Taff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhondda_Cynon_Taff) and [Caerphilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caerphilly), while retaining its stronghold of Gwynedd in the north-west. The results enabled the party to claim a greater number of ethnic minority councillors than all the other political parties in Wales combined,[^cite_note-109] along with gains in authorities such as Cardiff and [Swansea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swansea), where Plaid Cymru representation had been minimal. In the European Parliament elections of the same year, the party's vote share fell to 17.4%, and the reduction in the number of Welsh MEPs saw its representation reduced to one.[^cite_note-110]



Old logo (above) and new logo (below)

In the [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_United_Kingdom_general_election) of 5 May 2005, Plaid Cymru lost the [Ceredigion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceredigion_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)) seat to the [Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)); this result was a disappointment to Plaid, who had hoped to gain [Ynys Môn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ynys_M%C3%B4n_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)). Overall therefore, Plaid Cymru's Parliamentary representation fell to three seats, the lowest number for the party since 1992. The party's share of the vote fell to 12.6%.[^cite_note-111]

Since Plaid Genedlaethol Cymru reformation to 'Plaid Cymru' in 1933, the logo representing the party was the green 'triban' (three peaks) which symbolically represented Plaid's three key goals; self-government, cultural prosperity and economic prosperity, 'anchored in the bedrock of Welsh identity and history that is the Welsh upland landscape',[^cite_note-112] the logo would change in the late stages of 20th century to include the red dragon of Wales, however this version was short-lived. In 2006, the party voted constitutional changes to formally designate the party's leader in the assembly as its overall leader, with [Ieuan Wyn Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ieuan_Wyn_Jones) being restored to the full leadership and [Dafydd Iwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafydd_Iwan) becoming head of the voluntary wing of the party.[^cite_note-113] The party unveiled a radical change of image in 2006. In that year, the party opted to use "Plaid" as the party's name, although "Plaid Cymru — the Party of Wales" would remain the official title. Plaid would abandon the triban (apart from the merchandise) and adopt the yellow [Welsh poppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_poppy) (*Meconopsis cambrica*).[^cite_note-114]

#### Third National Assembly (2007–2011)

In the [National Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_National_Assembly_for_Wales_election) of 3 May 2007, Plaid Cymru increased its number of seats from 12 to 15, regaining [Llanelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llanelli_\(National_Assembly_for_Wales_constituency\)), gaining one additional list seat and winning the newly created constituency of [Aberconwy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberconwy_\(National_Assembly_for_Wales_constituency\)). The 2007 election also saw Plaid Cymru's [Mohammad Asghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Asghar) become the first ethnic minority candidate elected to the Welsh Assembly.[^cite_note-115] The party's share of the vote increased to 22.4%.[^cite_note-116]

After weeks of negotiations involving all four parties in the Assembly, Plaid Cymru and Labour agreed to form a [coalition government](6036.md). Their agreed "[One Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Wales)" programme included a commitment for both parties to campaign for a Yes vote in a [referendum on full law-making powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Welsh_devolution_referendum) for the Assembly, to be held at a time of the Welsh Assembly Government's choosing.[^cite_note-\:2-34] Ieuan Wyn Jones was subsequently confirmed as [Deputy First Minister of Wales](602076.md)[^cite_note-117] and [Minister for the Economy and Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_the_Economy_and_Transport). [Rhodri Glyn Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodri_Glyn_Thomas) was appointed Heritage Minister. He later stood down, and [Alun Ffred Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alun_Ffred_Jones) took over. Ceredigion AM [Elin Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elin_Jones) was appointed to the Rural Affairs brief in the new 10-member cabinet. [Jocelyn Davies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jocelyn_Davies) became Deputy Minister for Housing and Regeneration.[^cite_note-118]

In the [2010 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_general_election), Plaid returned three MPs to Westminster. They took part in the [Yes for Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yes_for_Wales) cross-party campaign for the March 2011 referendum.[^cite_note-119]

#### Fourth National Assembly (2011–16)

In the [2011 National Assembly election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_National_Assembly_for_Wales_election), Plaid slipped from second place to third, being overtaken by the [Welsh Conservatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Conservatives) and losing its deputy leader [Helen Mary Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Mary_Jones). The party held an inquiry into the election result.[^cite_note-120] The internal investigation led to the adoption of wide-ranging changes to its constitution, including a streamlining of the leadership structure.[^cite_note-121]

In May 2011, Ieuan Wyn Jones announced he would stand down as leader within the first half of the Assembly term.[^cite_note-122] A leadership election was held in which three candidates eventually stood: [Elin Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elin_Jones), [Dafydd Elis-Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafydd_Elis-Thomas) and [Leanne Wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leanne_Wood);[^cite_note-123] [Simon Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Thomas_\(politician\)) withdrew his candidacy before ballots were cast.[^cite_note-124]

On 15 March 2012, Plaid Cymru elected Leanne Wood as its new leader. She received 55% of the vote, over second-placed Elin Jones with 41%.[^cite_note-125] Wood was the party's first female leader, and its first not to be a fluent Welsh speaker.[^cite_note-\:3-35][^cite_note-126] Soon after her election as leader, she appointed former MP [Adam Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Price) to head an economic commission for the party "focussed on bringing together tailor-made policies in order to transform our economy".[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] On 1 May 2012, it was confirmed Leanne Wood would not be taking the £23,000 pay increase that every other party leader in the Assembly receives.[^cite_note-129]

On 12 November 2012, Wood announced she would aim to abandon her relatively safe list seat by winning a constituency at the 2016 National Assembly elections;[^cite_note-130] she later confirmed she would contest the [Rhondda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhondda_\(Assembly_constituency\)).[^cite_note-131] Adam Price was subsequently selected as the party's candidate for [Carmarthen East and Dinefwr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmarthen_East_and_Dinefwr_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)).[^cite_note-132] Lindsay Whittle confirmed he would stand solely in [Caerphilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caerphilly_\(National_Assembly_for_Wales_constituency\)).[^cite_note-133]

On 20 June 2013, former party leader Ieuan Wyn Jones stood down from the Assembly as the member for [Ynys Môn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ynys_M%C3%B4n_\(National_Assembly_for_Wales_constituency\)).[^cite_note-134] Plaid Cymru's candidate [Rhun ap Iorwerth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhun_ap_Iorwerth) was elected as the new Assembly Member for the constituency, receiving 12,601 votes (a 58% share) with a majority of 9,166 over the Labour candidate.[^cite_note-135]

#### Fifth National Assembly/Senedd (2016–2021)

At the [2016 Welsh Assembly elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_National_Assembly_for_Wales_election), Plaid Cymru gained one seat, [Rhondda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhondda_\(Assembly_constituency\)) from Labour, on what was an otherwise disappointing night,[^cite_note-136] and became the Assembly's second-largest party and briefly became the official opposition to the Welsh Government with 12 seats.[^cite_note-\:4-36] By January 2018 Plaid Cymru had been reduced to ten Assembly Members, following the resignation of [Dafydd Elis-Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dafydd_Elis-Thomas) in 2016[^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] and the permanent expulsion of [Neil McEvoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neil_McEvoy) from Plaid's Assembly group in 2018.[^cite_note-139]

Despite campaigning to leave in [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_United_Kingdom_European_Communities_membership_referendum),[^cite_note-auto-93] Plaid campaigned for a Remain vote in the [2016 referendum on the UK's membership of the EU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum),[^cite_note-140] spending £27,495 on the campaign.[^cite_note-141] In the referendum Wales voted 52.5% in favour of Leave.[^cite_note-142] Immediately after the referendum, Leanne Wood stated that voters 'must be respected' and criticised calls for a second EU referendum.[^cite_note-143] Plaid Cymru later modified their policy to support a [People's Vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Vote).[^cite_note-144]

In the [2017 United Kingdom general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_United_Kingdom_general_election_in_Wales), Plaid saw their popular vote fall, but narrowly gained Ceredigion and saw Arfon become highly marginal.[^cite_note-145]



A campaign board for a Plaid Cymru election candidate in 2019

In September 2018, Adam Price won the party's [leadership election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Plaid_Cymru_leadership_election), defeating the incumbent Leanne Wood and fellow challenger [Rhun ap Iorwerth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhun_ap_Iorwerth).[^cite_note-146] In October 2018, it was regarded as [left-wing populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-wing_populism) by Massetti Emmanuel in the [Comparative European Politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparative_European_Politics) academic journal.[^cite_note-\:8-62]

In the [Brecon and Radnorshire by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Brecon_and_Radnorshire_by-election) Plaid Cymru decided not to put up a candidate, but instead to support the Liberal Democrat candidate [Jane Dodds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Dodds) in order to maximise the chance of an anti-Brexit candidate winning.[^cite_note-Mason_2019-147]

In the [2019 United Kingdom general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_United_Kingdom_general_election_in_Wales), Plaid stood aside in four seats to endorse [Unite to Remain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unite_to_Remain) candidates.[^cite_note-148] Plaid held their four seats but saw a decrease in their popular vote.[^cite_note-149] Also ahead of the election, Price announced the creation of an independence commission, led by former [Dwyfor Meirionydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwyfor_Meirionydd) MP [Elfyn Llwyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elfyn_Llwyd), to examine the practicality of Welsh independence and how a referendum might be held.[^cite_note-150] The commission published its report in September 2020, recommending that an independent Wales seek EU membership, explore a confederal relationship with England and Scotland, and hold an initial multi-option referendum to gauge public opinion.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-\:0-152] The report was criticised by the [Welsh Liberal Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Liberal_Democrats) as a mix of "fanatical politics" and "pie in the sky economics".[^cite_note-153] In December 2020, Price pledged that a Plaid government would hold an independence referendum in its first term if the party won a majority at the [2021 Senedd election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Senedd_election).[^cite_note-154]

#### Sixth Senedd (2021–2026)

In the run-up to the [2021 Senedd election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Senedd_election), polling suggested that Welsh Labour would win the highest number of seats but fall short of an overall majority. Pollsters and commentators suggested that the most likely outcome would be another Labour–Plaid Cymru coalition,[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-coalition-156] an option First Minister [Mark Drakeford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Drakeford) said he would be open to.[^cite_note-157] Price insisted that his party would not be Labour's "junior partner",[^cite_note-158] nor would they work with the Conservatives under any circumstances.[^cite_note-159] He stated that Plaid would be willing to join forces with Labour, but only if the former were the largest party or if it were an equal partnership.[^cite_note-coalition-156] Price also said that he did not consider [Welsh independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_independence) to be "a distraction or a constitutional abstraction", but rather "a practical necessity".[^cite_note-160]

At the election, Plaid increased their seat total to thirteen, up one from the twelve they won in 2016, but lost out in their target constituencies, and lost [Rhondda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhondda_\(Senedd_constituency\)) where former leader Leanne Wood lost her seat to Labour.[^cite_note-161] Price said he would not resign, telling [ITV Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Wales): "My job is to lead, its not to give up at a set back or disappointment. My job is to sustain the hope – all those young people who voted for Plaid because they were inspired by our message of the potential we believe is there in Wales to deliver a decent society for our people. I firmly believe that we have sown a lot of seed at this election. A lot of young people in particular who did come with us this time has laid the foundations for the future which I think will set us up for growth in the years to come."[^cite_note-162]

On 22 November 2021, despite Price's earlier comments about refusing to work with Labour,[^cite_note-163] the two parties announced a [co-operation agreement consisting of almost 50 different policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Welsh_Labour%E2%80%93Plaid_Cymru_agreement), including providing free school meals for all primary school children, the establishment of a free-at-point-of-need national care system and building a railway between [North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Wales) and [South Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Wales).[^cite_note-164] Price called the agreement "a down-payment on independence" and claimed that the results of the Senedd election "confirmed Wales's status as an indy-curious nation. A curiosity that will give birth – sooner than many think – to an independent Wales." He went on to say, "For Wales to be free, we must first be united. And, that is what this Co-operation Agreement sets out to achieve. It launches us on a pathway to a united Wales, one that, sooner than we perhaps think, will find it both comfortable and natural, indeed essential, to join the world community of normal, independent nations."[^cite_note-165]

The co-operation agreement was ratified by Plaid's conference, with 94% voting in favour. "This is a huge step forward for Wales and our democracy," Price said. "The co-operation agreement will bring immediate, tangible and long-term benefit for the people of Wales. All primary school children will now receive free school meals; there will be free childcare for all two-year-olds; and radical action to tackle the housing crisis. There will be stability payments to support family farms; exploration of an accelerated pathway to net zero by 2035; the creation of [Ynni Cymru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ynni_Cymru) – a company to expand community-owned renewable energy generation; and a new and reformed Senedd – bigger, more diverse, and gender balanced in law. From feeding our children to caring for our elderly, this is a nation-building Programme for Government which will change the lives of thousands of people the length and breadth of our country for the better. And none of it would be happening without Plaid Cymru."[^cite_note-166]

In May 2023, the publication of a report which detailed failings by the party to prevent sexual harassment and bullying led to media coverage suggesting that Price had agreed to resign the party leadership,[^cite_note-\:6-38][^cite_note-167] and Price confirmed this in a statement on 10 May. Acknowledging that he "no longer had the united support of \[his] colleagues", Price stated that he would step down officially at the start of the following week, once the process for electing a new interim leader was finalised.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169] On 11 May Plaid Cymru announced that [Llyr Gruffydd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llyr_Gruffydd), Senedd member for North Wales, would replace him as interim party leader, and that this would be confirmed by the NEC on 13 May.[^cite_note-170] On 16 June 2023 [Rhun ap Iorwerth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhun_ap_Iorwerth) was announced as the new permanent leader after he was elected unopposed.[^cite_note-\:7-39]

At party conference in [Aberystwyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberystwyth) in October 2023, Rhun ap Iorwerth said the party was "not just for [Welsh speakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_language)".[^cite_note-171] Accomplishments like free school meals with the [Drakeford government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Drakeford_government) were promoted.[^cite_note-172]

In the [2024 UK general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_Kingdom_general_election) Plaid Cymru managed to get its best percentage of the vote share in Wales for a General Election and win two of its targets seats in [Caerfyrddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caerfyrddin_\(constituency\)) and [Ynys Môn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ynys_M%C3%B4n_\(UK_Parliament_constituency\)).[^cite_note-173] On 23 October 2025 Plaid Cymru won the [Caerphilly by-election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Caerphilly_by-election), [Laura McAllister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_McAllister) suggested that the by-election was a sign of the 'fundamental realignment of Welsh politics' and that Labour could soon lose its 'hegemony' in Wales.[^cite_note-174]

[Opinion polls before the next Senedd election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_2026_Senedd_election) in 2026 suggested Plaid Cymru winning the most number of seats.[^cite_note-175] Rhun ap Iorwerth in December 2025 suggested that Plaid was the local equivalent of [Zohran Mamdani](64618347.md), and a viable alternative to Welsh Labour.[^cite_note-176]

#### Seventh Senedd (2026–)

Plaid Cymru had their best result in the [2026 Senedd election](68245236.md), winning 43 of the 96 seats and 35.4% of the vote.[^cite_note-177] They went on to form the [Welsh Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government) as a [minority government](271434.md), with Rhun ap Iorwerth as the first Plaid Cymru [First Minister](602076.md).&#x20;

On 14 September 2026 Rhun ap Iorwerth the leader of Plaid Cymru signed the [Cardiff Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Cardiff_Agreement) alongside the [John Swinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Swinney) leader of the [Scottish National Party](28887.md) (SNP) and [Michelle O’Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_O'Neill) and [Mary Lou McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Lou_McDonald) the Vice President and President of [Sinn Féin](28175.md) setting out a shared commitment to independence from the [United Kingdom](31717.md) for Wales and [Scotland](26994.md), alongside [reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Ireland) for [Northern Ireland](21265.md) with the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md).[^cite_note-178][^cite_note-179][^cite_note-\:22-180][^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183][^cite_note-184]

## European Free Alliance

Plaid retains strong links with the [Scottish National Party](28887.md) (SNP),[^cite_note-213] with both parties' MPs co-operating closely with one another.[^cite_note-214] They work as a single parliamentary group within [Westminster](13964.md),[^cite_note-215] and were involved in joint campaigning, under the banner of a "Celtic alliance", during the [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_United_Kingdom_general_election) and [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_Kingdom_general_election) general elections.[^cite_note-216][^cite_note-217] Both Plaid and the SNP, along with [Mebyon Kernow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mebyon_Kernow) of [Cornwall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornwall),[^cite_note-218] are members of the [European Free Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Free_Alliance) (EFA), a [European political party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_political_party) for [regionalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionalism_\(politics\)), [autonomist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomism_\(political_doctrine\)) and pro-independence political parties across Europe. The EFA co-operates with the larger [European Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Green_Party) to form [The Greens–European Free Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Greens%E2%80%93European_Free_Alliance) (Greens/EFA) [political group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_groups_of_the_European_Parliament) in the [European Parliament](9581.md), although the UK is no longer a member of the European Union.

## Sections

Plaid Cymru has several official sections and affiliated groups, with the aim of representing all parts of wider society. The official sections of Plaid[^cite_note-219] are as follows:

- Plaid Anabledd - Section for the Disabled & Neurodivergent members of the party.

- Plaid BME - Section for the BME members of the party.

- Councillors' Association - Section for both elected councillors' and encouraging members to stand as a councillor for Plaid Cymru.

- [Plaid Ifanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaid_Ifanc) - Section for youth and student members of the party.

- Merched Plaid - Section for women members of the party.

- Plaid Pride - Section for LGBTQIA+, and ally members of the party.

- Undeb - Plaid Cymru's network of trade union activists.
