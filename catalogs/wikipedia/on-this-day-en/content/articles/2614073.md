**El Alto International Airport** (Spanish: *Aeropuerto Internacional El Alto*) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **LPB**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **SLLP**) is an [international airport](1519705.md) serving [La Paz](42821.md), [Bolivia](3462.md). It is located in the city of [El Alto](3475756.md),[^cite_note-2] 13 kilometres (8 mi) west of La Paz.[^cite_note-3] At an elevation of 4,061.5 metres (13,325 ft), it is the highest international airport in the world, the seventh [highest commercial airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest_commercial_airports) in the world and the highest commercial airport outside of [China](5405.md).[^cite_note-4]

The airport has been in service since the first half of the 20th century, but was modernized in the late 1960s, when its runway was lengthened and a new passenger terminal with modern facilities was built. The new airport was inaugurated in 1965.[^cite_note-5] El Alto airport was a primary hub for the former [Lloyd Aéreo Boliviano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_A%C3%A9reo_Boliviano), Bolivia's [flag carrier](465816.md) which ceased operations in 2007. It serves also as a [focus city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Focus_city) for [Boliviana de Aviación](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boliviana_de_Aviaci%C3%B3n), Bolivia's flag carrier and state-owned airline.[^cite_note-6]

## History



The airport during the 1960s. A [LAB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lloyd_A%C3%A9reo_Boliviano) [Boeing 727](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727) is seen in the foreground with the old terminal and its iconic [Tiwanakotan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiwanaku) façade in the background.



Old terminal

Bolivia is characterized by an extremely varied terrain including rugged mountains, high plateaus, low valleys and tropical forests; this topography has had a negative effect on national transportation, making communication difficult between elevated and low cities along with isolated tropical towns. Aviation is an important means of access for isolated communities in this environment.

Bolivian air transport started in 1916, when the Military School of Aviation (Spanish: *Escuela Militar de Aviación*) was formed in La Paz. In 1929, the local La Paz Government first planned the construction of an airport; however, it was not officially inaugurated until the 1960s. In 1974, the airport was reconstructed, a new terminal was built, and its facilities were enlarged and modernized.[^cite_note-7]

Until 1999, the airport had **John F. Kennedy** (JFK) as its official name, although in practice this name was never publicly used. In that year, before the pressure of different sectors, by means of the Law 1944 during the government of [Hugo Banzer Suárez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Banzer_Su%C3%A1rez), the airport changed its name officially to **El Alto International Airport**.

### Modernization

In 2006, SABSA (Spanish: *Servicios Aeroportuarios Bolivianos S.A.*) invested nearly 2.3 million dollars in the reconstruction of the main terminal.[^cite_note-8] The new terminal consists of the enlargement, reshaping and construction of the baggage claim room, the check-in area and the corridor to the air bridges. The administrative offices of AASANA as well as the main hall and the international area were completely modernized.[^cite_note-9]

## Characteristics



At more than 4,000 m (13,000 ft) above sea level, El Alto is the highest international airport in the world

The new terminal includes various shops offering Bolivian handicrafts as well as bookstores and [duty-free shops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duty-free_shop). There are also restaurants and cafes in the main lobby and in both domestic and international departure areas.

The airport has two runways: The main one "10R/28L" has a concrete surface and is 4,000 metres (13,123 ft) long, allowing large aircraft operations at higher altitudes. A second runway, "10L/28R" is located parallel to the main runway and has a grass surface. The airport is equipped with [VOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VHF_omnidirectional_range)/[DME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distance_measuring_equipment), DVOR/DME navigation systems, as well as [ILS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_landing_system) CAT I approach systems.

El Alto is the world's highest international airport, located at 4,062 metres (13,327 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level). The average temperature at the airport is 6 °C (43 °F). Because of the thin high altitude air, most commercial [wide-body aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wide-body_aircraft) such as the [Boeing 747](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747) and [Airbus A330](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A330) cannot operate at full load out of El Alto International Airport. As a result, much of the international traffic to and from Bolivia operates out of [Viru Viru International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viru_Viru_International_Airport) in [Santa Cruz de la Sierra](673987.md) which is located at a much lower elevation, allowing heavy aircraft operations.

### Flight testing

As one of the world's highest-elevation airports, El Alto offers features taken advantage of by aircraft manufacturers such as [Airbus](26220236.md), [Bombardier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Aviation), and [Boeing](18933266.md) to test high-altitude takeoff and landing. Modern aircraft such as the [Airbus A350 XWB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A350_XWB), [Airbus A330neo](43315216.md) and [Boeing 787 Dreamliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner) have used El Alto to perform flight tests for their certification processes.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## Operators

From 1997, the airport was managed by [TBI plc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBI_plc) which owned concessions of the three biggest airports in the country: El Alto International Airport, [Jorge Wilstermann International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Wilstermann_International_Airport) and [Viru Viru International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viru_Viru_International_Airport) through its subsidiary **Servicio de Aeropuertos Bolivianos S.A.** (SABSA). In 1999 Airport Group International was purchased by [TBI plc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TBI_plc). In 2004, the company was acquired by the Spanish conglomerate [Abertis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abertis), hence taking ownership of SABSA. In February 2013, the [Government of Bolivia](69013.md) announced the [nationalization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) of SABSA, taking full ownership and operations of Bolivia's main international gateways.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

SABSA has been substituted in March 2022 by the newly established government agency **[Navegación Aérea y Aeropuertos Bolivianos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navegaci%C3%B3n_A%C3%A9rea_y_Aeropuertos_Bolivianos)** (NAABOL).[^cite_note-15]

## Accidents and incidents

- On 14 July 1970, [Douglas DC-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_DC-3) TAM-17 of [TAM – Transporte Aéreo Militar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TAM_%E2%80%93_Transporte_A%C3%A9reo_Militar) was damaged beyond repair in an accident.

  [^cite_note-ASN140770-30]

- On 4 May 1971, [Douglas C-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-47) TAM-22 of TAM – Transporte Aéreo Militar crashed shortly after take-off on a cargo flight to El Jovi Airport.

  [^cite_note-ASN040571-31]

- On 17 September 1972, Douglas C-47A CP-565 of Aerolíneas Abaroa crashed on take-off. The aircraft was operating a non-scheduled passenger flight. All four people on board survived.

  [^cite_note-ASN170972-32]

- On 18 August 1974, a C-141 Starlifter [impacted a mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled_flight_into_terrain) during approach. All seven crew members were killed.

  [^cite_note-33]

- On 25 November 1976, Douglas C-47 CP-755 of Aerolíneas La Paz was damaged beyond economic repair in a landing accident. The aircraft was on a cargo flight, all four people on board survived.

  [^cite_note-ASN251176-34]

- On 1 January 1985, [Eastern Air Lines Flight 980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Air_Lines_Flight_980) from Asuncion hit Mount [Illimani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illimani) during its descent towards El Alto, killing all 29 people on board the [Boeing 727-225](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_727-225); the flight was scheduled to continue to Lima, Guayaquil, Panama City, Miami, and Chicago.

- On 27 February 2026, a [C-130 Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130_Hercules) aircraft of the [Bolivian Air Force](9386005.md) overshot the airport's runway during landing and [collided](82528353.md) with several vehicles, killing at least 22 people and injuring 29 others.

  [^cite_note-35]
