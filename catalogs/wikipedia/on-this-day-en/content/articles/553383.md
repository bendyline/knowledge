# Moorland

For other uses, see [Moorland (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moorland_\(disambiguation\)).



Extensive moorland in the [Desert of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_of_Wales)

**Moorland** or **moor** is a type of [habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_\(ecology\)) found in [upland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upland_\(geology\)) areas in [temperate grasslands, savannas, and shrublands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_grasslands,_savannas,_and_shrublands) and the [biomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biome) of [montane grasslands and shrublands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montane_grasslands_and_shrublands), characterised by low-growing vegetation on [acidic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_pH) soils. Moorland today generally means uncultivated [hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill) land (such as [Dartmoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmoor) in [South West England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_England)), but also includes low-lying [wetlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetland) (such as [Sedgemoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedgemoor), also South West England). It is closely related to [heath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath), although experts disagree on the exact distinction between these types of vegetation. Generally, moor refers to [highland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_\(geography\)) and high rainfall areas, while heath refers to [lowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowland) zones which are more likely to be the result of human activity.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERackham1986305-1]

Moorland habitats are found mainly in [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropics) [Africa](5334607.md), [northern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Europe) and [western Europe](33800.md), and [South America](26769.md). Most of the world's moorlands are diverse [ecosystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecosystem). In the extensive moorlands of the tropics, [biodiversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity) can be extremely high. Moorland is also related to [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra) (where the [subsoil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsoil) is [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost) or permanently frozen soil),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERackham1986-2] appearing as the tundra and the natural [tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tree) zone. The boundary between tundra and moorland constantly shifts with [climatic change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_\(general_concept\)).

## Heather moorland



Heather moorland on the [North York Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_York_Moors) mainly consisting of *[Calluna vulgaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calluna_vulgaris)*

[Heathland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heath) and moorland are the most extensive areas of semi-natural vegetation in the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles). The moorlands of eastern Britain are similar to heaths, but are distinguished by having a covering of [peat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peat). On western moors, the peat layer can be several metres thick. Scottish "muirs" are generally heather moors, but also have extensive cover of [grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass), [cotton-grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton-grass), [mosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moss), [bracken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bracken) and undergrowth such as [crowberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowberry), with the wetter moorland having [sphagnum moss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphagnum_moss) merging into [bog-land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bog).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERackham1986305-1]

The extent to which human activity was responsible for the creation of moors is a subject with a considerable degree of uncertainty. [Oliver Rackham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Rackham) writes that [pollen analysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollen_analysis) shows that some moorland, such as those on the islands and in the far north of Scotland, are clearly natural, never having had trees,[^cite_note-3] whereas much of the [Pennine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennines) moorland area was forested in [Mesolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic) times.[^cite_note-4] How much the deforestation was caused by climatic change and how much by human activity is uncertain.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERackham1986306–307-5]

## Ecology



Moorland of [Kilimanjaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kilimanjaro)

A variety of different [habitat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_\(ecology\)) types can be found in different world regions of moorland. The wildlife and vegetation forms often lead to high [endemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endemism) due to the chemically harsh soil and [microclimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microclimate) characteristics. An example of this is the [Exmoor Pony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exmoor_Pony), a rare [horse breed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_breed) which has adapted to the harsh conditions in England's [Exmoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exmoor).

In Europe, the associated [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna) consists of bird species such as [red grouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_grouse), [hen harrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hen_harrier), [merlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merlin_\(bird\)), [golden plover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_golden_plover), [curlew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_curlew), [skylark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skylark), [meadow pipit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadow_pipit), [whinchat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whinchat), [ring ouzel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ring_ouzel), and [twite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twite). Other species dominate in moorlands elsewhere. Reptiles are few due to the cooler conditions. In Europe, only the [common viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vipera_berus) is frequent, though in other regions moorlands are commonly home to dozens of [reptile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptile) species. [Amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian) such as [frogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frog) are well represented in moorlands. When moorland is [overgrazed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overgrazing), woody vegetation is often lost, being replaced by coarse, unpalatable [grasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaceae) and [bracken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bracken), with a greatly reduced fauna.

Some hill [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep) breeds, such as the [Scottish Blackface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Blackface) and the [Lonk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonk_\(sheep\)), thrive on the austere conditions of [heather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ericaceae) moors.[^cite_note-6]

## Management

Burning of moorland has been practised for a number of reasons, including when [grazing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grazing) is insufficient to control growth. This is recorded in Britain in the fourteenth century.[^cite_note-7] Uncontrolled burning frequently caused (and causes) problems and was banned by [statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute) in 1609. With the rise of [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep) and [grouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouse) management in the nineteenth century, it again became common practice. Heather is burnt at about 10 or 12 years old when its regenerative capacity is optimal. However, if the stems are left to mature for a longer period, they will tend to burn more aggressively, hindering the process of regeneration. Burning of moorland vegetation needs to be very carefully controlled, as the peat itself is susceptible to combustion, which can be challenging, if not impossible, to extinguish. Moreover, uncontrolled burning of heather can promote the growth of [bracken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bracken) and rough grass, which ultimately leads to a reduction in value of the vegetation for grazing.[^cite_note-8] As a result, burning is now a controversial practice; Rackham calls it "second-best land management".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERackham1986320–321-9]

Mechanical cutting of the heather has been used in Europe, but it is important that the material is removed to avoid choking regrowth. If heather and other vegetation is left standing for too long, a large amount of dry and combustible material will accumulate. This can lead to a [wildfire](56106.md) burning out a large area, although heather seeds have been found to germinate better when exposed to the brief heat of controlled burning.

In terms of managing moorland for wildlife in the UK, vegetation characteristics are important for [passerine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passerine) abundance, while predator control benefits red grouse, golden plover, and curlew abundances. Many management options are needed to benefit multiple species. However, management must be carried out in locations that are also suitable for species in terms of physical characteristics such as topography, climate and soil.[^cite_note-10]

## In literature



Dartmoor in winter covered in snow. Several [tors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tor_\(rock_formation\)) top the sparsely vegetated hills.

In Western cultures, the development of a sensitivity to nature and one's physical surroundings grew with the rise of interest in [landscape painting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landscape_painting), and particularly the works of artists that favoured wide and deep prospects, and rugged scenery.[^cite_note-11] To the English [Romantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanticism) imagination, moorlands fitted this image perfectly, enhancing the emotional impact of the story by placing it within a heightened and evocative landscape. Moorland forms the setting of various works of late Romantic English literature, ranging from the [Yorkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkshire) moorland in [Emily Brontë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Bront%C3%AB)'s *[Wuthering Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuthering_Heights)* and *[The Secret Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Secret_Garden)* by [Frances Hodgson Burnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frances_Hodgson_Burnett) to [Dartmoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmoor) in [Arthur Conan Doyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Conan_Doyle)'s [Holmesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherlock_Holmes) mystery *[The Hound of the Baskervilles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hound_of_the_Baskervilles)*. They are also featured in [Charlotte Brontë](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlotte_Bront%C3%AB)'s *[Jane Eyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane_Eyre)* representing the heroine's desolation and loneliness after leaving [Mr Rochester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr_Rochester).

[Enid Blyton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enid_Blyton)'s *[The Famous Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Famous_Five)* series featured the young protagonists adventuring across various moorlands where they confronted criminals or other individuals of interest. Such a setting enhanced the plot as the drama unfolded away from the functioning world where the children could solve their own problems and face greater danger. Moorland in the [Forest of Bowland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_of_Bowland) in [Lancashire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lancashire) is the setting for Walter Bennett's *[The Pendle Witches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pendle_Witches)*, the true story of some of England's most notorious witch trials. In [Erin Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erin_Hunter)'s *[Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warriors_\(novel_series\))* series, one of the four Clans, WindClan, lives alone on the moor.

[Michael Jecks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Jecks), author of Knights Templar Mysteries, sets his books in and around [Dartmoor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dartmoor), England. [Paul Kingsnorth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Kingsnorth)’s *Beast* is also set on a western English moor, using the barren landscape and fields of heather to convey themes of timelessness and distance from civilisation.

#### Italy

In Italy, moorlands are known as *brughiera*. The term derives from *brugo*, the Italian name for *[Calluna vulgaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calluna)*, one of the dominant species in these habitats. Italian moorlands are often highly fragmented and difficult to map, as they typically occur on poor, acidic or sandy soils and survive today only in relict patches. They are most widespread in [Northern Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy), especially in [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) and [Lombardy](43807.md), but environments with similar ecological features can also be found in parts of [Sicily](27619.md) and [Sardinia](29376.md).

Notable Italian moorland areas include:

- [Parco delle Groane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parco_delle_Groane), [Lombardy](43807.md) – One of the southernmost heathland systems in Europe, characterised by extensive [Calluna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calluna) heath, [Scots pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_sylvestris), and [birch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birch).

- [Riserva naturale della Vauda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riserva_naturale_della_Vauda?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) ([Canavese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canavese)) – A large protected area featuring dry heathland, grasslands, and open woodland mosaics.

- *Magredi*, [Friuli-Venezia Giulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friuli-Venezia_Giulia) – Stony, arid grasslands with heath-like vegetation on alluvial fans at the foothills of the Alps.

- *Gerbido*, [Piedmont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piedmont) – A patchwork of dry heath, xeric grasslands, and poor soils supporting typical *brughiera* vegetation.

- *Vaccinieto* communities in the [Cottian Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cottian_Alps) and in the [Appennino Tosco-Emiliano National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appennino_Tosco-Emiliano_National_Park) – High-altitude heaths dominated by *[Vaccinium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaccinium)* species, ecologically related to moorland environments

#### Brazil

Brazil's "moorland" or high-altitude grassland ecosystems, known as Campos Rupestres (rupestrian grasslands), are biodiverse, rocky, and often misty environments located mostly in the Espinhaço Range of Minas Gerais and Bahia. These unique areas are characterised by nutrient-poor, shallow soils, high levels of biodiversity and endemic species.

Moorlands are upland areas with acidic, low-nutrient and often water-logged soils. In their cold, windy and wet conditions colourful heather plants dominate, growing on the deep peaty layers. These seas of pinks and purples are a haven for many small mammals and insects, but fewer reptiles than on lowland heaths. British moorland may look like wilderness, but it is actually a man-made and highly managed landscape, with regular burning to allow new heather growth. Some 10–15% of the world's moorland is found in the UK, mainly in the north and west of the country.
