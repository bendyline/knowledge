**Bydgoszcz–Toruń metropolitan area** (Polish: *bydgosko-toruński obszar metropolitalny*) is the name of the bi-polar [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) in the middle of the [Vistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula) river centered on the cities of [Bydgoszcz](74593.md) and [Toruń](31258.md) in north-central [Poland](22936.md). The distance between the built-up areas of the cities is about 30 km. They are the administrative capitals and economic centers of [Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship).&#x20;

The cities have traditionally been competitors through the centuries. In September 2004, the Medical Academy in Bydgoszcz joined [Toruń University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_University) as Collegium Medicum in Bydgoszcz. This has usually been considered an important step in the integration process.&#x20;

## History



Bydgoszcz in 1657



Toruń in 1500

Historically, Bydgoszcz and Toruń were the most important [royal cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_city_in_Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) in the area. Both cities hosted sessions of the Polish Parliament, i.e. Bydgoszcz in 1520, and Toruń in 1576 and 1626.[^cite_note-7] It is also where [Peaces of Thorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Thorn_\(disambiguation\)) and [Treaty of Bromberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Bromberg) were signed. The two were parts of different administrative units for centuries, with Bydgoszcz starting as Polish and Toruń as Teutonic settlements, then becoming the biggest cities of [Inowrocław Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inowroc%C5%82aw_Voivodeship) and [Chełmno Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82mno_Voivodeship) in the [Polish-Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish-Lithuanian_Commonwealth), later in Germany of [Bromberg district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bromberg_\(region\)) ([Province of Posen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Posen)) and [Marienwerder district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marienwerder_\(region\)) ([West Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Prussia)).&#x20;

This would change only in the year of 1938, when Bydgoszcz was moved from the Poznań to the Pomeranian Voivodeship with Toruń, soon to take the role of the capital of the whole region.[^cite_note-8]&#x20;

The postwar growth of the cities and the new political administrative landscape led to application of the term "Bydgoszcz-Toruń metropolitan area" which first appeared in the 1960s.[^cite_note-9]&#x20;

## Sights



Old Town of [Toruń](31258.md), [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md)

The main sights of the agglomeration are the old towns of Bydgoszcz and Toruń, with the latter listed as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md). The local [spa town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa_town) is [Ciechocinek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciechocinek), and its [graduation towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciechocinek_graduation_towers), saline and spa parks are declared a [Historic Monument of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Historic_Monuments_\(Poland\)).[^cite_note-10]&#x20;

The metropolitan area is rich in historic architecture ranging from [Romanesque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanesque_architecture_in_Poland) and [Gothic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) to [Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_architecture), [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_architecture) and [Art Nouveau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Nouveau). There are also several castles and palaces, including [Dybów](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dyb%C3%B3w_Castle), [Zamek Bierzgłowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamek_Bierzg%C5%82owski), [Ostromecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaces_and_park_ensemble_in_Ostromecko), [Żołędowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%BBo%C5%82%C4%99dowo,_Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship). The Dybów Castle was the place where in 1454 King [Casimir IV Jagiellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_IV_Jagiellon) issued the famous [Statutes of Nieszawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statutes_of_Nieszawa), covering a set of privileges for the [Polish nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szlachta); an event that is regarded as the birth of the noble democracy in Poland, which lasted until the late-18th-century [Partitions of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland).&#x20;

Bydgoszcz and Toruń host major museums and art galleries, including the [Leon Wyczółkowski Regional Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Museum,_Bydgoszcz) in Bydgoszcz and [District Museum in Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Museum_in_Toru%C5%84). The more unique museums include:&#x20;

- [Copernicus House in Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copernicus_House_in_Toru%C5%84), museum dedicated to [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus)

- European Museum of Money at the former [Polish Royal Mint in Bydgoszcz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bydgoszcz_Mint)

- Museum of [Toruń Gingerbread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toru%C5%84_gingerbread) in Toruń

- Museum of Far Eastern Art in the [Under the Star Tenement House in Toruń](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Under_the_Star_Tenement_House_in_Toru%C5%84)

- Andrzej Szwalbe Collection of Historical Pianos at the palace in [Ostromecko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostromecko), one of two largest such collections in Poland

- [Exploseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploseum) in Bydgoszcz, operated at the site of a former Nazi German arms factory.
