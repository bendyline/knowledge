**Gabriel Boric Font** (Spanish: [\[ɡaˈβɾjel ˈβoɾitʃ ˈfont\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish);[^cite_note-3]; born 11 February 1986) is a Chilean politician who served as [President of Chile](11885300.md) from 2022 to 2026. He previously served two consecutive terms as a member of the [Chamber of Deputies of Chile](716259.md) from 2014 to 2022.

Born in [Punta Arenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_Arenas) to a family of [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Chilean) and [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalans) descent, Boric studied law at the [University of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chile) without completing his degree. He rose to national prominence as a student leader during the [2011–2013 Chilean student protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%932013_Chilean_student_protests), heading the [University of Chile Student Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chile_Student_Federation). First elected to the Chamber of Deputies as an independent in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Chilean_general_election), he was re-elected in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Chilean_general_election) under the [Broad Front coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Front_\(Chilean_political_coalition\)). In 2018, he co-founded the [Social Convergence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Convergence) party. During the [2019 civil unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932021_Chilean_protests), he helped broker the agreement that led to the [2020 constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chilean_national_plebiscite).

In December 2021, Boric won the [presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Chilean_general_election), defeating [José Antonio Kast](55052190.md) in the second round with 55.9% of the vote. Upon taking office on 11 March 2022, he became the youngest president in Chilean history. His cabinet was the first in the [Americas](29833.md) with a female majority. A proposed new constitution supported by his government was [rejected by voters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Chilean_constitutional_referendum) in September 2022.

## Early life

### Family

Gabriel Boric was born in [Punta Arenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_Arenas) in 1986. On his father's side, Boric hails from a [Croatian-Chilean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Chilean) family with roots in [Ugljan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ugljan), an [island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Croatia) off the [Adriatic](23275478.md) coast of [Croatia](5573.md).[^cite_note-4] Despite his ancestors'[^cite_note-5] migration from the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire) to Chile in 1897, Boric maintains connections with his relatives residing in Ugljan.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] His great-grandfather, Juan Boric (Ive Borić Barešić), along with his brother Simón (Šime), arrived in Punta Arenas around 1885, being among the initial ten [Croats](69801.md) to settle in [Magallanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magallanes_Region).[^cite_note-Emolorigen-8] They ventured into the [Tierra del Fuego gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierra_del_Fuego_gold_rush) in the Magallanes region, spending time on the islands south of the [Beagle Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beagle_Channel).[^cite_note-Emolorigen-8] Subsequently, Juan Boric briefly returned to Ugljan to marry, and brought his wife, Natalia Crnosija, back to Magallanes, where ten of their eleven children were born.[^cite_note-Emolorigen-8] Boric's grandfather, Luis Boric Crnosija, born in 1908, was one of them.[^cite_note-Emolorigen-8]

Gabriel Boric's father, Luis Boric Scarpa, is a [chemical engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering) who served as a government employee at [Empresa Nacional del Petróleo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empresa_Nacional_del_Petr%C3%B3leo) for over 40 years.[^cite_note-9] His mother, María Soledad Font Aguilera, is of [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalans) descent.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Gabriel Boric has two brothers, Simón and Tomás.[^cite_note-12] In the [Patagonian](65433.md) region of Magallanes, Boric's granduncle, [Vladimiro Boric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimiro_Boric_Crnosija), became the first bishop of Punta Arenas.[^cite_note-13] Another granduncle, Roque Scarpa Martinich, assumed the role of the first intendant of the Magallanes Region following the [military dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dictatorship_of_Chile_\(1973%E2%80%931990\)). Both Roque Scarpa and Gabriel Boric's father were members of the [Christian Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_Party_\(Chile\)). Yet another granduncle, [Roque Esteban Scarpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roque_Esteban_Scarpa), won the 1980 [Chilean National Prize for Literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_National_Prize_for_Literature), and his granduncle [Vicente Boric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicente_Boric?action=edit\&redlink=1) was also a writer.[^cite_note-\:3-14]

### Education

Boric studied at The British School in his hometown[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] before moving to Santiago in 2004 to attend law school at the University of Chile.[^cite_note-17] He completed his coursework in 2009, coinciding with his appointment as the President of the Law School students' union. Afterward, he focused on preparing for his [final exam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_examination) and fulfilling his mandatory internship. However, he did not pass the test in 2011 and chose not to retake it.[^cite_note-18] Boric did not obtain a [law degree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_degree) and has expressed in interviews that he never intended to pursue a career as a lawyer, instead aspiring to become a writer.[^cite_note-nometitule-2]

During his university years, Boric had the opportunity to work as an assistant to Professor [José Zalaquett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Zalaquett) in his [human rights](13831.md) course.[^cite_note-retiroZalaq-19][^cite_note-ExAntedebes-20] Zalaquett commended Boric for his inclination to question and doubt, as revealed in an interview.[^cite_note-retiroZalaq-19]

## Political career

### Student politics



Boric as President of the [University of Chile Students Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Chile_Student_Federation), 2012

In 1999 and 2000, Boric played an active role in re-establishing the Federation of Secondary School Students of Punta Arenas.[^cite_note-bcn-21] While attending university, he became a member of the political collective [Autonomous Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Left) (*Izquierda Autónoma*), originally known as Autonomous Students (*Estudiantes Autónomos*). In 2008, he served as an advisor to the Students' Union of the Law Department, and in 2009, he assumed the presidency. During his tenure, he led a 44-day protest against the dean, Roberto Nahum.[^cite_note-\:11-22] From 2010 to 2012, Boric represented students as a university senator.[^cite_note-\:11-22]

Boric ran for the presidency of the University of Chile Student Federation (FECh) in the 5–6 December 2011 elections, as part of the *Creando Izquierda* list. He won the election with 30.52% of the votes, defeating [Camila Vallejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camila_Vallejo), who was the incumbent president of the federation and sought re-election as part of the [Communist Youth of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Youth_of_Chile) list.[^cite_note-23] As president of the FECh, Boric played a prominent role in the second phase of the student protests that originated in 2011, emerging as one of the primary spokespersons for the Federation of Chilean Students.[^cite_note-24] In 2012, he was featured on the list of 100 young leaders of Chile, published by *El Sábado* magazine of the newspaper *[El Mercurio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Mercurio)* in collaboration with [Adolfo Ibáñez University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolfo_Ib%C3%A1%C3%B1ez_University).[^cite_note-25]

### Member of Chamber of Deputies (2014–2022)

Official portraits of Gabriel Boric as a deputy

Boric participated in the 2013 parliamentary elections as an independent candidate for District 60, currently District 28, representing the Region of Magallanes and the Chilean Antarctic ([Región de Magallanes y de la Antártica Chilena, in spanish](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regi%C3%B3n_de_Magallanes_y_de_la_Ant%C3%A1rtica_Chilena)). He achieved a significant victory with 15,418 votes (26.2%), the highest number received by any candidate in the region.[^cite_note-diputado-26][^cite_note-27] Notably, Boric's successful election outside of an electoral coalition was widely acknowledged by the media,[^cite_note-28] as it broke through the Chilean binomial election system.[^cite_note-\:12-29][^cite_note-\:13-30][^cite_note-\:14-31][^cite_note-\:15-32] On 11 March 2014, Boric was sworn in as a member of the Chamber of Deputies.[^cite_note-\:12-29][^cite_note-\:13-30][^cite_note-\:14-31][^cite_note-\:15-32]

During his first term, Boric actively served on several commissions, including Human Rights and Indigenous Peoples; Extreme Zones and the Chilean Antarctic; and Labour and Social Security.[^cite_note-bcn-21] He was part of the "student bench" (*bancada estudiantil*), which consisted of other young elected deputies, such as Camila Vallejo, [Giorgio Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Jackson), and [Karol Cariola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karol_Cariola). Together, they played a prominent role in debates concerning the educational reforms proposed by [Michelle Bachelet](986814.md)'s second government.[^cite_note-33] Boric's popularity in Chile was reflected in various opinion polls.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

In 2016, the Autonomous Left disbanded due to disagreements regarding the collective's future. Boric advocated for a more institutional approach and dialogue with Bachelet's center-left government.[^cite_note-36] However, the leadership of the Autonomous Left preferred to focus on student politics. Boric and his allies, driven by a "compulsion for rapid rise,"[^cite_note-ExAntedebes-20] according to Carlos Ruiz of the Autonomous Left, founded the Autonomist Movement. They intended to collaborate with other political forces and establish a new leftist coalition similar to the Uruguayan [Broad Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frente_Amplio_\(Uruguay\)).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-ExAntedebes-20] The Autonomist Movement achieved favorable electoral results, as exemplified by [Jorge Sharp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorge_Sharp), one of Boric's close friends, who was elected mayor of [Valparaíso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valpara%C3%ADso) in the 2016 municipal elections.[^cite_note-\:0-39]

In January 2017, Boric's movement, together with other new parties and collectives including Jackson's [Democratic Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Revolution), launched the Chilean Broad Front (*Frente Amplio*).[^cite_note-40] Boric played a leadership role in [Beatriz Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatriz_S%C3%A1nchez_\(journalist\))'s campaign during the [2017 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Chilean_general_election). After Sánchez placed third and failed to qualify for the second round, Boric reluctantly supported [Alejandro Guillier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Guillier) as a means to defeat [Sebastián Piñera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Pi%C3%B1era), although Piñera ultimately emerged victorious.[^cite_note-41]

In the 2017 general election, Boric sought re-election as an independent candidate supported by the [Humanist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanist_Party_\(Chile\)), one of the founding members of the Broad Front. He received 18,626 votes (32.8%),[^cite_note-\:1-42] an increase compared to 2013, making him the second most voted deputy in the country at that time.[^cite_note-bcn-21] Boric served on the Commissions for Extreme Zones and the Chilean Antarctic, as well as Constitution, Legislation, Justice, and Regulation.[^cite_note-bcn-21]

The Broad Front's commendable performance in the 2017 elections, where it became Chile's third largest political force, prompted the coalition and its members to undergo reorganization.[^cite_note-43] In 2018, the Autonomist Movement, along with the [Libertarian Left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_Left_\(Chile\)) and other smaller movements, decided to merge and establish a political party called Social Convergence.[^cite_note-CNN-CS-44]

In 2019 Comisión de Ética sanctioned Boric for displaying a t-shirt with right-wing senator Jaime Guzmán gunned. As result of this Boric lost 5% of his salary.[^cite_note-45]

### Role in the *Estallido social*

Main article: [2019–2021 Chilean protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%932021_Chilean_protests)

On 18 October 2019, protests against the increased tariffs in the [Santiago transport system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Metropolitana_de_Movilidad) sparked the *[Estallido social](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estallido_social)* ("Social Outburst"), the largest civil unrest in the country since the end of the military dictatorship. As riots erupted in various parts of the capital, President Piñera declared a [state of emergency](298608.md) in Santiago, which was later extended to all major cities as the protests escalated. The protesters incorporated demands concerning the high [cost of living](522934.md), [corruption](3608404.md), and [inequality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_inequality), among other issues.[^cite_note-aljazeera-46][^cite_note-47]

Boric emerged as a vocal critic of the government's response and strongly opposed the use of the Chilean Armed Forces to suppress the protests. He even confronted a group of soldiers deployed in [Plaza Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plaza_Baquedano).[^cite_note-48] Additionally, he played a significant role as one of the accusers in the impeachment trial against Interior Minister [Andrés Chadwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Chadwick). Chadwick was found guilty of serious constitutional violations and a failure to safeguard human rights during the state of emergency, resulting in a five-year ban from public office.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-\:2-51][^cite_note-52] Boric also supported the impeachment of President Piñera, although the attempt was ultimately rejected.[^cite_note-\:2-51]

Despite being a prominent critic of the government's handling of the protests, Boric was willing to engage in dialogue with other political forces to find a solution to the crisis. Conversations between him and right-wing politicians contributed to an agreement that paved the way for the establishment of the [Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Convention_\(Chile\)), tasked with writing a [new Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_proposed_Political_Constitution_of_the_Republic_of_Chile).[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] On 15 November 2019, the "Agreement for Social Peace and the New Constitution" was signed by the presidents of the political parties represented in parliament, excluding the Communist Party and some members of the Broad Front, including Social Convergence. Boric signed the agreement as an individual, which led to accusations against him from some members of his party.[^cite_note-55] As a result, several individuals, including his personal friend Jorge Sharp,[^cite_note-\:0-39] resigned from the party. Other parties such as the [Green Ecologist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Ecologist_Party_\(Chile\)), the [Humanist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humanist_Party_\(Chile\)), the [Equality Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equality_Party_\(Chile\)), the Pirate Party, and the Libertarian Left also opposed the agreement and left the Broad Front.[^cite_note-56]

On 20 December, Boric faced an attack at [Parque Forestal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parque_Forestal) where individuals threw spit and beer at him while calling him a "traitor" and "sell-out" due to his involvement in the "Agreement for Social Peace and a New Constitution" reached with traditional politicians. Despite the provocation, Boric remained composed and did not leave his position.[^cite_note-57]

### 2021 presidential candidacy

Main article: [2021 Chilean general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Chilean_general_election)



Boric presents the signatures required to run as a presidential candidate

During 2020, the conflict between the Chilean government and its citizens escalated due to the impact of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Chile). While the country was under lockdown, civil unrest was temporarily suspended. This situation, coupled with the shared campaign for the [constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chilean_national_plebiscite), helped to unite the left and center-left opposition, particularly the Broad Front and [Chile Digno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_Digno) ("Chile of Dignity"), an alliance led by the Communist Party. In the October 2020 referendum, 78% of voters supported the idea of drafting a new Constitution, leading to discussions on how to form a united opposition for the May 2021 elections, which included [mayors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Chilean_municipal_elections), [regional governors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Chilean_gubernatorial_elections), and [members of the Constitutional Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Chilean_Constitutional_Convention_election).

Gabriel Boric, a key figure, advocated for coordination among all parties and the formation of fewer electoral lists to prevent voter dispersion.[^cite_note-58] Eventually, the Broad Front and Chile Digno reached an agreement and presented a joint list called Apruebo Dignidad ("I Approve of Dignity"), which became the second largest bloc in the Constitutional Convention, behind the united pact of the right called [Vamos por Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vamos_por_Chile) ("Let's Go for Chile"). Apruebo Dignidad also saw increased support in local and regional elections, positioning itself as a competitive option for the general election in November 2021.

[Daniel Jadue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Jadue), the Communist mayor of [Recoleta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recoleta,_Chile), was initially the favored candidate to represent the left in the presidential election, according to preliminary [opinion polls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opinion_polling_for_the_2021_Chilean_presidential_election).[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] The Broad Front initially supported Beatriz Sánchez, their former presidential candidate, to run again, but she declined and opted to run for the Constitutional Convention instead.[^cite_note-61] With their main candidate out of the race, the Broad Front searched for alternatives, but most of their candidates lacked popularity or did not meet the minimum age requirement for presidential candidates.[^cite_note-62]&#x20;



Boric in the celebrations after his victory in the [2021 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Chilean_general_election)

Eventually, Gabriel Boric, who was 35 years old at the time, emerged as an option to participate in a primary election against Jadue. However, Boric's party, Social Convergence, did not have the minimum number of members required to present a presidential candidate. In a remarkable turn of events, a campaign was quickly organized, allowing Boric to gather the necessary number of signatures just one day before the deadline.[^cite_note-63]

Contrary to expectations, Boric won the Apruebo Dignidad [primary election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Chilean_presidential_primaries) on 19 July 2021, receiving 1,059,060 votes (60.4%), while Jadue garnered 39.6%. Boric also emerged as the most voted candidate in the general primary, surpassing all candidates from the [Chile Vamos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chile_Vamos) coalition, whose primary was held simultaneously.[^cite_note-64] Following his primary victory, Boric announced on [Twitter](9988187.md) that he would collaborate with Jadue during the general election to present a united front.[^cite_note-65]

Initially, opinion polls indicated that Boric and [Sebastián Sichel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Sichel), the Chile Vamos candidate, were the frontrunners for the presidential election. However, Sichel's popularity declined in the following months, and he was overtaken in the polls by far-right candidate José Antonio Kast. In the first round of the election held on 21 November 2021, Boric obtained 25.82% of the vote, placing second behind Kast's 27.91%, which secured their spots in the second round. On 19 December 2021, Boric emerged victorious in the runoff with 55.85% of the vote.[^cite_note-bbc_2021-66] His inauguration took place on 11 March 2022.[^cite_note-yahoo_2021-67]

## Presidency (2022–2026)



Gabriel Boric during his inauguration ceremony, on 11 March 2022

### Cabinet



Official photograph of the first cabinet of the Boric government

In January 2022,[^cite_note-aljazeera_cabinet-68] Boric announced his cabinet, which included members from the Apruebo Dignidad and [Democratic Socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Socialism_\(Chile\)) coalitions, as well as independents.[^cite_note-69] Fourteen of the 24 ministers were women, making it the first cabinet in the [Americas](29833.md) with a female majority.[^cite_note-guardian_cabinet-70][^cite_note-73] The cabinet also featured Chile's first openly LGBT ministers, [Alexandra Benado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandra_Benado) and [Marco Antonio Ávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Antonio_%C3%81vila),[^cite_note-74] along with [Maya Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_Fern%C3%A1ndez_Allende), the granddaughter of former president [Salvador Allende](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvador_Allende).[^cite_note-guardian_cabinet-70]

Boric also appointed three fellow former student leaders to his cabinet. [Camila Vallejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camila_Vallejo), who became the [government spokesperson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_General_Secretariat_of_Government_\(Chile\)), [Giorgio Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgio_Jackson), who assumed the role of [secretary-general of the presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_General_Secretariat_of_the_Presidency_\(Chile\)), and [Nicolás Grau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicol%C3%A1s_Grau), who became the [Minister of Economy, Development, and Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy,_Development_and_Tourism_\(Chile\)).[^cite_note-aljazeera_cabinet-68][^cite_note-75]

[Ricardo Ffrench-Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Ffrench-Davis), amidst speculation of being appointed as [Minister of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Finance_\(Chile\)), explained that, at the age of 85, he considered himself too old to fulfill the task but expressed his willingness to provide advice.[^cite_note-76] When [Mario Marcel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Marcel) was announced as the future finance minister in January 2022, the [Santiago Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_Stock_Exchange) reacted positively, with the IPSA rising by 2.35%.[^cite_note-77]

In August 2023, amid low approval ratings, voter concerns over crime and inflation, and a corruption probe focused on graft accusations, Boric reorganized his Cabinet for the third time in one-and-a-half years.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

### Violence against government officials

During the initial months of the Boric government, both the president and individuals associated with his administration have faced threats, violent crimes, and physical aggression. In mid-March, [Minister of Interior and Public Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_the_Interior_and_Public_Security_\(Chile\)) [Izkia Siches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izkia_Siches) encountered gunfire during a visit to [Temucuicui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temucuicui) and had to be evacuated for her safety.[^cite_note-Emol15marz-80] In April, President Boric himself was targeted in an attack by a man who tried to throw a stone at him.[^cite_note-81] On the night of 13 May, one of Boric's bodyguards was kidnapped and shot in the arm by unidentified assailants.[^cite_note-clarin-82] Additionally, on the same day, the residence of [Minister of National Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_National_Defense_\(Chile\)) Maya Fernández was burglarized.[^cite_note-clarin-82] The Chilean police have stated that these latter two incidents are unrelated.[^cite_note-83]

### Mapuche conflict

In May 2022, Boric made the decision to deploy troops to the southern part of the country due to escalating violence in the [Mapuche conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapuche_conflict). According to the Argentine newspaper *[Clarín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clar%C3%ADn_\(Argentine_newspaper\))*, this move caused Boric to lose support from the Communist Party. Previously, Boric had distanced himself from similar measures taken by his predecessor, Piñera.[^cite_note-84] Prior to Boric's decision, Héctor Llaitul, the leader of one of the Mapuche militias, had called for "preparing forces and organizing armed resistance".[^cite_note-85] In response to Llaitul's statement, Boric's government initially dismissed the idea of filing a formal lawsuit against him, stating that the state "does not persecute ideas." Instead, they planned to incorporate these statements into existing complaints rather than initiating new ones.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87]

### Vote on proposed constitution

In September 2022, the Constitutional Convention presented a draft constitution described as progressive. The drafting process spanned from 4 July 2021—during the administration of former President Sebastián Piñera—to 4 July 2022, four months into President Gabriel Boric's term. The proposed constitution was [rejected in a national plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Chilean_constitutional_referendum), with 62% voting against and 38% in favor.[^cite_note-\:10-88][^cite_note-89] Critics described the draft as excessively lengthy, overtly left-leaning, and overly radical.[^cite_note-90]

### Economy

When Boric assumed the presidency, [Chile's inflation rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflation_in_Chile) had reached its highest level in nearly 30 years.[^cite_note-91] In April 2022, Boric announced a \$3.7 billion economic recovery plan, which included a minimum wage hike to alleviate the impact of rising prices.[^cite_note-92] Although year-over-year inflation briefly rose to a high of 14.1% in August 2022, representing the highest level in 28 years, it then decreased dramatically during Boric's leadership, dropping to 5.1% as of September 2023.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

Boric's government reduced the statutory working week to 40 hours, though this reform will be phased in over 5 years. His government also secured free public healthcare for the poorest Chileans, raised the monthly minimum wage to around [CLP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_peso)\$500,000, and passed a mining tax to support underfunded municipalities.[^cite_note-95]

### Foreign relations



Gabriel Boric with Japanese prime minister [Sanae Takaichi](8125532.md) at the [APEC South Korea 2025](78485796.md) summit on 1 November 2025

President Boric condemned the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), stating in a tweet: "Russia has chosen war as a means of resolving conflicts. From Chile, we condemn the invasion of Ukraine, the violation of its sovereignty, and the illegitimate use of force. We stand in solidarity with the victims and will strive for peace through our humble efforts."[^cite_note-96]

Boric's first international trip as president took place in April 2022, with a visit to Argentina, where he met with President [Alberto Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_Fern%C3%A1ndez). During the visit, Boric emphasized the importance of resolving outstanding territorial disputes and fostering a sense of fraternity between the two nations, irrespective of the administrations in power. He also reiterated Chile's support for [Argentina's claim of sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkland_Islands_sovereignty_dispute) over the [Falkland Islands](7550182.md).[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

On 15 September 2022, Boric declined to receive the credentials of Israeli Ambassador Gil Artzyeli in protest of child casualties during the [2022 Gaza–Israel clashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Gaza%E2%80%93Israel_clashes). The decision, made when Artzyeli had already arrived at Chile's presidential palace, triggered a diplomatic crisis between Chile and [Israel](9282173.md).[^cite_note-\:9-100] In response, the [Jewish Community of Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_Community_of_Chile) criticized Boric's actions,[^cite_note-\:9-100] while Chilean [Foreign Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Chile\)) [Antonia Urrejola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonia_Urrejola) apologized to Israel and rescheduled Artzyeli's credential ceremony for later that month.[^cite_note-\:9-100] Boric, however, refrained from issuing a personal apology, stating that no individual in Chile would face persecution or intimidation for their beliefs unless they contravened the law.[^cite_note-\:9-100]

### Reparations related to the 2019–2020 protests

See also: [Eye injuries in the 2019–2020 Chilean protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eye_injuries_in_the_2019%E2%80%932020_Chilean_protests) and [Prisoners of the 2019–2021 Chilean protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoners_of_the_2019%E2%80%932021_Chilean_protests)

The government of Gabriel Boric has established grace pensions of up to 515,672 [Chilean pesos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_peso) for individuals who endured human rights violations during the 2019–2020 protests.[^cite_note-Pensionlate-107] Those who suffered such violations between 18 October 2019, and 30 June 2020, are eligible for these pensions.[^cite_note-Pensionlate-107] However, the National Institute of Human Rights (INDH) has raised concerns about the uncertainty regarding the compatibility of these pensions with disability pensions.[^cite_note-Pensionlate-107] Additionally, some members of Congress have expressed concerns that grace pensions have been granted to individuals whose trials have not yet concluded, thereby leaving the possibility that their injuries were caused by severe acts of violence unresolved.[^cite_note-108]

### Chile Abortion Rights Bill (2025)

In May 2025, the Chilean government introduced a bill to decriminalize abortion up to 14 weeks of pregnancy for any reason, fulfilling a campaign promise by President Gabriel Boric. The proposal, led by Minister of Women and Gender Equity Antonia Orellana, aims to expand reproductive rights and reduce unsafe abortions. Currently, abortion is only permitted under three circumstances: risk to the mother's life, non-viability of the fetus, or rape.[^cite_note-109]

The bill faces strong opposition from conservative and religious groups, and legislative leaders have stated they will not prioritize its debate. As of June 2025, there is no scheduled date for a parliamentary vote, and the bill's future is uncertain due to limited support in Congress and the [2025 Chilean general election](74379670.md).[^cite_note-110]

### Approval ratings

See also: [List of heads of the executive by approval rating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heads_of_the_executive_by_approval_rating)



Boric took office with a 50% approval rating.[^cite_note-111] After his first 100 days, his approval rating plummeted to 32.8%, marking the most significant decline in popularity for a Chilean President during their initial 100 days since 1990.[^cite_note-\:16-112] By the end of 2022, his approval rating remained persistently low, hovering around 33%,[^cite_note-\:16-112] while nearly six out of ten people expressed disapproval of Boric's performance. Factors such as increasing crime, economic challenges, and instability within his Cabinet contributed to the decline in public support. Consequently, Boric's approval rating dropped even further to 25% by January 2023.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] By May 2023, Boric's approval rating among the public was 28% and his disapproval rating was at 66%.[^cite_note-115] Following a State of the Nation address in early June 2023, Boric's approval rating rose from 31% to 41%, improving across all population segments;[^cite_note-116] due to the [Democracia Viva case](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democracia_Viva_case?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-117] it fell back to 28% in late June according to weekly polls by Cadem.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] In August 2023, Boric's approval rating hovered around 30%, with still a majority of disapproval.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121]

By May 2025, Boric's approval rating declined to its lowest level of his presidency, falling to 27% approval.[^cite_note-122]

### Harassment allegations

On 26 November 2024, prosecutors announced a criminal case against Boric after a woman made allegations of sexual harassment that occurred in 2013 and 2014 when he was an intern in [Punta Arenas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta_Arenas). Boric denied the charges and said that he was being sent explicit messages by the complainant through email at the time.[^cite_note-123]

President [Sebastián Piñera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Pi%C3%B1era) receives President-elect Boric in [La Moneda Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Moneda_Palace) after his election, December 2021

Boric is a [left-wing](18499.md) politician,[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] who has been associated with various positions, including [socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism)[^cite_note-129] and [social democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_democracy).[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] But his advisor is Guillermo Tellier, President of the Communist Party. Important ministries such as the Interior, Government Spokesperson, and Education are in the hands of Communists. The Broad Front (FA) party was created imitating the Broad Front of the Spaniard Pablo Iglesias, Spanish far left and friend of Gabriel Boric |url=<https://www.dw.com/es/gabriel-boric-el-joven-que-promete-llevar-a-chile-hacia-la-socialdemocracia/a-60146291> [libertarian socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libertarian_socialism).[^cite_note-132] In this regard, Boric has said, "I come from the Chilean libertarian socialist tradition, that is my ideological space of reference. I am a democrat, and I believe that democracy has to change and adapt and not petrify."[^cite_note-\:8-133] He has expressed some "ideological proximity" with [Álvaro García Linera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81lvaro_Garc%C3%ADa_Linera), the former vice president of Bolivia.[^cite_note-134]

Boric has been described by *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* as "[woke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woke)" and as part of the [millennial left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennials), "with a program focused on social justice, human rights, the environment and feminism".[^cite_note-135] Both [Cristián Warnken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristi%C3%A1n_Warnken) and [Carlos Peña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Pe%C3%B1a_Gonz%C3%A1lez) characterize Boric as a "[postmodern leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodernism)".[^cite_note-Pauta-136] Peña credits Boric and the Broad Front for successfully uniting diverse demands that have emerged as Chilean society has modernized.[^cite_note-Pauta-136]

Boric has been critical of the [social and economic model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle_of_Chile) established in Chile during the dictatorship, arguing that it has continued even after the [transition to democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilean_transition_to_democracy). During the 2021 election, he pledged to dismantle the country's [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberal) economic model, stating that, "if Chile was the cradle of neoliberalism, it will also be its grave."[^cite_note-20DEC2021_BBC-137][^cite_note-138] Boric has said "there were successes and things that didn't go well."[^cite_note-139]

After the failure of the 2022 constitutional proposal, Boric adopted a more moderate position, which drew criticism from his initial supporters who felt he was trying to appease right-wing groups disconnected from the average Chilean.[^cite_note-\:32-140] Throughout his presidential campaign, Boric supported the recognition of [LGBT rights in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_Chile),[^cite_note-141] advocating for legal recognition of [non-binary identities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-binary) and the expansion of [gender identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_identity) laws.[^cite_note-142]

In 2016, Boric defended a program to put 400 convicts in Valparaíso on [probation](406786.md) due to overcrowding in Chilean prisons. He criticized what he saw as [penal populism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penal_populism) and opposed other deputies who were against probation measures.[^cite_note-143] During a debate in the 2021 Apruebo Dignidad primaries, Boric once again criticized the shortcomings of penal populism in providing effective solutions.[^cite_note-144]

Boric has been critical of [pine and eucalyptus plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry_in_Chile), which he believes have contributed to [drought](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drought_in_Chile) among indigenous Mapuche communities in "[Wallmapu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallmapu)."[^cite_note-forestalBoric1-145] He pledged in 2021 to impose restrictions on large forestry companies during his presidency.[^cite_note-forestalBoric1-145] In a May 2022 interview on [Televisión Nacional de Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Televisi%C3%B3n_Nacional_de_Chile), Boric reiterated his concerns about the forestry industry.[^cite_note-146]

In a 2023 interview, Boric argued that a part of him wants to overthrow [capitalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitalism) and expressed his belief that capitalism "is not the best way to solve our problems in society." He described his view on the feasibility of overthrowing capitalism, stating, "I don't think it can be overthrown without further ado if an alternative is not proposed that is viable and that is better for the people. One of the things that I have learned in office, not only in office, it is something obvious, but now it is as clear as crystal, is that you cannot re-found a country. All changes that last over time must be progressive and must be with strong majorities. And you have to build those strong majorities and those strong majorities are not easy to build."[^cite_note-147]

### Economic policy

Boric has criticized the *Crédito con Aval del Estado* (CAE), a [student loan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_loan) program created during [Ricardo Lagos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Lagos)'s government.[^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149] Throughout his political career, Boric has consistently emphasized that education should be a right and not a profit-driven endeavor. He has pledged to forgive student loans and put an end to the program if elected as president.[^cite_note-150] Additionally, Boric has advocated for reducing private involvement in critical sectors.

Concerning the healthcare system, Boric has called for the establishment of a universal [publicly funded healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Publicly_funded_health_care) system, citing the British [National Health Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Health_Service) (NHS) as an example.[^cite_note-151] He has also advocated for the abolition of the [AFP pension system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pensions_in_Chile), proposing a public autonomous entity to administer pension funds instead.[^cite_note-\:4-152] Boric has further supported a law to implement a [40-hour working week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-hour_day) and increase the [minimum wage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minimum_wage).[^cite_note-\:4-152] Additionally, he has proposed the inclusion of workers' representatives and gender equality in the composition of boards of large companies.[^cite_note-153]

Given that [mining](22025047.md) is Chile's largest industry, Boric has put forth proposals such as creating a state-owned company for [lithium](17561.md) extraction, increasing the [royalties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royalty_payment) paid by mining companies, and prioritizing environmental protection.[^cite_note-\:5-154] Addressing the impact of [climate change](5042951.md) and promoting a [green economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_economy) are key pillars of Boric's presidency.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-\:5-154]

On 8 March 2023, Gabriel Boric suffered a setback: by just one vote, the Chamber of Deputies refused to open a debate on the major tax reform he had promised during his presidential campaign to finance his social reforms. Following this setback, he had to wait a year before being able to present a similar text again. Presented to deputies in July 2022, it included a tax on large estates, a tax on the mining sector, and the reinforcement of the fight against fraud. This setback jeopardizes the pension reform project, which was intended to introduce a pay-as-you-go pension system, whereas the current system is privately funded, and to raise the value of the basic solidarity pension for elderly people who are not entitled to any pension at all.[^cite_note-156]

Boric stated that the democratic left should not uphold a double standard when it comes to human rights or employ the [principle of self-determination](29269.md) to justify violations of human rights. He believes that, "just as the left must condemn the violation of [human rights in Chile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Chile) during the dictatorship and also today, the [soft coups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_coup) [in Brazil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_of_Dilma_Rousseff), [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Honduran_constitutional_crisis) [and Paraguay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impeachment_of_Fernando_Lugo), the [Israeli-occupied territories](2781576.md), or the [interventionism of the United States](37258993.md), we must from the left with the same force condemn the permanent restriction of freedoms in Cuba, the repressive government of [Ortega](8778.md) in Nicaragua, the dictatorship in China and the weakening of the basic conditions of democracy in Venezuela".[^cite_note-157]

After assuming the presidency, Boric asserted that Venezuela serves as a failed example, with the six million Venezuelans in diaspora being a significant demonstration of this failure.[^cite_note-158] During his presidential campaign in 2021, he labeled the [Nicaraguan general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Nicaraguan_general_election) as fraudulent and called upon the Communist Party of Chile, one of his allies, to retract its initial statement supporting [Daniel Ortega](8778.md)'s government.[^cite_note-159]

Boric criticized Brazilian President [Jair Bolsonaro](46603496.md) and Bolsonaro's stance regarding the crimes committed during Brazil's [military dictatorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_dictatorship_in_Brazil) from 1964 to 1985. He referred to Bolsonaro as "a danger to the environment and humanity."[^cite_note-Tercera24Bolsonaro-160] In response, Bolsonaro showed a cold attitude towards Boric since his election in December 2021,[^cite_note-Tercera24Bolsonaro-160] announcing in January 2022 that he would not attend Boric's inauguration as president.[^cite_note-161]

Regarding [Bolivia](3462.md), Boric expressed his intention to re-establish [diplomatic relations between Chile and Bolivia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolivia%E2%80%93Chile_relations), which were severed in 1978.[^cite_note-162] He voiced support for Argentina's position on the Falkland Islands and extended sympathy towards the government of Alberto Fernández. Boric pledged to assist Argentina during its [debt restructuring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_debt_restructuring) process and negotiations with the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md).[^cite_note-163]

Boric stated that if [Lula da Silva](99328.md) and [Gustavo Petro](13391962.md) were to win the presidential elections in their respective countries, an "interesting axis" could be formed.[^cite_note-\:8-133] He strongly condemned the 2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine as an "unacceptable war of aggression."[^cite_note-164] During his presidency, Chile supported [United Nations resolutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_resolutions) demanding the immediate withdrawal of [Russian](25391.md) troops from [Ukraine](31750.md)[^cite_note-165] and offered assistance to the Ukrainian government in clearing [landmines](18172.md) left by Russian forces.[^cite_note-Ukraine_FT_article-166] Boric declined requests to supply Ukraine with weapons. The [United States](3434750.md) offered to replace any equipment donated by the [Chilean Armed Forces](5497.md) to support the Ukrainian armed forces, an offer which Boric rejected.[^cite_note-Ukraine_FT_article-166]

With regard to the [Israeli occupied territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_occupied_territories), Boric has expressed support for the [State of Palestine](241405.md) on multiple occasions. In 2019, after receiving a gift from the Jewish Community of Chile, he called for Israel to return the occupied Palestinian territories in a tweet.[^cite_note-167] He described Israel as a "genocidal and murderous state" that violates international treaties, stressing the importance of defending international principles and human rights regardless of a country's power.[^cite_note-168] Boric rejected accusations of [antisemitism](1078.md), asserting his opposition to all forms of discrimination. He considers the Israeli occupation of territories beyond the [1967 borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Israel\)) as a violation of international law.[^cite_note-169] In October 2021, Boric and other deputies introduced a bill to prohibit the import of products originating from [Israeli settlements](15123.md), which are considered illegal by the international community.[^cite_note-170] In October 2023, Boric stated that Israel's attacks in the [Gaza war](74998519.md) "primarily" affected unarmed civilians, and were a potential [war crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_crimes_in_the_2023_Israel%E2%80%93Hamas_war).[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

## Personal life

Boric has been vocal about mental health issues and his personal struggles with [obsessive–compulsive disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsessive%E2%80%93compulsive_disorder), a condition he was diagnosed with as a child. In 2018, he took a leave of absence from Congress after being hospitalized due to his condition.[^cite_note-173][^cite_note-174] The improvement of mental health services, particularly in the [aftermath of the COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health_during_the_COVID-19_pandemic), was one of the central themes of his presidential campaign.[^cite_note-175]

Raised in a devout [Catholic](606848.md) family, with his mother actively involved in the [Schoenstatt Apostolic Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schoenstatt_Apostolic_Movement),[^cite_note-\:3-14] Boric now identifies as agnostic.[^cite_note-176] Between 2019 and 2023, he was in a relationship with anthropologist and sociologist [Irina Karamanos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irina_Karamanos). During Boric's presidential campaign, Karamanos expressed her belief that the role of the [First Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lady_of_Chile) should be reevaluated to better suit modern times.[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178] Karamanos assumed the role of First Lady and worked to dissolve the institutional prerogatives of the role, which happened in December 2022. In November 2023, Boric and Karamanos announced the end of their relationship.[^cite_note-179]



Boric wearing a [Nine Inch Nails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Inch_Nails) hat, 2015

Boric's love for [rock](25423.md) and [metal music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) gained widespread attention during his presidential campaign. He frequently shared posts on social media about some of his favorite bands, such as [Deftones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deftones), [Tool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tool_\(band\)), [Nine Inch Nails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nine_Inch_Nails), and [Rammstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rammstein).[^cite_note-180] However, he has also mentioned enjoying musicians from other genres, such as [Laura Pausini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laura_Pausini), [Taylor Swift](5422144.md) and [Jeongyeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeongyeon).[^cite_note-\:6-181][^cite_note-182] Additionally, Boric is a supporter of the football team [Universidad Católica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Deportivo_Universidad_Cat%C3%B3lica)[^cite_note-183] and a *[Magic: the Gathering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magic:_the_Gathering)* player.[^cite_note-184]

Boric's appearance and style have faced scrutiny since his election as a deputy.[^cite_note-\:7-185] As one of the youngest members of the Chamber of Deputies, he often wore casual attire, including jeans and t-shirts, during sessions of Congress. At one point, he even sported a [mohawk hairstyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohawk_hairstyle) for several months.[^cite_note-\:7-185][^cite_note-\:6-181] In 2014, controversy arose when Boric entered the Chamber without wearing a tie or formal jacket, prompting a public complaint from a right-wing deputy.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187] During his presidential campaign, Boric adopted a more formal look, though he continued to abstain from wearing ties. Notably, he is also the first Latin American head of state to have visible tattoos. The designs on his arms and back represent his home region and include a map of the Magallanes Region, a [lenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nothofagus_pumilio) tree, and a lighthouse.[^cite_note-188]

On 2 December 2024, Boric announced via [Instagram](31591547.md) that he and his partner, [Paula Carrasco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Carrasco) – a member of the Chilean women's national basketball team – were expecting their first child together.[^cite_note-\:172-189] Carrasco has a child from a previous relationship.[^cite_note-\:172-189] In February 2025, Boric revealed that they were expecting a daughter.[^cite_note-190] Their daughter, Violeta Boric Carrasco, was born on 25 June 2025, at the Clinical Hospital of the University of Chile in Santiago. She is the first child born to a sitting Chilean president in 95 years.[^cite_note-191]
