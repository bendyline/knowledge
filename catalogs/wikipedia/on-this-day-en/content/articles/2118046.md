**Bontoc**, officially the **Municipality of Bontoc** ([Bontok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bontok_language): *Ili nan Bóntoc*; [Balangao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balangao_language): *Fabrey hen Funtok*; [Kankanaey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankanaey_language): *Ili di Bóntoc;* [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language): *Ili ti Bóntoc*; [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Bayan ng Bóntoc*; [Spanish](26825.md): *Municipio de Bóntoc*), is a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_the_Philippines) and capital of the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Province) of [Mountain Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province), [Philippines](23440.md). According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 23,466 people.[^cite_note-6]

Bontoc is the historical capital of the entire Cordillera region since the inception of governance in the [Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Administrative_Region). The municipality celebrates the annual Lang-ay Festival.[^cite_note-PIAGovPH-Lang-ay-7]

Bontoc is home to the Indigenous [Bontoc people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bontoc_people). The town also hosts the UNESCO tentatively-listed Alab petroglyphs.

## Etymology

The name **Bontoc** is derived from the morphemes *bun* (heap) and *tuk* (top), collectively meaning "mountains." The term is used to refer to the people of the Mountain Province, the capital municipality, including its cultural practices, and its native language.[^cite_note-8]

## History

Aerial view of Bontoc, 1933

Samuel E. Kane, the American supervisor and then Governor, established the capital here after the [Philippine Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commission) passed the Mountain Province Act in 1908,[^cite_note-9] building a provincial building, hospital, doctor's office, nurse's home, a school, and provincial prison.[^cite_note-Kane-10]<sup>: 281–284 </sup> He also built the [Tagudin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagudin)-Bontoc trail, which by 1926, could accommodate a small car.[^cite_note-Kane-10]<sup>: 329 </sup>

Bontoc was one of several municipalities in Mountain Province which would have been flooded by the [Chico River Dam Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chico_River_Dam_Project) during the [Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) administration, alongside [Bauko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauko), [Sabangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabangan), [Sagada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagada), [Sadanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadanga), and parts of [Barlig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barlig).[^cite_note-Asiaweek1980-11] However, the indigenous peoples of [Kalinga Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalinga_\(province\)) and Mountain Province resisted the project and when hostilities resulted in the murder of [Macli-ing Dulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macli-ing_Dulag), the project became unpopular and was abandoned before Marcos was ousted by the 1986 [People Power Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Power_Revolution).[^cite_note-Doyo2015-12]

In the 2024 census, the population of Bontoc was 23,466 people,[^cite_note-19] with a density of 59 inhabitants per square kilometer or 150 inhabitants per square mile.

### Languages

Most inhabitants speak the [Bontoc language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bontoc_language), with other major languages being [Kankana-ey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kankana-ey_language) and [Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language). Minor languages spoken include [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language), [Pangasinan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pangasinan_language), [Cuyonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyonon_language), and [Butuanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuanon_language).[^cite_note-AnnualReport-2011-20]

## Government

### Local government

Main article: [Sangguniang Bayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangguniang_Bayan)

Bontoc, belonging to the [lone congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province's_at-large_congressional_district) of the province of [Mountain Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province), is governed by a mayor designated as its local chief executive and by a municipal council as its legislative body in accordance with the Local Government Code. The mayor, vice mayor, and the councilors are elected directly by the people through an election which is being held every three years.

## Culture





Bontoc woman with a snake skeleton in hair (a charm against lightning) and Bontoc man, c. 1903, (right)



Bontoc Museum

The highland town of Bontoc is home to two [National Cultural Treasures of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Cultural_Treasures_in_the_Philippines). These are the Stone Agricultural Calendar of Bontoc and *Petroglyphs of Alab*.[^cite_note-GovPH-PD260-32]

The Alab [petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyphs) are ancient figures carved on mountain walls by the prehistoric people of Bontoc.[^cite_note-NationalMuseumGovPH-2010-33] The petroglyphs are the most important ancient rock art carvings in the Cordilleras and the second oldest in the entire country, second only to the Angono petroglyphs of Rizal. Due to its high significance, it was submitted by the [National Commission for Culture and the Arts of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Commission_for_Culture_and_the_Arts_of_the_Philippines) to the [UNESCO](21786641.md) Tentative List of Heritage Sites in 2006, pending its inclusion in the World Heritage List along with the Singanapan charcoal-drawn petrographs of southern Palawan, Angono petroglyphs of Rizal province, charcoal-drawn Peñablanca petrographs of Cagayan, and the Anda red hermatite print petrographs of Bohol.
