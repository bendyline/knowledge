**Pituffik Space Base** ([/biːduːˈfiːk/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [*bee-doo-FEEK*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key);[^cite_note-sn-20230406-4] Greenlandic: [\[pitufːik\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Greenlandic); [IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **THU**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **BGTL**), formerly **Thule Air Base** ([/ˈtuːliː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [*TOO-lee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)), is a [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force) base located on the northwest coast of [Greenland](12118.md) in the [Kingdom of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Denmark) under a defense agreement between Denmark and the [United States](3434750.md).

Denmark was a founding member of [NATO](21133.md) in 1949, and the 1951 Greenland Defense Agreement allowed the United States to operate the base under a NATO framework, as long as both Denmark and the United States remain NATO members. Under the agreement, the United States has exclusive jurisdiction over its facilities at the base, but with the Danish flag being required to be flown over the base alongside the American flag and the Danes having the right to station a consulting [liaison officer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liaison_officer) at the base.[^cite_note-5] The 1951 agreement was modified in 2004 to require that the Greenlandic flag also be flown over the base and gave the Greenlandic government the right to appoint a liaison officer for the base.[^cite_note-6] As of 2025, about 150 United States service members are permanently stationed there, down from some 6,000 during the [Cold War](325329.md).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

It is the northernmost [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense_\(United_States\)) installation, 1,210 km (750 mi) north of the [Arctic Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctic_Circle) and 1,524 km (947 mi) from the [North Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Pole). Pituffik's Arctic environment includes icebergs in [North Star Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Star_Bay), two islands ([Saunders Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saunders_Island,_Greenland) and [Wolstenholme Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolstenholme_Island)), a polar ice sheet, and [Wolstenholme Fjord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolstenholme_Fjord). The base is home to a substantial portion of the global network of missile warning sensors of [Space Delta 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Delta_4), and space surveillance and space control sensors of [Space Delta 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Delta_2), providing space awareness and advanced missile detection capabilities to [North American Aerospace Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aerospace_Defense_Command) (NORAD), the United States Space Force, and joint partners.

Pituffik Space Base is also home to the 821st Space Base Group and is responsible for space base support within the Pituffik Defense Area for the multinational "Team Pituffik" population. The base hosts the [12th Space Warning Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Space_Warning_Squadron) (12 SWS), which operates a [Ballistic Missile Early Warning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_Missile_Early_Warning_System) (BMEWS) designed to detect and track [ICBMs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICBM) launched against North America. The base is also host to Detachment 1 of the [23rd Space Operations Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Space_Operations_Squadron), part of the [Space Delta 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Delta_6)'s global satellite control network. The airfield's 3,000 m (10,000 ft) runway handles more than 3,000 US and international flights per year. The base is also home to the northernmost deep water port in the world.[^cite_note-9]

The base was formally transferred to the Space Force in 2020 and was renamed Pituffik Space Base in 2023. It drew attention during the [Greenland crisis](82044223.md) of 2025 when Colonel [Susannah Meyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susannah_Meyers), the base commander, was relieved of her command after opposing President [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s [statements about annexing Greenland](61530125.md).[^cite_note-bbcapr-10][^cite_note-11]

## History

### Location and original population

In 1818, Sir [John Ross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ross_\(Royal_Navy_officer\))'s expedition made first contact with nomadic [Inughuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inughuit) in the area. [James Saunders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Saunders_\(naval_commander\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)'s expedition aboard [HMS *North Star*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_North_Star_\(1824\)) was marooned in [North Star Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Star_Bay) in 1849–50 and named landmarks.[^cite_note-RN-12] In 1910 explorer [Knud Rasmussen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knud_Rasmussen) established a missionary and trading post there. He called the site "Thule" after classical *[ultima Thule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule)*; the Inuit called it *Umanaq* or *Uummannaq* ("heart-shaped"), and the site is commonly called "Dundas" today. Whaling captain, explorer, and ethnologist [George Comer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Comer) discovered a midden, dubbed [Comer's Midden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comer's_Midden), at Umanaq in 1916, and an archaeological excavation subsequently revealed a village of the proto-Inuit who came to be called the [Thule people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule_people). The United States abandoned its territorial claims in the area in 1917 in connection with the purchase of the [Virgin Islands](32135.md). Denmark assumed control of the village in 1937.

A cluster of huts known as [Pituffik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pituffik) ("quay, dock")[^cite_note-13] stood on the wide plain where the base was built in 1951; a main base street was named Pituffik Boulevard. The population was [forcibly relocated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forcible_relocation) to Thule. Later in 1953, the USAF planned to construct an air defense site near that village, and in order to limit contact with soldiers, the Danish government again relocated 130 inhabitants of "Old Thule", settling them 97 km (60 mi) north in a newly constructed village also named Thule (colloquially "New Thule", now [Qaanaaq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaanaaq)).

In a Danish Supreme Court judgment of 28 November 2003, the move was considered an [expropriative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expropriative) intervention. During the proceedings, the Danish government recognized that the movement was a serious interference and an unlawful act against the local population. The Thule tribe was awarded damages of 500,000 [kroner](236323.md), and the individual members of the tribe who had been exposed to the transfer were granted compensation of 15,000 or 25,000 kroner each. A Danish radio station continued to operate at Dundas, and the abandoned houses remained. The USAF used that site only for about a decade, and it has since returned to civilian use.

Knud Rasmussen was the first to recognize the Pituffik Plain as ideal for an airport. USAAF Colonel [Bernt Balchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernt_Balchen), who built [Sondrestrom Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sondrestrom_Air_Base), knew Rasmussen and his idea. Balchen led a flight of two [Consolidated PBY Catalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_PBY_Catalina) flying boats to Thule on 24 August 1942 and then sent a report advocating an air base to [USAAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAAF) chief [Henry "Hap" Arnold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_%22Hap%22_Arnold). However, the 1951 air base site is a few kilometers inland from the original 1946 airstrip and across the bay from the historical Thule settlement; an [ice road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_road) connects it. The joint Danish-American defense area, designated by treaty, also occupies considerable inland territory in addition to the air base itself.[^cite_note-14]

### World War II

After the [German occupation of Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_in_World_War_II) on 9 April 1940, [Henrik Kauffmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Kauffmann), Danish Ambassador to the United States, agreed "In the name of the king" with the United States, authorizing the United States to defend the Danish colonies on Greenland from German aggression. This agreement faced Kauffmann with a charge of [high treason](31292.md) by the [protectorate Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_in_World_War_II#Protectorate_Government_1940%E2%80%931943). Beginning in the summer of 1941, the United States Coast Guard and the War Department established weather and radio stations at [Narsarsuaq Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narsarsuaq_Airport) ([Bluie West-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narsarsuaq_Air_Base)), [Sondrestrom Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sondrestrom_Air_Base) (Bluie West-8), Ikateq ([Bluie East Two](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluie_East_Two)), and [Gronnedal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kangilinnguit) (Bluie West-9). In 1943 the [Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) set up [weather stations](206018.md) [Scoresbysund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ittoqqortoormiit) (Bluie East-3) on the east coast around the southern tip of Greenland, and Thule ([Bluie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluie) West-6) to be operated by Danish personnel. Many other sites were set up, but BW-6, isolated in the far North, was then of very minor importance.[^cite_note-15]

### Joint weather station

After liberation, Denmark ratified the Kauffmann treaty but began efforts to take over US installations. Nonetheless, in the summer of 1946, the radio and weather station was enhanced with a gravel airstrip and an upper-air (balloon) observatory. This was part of an American-Canadian initiative to construct joint weather stations in the High Arctic. This station was under joint US-Danish operation. The location changed from the Thule (Dundas) civilian village to mainland Pituffik. From 1946 to 1951, the airstrip played an important role in Arctic resupply, aerial mapping, research, and search-and-rescue.

The treaty's ratification in 1951 did not change much, except that the [Danish national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Denmark) must be side by side with the [US national flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_US) on the base.

### Thule Air Base

In 1949, Denmark joined the [North Atlantic Treaty Organization](21133.md) (NATO) and abandoned its attempt to remove the United States bases. By the outbreak of the [Korean War](16772.md) the next year, the USAF embarked on a global program of base-building in which Thule (at the time) would be considered the crown jewel owing to its location across the Pole from the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), as well as its merit of being the northernmost port to be reliably resupplied by ship. Thule became a key point in American nuclear retaliation strategy. [Strategic Air Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Air_Command) (SAC) bombers flying over the [Arctic](36971.md) presented less risk of early warning than using bases in the United Kingdom. Defensively, Thule could serve as a base for intercepting bomber attacks along the northeastern approaches to Canada and the United States.

A board of Air Force officers headed by [Gordon P. Saville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_P._Saville) recommended pursuing a base at Thule in November 1950. It was subsequently supported by the [Joint Chiefs of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Chiefs_of_Staff) and approved by [President Truman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_S._Truman). To replace the agreement entered into during World War II between the US and Denmark, a new agreement concerning Greenland was signed on 27 April 1951 and ratified by Denmark on 1 June 1951[^cite_note-16] (effective on 8 June 1951). At the request of NATO, the agreement became a part of the NATO defense program. The pact specified that the two nations would arrange for the use of facilities in Greenland by NATO forces in defense of the NATO area known as the Greenland Defense Area.

Thule Air Base was constructed in secret under the code name [Operation Blue Jay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Blue_Jay), but the project was made public in September 1952. Construction for Thule Air Base began in 1951 and was completed in 1953. The construction of Thule is said to have been comparable in scale to the enormous effort required to build the [Panama Canal](24849.md).[^cite_note-17] The [United States Navy](20518076.md) transported the bulk of men, supplies, and equipment from the naval shipyards in [Norfolk, Virginia](57898.md). On 6 June 1951, an armada of 120 ships sailed from [Naval Station Norfolk](896196.md). On board were 12,000 men and 300,000 tons of cargo. They arrived at Thule on 9 July 1951. Construction, aided by continuous daylight in summer, took place around the clock. The workers lived on board the ships until quarters were built. Once they moved into the quarters, the ships returned home.

On 16 June 1951, the base was accidentally discovered by French cultural anthropologist and geographer [Jean Malaurie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Malaurie) and his Inuit friend Kutikitsoq, on their way back from the geomagnetic North Pole.[^cite_note-18]

#### Strategic Air Command



74th Fighter-Interceptor Squadron [F-89s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_F-89_Scorpion), Thule Air Base, Greenland, 1955

Originally established as a Strategic Air Command installation, Thule periodically served as a dispersal base for [B-36 Peacemaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convair_B-36_Peacemaker) and [B-47 Stratojet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-47_Stratojet) aircraft during the 1950s. It also provided an ideal site to test the operability and maintainability of these weapon systems in extreme cold weather. Similar operations were also conducted with [B-52 Stratofortress](18933037.md) aircraft in the 1950s and 1960s.

In 1954, the 378 m (1,240 ft) [Globecom Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globecom_Tower), a tower for military radio communication, was built at Northmountain. At the time of its completion, it was the third tallest human-made structure on earth and the tallest structure north of the Arctic Circle in the Western hemisphere.



Reconnaissance route from Thule Air Base to Soviet Union

In the winter of 1956–1957, three [KC-97](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KC-97_Stratofreighter) [tankers](237949.md) and alternately one of two [RB-47H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-47_Stratojet) aircraft made polar flights to inspect Soviet defenses. Five KC-97s were prepared for flight with engines running in temperatures of −46 °C (−50 °F) to ensure three could achieve airborne status. After a two-hour head start, a B-47 would catch up with them at the northeast coastline of Greenland where two would offload fuel to top off the B-47's tanks (the third was an air spare). The B-47 would then fly seven hours of reconnaissance, while the tankers would return to Thule, refuel, and three would again fly to rendezvous with the returning B-47 at northeast Greenland. The B-47 averaged ten hours and 4,500 km (2,800 mi) in the air, unless unpredictable weather closed Thule. In that case, the three tankers and the B-47 had to additionally fly to one of three equidistant alternates: England, [Alaska](624.md), or [Labrador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labrador). This sometimes occurred in moonless, 24-hour [Arctic darkness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_night), December through February. These flights demonstrated the capabilities of the US [Strategic Air Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Air_Command) to [Soviet Anti-Air Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Defence_Forces).

In 1959, the airbase was the main staging point for the construction of [Camp Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Century), some 240 km (150 mi) from the base.[^cite_note-19] Carved into the ice, and powered by a [nuclear reactor](22151.md), [PM-2A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PM-2A) Camp Century was officially a scientific research base, but in reality was the site of the top secret [Project Iceworm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Iceworm). The camp operated from 1959 until 1967.

In the late 1950s, the [DEW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distant_Early_Warning_Line) 1 to 4 were built as "weather stations". Thule Air Base would act as a supply station for the DYE bases.

Other nearby installations built at the time that received support from Thule Air Base included the [Cape Atholl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Atholl) ([LORAN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LORAN) station), [Camp TUTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_TUTO) (Ice Cap Approach Ramp and Airstrip), Sites 1 and 2 (Ice Cap Radar Stations), [Pingarssuit Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingarssuit_Mountain) ("P Mountain") (radar and communications site), [Thule Site J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule_Site_J) (BMEWS), North and South Mountains (research sites), and a research rocket firing site. It also was essential in the construction and resupply of High Arctic weather stations, including [CFS Alert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CFS_Alert) ([Alert Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alert_Airport)) and [Station Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nord,_Greenland).

#### Aerospace defense



Queen [Margrethe II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margrethe_II) during a visit to the base on 11 October 2021

In 1957, construction began on four [Nike Missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Nike) sites around the base, and they and their radar systems were operational by the end of 1958.

In 1961, a Ballistic Missile Early Warning System (BMEWS) radar was constructed at "J-Site", 21 km (13 mi) northeast of the main base. BMEWS was developed by the [RCA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCA_Corporation) Corporation to warn North America of a transpolar missile attack from the Russian mainland and submarine-launched missiles from the Arctic and North Atlantic oceans. At this time, Thule was at its peak, with a population of about 10,000. Starting in July 1965, activities at Thule were generally downsized. The base host unit, the [4683d Air Defense Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4683d_Air_Defense_Wing), was discontinued. By January 1968, the population of Thule was down to 3,370. On 21 January 1968, a B-52G bomber carrying four nuclear weapons [crashed just outside Thule](#B-52_nuclear_bomber_crash_\(1968\)).

Thule is the location where the fastest recorded sea level surface wind speed in the world was measured when a peak speed of 333 km/h (207 mph) was recorded on 8 March 1972, immediately prior to the instrument's destruction.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

#### Air Force Space Command



[Aurora](49658.md) over Thule Air Base in 2017

Thule became an Air Force Space Command base in 1982. The US and Denmark agreed to reduce the base to half its original area on 30 September 1986.[^cite_note-ECHR-22] It was home to the 821st Space Base Group, which exercised air base support responsibilities within the Thule Defense Area. The base hosts the [12th Space Warning Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Space_Warning_Squadron) (21st Operations Group, 21st Space Wing), a Ballistic Missile Early Warning Site designed to detect and track ICBMs launched against North America. Missile warning and space surveillance information flows to NORAD command centers located at [Peterson Space Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterson_Space_Force_Base), [Colorado](5399.md). Thule is also host to Detachment 1 of the [23rd Space Operations Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Space_Operations_Squadron), part of the [50th Space Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/50th_Space_Wing)'s global satellite control network, as well as operating many new weapons systems. In addition, the airfield boasts a 3,047 by 42 m (9,997 by 138 ft) asphalt runway, with 3,000 US and international flights per year.

The Dundas Peninsula, including Old Thule and Uummannaq, was relinquished by the US and returned to Danish jurisdiction on 20 February 2003.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] A delegation from the [NATO Parliamentary Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Parliamentary_Assembly) visited Thule in early September 2010 and were told by the base commander that, at that time (summer), approximately 600 personnel were serving at Thule, a mix of mostly US and Danish active duty personnel and contractors.[^cite_note-25]

In 2015-2016, the runway was repaved, the first time since 1991. The runway is white to reduce permafrost thawing. Previously painted white, during the re-paving, extruded polystyrene foam boards were used in 18% of the runway instead of white.[^cite_note-26]

There is only a brief period each year in the summer when sea ice thins sufficiently to send supply ships to the base. The US sends one heavy supply ship each summer in what is called [Operation Pacer Goose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pacer_Goose?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-27]

### Pituffik Space Base

In 2020, Thule Air Base was formally transferred to the [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force). On 6 April 2023, Thule was renamed Pituffik Space Base, reflecting its status as a Space Force base and the native name for the region.[^cite_note-sn-20230406-4][^cite_note-28]

#### Opposition to Trump Greenland threats

On 28 March 2025, Vice President [JD Vance](53396477.md), his wife [Usha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usha_Vance), and [Mike Waltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Waltz), the national security adviser, toured the base, as part of a trip arranged by the [Trump administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_expansionism_under_Donald_Trump). Vance was the most senior US government official ever to visit the base. The visit came during a time of renewed discussion of the [proposed acquisition of Greenland](61530125.md) by President [Donald Trump](4848272.md)[^cite_note-29] and was opposed by some Greenlanders.[^cite_note-30] On 11 April 2025, the base commander, Colonel [Susannah Meyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susannah_Meyers), was relieved of command by the Trump administration for "undermining" Vice President Vance after his visit by sending an email to base personnel (staffed by Americans, Canadians, Danes, and Greenlanders) that included: "I spent the weekend thinking about Friday's visit \[by VP Vance]—the actions taken, the words spoken, and how it must have affected each of you. I do not presume to understand current politics, but what I do know is the concerns of the US administration discussed by Vice President Vance on Friday are not reflective of Pituffik Space Base. I commit that, for as long as I am lucky enough to lead this base, all of our flags will fly proudly—together".[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-bbcapr-10]

## Remote tracking station

Thule Tracking Station (TTS) is operated by Pituffik Space Base, using the callsign POGO. The station [76°30′57″N 68°36′0″W  /  76.51583°N 68.60000°W  / 76.51583; -68.60000](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Pituffik_Space_Base\&params=76_30_57_N_68_36_0_W_)) is a US Space Force installation in Greenland, near the base, and has a [Remote Tracking Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_Tracking_Station) (callsign: Polar Orbiting Geophysical Observatory (POGO)) of the Satellite Control Network.[^cite_note-MilitaryOneSource-40]

It was originally the classified [6594th Test Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6594th_Test_Wing)'s Operating Location 5 designated by [Air Force Systems Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Systems_Command) on 15 October 1961: the station was operational on 30 March 1962, with "transportable antenna vans parked in an old [Strategic Air Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Air_Command) bomb assembly building."[^cite_note-MilitaryOneSource-40] The permanent RTS equipment was emplaced in 1964,[^cite_note-MilitaryOneSource-40] and a communications terminal was emplaced on [Pingarssuit Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingarssuit_Mountain)—Thule Site N-32[^cite_note-Fletcher-41] (moved to [Thule Site J](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule_Site_J) in 1983.[^cite_note-MilitaryOneSource-40]

### USAF tugboat

To assist with port operations, Pituffik is home to the only tugboat in the Department of the Air Force, the 71-foot *[Rising Star (USAF TG-71-9001)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rising_Star_\(ship,_1991\))*.[^cite_note-43] In the summertime, the *Rising Star* escorts fuel tankers and cargo ships, aligns them with the pier, and moves icebergs out of the way as vessels enter North Star Bay. It is also used for sightseeing tours of the surrounding bays and fjords during the summer. In the winter, it is hauled onto shore. In 2020, the tugboat was used to save a sinking ship and its crew of six 50 kilometers (31 mi) south of base, towing the distressed ship back to the port at Pituffik.[^cite_note-44]

## Accidents

### C-124 plane crash (1954)

In 1954, a [Douglas C-124C Globemaster II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-124C_Globemaster_II) operated by the US Air Force crashed on approach to the air base, killing ten people.[^cite_note-45]

### B-52 nuclear bomber crash (1968)

Main article: [B-52 Crash at Thule Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Thule_Air_Base_B-52_crash)

On 21 January 1968, a [B-52G Stratofortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-52G_Stratofortress) from the [380th Strategic Aerospace Wing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/380th_Strategic_Aerospace_Wing), [Plattsburgh Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plattsburgh_Air_Force_Base), New York, on a secret airborne nuclear alert crashed and burned on the ice near Thule Air Base. The impact detonated the [high explosives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_material#High_explosives) in the primary units of all four of the [B28 nuclear bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B28_nuclear_bomb) it carried, but nuclear and [thermonuclear reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fusion) did not take place due to the [PAL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permissive_Action_Link#Misfire) and [fail-safe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fail-safe) mechanisms in the weapons, thus preventing the actual detonation of the weapons themselves. The resulting fire caused extensive [radioactive contamination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioactive_contamination).[^cite_note-46] More than 700 Danish civilians and US military personnel worked under hazardous conditions, the former without protective gear, to clean up the nuclear material.[^cite_note-BBC-47] In 1987, nearly 200 Danish workers tried unsuccessfully to sue the United States. Kaare Ulbak, chief consultant to the Danish National Institute of Radiation Hygiene, said Denmark had carefully studied the health of the Thule workers and found no evidence of increased mortality or cancer.[^cite_note-Brookings-48][^cite_note-Nukestrat-49][^cite_note-BBC_2007-05-11-50]

The [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) maintained that all four weapons had been destroyed. Although many of the details of the accident are still classified, some information was released by the US authorities in September 1994[^cite_note-51] under the [Freedom of Information Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Information_Act_\(United_States\)). After reviewing these files, an investigative reporter from [BBC News](1139893.md) claimed in May 2007 that the USAF was unable to account for one of the weapons.[^cite_note-BBC-47] In 2009, the assertions of the BBC were refuted by a Danish report after a review of the available declassified documentation.[^cite_note-DIIS-52]

## Climate

Pituffik has a [tundra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tundra) [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_climate) ([ET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification)) with long, severely cold winters lasting most of the year and short and cool summers. Precipitation is very low year round, but peaks during summer. The structures of the base are built on [permafrost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permafrost), which makes them vulnerable to the effects of [climate change](5042951.md).[^cite_note-55]

## Film

Pituffik Space Base is depicted in the 2023 documentary film *The Color of Ice*,[^cite_note-IMDb:_The_Color_of_Ice-56] which follows scientists testing a hot-tip drill to melt into the ice sheet at the edge of the base.[^cite_note-57] The film highlights how the ice-sheet surface at TUTO Ramp Road, which was built by [United States Army Corps of Engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Corps_of_Engineers) for ice-sheet access to [Camp Century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Century) in the 1960s, has melted downwards by almost 100 feet (30 m). Pituffik Space Base itself is arguably presented as a [liminal space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liminal_space) in the film.
