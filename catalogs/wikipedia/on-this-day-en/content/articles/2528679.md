**Idlib** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): إِدْلِب, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *ʾIdlib*, pronounced [\[ʔid.lib\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic); also spelt **Idleb** or **Edlib**) is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) in northwestern [Syria](7515849.md), and is the capital of the [Idlib Governorate](7445043.md). It has an elevation of nearly 500 meters (1,600 ft) above sea level, and is 59 kilometers (37 mi) southwest of [Aleppo](159244.md). It is located near the border with [Turkey](11125639.md).

## History

A [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) settlement dating back to 8500 BCE was identified at Tell Ain el-Kerkh, near Idlib.

[^cite_note-2]

&#x20;The [Ebla tablets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebla_tablets) (2350 BCE) mention the city of 𒁺𒄷𒆷𒇥𒌝 (**du-ḫu-la-bu<sub>6</sub>-um** "Duhulabum") which is most probably located at Idlib as suggested by Michael Astour and Douglas Frayne; a similarity exists between the sounds of the ancient and modern names. In the tablets, Duhulabuum is 22 km south of "Unqi" which might correspond to the modern village of Kaukanya/Kukaniyeh ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): كوكانية), located approximately 22 km north of Idlib (see [Dead Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Cities)). [Thutmose III](155730.md) also mentioned the city with the name *Ytḥb*.

[^cite_note-3]

### Classical Antiquity



[Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) temple at [Ruweiha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Cities) near Idlib

Idlib, along with the rest of Syria, was conquered by the [Armenian](387816.md) king [Tigranes the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigranes_the_Great) and incorporated in the [Armenian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Empire), only to be later conquered by the Roman [Pompey the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompey_the_Great) around 64 BCE. The city was never of much significance, belonging to the province of [Roman Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Syria) under the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) and later to the [Eastern Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) province of Syria Secunda before being conquered by the [Arabs](2185.md) around the middle of the seventh century. Not much remains from the city's Roman and [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine) times, except in its museum. North of the city are the [Dead Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Cities), a collection of important archaeological sites from the Byzantine era.

### Ottoman era

During early [Ottoman](22278.md) rule in Syria, beginning in 1516, Idlib was a small *[timar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timar)* (fief). The village of Idlib was founded by [Fadil Ahmed Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6pr%C3%BCl%C3%BCzade_Faz%C4%B1l_Ahmed_Pasha), the son of Grand Vizier [Köprülü Mehmed Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6pr%C3%BCl%C3%BC_Mehmed_Pasha) (r. 1656–1661) who appointed him governor of [Damascus Eyalet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Damascus_Eyalet). In later years, it developed as a town with markets, [bathhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_bath) and [caravanserais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai), including Khan Abi Ali and Khan al-Ruz.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBakhit201060-4]

From the Köprülü period, Idlib was a center of olive production.[^cite_note-5] which in turn gave way to a prosperous [olive-based soap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleppo_soap) industry. Although the major markets for Idlib's soap were at [Aleppo](159244.md), [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antioch), and [Hama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hama),[^cite_note-Porter580-6] the product was exported as far as the [Ottoman](22278.md) capital of [Istanbul](3391396.md). Idlib was also a major producer of cotton fabrics.[^cite_note-7] Western traveler [Josias Leslie Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josias_Leslie_Porter) noted that Idlib was "encompassed in olive groves, rare in this bleak region",[^cite_note-Porter580-6] and remarked that its olive groves were larger than those of Damascus, [Beirut](37428.md), or [Gaza](55387.md). In the mid-19th century, the town had an estimated population of 8,000, including 500 [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Christian).[^cite_note-Porter580-6] In the late 19th century, Idlib was "flourishing" and still contained several Christian families, according to German orientalist [Albert Socin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Socin).[^cite_note-Baedeker376-8]

### Syrian civil war and HTS takeover

Main article: [Syrian civil war](30741795.md)



Syrian rebels deface a Ba'athist mural at the Mihrab roundabout in Idlib, after the city's [capture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Idlib_\(2015\)) by rebel forces in 2015

During the [uprising since 2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_uprising_phase_of_the_Syrian_Civil_War), Idlib was the focus of protests and fighting in the early phase of the Syrian war. As the uprising descended into armed conflict, Idlib became the focus of a rebel campaign, which temporarily captured the city and the governorate before a government offensive in April 2012. After this, government forces retook the city and the rebel-controlled province after a month of fighting before the attempted enforcement of the ceasefire proposed by [Kofi Annan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kofi_Annan). After the [2015 Idlib offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Idlib_offensive) in March, the rebel alliance [Army of Conquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Conquest), led by the [al-Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front) and [Ahrar al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham), succeeded in the [Second Battle of Idlib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Idlib_\(2015\)) and captured the city,[^cite_note-9] as well as besieging the Shi'a-majority towns of [Al-Fu'ah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Fu'ah) and [Kafriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafriya) to the north of Idlib city.[^cite_note-10] In April 2015, the interim seat of the [Syrian opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_opposition_\(2011%E2%80%932024\))'s [Syrian Interim Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Interim_Government) was proposed to be Idlib,[^cite_note-11] in Idlib Governorate. On 23 July 2017, [Tahrir al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tahrir_al-Sham), the successor to the al-Nusra Front, [expelled the remaining forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idlib_Governorate_clashes_\(July_2017\)) of [Ahrar al-Sham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahrar_al-Sham) from Idlib, capturing the entire city and bringing it under the control of the [Syrian Salvation Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Salvation_Government).[^cite_note-12]

On 17 September 2018, Russian President Vladimir [Putin](32817.md) and Turkish President Tayyip Erdoğan announced an [agreement to establish a demilitarized buffer zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idlib_demilitarization_\(2018%E2%80%932019\)) in Syria's Idlib province, intended to separate Syrian government forces from rebel factions. Under the agreement, Turkish and Russian troops would patrol the zone to ensure compliance. Putin stated that all heavy weaponry would be removed from the area and that radical rebel groups, such as the Nusra Front, must withdraw. The buffer zone was set to be operational by October 15, 2018.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## Climate

[Köppen climate classification system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) classifies its climate as [hot-summer Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) (*Csa*).[^cite_note-Climate-Data.org-15] Summers are hot and rainless, while winters are rainy and cool.

The all-time record high temperature was 44 °C (111 °F) on June 16, 2012.[^cite_note-Voodoo_Skies-16]

## Demographics

In the 2004 census by the [Central Bureau of Statistics of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Statistics_\(Syria\)), Idlib had a population of 98,791 and in 2010 the population was around 165,000. The inhabitants are mostly [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Muslim),[^cite_note-Mroue-18] although there was previously a significant [Christian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Christians) minority, but by 2022 there was only a single elderly Christian man left in the city.[^cite_note-AL-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Idlib is divided into six main districts: Ashrafiyah (the most populous), Hittin, Hejaz, Downtown, Hurriyah, and al-Qusour.

## Economy



Olive orchards at the outskirts of the city. Idlib is a major production center for olives.

Idlib is a major production center for [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive), [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), wheat and fruits, particularly cherries.[^cite_note-Casule56-22] Other principal crops include [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond), [sesame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sesame) seeds, figs, grapes and tomatoes.[^cite_note-Britannica-23] In 1995 there were roughly 300 [hectares](24919513.md) planted with various [citrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citrus) crop.[^cite_note-24] [Olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil) pressing and [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinning_\(textiles\)) are some of the city's local industries.[^cite_note-Britannica-23] The nearby city of [Aleppo](159244.md) has an important economic presence in Idlib.[^cite_note-Casule56-22]

Idlib is a major agricultural center in Syria. It is also historically significant, containing many "dead cities" and [tells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_\(archaeology\)).

Because of the rapidly declining value of the [Syrian pound](456708.md), the [Turkish lira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_lira) became widespread in Idlib and was adopted as legal tender in the city on 15 June 2020.[^cite_note-25] This was reversed in 2025.

## Culture

The Idlib Regional Museum in the city contains over 17,000 of the [Ebla tablets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebla_tablets) and serves as Idlib's main tourist attraction, excluding the nearby ancient site of Ebla itself. Under the Technical and Financial Cooperation Agreement between the governments of [Italy](14532.md) and Syria, the museum was to undergo a restoration and renovation project starting in 2010.[^cite_note-26]

## Sports

[Omaya SC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omaya_SC), founded in 1972, is the most popular [football](10568.md) team in the city. The club played in the [Syrian Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Premier_League) for the [2011-2012 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Syrian_Premier_League). [Idlib Municipal Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idlib_Municipal_Stadium) is the main football venue in the city.
