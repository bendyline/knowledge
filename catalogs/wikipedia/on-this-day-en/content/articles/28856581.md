**Karin Maria Keller-Sutter**[^cite_note-1] (Swiss Standard German: [\[ˈkaːriːn ˈkɛlər ˈzʊtər\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German); [née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%A9e) **Sutter**; born 22 December 1963) is a Swiss politician and [pedagogist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogist) who has served as a member of the [Federal Council of Switzerland](218299.md) since 2019 and as [President of the Swiss Confederation](33730924.md) for 2025.

Keller-Sutter is a member of [the Liberals](26707861.md) and serves as the head of the [Federal Department of Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Department_of_Finance).[^cite_note-2] She previously served on the [Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_States_\(Switzerland\)) from 2011 to 2019 and from 2017 to 2018 as [president of the Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Swiss_Council_of_States). Before that she held several political roles on the cantonal and municipal level.

In 2023, Keller-Sutter was listed as one of the most influential women worldwide by the *[Financial Times](136566.md)*.[^cite_note-3] She was primarily credited for her engagement with the [Acquisition of Credit Suisse by UBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquisition_of_Credit_Suisse_by_UBS).

## Early life and education

Keller-Sutter was born Karin Maria Sutter on 22 December 1963 in [Uzwil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzwil), Switzerland, the youngest of four children, to Walter Sutter Sr. (died 1989), a butcher-turned-chef, and Rosa Sutter ([née](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_name) Schnyder; born 1927).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] Her brothers are; Walter "Jesy" Sutter (1950–2025),[^cite_note-6] Rolf Sutter and Bernhard Sutter.[^cite_note-7]

Her paternal family hails from [Jonschwil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonschwil) where her parents were the tenants of the restaurant *Sonne* for many years. Her ancestors were mainly innkeepers and local politicians in [St. Gallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_St._Gallen).[^cite_note-8] Later, her parents took over the restaurant *Ilge* in [Wil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wil), where she was primarily raised and attended local schools, including [Catholic high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_high_school) before moving to [Neuchâtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neuch%C3%A2tel).

She studied [language interpretation](899930.md) at *Dolmetscherschule Zürich* (now part of the [Zurich University of Applied Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_University_of_Applied_Sciences)) followed by studies in [political science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_science) in London and at the [University of Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_de_Montr%C3%A9al). Later she completed a [post-graduate diploma](153981.md) in [pedagogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogy) at the [University of Fribourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Fribourg).

## Professional career

During her studies, Keller-Sutter worked as an independent translator and conference interpreter. She later became a professor at the [vocational school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_school). Keller-Sutter has formerly served as vice president of the board of trustees of the [St. Gallen Foundation for International Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Gallen_Symposium).[^cite_note-9]

## Political career

Keller-Sutter joined the FDP in 1987.[^cite_note-v624-10] She undertook a political career as a municipal councillor in Wil between 1992 and 2000. She presided over the municipal assembly in 1997. From 1996 to 2000, she was a deputy of the *[Kantonsrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cantonal_legislatures_of_Switzerland)* of the [canton of St. Gallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_St._Gallen), while presiding over the local arm of the FDP.

On 12 March 2000, Keller-Sutter was elected to the *[Regierungsrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cantonal_executives_of_Switzerland)* of the canton of St. Gallen, where she was appointed to the department for security and justice. She was also vice president of the conference of cantonal directors for justice and police. She presided over the government in 2006–2007.

On 22 September 2010, Keller-Sutter was a candidate for the [Swiss Federal Council](218299.md) to succeed [Hans-Rudolf Merz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans-Rudolf_Merz) but failed to win the election; [Johann Schneider-Ammann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johann_Schneider-Ammann), a member of the [National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Switzerland\)) for the [canton of Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Bern) since 1999, [won](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Swiss_Federal_Council_election) the seat instead.[^cite_note-11] On 23 October 2011, she was [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Swiss_federal_election)[^cite_note-v624-10] with 65% of the vote to represent the canton of St. Gallen in the [Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_States_\(Switzerland\)). She served as [president of the Council of States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_Swiss_Council_of_States) in 2017–2018.

On 8 October 2018, Keller-Sutter again announced her candidacy for the Swiss Federal Council, this time for the seat of recently retired Schneider-Ammann, who had defeated her eight years before.[^cite_note-12] On 5 December 2018, she was [elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Swiss_Federal_Council_election) for the Federal Council with 154 votes out of 237, alongside [Viola Amherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viola_Amherd) of the [Christian Democratic People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_People's_Party_of_Switzerland) (CVP/PDC).[^cite_note-13]

On 1 January 2025, Keller-Sutter was sworn in as president with [Guy Parmelin](48772769.md) as acting vice president.[^cite_note-14]

In August 2025, Keller-Sutter came under scrutiny after a call with President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) about trade tariffs resulted in 39% import tariffs for Switzerland.[^cite_note-15]

## Personal life

In 1989, Keller-Sutter married Morten Keller (born 1964), a medical doctor.[^cite_note-16] They have no children and reside in [Wil, St. Gallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wil). The couple owned a [Jack Russell Terrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Russell_Terrier) named *Picasso*, after [Pablo Picasso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pablo_Picasso).[^cite_note-17] The dog died in 2019.[^cite_note-18]
