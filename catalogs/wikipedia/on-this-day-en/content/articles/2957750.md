**Paphos International Airport** ([Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language): Διεθνής Αερολιμένας Πάφου; [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_language): *Baf Uluslararası Havalimanı*) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **PFO**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **LCPH**) is a [joint civil-military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint-use_airport) public [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) located 6.5 kilometres (4 miles) south-east[^cite_note-AIP-2] of the city of [Paphos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paphos) on the Mediterranean island of [Cyprus](5593.md). It is the country's second largest airport, after [Larnaca International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larnaca_International_Airport). Paphos Airport is commonly used by holidaymakers in western Cyprus, providing access to popular resorts such as [Coral Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coral_Bay,_Cyprus), [Limassol](57577.md) (about 50 km or 30 mi southeast), and Paphos itself.

## History

The airport first opened in 1982 (1982). In May [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_in_aviation), Hermes Airports Limited took over the construction, development, and operation of both [Larnaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larnaca_International_Airport) and Paphos airports for a period of 25 years.[^cite_note-4] According to the airport operator, Paphos Airport served 1,744,011 passengers in 2007.[^cite_note-airport_trafic-5] A new passenger terminal opened at Paphos in November 2008.[^cite_note-6]

In 2012, [Ryanair](262878.md) allocated two aircraft in Paphos with 15 new routes, offering over 80 flights a week.&#x20;

A new four-lane road has recently opened to link the airport and [Paphos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paphos), so passengers and staff can avoid using the [B6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B6_road_\(Cyprus\)) main road and the E603 secondary road which are often heavily congested.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

## Facilities



Check-in hall.

Passenger facilities include 28 check-in desks, one special baggage check-in, seven gates, 22 aircraft stands, a bank, restaurants, cafeterias, bars, a [duty-free shop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duty-free_shop), and a gift shop. Other facilities include a tourist help desk, car rental, first aid, a baby/parent room, and disabled access facilities. Refrigerated storage, health officials, and X-ray equipment are among some of the facilities provided for cargo. Furthermore, loading platforms and forklifts are also available.

## Andreas Papandreou airbase

The airport is also an asset of the [Cyprus National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprus_National_Guard), serving as a military air force base under the call sign 'Andreas Papandreou'.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Located on the north-eastern part of the airport, it is considered the most southeastern European air force base component of various EU air forces, as well as a safe base for humanitarian and emergency purposes for other countries.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

## Access

### Bus

There is a regular bus service from Paphos Harbour station to the airport, limited services also run to / from Paphos Town (Karavella) and Polis.[^cite_note-76] Direct buses to/from [Limassol](57577.md),[^cite_note-77] [Nicosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicosia) and [Larnaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larnaca)[^cite_note-78] are also available.

### Car

The airport is located 20.8 km (12+15⁄16 mi) southeast of Paphos and 61.2 km (38 mi) west of Limassol.

## Accidents and incidents

- On 21 September 2011, a [Thomson Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomson_Airways) [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-800) inadvertently landed on the [taxiway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxiway) parallel to the runway (Taxiway Bravo, formerly Runway 11L/29R). No other aircraft was on the taxiway at the time, and the Thomson taxied safely to the [apron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_apron).

  [^cite_note-79]

  &#x20;A [NOTAM](1517785.md) was published on 20 December 2011, warning pilots of the possibility of mistaking the runway with the parallel taxiway.

  [^cite_note-MCW\.gov.cy-80]

  &#x20;An additional NOTAM was published on 14 August 2012, recommending pilots to confirm their alignment with the runway by using the [ILS localiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Instrument_landing_system_localizer) when performing a [visual approach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visual_approach) to runway 29.

  [^cite_note-MCW\.gov.cy-80]

  &#x20;By 2014, yellow 'TAXI' markings were painted across the width of the parallel taxiway near either longitudinal end, facing approaching aircraft; and a 'TAXIWAY' marking was painted at its junction with Taxiway Charlie (about midway), facing the latter.

  [^cite_note-81]
