The **Madrid–Barcelona high-speed rail line** is a 621-kilometre (385.9 mi) [standard-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-gauge) railway line inaugurated on 20 February 2008. Designed for speeds of 350 km/h (217.5 mph) and compatibility with neighbouring countries' rail systems, it connects the cities of [Madrid](41188263.md) and [Barcelona](4443.md) in 2 hours 30 minutes. In Barcelona the line is connected with the [Perpignan–Barcelona high-speed rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpignan%E2%80%93Barcelona_high-speed_rail_line) leading into France which connects it to the European high speed network.

Trains are operated by the national railway [Renfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe) under the [AVE](523382.md) and [Avlo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avlo) brands, and by private competitors [Ouigo España](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ouigo_Espa%C3%B1a) and [Iryo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iryo).

## First stages

In 2003 construction of the first phase of a new standard gauge line from Madrid to the French border (Madrid–Zaragoza–Lleida) was completed and on 11 October of that year commercial service began. This service also stopped at Guadalajara–Yebes and [Calatayud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calatayud). The service began running at only 200 km/h (124.3 mph). On 19 May 2006, after two years of operation, speed was increased to 250 km/h (155.3 mph) when the Spanish [ASFA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_protection_system) signalling system was replaced with level 1 of the new European [ETCS/ERTMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Train_Control_System) system. On 16 October 2006 the trains on this line increased their operating speed to 280 km/h (174.0 mph).

On 18 December 2006 the [AVE](523382.md) started operating to [Camp de Tarragona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_de_Tarragona_railway_station), and on 7 May 2007 the service increased its speed to the maximum allowable for the line, 300 km/h (186.4 mph). This puts Tarragona at 30 minutes from Lleida. The extension to Barcelona was delayed various times due to technical problems; the Ministerio de Fomento having originally forecast the AVE's arrival in Barcelona by the end of 2007.[^cite_note-3]

The complete line was opened February 2008. As of 2012, seventeen trains now run every day between the hours of 6 am and 9 pm, covering the distance between the two cities in just 2 hours 30 minutes for the direct trains, and in 3 hours and 10 minutes for those calling at all intermediate stations. Before the high-speed line was built, the journey between the two cities took more than six hours.&#x20;

## Speed



Maximum speed profile of the "Madrid–Barcelona–French Border" line, in 2015

It was originally forecast that, after reaching Barcelona in 2004, the line would run at 350 km/h (217.5 mph), the maximum capable speed of the new Siemens AVE trains [S103](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe_Class_103) which have replaced the Talgo Bombardier AVE S102, after the installation of level 2 of the [ETCS/ERTMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Train_Control_System). But on the AVE's first day of operating at 300 km/h (186.4 mph) to Tarragona the Minister of Public Works, Magdalena Álvarez, stated that the maximum commercial operating speeds of the AVE on all lines would be 300 km/h (186.4 mph).[^cite_note-4]

On 11 December 2011, the speed was raised to 310 km/h (192.6 mph) on a 60 km (37.3 mi) section of the railway (between km 64.4, near Guadalajara, and km 124.4), with plans to further increase the speed to 320 km/h (198.8 mph) on a 24 km (14.9 mi) sub-section (from km 81.0 to km 105.24) and plans to rapidly extend the 310 km/h (192.6 mph) speed throughout the Spanish high-speed network.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-geotren-max-speeds-2][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] Nevertheless, after almost 5 years of operation with S103 trains running at up to 310 km/h (192.6 mph) on this section, the speed was decreased back to 300 km/h (186.4 mph) on 17 August 2016.[^cite_note-geotren-max-speeds-2] The tests performed by ADIF had revealed that, at speeds exceeding 300 km/h (186.4 mph), the air which the trains were moving underneath was causing some stones of the ballast to rise, hitting the trains and causing damage of various kinds (the Spanish high-speed railways follow the French high-speed rail model of laying the rails on a bed of stones known as ballast, instead of laying the tracks on concrete plates, as is done for high-speed rail in Germany).[^cite_note-leonoticias2015-8] Due to this technical limitation and the substantial increase in costs and power necessary to break the 300 km/h (186.4 mph) barrier, the AVE in Spain abandoned prospects of operating at speeds over 300 km/h (186.4 mph) in the short or medium term.[^cite_note-leonoticias2015-8]

## Usage

It was forecast that the AVE would substantially replace air traffic on the Madrid – Barcelona route (in the same way that the [Eurostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostar) has on the [London–Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGV_Nord)/London–Brussels routes and France's [TGV](57212.md) has on the [Paris–Lyon route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGV_Sud-Est)). Indeed, by the end of 2017, the line had already taken 63% of the traffic, taking most of it from aircraft.[^cite_note-9] A few years before the Madrid–Barcelona route was the [world's busiest passenger air route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_busiest_passenger_air_routes) in 2007 with 971 scheduled flights per week (both directions).[^cite_note-10] Similarly more than 80% of travelers between Madrid and Seville use the AVE, with fewer than 20% traveling by air.[^cite_note-11]

There was criticism during the construction of the Madrid–Barcelona line. A critical report by the consulting firm [KPMG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPMG), commissioned by ADIF (*Administrador de Infraestructuras Ferroviarias*) at the behest of the Ministry for Development (*_Ministerio de Fomento_*) on 23 June 2004, pointed to a lack of in-depth studies and over-hasty execution of works as the most important reasons for the problems that dogged construction of the AVE line. For example, during the construction of the [AVE tunnel near Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunnel_of_Proven%C3%A7a), a number of nearby buildings suffered damage from a sinkhole that appeared near a commuter rail station, damaging one of its platforms. The construction committee of Barcelona's famed [Sagrada Família](59545.md) church lobbied for a re-routing of the tunnel – it passes within metres of the massive church's foundations. It also passes equally near the [UNESCO](21786641.md)-recognized [Casa Milà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Mil%C3%A0) also designed by [Antoni Gaudí](31566384.md).

Until 2005 both [Siemens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens) and [Talgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talgo)/[Bombardier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombardier_Transportation) [train sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_multiple_unit) failed to meet scheduled speed targets, although in a test run during the homologation tests of the new S102 trains of [Renfe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renfe), a train-set [Talgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talgo) 350 (AVE S-102) reached a speed of 365 km/h (226.8 mph) on the night of the 25/26 June 2006, and in July 2006 a [Siemens Velaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_Velaro) train-set (AVE S-103) reached the highest top speed ever in Spain: 403.7 km/h (250.8 mph). At this time, it was a record for railed vehicles in Spain and a [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_speed_record) for *unmodified* commercial service trainsets, as the earlier [TGV](57212.md) and [ICE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCityExpress) records were achieved with specially modified and shortened trainsets, and the 1996 [Shinkansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shinkansen) record of 443 km/h (275.3 mph) was using a test (non-commercial) trainset.

## Extension to France

Main article: [Perpignan–Barcelona high-speed rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpignan%E2%80%93Barcelona_high-speed_rail_line)

### Barcelona to Figueres

Originally planned to open in 2009, the extension of some Madrid–Barcelona routes to [Figueres–Vilafant railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figueres%E2%80%93Vilafant_railway_station) via [Girona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girona), opened on 9 January 2013.[^cite_note-12] This made possible upon the completion of the 131-kilometre (81.4 mi) Barcelona–[Figueres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figueres) section of the [Perpignan–Barcelona high-speed rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpignan%E2%80%93Barcelona_high-speed_rail_line) that connected for the first time the Spanish [AVE](523382.md) high-speed network with the French [TGV](57212.md) high-speed network.[^cite_note-13] There have been delays in building a four-kilometre tunnel in [Girona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girona), the first phase of which was finished in September 2010,[^cite_note-14] and controversy over the route between [Sants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barcelona_Sants_railway_station) and [Sagrera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagrera_railway_station) stations in Barcelona.[^cite_note-15] As of January 2013 there are eight trains a day running from Madrid, connecting at Figueres Vilafant with two TGV services to Paris.[^cite_note-16]

### Figueres to Perpignan

This is an international [high-speed rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail) section between France and Spain. The section connects two cities on opposite sides of the border, [Perpignan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perpignan) in [Occitanie](45093609.md), France, and [Figueres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Figueres) in [Catalonia](6822.md), Spain. It consists of a 44.4-kilometre (27.6 mi) railway which crosses the French–Spanish border via the [Perthus Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perthus_Tunnel), an 8.3-kilometre (5.2 mi) tunnel bored under the Perthus Pass.[^cite_note-db-17] The section is open to high speed trains and freight. Construction was completed in February 2009, although services did not run until a station was built on the line at Figueres. As of March 2015, a daily TGV service connects Paris to Barcelona Sants via Perpignan-Figueres with 2 pairs of trips, plus other connections involving Lyon, Marseille, and Toulouse.[^cite_note-18]
