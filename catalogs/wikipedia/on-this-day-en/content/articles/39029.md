**Bamboos** are a diverse group of mostly [evergreen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen) [perennial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perennial_plant) flowering plants making up the [subfamily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subfamily_\(biology\)) **Bambusoideae** of the grass family [Poaceae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaceae).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-Ayer_100027-5] [Giant bamboos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giant_bamboo) are the largest members of the grass family, in the case of *[Dendrocalamus sinicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrocalamus_sinicus)* having individual stalks ([culms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culm_\(botany\))) reaching a length of 46 meters (151 ft), up to 36 centimeters (14 in) in diameter and a weight of up to 450 kilograms (1,000 lb).[^cite_note-6] It grows in tropical and subtropical regions, especially in Asia. The [internodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internodes) of bamboos can also be of great length. *[Kinabaluchloa wrayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinabaluchloa)* has internodes up to 2.5 meters (8 ft) in length,[^cite_note-7] and *[Arthrostylidium schomburgkii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthrostylidium_schomburgkii)* has internodes up to 5 meters (16 ft) in length,[^cite_note-8] exceeded in length only by [papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyperus_papyrus). By contrast, the stalks of the tiny bamboo [*Raddiella vanessiae*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raddiella) of the savannas of [French Guiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guiana) measure only 10–20 millimeters (0.4–0.8 in) in length by about 2 millimeters (0.08 in) in width.[^cite_note-9] The origin of the word "bamboo" is uncertain, but it most likely comes from the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) or [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language) language, which originally borrowed it from [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_language).[^cite_note-Ayer_100027-5][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-oed-11]



Fresh [bamboo shoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_shoot) emerging from the soil. These young, tender culms of bamboo plants are also widely harvested for culinary use in Asia.

In bamboo, as in other grasses, the [internodal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internode_\(botany\)) regions of the stem are usually hollow and the [vascular bundles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_bundle) in the cross-section are scattered throughout the walls of the stalk instead of in a cylindrical [cambium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambium) layer between the bark ([phloem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phloem)) and the wood ([xylem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xylem)) as in [dicots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicotyledon) and [conifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conifer). The dicotyledonous woody xylem is also absent. The absence of [secondary growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_growth) wood causes the stems of [monocots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monocotyledon), including the [palms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arecaceae) and large bamboos, to be columnar rather than tapering.[^cite_note-12]

Bamboos include some of the fastest-growing plants in the world,[^cite_note-Farrelly-13] due to a unique [rhizome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhizome)-dependent system. Certain species of bamboo can grow 91 centimeters (36 inches) within a 24-hour period, at a rate of almost 40 millimeters (1+1⁄2 in) an hour (equivalent to 1 mm (0.04 in) every 90 seconds).[^cite_note-Guinness-14] Growth up to 120 centimeters (47.2 in) in 24 hours has been observed in the instance of Japanese giant timber bamboo (*[Phyllostachys bambusoides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllostachys_bambusoides)*).[^cite_note-15] This rapid growth and tolerance for [marginal land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_land) make bamboo a good candidate for [afforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afforestation), [carbon sequestration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sequestration) and [climate change mitigation](2119179.md).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-Masisi_100009-17][^cite_note-18]

Bamboo is versatile and has economic and cultural significance in [South Asia](21566765.md), [Southeast Asia](28741.md), and [East Asia](19605700.md), being used for [building materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_construction), as a [food source](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_shoot), and as a raw product, and depicted often in arts, such as in [bamboo paintings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_painting) and [bambooworking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambooworking). Bamboo, like [wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood), is a natural [composite material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Composite_material) with a high strength-to-weight ratio useful for structures.[^cite_note-Lakkad-19][^cite_note-20] Bamboo's strength-to-weight ratio is similar to [timber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timber), and its strength is generally similar to a strong [softwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softwood) or [hardwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardwood) timber.[^cite_note-Kaminski1-21][^cite_note-Kaminski2-22] Some bamboo species have displayed remarkable strength under test conditions. *[Bambusa tulda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambusa_tulda)* of [Bangladesh](3454.md) and adjoining [India](14533.md) has tested as high as 60,000 psi (400 MPa) in [tensile strength](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tensile_strength).[^cite_note-23] Other bamboo species make extraordinarily hard material.

| [Phylogeny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogeny) of the bamboo within the BOP [clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clade) of grasses, as suggested by analyses of the whole of Poaceae[^cite_note-GPWGII-24] and of the bamboos in particular[^cite_note-Kelchner2013-1] |

Bamboos have long been considered the most basal grass genera, mostly because of the presence of [bracteate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bract), indeterminate inflorescences, "pseudospikelets", and flowers with three [lodicules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodicule), six [stamens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stamens), and three [stigmata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stigma_\(botany\)).[^cite_note-Clark,_LG_1995-25] Following more recent [molecular phylogenetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molecular_phylogenetic) research, many [tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe_\(biology\)) and genera of grasses formerly included in the Bambusoideae are now classified in other subfamilies, e.g. the [Anomochlooideae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anomochlooideae), the [Puelioideae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puelioideae), and the [Ehrhartoideae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehrhartoideae). The subfamily in its current sense belongs to the [BOP clade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BOP_clade) of grasses, where it is sister to the [Pooideae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pooideae) (bluegrasses and relatives).[^cite_note-GPWGII-24]

The bamboos comprise three clades classified as tribes, and these strongly correspond with geographic divisions representing the New World herbaceous species ([Olyreae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olyreae)), tropical woody bamboos ([Bambuseae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambuseae)), and temperate woody bamboos ([Arundinarieae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arundinarieae)).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-Ayer_100027-5] The woody bamboos do not form a [monophyletic group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monophyletic_group); instead, the tropical woody and herbaceous bamboos are sister to the temperate woody bamboos.[^cite_note-Kelchner2013-1][^cite_note-GPWGII-24] Altogether, more than 1,400 species are placed in 115 genera.[^cite_note-Kelchner2013-1]

The bamboo taxonomy is in agreement with molecular phylogeny based on [chloroplast DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plastid_DNA), reflecting the maternal line of descent. The nuclear genome of bamboos shows a more complex pattern of branching due to the presence of *subgenomes* from ancient [hybridization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_\(biology\)) events leading to [allopolyploidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allopolyploidy). The herbaceous bamboos are [diploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ploidy) (HH), the temperate woody ones are [tetraploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyploidy) (CCDD), the [neotropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neotropical_realm) woody ones are tetraploid (BBCC), and the paleotropical woody ones are [hexaploid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyploidy) (AABBCC).[^cite_note-27]

## Distribution



Worldwide distribution of bamboos (Bambusoideae)

Most bamboo species are native to warm and moist [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical) and to warm temperate climates.[^cite_note-28] Their range also extends to cool mountainous regions and highland [cloud forests](58903.md).[^cite_note-29]

In the [Asia-Pacific region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific), they occur across East Asia, from north to 50 °N latitude in [Sakhalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakhalin),[^cite_note-30] to south to [northern Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Australia), and west to India and the [Himalayas](37966.md). China, Japan, Korea, India and Australia, all have several endemic populations.[^cite_note-Bystriakova_et_al-31] They also occur in small numbers in [sub-Saharan Africa](27067.md), confined to tropical areas, from southern Senegal in the north to southern [Mozambique](19301.md) and [Madagascar](18964.md) in the south.[^cite_note-gorilla-32] In the Americas, bamboo has a native range from 47 °S in southern Argentina and the beech forests of central [Chile](5489.md), through the South American tropical rainforests, to the [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes) in Ecuador near 4,300 m (14,000 ft), with a noticeable gap through the [Atacama Desert](18952975.md).

Three species of bamboo, all in the genus *Arundinaria*, are native to the [Southeastern United States](816925.md).[^cite_note-33] Bamboo thickets called [canebrakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canebrake) once formed a dominant ecosystem in some parts of the Southeastern United States, but they are now considered critically endangered ecosystems.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36] Canada and continental Europe are not known to have any native species of bamboo.[^cite_note-rhs-37] Many species are also cultivated as garden plants outside of this range, including in Europe and areas of North America where no native wild bamboo exists.

Recently, some attempts have been made to grow bamboo on a commercial basis in the [Great Lakes region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Great_Lakes) of east-central Africa, especially in Rwanda.[^cite_note-38] In the United States, several companies are growing, harvesting, and distributing species such as *[Phyllostachys nigra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllostachys_nigra)* (Henon) and *[Phyllostachys edulis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllostachys_edulis)* (Moso).[^cite_note-39]

Bamboo canopy

The two general patterns for the growth of bamboo are "clumping", and "running", with short and long underground rhizomes, respectively. Clumping bamboo species tend to spread slowly, as the growth pattern of the rhizomes is to simply expand the root mass gradually, similar to ornamental grasses. Running bamboos need to be controlled during cultivation because of their potential for aggressive behavior. They spread mainly through their [rhizomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhizome), which can spread widely underground and send up new culms to break through the surface.[^cite_note-40] Running bamboo species are highly variable in their tendency to spread; this is related to the species, [soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil) and [climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate) conditions. Some send out runners of several meters a year, while others stay in the same general area for long periods. If neglected, over time, they can cause problems by moving into adjacent areas.

Bamboos include some of the fastest-growing plants on Earth, with reported growth rates up to 910 mm (36 in) in 24 hours.[^cite_note-Guinness-14] These depend on local soil and climatic conditions, as well as species, and a more typical growth rate for many commonly cultivated bamboos in temperate climates is in the range of 30–100 mm (1–4 in) per day during the growing period. Some of the largest timber bamboo grow over 30 m (100 ft) tall, and be as large as 250–300 mm (10–12 in) in diameter. The size range for mature bamboo is species-dependent, with the smallest bamboos reaching only several inches high at maturity. A typical height range covering many of the common bamboos grown in the United States is 4.5–12 m (15–39 ft), depending on species. [Anji County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anji_County) of China, known as the "Town of Bamboo", provides the optimal climate and soil conditions to grow, harvest, and process some of the most valued bamboo poles available worldwide.

Unlike all trees, individual bamboo [culms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culm_\(botany\)) emerge from the ground at their full diameter and grow to their full height in a single [growing season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growing_season) of three to four months. During this time, each new [shoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shoot_\(botany\)) grows vertically into a culm with no branching out until the majority of the mature height is reached. Then, the branches extend from the nodes and leafing out occurs. In the next year, the pulpy wall of each culm slowly hardens. During the third year, the culm hardens further. The shoot is now a fully mature culm. Over the next 2–5 years (depending on species), fungus begins to form on the outside of the culm, which eventually penetrates and overcomes the culm.[^cite_note-41] About 5–8 years later (species- and climate-dependent), the fungal growths cause the culm to collapse and decay. This brief life means culms are ready for harvest and suitable for use in construction within about three to seven years. Individual bamboo culms do not get any taller or larger in diameter in subsequent years than they do in their first year, and they do not replace any growth lost from pruning or natural breakage. Bamboo has a wide range of hardiness depending on species and locale. Small or young specimens of an individual species produce small culms initially. As the clump and its rhizome system mature, taller and larger culms are produced each year until the plant approaches its particular species limits of height and diameter.

Many tropical bamboo species die at or near freezing temperatures, while some of the hardier temperate bamboos survive temperatures as low as −29 °C (−20 °F). Some of the hardiest bamboo species are grown in [USDA plant hardiness zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone) 5,[^cite_note-42] although they typically defoliate and may even lose all above-ground growth, yet the rhizomes survive and send up shoots again the next spring. In milder climates, such as USDA zone 7 and above, most bamboo remain fully leafed out and green year-round.

### Mass flowering

Further information: [Bamboo blossom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_blossom)



Flowering bamboo



*[Phyllostachys glauca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllostachys_glauca)* 'Yunzhu' in flower



Bunches of bamboo seeds

Bamboos seldom and unpredictably flower and the frequency of flowering varies greatly from species to species. Once flowering takes place, a plant declines and often dies entirely. In fact, many species only flower at intervals as long as 65 or 120 years. These taxa exhibit [mass flowering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_flowering) (or gregarious flowering), with all plants in a particular 'cohort' flowering over a several-year period. Any plant derived through clonal propagation from this cohort will also flower regardless of whether it has been planted in a different location. The longest mass flowering interval known is 120 years, and it is for the species *[Phyllostachys bambusoides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllostachys_bambusoides)* (Sieb. & Zucc.).[^cite_note-43] In this species, all plants of the same stock flower at the same time, regardless of differences in geographic locations or climatic conditions, and then the bamboo dies.[^cite_note-44] The commercially important bamboo Guadua, or Cana brava (*[Guadua angustifolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guadua_angustifolia)*) bloomed for the first time in recorded history in 1971, suggesting a blooming interval well in excess of 130 years. The lack of environmental impact on the time of flowering indicates the presence of some sort of "alarm clock" in each cell of the plant which signals the diversion of all energy to flower production and the cessation of vegetative growth.[^cite_note-Soderstrom,_TR_1979-45] This mechanism, as well as the evolutionary cause behind it, is still largely a mystery.

### Invasive species

Because of their ability to rapidly populate and grow, some bamboo species are acknowledged as having high potential for becoming [invasive species](67554.md). A study commissioned by [International Bamboo and Rattan Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bamboo_and_Rattan_Organization), found that invasive species typically are varieties that spread via [rhizomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhizome) rather than by clumping, as most commercially viable woody bamboos do.[^cite_note-46] In the United States, the National Invasive Species Information Center agency of the [Department of Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Department_of_Agriculture) has golden bamboo (*[Phyllostachys aurea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllostachys_aurea)*) listed as an invasive species.[^cite_note-47] Other potentially invasive species include, but are not limited to, *Actinocladum verticillatum, Bambusa tuldoides, Bambusa vulgaris, Guadua sarcocarpa, Guadua tagoara, Phyllostachys aurea,* and *Phyllostachys pubescens*, all of which spread primarily through rhizomes. The harmful effects of these species include raising the pH of the soil, cramping and shading out native plants, hindering the spread of native seeds, altering food chains, and siphoning nutrients from other plants. As bamboo farming and cultivation become more prominent in modern agriculture, it is important to closely monitor the spread of potentially invasive bamboo species.[^cite_note-48]

### Animal diet



Bamboo is the main food of the [giant panda](12713.md), making up about 99% of its diet.

[^cite_note-49]

Bamboo contains large amounts of protein and very low amounts of carbohydrates allowing this plant to be the source of food for many animals.[^cite_note-50] Soft [bamboo shoots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_shoot), stems and leaves are the major food source of the [giant panda](12713.md)[^cite_note-51] of China, the [red panda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_panda)[^cite_note-52] of [Nepal](171166.md), and the [bamboo lemurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_lemur) of [Madagascar](18964.md).[^cite_note-Suresh-2023-53] The red panda can eat up to 9 pounds (4.1 kg) a day which is also about the full body weight of the animal.[^cite_note-Suresh-2023-53] With raw bamboo containing trace amounts of harmful cyanide with higher concentrations in bamboo shoots, the [golden bamboo lemur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_bamboo_lemur) ingests many times the quantity of the [taxiphyllin](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/taxiphyllin)-containing bamboo that would be lethal to a human.[^cite_note-Suresh-2023-53]

[Mountain gorillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_gorilla) of [Central Africa](539606.md) also feed on bamboo, and have been documented consuming bamboo [sap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sap) which was fermented and alcoholic;[^cite_note-gorilla-32] [chimpanzees](7844.md) and [elephants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant) of the region also eat the stalks. The larvae of the [bamboo borer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_borer) (the [moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moth) *[Omphisa fuscidentalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omphisa_fuscidentalis)*) of [Laos](17752.md), [Myanmar](19457.md), [Thailand](30128.md) and [Yunnan, China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan,_China) feed off the pulp of live bamboo. In turn, these [caterpillars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caterpillar) are considered a [local delicacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entomophagy). Bamboo is also used for livestock feed with research showing some bamboo varieties have higher protein content over other varieties of bamboo.[^cite_note-54]

### General

This section is an excerpt from [Bamboo cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_cultivation).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bamboo_cultivation\&action=edit)]



African Bamboo Product Innovation Lab where bamboo farming techniques and industrial uses are tested inside the company's facility in Addis Ababa, Ethiopia

[Bamboo forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_cultivation) (also known as bamboo farming, cultivation, agriculture or agroforestry) is a cultivation and raw material industry that provides the raw materials for the broader bamboo industry, worth over 72 billion dollars globally in 2019.[^cite_note-Bamboo_cultivation_\:0-55]

Historically a dominant raw material in South and South East Asia, the global bamboo industry has significantly grown in recent decades in part because of the high sustainability of bamboo as compared to other biomass cultivation strategies, such as traditional timber [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry). For example, as of 2016, the U.S. Fiber corporation Resource Fiber is contracting farmers in the United States for bamboo cultivation.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-Bamboo_cultivation_\:0-55] Or in 2009, [United Nations Industrial Development Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Industrial_Development_Organization) published guidelines for cultivation of bamboo in semi-arid climates in Ethiopia and Kenya.[^cite_note-57]

Because bamboo can grow on otherwise [marginal land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marginal_land), bamboo can be profitably cultivated in many degraded lands.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] Moreover, because of the rapid growth, bamboo is an effective [climate change mitigation](2119179.md) and [carbon sequestration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_sequestration) crop, absorbing between 100 and 400 tonnes of carbon per hectare (40–160 tonnes per acre).[^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] In 1997, an international intergovernmental organization was established to promote the development of bamboo cultivation, the [International Bamboo and Rattan Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Bamboo_and_Rattan_Organisation).[^cite_note-62]

Bamboo is harvested from both cultivated and wild stands, and some of the larger bamboos, particularly species in the genus *[Phyllostachys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllostachys)*, are known as "timber bamboos". Bamboo is typically harvested as a source material for construction, food, crafts and other manufactured goods.[^cite_note-63]

Bamboo cultivation in South, South East Asia and East Asia stretches back thousands of years. One practice, in South Korea, has been designated as a [Globally Important Agricultural Heritage Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globally_Important_Agricultural_Heritage_Systems).

In Brazil, the [Brazilian Center for Innovation and Sustainability - CEBIS](http://www.cebis.org.br/), a non-profit organization, promotes the development of Brazil's bamboo production chain. Last year, it helped with the approval of law n\~21,162 in the state of [Paraná](229411.md), which encourages Bamboo Culture aiming at the dissemination of its agricultural cultivation and the valorization of bamboo as an instrument for promoting the sustainable socioeconomic development of the State through its multiple functionalities. Bamboo cultivation neutralizes carbon emissions. Bamboo cultivation is cheap and in addition to adding value to its production chain, it is a sustainable crop that brings environmental, economic and social benefits. Its production can be used from construction to food. Recently, it was qualified and classified for the National Commission for Sustainable Development Objectives - CNDOS of the Presidency of the Republic of the federal government of Brazil.

### Harvesting



Bamboo harvested at [Murshidabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murshidabad)



Harvested bamboo transported by river near Ramsai, [Jalpaiguri](2186661.md)

Bamboo used for construction purposes must be harvested when the culms reach their greatest strength and when sugar levels in the [sap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sap) are at their lowest, as high sugar content increases the ease and rate of [pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest_\(organism\)) infestation. As compared to forest trees, bamboo species grow fast. Bamboo plantations can be readily harvested for a shorter period than tree plantations.[^cite_note-64]

Harvesting of bamboo is typically undertaken according to these cycles:

- **Lifecycle of the culm**: As each individual [culm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culm_\(botany\)) goes through a five to seven-year lifecycle, they are ideally allowed to reach this level of maturity prior to full capacity harvesting. The clearing out or thinning of culms, particularly older decaying culms, helps to ensure adequate light and resources for new growth. Well-maintained clumps may have a productivity three to four times that of an unharvested wild clump. Consistent with the lifecycle described above, bamboo is harvested from two to three years through to five to seven years, depending on the species.

  [^cite_note-65]

  [^cite_note-66]

- **Annual cycle**: Most growth of new bamboo occurs during the [wet season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_season) and disturbing the clump during this phase will potentially damage the upcoming crop, while harvesting immediately prior to the wet/growth season may also damage new shoots, therefore harvesting is best a few months prior to the start of the wet season.

  [^cite_note-67]

  &#x20;Also during this high-rainfall period, sap levels are at their highest, and then diminish towards the [dry season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_season).

  [^cite_note-68]

- **Daily cycle**: During the height of the day, [photosynthesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photosynthesis) is at its peak, producing the highest levels of sugar in sap, making this the least ideal time of day to harvest and many traditional practitioners believe the best time to harvest is at dawn or dusk on a waning moon.

  [^cite_note-69]

### Leaching

Leaching is the removal of sap after harvest. In many areas of the world, the sap levels in harvested bamboo are reduced either through leaching or post-harvest photosynthesis. For example:[^cite_note-70]

- Cut bamboo is raised clear of the ground and leaned against the rest of the clump for one to two weeks until leaves turn yellow to allow full consumption of sugars by the plant.

- A similar method is undertaken, but with the base of the culm standing in fresh water, either in a large drum or stream to leach out sap.

- Cut culms are immersed in a running stream and weighted down for three to four weeks.

- Water is pumped through the freshly cut culms, forcing out the sap (this method is often used in conjunction with the injection of some form of treatment).

In the process of water leaching, the bamboo is dried slowly and evenly in the shade to avoid cracking in the outer skin of the bamboo, thereby reducing opportunities for pest infestation.[^cite_note-71]

Durability of bamboo in construction is directly related to how well it is handled from the moment of planting through harvesting, transportation, storage, design, construction, and maintenance. Bamboo harvested at the correct time of year and then exposed to ground contact or rain will break down just as quickly as incorrectly harvested material.[^cite_note-72]

## Toxicity

Gardeners working with bamboo plants have occasionally reported [allergic reactions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allergic_response) varying from no effects during previous exposures, to immediate itchiness and rash developing into red welts after several hours where the skin had been in contact with the plant ([contact allergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_dermatitis)), and in some cases into swollen eyelids and breathing difficulties (dyspnea). A [skin prick test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_allergy_test) using bamboo extract was positive for the [immunoglobulin E (IgE)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunoglobulin_E) in an available case study.[^cite_note-Kitajima1986-73][^cite_note-Hipler2005-74][^cite_note-Garneners2012-75]

In addition, the shoots (newly emerged culms) of bamboo contain the toxin taxiphyllin (a [cyanogenic glycoside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanogenic_glycoside)), which produces [cyanide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyanide) in the gut.[^cite_note-76] Taxiphyllin levels are highest in young shoots. The level of the toxicant declines with the advancement of maturity. Proper cooking (boiling / steaming for a minimum of 20 minutes with several changes of water) breaks down the glycoside and releases [hydrogen cyanide gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_cyanide).[^cite_note-77] Ultimately, this renders the shoots harmless. Bamboo shoots, eaten raw or undercooked, can cause symptoms of acute [cyanide poisoning](13798915.md), including nausea, dizziness, rapid heartbeat, and in some cases coma or death, though this is rare with the traditional methods employed in Asian cuisines.[^cite_note-78] Individuals with a sensitivity to cyanide, such as those with [G6PD deficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glucose-6-phosphate_dehydrogenase_deficiency), should avoid them altogether, even when cooked.

[Contact dermatitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_dermatitis) due to bamboo leaves or culms appears to be [immunoglobulin E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunoglobulin_E)-mediated in sensitized individuals, although specific allergens are unknown.[^cite_note-79] Possible suspects include silica bodies, phenolic compounds or a fungal contaminant on the plant surface. As a result, gloves and long sleeves are typically recommended when using the herbicide. Moreover, topical corticosteroids or antihistamines may be applied to the skin if there is a mild reaction.[^cite_note-80] In individuals sensitive to bamboo, the pollen can also occasionally cause respiratory allergies during the flowering season.

## Uses

### Culinary



Unprocessed bamboo shoots in a Japanese market



Korean bamboo tea

The shoots of most species are edible either raw or cooked, with the tough sheath removed. Cooking removes the slight bitterness.[^cite_note-Skyhorse_Publishing-2009-81] The shoots are used in numerous Asian dishes and broths, and are available in supermarkets in various sliced forms, in both fresh and canned versions.

The empty hollow in the stalks of larger bamboo is often used to cook food in many Asian cultures. Soups are boiled and rice is cooked in the hollows of fresh stalks of bamboo directly over a flame. Similarly, steamed tea is sometimes rammed into bamboo hollows to produce [compressed forms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_tea) of [pu'er tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pu'er_tea). Cooking food in bamboo is said to give the food a subtle but distinctive taste.[^cite_note-82]

#### India

The bamboo shoot in its fermented state forms an important ingredient in cuisines across the Himalayas. In [Assam](186162.md), India, for example, it is called *khorisa*.[^cite_note-83] In [Nepal](171166.md), a delicacy popular across ethnic boundaries consists of bamboo shoots fermented with [turmeric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turmeric) and oil, and cooked with potatoes into a dish that usually accompanies rice (*alu tama*[^cite_note-84] (आलु तामा) in [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language)).

In [Sambalpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambalpur), India, the tender shoots are grated into [juliennes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julienning) and [fermented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermentation_\(food\)) to prepare *kardi*. The name is derived from the Sanskrit word for bamboo shoot, *karira*. This fermented bamboo shoot is used in various culinary preparations, notably *amil*, a sour vegetable soup. It is also made into pancakes using [rice flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_flour) as a binding agent.[^cite_note-85] The shoots that have turned a little fibrous are fermented, dried, and ground to sand-sized particles to prepare a garnish known as *hendua*. It is also cooked with tender pumpkin leaves to make sag green leaves.

The Indian state of [Sikkim](140372.md) has promoted bamboo water bottles to keep the state free from plastic bottles.[^cite_note-86]

In [Konkani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkani_people) cuisine, the tender shoots (*kirlu*) are grated and cooked with crushed jackfruit seeds to prepare *kirla sukke*.

In southern India and some regions of southwest China, the seeds of the dying bamboo plant are consumed as a grain known as "bamboo rice". The taste of cooked bamboo seeds is reported to be similar to wheat and the appearance similar to rice, but bamboo seeds have been found to have lower nutrient levels than both.[^cite_note-87] The seeds can be pulverized into a flour with which to make cakes.[^cite_note-Skyhorse_Publishing-2009-81]

#### Indonesia

In [Indonesia](14579.md), they are sliced thin and then boiled with *santan* (thick coconut milk) and spices to make a dish called *gulai rebung*. Other recipes using bamboo shoots are *[sayur lodeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sayur_lodeh)* (mixed vegetables in coconut milk) and *lun pia* (sometimes written *[lumpia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumpia)*: fried wrapped bamboo shoots with vegetables). The shoots of some species contain toxins that need to be leached or boiled out before they can be eaten safely.[^cite_note-88]

Pickled bamboo, used as a condiment, may also be made from the [pith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pith) of the young shoots.[^cite_note-89]

#### China

The sap of young stalks tapped during the rainy season may be [fermented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermentation_\(wine\)) to make *ulanzi* (a sweet wine)[^cite_note-90] or made into a soft drink.[^cite_note-91] Bamboo leaves are also used as wrappers for steamed [dumplings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zongzi) which usually contain glutinous rice and other ingredients, such as the zongzi from China.[^cite_note-92]



*Khao lam* ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): ข้าวหลาม) is [glutinous rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutinous_rice) with sugar and coconut cream cooked in specially prepared bamboo sections of different diameters and lengths.

#### Nepal

Pickled bamboo shoots (Nepali: तामा *tama*) are cooked with black-eyed beans as a delicacy in Nepal. Many Nepalese restaurants around the world serve this dish as [Aloo tama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aloo_tama).[^cite_note-93] Fresh bamboo shoots are sliced and pickled with mustard seeds and turmeric and kept in a glass jar in direct sunlight for the best taste. It is used alongside many dried beans in cooking during winters. Baby shoots ([Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language): *tusa*) of a very different variety of bamboo (Nepali: निगालो *Nigalo*) native to Nepal is cooked as a curry in hilly regions.



In [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Timor), cooking food in bamboo is called *tukir*.

### Fuel

Bamboo charcoal

[Bamboo charcoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_charcoal) is [charcoal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charcoal) made from species of bamboo. It is typically made from the [culms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culm_\(botany\)) or refuse of mature bamboo plants and burned in ovens at temperatures ranging from 600 to 1,200 °C (1,100 to 2,200 °F). ### Working

&#x20;Bamboo&#x20;

[Bambooworking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambooworking) is the activity or skill of making items from bamboo, and includes [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture), [carpentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpentry), [furniture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Furniture) and [cabinetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinetry), [carving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carving), [joinery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joinery_\(woodworking\)), and [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving). Its historical roots in Asia span cultures, civilizations, and millennia, and is found across [East](19605700.md), [South](21566765.md), and [Southeast Asia](28741.md).

#### Writing surface

Further information: [Bamboo and wooden slips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_and_wooden_slips)

Bamboo was in widespread use in early China as a medium for written documents. The earliest surviving examples of such documents, written in ink on string-bound bundles of bamboo strips (or "slips"), date from the fifth century BC during the [Warring States period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warring_States_period). References in earlier texts surviving on other media indicate some precursor of these Warring States period bamboo slips was used as early as the late [Shang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang) period (from about 1250 BC).

Bamboo or wooden strips were used as the standard writing material during the early [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty), and excavated examples have been found in abundance.[^cite_note-97] Subsequently, [paper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_paper) began to displace bamboo and wooden strips from mainstream uses, and by the fourth century AD, bamboo slips had been largely abandoned as a medium for writing in China.[^cite_note-98]

Bamboo fiber has been used to make paper in China since early times. A high-quality, handmade bamboo paper is still produced in small quantities. Coarse bamboo paper is still used to make [spirit money](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joss_paper) in many Chinese communities.[^cite_note-spiritpaper-99]

Bamboo [pulps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_\(paper\)) are mainly produced in China, [Myanmar](19457.md), Thailand, and India, and are used in [printing and writing papers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_and_writing_paper).[^cite_note-Market_pulp-100] Several paper industries are surviving on [bamboo forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_forests). [Ballarpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballarpur) Chandrapur, Maharashtra paper mills use bamboo for paper production. The most common bamboo species used for paper are *[Dendrocalamus asper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dendrocalamus_asper)* and *[Bambusa blumeana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambusa_blumeana)*. It is also possible to make [dissolving pulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolving_pulp) from bamboo. The average fiber length is similar to [hardwoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardwoods), but the properties of bamboo pulp are closer to [softwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softwood) pulps due to it having a very broad fiber length distribution.[^cite_note-Market_pulp-100] With the help of molecular tools, it is now possible to distinguish the superior fiber-yielding species/varieties even at juvenile stages of their growth, which can help in unadulterated merchandise production.[^cite_note-101]

In Central India, there are regular bamboo working circles in forest areas of [Maharashtra](20629.md), [Madhya Pradesh](47945.md), [Odisha](250724.md) and [Chhattisgarh](47734.md). Most of the bamboo is harvested for [papermaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papermaking). Bamboo is cut after three years of its germination. No cutting is done during the rainy season (July–September); broken and malformed culms are harvested first.[^cite_note-Working_Plan-102]

#### Writing pen

Main article: [Reed pen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_pen)

In olden times, people in India used hand-made pens (known as Kalam or boru (बोरू)) made from thin bamboo sticks (with diameters of 5–10 mm and lengths of 100–150 mm) by simply peeling them on one side and making a nib-like pattern at the end. The pen would then be dipped in ink for writing.[^cite_note-103]

#### Textiles

Since the fibers of bamboo are very short (less than 3 mm or 1⁄8 in), they are not usually transformed into yarn by a natural process. The usual process by which textiles labeled as being made of bamboo are produced uses only [rayon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayon) made from the fibers with heavy employment of chemicals. To accomplish this, the fibers are broken down with chemicals and extruded through mechanical spinnerets; the chemicals include [lye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lye), [carbon disulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_disulfide), and strong acids.[^cite_note-Scientific_American-104] Retailers have sold both end products as "bamboo fabric" to cash in on bamboo's current ecofriendly cachet. The Canadian [Competition Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Competition_Bureau)[^cite_note-ccb-105] and the US [Federal Trade Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Trade_Commission),[^cite_note-FTC-106] as of mid-2009, are cracking down on the practice of labeling bamboo rayon as natural bamboo fabric. Under the guidelines of both agencies, these products must be labeled as rayon with the optional qualifier "from bamboo".[^cite_note-FTC-106]

##### Fabric

This section is an excerpt from [Bamboo textile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_textile).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Bamboo_textile\&action=edit)]



A scarf made of bamboo yarn and synthetic ribbon

[Bamboo textile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_textile) is any cloth, yarn or clothing made from bamboo fibres.

Modern textiles which claim to be made from bamboo are usually made from viscose [rayon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rayon) instead, a regenerated cellulose fibre using wood pulp as its raw material. Many jurisdictions prohibit labeling such products as bamboo unless they contain actual bamboo fiber. This process removes the natural characteristics of the raw material, rendering it identical to rayon from other cellulose sources.

#### Construction

Further information: [Bamboo construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_construction)

Bamboo, like true [wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood), is a natural [building material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Building_material) with a high strength-to-weight ratio useful for structures.[^cite_note-Lakkad-19] In its natural form, bamboo as a construction material is traditionally associated with the cultures of South Asia, East Asia, and the South Pacific, to some extent in Central and South America, particularly in Colombia and Ecuador,[^cite_note-107] and by extension in the aesthetic of [Tiki culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiki_culture).[^cite_note-108]

In China and India, bamboo was used to hold up [simple suspension bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simple_suspension_bridge), either by making cables of split bamboo or twisting whole culms of sufficiently pliable bamboo together. One such bridge in the area of Qian-Xian is referenced in writings dating back to 960 AD and may have stood since as far back as the third century BC, due largely to continuous maintenance.[^cite_note-109]



Bamboo has long been used as an assembly material in [Hong Kong](13404.md) because of its versatility.

Bamboo has also long been used as scaffolding; the practice has been banned in China for buildings over six stories, but is still in continuous use for skyscrapers in Hong Kong.[^cite_note-nytimes-110]



A modern resort guesthouse in [Palawan](240395.md), Philippines, with traditional woven bamboo walls ([sawali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawali))

In the Philippines, the [nipa hut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nipa_hut) is a fairly typical example of the most basic sort of [housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House) where bamboo is used; the walls are split and woven bamboo, and bamboo slats and poles may be used as its support.[^cite_note-111]

In [Japanese architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_architecture), bamboo is used primarily as a supplemental or decorative element in buildings such as fencing, fountains, grates, and gutters, largely due to the ready abundance of quality timber.[^cite_note-BambooJapan-112]

Many ethnic groups in remote areas that have water access in Asia use bamboo that is 3–5 years old to make rafts. They use 8 to 12 poles, 6–7 m (20–23 ft) long, laid together side by side to a width of about 1 m (3 ft). Once the poles are lined up together, they cut a hole crosswise through the poles at each end and use a small bamboo pole pushed through that hole like a screw to hold all the long bamboo poles together. Floating houses use whole bamboo stalks tied together in a big bunch to support the house floating in the water.[^cite_note-113]

#### Firecrackers

Bamboo has been traditionally used in [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Indonesia](14579.md) as a firecracker called a *meriam buluh* or [bamboo cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_cannon). Four-foot-long sections of bamboo are cut, and a mixture of water and [calcium carbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calcium_carbide) are introduced. The resulting acetylene gas is ignited with a stick, producing a loud bang.[^cite_note-115]

A [bamboo steamer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_steamer), used to cook foods like [dim sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dim_sum)

### China



*Bamboo*, by [Xu Wei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Wei), [Ming Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_Dynasty)

Bamboo's long life makes it a Chinese symbol of uprightness and an Indian symbol of friendship. The rarity of its [blossoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamboo_blossom) has led to the flowers' being regarded as a sign of impending famine. This may be due to rats feeding upon the profusion of flowers, then multiplying and destroying a large part of the local food supply. The most recent flowering began in May 2006 (see [Mautam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mautam)). Various bamboo species bloom in this manner about every 28–60 years.[^cite_note-138]

In [Chinese culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_China), the bamboo, [plum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prunus_mume) blossom, [orchid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchid), and [chrysanthemum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chrysanthemum) (often known as *méilánzhújú* 梅蘭竹菊 in Chinese) are collectively referred to as the [Four Gentlemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Gentlemen).[^cite_note-139] These four plants also represent the four seasons and, in [Confucian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confucianism) ideology, four aspects of the *[junzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junzi)* ("prince" or "noble one"). The [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine) (*sōng* 松), the bamboo (*zhú* 竹), and the plum blossom (*méi* 梅) are also admired for their perseverance under harsh conditions, and are together known as the "[Three Friends of Winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Friends_of_Winter)" (*suìhán sānyǒu* 歲寒三友) in Chinese culture.[^cite_note-140]

#### Attributions of character



A cylindrical bamboo brush holder or holder of poems on scrolls, created by Zhang Xihuang in the 17th century, late Ming or early Qing Dynasty – in the [calligraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_calligraphy) of Zhang's style, the poem *Returning to My Farm in the Field* by the fourth-century poet [Tao Yuanming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao_Yuanming) is incised on the holder.



Photo of carved Chinese bamboo wall vase. 1918. Brooklyn Museum Archives, Goodyear Archival Collection.

Bamboo, one of the "[Four Gentlemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Gentlemen)" (bamboo, orchid, plum blossom and chrysanthemum), plays such an important role in traditional Chinese culture that it is even regarded as a behavior model of the gentleman.[^cite_note-141] As bamboo has features such as uprightness, tenacity, and modesty, people endow bamboo with integrity, elegance, and plainness, though it is not physically strong. Countless poems praising bamboo written by ancient Chinese poets are actually metaphorically about people who exhibited these characteristics. An ancient poet, [Bai Juyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_Juyi) (772–846), thought that to be a gentleman, a man does not need to be physically strong, but he must be mentally strong, upright, and perseverant.[^cite_note-142] Just as a bamboo is hollow-hearted, he should open his heart to accept anything of benefit and never have arrogance or prejudice.

Bamboo is not only a symbol of a gentleman, but also plays an important role in Buddhism, which was introduced into China in the first century. As canons of Buddhism forbid cruelty to animals, flesh and egg were not allowed in the diet. The tender bamboo shoot (*sǔn* 筍 in Chinese) thus became a nutritious alternative. Preparation methods developed over thousands of years have come to be incorporated into Asian cuisines, especially for monks. A Buddhist monk, Zan Ning, wrote a manual of the bamboo shoot called *Sǔn Pǔ* (筍譜) offering descriptions and recipes for many kinds of bamboo shoots.[^cite_note-143] Bamboo shoots have traditionally been a dish on the Chinese dinner table, especially in southern China. In ancient times, those who could afford a big house with a yard would plant bamboo in their garden.

#### Mythology

In a Chinese legend, the [Emperor Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Yao) gave two of his daughters to the future [Emperor Shun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Shun) as a test for his potential to rule. Shun passed the test of being able to run his household with the two emperor's daughters as wives, and thus Yao made Shun his successor, bypassing his unworthy son. After Shun's death, the tears of his two bereaved wives fell upon the bamboos growing there explains the origin of [spotted bamboo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spotted_bamboo). The two women later became goddesses [Xiangshuishen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangshuishen) after drowning themselves in the [Xiang River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_River).

### Japan



*Bamboo [kadomatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadomatsu)* made for [Japanese New Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_New_Year)

Bamboo is a symbol of prosperity in Japan, and is used to make [New Year's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_New_Year) decorations called *[kadomatsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadomatsu)*. Bamboo forests sometimes surround [Shinto shrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinja_\(Shinto\)) and Buddhist temples as part of a sacred barrier against [evil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evil). In the [folktale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_folktales) *[Tale of the Bamboo Cutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tale_of_the_Bamboo_Cutter)* (*Taketori Monogatari*), princess Kaguya emerges from a shining bamboo section.[^cite_note-144]

In Japan, the Chinese "Three Friends of Winter" (*kansai sanyū*) concept is traditionally used as a ranking system, where pine (松 *matsu*) is the first rank, bamboo (竹 *take*) is the second rank, and plum (梅 *ume*) is the third rank. This system is used in many traditional arts like with [sushi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sushi) sets, embroidering kimono or tiers of accommodations at traditional *[ryōkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryokan_\(inn\))* taverns.

Bamboo is known to be a strong material and able to withstand extreme heat. It is the only plant known to have survived the [atomic bombings of Hiroshima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bombings_of_Hiroshima_and_Nagasaki) in 1945.[^cite_note-145]

### Vietnam



[Cây nêu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A2y_n%C3%AAu) – A Vietnamese New Year tree made from bamboo

#### Attributions of character

Bamboo plays an important part of the [culture of Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Vietnam). Bamboo symbolizes the spirit of [Vovinam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vovinam) (a Vietnamese martial arts): *cương nhu phối triển* (coordination between [hard and soft (martial arts)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_and_soft_\(martial_arts\))).[^cite_note-148] Bamboo also symbolizes the Vietnamese hometown and Vietnamese soul: the gentlemanlike, straightforwardness, hard working, optimism, unity, and adaptability. A Vietnamese proverb says, "*Tre già, măng mọc*" (When the bamboo is old, the bamboo sprouts appear), the meaning being Vietnam will never be annihilated; if the previous generation dies, the children take their place. Therefore, the Vietnamese nation and Vietnamese values will be maintained and developed eternally. Traditional Vietnamese villages are surrounded by thick bamboo hedges (*lũy tre*).[^cite_note-149]

During [Ngô Đình Diệm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ng%C3%B4_%C4%90%C3%ACnh_Di%E1%BB%87m)'s presidency, bamboo was the national symbol of [South Vietnam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Vietnam), it was featured on the national coat of arms, presidential standard, and South Vietnamese đồng coins at the time.

#### Mythology

A bamboo cane is also the weapon of [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_people) legendary hero, [Thánh Gióng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Th%C3%A1nh_Gi%C3%B3ng), who had grown up immediately and magically since the age of three because of his wish to liberate his land from [Ân](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shang_dynasty) invaders. The ancient Vietnamese legend *Cây tre trăm đốt* ([The Hundred-knot Bamboo Tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hundred-knot_Bamboo_Tree)) tells of a poor, young farmer who fell in love with his landlord's beautiful daughter. The farmer asked the landlord for his daughter's hand in marriage, but the proud landlord would not allow her to be bound in marriage to a poor farmer. The landlord decided to foil the marriage with an impossible deal; the farmer must bring him a "bamboo tree of 100 [nodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plant_stems)". But [Gautama Buddha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gautama_Buddha) (*Bụt*) appeared to the farmer and told him that such a tree could be made from 100 nodes from several different trees. *Bụt* gave to him four magic words to attach the many nodes of bamboo: *Khắc nhập, khắc xuất*, which means "joined together immediately, fell apart immediately". The triumphant farmer returned to the landlord and demanded his daughter. Curious to see such a long bamboo, the landlord was magically joined to the bamboo when he touched it, as the young farmer said the first two magic words. The story ends with the happy marriage of the farmer and the landlord's daughter after the landlord agreed to the marriage and asked to be separated from the bamboo.

### Africa

#### Tanzania

Tanzania possesses a large diversity of bamboo species.[^cite_note-150][^cite_note-Masisi_100009-17]

#### Bozo

The [Bozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozo_people) ethnic group of [West Africa](67393.md) take their name from the [Bambara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bambara_language) phrase *bo-so*, which means "bamboo house".[^cite_note-151]

### Saint Lucia

Bamboo is also the national plant of [St. Lucia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lucia).[^cite_note-152]

### Hawaiian

[Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawaiian_Islands) bamboo *('ohe)* is a *kinolau* or body form of the [Polynesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polynesian_mythology) creator god [Kāne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%81ne).

### North America

[Arundinaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arundinaria) bamboos, known as giant cane or river cane, are a central part of the material cultures of Southeastern Native American nations, so much so that they have been called "the plastic of the Southeastern Indians".[^cite_note-conservationgateway-153] Among the [Cherokee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherokee), river cane has been used to make waterproof baskets, mats, fishing poles, flutes, blowguns, arrows, and to build houses, among other uses; the seed and young shoots are also edible.[^cite_note-conservationgateway-153][^cite_note-154] Traditional Cherokee double-woven baskets, crafted from river cane that has been split and dyed in various colors, are sometimes considered among the finest in the world. Since the North American bamboos are now rare, with 98% of their original extent eliminated, the Cherokee have initiated an effort to restore it.[^cite_note-155]
