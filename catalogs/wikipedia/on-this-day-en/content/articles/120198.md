**Hopkins** is a [suburban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburban) city in [Hennepin County, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hennepin_County,_Minnesota), United States, located west of [Minneapolis](6097240.md). The population was 19,079 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-2020_Census_(City)-3] The city is four square miles in size and is surrounded by the larger suburban communities of [Minnetonka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnetonka,_Minnesota), [Saint Louis Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Park,_Minnesota), and [Edina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edina,_Minnesota). The city's main street was added to the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) in 2022 as the [Hopkins Commercial Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopkins_Commercial_Historic_District).

## Geography

According to the [United States Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau), the city has a total area of 4.09 square miles (10.59 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 4.06 square miles (10.52 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 0.03 square miles (0.08 km<sup>2</sup>) is water.[^cite_note-CenPopGazetteer2020-1] There are several small ponds on the western side of Hopkins, and creeks to the north and south. One of these creeks includes [Minnehaha Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnehaha_Creek). The north branch of Nine Mile Creek has its headwaters in Hopkins at the intersection of 13th Avenue South and Excelsior Boulevard.

The southern end of Hopkins is adjacent to main line of the [Twin Cities and Western Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_Cities_and_Western_Railroad). Two main highways, [U.S. Route 169](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_169_in_Minnesota) and [Minnesota State Highway 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_Highway_7), service the Hopkin's area.

Hopkins will have three stations on the [Metro Green Line Extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Green_Line_Extension_\(Minnesota\)). There will be stations at [Shady Oak Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shady_Oak_Road_station?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Downtown Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Hopkins_station), and at [Blake Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blake_Road_station?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-6]

## Housing

Hopkins has a much lower homeownership rate than neighboring communities. The city's 39 percent homeownership rate is 22 percentage points less than the 61 percent of St. Louis Park, which has the next lowest rate among Hopkins’ neighbors.[^cite_note-Community_Profile-13] Other west metro communities fall in the mid-70 percent range.

Housing also tends to be more affordable than most west metro communities. Hopkins’ \$225,200 median value is the lowest among its neighbors.[^cite_note-Community_Profile-13] The median housing value for St. Louis Park, which is next lowest, is 6.75 percent higher at \$240,400.

## Government

### City charter

Hopkins’ government structure is set by its city charter. Hennepin County district court judges appointed the first Hopkins Charter Commission on February 8, 1946, in order to create a proposed charter that would be voted on.[^cite_note-14] The commission submitted the proposed charter to the Village Council on November 4, 1947. Voters approved the charter December 2, 1947. The charter has been amended numerous times since then, most recently in October 2012.[^cite_note-hopkinsmn.com-15]

### Government structure

The charter specifies that Hopkins use a [council-manager plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council%E2%80%93manager_government). The council controls city administration but does so exclusively through the [city manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_manager).[^cite_note-City_Council_Structure_&_Salaries-16] The charter contains an “interference with administration” clause that expressly forbids the council from telling the city manager whom to hire or preventing the city manager from using his or her judgment to make administrative appointments.[^cite_note-hopkinsmn.com-15] It also prohibits the council from issuing orders to any of the city manager's subordinates.

#### Mayor and council

The council is made up of the mayor and four council members elected [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large). The mayor serves a two-year term in office. Council members have four-year terms—with two of the seats on the ballot in one election and the other two seats up in the following election. Regular elections take place in odd years.

The mayor votes on all motions before the council like the council members. The position is also the head of the city for ceremonial purposes, serving legal processes and martial law.

The mayor is paid \$6,000 per year and council members are paid \$4,600 per year.[^cite_note-City_Council_Structure_&_Salaries-16] The current salaries were set in 1998.

#### City manager

The city manager is the chief administrator of the City of Hopkins. The Hopkins City Council appoints the city manager for an indefinite period and may remove the manager at any time.

#### Subordinate employees

The charter specifies just two other administrative positions by name. It requires the city to have a clerk, who is subordinate to the city manager, and allows for, but doesn't require, a city attorney to advise the council on legal matters.

However, the charter specifies that the city manager can create city departments and divisions and alter them when necessary. Hopkins has seven departments, each with a department head that reports to the city manager.[^cite_note-18] The departments are:

- Administration

- Community Services (Assessing, City Clerk, Inspections)

- Finance

- Fire

- Planning & Economic Development

- Police

- Public Works

- Recreation (operated jointly with [Minnetonka, Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnetonka))

## History



Harley H. Hopkins, namesake of Hopkins, in 1855, note the [revolver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolver).

The first non-indigenous settlers in the Hopkins area arrived in 1852[^cite_note-21] as land around the growing [Minneapolis–Saint Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis%E2%80%93Saint_Paul) area was opening up and being explored by members stationed at [Fort Snelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Snelling). However, the roots of the town begin in 1887 with the building of the [Minneapolis Threshing Machine Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis_Threshing_Machine_Company?action=edit\&redlink=1), later called [Minneapolis-Moline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis-Moline), to make farm equipment. At the time, Minneapolis Moline employed most of the Hopkins residents. In 1887, the West Minneapolis Land Company was founded and formed to build housing for the Minneapolis Moline factory workers.

In 1893, residents of Hopkins sent the [Hennepin County Board of Commissioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hennepin_County,_Minnesota#Governance) a petition signed by 41 residents, asking that a separate [village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village) be formed from unincorporated portions of then-[Minnetonka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnetonka,_Minnesota) and [Richfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richfield,_Minnesota) Townships. Following an election, the community was then incorporated as the Village of West Minneapolis with a population of 1,105. The original village consisted of about three-square miles, and it has been enlarged by annexation to its present size of about four-square miles.



The Hopkins train station, which determined the town's eventual name, is now a student-run coffee house.

In 1928, the name of the village was changed to Hopkins after Harley H. Hopkins, who was among its first [homesteaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Act) and was the community's first [postmaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmaster). Mr. Hopkins allowed the town to build the train depot on his land in 1871 (now [The Depot Coffee House](http://www.thedepotcoffeehouse.com/)) with the agreement that the train station would say "Hopkins" on it, and in 1872 the post office was established with the same name. People getting off the train assumed the name of the town was Hopkins and it stuck. In August of 1928, the council changed the name of the town to Hopkins and on December 2, 1947, the village became the [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) of Hopkins, upon adoption of a council–city manager charter.[^cite_note-22]

Hopkins was the headquarters of [Minneapolis-Moline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minneapolis-Moline), which was a large manufacturer of tractors and agricultural equipment in the United States until the 1960s.[^cite_note-23]

### Hopkins Raspberry Festival

The Hopkins Raspberry Festival is an annual event in Hopkins. The Hopkins Raspberry Festival was founded in 1935 as a way to boost business during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) of the 1930s. A date of July 21 was chosen to hold the event to coincide with the peak of raspberry-picking season. The festival now takes place the third weekend in July every year.

The Raspberry Festival is overseen by a board of directors supported by many additional volunteers and local civic organizations each year. Since its inception, it has evolved into a dynamic community celebration with activities including music, sporting events, royalty coronations, craft fair, and parade.

### National Register of Historic Places

The main street of Hopkins, from 8th to 11th avenues, was officially listed on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places) in January 2022 as the [Hopkins Commercial Historic District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopkins_Commercial_Historic_District). The designation was revealed during a ceremony in April 2022.[^cite_note-26]

## Education

### Public schools

The [Hopkins School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hopkins_Public_Schools_\(Minnesota\)) serves all or parts of seven Minneapolis west suburban communities: Hopkins, [Minnetonka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnetonka,_Minnesota), [Golden Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Valley,_Minnesota), [Plymouth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plymouth,_Minnesota), [Edina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edina,_Minnesota), [Eden Prairie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Prairie,_Minnesota), and [Saint Louis Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Park,_Minnesota). Approximately 8,100 students attend seven [elementary schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elementary_schools) ([K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kindergarten)–[fifth grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_grade)), two [middle schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_school) ([6th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sixth_grade)–[8th grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_grade)), and one [high school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_school_\(North_America\)) ([9th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninth_grade)–[12th grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twelfth_grade)). Some students attend public schools in other school districts chosen by their families under Minnesota's open enrollment statute, as some students from outside Hopkins school district enroll in Hopkins schools on that basis.[^cite_note-27]
