**Starship flight test 13** was the thirteenth [flight test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_SpaceX_Starship_flight_tests) of the [SpaceX Starship](59118602.md) launch system, using [Booster 20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Super_Heavy_boosters#B19) and [Ship 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Starship_vehicles#Ship_39).[^cite_note-4]

During the initial launch attempt on July 16, 2026, the vehicle's flight software triggered an automatic abort at T−0 just prior to liftoff after six [Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Raptor) engines had problems with their oxidizer turbo-pumps and failed to meet flight speeds, protecting both the vehicle and the launch pad from damage.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] After another scrub due to weather conditions, the third launch attempt on July 24 was successful and became the first Starship flight to deploy operational satellites with 20 Starlink satellites deployed in suborbit that made contact with the Starlink constellation.[^cite_note-7] Booster 20's ocean landing burn failed when only ten of 13 Raptor engines relit, with subsequent shutdowns causing destruction on impact.[^cite_note-\:6-2]

## Background

### Federal clearance for flight

Following [Starship flight test 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_12), a mishap investigation report was completed to secure flight clearance.[^cite_note-8] The US [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) concluded its review on July 13, clearing the path for subsequent Starship launches.[^cite_note-9]

### Vehicle testing ahead of launch

#### Booster 20

Following assembly, Booster 20 (B20) was transported to the [Massey's test site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massey's_Gun_Shop_and_Range) on June 5, 2026, where it completed a cryogenic proof test the following day.[^cite_note-10] On July 10, after moving to [Orbital Launch Pad 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_Launch_Pad_2), the booster completed a major pre-flight milestone by executing a successful, full-duration, 33-engine static fire.[^cite_note-11]

#### Ship 40

Stacking operations for Ship 40 (S40) concluded on March 2, 2026,[^cite_note-\:52-12] with cryogenic testing completing at Massey's in early May.[^cite_note-13] Following engine installation, the vehicle returned to Massey's on June 23. S40 completed a successful single-engine static fire on June 25 after a brief diagnostic run.[^cite_note-\:02-14] After returning briefly to the production site for final checks,[^cite_note-\:02-14] S40 was transported back to Massey's, where it fired all six engines on July 1.[^cite_note-15]

### Launch attempts

SpaceX initially targeted Thursday, July 16, for flight 13; however, the flight was scrubbed at T-minus 0 seconds as four of the 33 Raptor engines failed to ignite.[^cite_note-Reuters_1-16] Initially, the second launch attempt was scheduled for Monday, July 20, but was later rescheduled for Thursday, July 23.[^cite_note-Reuters_1-16][^cite_note-17] The date was moved to July 24 due to [Tropical Storm Bertha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Bertha_\(2026\)).[^cite_note-Dinner-1]

The flight profile built upon previous test flights. Starship flew on a controlled [suborbital trajectory](455295.md) designed to fall just short of orbit,[^cite_note-20] while the Super Heavy booster executed a flip and boostback burn before suffering multiple engine failures and splashing down hard in the Gulf of Mexico.[^cite_note-\:6-2][^cite_note-21]

The upper stage carried 20 next-generation [Starlink](45111627.md) satellites, six of which were equipped with advanced cameras to inspect S40 in space, mirroring the [Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_orbiter)'s [rendezvous pitch maneuver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rendezvous_pitch_maneuver) (RPM). Because the flight was intentionally suborbital, the satellites reentered the atmosphere about 20 minutes after deployment.[^cite_note-\:6-2] To further demonstrate its advancing capabilities, the ship performed an in-space [Raptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Raptor) relight before executing a targeted splashdown and landing flip maneuver in the Indian Ocean.[^cite_note-\:6-2] The ship splashed down and remained intact and operational after tipping over, a first for the vehicle, allowing a drone to inspect the heat shield and engines.[^cite_note-\:6-2]

## Recovery

[Elon Musk](909036.md) confirmed that SpaceX would attempt recovery efforts of Ship 40 on July 28.[^cite_note-22] The SpaceX recovery vessel *Go Australis* remained near Ship 40 for a few days after splashdown, later joined by tugs *Normand Ranger* and *Skimmer Tide* a half day later.[^cite_note-23] Musk stated on X that recovery of Ship 40 was looking doubtful on August 7.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] *Normand Ranger* and *Go Australis* towed Ship 40 to waters off [Christmas Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Island) on August 18, with the [Australian Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Space_Agency) confirming the [Australian government](1222540.md) was aiding in recovery operations.[^cite_note-26] SpaceX stated on X that engineers will conduct additional analysis on site before attempting to return Ship 40 to [Starbase, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Starbase).[^cite_note-27] An exclusion zone was established around Ship 40 and *Normand Ranger* at Christmas Island.[^cite_note-28] On August 23, [semi-submersible heavy-lift ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy-lift_ship) [*Forte*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boskalis_Forte) arrived at Christmas Island, to transport Ship 40 back to Starbase.[^cite_note-29] Ship 40 was loaded onto *Forte* on August 26 after engineers collected heatshield samples and data from the ship.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-sx20260916-31]

## Aftermath

Flight Test 13 resulted in a successful test of Ship 40, from stage separation to splash down, completing verification for a future orbital flight with testing of an in-space Starship engine relight.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] Shortly after flight test 13, Elon Musk announced that [flight test 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starship_flight_test_14) would include catching the Starship with the "[Mechazilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechazilla)" launch tower which would first require the Starship to have entered orbit.[^cite_note-34]
