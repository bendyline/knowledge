The **M142 High Mobility Artillery Rocket System** (**HIMARS** [/ˈhaɪmɑːrz/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is a light [multiple rocket launcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiple_rocket_launcher) developed in the late 1990s for the [United States Army](32087.md) and mounted on a standard U.S. Army [Family of Medium Tactical Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_of_Medium_Tactical_Vehicles) (FMTV) M1140 truck frame.

The HIMARS carries one pod with either six [Guided Multiple Launch Rocket System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guided_Multiple_Launch_Rocket_System) (GMLRS) rockets or one [Army Tactical Missile System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM-140_ATACMS) (ATACMS) missile. It is based on the U.S. Army's FMTV five-ton truck, and is capable of launching all rockets in the [Multiple Launch Rocket System Family of Munitions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M270_Multiple_Launch_Rocket_System#Rockets_and_missiles). HIMARS ammunition pods are interchangeable with the [M270 MLRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M270_multiple_launch_rocket_system). It has a single pod, as opposed to the standard two for the M270 and its variants.

The launcher can be transported by C-17 Globemaster, C-5 Galaxy, and [Lockheed C-130 Hercules](7697.md) aircraft.[^cite_note-videoclip-15] The FMTV truck that transports the HIMARS was initially produced by [Armor Holdings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armor_Holdings,_Inc.) Aerospace and Defense Group Tactical Vehicle Systems Division, the [original equipment manufacturer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_equipment_manufacturer) of the FMTV. It was produced by the [Oshkosh Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oshkosh_Corporation) from 2010 to 2017.[^cite_note-16] with all units since then being manufactured by Lockheed Martin's Missiles and Fire Control division.

## Development

The requirement for HIMARS came about in 1982, when the [9th Infantry Division (Motorized)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Infantry_Division_\(United_States\)) saw the need to acquire a light multiple rocket launcher as a counterfire asset. The requirement failed to gather support from the [Field Artillery School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Field_Artillery_School) and languished for a number of years.[^cite_note-17] The institutional bias at the time was oriented towards heavy forces.[^cite_note-\:0-18] With the waning of the [Cold War](325329.md) and the growing interest in low-intensity operations, both the Field Artillery School and [Missile Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Aviation_and_Missile_Command) realized that the M270 MLRS was too heavy for rapid deployment and pushed for the funding of HIMARS.[^cite_note-\:0-18]

The [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) gave new impetus towards fielding a lightweight MLRS, when the M270 proved too costly in airlift assets to deploy in theater and the launchers did not arrive with the initial wave of U.S. troops.[^cite_note-\:0-18] In April 1991, the HIMARS concept was tested at [White Sands Missile Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_Missile_Range), using a modified [Honest John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGR-1_Honest_John) launcher.[^cite_note-19]



A proof-of-concept prototype of HIMARS at [White Sands Missile Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sands_Missile_Range), April 1991

HIMARS was then developed as a private venture by [Loral Vought Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loral_Vought_Systems), later [Lockheed Martin Missiles and Fire Control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_Missiles_and_Fire_Control), to meet this requirement. The system first appeared publicly in 1993. In 1996, the [U.S. Army Missile Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Aviation_and_Missile_Command) awarded Lockheed Martin a \$23.2 million contract to build four prototypes. In April 1998, the vehicles were delivered to the [XVIII Airborne Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/XVIII_Airborne_Corps) for a two-year evaluation with 3rd Battalion, [27th Field Artillery Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/27th_Field_Artillery_Regiment).[^cite_note-20]

In July 1998, the Army conducted a test firing of the [ATACMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM-140_ATACMS). In December 1999, the Aviation and Missile Command awarded Lockheed Martin a \$65 million contract for engineering and manufacturing development. Under this contract, Lockheed Martin delivered six HIMARS in late 2001 for Army evaluation. In April 2003, the Army awarded Lockheed Martin a \$96 million contract to begin [low rate initial production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_rate_initial_production). Around this time, the Marine Corps placed an order for two units for evaluation purposes.[^cite_note-Janes_2011-21]

The launcher system and chassis are produced by Lockheed Martin Missiles & Fire Control in [Camden, Arkansas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camden,_Arkansas) as of 2019.[^cite_note-22]

HIMARS FLEX was introduced in 2026. It carries 2 rocket pods and can fire PAC-3 missiles as well.[^cite_note-23]

## Design

The HIMARS is similar in design to the [M270 Multiple Launch Rocket System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M270_Multiple_Launch_Rocket_System) (MLRS), with the main exception being that it is a wheeled vehicle as opposed to a [tracked vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuous_track). The HIMARS can carry the same type of pods as the M270, but carries one pod while the M270 carries two pods. The HIMARS windows are made of sheets of [sapphire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapphire) laminated with glass and polycarbonate.[^cite_note-24]

The HIMARS was also tested as a unified launch system for both artillery rockets and the [SLAMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SLAMRAAM) surface-launched variant of the [AMRAAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMRAAM) anti-aircraft missile.[^cite_note-25]

In October 2017, a Marine Corps HIMARS fired a rocket while at sea against a land target for the first time from the deck of the [amphibious transport dock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibious_transport_dock) [USS *Anchorage*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Anchorage_\(LPD-23\)), demonstrating the system's ability to operate while on ships to deliver precision fire from a standoff range against shore defenses.[^cite_note-26] The vehicle's targeting software was reworked so it can better fire while on a launch platform in motion.[^cite_note-27]

By early 2022, Lockheed Martin was producing HIMARS at a rate of 48 launchers annually, but following the start of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) that rate was increased to 60. In October 2022 the company announced it would boost production to 96 systems annually in response to increased demand caused by the war. Limitations in building new industrial capacity means it will be several months before production can be ramped up from five to eight vehicles monthly.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

## Operational history

HIMARS first entered service in June 2005 with the 27th Field Artillery, 18th Airborne Corps at Fort Bragg, in North Carolina.[^cite_note-army_technology-30]

### Afghanistan and the Middle East

Three prototype HIMARS launchers were successfully used during the [Iraq War](5043324.md).[^cite_note-army_technology-30] The first US Marine Corps battalion equipped with HIMARS, the 2nd Battalion, 14th Marine Regiment, was deployed to Iraq in July 2007.[^cite_note-army_technology-30]



A HIMARS launcher with armored cab



A HIMARS launcher being loaded into a [C-130 Hercules](7697.md) aircraft in 2011



Reloading a HIMARS with a pod of six [training rounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dummy_round) in Afghanistan

In February 2010, the [International Security Assistance Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Security_Assistance_Force) (ISAF) for Afghanistan indicated in a press release that two rockets fired from a HIMARS were believed to have fallen 300 metres short of their intended target, killing 12 civilians during [Operation Moshtarak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Moshtarak). ISAF suspended the use of the HIMARS until a full review of the incident was completed.[^cite_note-31] A British officer later said that the rockets were on target, that the target was in use by the [Taliban](30635.md), and that use of the system had been reinstated.[^cite_note-32] Reports indicated that the civilian deaths were due to the Taliban's use of [human shields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_shield). The presence of civilians at that location had not been known to the ISAF forces.[^cite_note-33] A report in the *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)* in October 2010 credited the HIMARS with aiding the [NATO](21133.md) offensive in [Kandahar](17260.md) by targeting Taliban commanders' hideouts, forcing many to flee to Pakistan, at least temporarily.[^cite_note-34]

In November 2015, the U.S. Army revealed that it had deployed the HIMARS to Iraq, firing at least 400 rockets at [Islamic State](9087364.md) (ISIL) targets since the beginning of that summer.[^cite_note-35] HIMARS detachments were sent to [Al Asad Airbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Asad_Airbase) and [Al-Taqaddum Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Taqaddum_Air_Base) in [Al Anbar Governorate](467036.md). In March 2016, a U.S. Army HIMARS fired rockets into Syria for the first time in support of Syrian rebels fighting ISIL, from launchers based in neighboring Jordan.[^cite_note-36]

In January 2016, Lockheed announced that the HIMARS had reached 1 million operational hours with U.S. forces, achieving a 99 percent operational readiness rate.[^cite_note-37]

In April 2016, it was announced that the U.S. would be deploying the HIMARS in Turkey near the border with Syria as part of the battle with ISIL.[^cite_note-38] In early September, international media and the [U.S. State Department](31975.md) reported a newly deployed HIMARS had engaged ISIL targets in Syria near the Turkish border.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

In October 2016, HIMARS were stationed at [Qayyarah Airfield West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qayyarah_Airfield_West), some 65 kilometres (40 mi) south of Mosul, taking part in the [Battle of Mosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Mosul_\(2016\)).[^cite_note-42]

In June 2017, a HIMARS was deployed at [Al-Tanf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Tanf_\(U.S._military_base\)), Syria, to support U.S.-backed rebels in the area.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

On 24 May 2018, a HIMARS strike killed 50 Taliban fighters and leaders in [Musa Qala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_Qala), Afghanistan.[^cite_note-45] Three rockets struck the building within a 14-second timespan.[^cite_note-46]

In September 2018, US support forces coordinated with [Syrian Democratic Forces](48205248.md) fighting to defeat ISIS in east Syria in the [Deir ez-Zor campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_ez-Zor_campaign_\(2017%E2%80%932019\)), sometimes striking ISIS positions with GMLRS rockets 30 times per day.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] The HIMARS used in this support operation were located in the Omar oilfields, some 25 km (16 mi) north of the ISIS-controlled targets.[^cite_note-52]

In March 2026, a pair of HIMARS launchers were observed in Bahrain launching missiles towards Iran during the [Iran war](82533582.md). This was the first instance of attacks against Iran originating from other Gulf countries being observed. It is unclear who was operating the weapons systems, as HIMARS are used by both the United States and Bahrain (having acquired the launchers in 2025).[^cite_note-Nereim_NYT-53]

### Ukraine



A Ukrainian HIMARS in [Zaporizhzhia Oblast](494936.md), July 2022

On 1 June 2022, the US announced that it would be supplying four HIMARS to Ukraine with M31 GMLRS unitary rockets.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] On 23 June, the first HIMARS arrived in Ukraine, according to [Ukrainian Defense Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ministers_of_Defense_\(Ukraine\)) [Oleksii Reznikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksii_Reznikov).[^cite_note-57] On 25 June 2022, Ukraine started deploying the system against Russian forces during the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md). According to the [Commander-in-Chief of the Armed Forces of Ukraine](63525946.md), [Valeriy Zaluzhnyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeriy_Zaluzhnyi), "Artillerymen of the Armed Forces of Ukraine hit ... military targets of the enemy on our, Ukrainian, territory".[^cite_note-Marsi-58] The Ukrainian military stated that this first strike, on a Russian base in Izyum, killed over 40 soldiers. The day before, a second batch of four was announced to be delivered in mid-July.[^cite_note-59]

On 1 July, a US defense official told reporters that Ukraine had been using the system to destroy Russian command posts: "selecting targets and then accurately hitting them ... degrading Russian capability".[^cite_note-60] On 18 July, Zaluzhnyi said: "An important factor contributing to our retention of defensive lines and positions is the timely arrival of M142 HIMARS, which deliver surgical strikes on enemy control posts, ammunition and fuel storage depots."[^cite_note-61]

Another four HIMARS were announced for delivery on 8 July, the delivery spacing driven by the weeks-long process to train Ukrainian troops on how to use the platform. To avoid escalating the conflict, the US restricted Ukraine from using HIMARS to attack targets on Russian territory.[^cite_note-62] For the same reason, the US had not provided Ukraine with the longer-range [ATACMS](147015.md) missile, which could easily engage targets inside of Russia.[^cite_note-WSJ-63][^cite_note-64]

A fourth batch of four was announced on 20 July, bringing the total number of HIMARS committed to Ukraine to 16. Ukrainian Defense Minister Reznikov stated that the country needed "at least 100" of the system and that by that point, eight systems had destroyed 30 command stations and ammunition storage facilities, decreasing the intensity of Russian shelling and slowing their advance.[^cite_note-65] In that announcement, it was revealed that the number delivered had reached 12 launchers.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] That number had increased to 16 by 1 August.[^cite_note-69]

On 30 August 2022, *[The Washington Post](102226.md)* reported on Ukrainian claims to have successfully used decoy HIMARS units made out of wood to draw at least 10 Russian [3M-54 Kalibr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3M-54_Kalibr) cruise missiles. One US diplomat stated that Russian sources had claimed more HIMARS destroyed than the US had sent. A [Pentagon](7279897.md) official had earlier in the month asserted that no HIMARS had been destroyed at that time.[^cite_note-70] On 8 September, US General [Mark Milley](38091005.md), chairman of the Joint Chiefs of Staff, told reporters: "We are seeing real and measurable gains from Ukraine in the use of these systems. For example, the Ukrainians have struck over 400 targets with the HIMARS and they've had devastating effect".[^cite_note-71]

A further 18 HIMARS were announced on 28 September, as part of an aid package aimed at meeting Ukraine's mid- and long-term needs, so deliveries are to begin in six months at the earliest.[^cite_note-USDoD28sep-72][^cite_note-auto2-73] Ukraine had previously been provided with only M31 Unitary Warhead missiles, which are "not ideal against targets spread over large areas, as the deadly chunks are not designed to fly far." As of early October they have been granted the M30A1 which uses the Alternative Warhead that can cover up to "half a square mile of land in a single salvo" with 180,000 [tungsten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tungsten) steel [BB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BB_gun) sized balls.[^cite_note-74] The US announced on 4 October that four more HIMARS launchers would be provided from US military stockpiles, to increase the total to 20 HIMARS in Ukrainian service.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

HIMARS attacks by Ukraine have been credited with "destroy\[ing] Russian command nodes, tens of thousands of howitzer artillery rounds and a staggering 20 million small-arms rounds." As of 11 November 2022, a senior U.S. official stated no HIMARS systems have been destroyed after five months in operational use.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] As of February 2023, CNN reported that Ukraine had expended approximately 9,500 GMLRS rockets.[^cite_note-cnn_russia_jamming-81] In response to the effects of HIMARS, [Russian Defense Minister](30730416.md) [Sergei Shoigu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Shoigu) declared the HIMARS system as a high priority target for Russian troops. Ukrainian officials identified Russia's [loitering munitions](53703349.md) as the biggest threat to the HIMARS.[^cite_note-WSJ-63]

HIMARS has been used to strike Russian troop concentrations with GMLRS. Groups of Russian troops out in the open have been killed in these strikes, with a strike in February 2024 killing up to 65 Russian soldiers.[^cite_note-82] HIMARS has also been used to strike Russian troop concentrations in hard cover, with a HIMARS strike on a Russian base in Makiivka killing 89 Russian soldiers on the admission of the Russian government, although BBC News Russian claimed at least 139 dead Russian soldiers.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

On 5 May 2023, it was reported that Russia was able to [jam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_jamming) GMLRS rockets' GPS guidance system, making strikes less precise. In addition to GPS guidance, GMLRS rockets have an [inertial navigation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_navigation_system) which, by definition, cannot be jammed, but it is less precise alone than when it is coupled with GPS guidance.[^cite_note-cnn_russia_jamming-81]

On 10 February 2024, two Ukrainian HIMARS systems were seen arriving onboard an [Antonov Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_Airlines) [An-124-100M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonov_An-124_Ruslan) at [Harrisburg International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg_International_Airport) in Pennsylvania. Both systems appeared damaged, with one having cracked windows in the crew compartment, and the other missing its front right wheel.[^cite_note-2dmgd-85]

On 5 March 2024, a Ukrainian HIMARS system was destroyed for the first confirmed time, after being tracked by a Russian drone and targeted with a missile near [Nykanorivka, Donetsk Oblast](https://uk.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9D%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%96%D0%B2%D0%BA%D0%B0).[^cite_note-86] On 15 August 2024, the second confirmed loss of a HIMARS system occurred in the Sumy region, making the total loss to two destroyed and two damaged.[^cite_note-\:4-87]

In June 2024, after Ukraine was given permission to use GMLRS on internationally recognized Russian territory, HIMARS strikes destroyed and damaged launchers along with other components of the Russian long-range S-300/S-400 surface-to-air missile system in Belgorod region of Russia.[^cite_note-88] On 9 August 2024, HIMARS [destroyed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_2024_Kursk_Oblast_incursion) a convoy of Russian troops in the [Kursk Oblast](480916.md) of Russia, with Russian milbloggers acknowledging the attack and calling for punishment of Russian commanders.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] Drone video of the HIMARS strike subsequently emerged, showing the Russian convoy being struck with GMLRS, numerous Russian vehicles being destroyed, and Russian troops fleeing the convoy.[^cite_note-92]

In May 2025, a HIMARS system was destroyed by an [Unmanned Aerial Vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_Aerial_Vehicle) (UAV) attack.[^cite_note-93] The Polish publication notes that unlike the other confirmed cases of the HIMARS system being hit by missiles, this is the first documented case of destruction by a [first-person view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_view_\(radio_control\)) (FPV) drone.[^cite_note-94]

On 21 May 2025, it was reported that a Ukrainian HIMARS rocket launcher had recently been destroyed just 10 kilometers from the frontline near Chasiv Yar in Donetsk Oblast by a Russian drone operated by [Rubicon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Advanced_Unmanned_Technologies_%22Rubicon%22)—an elite unmanned aerial unit.[^cite_note-95]

On 28 September 2025, Ukraine's HIMARS [struck](70539619.md) the Belgorod thermal power plant, causing a "complete power outage" in [Belgorod](478861.md) and surrounding areas.[^cite_note-96]

As of March 2026, four HIMARS system have been destroyed and another four damaged.[^cite_note-97]

#### GMLRS

Guided Multiple Launch Rocket System (GMLRS) 227 mm rockets have an extended range and add [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPS)-aided guidance to their [Inertial Navigation System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inertial_Navigation_System). GMLRS rockets were introduced in 2005 and the M30 and M31 rockets are, except for their warheads, identical.[^cite_note-Turner-98] As of 1 December 2021 50,000 GMLRS rockets have been produced,[^cite_note-104] with yearly production now exceeding 9,000 rockets. Each rocket pod contains 6 identical rockets. Both Lockheed Martin and the U.S. Army report that the GMLRS has a maximum range of 70+ km (43+ mi).[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-Lockheed_Martin_2023-106] According to a U.S. Department of Defense document the maximum demonstrated performance of a GMLRS is 84 km (52 mi),[^cite_note-107] a figure also reported elsewhere.[^cite_note-Marine_Corps_Gazette-99][^cite_note-Turner-98] Another source reports a maximum range of about 90 km (56 mi).[^cite_note-Global_Defense_Corp_2022-108] In 2009 Lockheed Martin announced that a GMLRS had been successfully test fired 92 km (57 mi).[^cite_note-q3-100]

- **M30** rockets carrying 404 DPICM M101 submunitions. Range: 15–92 km (9.3–57.2 mi). 3,936 produced between 2004 and 2009, production ceased in favor of the M30A1.

  [^cite_note-Turner-98]

  &#x20;The remaining M30 rockets are being updated with either the M30A1 or M31A1 warhead.

  [^cite_note-Marine_Corps_Gazette-99]

- **M30A1** rockets with Alternative Warhead (AW). Range: 15–92 km (9.3–57.2 mi). GMLRS rocket that replaces the M30's submunitions with approximately 182,000 pre-formed tungsten fragments for area effects without unexploded ordnance.

  [^cite_note-109]

  &#x20;Entered production in 2015.

  [^cite_note-Turner-98]

  [^cite_note-Marine_Corps_Gazette-99]

  &#x20;This warhead is superior not just because it does not use [cluster munitions](79745.md) but is also superior to a normal high explosive round: "A high explosive round is very impressive because it produces a big bomb and large pieces of shrapnel, but this round is small pellets and covers a much larger area."

  [^cite_note-110]

  &#x20;The M30A1 uses a proximity sensor fuze mode with a 10 meter burst height.

  [^cite_note-111]

- **M30A2** rockets with Alternative Warhead (AW). Range: 15–92 km (9.3–57.2 mi). Improved M30A1 with Insensitive Munition Propulsion System (IMPS). Only M30 variant in production since 2019.

  [^cite_note-112]

- **M31** rockets with 200 lb (91 kg) high-explosive unitary warhead. Range: 15–92 km (9.3–57.2 mi). Entered production in 2005. The warhead is produced by [General Dynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics) and contains 51 pounds (23 kg) of [PBX-109](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polymer-bonded_explosive) high explosive in a steel blast-fragmentation case.

  [^cite_note-113]

- **M31A1** rockets with 200 lb (91 kg) high-explosive unitary warhead. Range: 15–92 km (9.3–57.2 mi). Improved M31 with new multi-mode fuze that added airburst to the M31's fuze point detonation and delay.

  [^cite_note-114]

- **M31A2** rockets with 200 lb (91 kg) high-explosive unitary warhead. Range: 15–92 km (9.3–57.2 mi). Improved M31A1 with Insensitive Munition Propulsion System (IMPS). Only M31 variant in production since 2019.

- **ER GMLRS** rockets with extended range beyond 150 km (93 mi).

  [^cite_note-q4-101]

  &#x20;Rockets use a slightly increased rocket motor size, a newly designed hull, and tail-driven guidance while still containing six per pod. It will come in unitary and AW variants.

  [^cite_note-115]

  &#x20;The first successful test flight of an ER GMLRS occurred in March 2021.

  [^cite_note-116]

  &#x20;Lockheed Martin anticipates adding the ER to its production line in the fiscal year 2023 contract award, and is planning to produce the new rockets at its [Camden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camden,_Arkansas) facility. Full operational capability is planned for 2025.

  [^cite_note-117]

  &#x20;In 2022 Finland became the first foreign customer to order ER GMLRS.

  [^cite_note-118]

  &#x20;In November 2022 Lithuania announced that it will obtain GMLRS-ER.

  [^cite_note-119]

  &#x20;In February 2023, Poland ordered GMLRS-ER AW missiles.

  [^cite_note-auto1-120]

##### Australian production

In December 2025, the Australian government announced that local production of GMLRS in co-operation with Lockheed Martin Australia would commence in a new factory in [Port Wakefield, South Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Wakefield,_South_Australia). The new factory would be the second in the world to produce GMLRS.[^cite_note-121] In March 2026, the Australian government announced that the first batch of GMLRS were expected to be completed by mid-March 2026.[^cite_note-122] On 8 April 2026, the first Australian-made GMLRS were test-fired by an Australian Army HIMARS.[^cite_note-123]

### GLSDB

Main article: [Ground Launched Small Diameter Bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_Launched_Small_Diameter_Bomb)

The Ground Launched Small Diameter Bomb (GLSDB) is an M26-rocket based weapon made by [Boeing](18933266.md) and the [Saab Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saab_Group), who modified Boeing's [GBU-39 Small Diameter Bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GBU-39_Small_Diameter_Bomb) (SDB) with the addition of an obsolete M26 rocket motor. It has a range of up to 150 km (93 mi).[^cite_note-Selinger_2022-103]

### ATACMS

Main article: [ATACMS](147015.md)

The Army Tactical Missile System (ATACMS) is a series of 610 mm [surface-to-surface missile](1483499.md) (SSM) with a range of up to 300 km (190 mi).[^cite_note-q5-102] Each rocket pod contains one ATACMS missile. As of 2022, only the M48, M57, and M57E1 remain in the US military's arsenal.

- **M39** (ATACMS BLOCK I) missile with inertial guidance. The missile carries 950 M74 Anti-personnel and Anti‑materiel (APAM) bomblets. Range: 25–165 km (16–103 mi). 1,650 M39 were produced between 1990 and 1997, when production ceased in favor of the M39A1. During [Desert Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Storm) 32 M39 were fired at Iraqi targets, and during [Operation Iraqi Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Iraqi_Freedom) a further 379 M39 were fired.

  [^cite_note-Turner-98]

  [^cite_note-Marine_Corps_Gazette-99]

  &#x20;The remaining M39 missiles are being updated to M57E1 missiles.

  [^cite_note-MOD-124]

  [^cite_note-Keller-125]

  &#x20;The M39 is the only ATACMS variant, which can be fired by all MLRS and HIMARS variants.

- **M39A1** (ATACMS BLOCK IA) missile with GPS-aided guidance. The missile carries 300 M74 APAM bomblets. Range: 20–300 km (12–186 mi). 610 M39A1 were produced between 1997 and 2003. During Operation Iraqi Freedom 74 M39A1 were fired at Iraqi targets.

  [^cite_note-Turner-98]

  [^cite_note-Marine_Corps_Gazette-99]

  &#x20;The remaining M39A1 missiles are being updated to M57E1 missiles.

  [^cite_note-MOD-124]

  [^cite_note-Keller-125]

  &#x20;The M39A1 and all subsequently introduced ATACMS missiles can be used only with the M270A1 (or variants thereof) and the HIMARS.

- **M48** (ATACMS Quick Reaction Unitary; QRU) missile with GPS-aided guidance. The missile carries the 500-pound (230 kg) WDU-18/B penetrating high explosive blast fragmentation warhead of the [US Navy](20518076.md)'s [Harpoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harpoon_\(missile\)) anti-ship missile, which was packaged into the newly designed WAU-23/B warhead section. Range: 70–300 km (43–186 mi). 176 M48 were produced between 2001 and 2004, when production ceased in favor of the M57. During Operation Iraqi Freedom 16 M48 were fired at Iraqi targets; a further 42 M48 were fired during [Operation Enduring Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Enduring_Freedom).

  [^cite_note-Turner-98]

  [^cite_note-Marine_Corps_Gazette-99]

  &#x20;The remaining M48 missiles remain in the U.S. Army and [US Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Marine_Corps)' arsenal.

- **M57** (ATACMS TACMS 2000) missile with GPS-aided guidance. The missile carries the same WAU-23/B warhead section as the M48. Range: 70–300 km (43–186 mi). 513 M57 were produced between 2004 and 2013.

  [^cite_note-Turner-98]

  [^cite_note-Marine_Corps_Gazette-99]

- **M57E1** (ATACMS Modification; MOD) missile with GPS-aided guidance. The M57E1 is the designation for upgraded M39 and M39A1 with re-grained motor, updated navigation and guidance software and hardware, and a WAU-23/B warhead section instead of the M74 APAM bomblets. The M57E1 ATACMS MOD also includes a proximity sensor for airburst detonation.

  [^cite_note-MOD-124]

  &#x20;Production commenced in 2017 with an initial order for 220 upgraded M57E1.

  [^cite_note-Turner-98]

  [^cite_note-Marine_Corps_Gazette-99]

  &#x20;The program is slated to end in 2024 with the introduction of the Precision Strike Missile (PrSM), which will replace the ATACMS missiles in the US arsenal.

### PrSM

Main article: [Precision Strike Missile](71176097.md)

The Precision Strike Missile (PrSM) is a new series of GPS-guided missiles, which will begin to replace ATACMS missiles in 2024. PrSM carries a newly designed area-effects warhead and has a range of 60–499 km (37–310 mi). PrSM missiles can be launched from the M270A2 and the HIMARS, with rockets pods containing 2 missiles. As of 2022, the PrSM is in [low-rate initial production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low_rate_initial_production), with 110 missiles being delivered to the US military over the year. PrSM will enter operational service in 2023.[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-Turner-98][^cite_note-127]

## Related developments

Lockheed Martin UK and [INSYS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INSYS?action=edit\&redlink=1) had jointly developed a demonstrator rocket artillery system similar to HIMARS for the British Army's "Lightweight Mobile Artillery Weapon System/Rocket" (LIMAWS(R)) program. The system consisted of a single MLRS pod, mounted on a [Supacat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supacat) SPV600 chassis.[^cite_note-128] The LIMAWS(R) program was canceled in September 2007.[^cite_note-129]



The Autonomous Multi-domain Launcher at Yuma Proving Ground, April 2024

### Autonomous Multi-domain Launcher

The Autonomous Multi-domain Launcher (AML) is an [unmanned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unmanned_ground_vehicle) variant of the HIMARS. The AML is equipped with remote controlled launcher and [fire-control system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-control_system) that ensures compatibility with current munitions used onboard both [M270 MLRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M270_MLRS) and HIMARS.[^cite_note-130] The concept video shows the AML can carry two pods compared to one on HIMARS and is expected to be compatible with munitions from other services or in development.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]

### Homar-A

Homar-A (English: [Lobster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobster), A for American) is a program by [Polish Ministry of National Defense](7425373.md) to acquire the M142 HIMARS, and, via technology transfers, integrate the launcher with the domestically produced [Jelcz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelcz) 663.45 6x6 truck chassis, *Topaz* [battle management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_command) system and communication system. The Armaments Agency of the Polish Ministry of National Defense has signed a framework agreement with Lockheed Martin on 11 September 2023. Under the terms of this agreement, 486 Homar-A vehicles will be assembled in Poland, with first deliveries starting in 2026.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

### GMARS

Main article: [GMARS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMARS)

In 2023, [Rheinmetall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rheinmetall) and [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin) signed a collaboration agreement for the development of the Global Mobility Artillery System (GMARS) to address the need for future long-range artillery. The GMARS launcher consists of two pods as opposed to HIMARS' single pod. Current munition options include the MFOM family of munitions such as GMLRS, ER-GMLRS, [MGM-140 ATACMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MGM-140_ATACMS) and [Precision Strike Missile](71176097.md). Surfaced launched version of [AGM-158 JASSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AGM-158_JASSM) and 122 mm unguided rockets are expected to be integrated later.[^cite_note-135] It was unveiled during the Eurosatrory 2024 and mounted on Rheinmetall's HX 8x8 chassis. Both Rheinmetall and Lockheed Martin are actively engaging with European customers for potential sales.[^cite_note-136]
