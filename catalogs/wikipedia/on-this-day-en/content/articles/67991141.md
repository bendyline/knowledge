The **2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup** was the 21st edition of the [AFC Women's Asian Cup](2428648.md), the quadrennial international [football](10568.md) tournament in Asia competed by the women's national teams in the [Asian Football Confederation](715249.md) (AFC).

Australia was officially selected as the host nation by the AFC Women's Football Committee on 15 May 2024.[^cite_note-hosts-1]

The tournament served as the final stage of Asian [qualification](62284023.md) for the [2027 FIFA Women's World Cup](60983910.md) in Brazil. This will be the last time the qualifications will be linked, as a standalone qualifier for the FIFA Women's World Cup will be held starting from the [2031 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2031_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) onwards. For the first time, the tournament also served as the penultimate stage of Asian qualification for the [2028 Summer Olympics](12695048.md) in Los Angeles, with all eight quarter-finalists qualifying for the [2028 AFC Women's Olympic Qualifying Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_AFC_Women's_Olympic_Qualifying_Tournament).[^cite_note-2] This also marked the second time a country hosted the AFC Women's Asian Cup having hosted the FIFA Women's World Cup before. The first time was China when they hosted the [2010 Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup) after the [World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) three years earlier.&#x20;

[China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_women's_national_football_team) were the defending champions, but were eliminated in the semi-finals by hosts [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_women's_national_football_team). [Japan](3190583.md) won the tournament for the third time by defeating Australia in the final.

## Host selection

Main article: [2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup bids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_bids)

The following four football associations submitted their interest to host the tournament by the 31 July 2022 deadline.[^cite_note-3] [Australia](4689264.md) was selected as the host nation by the AFC Women's Football Committee on 15 March 2024 following the withdrawals of Jordan, Saudi Arabia and Uzbekistan.[^cite_note-4]

- [Australia](4689264.md) [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Australia) – Australia submitted its interest in hosting the tournament also on 31 July. The country has previously hosted the [2006 AFC Women's Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup), where they finished runners-up. Australia also hosted the [2023 FIFA Women's World Cup](45271353.md) alongside New Zealand, which is the first [FIFA Women's World Cup](244862.md) to be hosted by two nations. The country also hosted the [2015 Men's AFC Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_AFC_Asian_Cup).

### Cancelled bids

- [Jordan](7515964.md) [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_Football_Association) – Jordan previously hosted the [2018 AFC Women's Asian Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup), where they finished bottom of the group stage. Jordan also hosted the [2016 FIFA U-17 Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_FIFA_U-17_Women's_World_Cup), the first in an Arab country.

* [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) [Saudi Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabia_Football_Federation) – On 21 April 2022, Saudi Arabia submitted its bid to host the tournament. Saudi Arabia had never hosted any major women's football tournament, although it hosted the men's [FIFA Confederations Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Confederations_Cup) from 1992 to 1997 and will host the men's Asian Cup in [2027](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_AFC_Asian_Cup).

## Ceremonies

### Opening ceremony

On 27 January 2026, the organising committee announced that [Audrey Nuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audrey_Nuna) would be performing at the opening ceremony ahead of the first game at [Perth Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perth_Stadium) on 1 March.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] She also performed an exclusive half-time performance that was not broadcast.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-\:0-20]

As well as Nuna's performance, the ceremony also featured [Torres Strait Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torres_Strait_Islanders) artist Zipporah performing the tournament's first-ever official anthem called "That's How We Win" which was written by [Nat Dunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nat_Dunn), and a [Welcome to Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welcome_to_Country).[^cite_note-\:0-20] Representatives from all competing nations were officially welcomed in their respective native languages.[^cite_note-21]

### Closing ceremony

On 14 March 2026, it was announced that Australian artist [G Flip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_Flip) would be performing at the closing ceremony, to take place before the Final at [Stadium Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium_Australia) on 21 March.[^cite_note-22]

## Group stage

The top two teams of each group and the two best third-placed teams qualified for the quarter-finals.[^cite_note-regulations-23]

Tiebreakers

Teams were ranked according to [points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Points_\(association_football\)) (3 points for a win, 1 point for a draw, 0 points for a loss), and if tied on points, the following tiebreaking criteria were applied, in the order given, to determine the rankings:[^cite_note-regulations-23]

1. Points in head-to-head matches among tied teams;

2. [Goal difference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_difference) in head-to-head matches among tied teams;

3. Goals scored in head-to-head matches among tied teams;

4. If more than two teams were tied, and after applying all head-to-head criteria above, a subset of teams were still tied, all head-to-head criteria above were reapplied exclusively to this subset of teams;

5. Goal difference in all group matches;

6. Goals scored in all group matches;

7. [Penalty shoot-out](511302.md) if only two teams were tied and they met in the last round of the group;

8. Disciplinary points (yellow card = 1 point, red card as a result of two yellow cards = 3 points, direct red card = 3 points, yellow card followed by direct red card = 4 points);

9. Drawing of lots.

All times are local.[^cite_note-24]

## Knockout stage

Main article: [2026 AFC Women's Asian Cup knockout stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_AFC_Women's_Asian_Cup_knockout_stage)

The four quarter-final winners qualified directly for the 2027 Women's World Cup, while the four quarter-final losers competed in the [play-in matches](#Play-in_matches) to determine the final two direct qualifiers, and the two Asian representatives in the [inter-confederation play-offs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_qualification_\(inter-confederation_play-offs\)). In addition, the eight quarter-finalists will make up the entrants into the [2028 AFC Women's Olympic Qualifying Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_AFC_Women's_Olympic_Qualifying_Tournament).[^cite_note-26]

In the knockout stage, [extra time](633242.md#Association_football) and a [penalty shoot-out](511302.md) were used to decide the winner if necessary.

## Controversies

### Threats towards the Iranian national team

See also: [Defection of Iran women's national football team](82634971.md)

In February 2026, after a number of players resigned from the [Iran women's national football team](9896170.md) and refereeing organization following [the massacres in the country](82036668.md), the [Iranian Football Federation](1262861.md) reportedly threatened them with multi-year bans from professional football activities, judicial action, and long prison sentences.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] In March 2026, after the Iran women's national football team players refused to sing the national anthem of the Islamic Republic as a form of silent protest ahead of their Women's Asian Cup opener against [South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Korea_women's_national_football_team), concerns grew for their safety following threats from Iranian state media.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] Ahead of the following match against Australia, the national team players were reportedly forced to sing the national anthem of the Islamic Republic of Iran, with threats to the players' family members if they did not.[^cite_note-42] After the team's exit from the tournament on 8 March, members of the team gave what appeared to be [SOS](145605.md) hand signals from the bus as they were leaving, leading to protests and growing calls for Australia to offer the team refuge after the players were accused of being wartime traitors by Iranian state media for not singing the national anthem of the Islamic Republic in their opening game amid the [Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

On 9 March, five members of the Iranian women’s national football team – [Fatemeh Pasandideh](81929593.md), [Zahra Ghanbari](64417617.md), [Zahra Sarbali](64381444.md), [Atefeh Ramezanizadeh](74434878.md), and [Mona Hamoudi](64417968.md) – left the team’s training camp in Australia and sought refuge due to fears of retaliation from Iranian authorities.[^cite_note-47] On 10 March, US president [Donald Trump](4848272.md) publicly urged the Australian government to grant asylum to the players, stating they could face persecution if they returned to Iran.[^cite_note-48] It was reported that the women had been helped to escape by the [Australian Federal Police](341221.md) around 1\:30 am, and had been granted humanitarian visas to stay in Australia. [Home Affairs Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Home_Affairs_\(Australia\)) [Tony Burke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Burke) had been working with the AFP on the issue for some time, and Prime Minister [Anthony Albanese](556745.md) said that the team were safe in Australia.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

### Removal of Chen Kuei-jen

Former [Chinese Taipei national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_national_football_team) coach [Chen Kuei-jen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Kuei-jen) was removed during the match between the [Chinese Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Taipei_women's_national_football_team) and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_women's_national_football_team) in [Sydney](27862.md) for leading fans to chant "[Taiwan](25734.md) Jiayou" ("Go Taiwan"), after refusing to follow officials who ordered him to only use Chinese Taipei as the use of Taiwan was considered "political". Despite this, following Chinese Taipei's victory, Chen continued the Taiwan chant with the players on their bus. Following this incident, Taiwanese politician Lee Po-yi stated that he would contact the [Ministry of Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Sports_\(Taiwan\)) and the [Australia's Taipei representative office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Office,_Taipei) to demand an explanation from the organisers. Chinese Taipei has been the name used since 1979 to represent Taiwan following the [Nagoya Resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagoya_Resolution) to prevent sporting conflict with [China](5405.md), but the name has become controversial in Taiwan as many citizens have voiced support for being internationally recognised as Taiwan.[^cite_note-51]
