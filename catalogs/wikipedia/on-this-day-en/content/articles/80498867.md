Israel was represented at the [Eurovision Song Contest 2026](79992910.md) by the song "[Michelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_\(Noam_Bettan_song\))", written by [Nadav Aharoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadav_Aharoni?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Noam Bettan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Bettan), [Tzlil Klifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzlil_Klifi?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Yuval Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuval_Raphael), and performed by Bettan himself. The Israeli participating broadcaster, the [Israeli Public Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Public_Broadcasting_Corporation) (IPBC/Kan), selected Bettan as its representative through the show *[HaKokhav HaBa L'Eirovizion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rising_Star_\(Israeli_TV_series\))*, in collaboration with commercial broadcaster [Keshet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keshet_Media_Group) and [Tedy Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tedy_Productions?action=edit\&redlink=1), while the song "Michelle" was chosen internally. As with the previous two years, Israel's participation was the subject of controversy due to the [Gaza war](74998519.md).

Israel was drawn to compete in the first semi-final of the Eurovision Song Contest which took place on 12 May 2026. Performing during the show in position 10, Israel was announced among the top 10 entries of the first semi-final and therefore qualified to compete in the final on 16 May. It was later revealed that Israel placed first out of the 15 participating countries in the semi-final with 269 points.[^cite_note-1] In the final, Israel performed in position 3 and placed second out of the 25 participating countries, scoring 343 points.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Background

Main article: [Israel in the Eurovision Song Contest](3190362.md)

See also: [Eurovision Song Contest 2026 § Boycotts due to Israeli participation](79992910.md#Boycotts_due_to_Israeli_participation)

Prior to the 2026 contest, the [Israel Broadcasting Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_Broadcasting_Authority) (IBA) until 2017, and the [Israeli Public Broadcasting Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Public_Broadcasting_Corporation) (IPBC/Kan) since 2018, had participated in the [Eurovision Song Contest](9954.md) representing Israel forty-seven times since the IBA's first entry in [1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_1973).[^cite_note-history-4] They had won the contest on four occasions: in [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1978) with the song "[A-Ba-Ni-Bi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A-Ba-Ni-Bi)" by [Izhar Cohen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izhar_Cohen) and the Alphabeta, in [1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1979) with the song "[Hallelujah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallelujah_\(Milk_and_Honey_song\))" by [Milk and Honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk_and_Honey_\(Israeli_group\)), in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_1998) with the song "[Diva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diva_\(Dana_International_song\))" by [Dana International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_International), and in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2018) with the song "[Toy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toy_\(song\))" by [Netta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netta_Barzilai). Since the introduction of semi-finals in 2004, they had, to this point, managed to qualify to the final 14 times, achieving, besides its 2018 victory, six top ten results, most recently in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2025) with "[New Day Will Rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Day_Will_Rise)" by [Yuval Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuval_Raphael) placing second.[^cite_note-history-4]

As part of its duties as participating broadcaster, Kan selects an entry to represent Israel in the Eurovision Song Contest and broadcasts the event in the country. Kan confirmed its intention to participate in the 2026 contest on 28 June 2025,[^cite_note-\:8-5] announcing on 29 September that its representative would continue to be selected through the reality singing competition *[HaKokhav HaBa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rising_Star_\(Israeli_TV_series\))* (*Rising Star*).[^cite_note-6]

The [Gaza war](74998519.md) has made Israel's participation in the contest increasingly controversial, prompting calls for its exclusion and protests at the previous two editions.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Broadcasters from [Ireland](3014113.md),[^cite_note-\:2-11] the [Netherlands](530870.md),[^cite_note-\:3-12] [Slovenia](1992733.md),[^cite_note-\:4-13] and [Spain](2996040.md)[^cite_note-\:5-14] announced their intention to boycott the 2026 contest if Israel were allowed to compete. Following the [European Broadcasting Union](10380.md) (EBU) general assembly meeting on 4 December 2025, which permitted Israel's participation,[^cite_note-\:0-15] all four broadcasters proceeded with their boycott,[^cite_note-BBCNews-16] joined by [Iceland](3512313.md) six days later.[^cite_note-\:82-17]

## Before Eurovision

### *HaKokhav HaBa L'Eirovizion*

The artist who would represent Israel in the Eurovision Song Contest 2026 was selected through the reality singing competition *[HaKokhav HaBa L'Eirovizion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rising_Star_\(Israeli_TV_series\))* (transl. The Next Star for Eurovision), produced by [Tedy Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tedy_Productions?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Keshet Media Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keshet_Media_Group), and broadcast on [Keshet 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keshet_12) as well as online via *mako.co.il*. The shows took place at the [Neve Ilan Communications Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Ilan_Communications_Center?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Neve Ilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neve_Ilan), and were hosted by [Assi Azar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assi_Azar) and [Rotem Sela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotem_Sela). The judging panel for the competition composed of [Assaf Amdursky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assaf_Amdursky), [Keren Peles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keren_Peles), [Shiri Maimon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiri_Maimon) (who represented [Israel in 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2005)), [Itay Levi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Itay_Levi), and [Eden Hason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eden_Hason).[^cite_note-\:1-18] A rotating panel of several guest judges also joined, though they were only called upon to vote if a main judge was absent from a performance.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

#### Final

The final took place on 20 January 2026 and was divided into a first round, eliminating one finalist, and a superfinal among the remaining three artists, determining the winner. ### Song selection

The song that Noam Bettan would perform in the contest was internally selected by a professional committee. Any member of the [Society of Authors, Composers and Music Publishers in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Authors,_Composers_and_Music_Publishers_in_Israel) was able to submit entries from 21 December 2025 to 26 January 2026; submissions were required to include at least two lines of Hebrew lyrics.[^cite_note-\:7-62][^cite_note-63] Bettan also submitted his own entries.[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] Approximately 200 submissions were received at the closing of the deadline.[^cite_note-66] The selected song, titled "[Michelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michelle_\(Noam_Bettan_song\))" and written by Bettan with [Nadav Aharoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadav_Aharoni?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Tzlil Klifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tzlil_Klifi?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Yuval Raphael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuval_Raphael), was presented on 5 March 2026 during a special broadcast on [Kan 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kan_11), aired from the Neve Ilan Communications Center and hosted by [Hila Korach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hila_Korach).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

## At Eurovision

The [Eurovision Song Contest 2026](79992910.md) took place at the [Wiener Stadthalle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiener_Stadthalle) in [Vienna](55866.md), Austria, and consist of two semi-finals held on the respective dates of 12 and 14 May and the final on 16 May 2026. All nations with the exceptions of the host country and the "[Big Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Four_\(Eurovision\))" (France, Germany, Italy and the United Kingdom) were required to qualify from one of two semi-finals to compete for the final; the top ten countries from each semi-final progressed to the final. On 12 January 2026, an allocation draw was held to determine which of the two semi-finals, as well as which half of the show, each country performed in; the [European Broadcasting Union](10380.md) (EBU) split up the competing countries into different pots based on voting patterns from previous contests, with countries with favourable voting histories put into the same pot.[^cite_note-70] Israel was scheduled for the second half of the first semi-final.[^cite_note-71] The shows' producers then decided the running order for the semi-finals; Israel was set to perform in position 10.[^cite_note-\:9-72]

The host broadcaster [ORF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ORF_\(broadcaster\)) confirmed that any boos for Israel would not be drowned out or replaced with pre-recorded applause, as they had been in the previous two years.[^cite_note-73]

### Performance



Noam Bettan during a dress rehearsal before the final

Noam Bettan took part in technical rehearsals on 3 and 7 May, followed by dress rehearsals on 11 and 12 May.[^cite_note-74] His performance of "Michelle" was staged by Yuval Cohen and choreographed by Avichai Hacham, with Daniel Ben Avraham, Eden Zino, Jenia Gaponovam, Lihi Freud, and Yasmin Hachmon as supporting dancers; a large spinning "diamond" platform was featured as the central element of the staging.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76]

### Controversies

#### Advertising campaigns

See also: [Israel in the Eurovision Song Contest 2025 § After Eurovision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2025#After_Eurovision), and [Eurovision Song Contest 2026 § Voting system](79992910.md#Voting_system)

As part of changes to the contest's voting system in response to Israel's result the previous year, voting instructions were updated to "discourage disproportionate promotion campaigns… particularly when undertaken or supported by third parties, including governments or governmental agencies". Despite this, ahead of the first semi-final, reports circulated of the Israeli delegation again issuing promotional videos in several languages which instructed viewers to give the maximum 10 votes for Israel. The director of the Eurovision Song Contest, [Martin Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Green_\(producer\)), stated that he was made aware of the videos, as well as that Kan was given a formal warning. Kan later responded that the video was not backed by third-parties, and also cited similar advertising campaigns by other countries' delegations; despite that, the Israeli delegation took down the videos per the EBU's request.[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

#### Social media post on Croatia's performance

The [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026) representatives, [Lelek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lelek_\(band\)), posted on their Instagram that they were "disturbed" by a social media post by Kan about their performance with the caption, "When you overdo it with the [henna tattoos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henna_tattoos) in [Eilat](66162.md)". Lelek stated, "We are disturbed by the comments made by the Israeli broadcaster KAN, in which our culture and the sacrifices made by oppressed Catholic women were mocked. It is especially disturbing to ridicule women singing about female pain and suffering while showing such a complete lack of empathy and respect for the suffering of others". Kan later removed the post and issued an apology.[^cite_note-80]

### Semi-final

Israel performed in position 10, following the entry from [Estonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estonia_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026) and before the entry from [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026).[^cite_note-\:9-72] During the performance, there were pro-Palestine chants and booing, with some audience members removed.[^cite_note-81] At the end of the show, the country was announced as a qualifier for the final.[^cite_note-82] It was later revealed that Israel placed first out of the fifteen participating countries in the first semi-final with 269 points; 163 points from the public televoting and 106 points from the juries.

### Final

Following the semi-final, Israel drew producer's choice for the final.[^cite_note-83] Israel performed in position 3, following the entry from [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest_2026) and before the entry from Belgium.[^cite_note-84] Bettan once again took part in dress rehearsals on 15 and 16 May before the final, including the jury final where the professional juries cast their final votes before the live show on 16 May. Israel placed second in the final, scoring 343 points; 220 points from the public televoting and 123 points from the juries.[^cite_note-85]

#### Detailed voting results
