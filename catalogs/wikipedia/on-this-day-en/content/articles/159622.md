**Tourism in Greece** has been a key element of the economic activity in the country, and is one of the country's most important sectors. [Greece](12108.md) has been a [major tourist destination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Tourism_rankings) and attraction in [Europe](9239.md) since the 1970s for its rich [culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Greece) and [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Greece), which is reflected in large part by its 21 [UNESCO World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_Heritage_Sites_in_Greece), among the [most in Europe and the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Table_of_World_Heritage_Sites_by_country)[^cite_note-Unesco-1] as well as for its [long coastline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_length_of_coastline), many [islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Greece), and beaches.[^cite_note-2]

Greece attracted 40.7 million inbound travelers in 2024, while travel receipts reached 21.59 billion euros, according to the Bank of Greece.[^cite_note-4]

Greece is one of [Europe](9239.md)'s most popular [tourist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism) destinations. The [religious tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_tourism) and [pilgrimages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilgrimages), the [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism), the [conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_conference) tourism, and the [medical tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_tourism) are prominent, and initiatives are being made to promote the seasonal tourism as well. Some of the country's major tourist destinations include the capital city [Athens](1216.md), the islands of [Santorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santorini), [Mykonos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykonos), [Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes), [Corfu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corfu) and [Crete](6591.md), as well as the peninsula of [Chalkidice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalkidice).

## History

Tourism in Greece traces its roots to ancient times. Cultural exchange took place between the Greek colonies of [Magna Graeca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magna_Graeca) and the young Roman Republic before Rome's rise to dominance of the Western Mediterranean. When [Greece](12108.md) was annexed by the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) centuries later, the cultural exchange that started between the two civilizations triggered as a result a large number of [ancient Roman tourists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_ancient_Rome) visiting the famous centers of Greek philosophy and science, such as Athens, [Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Corinth) and [Thebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Thebes_\(Boeotia\)), partly because Greece had become a province of the Roman Empire and [Greeks](42056.md) were granted [Roman citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_citizenship).

Tourism in modern-day Greece started to flourish in the 1960s and 1970s, in what became known as [mass tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_tourism).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-goldgrec-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] During that time, large-scale construction projects for hotels and other such facilities were undertaken, and the country saw an increase in international tourists over the years.[^cite_note-goldgrec-6] International events such as the [2004 Summer Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Summer_Olympic_Games) and the [Eurovision Song Contest 2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2006), both held in Athens, greatly helped to boost tourism in the country, while large-scale nationally funded cultural infrastructure such as the [New Acropolis Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Acropolis_Museum) also contributed to the flow of tourists in the country. [Thessaloniki](40471.md) was the [European Youth Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Youth_Capital) in 2014.&#x20;

## Land and climate

Greece has a Mediterranean climate along its coasts and islands. Once inland, many areas and cities experience a continental climate.[^cite_note-10]

Summers are usually hot and dry, while winters are generally mild and wet. Northern Greece can experience cold winters, while Southern Greece and the islands experience considerably milder winters.[^cite_note-11]

## Infrastructure

As a developed country highly dependent on tourism, Greece offers a wide variety of tourist facilities. Tourism infrastructure in Greece has been greatly improved since the [2004 Athens Olympic Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Athens_Olympic_Games) and continues to expand with a number of important projects particularly in areas of less mass-tourism.[^cite_note-12]

### Marinas

Greece has 51 [marinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marinas) and 14,661 mooring places that provide such services as berths, fuel, water and electricity, telephony, and repairs.

Some of the most developed and busiest marinas in Greece are just a few kilometres from the centre of Athens. The marinas of [Alimos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alimos) and [Flisvos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flisvos?action=edit\&redlink=1), on the south coast of Athens, have an aggregated capacity of more than 1,800 vessels.

### Spas and thermal springs

Greece has 752 thermal [springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_\(hydrosphere\)). Many have been classified as therapeutic by the National Institute for Geographical and Mineral Research. Several of them were known and exploited already since antiquity. Thermal or curative tourism was, after all, one of the earliest forms of tourism in the ancient world. In 1983 was founded the Hellenic Association of Municipalities with [Thermal Springs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_spring).[^cite_note-17] Some of these ancient "spa resorts" were situated in [Aidipsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidipsos), known from the time of [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle),[^cite_note-aidipsoscite-18] Loutraki, mentioned by [Xenophon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophon),[^cite_note-loutraki158-19] Traianoupoli,[^cite_note-20] founded by the emperor [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan) in the 2nd century A.D., whereas some others are attested in the Byzantine period, such as the Thermal baths of [Lagkadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagkadas).[^cite_note-21] In the late 19th and the beginning of the 20th century these hot springs were surrounded by cosmopolitan facilities, namely hotels and restaurants, whereas several prominent members of the society of both the Modern Greek State and the Ottoman State (for regions still incorporated in it) invested in touristic infrastructure and private estates.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] Thermal tourism became particularly widespread in the 1960s and 1970s, whereas in the 1980s it was widely supported by a social tourism program, which subsidized large part of the expenses for the elderly users of the facilities. Nowadays, however, there is an urgent need to refurbish, restore and elevate these spas to modern standards and create an international clientele. Visitors, however, can already find high-standard touristic facilities in [Pozar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pozar_Baths),[^cite_note-26] in [Aidipsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidipsos),[^cite_note-aidipsoscite-18] and in [Kamena Vourla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamena_Vourla),[^cite_note-27] in Loutraki close to Corinth,[^cite_note-loutraki158-19] as well as at Kaiafas, on the western shores of the Peloponnese.[^cite_note-28] A detailed [List of spa towns in Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_spa_towns_in_Greece) can give an insight in the inexorable richness of thermal springs of the country.

There are numerous archaeological sites dotted all around Greece, many of which are popular with tourists.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

The Greek capital, [Athens](1216.md), has many archaeological sites, the most famous being the [Acropolis](2076.md), the [Temple of Olympian Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Olympian_Zeus,_Athens), the [Ancient Athenian cemetery of Kerameikos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerameikos), the [Philopappou Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philopappou_Hill), the [Tower of the Winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_of_the_Winds), [Plato's Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platonic_Academy) and the [Ancient Agora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Agora_of_Athens).

In the adjacent area of [Attica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attica) are the [Marathon tumuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathon_tumuli), burying mounds in [Marathon](49132.md) that house the ashes of the [Athenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Athens) and [Plataean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plataean) hoplites that were killed in the [homonymous battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Marathon). Findings from the area and from the battle of Marathon are preserved in the [Archaeological Museum of Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_Museum_of_Marathon) nearby. In western Attica is [Eleusis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleusis), where, from as early as 1700 BC up to the 4th century AD, it was the site of the Eleusinian Mysteries, or the Mysteries of Demeter and Kore. At the eastern tip of Attica is [Sounion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sounion), with the ruins of the Temple of Poseidon.

Central Greece is the location of [Thebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thebes_\(Greece\)), an ancient city that once rivalled Athens, and featured in [Greek myth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_myth). [Delphi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delphi) has a distinguished ancient theatre, the site of the Oracle. [Thermopylae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermopylae) is primarily known for the battle that took place there in 480 BC, in which an outnumbered Greek force probably of seven thousand (including the famous 300 Spartans) held off a substantially larger force of Persians estimated in the range 70,000-300,000 under Xerxes.

The [Peloponnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peloponnese) peninsula, dominating the south of the mainland, has a wide variety of archaeological sites. Beginning at the north end, there is [Ancient Corinth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Corinth), near the modern town of the same name. In the northeast of the peninsula are [Epidaurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidaurus), with its ancient theatre, now restored; [Argos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Argos), with several ancient ruins, including the [Heraion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heraion_of_Argos); and [Nemea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemea), where, in Greek mythology, [Heracles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heracles) overcame the [Nemean Lion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemean_Lion) of the Lady [Hera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hera), and during Antiquity the [Nemean Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemean_Games) were played. Most important of all, however, is [Mycenae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenae), which, in the second millennium BC, was one of the major centres of Greek civilization, a military stronghold which dominated much of southern Greece and parts of southwest Anatolia. The period of Greek history from about 1600 BC to about 1100 BC is called [Mycenaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greece) in reference to Mycenae. [Tiryns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiryns), also in the area, is a Mycenaean archaeological site.

In the centre of the Peloponnese are [Sparta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparta_\(modern\)), where, near the modern town, are ancient ruins, the most important being the tomb of [Leonidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonidas_I); [Tegea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tegea), an important religious center of ancient Greece, containing the Temple of [Athena Alea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athena_Alea); and [Lycosura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycosura), said by Pausanias to be the oldest city in the world.

On the western side of the peninsula are [Bassae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassae), an archaeological site in the northeastern part of [Messenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messenia); [Messene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Messene) itself, the ruins of a large classical city-state refounded by [Epaminondas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epaminondas) in 369 BC; and, at Pylos, the ruins of the [Palace of Nestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palace_of_Nestor), which is the primary structure within a larger Late Helladic era settlement, likely once surrounded by a fortified wall. In the northwest is [Olympia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia,_Greece), with many ancient ruins, including the [Temple of Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Zeus,_Olympia), the [Temple of Hera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temple_of_Hera,_Olympia), the [Palaestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palaestra_at_Olympia) and the [Leonidaion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonidaion).

Archaeological sites in [Epirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epirus), the western part of mainland Greece, include [Ambracia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambracia), the ruins of the ancient capital of [Pyrrhus of Epirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyrrhus_of_Epirus); [Dodona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodona), whose shrine was regarded as the oldest Hellenic oracle, possibly dating to the second millennium BC according to Herodotus; [Elaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elaea_\(Epirus\)), near the mouth of the [Acheron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acheron); and [Gitanae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gitanae). [Nicopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicopolis) or Actia Nicopolis, also in Epirus, was founded 31 BC by Octavian in memory of his victory over Antony and Cleopatra at Actium the previous year. In addition, there are the ruins believed by some to be those of the [Necromanteion of Acheron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necromanteion_of_Acheron), an ancient Greek temple of [necromancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Necromancy) devoted to [Hades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hades) and [Persephone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persephone).

[Thessaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thessaly), another region of central Greece, has an archaeological site in [Larissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larissa), the [Frourio Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frourio_Hill), which is the location of an [ancient theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Ancient_Theatre,_Larissa) and the ruins of a [basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_St._Achillios).

The second city of Greece, [Thessaloniki](40471.md), is nicknamed the "Co-capital". It has many historic buildings, some [World Heritage Sites](44940.md), including the [Arch and Rotunda of Galerius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch_of_Galerius_and_Rotunda), the [Church of Panagia Chalkeon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Panagia_Chalkeon) and the [White Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Tower_of_Thessaloniki).

In the adjacent region of Macedonia, there is [Dion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dion,_Pieria), the sacred place of the Ancient Macedonians. Dion is the site of a large temple dedicated to Zeus, as well as a series of temples to Demeter and to Isis. [Pella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pella), also in the region, is the capital of [Ancient Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Macedonia) and birthplace of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) and [Philip II of Macedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_II_of_Macedon).

Other archaeological sites in Macedonia include [Olynthus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olynthus) in Chalcidice; [Philippi](24425.md), established by the king of Macedon, Philip II, on the site of the Thracian colony of Krinides or Crenides; [Amphipolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphipolis), an ancient city once inhabited by the [Edoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edoni) people; [Leibethra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leibethra), an ancient city close to [Olympus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Olympus) where [Orpheus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orpheus) was buried by the [Muses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muses); [Stagira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagira_\(ancient_city\)), ruins of the ancient city known as the birthplace of [Aristotle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aristotle); and [Vergina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vergina), a [World Heritage Site](44940.md), the location of the Macedonian Royal Tombs and the ruins of the ancient Macedonian capital.

[Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrace), of which only the western part belongs to modern Greece, has an archaeological site at [Abdera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdera,_Thrace). [Didymoteicho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didymoteicho) is a historical town built near the Turkish border which has many Ancient Greek, Byzantine and Ottoman landmarks, including the ruins of the ancient city of Plotinopolis, the Byzantine fortifications built around the town, the [Bayezid Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayezid_Mosque) built in 1420 and the Silent Baths, the oldest [hamam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_bath) in Europe.

The Greek islands have numerous historical sites of their own. [Chalcis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcis) is located on Euboea. [Delos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delos) is an uninhabited island in the Cyclades famous for its numerous archaeological sites, including the [Stoivadeion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoivadeion), the Temple of the Delians, the Terrace of the Lions and the House of the Dolphins. On [Syros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syros), near Piraeus, and linked to the latter by a ferry taking only 2.5 hours, is the neoclassical city of [Hermoupolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermoupolis), where two civilizations and two religions lived harmonically and peacefully together. It has beaches, classical theatre, casino, general hospital and many places to see.

[Santorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santorini), one of the Cyclades, is the location of [Ancient Thera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Thera), an antique city on a ridge of the steep, 360 m high Messavouno mountain, and of [Akrotiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akrotiri_\(prehistoric_city\)), a Minoan Bronze Age settlement.

The [Samothrace temple complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samothrace_temple_complex) is one of the principal Pan-Hellenic religious sanctuaries, located on the island of Samothrace.

[Crete](6591.md), the largest Greek island, has archaeological sites at [Phaistos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phaistos) in the south, [Zakros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakros) in the east, and most important of all, [Knossos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knossos) in the centre, which is famous for its ruined [Minoan palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoan_palace), with bull motifs.

[Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes), one of the [Dodecanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodecanese) islands, is the location of the Acropolis of [Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhodes), an acropolis dating from the Classical Greek period (5th–3rd century BC); the Acropolis of [Lindos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindos), a natural citadel which was fortified successively by the [Greeks](42056.md), the [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), the [Byzantines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), the [Knights of St John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_of_St_John) and the [Ottomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Turks); and the ancient city of [Kameiros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kameiros). On the nearby island of [Kos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kos) is the [Asclepeion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asclepeion), the ruins of one of the greatest healing temples of the Ancient World and the place where [Hippocrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocrates), the father of modern medicine was trained. On [Kastellorizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kastellorizo), the easternmost island of Greece, is the [Lycian Tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycian_Tomb,_Kastellorizo?action=edit\&redlink=1), a rock cut tomb built by the ancient [Anatolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) civilization of the [Lycians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycians).

## Alternate tourism

### LGBT tourism

Greece also is one of Europe's most popular [LGBT tourist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_tourism) destinations, particularly its largest cities [Athens](1216.md) and [Thessaloniki](40471.md) and some of its islands such as [Lesbos](2633994.md), [Santorini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santorini), [Skiathos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skiathos) and [Mykonos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykonos).[^cite_note-LGBTTourismGreece1-38][^cite_note-LGBTTourismGreece2-39] The latter is a well-known [Gay-friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay-friendly) destination with many establishments catering for the [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) community.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] Skiathos and Mykonos are ranked among the most [LGBT-friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay-friendly) destinations in Europe and the world.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

### Religious tourism

Greece is promoting [religious tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_tourism) and pilgrimages to regions with a significant historical religious presence, such as the Christian monasteries in [Meteora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meteora) and [Mount Athos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Athos), in cooperation with other countries.[^cite_note-46]

### Medical tourism

Greece's temperate climate, medical staff, and medical innovations also are contributing to the country's [medical travel industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_tourism).[^cite_note-47]

### Ecotourism

Greece has become in recent years a destination for [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) (especially [hiking](147313.md), [canoeing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoeing), [caving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caving) and [climbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climbing)).[^cite_note-48] The main destinations for skiing in Greece are [Arachova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachova), [Kalavryta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalavryta), [Karpenisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karpenisi) and [Metsovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metsovo).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

The Greek government is trying to bring more and more ecotourism to help benefit the environment.&#x20;

### Conference tourism

[Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_conference) tourism, targeted at academic, business, or cultural markets, is a cornerstone of the Greek national tourism policy. As a result, the Greek government, with strong support from local authorities, has been offering lucrative cash grants, leasing and employment subsidies and tax allowances to establish new conference facilities and expand existing ones. In a recent report in Meeting and Incentive Travel, Greece was ranked eighth in the world in overnight stays for conferences. Figures from the Tourism Satellite Accounting Research, conducted by WTTC (World Travel & Tourism Council) project a worldwide increase in revenues in business travel to Greece from US\$1.51 bn. in 2001 to US\$2.69 bn. in 2011. In 1998, the figure stood at US\$1.18 bn.

### Four Seasons & Winter tourism

The government intends to promote four seasons & winter tourism in Greece, which could potentially increase international arrivals even further.[^cite_note-51]

## Economic impact

At the same time, tourism consumption increased considerably since the turn of the millennium, from US\$17.7 bn. in 2000 to US\$29.6 bn. in 2004. The numbers of jobs directly or indirectly related to the tourism [sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_recognized_economic_sectors) were 659,719 and represented 16.5% of the country's total employment for that year.
