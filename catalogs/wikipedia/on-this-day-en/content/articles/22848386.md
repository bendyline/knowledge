The **Loukkos River** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): واد لوكوس; [Spanish](26825.md): *Río Luco*) is a major river in northern [Morocco](19291.md). Although it is relatively short (about 100 km), the river is the third largest in Morocco with an average flow of 50 m³/s. The Loukkos river's source is located in the [Rif Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rif_Mountains) and flows into the Atlantic Ocean in the city of [Larache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larache); the city's port is on the river. The Loukkos river basin is 3,730 km<sup>2</sup> and contains one of the most fertile and productive agricultural lands in the country. One of the river's tributaries, Oued Makhazine, bears great historical importance since it witnessed one of the most decisive battles in the history of Morocco: the [Battle of Alcácer Quibir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Alc%C3%A1cer_Quibir). In the battle on 4 August 1578, the joint army of deposed Moroccan monarch [Abu Abdallah Mohammed II Saadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Abdallah_Mohammed_II_Saadi) and his ally, King [Sebastian of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_of_Portugal), was defeated by the army of the new Moroccan sultan from the same [Saadi dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saadi_dynasty) (who was also Abu Abdallah's uncle).

The Lukkus is the largest river in northern Morocco. Although it is relatively short, this river carries the third largest water flow in Morocco. The river can cause great flooding in times of flood. In 1775 the river appeared on a map by Tomás López and Vargas with the denomination of "River Lucos.[^cite_note-2] For a time the river formed a border between Morocco and the French imperial lands.

## History

### Ancient history

Ancient [Lixus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lixus_\(ancient_city\)) is located on [Tchemmich Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tchemmich_Hill) on the right bank of the Loukkos River (other names: Oued Loukous; Locus River), just to the north of the modern seaport of [Larache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larache).[^cite_note-3] The site lies within the urban perimeter of Larache, and about three kilometres inland from the mouth of the river and the Atlantic Ocean. From its 80 metres above the plain the site dominates the marshes through which the river flows.

Lixus was established as a [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) colony, subsequent to Phoenician navigators traversing the Alboran Sea to reach the Atlantic Ocean.[^cite_note-4] Among the ruins at Lixus there are baths, temples, 4th century AD walls, a mosaic floor, and the intricate remains of the Capitol.

Further upstream, El-Ksar el Kebir, was established as a Carthaginian colony which after the [Punic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic_Wars) came under [Roman control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) with the name Oppidum Novum.[^cite_note-5]

### Modern history



Lukkus River at [Ksar El Kebir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ksar_El_Kebir) in 1900

The [confluence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confluence) of the Loukkos River and its tributaries, the al-Mahazin, was the site of a significant battle in 1578 - the [Battle of Alcácer Quibir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Alc%C3%A1cer_Quibir),[^cite_note-chisholm-6] in which two rival claimant to the throne of Morocco were both killed as was the king of [Portugal](23033.md). [Sebastian of Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_of_Portugal), king [Abu Marwan Abd al-Malik I Saadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Marwan_Abd_al-Malik_I_Saadi) and his rival [Abu Abdallah Mohammed II Saadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Abdallah_Mohammed_II_Saadi) were all killed and ended Portugal's ambitions to invade and Christianize the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb).[^cite_note-chisholm-6] One of the main reasons for the defeat of the [Portuguese](23033.md) troops was the rise in the water Loukkos River during the battle. As a result of this battle, Portugal lost its status as a great power.

In 1970 [Thor Heyerdahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thor_Heyerdahl) visited Loukkos River [estuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estuary), and studied the [reed boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reed_boat), manufactured by local [fishermen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishermen) in his plans to sail "[Ra II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra_II). According to Heyerdahl ancient [Phoenician](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) and [Egyptian](8087628.md) sailors who became the founders of Mesoamerican cultures. set out from somewhere like the Lukkus.[^cite_note-7]
