# China's core interests

**China's core interests** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 中国核心利益; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Zhōngguó Héxīn Lìyì*) is a term used by the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) (CCP) to signify issues that are non-negotiable parts of foreign and domestic policy of the [People's Republic of China](5405.md).

## History

The term was first coined by the [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) (CCP) in 2003 in a discussion of [Taiwanese independence](31156.md).[^cite_note-1] The term's definition was expanded to include [Tibet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibet_Autonomous_Region) and [Xinjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xinjiang) in 2006. In July 2009, CCP's [Central Foreign Affairs Leading Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Foreign_Affairs_Commission) Office Director [Dai Bingguo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dai_Bingguo) gave remarks at the [U.S.–China Strategic and Economic Dialogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93China_Strategic_and_Economic_Dialogue), expanding the definition to define the primary goals that determine the country's foreign policy choices.[^cite_note-\:92222-2]<sup>: 193 </sup> The core interests are: maintaining the power of the Chinese Communist Party, continuing China's social and economic growth, and preservation of China's sovereignty and territorial integrity.[^cite_note-\:92222-2]<sup>: 193 </sup> China also views these core interests as red lines that other countries' behavior should not cross.[^cite_note-\:92222-2]<sup>: 193 </sup>

White Paper on China's Peaceful Development, released by the Chinese government in 2011, also confirmed the expanding definition.[^cite_note-3] The white paper defined China's core interests as national sovereignty, national security, territorial integrity, national unity, stability of the political system and society in line with the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_China), and sustainable socio-economic development.[^cite_note-4] The [National Security Law of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Law_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), which took into effect in 2015, defined the core interests as "the political regime; the sovereignty, unity and territorial integrity of the nation; and people’s livelihoods, sustainable economic development of society and other major interests". The new definition also includes any sovereignty issues of importance such as the [South China Sea](74209.md), the [Arunachal Pradesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arunachal_Pradesh) and the [Senkaku Islands dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senkaku_Islands_dispute) as a "core interest".[^cite_note-\:0-5][^cite_note-6]
