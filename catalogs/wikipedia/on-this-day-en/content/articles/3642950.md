The **Argentina women's national field hockey team** (Spanish: *Selección femenina de hockey sobre césped de Argentina*) is governed by the [Argentine Hockey Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentine_Hockey_Confederation) (CAH). The current coach is [Fernando Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Ferrara), who was appointed after [Carlos Retegui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Retegui) was let go in late 2021. The team is currently second in the [FIH Women's World Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIH_Women's_World_Ranking).

*Las Leonas* (The Lionesses) have appeared in six [Hockey World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Hockey_World_Cup) finals, including the first final in 1974, which they lost 1–0 to the [Netherlands](7325406.md). Argentina had to settle with second place in two more finals before winning the tournament for the first time in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Women's_Hockey_World_Cup), beating the Netherlands 4–3 in the final on [penalty strokes](42538249.md#Penalty_stroke_competition_\(before_2011\)) after a 1–1 draw. Argentina, led by eight-time [FIH Player of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIH_Player_of_the_Year_Awards) [Luciana Aymar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciana_Aymar) won again in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Women's_Hockey_World_Cup), a 3–1 victory over the Netherlands. Argentina's World Cup-winning coaches are [Sergio Vigil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Vigil) in 2002 and [Carlos Retegui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Retegui) in 2010.

Argentina has been very successful at the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_Summer_Olympics), winning four consecutive medals (two silver, two bronze) since the [2000 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics), when they became the first women's team in any sport to win an Olympic medal for their country. Luciana Aymar is the only player that has participated and won those four medals. Also, after their first title in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Women's_Hockey_Champions_Trophy) at a [Hockey Champions Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Champions_Trophy), they have won the tournament six more times. In front of a home crowd, they won the [2014–15 Hockey World League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Women's_FIH_Hockey_World_League) as the first international title after Aymar's retirement from the national team the previous year.

At a continental level, Argentina has dominated and won every tournament they played, including the [Pan American Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Pan_American_Cup) and the [Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_Pan_American_Games) leaving the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_field_hockey_team) with second place on most events until they lost the [2011 Pan American Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_2011_Pan_American_Games_%E2%80%93_Women's_tournament) final for the first time.

In July 2003, after the implementation of an official World Ranking System, Argentina reached the top of the [FIH Women's World Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIH_Women's_World_Ranking) for the first time, reaching it again in 2010 after obtaining the World Cup title and once more in late 2013.

## History

Hockey was introduced in Argentina by [English immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_settlement_in_Argentina) at the beginning of the 20th century, and the first women's teams were officially formed in 1909.[^cite_note-2] In 1997, [Sergio Vigil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergio_Vigil), a former player for the [men's national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_men's_national_field_hockey_team), was appointed coach. Under his leadership, *Las Leonas* achieved their first [World Hockey Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Hockey_Cup) title, their first [Olympic](22576.md) medals, their first [Champions Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Champions_Trophy) medals, and many other achievements. The team went from having a rather limited audience to becoming a national sensation, with some of the players even appearing as models in advertising campaigns.

### Nickname

Throughout its history, the team has developed a reputation for being tenacious even when a match appears to be lost. For this reason, a [lioness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion) was chosen as their symbol when the team qualified for the [2000 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Summer_Olympics). During the second round of games, Argentina played against the powerful [Dutch team](7325406.md), and they chose this occasion to place the image of a lioness on their shirts for the first time.

The image was designed by then-player [Inés Arrondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In%C3%A9s_Arrondo) together with Vigil's sister-in-law.[^cite_note-3] Argentina won that match, went on to win the silver medal, and *Las Leonas* were born. Subsequently, the junior (under 21) team is called *[Las Leoncitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_women's_national_under-21_field_hockey_team)* ("the baby lionesses" or "the lioness cubs").

The lioness logo was redesigned in 2006 by the team kit supplier, [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas), along with [Confederación Argentina de Hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederaci%C3%B3n_Argentina_de_Hockey) and even some of the most representative players. This is slightly different from the original, showing the lioness' tail pretending to be a hockey stick while holding a ball.[^cite_note-4]

The nickname also falls in line with an unwritten Argentine tradition of naming national teams after [big cats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_cats): the men's [field hockey](10886.md) team is called *[Los Leones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Leones)* ("The Lions"), the men's [rugby union](25405.md) team is called *[Los Pumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Pumas)* ("The [Pumas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cougar)"), and the women's [volleyball](32558.md) team is known as *[Las Panteras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argentina_women's_national_volleyball_team)* ("The [Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaguar)").

[^cite_note-teleg-13]

[^cite_note-allabout-14]

When [Luciana Aymar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciana_Aymar) (eight-time [FIH Player of the Year Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIH_Player_of_the_Year_Award) winner and regarded as the best player in the history of the sport),[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-teleg-13][^cite_note-allabout-14] retired from the national team in 2014 after 376 international matches played, some of Aymar's teammates (such as [Carla Rebecchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carla_Rebecchi)[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]) asked the Confederation for the retirement of her iconic number 8 worn by her during 17 years with the national team.[^cite_note-18] Nevertheless, the number is not officially retired by the CAH, although it has not been assigned to other players since.
