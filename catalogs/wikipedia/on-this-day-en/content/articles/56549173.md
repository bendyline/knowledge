**Carlos Cordeiro** (born 1956) is an American sports executive and retired investment banker. He was the senior advisor to the [FIFA President](11052.md), and had served as senior advisor to the [White House](33057.md) Task Force on the [FIFA World Cup 2026](36581929.md).

Previously he was the president of the [United States Soccer Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Soccer_Federation) from February 10, 2018[^cite_note-USSF-2018-1] until March 12, 2020[^cite_note-2] when he resigned after criticism[^cite_note-3] over the official legal position taken by U.S. Soccer under his administration toward the U.S. [women's national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_women's_national_soccer_team). In 2021, he was appointed the Senior Advisor to FIFA for Global Strategy and Governance, and, later, the Senior Advisor to the FIFA President. In May 2025, Cordeiro was appointed by President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) to serve as the Senior Advisor to the White House Task Force on the FIFA World Cup 2026.[^cite_note-4]

## Biography

Cordeiro was born to a Colombian mother and father of [Luso-Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luso-Indian) descent[^cite_note-5] in [Bombay](19189.md). He moved to [Miami](53846.md) at the age of 15 and is a graduate of [Harvard University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvard_University), where he earned an AB and MBA. Prior to joining the USSF in 2007 as an independent director, Cordeiro was a partner at [Goldman Sachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goldman_Sachs) and an independent director at [BHP](200825.md).[^cite_note-USSF-2018-1][^cite_note-6]
