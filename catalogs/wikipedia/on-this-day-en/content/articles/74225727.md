**Loek Hartog** (born 5 October 2002) is a [Dutch](21148.md) racing driver competing in the [GT World Challenge Europe Endurance Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GT_World_Challenge_Europe_Endurance_Cup) for [Rutronik Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutronik_Racing). He is the 2025 [Porsche Cup](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche%20Cup) award winner.

## Career

### Porsche competition

After making a brief cameo in [karting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karting), in the BNL Golden Trophy, Hartog began his full time racing debut in the 2019 [Porsche Carrera Cup Benelux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Carrera_Cup_Benelux), where he finished in third, picking up two wins, four pole positions and eight podiums, with [Bas Koeten Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas_Koeten_Racing). He also made an appearance in the 2019 [Porsche Carrera Cup France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Carrera_Cup_France) with the team and then drove for MRS GT-Racing in the [2019 Porsche Supercup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Porsche_Supercup) for the seventh round in [Spa-Francorchamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Spa-Francorchamps), where he finished in 18th.

In 2020, Hartog made cameos in the [2020 Porsche Supercup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Porsche_Supercup) and the [Porsche Carrera Cup Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Carrera_Cup_Germany) for [Bas Koeten Racing Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bas_Koeten_Racing). He came tenth in his appearance at [Spa-Francorchamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa-Francorchamps) in the [2020 Porsche Supercup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Porsche_Supercup), but as he was guest driver, he was ineligible to score points. He also won the [Porsche Carrera Cup Benelux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Carrera_Cup_Benelux) that year as well.

Hartog's career perked up in 2021, where he got a full time drive in the [2021 Porsche Carrera Cup Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Porsche_Carrera_Cup_Germany) for Black Falcon Team TEXTAR.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] Hartog was one of the standout rookies of the series, as he fought with [Bastian Buus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bastian_Buus) for the rookie title, with Hartog pipping him to the title. He also got his first podium in the series at his home race at [Circuit Zandvoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Zandvoort).

Hartog also drove in the [2021 Porsche Supercup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Porsche_Supercup) for three races with [Team Parker Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Parker_Racing), coming in points paying positions in [Spa-Francorchamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa-Francorchamps) and his home race of [Circuit Zandvoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Zandvoort), he was ineligible for points due to being a guest driver.[^cite_note-4] In 2022, Hartog drove in the [Porsche Supercup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Porsche_Supercup) full time with Ombra Racing, scoring points in all races bar the exception of [Circuit Paul Ricard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Paul_Ricard), where he retired from the race.[^cite_note-5] He finished eighth in the overall classifications with 43 points and came runner-up to the rookies title.

Hartog also contested the [2022 Porsche Carrera Cup Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Porsche_Carrera_Cup_Germany) full time, driving for Black Falcon, where he got his maiden pole position in the [Red Bull Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Bull_Ring), followed by a second pole a round later in [Imola Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imola_Circuit).[^cite_note-6] He finished the season in fifth with four podiums and 185 points.



Hartog during the Supercup Austrian round in 2023.

In 2023, Hartog drove for Dutch team Team GP Elite in the [2023 Porsche Supercup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Porsche_Supercup) and the [2023 Porsche Carrera Cup Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Porsche_Carrera_Cup_Germany).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] At [Zandvoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zandvoort_Circuit) he would get his first podium in the Porsche Supercup, with a second place, and in the following race, also staged at Zandvoort, he got his maiden pole in the series, which was followed by a maiden win.[^cite_note-9] He finished the championship in sixth place with 75 points.

Meanwhile, in the [2023 Porsche Carrera Cup Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Porsche_Carrera_Cup_Germany), Hartog had a rather successful campaign where he fought for the championship title with teammate, two-time [Porsche Supercup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_Supercup) champion, [Larry ten Voorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_ten_Voorde). He started his title bid in the first race of the first round at [Spa-Francorchamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Spa-Francorchamps), where he got a podium and a maiden win in the second race.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Hartog got two pole positions at the second round at the [Hockenheimring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockenheimring), coming second and first in the races.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] Despite getting no podiums in the next rounds, he was consistent with points until he repeated a similar exploit to the one he did at the [Hockenheimring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockenheimring), in the fifth round at the [Lausitzring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausitzring), where he claimed two poles and won the first race, but would retire from the second one.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

In the final few rounds, Hartog would claim two more poles and three more podiums, including a win. He finished the championship in the runner-up position, behind [Larry ten Voorde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_ten_Voorde).[^cite_note-17]

In 2024, Hartog joined Kellymoss to make his debut in the [Porsche Carrera Cup North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Porsche_Carrera_Cup_North_America) and dominated the championship, throughout the sixteen races, he won seven of them, getting eight pole positions and bagging a podium in all races but two, as he ended the championship with 339 points, which was more than 100 points ahead of his main rival, New Zealander Ryan Yardley.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

Hartog also made a cameo in the fifth round of the [2024 Porsche Supercup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Porsche_Supercup) at the [Hungaroring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungaroring) with [Dinamic Motorsport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinamic_Motorsport), where he came ninth in the race, but as he was a guest driver, he was ineligible to score points.

Hartog also competed in the inaugural edition of the [2024 992 Endurance Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_992_Endurance_Cup), a 12-hour endurance race at the [Circuit de Spa-Francorchamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Spa-Francorchamps) with German team BLACK FALCON Team 48 LOSCH and German drivers, [Christopher Mies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Mies), [Tobias Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobias_M%C3%BCller_\(racing_driver\)) and Noah Nagelsdiek. The quartet would win the race in twelve hours, one minute and fifteen seconds after qualifying in fifth position.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

### GT3 career



Hartog's No. 97 car during the Nürburgring round of the 2024 GT World Challenge Europe Endurance Cup. They converted their maiden pole position into a victory.

In early 2024, Hartog made his [GT3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_GT3) debut with [Dennis Marschall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Marschall) and [Dustin Blattner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dustin_Blattner) for Car Collection Motorsport in the second round of the [Middle East Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_Middle_East_Trophy) at the [6 Hours of Abu Dhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yas_Marina_Circuit), where they would win the race. The young Dutchman would continue with GT's and would drive a [Porsche 911 GT3 R (992)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_911_GT3_R_\(992\)) for [Rutronik Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutronik_Racing) with Marschall and Blattner in the Bronze Cup of the [2024 GT World Challenge Europe Endurance Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_GT_World_Challenge_Europe_Endurance_Cup).[^cite_note-RutronikGTWC-22] After a rough start in the first race of the season, and retiring from the [2024 24 Hours of Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_24_Hours_of_Spa), they got a maiden a pole position and converted that to a win in the third round at [Nürburgring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring). Hartog wouldn't participate in the fourth round due to a clash with his [Porsche Carrera Cup North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Porsche_Carrera_Cup_North_America) duties, but did the fifth and final round at the [Jeddah Corniche Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeddah_Corniche_Circuit), where his team followed the same pattern they did at the [Nürburgring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BCrburgring). He came fifth in the Bronze category of the drivers' championship with 33 points.[^cite_note-23]

To round off his 2024 season, Hartog partnered up with drivers [Patric Niederhauser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patric_Niederhauser) and [Antares Au](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antares_Au) to compete in the [2024 Indianapolis 8 Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Indianapolis_8_Hours) in the Pro-Am class with Herberth Motorsport.[^cite_note-24] Niederhauser got the event's overall pole position, and the trio finished the race in seventh place in their category with a fastest lap as well. They were ineligible for points as they entered the race as guest drivers.

In 2025, Hartog continued in the [GT World Challenge Europe Endurance Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GT_World_Challenge_Europe_Endurance_Cup) with [Rutronik Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutronik_Racing), partnering [Morris Schuring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_Schuring) and Au. He won the Bronze Cup class at [Monza](952083.md) and [Barcelona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_de_Barcelona-Catalunya) to finish runner-up. Hartog's season included entry into all three major 24-hour races which took place consecutively in June: the [24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) for [Manthey Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manthey_Racing), the [24 Hours of Nürburgring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_24_Hours_of_N%C3%BCrburgring) for [Dinamic GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dinamic_GT) (where he finished 3rd overall), and the [24 Hours of Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_24_Hours_of_Spa) for Rutronik.[^cite_note-72hours-25]

In late 2025, Hartog won the [Porsche Cup](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche%20Cup), an annual award presented by [Porsche AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_AG) to recognise the world's most successful privateer racing driver competing with Porsche machinery in a customer racing team.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

On 5 September 2026, Hartog rescued fellow driver [Ollie Millroy](84177892.md) from a burning car following an accident at the [Shanghai](952143.md) round of the [2026 China GT Championship](82802381.md).[^cite_note-28] Millroy was hit by two cars at the turn 14 hairpin, causing his Ferrari to burst into flames. Hartog, who was several positions back, stopped to assist Millroy, using a fire extinguisher handed to him by a marshal before pulling Millroy out of the car.[^cite_note-MillroyS365-29] Millroy was later taken to hospital with several fractures, where he thanked Hartog for "his incredible act of bravery", stating "I owe my life to him".[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Hartog said on a social media post that there was "never a moment of doubt" about helping Millroy.[^cite_note-MillroyS365-29]

## Personal life

Hartog is one of the tallest drivers in racing, at 1.98 m (6 ft 6 in).[^cite_note-72hours-25][^cite_note-32]
