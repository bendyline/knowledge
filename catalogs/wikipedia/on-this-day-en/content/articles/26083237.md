**[Free Trade Agreement](10883.md) between [Mexico](3966054.md) and the [European Union](9317.md)** (FTA EU-MX), is a [trade agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_pact) between the European Union and Mexico. It was signed on December 8, 1997, in the city of [Brussels](3708.md), under the designation "Agreement of [Economic Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_partnership_agreement), Political Coordination and Cooperation between the United Mexican States and the European Community[^cite_note-1] and its members". On October 1, 2000 the agreement came into force,[^cite_note-2] and taxes applying to a large quantity of importing goods were eliminated or reduced.

The goal of this trade agreement is to establish a [framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_framework) to encourage the development of trade in goods and services and their [bilateral](429154.md) and preferential, progressive and reciprocal, taking into account the sensitivity of certain products and services sectors, and in accordance with relevant [WTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization) rules. The Joint Council is responsible for deciding the arrangements and timetable for the liberalization of duties and non-duties barriers to trade in goods, in accordance with the relevant WTO rules. The decision shall include the following subjects:[^cite_note-3]

- [Customs duties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs) on imports and exports and charges having equivalent effect.

- Quantitative restrictions on imports and exports and measures having equivalent effect.

- Anti-dumping/countervailing measurements.

- [Safeguards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safeguard) and monitoring measurements.

- Origin regime and customs co-operation.

- [Customs valuation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Customs_valuation).

- Standards and technical regulations, [sanitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitation) and phytosanitary legislation, mutual recognition of conformity assessment, [certification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Certification).

- General exceptions justified on grounds of public morality, public order or [public security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_security), protection of life or health of humans, animals or plants, protection of [industrial property](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_property), intellectual and commercial.

- Restrictions in case of difficulties in the balance of payments.

Mexico and the European Union successfully ended negotiations on an updated trade agreement on 28 April 2020, pending approval by both sides.[^cite_note-4]
