***Dragon Ball Z***[^cite_note-5] (***DBZ***) is a Japanese [anime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anime) television series produced by [Toei Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toei_Animation). Part of the [*Dragon Ball* media franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball), it serves as a direct sequel to the 1986 *[Dragon Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_\(TV_series\))* television series, adapting the remaining 325 chapters of the [manga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manga) series *[Dragon Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_\(manga\))* by [Akira Toriyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akira_Toriyama).[^cite_note-Chapters-3] The series aired in Japan on [Fuji Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji_Television) and its [FNS affiliates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FNN_and_FNS_affiliates) from April 1989 to January 1996 and was later dubbed for broadcast in at least 81 countries worldwide.[^cite_note-6]

*Dragon Ball Z* continues the adventures of [Son Goku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goku) in his adult life as he and his companions defend the Earth against villains including a warrior race ([Vegeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegeta)), evil forces ([Frieza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frieza)), androids ([Cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_\(Dragon_Ball\))), and magical creatures ([Majin Buu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majin_Buu)). At the same time, the story follows the life of Goku's son, [Gohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gohan), as well as the development of his rivals, [Piccolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piccolo_\(Dragon_Ball\)) and Vegeta.

Due to the massive success of *Dragon Ball Z* in the United States, the manga chapters making up its story were initially released by [Viz Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viz_Media) under the same title. The anime's popularity has also spawned various media and merchandise that has come to represent the majority of the material within the *Dragon Ball* franchise, including films, home media releases, soundtracks, trading cards, and video games. *Dragon Ball Z* remains a [cultural icon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_icon) through numerous adaptations and re-releases, including a recut and [remastered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remastered) version titled *[Dragon Ball Z Kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Z_Kai)* that aired on Fuji Television from April 2009 to June 2015. *Dragon Ball Z* has since been followed by an anime-exclusive sequel series titled *[Dragon Ball GT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_GT)* (1996–1997), a "[midquel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midquel)" series titled *[Dragon Ball Super](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Super_\(TV_series\))* (2015–2018), and another midquel series titled *[Dragon Ball Daima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Daima)* (2024–2025).

### Saiyan Saga

*Dragon Ball Z* picks up five years after the end of the *[Dragon Ball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_\(TV_series\))* series, with [Son Goku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goku) now a young adult and father to his son, [Gohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gohan).

A humanoid alien named [Raditz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Raditz) arrives on Earth in a spacecraft and tracks down Goku, revealing to him that he is his long-lost older brother and that they are members of a near-extinct elite alien warrior race called [Saiyans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saiyan_\(Dragon_Ball\)) (サイヤ人, *Saiya-jin*). Goku (originally named Kakarot (カカロット, *Kakarotto*) had been sent to Earth to escape the destruction of Planet Vegeta, the Saiyan planet.

Raditz tells Goku that along with two stronger elites, [Vegeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegeta) and [Nappa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Nappa), they are the only remaining Saiyans after their home planet Vegeta was destroyed. Raditz asks Goku to enlist in helping conquering planets and joining the remaining Saiyans. When Goku refuses to join and help them, Raditz takes Goku and Krillin down with one strike, kidnaps Gohan, and threatens to murder him if Goku does not kill 100 humans within the next 24 hours. Goku decides to team up with his arch-enemy [Piccolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piccolo_\(Dragon_Ball\)), who was also defeated by Raditz in an earlier encounter, to defeat him and save his son. During the battle, Gohan's rage momentarily makes him stronger than Piccolo and Goku as he attacks Raditz to protect his father. The battle ends with Goku restraining Raditz so that Piccolo can hit them with a deadly move called Special Beam Cannon (魔貫光殺砲, *Makankōsappō*; lit. 'Demon Penetrating, Killing Ray Gun'), mortally wounding them both, and kills them after a short while. But before Raditz succumbs to his injuries, he reveals to Piccolo that the other two Saiyans are much stronger than him and will come for the Dragon Balls in one year.

Having witnessed Gohan's latent potential, Piccolo takes him into the wild to train for the upcoming battle against the Saiyans. In the afterlife, Goku travels the million-kilometer Snake Way so that he can train under the ruler of the North Galaxy, [King Kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Kai). King Kai teaches Goku the Kaio-ken (界王拳, *Kaiōken*; lit. 'Fist of Kings of Worlds') and the Spirit Bomb (元気玉, *Genki Dama*; lit. 'Energy Sphere') techniques. Despite his gruff and villainous nature, Piccolo grows fond of Gohan while he oversees him learning to fend for himself. This forges an unlikely emotional bond between the two.

After a year, Goku is revived with the Dragon Balls, but King Kai panics as he realizes that Goku will have to take Snake Way again to get back and will not make it until hours after the Saiyans arrive. Goku's allies group up to fight until Goku gets back, but prove to be no match for Nappa and the "Prince of All Saiyans", Vegeta. [Yamcha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamcha), [Tien Shinhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tien_Shinhan), [Chiaotzu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Chiaotzu) and Piccolo all perish in the battle, with Piccolo's death causing both [Kami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Kami) and the Dragon Balls to fade from existence. When Goku finally arrives at the battlefield, he avenges his fallen friends by easily defeating Nappa before crippling him by breaking his spine in half. A furious Vegeta then executes Nappa for his failure to kill Goku.

Goku uses several grades of the Kaio-ken to win the first clash with Vegeta, which concludes with a climactic *ki* beam struggle, but it comes at a great cost to his body. Vegeta comes back and creates an artificial moon to transform into a Great Ape, which he uses to torture Goku. [Krillin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krillin) and Gohan sense that Goku is in trouble, and they return for a group fight with the now-seemingly unstoppable Vegeta. They are aided at key moments by [Yajirobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Yajirobe), who cuts Vegeta's tail to revert him into his normal state. Goku gives Krillin a Spirit Bomb that he made, and Krillin uses it to severely damage Vegeta. Vegeta is ultimately defeated when he is crushed by Gohan's Great Ape form, and he retreats to his spaceship as Krillin approaches to finish him off. Goku convinces Krillin to spare Vegeta's life and allow him to escape Earth, with Vegeta vowing to return and destroy the planet in revenge for his humiliation at Goku's hands.

### Frieza Saga

During the battle, Krillin overhears Vegeta mentioning the original set of Dragon Balls from Piccolo's homeworld, Namek (ナメック星, *Namekku-sei*). While Goku recovers from his injuries, Gohan, Krillin, and Goku's oldest friend [Bulma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulma) depart for Namek to use these Dragon Balls to revive their fallen friends in the battle.

Upon their arrival on Namek, Krillin, Gohan, and Bulma discover that Vegeta and his superior, the galactic tyrant [Frieza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frieza), are already there, each looking to use the Dragon Balls to obtain [immortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortality). Vegeta is stronger than before, as Saiyans become stronger when they recover from the brink of death, so he seizes the opportunity to rebel against Frieza. A triangular game of cat-and-mouse ensues, with Frieza, Vegeta, and Gohan plus Krillin alternately possessing one or more of the Dragon Balls, with no one managing to possess all seven at any given time.

Vegeta manages to isolate Frieza's lieutenants one by one and kill them. When Frieza sees that Vegeta is posing too big of a problem, he summons the [Ginyu Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Ginyu_Force), a team of elite mercenaries led by Captain Ginyu, who can switch bodies with his opponents. Vegeta reluctantly teams up with Gohan and Krillin to fight them, knowing that they are too much for him to handle alone. The Ginyu Force proves too powerful, but Goku finally arrives and defeats them single-handedly, saving Vegeta, Gohan, and Krillin. Vegeta believes Goku may have become the legendary warrior of the Saiyans, the [Super Saiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saiyan_\(Dragon_Ball\)#Super_Saiyan) (超(スーパー)サイヤ人, *Sūpā Saiya-jin*). As Goku heals from a brutal fight with Captain Ginyu, Krillin, Dende, and Gohan secretly use the Dragon Balls behind Vegeta's back to wish for Piccolo's resurrection and teleport him to Namek. Vegeta finds them using the Dragon Balls without him, but the [Grand Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Namekians) dies and rendering the Dragon Balls inert before he can wish for immortality. Just as this happens, Frieza arrives and decides to kill the four of them for denying him his wish for immortality.

Piccolo arrives on Namek but is accidentally separated from the others due to a badly-worded wish. He finds the strongest Namekian warrior, Nail, who was defeated by Frieza and fuses with him to greatly increase his power.

Despite both Piccolo and Vegeta's advances in power, they are greatly outclassed by Frieza, who goes through several transformations before reaching his final form, which he then uses to kill Dende.

Goku arrives after healing from his injuries, and Vegeta tells him that Frieza was the one who destroyed the Saiyan homeworld and massacred the Saiyan race, as he feared that he would one day be overthrown by a Super Saiyan. Frieza then kills Vegeta in front of Goku.

Though Goku's power exceeds Vegeta's, he is still no match for Frieza. Goku uses his last resort, a massive Spirit Bomb with the absorbed energy of Namek and the surrounding worlds, and it seemingly defeats the tyrant. However, Frieza manages to survive, and he unleashes his wrath upon the group by gravely wounding Piccolo and murdering Krillin. Goku's rage finally erupts, and he undergoes a strange transformation that turns his hair blond, his eyes green, and causes a golden aura to radiate from his body. Goku has finally become a Super Saiyan.

Meanwhile, the revived Kami uses Earth's Dragon Balls to resurrect everyone on Namek that was killed by Frieza and his henchmen, which also allows the Grand Elder to be resurrected for a short time, and the Namekian Dragon to return. Dende uses the final wish to teleport everyone on Namek to Earth except for Goku and Frieza.

Even at 100% power, Frieza cannot manage to defeat the Super Saiyan transformation. Goku outwits Frieza, cutting him in half with his own attack, before escaping Namek as the planet is destroyed in a massive explosion.

### Garlic Jr. Saga

After the battle with Frieza in an anime exclusive saga, Goku's friends and family are waiting for word on his return when a demonic star drifts into Earth's orbit and opens up a rift in space, allowing the malevolent immortal [Garlic Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Garlic_Jr.) to break free from his imprisonment inside the Dead Zone. Seeking revenge for [a past defeat at the hands of Gohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Z:_Dead_Zone), Garlic Jr. traps Kami and Mr. Popo inside a bottle and uses his Black Water Mist to turn all of Earth's inhabitants into bloodthirsty, vampire-like beings. Gohan, Krillin, Piccolo, Krillin's then-girlfriend Maron, and Gohan's pet dragon Icarus are the only ones unaffected and set out to stop Garlic Jr. and restore the Earth and its inhabitants. This proves to be easier said than done, as Garlic Jr. has complete immortality, making him impossible to kill. Luckily, Gohan's hidden potential gives him the edge he needs to eradicate Garlic Jr.'s forces and send him back into the Dead Zone. He also destroys the star, ensuring that Garlic Jr. will remain trapped in the Dead Zone for all eternity.

### Androids and Cell Saga

One year later, Frieza is revealed to have survived and arrives on Earth with his father, [King Cold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Cold), seeking revenge. However, a mysterious young man named [Trunks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trunks_\(Dragon_Ball\)) appears, transforms into a Super Saiyan, and kills Frieza and King Cold. Goku returns a few hours later, having spent the past year on the alien planet Yardrat learning a new technique: Instant Transmission, which allows him to teleport to any location he desires. Trunks reveals privately to Goku that he is the son of Vegeta and Bulma, and has traveled from 17 years in the future to warn Goku that two [Androids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_\(robot\)) (人造人間, *Jinzōningen*; lit. 'Artificial Humans') created by [Dr. Gero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Gero) will appear in three years to seek revenge against Goku for destroying the [Red Ribbon Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Red_Ribbon_Army) when he was a child. Trunks says all of Goku's friends will fall to them - while Goku himself will die from a heart virus six months before their arrival.

Trunks gives Goku medicine from the future that will save him from the heart virus and departs back to his own time. When the androids arrive, Goku falls ill during his fight with [Android 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_19) but is saved by Vegeta, who reveals that he has also achieved the Super Saiyan transformation. Vegeta and Piccolo easily defeat Android 19 and Dr. Gero (who turned himself into "Android 20"), but Trunks returns from the future to check on their progress and reveals that the androids they defeated are not the ones that killed all of them in the future.

Goku is out of commission and his allies are overwhelmed by the arrival of Androids [16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Ribbon_Army#Android_16), [17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Ribbon_Army#Android_17) and [18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Android_18), while an even stronger bio-Android called [Cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_\(Dragon_Ball\)) emerges from a different timeline and embarks on a quest to find and absorb Androids 17 and 18, allowing him to attain his "perfect form".

Cell successfully absorbs Android 17, becoming considerably more powerful, but Vegeta returns to the battle, having greatly elevated his power, and easily overpowers him. However, Vegeta allows Cell to absorb Android 18, believing that his "perfect form" will be no match for his Super Saiyan power. Vegeta is subsequently defeated, with Cell mockingly thanking him for helping him achieve perfection.

Cell allows everyone to live for the time being and announces a fighting tournament to decide the fate of the Earth, known as the "Cell Games". Goku, recovered from the heart virus and having reached the zenith of the Super Saiyan form, takes on Cell at the tournament. Goku eventually realizes that Cell is far too powerful for him to handle, and forfeits the fight to the astonishment of everyone else. Goku proclaims that Gohan will be able to defeat Cell. Though initially outclassed, Gohan is eventually able to tap into his latent power and achieve the Super Saiyan 2 transformation after Android 16 sacrifices himself in a failed attempt to kill Cell. Refusing to accept defeat, Cell prepares to self-destruct and destroy the Earth.

Goku uses his Instant Transmission ability to teleport himself and Cell to King Kai's planet, where Cell explodes and kills everyone there. However, Cell survives the blast and, being super perfect than ever, returns to Earth where he promptly murders Trunks, but Gohan unleashes the totality of his power in a massive Kamehameha wave and obliterates Cell for good.

The Dragon Balls are then used to revive everyone that was killed by Cell, while Goku chooses to remain in the afterlife, refusing an offer by his friends to use the Namekian Dragon Balls to bring him back. Trunks returns to his timeline and uses his bolstered power to finally slay the Future Androids and Future Cell.

### Majin Buu Saga

Seven years later, Goku is allowed to go back to Earth for one day to reunite with his loved ones and meet his youngest son, [Goten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Son_Goten), at the World Martial Arts Tournament (天下一武道会, *Tenkaichi Budōkai*; "Number One Martial Arts Gathering Under the Heavens"). Soon after, Goku and his allies are drawn into a fight by the [Supreme Kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Supreme_Kai) against a magical being named [Majin Buu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majin_Buu) (魔人ブウ, *"Majin Buu"*) summoned by the evil wizard [Babidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Babidi). All efforts to stop the resurrection prove to be futile as Buu is successfully revived and begins slaughtering Earth's inhabitants. Goten and Trunks are taught the fusion technique by Piccolo, while Gohan gets his latent potential unlocked by the [Elder Supreme Kai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Supreme_Kai).

Meanwhile, Buu befriends Mr. Satan and vows to never kill anyone ever again, but is interrupted when a deranged gunman shoots and nearly kills Mr. Satan. As a result, Majin Buu becomes so angry that he expels all the evil within himself, creating an Evil Buu that proceeds to absorb the Good Buu. The result is Super Buu, a psychopathic monster who wants nothing more than the destruction of the universe. After numerous battles that result in the deaths of many of Goku's allies as well as the destruction of Earth, Goku is fully restored by the Elder Supreme Kai and defeats Kid Buu (the original form of Majin Buu) with a Super Spirit Bomb attack containing the energy of all the inhabitants of Earth, who were resurrected along with the planet by the Namekian Dragon Balls. Goku makes a wish for Kid Buu to be reincarnated as a good person and, ten years later at another Tenkaichi Budōkai, he meets Kid Buu's human reincarnation, [Uub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_characters#Uub). Leaving the match between them unfinished, Goku departs with Uub so he can train him to become Earth's new defender.

## Production and broadcasting

Further information: [List of *Dragon Ball Z* episodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_Z_episodes)

[Kazuhiko Torishima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazuhiko_Torishima), Akira Toriyama's editor for *[Dr. Slump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Slump)* and the first half of *Dragon Ball*, felt that the *Dragon Ball* anime's ratings were gradually declining because it had the same producer that worked on *Dr. Slump*. Torishima said this producer had this "cute and funny" image connected to Toriyama's work and was missing the more serious tone in the newer series, and therefore asked the studio to change the producer. Impressed with their work on *[Saint Seiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Seiya_\(TV_series\))*, he asked its director Kōzō Morishita and writer [Takao Koyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takao_Koyama) to help "reboot" *Dragon Ball*, which coincided with Goku growing up. The new producer explained that ending the first anime and creating a new one would result in more promotional money. The result was the start of *Dragon Ball Z*.[^cite_note-Forbes-7] Toriyama suggested the title because Z is the last letter of the alphabet. He wanted to finish the series because he was running out of ideas for it.[^cite_note-8] Ironically enough, the sequel series would end up producing more episodes than its predecessor.

*Dragon Ball Z* is adapted from the final 324 chapters of the manga series which were published in *[Weekly Shōnen Jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weekly_Sh%C5%8Dnen_Jump)* from 1988 to 1995. It premiered in Japan on Fuji Television on April 26, 1989, taking over its predecessor's time slot, and ran for 291 episodes until its conclusion on January 31, 1996.[^cite_note-AnimeEncyc-9] Because Toriyama was writing the manga during the production of the anime,[^cite_note-10] *Dragon Ball Z* added original material not adapted from the manga, including lengthening scenes or adding new ones, and adding new attacks and characters not present in the manga. For example, Toriyama was asked to create an additional character for Goku's training with King Kai, resulting in the cricket Gregory.[^cite_note-Animation1-11]

Throughout the production, the voice actors were tasked with playing different characters and performing their lines on cue, switching between roles as necessary.[^cite_note-dai9-12] The voice actors were unable to record the lines separately because of the close dialogue timing. When asked if juggling the different voices of Goku, Gohan and Goten were difficult, [Masako Nozawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masako_Nozawa) said that it was not and that she was able to switch roles simply upon seeing the character's picture.[^cite_note-dai9-12] She did admit that when they were producing two films a year and television specials in addition to the regular series, there were times when they had only line art to look at while recording, which made giving finer nuanced details in her performance difficult.[^cite_note-Chozenshu_4-13]

One of the character designers for *Dragon Ball Z* was Tadayoshi Yamamuro. He was responsible for designing and animating Goku's [Super Saiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Saiyan) form in the series. He used the martial artist [Bruce Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruce_Lee) as a reference for Goku's Super Saiyan form, stating that, when he "first becomes a Super Saiyan, his slanting pose with that scowling look in his eyes is all Bruce Lee."[^cite_note-14] In the original manga itself, Goku's piercing eyes in Super Saiyan form were also based on Bruce Lee's paralyzing glare.[^cite_note-15]

### English dub production and broadcasting

From 1993 to 1996, a *Dragon Ball Z* English-language dub was produced in the [Philippines](23440.md) by the Creative Products Corporation and aired on [RPN 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Philippines_Network) with English-language renditions of both the Japanese opening theme "[Cha-La Head-Cha-La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha-La_Head-Cha-La)" and "Detekoi Tobikiri Zenkai Power!" performed by the youth dance and singing group Age of Wonder.[^cite_note-16]

In 1996, [Funimation Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funimation) (now known as [Crunchyroll, LLC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crunchyroll,_LLC)) licensed *Dragon Ball Z* for an English-language release in North America, after cancelling their initial [dub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dubbing_\(filmmaking\)) of *Dragon Ball* half-way through their originally-planned 26-episode first season.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Funimation's 1996 release was not the first broadcast in the United States, as some networks had already aired versions of the series in other languages on a smaller scale. This included [Nippon Golden Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nippon_Golden_Network)'s broadcast of a subtitled Japanese version in Hawaii from 1994 and later an unedited broadcast in 1997 on the [International Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AZN_Television).[^cite_note-19] Funimation worked with [Saban Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saban_Entertainment) to syndicate the series on television, and [Pioneer Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneon_USA) to handle home video distribution.[^cite_note-20] A [Vancouver](32706.md)-based cast recording at the [Ocean Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_Productions) were hired by Funimation to dub the anime (Funimation had previously used a similar Vancouver-based voice cast in their initial *Dragon Ball* dub, recorded at Dick & Roger's Sound Studio).[^cite_note-1996credits-21][^cite_note-1995credits-22] Contract musicians for Saban, [Ron Wasserman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Wasserman)[^cite_note-wasserman-23] and Jeremy Sweet,[^cite_note-1996credits-21] known for their work on the *[Power Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_Rangers)* franchise, composed a new guitar-driven soundtrack. The dub's opening theme (nicknamed "[Rock the Dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_the_Dragon)") was sung by Sweet, and afterwards Wasserman got hired by Saban to do background music for the dub. For contractual reasons, the background music and opening theme was officially credited to Saban founders [Shuki Levy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuki_Levy) and [Haim Saban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haim_Saban) (under the alias Kussa Mahehi), with the actual extent of their involvement in the soundtrack being unclear.[^cite_note-pau-24][^cite_note-25]

Funimation's initial English dub of *Dragon Ball Z* had mandated cuts to [content and length](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Editing_of_anime_in_American_distribution), which reduced the first 67 episodes into 53 (though TV episode 53 actually ends half-way through uncut episode 67).[^cite_note-ann17-26][^cite_note-27] Most of the edits were done to make the anime more tame and kid-friendly, having references to death sidestepped with phrases like "sent to the next dimension".[^cite_note-28] It premiered in the United States on September 13, 1996, in [first-run syndication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_syndication#First-run_syndication), including local [Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_\(TV_network\)), [WB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_WB), and [UPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPN) stations, but halted production in 1998 after two seasons[^cite_note-ann17-26] despite strong ratings.[^cite_note-29] This was due to Saban scaling down its syndication operations, in order to focus on producing original material for the [Fox Kids Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox_Kids_Network) and its newly acquired [Fox Family Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freeform_\(TV_channel\)).[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] Pioneer also ceased its home video release of the series at volume 17 (the end of the dub) and retained the rights to produce an uncut subtitled version,[^cite_note-ann17-26] but did not do so. They did, however, release uncut dubs of the first three *Z* movies on home video.



[Christopher Sabat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Sabat) (left) and [Sean Schemmel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Schemmel) (right) have provided Funimation's English dub voices for Vegeta and Goku, respectively, since 1999.

On August 31, 1998, reruns of this canceled dub began airing on [Cartoon Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon_Network) as part of the channel's weekday afternoon [Toonami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toonami) block. Cartoon Network eventually ordered more episodes of *Dragon Ball Z*, and Funimation resumed production on the series' English dub without Saban's assistance. Cartoon Network replaced the original Vancouver-based cast with an in-house voice cast at their [Texas](29810.md)-based studio, with the goal of the new voices sounding similar to the Vancouver cast.[^cite_note-32] The Saban-produced soundtrack from the first two seasons was replaced with a new background score composed by Bruce Faulconer and his team of musicians, which was used throughout the rest of Funimation's *Dragon Ball Z* dub.[^cite_note-wasserman-23] This renewed dub featured less censorship due to both Saban's absence and fewer restrictions on [cable programming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cable_television), and aired new episodes on Cartoon Network's Toonami block from September 13, 1999, to April 7, 2003 (continuing in re-runs through 2008). [Kids' WB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kids'_WB) briefly ran *Dragon Ball Z* in 2001 on its short-lived Toonami block.[^cite_note-33]

In 2005, Funimation began to re-dub episodes 1–67 with their in-house voice cast, including content originally cut from their dub with Saban.[^cite_note-scan-34] This dub's background score was composed by [Nathan M. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathan_M._Johnson) (Funimation had ceased working with Faulconer Productions after the final episode of *Dragon Ball Z* in 2003). Funimation's new uncut dub of these episodes aired on Cartoon Network beginning in June 2005.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Funimation's later remastered [DVDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD) of the series saw them redub portions of the dialogue, mostly after episode 67, and had the option to play the entire series' dub with both the American and Japanese background music.

In January 2011, Funimation and Toei announced that they would stream *Dragon Ball Z* within 30 minutes before their simulcast of *[One Piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece_\(1999_TV_series\))*.[^cite_note-38]

On April 1, 2012, the [Toonami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toonami) block which had aired *Dragon Ball Z* in the United States aired one episode of the show (episode 191) as part of what was initially a one-night-only revival of the block on [Adult Swim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_Swim).[^cite_note-39]

The Funimation dubbed episodes also aired in Canada, Ireland, the United Kingdom, the Netherlands, Belgium, Australia, and New Zealand. However, beginning with episode 108 (123 uncut), Westwood Media (in association with Ocean Studios) produced an alternate English dub, distributed to Europe by [AB Groupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AB_Groupe). The alternate dub was created for broadcast in the UK, the Netherlands and Ireland on [Cartoon Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon_Network)'s UK (later [CNX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toonami_UK) and Toonami) and [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon_Network_\(Western_Europe\)) channels and later on the [Yorin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorin) network's [Yorkiddin'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yorkiddin') block which rebranded to Toonami, although it also aired in Canada beginning from episode 168 (183 uncut) to fulfill [Canadian content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_content) requirements on [YTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YTV_\(Canada\)). Funimation's in-house dub continued to air in the U.S., Australia, and New Zealand. The Westwood Media production used the same voice cast from the original 53-episode dub produced by Funimation, it featured an alternate soundtrack by [Tom Keenlyside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Keenlyside) and John Mitchell,[^cite_note-40] though most of this score was pieces Ocean reused from other productions Keenlyside and Mitchell had scored for them, and it used the same scripts and video master as the TV edit of Funimation's in-house dub. The Westwood Media dub never received a home video release. In Australia, *Dragon Ball Z* was broadcast by the free-to-air commercial network, [Network 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_10) during morning children's programming, [Cheez TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheez_TV), originally using the censored Funimation/Saban dub before switching to Funimation's in-house dub. In addition to airing on [Network 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_10), *Dragon Ball Z* was also aired in Australia and New Zealand on a localized version of [Cartoon Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon_Network_\(Australia_and_New_Zealand\))'s [Toonami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toonami) block. *Dragon Ball Z* originally aired on the British [Comedy Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comedy_Network) in Fall 1998 before moving to [Cartoon Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon_Network_\(United_Kingdom_and_Ireland\)) on March 6, 2000 and later to [CNX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toonami_\(United_Kingdom_and_Ireland\)) (later Toonami) in 2002.[^cite_note-41]

The series was also available on the Funimation video streaming service (formerly FunimationNOW) upon its launch in 2016. In March 2022, *Dragon Ball Z* was added to [Crunchyroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crunchyroll), a service that Funimation acquired a year before, which in turn was acquired in 2018 by its current parent company, [Sony Pictures Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sony_Pictures_Television).[^cite_note-42]

### Editing

*Dragon Ball Z*'s original North American release was the subject of heavy editing which resulted in a large amount of removed content and alterations that greatly changed the original work. Funimation CEO [Gen Fukunaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen_Fukunaga) is often criticized for his role in the editing; but it was the initial distributor Saban which required such changes or they would not air the work, as was the case with the episode dealing with orphans.[^cite_note-geninterview-43][^cite_note-Note02-44] These changes included altering every aspect of the show from character names, clothing, scenes and dialogue of the show. The character [Mr. Satan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr._Satan) was renamed Hercule and this change has been retained in other English media such as Viz's *Dragon Ball Z* manga and video games, though the English dubs of *Dragon Ball Kai* and *[Dragon Ball Super](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Super)* changed the name back to Mr. Satan.[^cite_note-45] The dialogue changes would sometimes contradict the scenes itself; after the apparent fatal explosion of a helicopter, one of the characters said, "I can see their parachutes; they're okay!"[^cite_note-geninterview-43] Funimation's redub for the 2005 release would address many of the issues raised by Saban, with the uncut releases preserving the integrity of the original Japanese release.

Steven Simmons, who did the subtitling for Funimation's home video releases, offered commentary on the subtitling from a project and technical standpoint, addressing several concerns.[^cite_note-annsub-46][^cite_note-Note03-47] Simmons said that [Gen Fukunaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gen_Fukunaga) did not want any swearing on the discs, but because there was no taboo word list, Simmons would substitute a variation in the strength of the words by situation with the changes starting in episode 21.[^cite_note-subs-48] The typographical errors in the script were caused by dashes (—) and double-quotes (") failing to appear, which resulted in confusing dialogue.[^cite_note-subs-48]

## Music

Main article: [List of *Dragon Ball* soundtracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_soundtracks)

*Dragon Ball Z* has released a series of 21 soundtracks as part of the *[Dragon Ball Z Hit Song Collection series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Z_Hit_Song_Collection_series)*. In total, dozens of releases exist for *Dragon Ball Z* which includes Japanese and foreign adapted releases of the anime themes and video game soundtracks.

[Shunsuke Kikuchi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunsuke_Kikuchi) composed the score for *Dragon Ball Z*. The opening theme for the first 199 episodes is "[Cha-La Head-Cha-La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha-La_Head-Cha-La)" performed by [Hironobu Kageyama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hironobu_Kageyama). The second opening theme used up until the series finale at episode 291 is "[We Gotta Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/We_Gotta_Power)" also performed by Kageyama. Both opening themes were replaced with an original instrumental piece by Mark Menza, the composer of the alternate soundtrack, in the remastered Funimation dub due to licensing issues. The ending theme used for the first 199 episodes is "[Detekoi Tobikiri Zenkai Power!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cha-La_Head-Cha-La)" (でてこいとびきりZENKAIパワー!; "Come Out, Incredible Full Power!") performed by [Manna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manna_\(singer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1). The second ending theme used for the remaining episodes is "Bokutachi wa Tenshi Datta" (僕達は天使だった; "We Were Angels") performed by Kageyama. The initial English-Language release used a completely new musical score composed by Faulconer Productions. It was used for the North American broadcasts of the show from 1999 to 2005.[^cite_note-49]

## Related media

### *Dragon Ball Z Kai*

Main article: [*Dragon Ball Z Kai*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Z_Kai)

See also: [List of *Dragon Ball Z Kai* episodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_Z_Kai_episodes)

In February 2009, [Toei Animation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toei_Animation) announced that it would begin broadcasting a [remastered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remastered) version of *Dragon Ball Z* as part of the series' 20th anniversary.[^cite_note-50] The series premiered on [Fuji Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuji_Television) and its [FNS affiliates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_FNN_and_FNS_affiliates) in Japan on April 5, 2009, under the title *Dragon Ball Kai*. The ending suffix Kai (改（カイ）) in the name means "updated" or "altered", reflecting the improvements and corrections of the original work.[^cite_note-remkai-51] The original *Z* footage was remastered into [HD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_television), featuring new animated opening and ending sequences, a new original soundtrack, and a re-recording of the vocal tracks.[^cite_note-remkai-51][^cite_note-52] The original *Z* footage was used for the series, with any damaged or erroneous [cel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cel) frames being redrawn over. A majority of the [filler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filler_\(media\)) content from *Z* was omitted to more closely align with the manga's story, resulting in a faster-paced anime series.[^cite_note-53] According to Torishima, *Kai* was conceived when [Bandai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandai) asked Toriyama if a completely new *Dragon Ball* anime could be made to increase the franchise's merchandise sales. Toriyama declined to write a new story, so it was decided that a new version of *Dragon Ball Z* that better aligns with the manga would be produced. Toriyama's reaction to the concept of *Kai* was positive, with Torishima noting "so it all worked out".[^cite_note-TorishimaVideo-54]

The series initially concluded with the 97th episode in Japan on March 27, 2011, with the finale of the Cell Games. The series was originally scheduled to run for 98 episodes; however, due to news coverage regarding the [2011 Tōhoku earthquake and tsunami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_T%C5%8Dhoku_earthquake_and_tsunami), the last episode was not aired during the original run and instead released as a direct-to-video exclusive in Japan on August 2, 2011.[^cite_note-55]

In November 2012, [Mayumi Tanaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayumi_Tanaka), the Japanese voice actor of Krillin, announced that she and the rest of the cast were recording more episodes of *Dragon Ball Kai*.[^cite_note-kai_continued-56] In February 2014, the *Kai* adaptation of the Buu Saga was officially confirmed. The continued run of the series, titled *Dragon Ball Z Kai: The Final Chapters* internationally, began airing in Japan on Fuji Television and its FNS affiliates on April 6, 2014, and concluded its run on June 28, 2015.[^cite_note-Buu_Kai-57] The continuation of *Kai* was produced to run for a total of 69 episodes.[^cite_note-58] However, the Japanese broadcast was edited down to 61 episodes to fit into a timeslot scheduled to be taken over by a new anime series for the franchise, titled *[Dragon Ball Super](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Super_\(TV_series\))*, which premiered on July 5, 2015.

### Home media releases

In Japan, *Dragon Ball Z* did not receive a home video release until 2003, seven years after its broadcast. [Pony Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pony_Canyon) announced a remastering of the series in two 26-disc DVD box sets, that were [made-to-order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Made-to-order) only, released on March 19 and September 18 and referred to as "Dragon Box". Since then, Pony Canyon content of these sets began being released on mass-produced individual 6-episode DVDs on November 2, 2005, and finished with the 49th volume released on February 7, 2007.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] In July 2009, [Funimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funimation) announced that they would be releasing the Japanese frame-by-frame "Dragon Box" restoration of *Dragon Ball Z* in North America. These seven limited edition collector's DVD box sets were released uncut and unedited in the show's original 4\:3 fullscreen format between November 10, 2009, and October 11, 2011.[^cite_note-61]

The international home release structure of *Dragon Ball Z* is complicated by the licensing and release of the companies involved in producing and distributing the work. Releases of the media occurred on both VHS and DVD with separate edited and uncut versions being released simultaneously. Both versions of the edited and uncut material are treated as different entries and would frequently make *Billboard* rankings as separate entries. Home release sales were featured prominently on the [Nielsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nielsen_ratings) VideoScan charts.[^cite_note-scan-34] Further complicating the release of the material was Funimation itself; which was known to release "DVDs out of sequence in order to get them out as fast as possible"; as in the case of their third season.[^cite_note-62] [Pioneer Entertainment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneer_Entertainment) distributed the Funimation/Saban edited-only dub of 53 episodes on seventeen VHS between 1997 and 1999,[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] and seventeen DVDs throughout 1999.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] Two box sets separating them into the Saiyan and Namek arcs were also released on VHS in 1999,[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] and on DVD in 2001.[^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] In 1999, Funimation's own distribution of their initial onward dub, which began with episode 54, in edited or uncut VHS ran between 1999 and 2006.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] A DVD version was produced alongside these, although they were only produced uncut and contained the option to watch the original Japanese with subtitles.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

In 2005, Funimation began releasing their onward dub of the beginning of *Dragon Ball Z* on DVD, marking the first time the episodes were seen uncut in North America.[^cite_note-76] However, only nine volumes were released, leaving it incomplete.[^cite_note-77] Instead, Funimation remastered and [cropped](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cropping_\(image\)) the entire series into 16\:9 widescreen format and began re-releasing it to DVD in nine individual "season" box sets; the first set released on February 6, 2007, and the final on May 19, 2009.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] On August 13, 2013, Funimation released all 53 episodes and the three movies from their first *Dragon Ball Z* dub created with Saban and Ocean Studios in a collector's DVD box set, titled the *Rock the Dragon Edition*.[^cite_note-80]

In July 2011, Funimation announced plans to release *Dragon Ball Z* in [Blu-ray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blu-ray_Disc) format, with the first set released on November 8, 2011.[^cite_note-funimation.com-81] However, production of these 4\:3 sets was suspended after the second volume, citing financial and technical concerns over restoring the original film material frame by frame, with Funimation noting that the restoration costs incurred exceeded the retail price they were able to sell them for.[^cite_note-82] Only a year later, the company began producing a cropped 16\:9 remastered Blu-ray release in 2013, with nine sets released in total.[^cite_note-83]

In March 2019, Funimation announced plans to release a 30th anniversary Blu-ray release of *Dragon Ball Z*, with the box set being remastered in 4\:3 aspect ratio, and containing an artbook and a collectible figure.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] It would be crowdfunded, originally requiring a minimum of 2500 pre-orders in order to be manufactured, but was later increased to a minimum of 3,000 units.[^cite_note-86] The release sparked controversy amongst fans due to the framing of the video, color saturation and [digital video noise reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_reduction#In_images).[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] Funimation responded by stating that they cropped the release by going in "scene-by-scene to make judgements based on the image available in each frame of how much to trim to get to a consistent 4\:3 aspect ratio, while still attempting to cut as little out of the picture as possible," and that they felt the digital video noise reduction was "mandatory for this release based on the different levels of fan support from various past *DBZ* releases with different levels of noise reduction over the years."[^cite_note-89]

### Manga

Main articles: [*Dragon Ball* (manga)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_\(manga\)) and [List of *Dragon Ball Z* chapters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_Z_chapters)

While the manga was all titled *Dragon Ball* in Japan, due to the popularity of the *Dragon Ball Z* anime in the west, [Viz Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viz_Media) initially changed the title of the last 26 volumes of the manga to "*Dragon Ball Z*" to avoid confusion. The volumes were originally published in Japan between 1988 and 1995. It began serialization in the American *[Shonen Jump](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shonen_Jump_\(magazine\))*, beginning in the middle of the series with the appearance of Trunks; the *[tankōbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tank%C5%8Dbon)* volumes of both *Dragon Ball Z* and *Dragon Ball* were released simultaneously by Viz Media in the United States.[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91] In March 2001, Viz continued this separation by re-shipping the *Dragon Ball* and *Dragon Ball Z* titles starting with the first volumes of each work.[^cite_note-92] Viz's marketing for the manga made distinct the differences between *Dragon Ball* and *Dragon Ball Z* tone. Viz billed *Dragon Ball Z*: "More action-packed than the stories of Goku's youth, *Dragon Ball Z* is pure adrenaline, with battles of truly Earth-shaking proportions!"[^cite_note-93] Between 2008 and 2010, Viz re-released the two series in a format called "Viz Big Edition," which collects three individual volumes into a single large volume.[^cite_note-94] Viz Media republished the series in fourteen 3-in-1 volumes, including what they previously released as *Dragon Ball Z*, under the *Dragon Ball* name, between June 4, 2013, and September 6, 2016.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

### Films

Further information: [List of *Dragon Ball* films](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_films)

The *Dragon Ball Z* films comprise a total of 15 entries as of 2015. The first 13 films were typically released every March and July during the series' original run by the spring and summer vacations of Japanese schools. They were typically double features paired up with other anime films, and were thus, usually an hour or less in length. These films themselves offer contradictions in both chronology and design that make them incompatible with a single continuity. All 15 films were licensed in North America by Funimation, and all have received in-house dubs by the company. Before Funimation, the third film was a part of the short-lived Saban syndication, being split into three episodes, and the first three films received uncut English dubs in 1998 produced by Funimation with Ocean Studios and released by Pioneer. Several of the films have been broadcast on [Cartoon Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon_Network) and [Nicktoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicktoons_\(American_TV_channel\)) in the United States, [Toonami UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toonami_UK) in the United Kingdom (these featured an alternate English dub produced by an unknown cast by [AB Groupe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AB_Groupe)), and [Cartoon Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartoon_Network_\(Australia\)) in Australia.

### Television specials and OVAs

Three TV specials based on *Dragon Ball Z* were produced and broadcast on Fuji TV. The first two were *[Dragon Ball Z: Bardock – The Father of Goku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Z:_Bardock_%E2%80%93_The_Father_of_Goku)* in 1990 and *[Dragon Ball Z: The History of Trunks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Z:_The_History_of_Trunks)* in 1993, the latter being based on a special chapter of the original manga. Both were licensed by Funimation in North America and AB Groupe in Europe. In 2013, a two-part hour-long [crossover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crossover_\(fiction\)) with *[One Piece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Piece)* and *[Toriko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toriko)*, titled *Dream 9 Toriko & One Piece & Dragon Ball Z Chō Collaboration Special!!*, was created and aired.

Additionally, two [original video animations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Original_video_animation) (OVAs) bearing the *Dragon Ball Z* title have been made. The first is *[Dragon Ball Z Side Story: Plan to Eradicate the Saiyans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Z_Side_Story:_Plan_to_Eradicate_the_Saiyans)*, which was originally released in 1993 in two parts as "Official Visual Guides" for the video game of the same title. *[Dragon Ball: Plan to Eradicate the Super Saiyans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball:_Plan_to_Eradicate_the_Super_Saiyans)* was a 2010 remake of this OVA. None of the OVAs have been dubbed into English, and the only one to see a release in North America is the 2010 remake, which was subtitled and included as a bonus feature in *[Dragon Ball: Raging Blast 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball:_Raging_Blast_2)*.

### Video games

Further information: [List of *Dragon Ball* video games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Dragon_Ball_video_games)

Over 57 video games bear the *Dragon Ball Z* name across a range of platforms from the [Nintendo Entertainment System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nintendo_Entertainment_System) to the current generation consoles, with the most recent release being *[Dragon Ball Z: Kakarot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragon_Ball_Z:_Kakarot)* in 2020.

In North America, licensing rights had been given to both [Namco Bandai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namco_Bandai) and [Atari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atari,_Inc.). In 1999, Atari acquired exclusive rights to the video games through [Funimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funimation), a deal which was extended for five more years in 2005.[^cite_note-97] A 2007 dispute would end with Atari paying Funimation \$3.5 million.[^cite_note-dispute1-98] In July 2009, Namco Bandai was reported to have obtained exclusive rights to release the games for a period of five years.[^cite_note-99] This presumably would have taken effect after Atari's licensing rights expired at the end of January 2010.[^cite_note-dispute1-98]

## Reception

*Dragon Ball Z* was listed as the 78th best animated show in [IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN)'s "Top 100 Animated Series",[^cite_note-ign100-100] and was also listed as the 50th greatest animated show in [*Wizard* magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wizard_\(magazine\))'s "Top 100 Greatest Animated shows" list.[^cite_note-101] The series ranked sixth on *Wizard's Anime* Magazine on their "Top 50 Anime released in North America".[^cite_note-102]

In Asia, the *Dragon Ball Z* franchise, including the anime and merchandise, earned a profit of \$3 billion by 1999.[^cite_note-103] In the United States, the series sold over 14 million videos by 2002,[^cite_note-104] and over 25 million [DVDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DVD) by January 2012.[^cite_note-Funimation-105]

In 2005, [media historian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_historian) [Hal Erickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hal_Erickson_\(author\)) wrote that "*Dragon Ball* may be the closest thing on American television to an animated [soap opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap_opera)—though this particular genre is an old, established and venerated one in Japan, the series' country of origin."[^cite_note-106] Christopher J. Olson and CarrieLynn D. Reinhard note that "Western fans flocked to *Dragon Ball Z* because it offered exciting action not found in movies or television shows (animated or otherwise) at that time."[^cite_note-107] A key characteristic that set *Dragon Ball Z* (and later other anime shows) apart from American television shows at the time was a serialization format, in which a continuous [story arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Story_arc) stretches over multiple episodes or seasons. Traditional American television had an episodic format, with each episode typically consisting of a self-contained story. Serialization has since also become a common characteristic of American [streaming television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streaming_television) shows during the "[Peak TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peak_TV)" era.[^cite_note-108]

[Funimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funimation)'s in-house English dub though has received mixed reviews from some critics over the years. [IGN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IGN) criticized the dub for "having poor quality, along with some over the top, and quite annoying voice acting." [Frieza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frieza)'s female voice in particular left many fans confused over the character's gender.[^cite_note-109] Other criticisms have been towards the English dub's script, and inaccurately translating the source material, such as portraying [Goku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goku)'s character as a more stoic superhero.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111]

### Cultural impact and legacy

Further information: [Cultural impact of *Dragon Ball*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_impact_of_Dragon_Ball)

*Dragon Ball Z*'s popularity is reflected through a variety of data through online interactions which show the popularity of the media. In 2001, it was reported that the official website of *Dragon Ball Z* recorded 4.7 million hits per day and included 500,000+ registered fans.[^cite_note-112] The term "Dragonball Z" ranked fourth in 1999 and second in 2000 by [Lycos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lycos)' web search engine.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] For 2001, "Dragonball" was the most popular search on Lycos and "Dragonball Z" was fifth on [Yahoo!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yahoo!).[^cite_note-115] and "Dragonball" was the third most popular search term in 2002.[^cite_note-116]

[It's Over 9000!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/It's_Over_9000!) became a famous [Internet meme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_meme) and is referenced both within and outside Dragonball related media.

In 2015, [Ford Motor Company](30433662.md) released two commercials featuring characters from the series, the first advertising the [Ford Fusion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Fusion_\(Americas\)) and the second for the [Ford Focus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Focus).[^cite_note-117]

*Dragon Ball* fans set a [Guinness World Record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guinness_World_Record) for Largest Kamehameha attack move at [San Diego Comic-Con](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Comic-Con) on July 17, 2019.[^cite_note-118]

### Ratings

*Dragon Ball Z*'s Japanese run was very popular with an average viewer rating of 20.5% across the series. *Dragon Ball Z* also proved to be a rating success in the United States, outperforming top shows such as *[Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friends)* and *[The X-Files](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_X-Files)* in some parts of the country in [sweeps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweeps) ratings during its first season.[^cite_note-119] The premiere of season three of *Dragon Ball Z* in 1999, done by [Funimation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funimation_Entertainment)'s in-house dub, was the highest-rated program ever at the time on Cartoon Network.[^cite_note-120] In 2001, Cartoon Network obtained licensing to run 96 more episodes and air the original *Dragon Ball* anime and was the top rated show in the Toonami block of Cartoon Network.[^cite_note-121] Beginning March 26, 2001, Cartoon Network ran a 12-week special promotion "Toonami Reactor" which included a focus on *Dragon Ball Z*, which would stream episodes online to high-speed internet users.[^cite_note-122] Many home video releases were met with both the edited and unedited versions placing on in the top 10 video charts of [*Billboard*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billboard_magazine). For example, "The Dark Prince Returns" (containing episodes 226–228) and "Rivals" (containing episodes 229–231) edited and unedited, made the *Billboard* magazine top video list for October 20, 2001.[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-Note04-124] In 2002, in the week ending September 22, *Dragon Ball Z* was the #1 program of the week on all of television with tweens 9-14, boys 9-14 and men 12-24, with the Monday, Tuesday and Wednesday telecasts of *Dragon Ball Z* ranked as the top three programs in all of television, broadcast or cable, for delivery of boys 9-14.[^cite_note-125]

## Merchandise

*Dragon Ball Z* merchandise was a success prior to its peak American interest, with more than \$3 billion in sales from 1996 to 2000.[^cite_note-bk-126] In 1996, *Dragon Ball Z* grossed \$2.95 billion in merchandise sales worldwide.[^cite_note-127] By January 2012, *Dragon Ball Z* grossed \$5 billion in merchandise sales worldwide.[^cite_note-Funimation-105]

In 1998, Animage-ine Entertainment, a division of Simitar, announced the sale of Chroma-Cels, mock animation cels to capitalize on the popularity of *Dragon Ball Z*.[^cite_note-chroma1-128] The original sale was forecasted for late 1998, but were pushed back to January 12, 1999.[^cite_note-chroma2-129]



An acrylic replica of the Five-Star Dragon Ball.

In 2000, MGA Entertainment released more than twenty toys, consisting of [table-top games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabletop_game) and [walkie-talkies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walkie-talkie).[^cite_note-130] [Irwin Toy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irwin_Toy) released more than 72 [figures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Action_figure) consisting of 2-inch and 5 inch action figures, which became top-selling toys in a market dominated by the [Pokémon Trading Card Game](431322.md).[^cite_note-131] Irwin Toys would release other unique *Dragon Ball Z* toys including a battery powered Flying Nimbus Cloud which hovered without touching the ground and a die-cast line of vehicles with collector capsules.[^cite_note-irwinflyingnimbus-132] In June 2000, [Burger King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burger_King) had a toy promotion which would see 20 million figures; Burger King bore the cost of the promotion which provided free marketing for Funimation.[^cite_note-bk-126] The Halloween Association found *Dragon Ball Z* costumes to be the fourth most popular costumes in their nationwide survey.[^cite_note-133]

In December 2002, [Jakks Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jakks_Pacific) signed a three-year deal for licensing *Dragon Ball Z* toys, which was possible because of the bankruptcy of Irwin Toy.[^cite_note-134] Jakks Pacific's *Dragon Ball Z* 5-inch figures were cited as impressive for their painting and articulation.[^cite_note-135]

In 2010, Toei closed deals in Central and South American countries which included Algazarra, Richtex, Pil Andina, DTM, Doobalo and Bondy Fiesta.[^cite_note-latin-136] In 2012, Brazil's Abr-Art Bag Rio Comercio Importacao e Exportacao closed a deal with Toei.[^cite_note-137]
