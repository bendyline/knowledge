**Slavonski Brod** (pronounced [\[slǎʋoːnskiː brôːd\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Serbo-Croatian), lit. '[Slavonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonia) Brod') is a [city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City) in eastern [Croatia](5573.md), near the border with [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md). As one of the principal cities in the historical regions of [Slavonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonia) and [Posavina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posavina), Slavonski Brod is the 7th largest city in the country, with a population of 59,141 as of the 2011 census.[^cite_note-census2011-3] It is the centre of [Brod-Posavina County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brod-Posavina_County) and a major [river port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_port) on the [Sava](587845.md) river.

## Names

In [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language), the term *brod*, derived from [Old Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Slavic) brodъ, is an archaic word for 'ford', a shallow section of a river that can be crossed on foot or horseback.[^cite_note-HJP-4] The other meaning of *brod*, having little to do with the city's name, is 'ship', by [metathesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metathesis_\(linguistics\)) derived from the [Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanic_languages) word *board* – from the phrase "on board".

Among the names historically in use were *Marsonia* in the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire),[^cite_note-5] *Brood (in Slawonien)*[^cite_note-6] in the German speaking Austrian period, *Brod na Savi* after 1934.

## Geography



[Sava](587845.md) river promenade

The city is located 197 kilometres (122 miles) southeast of [Zagreb](55906.md) and at an elevation of 96 metres (315 feet). It developed at the strategically important crossing over the [Sava](587845.md) river toward [Brod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brod,_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) in [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md). The Bosnian city was called Bosanski Brod until 2009.

Slavonski Brod is an important intersection of the [Posavina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posavina) part of the highway and a railway junction, since it is located on the highway linking [Zagreb](55906.md)–[Lipovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipovac,_Croatia)–[Belgrade](55904.md) ([E70](8130337.md), A3) and the [Zagreb](55906.md)–[Vinkovci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinkovci)–[Belgrade](55904.md) railway. The ship port "Brod" on the [Sava](587845.md) river is under construction.

There is also a new modern highway under construction, on the Pan-European [Corridor Vc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corridor_Vc), which will connect Northern Europe and Hungary with Croatia (through the region of Slavonia), and Bosnia and [Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herzegovina) with Croatia (through the coastal region of [Dalmatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalmatia)). The alignment of this highway is approximately at a 19 km (12 mi) distance from the center of the town. With the completion of this route, the area of the town Slavonski Brod will become one of the major highway intersections in this part of Europe connecting directions west–east and north–south.

## History

A favorable geographical location, pleasant continental climate, fertile soil and being in the vicinity of the river Sava have all resulted with the city being inhabited since a prehistoric age.

### Prehistory

The northeastern part of the city, at Galovo, recently revealed a rich deposit of the [Starčevo culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star%C4%8Devo_culture), dating in the Early Stone Age. Besides the fact that this is the largest archaeological finding in northern Croatia, it proves that the locality of Brod has been inhabited for at least 8,000 years.

### Roman period

The first historical settlement in Brod dates from [Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire) times, the settlement being known as *Marsonia*. It is still disputed whether Marsonia was a postal station, lodging for the night or an actual settlement.

The [Peutinger map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peutinger_map) refers to Marsonia as Marsonie, while the work "[Notitia Dignitatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notitia_Dignitatum)" has Marsonia under the name of "Auxilia Ascaria Tauruno sive Marsonia". The issue of Marsonia becoming a city has been tackled by the publishers of [Ptolemy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy)'s [geography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_\(Ptolemy\)), dating from the 16th century. The first geographer who located the position of Marsonia at the locality of Brod was [Abraham Ortelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Ortelius), who, in his atlas [Theatrum orbis terrarum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatrum_orbis_terrarum) ([Antwerp](32149462.md) 1590) published a map, the objective of which was to reconstruct and connect the antic names of [Panonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panonia) and [Illyric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illyria) settlements with the towns of that time. It was he who placed Marsonia at today's location of Brod.

Peutinger's table clearly shows that Marsonia initially existed next to the Sava river, at the place where the Roman road crossed the river. The first Croatian expert, claiming that the Roman MARSVNNIA was located where Brod is now is [Matija Petar Katančić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matija_Petar_Katan%C4%8Di%C4%87), giving this assertion additional support.

### Modern history

The [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) settled in the area of the city in the 6th century. The name of the town is mentioned for the first time in [Bela IV of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A9la_IV)'s charter, dated 1224. The Vukovac fortress was built in the 15th century, subsequently damaged by the Turks. The [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) controlled the settlement from 1536[^cite_note-7] until 1691, when it was ceded to the [Empire of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Austria), as confirmed by the [Treaty of Karlowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Karlowitz) in 1699. Until 1918, Brod (named *Brood* before 1850)[^cite_note-8] remained in the [Austrian monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_monarchy) ([Kingdom of Croatia-Slavonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Croatia-Slavonia) after the [compromise of 1867](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compromise_of_1867)), in the [Slavonian Military Frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonian_Military_Frontier), under the administration of the *Brooder Grenz-Infanterie-Regiment N°VII* until 1881. A huge [Vauban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vauban) type fortress was built east of Vukovac.

Alongside its defence role the city saw steady development of crafts and commerce. The education and culture were strongly dominated by the [Franciscans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscans). The 20th century was a period of strong economic growth of the city, with the 1920s being called "the Golden Age of Brod". The city's current name dates from 1934 when it was changed from Brod na Savi.

From 1941 to 1945 the city was part of the [Independent State of Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_State_of_Croatia). The town was heavily bombed by the Allies in 1944 and 1945. The bombing resulted in damage to 80% of the buildings in Slavonski Brod.[^cite_note-9] The bombing resulted in 897 civilian deaths, 244 military deaths, 208 wounded civilians, and 28 military wounded in Slavonski Brod and [Bosanski Brod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosanski_Brod).[^cite_note-Obradov-10] [Historical Archive of Slavonski Brod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Archives_in_Slavonski_Brod) was established in the city in 1959.[^cite_note-Arhinet-11]

In 1990 democratic reforms legalised the formation of political parties other than the League of Communists of Croatia. In Slavonski Brod, the first parties formed were the [Croatian Democratic Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Democratic_Union) and the [Croatian Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Democratic_Party).[^cite_note-12]

### During the Croatian War of Independence

During the [Croatian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_War_of_Independence), Slavonski Brod came under heavy bombardment from [Bosnian Serb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_Republika_Srpska) forces, positioned across the border in [Bosanska Posavina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosanska_Posavina), between April and October 1992, during [Operation Corridor 92](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Corridor_92). A total of 11,651 artillery shells and fourteen [9K52 Luna-M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9K52_Luna-M) rockets were fired against the city, and 130 bombs were dropped from the air, resulting in the deaths of 116 civilians,[^cite_note-13] including 27 children.[^cite_note-14]

## Economy



Slavonski Brod agricultural fair

The city's [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economics) is based on farming, viticulture, [fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit) growing, metal-processing, timber (furniture wood, [parquetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parquetry)), textiles (ready-made), leather, foodstuffs (brewery), building material ([lime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lime_\(mineral\))), and printing industries.

Slavonski Brod is also home to some of the most important metal companies in Southeastern Europe, the [Djuro Djakovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90uro_%C4%90akovi%C4%87_\(factory\)) consortium, consisting of a number of factories producing very diverse products, mainly for export. The consortium is capable of producing: [locomotives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Locomotive), tramways, wagons, bridges, industrial plants, nuclear reactors, car parts, heavily armed vehicles, [armoured fighting vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armoured_fighting_vehicles), including [main battle tanks](26595097.md) and [mine warfare](2206580.md) vehicles, agricultural machinery, etc.

Service industries are becoming rapidly important, especially tourism. The city has an important cultural heritage, including a [fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress) and a [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) monastery with a rich library.

## Cultural heritage



Franciscan monastery



Faculty of Engineering

[The Brod Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Brod_Fortress) from the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) period, was constructed during the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austro-Hungarian_Empire) to serve as a stronghold against the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), which was situated on the other bank of the [Sava](587845.md) river. It is in the [Vauban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vauban) style, named after one of the best European experts in the building of fortifications in 17th and 18th centuries. It is one of Europe's best preserved fortresses, and also one of the biggest on the former [Austro-Hungarian Military Frontier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_Frontier). In a way, it is the continental obverse of [Diocletian's Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocletian's_Palace) in [Split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split,_Croatia), by its monumentality.

The city's [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) monastery dates from the 18th century, and is also [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) in style, with exceptional architecture, especially of the church yard, and monastery church interior, with its beautiful altar and paintings.[^cite_note-17] In 1720, a faculty of philosophy was opened here.

The most important annual cultural event in the town is the children's festival 'In the World of the Fairy Tales of Writer [Ivana Brlić-Mažuranić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivana_Brli%C4%87-Ma%C5%BEurani%C4%87)', in April and May. The [Brodsko kolo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brodsko_kolo), an annual show of original [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore), is held in mid-June, while the Festival of Patriotic Folk Songs takes place in May. The artistic 'Sava' colony[^cite_note-18] cherishes the tradition of [watercolour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Watercolour) painting.



Croatian Home in Slavonski Brod

One of the attractions of Slavonski Brod is a central town square, one of the two or three biggest in all of Croatia, [Ivana Brlić-Mažuranić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivana_Brli%C4%87-Ma%C5%BEurani%C4%87) square, named after a popular children's writer, whose house is on the square. This square is also a home to numerous cultural events and has a perfect view of the beautiful Sava river. The square is also dotted with galleries, book stores, cafes, night clubs, and shops, making it a center for entertainment. Close to the square, another attraction is the romantic promenade next to the Sava river - 'Kej' as it is called by citizens of town.

[Slavonski Brod Synagogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonski_Brod_Synagogue), destroyed during [World War II](32927.md), was among the largest and most prestigious synagogues in Croatia. Local [Serbian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_Orthodox_Church) built in 1793 was also destroyed by [Ustaše](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usta%C5%A1e) regime during the war.[^cite_note-Škiljan2025-19] A new church, constructed at a different location and completed in 1990, was again destroyed in 1991.[^cite_note-Škiljan2025-19] Reconstruction of that second church began in 2016.[^cite_note-Škiljan2025-19]

Slavonski Brod is home to monuments to bishop [Josip Stadler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Stadler) and Croatia's first president [Franjo Tuđman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franjo_Tu%C4%91man).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Slavonski Brod and its rail station are featured in [Agatha Christie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agatha_Christie)'s *[Murder on the Orient Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_on_the_Orient_Express)* as the place near which the [Orient Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient_Express) train breaks down.

## Education

Slavonski Brod has very well developed educational institutions in pre-school, primary, secondary and higher education.

The city also hosts the [University of Slavonski Brod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Slavonski_Brod), and the Primary School Teacher Training College. Some other faculties are expected to be opened soon. In October 2006. an associate-degree college has been founded with several other faculties.

## Transportation



Slavonski Brod Train Station

Slavonski Brod has a [train station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavonski_Brod_railway_station) on [M104 railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M104_railway_\(Croatia\)) corridor - it serves as a stop for all trains on international routes which passes through the town (such as Vinkovci - [Villach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villach) or Zagreb - [Belgrade](55904.md)), also being directly connected by a large number of domestic local and limited-stop services running between the capital city of [Zagreb](55906.md) and [Vinkovci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinkovci)/([Tovarnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tovarnik), state border with [Serbia](29265.md)).[^cite_note-25]

By regular bus lines from various companies, Slavonski Brod bus station is well connected with the surrounding villages, municipalities and cities in Slavonia, such as: [Đakovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%90akovo), [Požega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po%C5%BEega,_Croatia) and [Nova Gradiška](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Gradi%C5%A1ka), as well as with all major cities throughout Croatia: [Pula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pula), [Rijeka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijeka), [Umag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umag), [Poreč](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pore%C4%8D), [Rovinj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rovinj), [Osijek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osijek), Zagreb, [Karlovac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karlovac), [Zadar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zadar), [Split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Split,_Croatia), [Šibenik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0ibenik), etc. From the international lines there are regular departures in the direction of [Germany](11867.md) and [Switzerland](26748.md).[^cite_note-26]

## Surroundings

In the immediate surroundings of the town there are interesting hunting grounds ([Migalovci](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migalovci)), fishponds ([Jelas Polje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelas_Polje?action=edit\&redlink=1)), and a lake ([Petnja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petnja)). There are pleasant orchards and [vineyards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vineyards), [Dilj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dilj) mountain (or 'Dilj Gora'), and swimming opportunities.

## Climate

Slavonski Brod has a warm-summer [humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Dfb*).

Since records began in 1963, the highest temperature recorded at the local weather station at an elevation of 88 metres (289 ft) was 40.5 °C (104.9 °F), on 6 August 2012.[^cite_note-27] The coldest temperature was −27.8 °C (−18.0 °F), on 24 January 1963.[^cite_note-28]

## Environment

The [European Environment Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Environment_Agency) reported in 2023 that Slavonski Brod has the poorest outdoor [air quality](10934212.md) of 372 European cities, based on average concentration levels of [fine particulate matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_particulate_matter), (or [PM2.5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PM2.5)) over the past two calendar years. The two-year average concentration of PM2.5 was reported to be 26.5μg/m<sup>3</sup>, which is considered as "very poor" in the categories laid out in [Directive 2008/50/EC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directive_2008/50/EC).[^cite_note-31]
