The **Department of International Relations and Cooperation** (**DIRCO**) is the [foreign ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs) of the [South African government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_South_Africa). It is responsible for [South Africa](17416221.md)'s relationships with foreign countries and international organizations, and runs [South Africa](17416221.md)'s [diplomatic missions](8970.md). The department is headed by the [Minister of International Relations and Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_International_Relations_and_Cooperation), currently [Ronald Lamola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Lamola).

## Background

Prior to 1927, the British government attended to the external affairs of South Africa, though the latter did have its own trade commissioners that were sent to various countries.[^cite_note-Year1-3] In November 1926, at the [Imperial Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1926_Imperial_Conference), the various prime ministers of the [Dominions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominions) attended, with conference negotiating what became the [Balfour Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balfour_Declaration_of_1926) and an autonomy for the domains within a [Commonwealth of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations).[^cite_note-Year1-3] The South Africans could now communicate with the commonwealth countries and others directly without communicating through the countries governors-general.[^cite_note-Year1-3] A Department of External Affairs was created inside the prime minister's office on 1 June 1927.[^cite_note-Year1-3] In 1931, the Statute of Westminster gave South Africa full sovereignty in regards to external affairs, and confirmed by the [Status of the Union Act, 1934](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Status_of_the_Union_Act,_1934).[^cite_note-Year1-3]

In 1955, a separate department was created, the Department of External Affairs with its own minister.[^cite_note-Year-4] Prior to that date, foreign affairs were conducted through the office of the prime minister of South Africa.[^cite_note-Year-4] Later known as the Department of Foreign Affairs, it was renamed the Department of International Relations and Cooperation by President [Jacob Zuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Zuma) in May 2009. In the 2010 national budget, it received an [appropriation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appropriation_\(law\)) of 4,824.4 million [rand](298686.md), and had 4,533 employees.[^cite_note-5]

According to [OECD](33853117.md) estimates, 2019 [official development assistance](1316305.md) from [South Africa](17416221.md) decreased to US\$106 million.[^cite_note-6] In 2022, when [Cuba](5042481.md) asked for humanitarian medical and food aid, [AfriForum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AfriForum) managed to obtain an interdict against a pending R50 million payout by the department, labelling it "squandering of taxpayers' money". The chairperson of the [Portfolio Committee on International Relations and Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portfolio_Committee_on_International_Relations_and_Cooperation), [Supra Mahumapelo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supra_Mahumapelo), decried the ruling due to its perceived impact on South Africa's solidarity work with countries like [Cuba](5042481.md).[^cite_note-pat1-7]

## Apostille and document legalisation services

As the designated competent authority under the 1961 [Hague Apostille Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostille_Convention), to which South Africa acceded in 1995, DIRCO issues apostille certificates for documents originating from South African government departments destined for use abroad in Convention member states.[^cite_note-8] Eligible documents include birth, marriage and death certificates issued by the [Department of Home Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Home_Affairs_\(South_Africa\)), South African Police Service clearance certificates, and academic qualifications evaluated by the [South African Qualifications Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_African_Qualifications_Authority).[^cite_note-9]

Documents that have been notarised by a [notary public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notary_public) follow a parallel route via the Registrars of the [High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Court_of_South_Africa) rather than DIRCO.[^cite_note-10]

For documents destined for countries not party to the Apostille Convention — such as the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), [Qatar](8238258.md) and [Kuwait](7515890.md) — DIRCO performs an authentication step within a longer embassy legalisation chain rather than issuing an apostille.[^cite_note-11]

In February 2026, DIRCO launched a Registered Agent Programme to formally list private service providers authorised to submit documents on behalf of clients.
