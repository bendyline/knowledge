**Haryana**[^cite_note-11] is a [state](375986.md) located in the northwestern part of India. It is bordered by [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India) and [Himachal Pradesh](14190.md) to the north, by [Rajasthan](26291.md) to the west and south, by [Delhi](37756.md) to the southeast, while river [Yamuna](354286.md) forms its eastern border with [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md). The state capital is [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandigarh), which it shares with the neighbouring state of Punjab. The city of [Gurgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurgaon) is among India's largest financial and technology hubs.[^cite_note-12] The most populous city is [Faridabad](1180794.md), a part of the [National Capital Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_\(India\)). In terms of area, it ranks 21st in India, with around 1.5% (44,212 km<sup>2</sup> or 17,070 sq mi) of India's land area.[^cite_note-profile-1][^cite_note-harec1-13]. Haryana has 6 [administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisions_of_Haryana), 22 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_of_Haryana), 72 sub-divisions, 93 [revenue tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil), 50 sub-tehsils, 140 [blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_development_block_in_India), 154 [cities and towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Haryana_by_population), 7,356 villages, and 6,222 [villages panchayats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram_panchayat).[^cite_note-harec1-13][^cite_note-FOOTNOTENIDM4-14]

The modern state of Haryana was formed on 1 November 1966 after the [Punjab Reorganisation Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Reorganisation_Act,_1966) was passed by the [Indian Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Parliament). Haryana contains 32 [special economic zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zone) (SEZs), mainly located within the industrial corridor projects connecting the National Capital Region.[^cite_note-harec1-13][^cite_note-harec2-15] [Gurgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurgaon) is considered one of the major [information technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technology) and [automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automobile) hubs of India.[^cite_note-IT3-16][^cite_note-percapita3-17] Haryana [ranks 11th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_territories_by_Human_Development_Index) among Indian states in [human development index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_development_index).[^cite_note-snhdi-gdl-18] The [economy of Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Haryana) is the [13th largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP) in India, with a gross state domestic product (GSDP) of [₹](490783.md)7.65 trillion (US\$79 billion) and has the country's [5th-highest GSDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP_per_capita) of ₹240,000 (US\$2,500).[^cite_note-Survey1-19]

The state is also rich in [ancient heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_State_Protected_Monuments_in_Haryana), [monuments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Monuments_of_National_Importance_in_Haryana), [flora and fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_protected_areas_of_Haryana) and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Haryana), with a well-developed [economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Haryana), [national and state highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highways_in_Haryana). Haryana surrounds the country's capital territory of [Delhi](37756.md) on three sides (north, west and south), consequently, a large area of Haryana state is included in the economically important National Capital Region of India for the purposes of planning and development.

## Etymology

Some anthropologists believe that the name "Haryana" originated in the period following the *[Mahābhārata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C4%81bh%C4%81rata)*, when the Ābhiras are thought to have settled in the region. This community was known for developing advanced agricultural techniques.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

Historian Pran Nath Chopra suggests that the name evolved linguistically over time—from Ābhirāyana (derived from ābhira meaning "Abhira" and ayana meaning "path" or "way") to Ahirāyana, and eventually became Hariyānā or Haryana.[^cite_note-22]

Manuscript illustration of the [Battle of Kurukshetra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kurukshetra), which is a war described in the [Indian epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_epic) poem *[Mahābhārata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mah%C4%81bh%C4%81rata)*. The conflict arose from a dynastic succession struggle between two groups of cousins, the [Kauravas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaurava) and [Pandavas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandava), for the throne of [Hastinapura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastinapura) in an Indian kingdom called [Kuru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuru_Kingdom).

The villages of [Rakhigarhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakhigarhi) in [Hisar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisar_district) and [Bhirrana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhirrana) in [Fatehabad district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatehabad_district) are home to ancient sites of the [Indus Valley Civilisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation), which contain evidence of paved roads, a drainage system, a large-scale rainwater collection storage system, terracotta brick and statue production, and skilled metalworking (in both bronze and precious metals).[^cite_note-IVC-Rakhigarhi-23]

During the [Vedic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedic_era), Haryana was the site of the [Kuru kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuru_kingdom), one of India's great [Mahajanapadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahajanapadas). The south of Haryana is the claimed location of [Manu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manusm%E1%B9%9Bti)'s state of [Brahmavarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmavarta).[^cite_note-24] The area surrounding [Dhosi Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhosi_Hill), and districts of [Rewari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewari_district) and [Mahendragarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahendragarh_district) had Ashrams of several Rishis who made valuable contributions to important Hindu scriptures like [Vedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vedas), [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishads), [Manusmriti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manusmriti), [Brahmanas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmana) and [Puranas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puranas).[^cite_note-25] As per Manusmriti,[^cite_note-26] Manu was the king of [Brahmavarta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmavarta), the flood time state 10,000 years ago surrounded by oldest route of Sarasvati and Drishadwati rivers on the banks of which Sanatan-Vedic or present-day Hindu ethos evolved and scriptures were composed.

### Medieval period

Ancient bronze and stone idols of Jain [Tirthankara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirthankara) were found in archaeological expeditions in [Badli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badli), [Bhiwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhiwani) ([Ranila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranila), [Charkhi Dadri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charkhi_Dadri) and [Badhra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badhra)), [Dadri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadri), [Gurgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurgaon) ([Gurugram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurugram)), [Hansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hansi), [Hisar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisar_\(city\)), Kasan, Nahad, [Narnaul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narnaul), [Pehowa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pehowa), [Rewari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewari), Rohad, [Rohtak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohtak) ([Asthal Bohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthal_Bohar)) and [Sonepat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonepat) in Haryana.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAtul_Kumar_SinhaAbhay_Kumar_Singh2007401-27]



*Harsha Ka Tila* mound west of [Sheikh Chilli's Tomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheikh_Chilli's_Tomb) complex, with ruins from the reign of 7th-century ruler [Harsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harsha)

[Pushyabhuti dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pushyabhuti_dynasty) ruled parts of northern India in the 7th century with its capital at [Thanesar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thanesar). [Harsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harsha) was a prominent king of the dynasty. [Tomara dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomara_dynasty) ruled the south Haryana region in the 10th century. [Anangpal Tomar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anangpal_Tomar) was a prominent king among the Tomaras.[^cite_note-28]

After the sack of [Bhatner fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhatner_fort) during the [Timurid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty) conquests of India in 1398, [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) attacked and sacked the cities of [Sirsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirsa), [Fatehabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatehabad,_Haryana), [Sunam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunam), [Kaithal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaithal) and [Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panipat).[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]



Portrait of [Hem Chandra Vikramaditya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemu), who fought and won across North India from the [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_region) to [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal), winning 22 straight battles

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESarkar196066-31]

[Hem Chandra Vikramaditya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hem_Chandra_Vikramaditya), also called [Hemu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemu), claimed royal status and the throne of Delhi after defeating Akbar's Mughal forces on 7 October 1556 in the [Battle of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Delhi_\(1556\)), and assumed the ancient title of [Vikramaditya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemachandra_Vikramaditya). The area that is now Haryana has been ruled by some of the major empires of India. [Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panipat) is known for three seminal battles in the history of India. In the [First Battle of Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Panipat) (1526), [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur) defeated the [Lodis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodi_Empire). In the [Second Battle of Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Panipat) (1556), [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar) defeated the local Haryanvi Hindu Emperor of Delhi, who belonged to [Rewari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewari). [Hem Chandra Vikramaditya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hem_Chandra_Vikramaditya) had earlier won 22 battles across India from 1553 to 1556 from [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab) to [Bengal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengal), defeating the Mughals and Afghans. Hemu had defeated Akbar's forces twice at Agra and the [Battle of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tughlaqabad) in 1556 to become the last Hindu Emperor of India with a formal coronation at [Purana Quila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purana_Quila) in Delhi on 7 October 1556. In the [Third Battle of Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Panipat) (1761), the Afghan king [Ahmad Shah Abdali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Abdali) defeated the [Marathas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Confederacy).

The growth of trade centers like Bhiwani was supported by influential merchants such as Seth Nand Ram Ji Katla Wale, who promoted social development and commerce during the late 18th and early 19th centuries. He was one of the prominent leaders who devoted his life to establishing Bhiwani as a trade centre in the 18th century. [^cite_note-32]

### British period

The state was part of the British [Punjab province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Province_\(British_India\)). The [Delhi division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Province_\(British_India\)#Administrative_divisions) of Punjab province formed the bulk of Haryana. Among the princely states that were located in the state were [Jind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jind_State), [Kalsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalsia_State), [Loharu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loharu_State), [Dujana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dujana) and [Pataudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pataudi_State), as well as parts of the [Patiala State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patiala_State).



Map of the [Punjab Province of British India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Province_\(British_India\)); Haryana formed the southeastern areas of the province

### Partition and aftermath

During the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India), the Punjab province was one of two British Indian provinces, alongside Bengal, to be partitioned between India and Pakistan. Haryana, along with other Hindu and Sikh-dominated areas of Punjab province, became part of India as [East Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Punjab) state. As a result, a significant number of Muslims left for the newly formed country of [Pakistan](23235.md). Similarly, a huge number of Hindu and Sikh refugees poured into the state from [West Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Punjab). [Gopi Chand Bhargava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gopi_Chand_Bhargava), who hailed from Sirsa in present-day Haryana, became the first Chief Minister of East Punjab.

### Formation of Haryana

Main article: [Punjab Reorganisation Act, 1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Reorganisation_Act,_1966)

Haryana as a state came into existence on 1 November 1966 the Punjab Reorganisation Act (1966). The Indian government set up the Shah Commission under the chairmanship of Justice JC Shah on 23 April 1966 to divide the existing state of [Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab,_India) and determine the boundaries of the new state of Haryana after consideration of the languages spoken by the people. It encompassed the predominantly Hindi-speaking southern part of former Punjab, while the state of Punjab was reduced to the area where Punjabi speakers formed the majority population. The commission delivered its report on 31 May 1966 whereby the then-districts of [Hisar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hisar_\(district\)), [Mahendragarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahendragarh), [Gurgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurgaon), [Rohtak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohtak) and [Karnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnal) were to be a part of the new state of Haryana. Further, the [tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) of [Jind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jind) and [Narwana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narwana) in the [Sangrur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangrur) district – along with [Naraingarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naraingarh), [Ambala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambala) and [Jagadhri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagadhri) – were to be included.[^cite_note-33]

The commission recommended that the tehsil of [Kharar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharar,_SAS_Nagar), which includes [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandigarh), the state capital of Punjab, should be a part of Haryana. However, [Kharar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharar,_SAS_Nagar) was given to Punjab.[^cite_note-34] The city of Chandigarh was made a [union territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory), serving as the capital of both Punjab and Haryana.[^cite_note-35]

[Bhagwat Dayal Sharma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B._D._Sharma) became the first [Chief Minister of Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chief_Ministers_of_Haryana).[^cite_note-36] [Chaudhary Devi Lal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devi_Lal) is credited to be the individual who pushed for the creation of this commission. He was an instrumental figure in the separation of the Haryana state from Punjab in 1966.[^cite_note-37]

### Punjabi migration to Haryana in 1947 and 1980s

The Punjabi migration to Haryana occurred in two major waves—first during the Partition of India in 1947, and later during the 1980s, influenced by the Khalistani movement in Punjab.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

The Partition of India led to one of the largest mass migrations in history, as millions of people were displaced due to the creation of Pakistan. Many Punjabi Hindus and Sikhs fled West Punjab (now in Pakistan) and settled in Haryana, bringing with them their agricultural skills, business expertise, and cultural traditions. Their arrival helped in the economic development of Haryana, particularly in agriculture and trade, as they introduced advanced farming techniques and expanded local businesses.

The Khalistani movement, which emerged in Punjab during the 1980s, led to political instability and violent insurgency. The demand for a separate Sikh state (Khalistan) resulted in clashes between separatist groups and security forces, making the region unsafe for many civilians. This turmoil prompted Punjabi families, including both Hindus and Sikhs, to migrate to neighbouring Haryana, seeking security, stability, and economic opportunities.

The urban centers of Haryana witnessed an influx of Punjabi migrants, who contributed to the state's business and industrial sectors.

By the mid-1990s, the insurgency declined due to government interventions, weakening separatist factions, and changing political dynamics.

These migrations have shaped Haryana's demographic, cultural, and economic landscape, making Punjabis an integral part of the state's growth and identity.

## Geography



A [mustard field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_plant) in Haryana, near the state capital of [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandigarh)

Haryana is a landlocked state in northern India. It is between 27°39' to 30°35' N latitude and between 74°28' and 77°36' E longitude.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENIDM2-40] The total geographical area of the state is 4.42 m ha, which is 1.4% of the geographical area of the country.[^cite_note-agri-41] The altitude of Haryana varies between 700 and 3600 ft (200 metres to 1200 metres) above sea level.[^cite_note-42] Haryana has only 4% (compared with national 21.85%) area under forests.[^cite_note-harec1-13] [Karoh Peak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karoh_Peak), a 1,467-metre (4,813 ft) tall mountain peak in the Sivalik Hills range of the greater Himalayas range located near [Morni Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morni_Hills) area of Panchkula district, is the highest point in Haryana.[^cite_note-hillsofmorni-43][^cite_note-quickgs-44][^cite_note-peakbagger-45][^cite_note-uchicago-46] Most of the state sits atop the fertile [Ghaggar Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaggar_River), a subsection of the [Indo-Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain). Haryana has 4 states and 2 union territories on its border – Punjab, Rajasthan, Uttar Pradesh, Himachal Pradesh, Delhi, and Chandigarh.

### Plains and mountains

Haryana has four main geographical features.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTENIDM3-47]

- The [Yamuna](354286.md)-[Ghaggar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaggar) plain forming the largest part of the state is also called *Delhi [doab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doab)* consists of *Sutlej-Ghaggar doab* (between Sutlej in the north of Punjab and the Ghaggar river flowing through northern Haryana), *Ghaggar-Hakra doab* (between Ghaggar river and [Hakra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drishadvati_river) or Drishadvati river which is the paleochannel of the holy [Saraswati River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saraswati_River)) and *Hakra-Yamuna doab* (between Hakra river and Yamuna).

- The [Lower Shivalik Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivalik_Hills) to the northeast in foothills of [Himalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himalaya)

- The [Bagar region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagar_region) semi-desert dry sandy plain in north west of Haryana, covering northwest districts of Sirsa, western Fatehabad and northwestern Hisar.

- The [Aravali Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aravalli_Range)'s northernmost low rise isolated non-continuous outcrops in the [south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Haryana), covering [Nuh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuh_district), [Gurgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurgaon_district), [Faridabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faridabad_district), [Rewari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewari_district), and [Mahendragarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahendragarh_district).

### Climate

Haryana is hot in summer with maximum temperatures hovering around 40 °C (104 °F) and cool in winter with minimum temperatures around 6 °C (43 °F). The hottest months are May and June and the coldest are December and January.[^cite_note-autogenerated3-64] The climate is semi-arid to subhumid with an average rainfall of 592.93 mm.[^cite_note-100] Around 29% of rainfall is received during the months from July to September as a result of the [monsoon](57630.md), and the remaining rainfall is received during the period from December to February as a result of the [western disturbance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Disturbance).[^cite_note-agri-41]

#### Forests

Forest cover in the state in 2013 was 3.59% (1586 km<sup>2</sup>) and the tree cover in the state was 2.90% (1282 km<sup>2</sup>), giving a total forest and tree cover of 6.49%.[^cite_note-104] In 2016 and 2017, 18,412 hectares were brought under tree cover by planting 14.1 million seedlings.[^cite_note-harec1-13] Thorny, dry, deciduous forest and thorny shrubs can be found all over the state. During the [monsoon](57630.md), a carpet of grass covers the hills. [Shisham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalbergia_sissoo), [khair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senegalia_catechu), [babul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vachellia_nilotica), [dhok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminalia_pendula), and [neem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azadirachta_indica) are the dominant trees of the [subtropical dry deciduous forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_and_subtropical_dry_broadleaf_forests) typical of northern India. The species of [fauna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fauna) found in the state of Haryana include [black buck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_buck), [nilgai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nilgai), [panther](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leopard), [fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox), [mongoose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongoose), [jackal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackal) and wild dog. More than 450 species of birds are found here.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107]

### Environmental and ecological issues

[Haryana Environment Protection Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryana_Environment_Protection_Council) is the advisory committee and the Department of Environment, Haryana is the department responsible for the administration of the environment. The official language of Haryana is [Modern Standard Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Standard_Hindi)[^cite_note-2011lang-129] spoken by 48%, and the second official language is [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language),[^cite_note-punjabiofficial-4] spoken by 7.36%. Several regional languages or dialects, often subsumed under the name "[Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi_languages)", are spoken in the state. Predominant among them is [Haryanvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryanvi_language) (or Bangru) spoken by 37.2%, of the population. It is mainly spoken in the central and eastern parts of Haryana. Haryana also has many [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu) and Punjabi speakers. Punjabi was make the second official language for government use in 2010.[^cite_note-2011lang-129][^cite_note-punjabiofficial-4]

Telugu was made the state's "second language" for schools after Haryana was formed, but was not used for official communication. It was lagee dropped due to low student enrollment.[^cite_note-130] In 1969, Chief Minister [Bansi Lal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bansi_Lal) declared Tamil the second language to distinguish Haryana from Punjab, despite having no Tamil speakers.[^cite_note-DNA-131] Tamil lost this status in 2010 because of the same reason.[^cite_note-DNA-131]

Hindi and Punjabi is spoken in the northeast, [Bagri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagri_language) in the west, Deshwali in the East. [Gujari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujari) is mainly spoken in the [Faridabad division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faridabad_division). [Ahirwati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahirwati), [Mewati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mewati_language) and [Braj Bhasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braj_Bhasha) in the south.[^cite_note-Sharada1979-7]

#### Other languages

There are also some speakers of several major regional languages of neighbouring states or other parts of the subcontinent, like [Bengali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bengali_language), [Bhojpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhojpuri_language), [Marwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwari_language), [Mewari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mewari_language), and [Nepali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepali_language),[^cite_note-132] as well as smaller communities of speakers of languages that are dispersed across larger regions, like [Bauria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauria_language), [Bazigar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazigar_language), [Gade Lohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gade_Lohar_language), [Oadki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oadki_language), and [Sansi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sansi_language).[^cite_note-Ethnologue2019-133]

Haryana consists of six divisions comprising 22 districts. Each district is headed by a Deputy Commissioner (DC), an [IAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) officer who also functions as the District Magistrate (DM) for maintaining law and order and the [District Collector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_magistrate) for land revenue administration.

The districts are further divided into [sub-divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivision_in_India) and [tehsils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsil) for land revenue administration, and into [blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_development_block) for development administration. The subdivisions and tehsils are headed by [Sub-Divisional Magistrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-divisional_magistrate) (SDMs) and [Tehsildars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tehsildar), respectively.

### Law and order

The [Haryana Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryana_Police) force is the law enforcement agency of Haryana. [Five Police Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisions_of_Haryana#Police_ranges) are Ambala, Hissar, Karnal, Rewari and Rohtak.[^cite_note-range1-157] [Four Police Commissionerates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divisions_of_Haryana#Police_commissionerates) are Faridabad, Gurgaon, Panchkula and Sonipat.[^cite_note-harD1-151] Cybercrime investigation cell is based in Gurgaon's Sector 51.[^cite_note-158]

The highest judicial authority in the state is the [Punjab and Haryana High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_and_Haryana_High_Court), with the next higher right of appeal being to the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md). Haryana uses an e-filing facility.[^cite_note-159]

## Economy



A shopping mall in Gurgaon

Main articles: [Economy of Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Haryana), [SEZs in Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_special_economic_zones_in_India#Haryana), [HSIDC estates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryana_State_Industrial_and_Infrastructure_Development_Corporation), [Companies in Gurugram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_companies_based_in_Gurgaon), and [Tourism in Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Haryana)

Haryana's [14th placed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_states_and_union_territories_by_GDP) 12.96% 2012–17 [CAGR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_annual_growth_rate)[^cite_note-harec2-15] estimated a 2017–18 GSDP of US\$95 billion[^cite_note-harec1-13] split into 52% [services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_economy), 30% [industries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_sector) and 18% [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture_in_India).[^cite_note-harec1-13]

The services sector is split across 45% in real estate and financial and professional services, 26% trade and [hospitality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Haryana), 15% state and central government employees, and 14% transport and logistics & warehousing.[^cite_note-harec1-13]

The industrial sector is split across 69% manufacturing, 28% construction, 2% utilities and 1% mining.[^cite_note-harec1-13] In industrial manufacturing, Haryana produces 67% of passenger cars, 60% of motorcycles, 50% of tractors and 50% of the refrigerators in India.[^cite_note-harec1-13]

The service and industrial sectors are boosted by 7 operational [SEZs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zone) and an additional 23 formally approved SEZs (20 already notified and 3 approved in-principle) that are mostly spread along the [Delhi–Mumbai Industrial Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi%E2%80%93Mumbai_Industrial_Corridor_Project?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Amritsar Delhi Kolkata Industrial Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar_Delhi_Kolkata_Industrial_Corridor) and [Western Peripheral Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Western_Peripheral_Expressway).[^cite_note-harec1-13]

The agricultural sector is split across 93% crops and livestock, 4% commercial forestry and logging, and 2% fisheries.[^cite_note-harec1-13] Although Haryana has less than 1.4% of the total area of India, it contributes 15% of food grains to the [central food security public distribution system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_distribution_system),[^cite_note-harec1-13] and makes up 7% of total national agricultural exports, including 60% of total national [basmati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basmati) rice exports.[^cite_note-harec1-13]

### Agriculture

#### Crops



Green farms in Haryana

Haryana is traditionally an [agrarian society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrarian_society) of [zamindars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamindar) (owner-cultivator farmers). About 70% of Haryana's residents are engaged in agriculture.[^cite_note-rep1-164] The [Green Revolution in Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Revolution_in_India) of the 1960s[^cite_note-about_IARI-165] combined with the completion of [Bhakra Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhakra_Dam) in 1963[^cite_note-bdam1-166] and [Western Yamuna Command Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Yamuna_Canal#Western_Yamuna_Command_Network) canal system in 1970s resulted in the significantly increased food grain production.[^cite_note-about_IARI-165] This movement lead to large-scale investments towards improving irrigation systems, enhancing the quality of fertilizers, and investing in superior seeds.[^cite_note-167] As a result, Haryana is self sufficient in food production and the second largest contributor to India's central pool of food grains.[^cite_note-rep1-164]

In 2015–2016, Haryana produced the following principal crops: 13,352,000 [tonnes](31185.md) of [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), 4,145,000 tonnes of [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basmati), 7,169,000 tonnes of [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), 993,000 tonnes of [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton) and 855,000 tonnes of [oilseeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oilseeds) ([mustard seed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_seed), [sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower), etc.). In the northeastern area, dairy cattle, buffaloes, and bullocks play a prominent role, being utilised both for agricultural ploughing and as draft animals.[^cite_note-168]

## Culture

### Music

Main article: [Music of Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Haryana)

Haryana has its own unique traditional [folk music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Haryana), [folk dances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Indian_folk_dances#Haryana), [saang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saang) (folk theatre),[^cite_note-harcu2-200] [cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryanvi_cinema),[^cite_note-harcu1-201] belief system such as [Jathera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_folk_religion#Jathera) (ancestral worship),[^cite_note-harcu3-202][^cite_note-harcu4-203] and arts such as [Phulkari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery_of_India#Phulkari_\(Punjab_and_Haryana\)) and [Shisha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery_of_India#Shisha_or_Mirrorwork_\(Gujarat,_Haryana,_Rajasthan\)) embroidery.[^cite_note-harcu4-203]

#### Folk dances

Folk music and dances of Haryana are based on satisfying the cultural needs of primarily [agrarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agrarian_society) and [martial natures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_race) of Haryanavi tribes.[^cite_note-harmu3-204]

Haryanvi musical folk theatre's main types are [Saang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saang), [Raslila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raslila) and [Ragini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raga#R%C4%81ga-R%C4%81gini_system). The Saang and Ragini form of theatre was popularised by [Lakhmi Chand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakhmi_Chand).[^cite_note-harmu3-204]

Haryanvi folk dances and music have fast energetic movements. Three popular categories of dance are festive-seasonal, devotional, and ceremonial-recreational. The festive-seasonal dances and songs are [Gogaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gogaji)/[Gugga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gugga), [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi), [Phaag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalgun), [Sawan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shravana), [Teej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teej). The devotional dances and songs are [Chaupaiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaupai_\(poetry\)), [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi), [Manjira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taal_\(instrument\)), [Ras Leela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasa_lila), [Raginis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raga#R%C4%81ga-R%C4%81gini_system)). The ceremonial-recreational dances and songs are of following types: legendary bravery ([Kissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Qisse) and [Ragini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raga#R%C4%81ga-R%C4%81gini_system) of male warriors and female Satis), love and romance ([Been](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pungi) and its variant [Nāginī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C4%81gin%C4%AB) dance, and [Ragini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raga#R%C4%81ga-R%C4%81gini_system)), ceremonial ([Dhamal Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang_dance), [Ghoomar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghoomar), [Jhoomar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhumar) (male), Khoria, Loor, and [Ragini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raga#R%C4%81ga-R%C4%81gini_system)).[^cite_note-harcu3-202]

#### Folk music and songs

A tired wife tells her mother-in-law she’s been waiting since morning for her husband, asking him to leave everything and come see her.

Haryanvi folk music is based on day-to-day themes and injecting earthly humour enlivens the feel of the songs.[^cite_note-harcu3-202] Haryanvi music takes two main forms: classical folk music and Desi folk music (country music of Haryana),[^cite_note-harmu1-205] and sung in the form of ballads and love, valor and bravery, harvest, happiness and pangs of the parting of lovers.[^cite_note-harmu3-204][^cite_note-harmu1-205][^cite_note-harmu2-206]

##### Classical Haryanvi folk music

Classical Haryanvi folk music is based on [Indian classical music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_classical_music).[^cite_note-harmu1-205] [Hindustani classical ragas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindustani_classical_raga), learnt in [gharana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharana) [parampara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parampara) of [guru–shishya tradition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guru%E2%80%93shishya_tradition), are used to sing songs of heroic bravery (such as [Alha-Khand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alha-Khand) (1163–1202 CE) about the bravery of [Alha and Udal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alha), [Jaimal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaimal_Rathore) and [Patta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patta_Sisodia) of Maharana [Udai Singh II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udai_Singh_II)), [Brahmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahma) worship and festive seasonal songs (such as [Teej](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teej), [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi) and [Phaag songs of Phalgun month near Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalguna)).[^cite_note-harmu1-205][^cite_note-harmu2-206] Bravery songs are sung in high pitch.[^cite_note-harmu3-204]

##### Desi Haryanvi folk music

[Desi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desi) Haryanvi folk music, is a form of Haryanvi music, based on [Raag Bhairvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairavi_\(Hindustani\)), [Raag Bhairav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhairav_\(raga\)), [Raag Kafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kafi_\(raga\)), [Raag Jaijaivanti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaijaivanti), [Raag Jhinjhoti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhinjhoti) and [Raag Pahadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raga) and used for celebrating community bonhomie to sing seasonal songs, [ballads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballad), [ceremonial songs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_music) (wedding, etc.) and related religious legendary tales such as [Puran Bhagat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puran_Bhagat).[^cite_note-harmu1-205][^cite_note-harmu2-206] Relationship and songs celebrating love and life are sung in medium pitch. Ceremonial and religious songs are sung in low pitch.[^cite_note-harmu3-204] Young girls and women usually sing entertaining and fast seasonal, love, relationship and friendship-related songs such as [Phagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phalguna) (song for eponymous season/month), [Katak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kartik_\(month\)) (songs for the eponymous season/month), [Samman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shraavana) (songs for the eponymous season/month), *bande-bandi* (male-female duet songs), *sathne* (songs of sharing heartfelt feelings among female friends).[^cite_note-harmu3-204] Older women usually sing devotional Mangal Geet (auspicious songs) and ceremonial songs such as [Bhajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhajan), Bhat (wedding gift to the mother of bride or groom by her brother), [Sagai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_wedding_traditions#Sagai), [Ban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_wedding) (Hindu wedding ritual where pre-wedding festivities starts), [Kuan-Poojan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childbirth_in_India) (a custom that is performed to welcome the birth of a child by worshiping the well or source of drinking water), [Sanjhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjhi) and Holi festival.[^cite_note-harmu3-204]

##### Socially normative-cohesive impact

Music and dance for Haryanvi people is a way of lessening societal differences as folk singers are highly esteemed and they are sought after and invited for events, ceremonies and special occasions regardless of their caste or status. These inter-caste songs are fluid in nature, and never personalised for any specific caste, and they are sung collectively by women from different strata, castes, and dialects. These songs transform fluidly in dialect, style, words, etc. This adoptive style can be seen in the adoption of tunes of Bollywood movie songs into Haryanvi songs. Despite this continuous fluid transforming nature, Haryanvi songs have a distinct style of their own as explained above.[^cite_note-harmu3-204]

With the coming up of a strongly socio-economic metropolitan culture in the emergence of urban Gurgaon Haryana is also witnessing community participation in public arts and city beautification. Several landmarks across Gurgaon are decorated with public murals and graffiti with cultural cohesive ideologies and stand the testimony of a lived sentiment in Haryana folk.[^cite_note-207]

### Cuisine

See also: [Cuisine of Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Haryana)

As per a survey, 13% of males and 7.8% of females of Haryana are non-vegetarian.[^cite_note-208] The cuisine of Haryana, rooted in its predominantly agricultural society, has maintained a simple and uncomplicated essence.[^cite_note-Corporation-209] The [regional cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_cuisine#Haryana) features the staples of [roti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti), [saag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saag), [vegetarian sabzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curry) and milk products such as [ghee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee), milk, [lassi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lassi) and [kheer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kheer).[^cite_note-harfood12-210] Haryana is often referred to as the 'Land of Rotis' due to its residents' fondness for various types of rotis. Wheat rotis are ubiquitous, along with the popular baajre ki roti. In the past, rotis were commonly made from a blend of wheat, gram, and barley flour, offering a nutritious and wholesome combination. Another notable variety is the gochini atta, crafted from wheat and gram flour.[^cite_note-211] Haryana is renowned for its abundant livestock, including the famed Murrah buffalo and the Haryana cow. This cattle wealth ensures a plentiful supply of milk and dairy products in Haryanvi cuisine. Many households produce their own butter and ghee, which are generously incorporated into daily meals. Fresh homemade butter, known as "nooni" or "tindi ghee," is commonly churned on a daily basis. When a girl becomes a mother, it's customary for her family to present her with gifts of ghee, edible gum (gondh), laddus (sweetmeats made from gram flour), and dry fruits. Buttermilk, known as "chaaj," is a popular beverage and serves as an instant refresher during the summer months. Lassi, made from yogurt, is another beloved drink that can almost constitute a meal in itself. The Haryanvi fondness for lassi is evident in the fact that "thandai," a sweet milk-based drink, is referred to as "kachi lassi" in Haryana.[^cite_note-Corporation-209]

### Punjabi culture

Punjabi culture has a significant presence in Haryana, particularly in regions bordering Punjab and the GT road belt. The influence is evident in language, cuisine, festivals, and traditions. Many people in Haryana speak Punjabi alongside Hindi and Haryanvi, and Punjabi music and dance forms like Bhangra and Giddha are popular. Additionally, Punjabi cuisine, including dishes like Makki di Roti and Sarson da Saag, is widely enjoyed. Efforts to preserve and promote Punjabi identity in Haryana have been ongoing, with linguistic and cultural initiatives aimed at strengthening the connection between the Punjabi-speaking population and their heritage.[^cite_note-212]

### Society

[Haryanvi people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryanvi_people) have a concept of inclusive society involving the *"36 Jātis"* or communities. Castes such as [Jat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jat), [Rajput](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajput), [Gurjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurjar), [Saini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saini), [Pasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasi_\(caste\)), [Ahirs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahirs), [Ror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ror), [Meo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meo_\(ethnic_group\)), [Charan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charan), [Bishnoi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishnoi), [Harijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harijan), [Aggarwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aggarwal), [Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin), [Khatri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khatri) and [Tyagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyagi) are some of the notable of these 36 Jātis.[^cite_note-213][^cite_note-214]

### Roads and highways

Main article: [Highways in Haryana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highways_in_Haryana)

Haryana has a total road length of 26,062 kilometres (16,194 mi), including 2,482 kilometres (1,542 mi) comprising [29 national highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_National_Highways_in_India), 1,801 kilometres (1,119 mi) of [state highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_state_highways_in_Haryana),[^cite_note-215] 1,395 kilometres (867 mi) of Major District Roads (MDR) and 20,344 kilometres (12,641 mi) of Other District Roads (ODR) (c. December 2017).[^cite_note-trib1-216] A fleet of 3,864 [Haryana Roadways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryana_Roadways) buses covers a distance of 1.15 million km per day, and it was the first state in the country to introduce luxury video coaches.[^cite_note-217]

Ancient [Delhi Multan Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Multan_Road) and [Grand Trunk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Road), South Asia's oldest and longest major roads, pass through Haryana. GT Road passes through the districts of [Sonipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonipat_district), [Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panipat_district), [Karnal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karnal_district), [Kurukshetra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurukshetra_district) and [Ambala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambala_district) in north Haryana where it enters [Delhi](37756.md) and subsequently the industrial town of [Faridabad](1180794.md) on its way. The 135.6 kilometres (84.3 mi) [Kundli-Manesar-Palwal Expressway (KMP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Peripheral_Expressway) will provide a high-speed link to northern Haryana with its southern districts such as [Sonipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonipat), [Gurgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurgaon), and [Faridabad](1180794.md).[^cite_note-218]

The Delhi-Agra Expressway (NH-2) that passes through Faridabad is being widened to six lanes from the current four lanes.[^cite_note-219] It will further boost [Faridabad](1180794.md)'s connectivity with [Delhi](37756.md).

## Education

### Literacy

The literacy rate in Haryana has seen an upward trend and is 76.64 per cent as per the 2011 population census. Male literacy stands at 85.38%, while female literacy is at 66.67%. In 2001, the literacy rate in Haryana stood at 67.91%, of which males and females were 78.49% and 55.73% literate respectively.[^cite_note-236] As of 2013, [Gurgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurgaon) city had the highest literacy rate in Haryana at 86.30% followed by [Panchkula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchkula) at 81.9% and [Ambala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambala) at 81.7%.[^cite_note-237] In terms of districts, as of 2012, [Rewari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewari) had the highest literacy rate in Haryana at 74%, higher than the national average of 59.5%; male literacy was 79% and female literacy was 67%.[^cite_note-238] In 2011, Haryana's urban literacy rate stood at 84.98%, marking a notable rise from 79.92% in 2001. Similarly, the rural literacy rate in the state experienced an improvement, reaching 68.91% in 2011 compared to 58.74% in 2001.[^cite_note-239]

### Schools

[Haryana Board of School Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haryana_Board_of_School_Education), established in September 1969 and shifted to [Bhiwani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhiwani) in 1981, conducts public examinations at middle, matriculation, and senior secondary levels twice a year. Over 700,000 candidates attend annual examinations in February and March; 150,000 attend supplementary examinations each November. The Board also conducts examinations for Haryana Open School at senior and senior secondary levels twice a year.[^cite_note-240] The Haryana government provides free education to women up to the [bachelor's degree](188874.md) level.

In 2015–2016, there were nearly 20,000 schools, including 10,100 state government schools (36 Aarohi Schools, 11 Kasturba Gandhi Balika Vidyalayas, 21 Model Sanskriti Schools, 8,744 government primary school, 3386 government middle school, 1,284 government high school and 1,967 government senior secondary schools),[^cite_note-harsch4-241] 7,635 private schools (200 aided,[^cite_note-harsch1-242] 6,612 recognised unaided,[^cite_note-harsch2-243] and 821 unrecognised unaided private schools[^cite_note-harsch3-244]) and several hundred other central government and private schools such as [Kendriya Vidyalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kendriya_Vidyalaya), [Indian Army Public Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army_Public_Schools), [Jawahar Navodaya Vidyalaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawahar_Navodaya_Vidyalaya) and [DAV schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.A.V._College_Managing_Committee) affiliated to central government's [CBSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Board_of_Secondary_Education) and [ICSE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Certificate_of_Secondary_Education) school boards.

## Communication and media

Haryana has a statewide network of telecommunication facilities. Haryana Government has its own statewide area network by which all government offices of 22 districts and 126 blocks across the state are connected with each other, thus making it the first SWAN of the country.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258] [Bharat Sanchar Nigam Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharat_Sanchar_Nigam_Limited) and most of the leading private sector players (such as Reliance Infocom, [Tata Teleservices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Teleservices), [Bharti Telecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bharti_Telecom), [Idea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idea) [Vodafone Essar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone_Essar), [Aircel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircel), [Uninor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uninor) and [Videocon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Videocon)) have operations in the state. The two biggest cities of Haryana, [Faridabad](1180794.md) and [Gurgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurgaon), which are part of the [National Capital Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Capital_Region_\(India\)), come under the local Delhi Mobile Telecommunication System. The rest of the cities of Haryana come under Haryana Telecommunication System.

[Electronic media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_media) channels include [MTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MTV), [9XM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9XM), Star Group, [SET Max](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SET_Max), [News Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Time), [NDTV 24x7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDTV_24x7) and [Zee Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zee_Group). The [radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio) stations include [All India Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Radio) and other [FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FM_broadcasting) stations.

Panipat, Hisar, Ambala and Rohtak are the cities in which the leading newspapers of Haryana are printed and circulated throughout Haryana, in which [Dainik Bhaskar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dainik_Bhaskar), [Dainik Jagran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dainik_Jagran), [Punjab Kesari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Kesari), [The Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tribune_\(Chandigarh\)), *[Aaj Samaj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaj_Samaj)*, [Hari Bhoomi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Bhoomi)[^cite_note-259] and Amar Ujala are prominent.
