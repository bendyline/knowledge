**MetLife Stadium** is a [multi-purpose stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-purpose_stadium) located at the [Meadowlands Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowlands_Sports_Complex) in [East Rutherford, New Jersey](136878.md), five miles (8 km) west of [New York City](645042.md). Opened in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_New_York_Giants_season), it replaced [Giants Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_Stadium) and serves as the home for the [New York Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants) and [New York Jets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Jets) of the [National Football League](21211.md) (NFL). At an approximate cost of \$1.6 billion, it was the most expensive stadium built in the U.S. at the time of its completion.[^cite_note-11] It is the largest stadium in New Jersey and the New York metro area with a capacity of over 82,000, and is the [largest venue in the NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NFL_stadiums).

MetLife hosted [Super Bowl XLVIII](4057023.md) in February [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_NFL_season). It also hosted nine matches during the [2025 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup), including the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_final), and hosted eight matches during the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), including its [final](75845879.md). During the 2026 FIFA World Cup, the stadium was temporarily renamed **New York New Jersey Stadium**.[^cite_note-12]

MetLife Stadium is one of two stadiums shared by two NFL teams; the other is [SoFi Stadium](45525802.md) in [Inglewood, California](107640.md), home to the [Los Angeles Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Rams) and [Los Angeles Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Chargers). Additionally, MetLife Stadium is the fifth building in the [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area) to be home to multiple teams from the same sports league, after the [Polo Grounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo_Grounds), which was home to [the baseball Giants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Giants_\(NL\)) and [Yankees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Yankees) from 1913 to 1922; the third [Madison Square Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison_Square_Garden_\(1925\)), which hosted the [NHL](21809.md)'s [Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) and [Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Americans) from 1926 to 1942; [Shea Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shea_Stadium), which housed both the [Mets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Mets) and Yankees during the 1974 and 1975 seasons and both the Jets and Giants in 1975; and Giants Stadium, which hosted both the Giants and Jets from 1984 to 2009.

## History

As [Giants Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_Stadium) approached 30 years of age, it was becoming one of the older stadiums in the NFL. The Jets, who had been the tenants to the Giants, were looking to have their proposed [West Side Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_Stadium) built in [Manhattan](45470.md). Originally intended to be the 85,000-seat main stadium for [New York's bid for the 2012 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_City_bid_for_the_2012_Summer_Olympics), it was designed to be downsized to 75,000 seats for the Jets. However, it would have required significant public funding; progress on the project was halted in 2005 due to opposition from a number of sources, including [Cablevision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cablevision), who then owned the nearby [Madison Square Garden](75129.md).[^cite_note-13] The Giants then entered into a joint venture with the Jets to build a new stadium in which the two New York teams would be equal partners.

## Design and planning





MetLife Stadium under construction in 2007 (top) and 2008 (bottom) near Giants Stadium

The architects were tasked with designing a neutral stadium that would still embody the distinct personalities of both franchises. The Giants favored a traditional look of exposed [steel framework](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_framework) and rusticated stone while the Jets wanted a sleek and modern look highlighted by metal and glass. With those features in mind the designers used the column/tower dynamic seen in many of Manhattan's [skyscrapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skyscrapers) as inspiration for the stadium's design.[^cite_note-New_Meadowlands_Stadium-14]

The base of the stadium's facade is clad in [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone)-like stonework while the rest of the stadium is distinguished by an outer skin of [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminum) [louvers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louver) and glass and by interior lighting capable of switching colors, depending on which team is currently playing–blue for the Giants and green for the Jets.[^cite_note-Kaleidoscope-15] This idea originated at the [Allianz Arena](1363716.md) in [Munich](19058.md), Germany, which was previously shared between the city's two major [soccer](10568.md) clubs, [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) and [1860 Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TSV_1860_M%C3%BCnchen). Unlike Giants Stadium, MetLife Stadium can easily be reconfigured for the Giants or Jets within a matter of hours.[^cite_note-16] The louvers' total linear length is exactly 50,000 meters (50 kilometers) or 163,681 feet (31.1 miles).

To change the field decorations, two 4-person crews take approximately 18 hours using forklifts and other machinery to remove the 40 sections of [Act Global](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_Global) UBU Speed Series which make up the teams' respective [end zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End_zone).[^cite_note-17] Replaceable team logos at midfield were removed in August 2010, after [Domenik Hixon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domenik_Hixon) tore his anterior cruciate ligament at a practice at the stadium during training camp. For the next decade, the NFL's logo was painted at midfield, instead of the logo of one of the teams, also shortening the transition time.[^cite_note-18] However, in 2023, the Giants announced they would paint their team logo at midfield during their home games, with the Jets likewise taking the same approach.[^cite_note-19] The paint is formulated to be easily dissolved by water to ensure it can be quickly removed, and as such it is not expected to be used when precipitation is expected. If the two teams are playing each other, the designated home team will have its configuration around the stadium. During their annual preseason matchups, both the Giants and the Jets will have an end zone with their team logo. Additionally in 2023, the stadium installed a new artificial surface called the [FieldTurf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FieldTurf) Core System, the first multilayer dual-polymer monofilament fiber surface. The new surface also contains heavyweight infill. Overall, the system should be more durable and lower incidences of player injury.[^cite_note-20]

Unlike a number of other new NFL venues, MetLife Stadium does not have a roof, as proposals to include a roof failed over a dispute for funding.[^cite_note-21] Thus, indoor events such as the [Final Four](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_Four) cannot be held at the facility, which runs counter to the original aims for the stadium.[^cite_note-22]

Ten giant [HD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_video)-ready [light emitting diode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_emitting_diode) (LED) pylons, located at the north, south, east, and west entrances, display videos of the team currently in-house. The pylons measure approximately 54 feet (16 m) high by 20 feet (6.1 m) wide. The west entrance features a 400 feet (120 m) long and 40 feet (12 m) "Great Wall" that can rotate to display photographic murals of the Giants and Jets on game days and customized images for concerts and other events.[^cite_note-Kaleidoscope-15] Inside, are four 30 feet (9.1 m) by 116 feet (35 m) [HD video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-definition_video) displays, and hang from each corner of the upper deck.[^cite_note-Daktronics-23] In 2025, the four corner video boards were upgraded with 8MM high-resolution LED boards along with a new stadium wide audio system featuring over 1,000 new speakers in the seating bowl.[^cite_note-Upgrades-24]

The new stadium seating bowl is laid out similar to that of Giants Stadium[^cite_note-New_Meadowlands_Stadium-14] and has seating for 82,500[^cite_note-MLScapacity-2] people, including 10,005 club seats and approximately 218 [luxury suites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxury_suite), making it the [largest NFL stadium in terms of total seating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_National_Football_League_stadiums#List).[^cite_note-tour-25] The seating bowl is also raked in a way that eliminates overhangs from the upper decks that would impede views and allows fans to see the full arc of a 90-foot (30 yd) punt.[^cite_note-New_Meadowlands_Stadium-14]

MetLife Stadium includes a total of four locker rooms: one each for the Giants and Jets, as well as two for visiting teams. The home teams have locker rooms on opposite ends of the stadium with a visitors' locker room adjacent to it. On most game days the visitors use the locker room at the end opposite that of the home team while the unused visitors' locker room is used for spillover by the home team.[^cite_note-New_Meadowlands_Stadium-14][^cite_note-tour-25] For games in which neither the Giants or Jets are playing, each team uses one of the visitor's locker rooms. When the Giants and Jets play each other, each team uses its own locker room plus the adjacent visitor's room for spillover.

In 2012, [DLR Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DLR_Group) partnered with [NRG Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NRG_Energy) to design and install a "solar ring" on the upper rim of MetLife Stadium. The solar ring consists of 1,350 building-integrated photovoltaic (BIPV) [solar panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panels) assembled into 47 individual frames. The BIPV panels are illuminated with LED lighting and programmed to display the signature blue and green colors of the Giants and the Jets along with other hues for events such as concerts, soccer matches, and college sports. The panels generate about 350 KW, nearly 25 times the amount of electricity that's actually needed to power the LED display system. The excess power generated can go into general stadium use or back to the grid.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

In January 2024, renovations began on the lower bowl of MetLife Stadium to enlarge the playing field to meet necessary FIFA requirements to host the eight games, including the final, of the 2026 FIFA World Cup. To limit disruption of regular stadium usage, the project is being done in two phases. Phase I was completed in May 2024 and Phase II was completed in May 2025.

To expand the field's dimensions for the tournament and maintain the current NFL seating capacity and layout, four corners of the existing precast concrete seating bowl were demolished and replaced with a new modular steel composite seating system that includes related mechanical, electrical, audio visual and plumbing adjustments. An additional 1,740 permanent seats were also replaced with the new seating system.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

## Technical agreements

### Lease terms



View of MetLife Stadium (under construction) and [Giants Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giants_Stadium) (on right) in July 2009

The Giants and the Jets formed the New Meadowlands Stadium Company, LLC, a 50/50 joint venture, to build and operate the stadium. The two teams leased the parcel of land on which the stadium stands from the NJSEA for a 25-year term, with options to extend it which could eventually reach 97 years. After the 15th year of the lease and every five years hence, one of the two teams may opt out of the lease after giving the state 12 months notice. The first such opportunity to opt out will occur in 2025 with a notice date of 2024. There will then be an opportunity to opt out in 2030, 2035, 2040, etc. However, if one team leaves for a new stadium, the other team would have to remain for the remainder of the lease. However, the high cost of building and relocating to a stadium makes it very unlikely. The teams also get parking revenue from the Meadowlands' western parking lots year round, even when there are no events at the stadium (this would occur when other parts of the Meadowlands host events).[^cite_note-30]

### Naming rights

[Allianz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allianz), a financial services and insurance company based in [Munich](19058.md), Germany, expressed interest in purchasing naming rights to the stadium. The proposal was for a period of up to 30 years,[^cite_note-31] and was estimated to be valued at between \$20 million and US\$30 million. Allianz sponsors the venue that inspired the color-change technology for MetLife Stadium: [Allianz Arena](1363716.md#Stadium_specifics) in Munich.

Allianz's proposal drew protests from New York's [Jewish](25955086.md) community (the largest outside of Israel) and the [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League), due to Allianz previous close ties to the government of [Nazi Germany](21212.md) during [World War II](32927.md). Rabbi Jay Rosenbaum, secretary general of the North American Board of Rabbis, noted however that while the survivors' sensibilities were understandable, a naming deal is legitimate, noting "I have found Allianz to be receptive, to be sensitive and a friend of the Jewish people today."[^cite_note-32] No agreement was reached and talks between Allianz and the teams ended on September 12, 2008.[^cite_note-33]

In a 25-year deal signed on August 23, 2011, [New York City](645042.md)-based insurance company [MetLife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetLife) purchased naming rights to the stadium, which became *MetLife Stadium*.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

### EPA agreement

In June 2009, the New Meadowlands Stadium Corporation and the [EPA](58666.md) signed a memorandum of understanding that outlines plans to incorporate environmentally-friendly materials and practices into the construction and operation of MetLife Stadium. The agreement includes strategies to reduce air pollution, conserve water, and energy, improve waste management, and reduce the environmental impact of construction. The goal of the agreement is to save the emission of nearly 1.68 million metric tons of [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) during the stadium's construction and its first year of operation. Under this agreement, the stadium construction must use around 40,000 tons of recycled steel, recycle 20,000 tons of steel from Giants Stadium, install seating made from recycled plastic and [scrap iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrap_iron), and reduce air pollution from construction vehicles by using cleaner diesel fuel, diesel engine filters, and minimizing [engine idle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engine_idle) times. Other goals of this agreement include providing mass transit options for fans and replacing traditional concession plates, cups and carries with compostable alternatives. The New Meadowlands Stadium Corporation is to report the progress on its goals to EPA every six months. Based on the reports, the EPA has stated it will quantify the benefits of the venue's environmental efforts.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

## Accessibility and transportation



Meadowlands station provides New Jersey Transit rail service to MetLife Stadium on game days

MetLife Stadium is accessible by car via Exits 16W and 19W on the western spur of the [New Jersey Turnpike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Turnpike) ([I-95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_95_in_New_Jersey)) and is also located adjacent to [NJ Route 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Route_3) and [NJ Route 120](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Route_120).[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]

[Coach USA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coach_USA) provides the [351 Meadowlands Express Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NJ_Transit_bus_routes_\(300%E2%80%93399\)) service between MetLife Stadium and the [Port Authority Bus Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_Bus_Terminal).[^cite_note-42] [New Jersey Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Transit_Bus_Operations) provides the [353 Bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NJ_Transit_bus_routes_\(300%E2%80%93399\)) service for select events between MetLife Stadium and [Secaucus Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secaucus_Junction).[^cite_note-43] In 2026, NJ Transit opened the [Transitway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit_in_New_Jersey#Secaucus_to_Meadowlands_Transitway), a dedicated [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) (BRT) route for [articulated buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Articulated_bus) from Secaucus Junction to MetLife Stadium.[^cite_note-44]

The [Meadowlands Rail Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowlands_Rail_Line) operates on event days between [Meadowlands station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meadowlands_station) and [Hoboken Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoboken_Terminal) via Secaucus Junction, where there is connecting service to [New York Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Penn_Station), [Newark Penn Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newark_Penn_Station), and other [New Jersey Transit rail operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Transit_rail_operations). The line opened to the public on July 26, 2009.[^cite_note-45]

MetLife Stadium enforces a clear bag policy, allowing only transparent bags no larger than 12”x6”x12” and small clutches of 4.5”x6.5”, with exceptions for medically necessary items after inspection.[^cite_note-46]

## Criticism

Since its opening in 2010, MetLife Stadium has attracted criticism related to its design, playing surface and its accessibility.

### Playing surface

Metlife Stadium’s playing surface has been criticized by NFL players due to frequent injuries that occur. Both the Giants and Jets have seen their players injured often on the field. [Aaron Rodgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Rodgers) once suffered a torn Achilles at the stadium early on in his career with the Jets. [Jaelan Phillips](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaelan_Phillips), [Andrew Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Thomas_\(American_football\)) and [Malik Nabers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Nabers) have also suffered serious injuries at the stadium.[^cite_note-47] MetLife Stadium has consistently ranked as having one of the worst playing surfaces in the NFL as a result.[^cite_note-48] [Green Bay Packers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay_Packers) coach [Matt LaFleur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_LaFleur) complained about the playing surface after [Josh Jacobs](59225621.md) suffered a knee contusion during a game at MetLife Stadium. Despite these criticisms, NFL officials have insisted that the playing surface is safe.[^cite_note-49] During the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), the stadiums playing surface continued to elicit criticism despite the installation of a grass surface. [France](250197.md) manager [Didier Deschamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Deschamps) criticized the playing surface stating “The pitch in New York was really tough — it took a lot out of the players’ muscles.” Midfielder [Adrien Rabiot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adrien_Rabiot) said that it “seemed more like an artificial pitch” and described it as “hard and rigid.” [Vinicius Junior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vin%C3%ADcius_J%C3%BAnior) of [Brazil](149286.md) complained that the grass dried out quickly due to heat.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] Following this criticism, [FIFA](11049.md) downplayed these concerns.[^cite_note-52] Ahead of the [2026 FIFA World Cup final](75845879.md), these concerns were reiterated with players and coaches raising concerns about the condition of the pitch. The playing surface for soccer at the stadium had been a going concern since the [2025 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup).[^cite_note-53] Due to these concerns, the selection of MetLife Stadium to host the final had been questioned, considering that other venues have been better received by players and coaches.[^cite_note-54]

### Transit access

Transit access to MetLife Stadium has been a subject of discussion concerning the stadium. At [Super Bowl XLVIII](4057023.md) in 2014, spectators waited hours to get to and from MetLife Stadium, with many struggling to get to and from the venue. Dubbed as a “[Mass Transit Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_Transit_Super_Bowl)”, transit access to the stadium was deemed to be a logistical failure.[^cite_note-55] During the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), the location of the stadium was the subject of criticism due to the slow pace of transporting people out of the Meadowlands following the conclusion of games. Three hours after the stadium's first World Cup game concluded, spectators were still stuck at MetLife Stadium with [ridesharing companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridesharing_company) struggling to move spectators.[^cite_note-56] These challenges occurred due to less than expected demand for taking [New Jersey Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Transit) train and bus service to the stadium, although following the conclusion of the World Cup, the agency reported that they set an overall ridership volume record over the eight games held at the stadium, and deemed it a logistical success in contrast to the Super Bowl.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

### Design

The design of MetLife Stadium has been criticized, often in part due to its lack of a roof. This concern was raised ahead of Super Bowl XLVIII in 2014 given that the NFL does not award Super Bowls to stadiums in a cold climate that lack a roof.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60]

## Awards and recognition

In 2009, MetLife Stadium was named the "Greenest Stadium" in the NFL by the Environmental Protection Agency (EPA).[^cite_note-61]

In July 2017, MetLife Stadium was named "Venue of the Year" by the Stadium Business Summit. The award is awarded to the world's best stadium, arena or sports venue, that deserves recognition for an outstanding performance over a 12-month period.[^cite_note-62]

### Super Bowl XLVIII

Further information: [Super Bowl XLVIII](4057023.md) and [Mass Transit Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_Transit_Super_Bowl)



Exterior of MetLife Stadium prior to [Super Bowl XLVIII](4057023.md) in February 2014

On May 25, 2010, it was announced that [Super Bowl XLVIII](4057023.md) was awarded to the stadium, the first time a [Super Bowl](27718.md) would be played in the [New York metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_metropolitan_area), and the first time that a non-domed stadium in a cold-weather city would host it.[^cite_note-76] Normally, Super Bowls must be held in indoor climate-controlled stadiums if they are held in a city with an average temperature lower than 50 °F (10 °C). However, NFL commissioner [Roger Goodell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Goodell) waived this requirement and allowed MetLife Stadium on the ballot because of a "unique, once-only circumstance based on the opportunity to celebrate the new stadium and the great heritage and history of the NFL in the New York region."[^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] The game was played on February 2, 2014. The temperature at kickoff was 49 °F (9 °C), making it only the third-coldest Super Bowl on record.[^cite_note-79] The [Seattle Seahawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Seattle_Seahawks_season) defeated the [Denver Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Denver_Broncos_season) 43–8 for their first Super Bowl victory.[^cite_note-80]

### Professional wrestling



MetLife Stadium during WrestleMania 29



MetLife Stadium during WrestleMania 35

MetLife Stadium has twice hosted [WrestleMania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania)—the flagship [pay-per-view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pay-per-view) event of the [professional wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_wrestling) promotion [WWE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WWE). [WrestleMania 29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_29) was held at MetLife Stadium on April 7, 2013. With 80,676 spectators, it was the third [most-attended event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_WWE_attendance_records) in WWE history, and the highest-grossing event in WWE history at \$12.3 million. Six years later, MetLife Stadium hosted [WrestleMania 35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WrestleMania_35) on April 7, 2019, overtaking WrestleMania 29 with an attendance of 82,265, and \$16.9 million in revenue. Six years after WrestleMania 35, MetLife Stadium also hosted the first ever two-night [SummerSlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SummerSlam_\(2025\)), on August 2–3, 2025.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82]

### Soccer

MetLife Stadium is also designed for [soccer](10568.md). To prepare for a match, the stadium uses retractable seating in the field level corners to fit a [FIFA](11049.md)-sanctioned soccer field.[^cite_note-83] Along with being noted for providing exceptional sight-lines,[^cite_note-84] this has allowed the stadium to host several major international matches.

The first international exhibition match was between [Mexico](808402.md) and [Ecuador](1016797.md) on May 7, 2010, in front of 77,507 fans. The stadium hosted another international exhibition soccer match between the [United States](582488.md) and [Brazil](149286.md) on August 10, 2010. Brazil won 2–0 in front of a near-sellout crowd of 77,223; the game was played on a temporary grass field.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86]

On March 26, 2011, the stadium hosted an international friendly, between the United States and [Argentina](454699.md), which ended in a 1–1 draw and was played in front of a sellout crowd of 78,926.[^cite_note-87]

On June 18, 2011, the stadium hosted two quarterfinal matches of the [2011 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup). [Costa Rica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_Rica_national_football_team) played [Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team) to a 1–1 draw with Honduras winning on penalties 4–2 in the first match. The second match featured Mexico against [Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guatemala_national_football_team) with Mexico winning 2–1.

On June 9, 2012, Argentina and Brazil played a friendly match, with Argentina winning in a thrilling 4–3 match featuring a [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) [hat trick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hat_trick).[^cite_note-88] Another exhibition match in preparation for the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) was played on November 14, 2012, between [Colombia](680175.md) and [Brazil](149286.md), with Brazil acting as the local team despite a higher affluence of Colombian fans.

On August 4, 2013, the stadium hosted two second round matches of the [2013 International Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_International_Champions_Cup). The first match saw [Valencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_CF) defeat [Inter Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan) 4–0 and the second match saw [Chelsea](7473.md) defeat [AC Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Milan) 2–0.

On November 15, 2013, Argentina and Ecuador played an international friendly to a 0–0 draw.[^cite_note-89]

On June 10, 2014, the stadium hosted an international friendly before the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) between the [Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Ireland_national_football_team) and [Portugal](576328.md) with Portugal beating Ireland 5–1.[^cite_note-90]

On September 9, 2014, the stadium hosted an international friendly between Brazil and Ecuador with Brazil victorious 1–0.[^cite_note-91]

On March 31, 2015, the stadium hosted an international friendly rematch of Argentina and Ecuador with Argentina prevailing 2–1 in front of 48,000 fans.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

On July 19, 2015, the stadium hosted two quarterfinal matches of the [2015 CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup). [Trinidad and Tobago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinidad_and_Tobago_national_football_team) played [Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama_national_football_team) to a 1–1 draw with Panama winning on penalties 6–5. The second match saw Mexico defeat Costa Rica 1–0.

On August 3, 2016, MetLife Stadium hosted a [2016 International Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_International_Champions_Cup) match between [Real Madrid](26413.md) and [FC Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich); Real Madrid won the match 1–0.[^cite_note-94]

On July 22, 2017, a match of the [2017 International Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_International_Champions_Cup) was played between [FC Barcelona](68187.md) and [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC). Barcelona won the match 2–1 in front of 82,104 fans.[^cite_note-95]

MetLife Stadium hosted two matches as part of the [2018 International Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_International_Champions_Cup). The first match on July 25, 2018, between [Manchester City F.C.](165813.md) and [Liverpool F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.), with it ending 2–1 in favor of Liverpool, and the second match on August 7, 2018, between Real Madrid and [AS Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Roma), also ending in a 2–1 victory for Real Madrid.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97]

On September 7, 2018, the stadium hosted the United States and Brazil in an international friendly match as part of [U.S. Soccer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Soccer_Federation)'s "Kickoff Series",[^cite_note-98] which Brazil won by a score of 2–0 with goals from [Roberto Firmino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Firmino) and [Neymar](15326144.md). On September 11, the stadium hosted an international friendly between Colombia and Argentina.[^cite_note-99]

On July 29, 2019, during the [2019 International Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_International_Champions_Cup), the stadium hosted the first [Madrid derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid_derby) held outside of Europe between Real Madrid and [Atlético Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Madrid), which Atlético won 7–3.[^cite_note-100] On September 6, MetLife Stadium hosted the 70th all time meeting of the United States and Mexico with Mexico winning 3–0 in front of a crowd of 47,960.[^cite_note-101]

On July 23, 2023, the stadium hosted an international friendly between [Premier League](11250.md) clubs [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United) and [Arsenal](2174.md) in front of a stadium soccer record crowd of 82,262 with United winning 2–0.[^cite_note-102]

On August 3, 2024, MetLife Stadium hosted a preseason match of [El Clásico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cl%C3%A1sico) between Barcelona and Real Madrid with Barcelona winning 2–1 in front of 82,154 fans.[^cite_note-103]

MetLife Stadium was one of twelve venues that hosted the [2025 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup), including [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_final) won by [Chelsea F.C.](7473.md).

### College football

On October 16, 2010, [Rutgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_Scarlet_Knights_football) hosted [Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Black_Knights_football) in the first college football game to be played in the new stadium, with the [Scarlet Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rutgers_Scarlet_Knights_football) defeating the [Black Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Black_Knights_football) in overtime, 23–20. During the game's second half, Rutgers player [Eric LeGrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_LeGrand) was injured on a special teams play, defending a Rutgers kickoff, and paralyzed from the neck down.

[Syracuse University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse_Orange_football) has hosted selected home games at MetLife Stadium in lieu of the [Carrier Dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JMA_Wireless_Dome). The first of these games, branded as *New York's College Classic*, was played in 2012 against the visiting [USC Trojans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USC_Trojans_football), who won 42–29. In 2013, the team opened its season against its [rival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_State%E2%80%93Syracuse_football_rivalry) [Penn State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_State_Nittany_Lions_football) at the stadium, losing 23–17.[^cite_note-110] In 2014, the team hosted the [Notre Dame Fighting Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame_Fighting_Irish_football) at MetLife Stadium, losing 31–15. A rematch was held in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_NCAA_Division_I_FBS_football_season), once again losing to the Fighting Irish 50–33.

On November 16, 2019, MetLife Stadium hosted the 61st [Cortaca Jug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortaca_Jug) rivalry game between the [NCAA Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_III) [Cortland Red Dragons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cortland_Red_Dragons_football) and [Ithaca Bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ithaca_Bombers_football). With an attendance of 45,161, it set a record for the most-attended football game in Division III history. The game was held at MetLife Stadium as part of season-long commemorations of the 150th anniversary of college football.[^cite_note-111]

On December 11, 2021, MetLife Stadium hosted the 122nd [Army–Navy Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army%E2%80%93Navy_Game). This was the first time the historic matchup was held at MetLife. Previous matchups were held at Giants Stadium in 1989, 1993, 1997, and 2002. Navy defeated Army 17–13.

### Outdoor ice hockey

In 2024, MetLife Stadium hosted two [National Hockey League](21809.md) games as part of the [Stadium Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_NHL_Stadium_Series). The [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils) hosted the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) on February 17, 2024. Devils captain [Nico Hischier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nico_Hischier) scored the first NHL goal in MetLife Stadium history on a breakaway 32 seconds into the first period of the game, ultimately scoring two goals on the night en route to the Devils victory over the Flyers by a final score of 6–3 before a crowd of 70,328 fans.[^cite_note-112] The following day, the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) defeated the [New York Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Islanders) 6–5 in overtime following a 3-goal comeback from a 3–5 deficit in front of 79,690 fans in attendance, which was the third largest attendance in NHL history. Rangers forward [Artemi Panarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemi_Panarin) was awarded the game-winning goal 10 seconds into overtime as the net came off its moorings by Islanders defenseman [Noah Dobson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah_Dobson). After video review, it was confirmed that the puck would've crossed the goal line had the net stayed in place.[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] This was the first time MetLife Stadium had ever hosted NHL hockey and the first time outdoor NHL hockey had ever been played in the state of New Jersey. [Yankee Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yankee_Stadium) hosted two games as part of the [2014 NHL Stadium Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_NHL_Stadium_Series), in which the New York Rangers won both games, defeating the New Jersey Devils 7-3 and defeating the New York Islanders 2–1. [Citi Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citi_Field) previously hosted the [2018 NHL Winter Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_NHL_Winter_Classic) as well, with the Rangers defeating the [Buffalo Sabres](69918.md) 3–2 in overtime.

### Other events

The stadium hosted the 12th [Siyum HaShas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siyum_HaShas), a celebration of the completion of the Talmud through the 7+1⁄2-year Daf Yomi study program, on August 1, 2012. At 93,000 seats, it was the highest capacity crowd in the stadium's history, due to on-field seating and a ticket sell-out. The [siyum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siyum) was a Department of Homeland Security level two security event, the most critical short of a presidential visit.[^cite_note-181][^cite_note-182] On January 1, 2020, it hosted the [13th Siyum HaShas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siyum_HaShas#13th_Siyum_HaShas).[^cite_note-183] On June 6, 2027, it will host the 14th Siyum HaShas.[^cite_note-184]

In 2014, two of the "Keep Seeking First God's Kingdom!" International Conventions of [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) were held at the stadium.[^cite_note-185]

On July 14 and 15, 2017, the stadium hosted the [18th International Indian Film Academy Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_IIFA_Awards), the [Oscars](324.md) of [Bollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood), for the first time.[^cite_note-186]

On January 16, 2018, the stadium hosted the inaugural ball for newly elected New Jersey [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_New_Jersey) [Phil Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Murphy).[^cite_note-187]

## Incidents

On July 5, 2026, during the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md), a bomb threat was made during the [match](81667005.md#Brazil_vs_Norway) between [Brazil](149286.md) and [Norway](203277.md). Two individuals, including 26-year-old Amar Ramkissoon, sent FIFA a text message at around 4\:15 p.m. ET threatening to "blow up" the area.[^cite_note-190] Authorities quickly tracked the [threat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomb_threat), leading [New Jersey State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_State_Police) to arrest and charge Ramkissoon with creating a false public alarm and making terroristic threats. The threat ultimately did not disrupt the match.[^cite_note-191][^cite_note-192]
