The **2026 Pacific typhoon season** is an ongoing event in the annual cycle of [tropical cyclone](8282374.md) formation in the western Pacific Ocean. The season runs throughout 2026, although most tropical cyclones typically develop between May and October. The first named storm of the season, [Nokaen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Nokaen), developed on January 15, marking the first named storm to form in January since [Pabuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Pabuk_\(2019\)) during the [2019 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Pacific_typhoon_season), and the earliest start to a Pacific typhoon season since then. The season's first typhoon, [Sinlaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Sinlaku_\(2026\)), reached [typhoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon) intensity on April 10, and, subsequently, the season's first super typhoon on April 12. Sinlaku affected the [Northern Mariana Islands](21402.md), causing extensive damage in [Tinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinian,_Northern_Mariana_Islands) and [Saipan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saipan,_Northern_Mariana_Islands). [Typhoon Bavi](83602324.md) intensified into super typhoon on July 4 before making landfall on [Rota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rota,_Northern_Mariana_Islands) at peak intensity, becoming one of the strongest typhoons on record for the Northern Mariana Islands. Concurrently, [Tropical Storm Maysak](83605596.md) caused devastating flooding in [Southern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China), resulting in 171 fatalities, 159 of them in [Guangxi](47836950.md) alone. [Typhoon Dolphin](83805947.md) was upgraded to a super typhoon on July 29, struck the [Okinawa Islands](2113477.md) on August 7, and later made landfall in China's [Zhejiang](96602.md) province on August 9 near [Wenzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenzhou).

The scope of this article is limited to the Pacific Ocean north of the equator between [100°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100th_meridian_east) and the [180th meridian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/180th_meridian). Within the northwestern Pacific Ocean, two separate agencies assign names to tropical cyclones, which can result in a system having two names. The [Japan Meteorological Agency](457886.md) (JMA)[^cite_note-5] names a [tropical cyclone](8282374.md) when it is estimated to have 10-minute [sustained winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_sustained_wind) of at least 65 km/h (40 mph) anywhere in the basin. The [Philippine Atmospheric, Geophysical and Astronomical Services Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Atmospheric,_Geophysical_and_Astronomical_Services_Administration) (PAGASA) assigns names to tropical cyclones that move into or form as a tropical depression within the [Philippine Area of Responsibility](4272946.md) (PAR), defined as the area between 135°E and 115°E and between 5°N and 25°N, regardless of whether the JMA has already named the system. Tropical depressions monitored by the United States [Joint Typhoon Warning Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Typhoon_Warning_Center) (JTWC)[^cite_note-7] are given a numerical designation with a "W" suffix, which means "west", a reference to the western Pacific region.[^cite_note-STY-3]

Before and during the season, several national meteorological services and scientific agencies forecast the number of tropical cyclones, tropical storms, and typhoons that could form during the season and/or how many of them would affect a particular country. These agencies included the Tropical Storm Risk (TSR) Consortium of [University College London](52029.md), [PAGASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PAGASA), Vietnam's [National Center for Hydro-Meteorological Forecasting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Center_for_Hydro-Meteorological_Forecasting) (NCHMF) and Taiwan's [Central Weather Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Weather_Administration) (CWA).

The first outlook was released by PAGASA on December 15, 2025, in its monthly seasonal climate outlook, covering the first half of 2026. The agency forecast that zero to three tropical cyclones were expected to form or enter the PAR between January and March, while two to five were expected to form or enter the PAR between April and June.[^cite_note-PAG_1-12] PAGASA also mentioned that the short-lived and weak La Niña, which began during the fourth quarter of 2025, would persist for a month or two, with a neutral [El Niño–Southern Oscillation](317311.md) (ENSO) transition between January and March.[^cite_note-PAG_1-12]

The year began with a weak La Niña cycle that developed in 2025.[^cite_note-El_Niño_and_La_Niña_Years-14] According to the [National Oceanic and Atmospheric Administration](37876.md)'s (NOAA) forecast, the ENSO state was likely to return to neutral by mid-year and potentially transition to an El Niño phase during the second half of the year.

Main article: [Tropical Storm Nokaen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Nokaen)

On January 13, the JTWC started monitoring a [low-pressure area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-pressure_area) that developed 170 nautical miles (310 km) south of [Palau](23372.md), with [satellite imagery](1184557.md) indicating a disorganized low-level circulation center (LLCC).[^cite_note-15] The JMA later followed suit, identifying the same system as a tropical depression.[^cite_note-16] The following day, at 08\:00 [PHT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Standard_Time) (00\:00 [UTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordinated_Universal_Time)), PAGASA also classified the system, assigning it the name *Ada*,[^cite_note-17] At 15\:00 UTC, the JTWC upgraded the system to a tropical depression, designating it as *01W*.[^cite_note-18] At 06\:00 UTC on January 15, the JMA recognized 01W as a tropical storm and named it *Nokaen*.[^cite_note-19] Despite being in a favorable environment, Nokaen maintained its intensity while remaining stationary along the southern periphery of a subtropical high.[^cite_note-20] It then shifted northeastward slowly as it moved over marginal conditions.[^cite_note-21] On January 19, PAGASA noted that Nokaen weakened into a tropical depression;[^cite_note-22] the system further weakened into a remnant low on January 21[^cite_note-23] and dissipated on January 22.

Nokaen brought torrential rainfall to parts of the Philippines, triggering a landslide that resulted in two fatalities in [Matnog, Sorsogon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matnog).[^cite_note-24] Another fatality was reported after a maritime incident in the [Surigao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surigao_\(province\)) area.[^cite_note-25] Several areas across [Catanduanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catanduanes), [Albay](253167.md), and [Camarines Sur](255883.md) experienced flooding and landslides.[^cite_note-26] A total of 832,986 people were affected, while 60,384 were displaced across [Calabarzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calabarzon), the [Bicol Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicol_Region), [Eastern Visayas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Visayas), [Negros Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negros_Island), and [Caraga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caraga). The city government of [Maasin, Southern Leyte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maasin,_Southern_Leyte) postponed the "Sakay-Sakay Festival" to January 25. Additionally, a *[barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay)* in [Oras, Eastern Samar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oras,_Eastern_Samar) experienced a water crisis after flooding damaged the village's water system.[^cite_note-27]

### Tropical Storm Penha (Basyang)

| Tropical storm (JMA) |  |
| --- | --- |
| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
|  |  |
| Duration | February 3 – February 7 |
| Peak intensity | 65 km/h (40 mph) (10-min); 1002 [hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\)) ([mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\))) |

Main article: [Tropical Storm Penha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Penha)

On February 2, the JTWC began tracking a [low-pressure area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-pressure_area) that developed 128 nautical miles (237 km) east-northeast of [Yap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yap), with [satellite imagery](1184557.md) indicating a weak and broad low-level circulation center (LLCC).[^cite_note-28] The JMA followed suit the next day, identifying the system as a tropical depression.[^cite_note-29] On February 3, at 17\:30 PHT (09\:30 UTC), the system entered the PAR, with PAGASA assigning it the name *Basyang*.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] After the system organized further, the JTWC designating it as Tropical Depression 02W at 03\:00 UTC on February 4.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] Later that day, at 21\:00 [JST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Standard_Time) (12\:00 UTC), the JMA upgraded 02W to a tropical storm and assigned the name *Penha*.[^cite_note-34] At 23\:50 PHT (15\:50 UTC), Penha made its first landfall over [Bayabas, Surigao del Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayabas,_Surigao_del_Sur).[^cite_note-35] This was followed by additional landfalls in [Jagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagna,_Bohol) and [Dauis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dauis,_Bohol) in [Bohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohol), [Alcoy, Cebu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoy,_Cebu), and [Ayungon, Negros Oriental](1675316.md) on February 6.[^cite_note-36] After making landfall, Penha weakened into a depression as it moved over cooler [sea surface temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_surface_temperature) and encountered high [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear).[^cite_note-37] The system was downgraded to a remnant low the following day and dissipated a few hours later.[^cite_note-38]

Penha produced heavy rainfall, flooding, and landslides across the Philippines, leaving 12 dead, 36 injured,[^cite_note-39] and around 28,000 people displaced. A landslide claimed four lives in [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro),[^cite_note-40] while five people died in [Iligan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iligan).[^cite_note-41] Two people drowned in [Surigao del Sur](267469.md),[^cite_note-42] while another drowned in [Carmen, Agusan del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen,_Agusan_del_Norte).[^cite_note-43] A state of calamity was subsequently declared in Iligan and Surigao del Sur, where damages reached [₱](227672.md)1.48 billion ([US\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_dollar)25.24 million). A total of 1,373 houses were damaged.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45]

On March 10, an area of low pressure formed 74 nautical miles (137 km) northeast of [Yap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yap), with the JTWC assessing a medium chance of tropical cyclone formation.[^cite_note-46] The JMA also began issuing advisories on the system later that day, classifying it as a tropical depression.[^cite_note-47] The system was in a marginally favorable environment as it tracked northeastward.[^cite_note-48] On March 11, the JMA upgraded the system to a tropical storm and assigned it the name *Nuri*.[^cite_note-49] The JTWC followed suit at 03\:00 UTC, designating the system as Tropical Depression *03W*.[^cite_note-50] On March 12, Nuri weakened into a tropical depression and subsequently dissipated later that day, with both the JMA and JTWC issuing their final advisories on the system.[^cite_note-51]

### Typhoon Sinlaku

| Violent typhoon (JMA) |  |
| --- | --- |
| Category 5 super typhoon (SSHWS) |  |
|  |  |
| Duration | April 7 – April 19 |
| Peak intensity | 215 km/h (130 mph) (10-min); 905 [hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\)) ([mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\))) |

Main article: [Typhoon Sinlaku (2026)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Sinlaku_\(2026\))

On April 8, the [Joint Typhoon Warning Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Typhoon_Warning_Center) reported that an area of low pressure had formed 234 nautical miles (433 km) west-southwest of [Chuuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuuk_State), assessing a medium chance of development.[^cite_note-52] The [Japan Meteorological Agency](457886.md) later followed suit, classifying the system as a tropical depression.[^cite_note-53] The [low-level circulation center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Low-level_circulation_center) continued to wrap around the southern semicircle of the system.[^cite_note-54] Early the following day, the JTWC upgraded the system into a depression and designated it *Tropical Depression 04W*.[^cite_note-55] Later that day, the JMA upgraded 04W into a tropical storm, giving it the name *Sinlaku* as it moves westwards.[^cite_note-56] With low vertical windshear and high sea surface temperature, Sinlaku was upgraded to a severe tropical storm by the JMA at 12\:00 UTC.[^cite_note-57] On April 11, both the JMA and JTWC upgraded Sinlaku to a typhoon, with satellite imagery showing a rapidly consolidating storm and an expanding [central dense overcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_dense_overcast) (CDO).[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59] The storm would begin rapidly intensifying, and the JMA upgraded Sinlaku to a violent typhoon the following day.[^cite_note-60] By 18\:00 UTC the same day, the JTWC upgraded Sinlaku's 1-minute sustained wind speeds to 160 knots (295 km/h) and lowered its pressure to 890 mb (26.28 inHg).[^cite_note-61] By April 14, Sinlaku weakened to a Category 4-equivalent typhoon due to a long-lasting [eyewall replacement cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyewall_replacement_cycle), losing super typhoon status.[^cite_note-62] Later that day, the still very powerful typhoon made landfall on the northern side of [Tinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinian), with [Saipan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saipan) being located within the now much larger new eye. By the next day, Sinlaku had completed its eyewall replacement cycle, having weakened to a Category 3-equivalent typhoon as it began to turn towards the north.[^cite_note-63] Sinlaku further downgraded into a tropical storm on April 19 before it underwent extratropical transition on the following day, prompting the JTWC to issue its final warning at 21\:00 UTC.[^cite_note-64]

Sinlaku left one person dead and another missing in Micronesia.[^cite_note-65] In the Northern Mariana Islands, six people went missing after a cargo vessel overturned.[^cite_note-66]

### Tropical Storm Hagupit (Caloy)

| Tropical storm (JMA) |  |
| --- | --- |
| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
|  |  |
| Duration | May 4 – May 19 |
| Peak intensity | 65 km/h (40 mph) (10-min); 1002 [hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\)) ([mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\))) |

On May 4, JTWC marked an area of low-pressure 67 nautical miles (124 km) west-northwest of Chuuk, with a low chance of formation.[^cite_note-67] The following day, deep convection built over the center, with formative banding.[^cite_note-68] Later that day, at 21\:00 UTC, the JTWC designated the system *Tropical Depression 05W*.[^cite_note-69] The JTWC further upgraded the system to a tropical storm at 00\:00 the following day. At 06\:00 UTC, JMA followed suit and assigned the name *Hagupit*, as the storm passes through environmental conditions marginally favorable for development.[^cite_note-70] At 18\:00 UTC, a multispectral satellite imagery (MSI) showed that the storm had good cloud characteristics of anticyclonic outflow, while microwave imagery showed active, convective cloud clusters.[^cite_note-71] On May 9, Hagupit entered the PAR at 18\:00 PHT (10\:00 UTC), and was assigned the name *Caloy*.[^cite_note-72] At 03\:00 UTC on May 10, Hagupit weakened into a tropical depression as it moves north-northwest over unfavorable conditions.[^cite_note-73] The JTWC issued its final advisory on May 11 as Hagupit further weakened into a remnant low.[^cite_note-74] JMA continued to track Hagupit as a depression before it was downgraded into a low-pressure area on May 15. The remnants of Hagupit tried to redevelop, with the JTWC analyzing the remnants as a separate entity, however they failed to redevelop and later dissipated.

Main article: [Tropical Storm Jangmi (2026)](83299674.md)

On May 25, the JTWC began monitoring a disturbance east-southeast of Yap, noting favorable conditions for [tropical cyclogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclogenesis).[^cite_note-75] Development chances were raised to medium on May 26,[^cite_note-76] and the [Japan Meteorological Agency](457886.md) (JMA) classified it as a tropical depression later that day.[^cite_note-77] On May 27, the JMA upgraded the system to Tropical Storm *Jangmi*,[^cite_note-78] while the JTWC designated it as *06W*.[^cite_note-79] After entering the PAR on May 29, PAGASA assigned the name *Domeng*.[^cite_note-80] Jangmi intensified into a Category 1-equivalent typhoon on May 30 by the JTWC,[^cite_note-81] with the JMA upgrading it to a severe tropical storm.[^cite_note-82] It weakened to a tropical storm on June 1 due to a deteriorating structure and dry air intrusion.[^cite_note-83] Jangmi made landfall in [Wakayama Prefecture](179766.md) on June 3[^cite_note-84] before becoming extratropical later that day.[^cite_note-85]

According to the [National Disaster Risk Reduction and Management Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Disaster_Risk_Reduction_and_Management_Council) (NDRRMC), 85,295 people were displaced in the [Bangsamoro Autonomous Region of Muslim Mindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Region_of_Muslim_Mindanao) (BARMM), where infrastructure damage reached [₱](227672.md)3.4 million ([US\$](18717338.md)55,055.42).[^cite_note-86] In Japan, a total of 23 injuries were reported, including 17 in Okinawa and two in [Kagoshima Prefecture](217439.md); 48 houses were also partially damaged in Kagoshima.[^cite_note-87] Flooding occurred in [Saiwai-ku, Kawasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saiwai-ku,_Kawasaki) and [Ōta, Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cta,_Tokyo), while part of [National Route 371](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_National_Route_371) collapsed. Water outages affected 1,800 households in [Ōme, Tokyo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%8Cme,_Tokyo), and fallen branches disrupted the [Hachiko Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hachiko_Line) between [Haijima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haijima_Station) and [Komagawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komagawa_Station).[^cite_note-88]

On June 3, the JMA began issuing advisories on a tropical depression in the South China Sea.[^cite_note-89] The JTWC also started tracking the system the following day, as it became more broad and well-defined whilst drifting northeastwards towards [Taiwan](25734.md). On June 15, the JTWC began monitoring a disorganized low-pressure area 940 nautical miles (1,740 km) east-southeast of Guam in a marginal environment for [tropical cyclogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclogenesis).[^cite_note-93] On June 18, the JMA classified the system as a tropical depression.[^cite_note-94] Later that day, the JTWC designated it as Tropical Depression 07W.[^cite_note-95] On June 19, the JMA upgraded the system to Tropical Storm *Mekkhala*, with a persistent [CDO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_dense_overcast) partially obscuring its LLCC.[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] Mekkhala entered the PAR on June 20 and was named *Francisco* by PAGASA.[^cite_note-98] It strengthened into a severe tropical storm before becoming a Category 1-equivalent typhoon on June 21.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100] The storm intensified into a Category 2-equivalent typhoon, with PAGASA also upgrading it to typhoon status.[^cite_note-JMA_Mekkhala_Cat2-101][^cite_note-102] On June 22, Mekkhala strengthened into a Category 3-equivalent typhoon before peaking as a Category 4-equivalent typhoon, while PAGASA classified it as a super typhoon.[^cite_note-JMA_Mekkhala_Cat3-103][^cite_note-Digital_Typhoon_Mekkhala_Cat4-104][^cite_note-105] The storm weakened on June 23 as it encountered cooler waters and 20 to 25 knots (37 to 46 km/h; 23 to 29 mph) north-northeasterly wind shear.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107] By June 25, Mekkhala had weakened to a tropical storm, with the JMA downgrading it to a severe tropical storm as it exited the PAR.[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109][^cite_note-DeVeraRuiz2026-110] Storm warnings were issued for Okinawa as the system approached the islands.[^cite_note-111] Mekkhala later scraped the coast of Japan before becoming extratropical on June 27, prompting the JTWC to discontinue advisories.

Two people died in [Taiwan](25734.md), one in [Kaohsiung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaohsiung) and another in [Hsinchu County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hsinchu_County). One other person in Hsinchu County went missing.[^cite_note-France24-112] In [Japan](15573.md), the storm produced heavy rainfall which overflowed a reservoir in [Hiroshima Prefecture](179797.md) and impacted several residences. Two people were killed and another was injured due to a paddling incident related to the storm in [Ehime Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ehime_Prefecture) while a landslide caused another death in [Yamaguchi Prefecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yamaguchi_Prefecture).[^cite_note-113] Four other injuries occurred across Japan.[^cite_note-France24-112]

On June 21, the JTWC marked a poorly organized but consolidating low-pressure area that formed 441 nm (817 km) east of Guam. Although expecting a slow development, the disturbance was located in a favorable environment for tropical cyclogenesis.[^cite_note-114] On the following day, the JMA started issuing advisories on the system, recognizing it as a tropical depression.[^cite_note-115] The circulation consolidated, with persistent deep convection around the center.[^cite_note-jtwc-116] JTWC later designated the disturbance as *08W* at 15\:00 UTC.[^cite_note-117] At 00\:00 UTC of June 23, 08W was upgraded into a tropical storm, receiving the name *Higos* by the JMA.[^cite_note-118] On June 23, Higos weakened back to a tropical depression with winds of 35 mph and a [central pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_pressure) of 1003 hPa (mbar).[^cite_note-119] However, Higos regained tropical storm status at 21\:00 UTC of June 24.[^cite_note-120] At 15\:00 PHT (07\:00 UTC) of the following day, Higos entered the PAR, assigning the domestic name *Gardo* by PAGASA, with the system moving towards the north when it entered.[^cite_note-DeVeraRuiz2026-110][^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122] The system then exited the PAR at 09\:30 PHT (01\:30 UTC).[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-124] Then, Higos made a big turn towards the north and then to the northeast. On June 27 at 5\:25 AM JST (19\:25 UTC on June 26), Higos made landfall on the [Izu Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izu_Peninsula) as it raced towards the northeast, subsequently making landfall on the [Boso Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boso_Peninsula) an hour later. Later that evening, Higos completed its extratropical transition, and shortly after, it was absorbed into another extratropical cyclone system to the north.[^cite_note-125]

Bavi triggered heavy rains and landslides that killed 23 people and left 18 others missing across the Philippines.[^cite_note-141]

### Tropical Storm Haishen (Josie)

| Tropical storm (JMA) |  |

On July 7, the JTWC began tracking a disturbance situated over the [Marshall Islands](19147.md). Over the next several days, the disturbance drifted towards the southeast before heading northeastward. On July 12, the disturbance became better organized, prompting the JMA to classify the system as a tropical depression.[^cite_note-152] The following day, it further intensified into a tropical storm, giving it name *Haishen* by the JMA at 00\:00 UTC,[^cite_note-153] with the JTWC issuing a Tropical Cyclone Formation Alert for the system at 03\:00 UTC.[^cite_note-154] PAGASA also issued a tropical cyclone advisory the same day at 11\:00 PHT (03\:00 UTC).[^cite_note-155] At 00\:30 PHT (16\:30 UTC), Haishen entered the PAR, subsequently assigning the local name *Josie* by PAGASA.[^cite_note-156] However, the following day, at 03\:30 UTC, the JTWC cancelled its TCFA as Haishen dissipated due to moderate to strong easterly wind shear, without giving the system a formal designation.[^cite_note-157] Meanwhile, the JMA continued monitoring Haishen, downgrading into a tropical depression at 00\:00 UTC until further downgrading into a low-pressure area at 12\:00 UTC.[^cite_note-158]

Haishen is the first tropical storm to be recognized by the Japan Meteorological Agency, but not by the Joint Typhoon Warning Center since [Mulan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_Storm_Mulan_\(2022\)) in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Pacific_typhoon_season).

On July 19, the [Central Pacific Hurricane Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Pacific_Hurricane_Center) (CPHC) began monitoring a disturbance south of the Hawaiian Islands. When the disturbance had a high 90% chance of development in the next two days, it was designated [Invest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invest_\(meteorology\)) 92C.[^cite_note-172] Later on July 26, the disturbance crossed into the Western Pacific, and the JMA classified the system as a tropical depression upon crossover.[^cite_note-173] The following day at midnight, The JTWC upgraded the disturbance to a tropical depression, designating it as *12W*.[^cite_note-174] Shortly thereafter, the Japan Meteorological Agency upgraded the system to a tropical storm, naming it *Dolphin*.[^cite_note-175] Almost immediately after its nomenclature, Dolphin began a period of [rapid intensification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_intensification), the fastest since [Typhoon Noru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Noru) in 2022,[^cite_note-176] quickly becoming a typhoon and reaching a Category 4-equivalent initial peak intensity on the [Saffir–Simpson scale](255313.md) on July 28, before its intensification was stunted by an [eyewall replacement cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyewall_replacement_cycle). After finishing, Dolphin resumed intensifying once again, reaching super typhoon status the following day. Soon after reaching Category 5 strength on the Saffir–Simpson scale, Dolphin started undergoing a second eyewall replacement cycle, temporarily halting further development of the storm.[^cite_note-177] After completing its eyewall replacement cycle, it strengthened back to Category 5-equivalent strength but later started weakening again after started a third eyewall replacement cycle.[^cite_note-178] On August 4, after it completed a fourth eyewall replacement cycle, Dolphin rapidly intensified back into a Category 4-equivalent typhoon,[^cite_note-179] but a few hours later, it once again entered unfavourable conditions and weakened back into a category 2 typhoon. Despite the system remaining outside the PAR, significant rainfall was reported in Metro Manila and 23 provinces as Dolphin enhanced the southwest monsoon.[^cite_note-182]

### Tropical Storm Kujira (Luis–Maymay)

| Tropical storm (JMA) |  |
| --- | --- |
| Tropical storm (SSHWS) |  |
|  |  |
| Duration | July 31 – August 6[^cite_note-Kujira-183] |
| Peak intensity | 75 km/h (45 mph) (10-min); 994 [hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pascal_\(unit\)) ([mbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar_\(unit\))) |

Main article: [Tropical storms Kujira and Peilou](83873966.md)

On July 29, the JTWC and PAGASA began monitoring a low pressure area outside the PAR, citing an unlikely chance of development.[^cite_note-184] On July 30, the JMA upgraded the disturbance to a tropical depression, and PAGASA followed suit on two days later on August 1, assigning it the name *Luis*.[^cite_note-185][^cite_note-186] Later that day, at 18\:00 UTC, as Luis was situated east-northeast of [Legazpi, Albay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legazpi,_Albay), the JTWC raised the system's chances of becoming a tropical cyclone to medium, citing a slowly consolidating low-level circulation center (LLCC).[^cite_note-187] At 19\:30 UTC, the JTWC raised its chances to high and issued a TCFA for the system but canceled and downgraded the chance of formation to low after 12 hours.[^cite_note-jtwc-116] At 17\:30 PHT (09\:30 UTC), Luis made its first landfall over [Maconacon, Isabela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maconacon,_Isabela).[^cite_note-188] PAGASA stated it degenerated into a low pressure area later that day,[^cite_note-189] a part of it split into a second low pressure area in the South China Sea, meanwhile the original system became a remnant low and merged with the second system a few hours later.[^cite_note-190] The JMA also started issuing warnings once again at the same time. The JTWC also upgraded its chances to high and issued a TCFA again after passed over Luzon Island and moved into more favorable conditions.[^cite_note-jtwc-116] At 20\:00 PHT (12\:00 UTC), the disturbance redeveloped into a tropical depression and was retrospectively renamed *Maymay* by PAGASA.[^cite_note-191] At 18\:00 UTC, both the JMA and PAGASA upgraded it to a tropical storm and the JTWC upgraded it to a tropical depression, therefore being assigned the name *Kujira* by the JMA[^cite_note-192] and the designation *13W* from the JTWC, respectively. Early on August 6, at 02\:00 PHT (18\:00 UTC of the previous day), Kujira made its second landfall over [Magsingal, Ilocos Sur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magsingal,_Ilocos_Sur).[^cite_note-193] Later, the JMA, JTWC and PAGASA all downgraded the system to a tropical depression[^cite_note-194] and JMA later stated it dissipated. The following day, the system weakened into an remnant low over the Philippine sea[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196] and later it dissipated according to PAGASA.[^cite_note-197] Kujira was later absorbed into Typhoon Dolphin and became a trough.

In the Philippines, flooding was reported in seven areas of [Bangsamoro](37260549.md) and [Zamboanga Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zamboanga_Peninsula). Several landslides occurred in [Dipaculao, Aurora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipaculao,_Aurora) and a downed utility pole blocked a road in the same municipality.[^cite_note-198] A total of 80,633 people were affected by the storm.[^cite_note-199] Upon Kujira's second landfall in the Philippines, torrential rains fell across [Luzon](69375.md). Several roads were closed due to the effect of landslides and flooding. A wooden bridge was also destroyed by floods in [Quirino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quirino). More than 4,700 households were affected by flooding in [Buluan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buluan) after a river overflowed. At least 13 people died from the effects of the storm, with 9 missing.[^cite_note-200] Agricultural and infrastructural damage in [Pangasinan](1691800.md) reached ₱105.7 million (US\$1.73 million).[^cite_note-201] The Department of Agriculture reported a total of ₱689.66 million worth of agricultural damage from the storm along with the effects of the southwest monsoon.[^cite_note-202]

On August 15, the JMA noted that a tropical depression formed near Palikir, Micronesia,[^cite_note-231] though they downgraded it back to a low-pressure area 12 hours later.[^cite_note-232] The following day, the JMA classified it as a tropical depression again.[^cite_note-233] On August 18, the JTWC classified the system as a depression, giving it the designation *17W*.[^cite_note-234] The following day at 03\:00 UTC, the JMA upgraded the system to a tropical storm, assigning it the name *Saudel*.[^cite_note-235] The JTWC followed suit later that day, upgrading the system to a tropical storm.[^cite_note-236] On August 20, the JMA upgraded the system to a severe tropical storm.[^cite_note-237] The following day, both the JMA and JTWC upgraded Saudel to a typhoon after it underwent rapid intensification.[^cite_note-238][^cite_note-239] Saudel peaked with 1 minute winds of 209 km/h (130 mph) and a pressure of 940 hPa (mbar). The following day, the JTWC recognized its remnants, whilst stating that it had a low chance of redevelopment.[^cite_note-metoc_navy_mil_443-244] Later that day, the JTWC upgraded it to medium chance, as its structure began to organize.[^cite_note-245] The JMA upgraded Saudel back into a tropical storm after exiting out to the South China Sea.[^cite_note-246] On September 1, the JTWC resumed advisories on the system, as it upgraded Saudel to a tropical storm.[^cite_note-247] But the system was downgraded to a tropical depression later that day.[^cite_note-248] The next day, the JTWC upgraded Saudel once again to a tropical storm.[^cite_note-249] At 06\:30 CST (21\:30 UTC) on September 3, Saudel made its third and final landfall over [Zhangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhangzhou), Fujian.[^cite_note-250]

The HKO issued Standby Signal No. 1 at 18\:10 HKT (10\:10 UTC),[^cite_note-251] the SMG followed suit at 18\:30 MST (10\:30 UTC).

Saudel left three people injured in [Okinawa Prefecture](22477.md), Japan.[^cite_note-252] A mudslide induced by Saudel's remnants killed one person and left 11 others missing in [Suichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suichuan), [Jiangxi](173817.md), China, and damaged 12 houses.[^cite_note-253]

## Storm names

See also: [Tropical cyclone naming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclone_naming) and [History of tropical cyclone naming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_tropical_cyclone_naming)

Within the northwest Pacific Ocean, both the JMA and PAGASA assign names to tropical cyclones that develop in the region, which can result in a tropical cyclone having two names.[^cite_note-Padgett_Dec_99-409] The JMA's [RSMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Specialized_Meteorological_Centre) Tokyo–Typhoon Center assigns international names to tropical cyclones on behalf of the [World Meteorological Organization](33584.md)'s Typhoon Committee should they be judged to have 10-minute sustained wind speeds of 65 km/h (40 mph).[^cite_note-TC-410] PAGASA names tropical cyclones which move into or form as a tropical depression in their PAR, located between 135°E and 115°E and between 5°N and 25°N, even if the cyclone has had an international name assigned to it.[^cite_note-Padgett_Dec_99-409] Should the list of names for the region be exhausted, then names will be taken from an auxiliary list, the first ten of which are published each season. Unused names are marked in gray. The names of significant tropical cyclones will be retired by both [PAGASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_retired_Philippine_typhoon_names) and [the Typhoon Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_retired_Pacific_typhoon_names) in the spring of 2027.[^cite_note-TC-410]

See also: [List of retired Pacific typhoon names](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_retired_Pacific_typhoon_names)
