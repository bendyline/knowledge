**Ales Viktaravich Bialiatski**[^cite_note-1] ([Belarusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language): Алесь Віктаравіч Бяляцкі, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Belarusian): *Aleś Viktaravič Bialacki*; born 25 September 1962) is a Belarusian human rights defender, [pro-democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratization) activist, and writer. He is chair of the [Viasna Human Rights Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viasna_Human_Rights_Centre). He was held as a [prisoner of conscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_conscience). An activist for Belarusian independence and democracy since the early 1980s, Bialiatski is a founding member of Viasna and the [Belarusian Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Popular_Front), serving as leader of the latter from 1996 to 1999. He is also a member of the [Coordination Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_Council_\(Belarus\)) of the Belarusian opposition. He has been called "a pillar of the human rights movement in Eastern Europe" by *[The New York Times](30680.md)*, and recognised as a prominent pro-democracy activist in Belarus.

Bialiatski's defence of human rights in Belarus has brought him numerous international accolades. In 2020, he won the [Right Livelihood Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Livelihood_Award), widely known as the "Alternative Nobel Prize". In 2022, Bialiatski was awarded the [2022 Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nobel_Peace_Prize), along with the organisations [Memorial](570357.md) and [Centre for Civil Liberties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Center_for_Civil_Liberties_\(Ukrainian_civil_society_organization\)).

Bialiatski has been imprisoned twice; firstly from 2011 to 2014, and from 2021 to 2025, on both occasions on charges of tax evasion. Bialiatski, as well as other human rights activists, have called the charges politically motivated.

On 3 March 2023, Bialiatski was sentenced in Minsk to ten years in prison for "cash smuggling" as well as "financing actions and groups that grossly violated public order." Human rights activists view the charges as fabricated in order to silence Bialiatski and his movement after he was awarded the Nobel Peace Prize.[^cite_note-2]

## Life

### Background

Alexander Bialiatski was born in [Vyartsilya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyartsilya), in today's [Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelia), Russia, to Belarusian parents.[^cite_note-auto-3] His father Viktar Bialiatski is a native of the [Rahačoŭ District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rahachow_District), and his mother Nina comes from the [Naroŭlia District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naroulia_District). In 1965, the family returned to Belarus to settle in [Svietlahorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Svietlahorsk), [Gomel Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gomel_Region).

Bialiatski is a scholar of [Belarusian literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_literature)[^cite_note-auto-3] and graduated from [Homiel State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisk_Skorina_Gomel_State_University) in 1984 with a degree in Russian and Belarusian Philology. After graduation, Bialiatski worked as a schoolteacher in the [Lieĺčycy District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyelchytsy_District) in Gomel Region.

From 1985 to 1986, he served in the army as an armoured vehicle driver in an antitank artillery battalion near [Yekaterinburg](66524.md) (then Sverdlovsk), Russia.[^cite_note-4]

### In Belarus

Bialiatski was Secretary of the [Belarusian Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Popular_Front) (1996–1999) and deputy chairman of the BPF (1999–2001).[^cite_note-auto-3]

Bialiatski founded the [Viasna Human Rights Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viasna_Human_Rights_Centre) in 1996. The Minsk-based organization which was then called “Viasna-96”, was transformed into a nationwide NGO in June 1999. On 28 October 2003 the [Supreme Court of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Court_of_Belarus) cancelled the state registration of the Viasna Human Rights Centre for its role in the observation of the [2001 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Belarusian_presidential_election). Since then, the leading Belarusian human rights organization has been working without registration.[^cite_note-5]

Bialiatski was chairman of the Working Group of the Assembly of Democratic NGOs (2000–2004). In 2007–2016, he was vice-president of the International Federation for Human Rights ([FIDH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_for_Human_Rights)).[^cite_note-6]

Bialiatski is a member of the Union of Belarusian Writers (since 1995) and the Belarusian PEN-Centre (since 2009).[^cite_note-auto-3]

### August 2011 arrest and sentencing

On 4 August 2011, Bialiatski was arrested under charges of [tax evasion](2256795.md) (“concealment of profits on an especially large scale”, Article 243, part 2 of the Criminal Code of the Republic of Belarus).[^cite_note-7] The indictment was made possible by financial records released by prosecutors in [Lithuania](17675.md) and [Poland](22936.md).[^cite_note-8]

On 24 October 2011, Bialiatski was sentenced to 4½ years in prison and confiscation of property. Bialiatski pleaded not guilty, saying that the money had been received on his bank accounts to cover Viasna's human rights activities.[^cite_note-9]

#### Reaction

Belarusian human rights activists, as well as the [European Union](9317.md) leaders, EU governments, and the [United States](3434750.md) said that Bialiatski was a political prisoner, calling his sentencing politically motivated. They urged the Belarusian authorities to release the human rights activist. On 15 September 2011 a special resolution the [European Parliament](9581.md) called for Bialiatski's immediate release.[^cite_note-10] The activist's release was also requested by EP President [Jerzy Buzek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerzy_Buzek),[^cite_note-11] EU High Representative for Foreign Affairs and Security Policy [Catherine Ashton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Ashton), OSCE Chairman [Eamon Gilmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eamon_Gilmore),[^cite_note-12] and the UN Special Rapporteur on the situation of human rights in Belarus, [Miklós Haraszti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikl%C3%B3s_Haraszti).[^cite_note-13]

Several international human rights non-governmental organisations called for Bialiatski's "immediate and unconditional release".

- On 11 August, [Amnesty International](18947898.md) declared Bialiatski a [prisoner of conscience](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prisoner_of_conscience).

  [^cite_note-14]

- On 12 September, the [International Federation for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Federation_for_Human_Rights) (FIDH) launched a campaign to advocate for Bialiatski's release and inform more generally about political prisoners in Belarus.

  [^cite_note-15]

- Tatsiana Reviaka, Bialiatski's colleague at [Viasna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viasna) and the President of the Belarusian Human Rights House in [Vilnius](32597.md), said that "the reason behind these charges is the fact that our organisation Viasna has been providing different assistance to victims of political repressions in [Belarus](3457.md).

  [^cite_note-16]

- "Belyatsky's arrest is a clear case of retaliation against him and Viasna for their human rights work. It's the latest in a long series of efforts by the government to crush Belarus's civil society", [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) said in a statement.

  [^cite_note-17]

Bialiatski served his sentence in penal colony number 2 in the city of [Babruysk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babruysk), working as a packer in a sewing shop.

He was repeatedly punished by the prison administration for "violation of the prison rules", and was declared a "malicious offender", which prevented him from being amnestied in 2012 and deprived him of family visits and food parcels.

During his time in prison, Bialiatski wrote many texts on literary topics, essays, memoirs, which were posted to his associates.

An unprecedented campaign of international solidarity was launched during his imprisonment. Bialiatski was released from prison 20 months ahead of schedule on 21 June 2014 after spending 1,052 days of arbitrary detention in harsh conditions, including serving periods of solitary confinement.[^cite_note-18]

The date of Bialiatski's arrest, 4 August, is celebrated annually as the International Day of Solidarity with the Civil Society of Belarus. It was established in 2012 as a response to the activist's arrest.[^cite_note-19]

### Release in 2014 and arrest in 2021

Bialiatski was released on 21 June 2014.[^cite_note-20] The United Nations Special Rapporteur on the situation of human rights in Belarus, [Miklós Haraszti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikl%C3%B3s_Haraszti) welcomed his liberation.[^cite_note-21]

During the [2020 Belarusian protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Belarusian_protests), he became a member of the [Coordination Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coordination_Council_\(Belarus\)) of [Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatlana_Tsikhanouskaya).[^cite_note-22]

On 14 July 2021, the Belarusian police searched Viasna's employees' homes around the country and raided the central office. Bialiatski and his colleagues Vladimir Stephanovich and Vladimir Labkovich were arrested.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] On 6 October 2021, Bialiatski was charged with tax evasion with a maximum penalty of 7 years in prison.[^cite_note-25] As of 7 October 2022, he was still [in prison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prison_Number_8).[^cite_note-\:1-26]

### 2023 trial and sentencing

His trial alongside [Valentin Stefanovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valentin_Stefanovich?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Vladimir Labkovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Labkovich?action=edit\&redlink=1) started in January 2023.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] [Amnesty International](18947898.md) mentioned that "\[t]he trial against Ales Bialiatski and his fellow human rights defenders is a blatant act of injustice wherein the state is clearly seeking to enact revenge for their activism. In this shameful pretense of a trial, the defendants cannot even hope for a semblance of justice."[^cite_note-29]

On 3 March 2023, the Belarus judiciary convicted Bialiatski of smuggling and financing [political protests](6787314.md), as "actions grossly violating public order", and sentenced him to prison for 10 years.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31]

### Release

Bialiatski was released and exiled to Lithuania on 13 December 2025, as part of a deal between the Lukashenko government and the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-32]

## International recognition

### Awards



Bialiatski with [Jan Wyrowiński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Wyrowi%C5%84ski), Irena Lipowicz, and Olgierd Dziekoński in 2014

Referred to by *The New York Times* as "a pillar of the human rights movement in Eastern Europe since the late 1980s,"[^cite_note-33] Bialiatski has received widespread international recognition as a prominent voice for human rights activism in Belarus.[^cite_note-\:1-26]

Bialiatski's work has been recognised by human rights organisations globally. In March 2006, Bialiatski and Viasna won the 2005 [Homo Homini Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homo_Homini_Award) of the Czech NGO [People in Need](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_in_Need_\(Czech_Republic\)), which recognizes "an individual who is deserving of significant recognition due to their promotion of human rights, democracy and non-violent solutions to political conflicts".[^cite_note-34] The prize was awarded by former Czech President and dissident [Václav Havel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel). In 2006, Bialiatski won the Swedish [Per Anger Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_Anger_Prize),[^cite_note-35] as well as the [Andrei Sakharov Freedom Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Sakharov_Freedom_Award) of the [Norwegian Helsinki Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norwegian_Helsinki_Committee).[^cite_note-36]

In 2012, the [Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe) awarded him its [Václav Havel Human Rights Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel_Human_Rights_Prize) for his work as a human rights defender, "so that the citizens of Belarus may one day aspire to our European standards".[^cite_note-37] As he was detained at the time, the award was received on his behalf by his wife. After his release, he visited Strasbourg to thank the Assembly for its support.[^cite_note-38] He was also awarded the Lech Wałęsa Award for "democratisation of the Republic of Belarus, his active promotion of human rights and aid provided for persons currently persecuted by Belarusian authorities" that year,[^cite_note-39] as well as, together with Uganda's Civil Society Coalition on Human Rights and Constitutional Law, the 2011 Human Rights Defenders Award by the United States Department of State. As he was still imprisoned at the time of the Ales Bialiatski was awarded the prize *in absentia*, and the award was passed to his wife, Natallia Pinchuk, in the U.S. Embassy in Warsaw, Poland on 25 September 2012.[^cite_note-40]

Bialiatski was declared civil rights defender of the year by the Swedish [Civil Rights Defenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_Rights_Defenders) group in 2014.[^cite_note-41] In 2020, he shared [Right Livelihood Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_Livelihood_Award), widely known as "Alternate Nobel Prize" with [Nasrin Sotoudeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasrin_Sotoudeh), [Bryan Stevenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Stevenson), and [Lottie Cunningham Wren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lottie_Cunningham_Wren).[^cite_note-42] In December of the same year, Bialiatski was named among the representatives of the [Belarusian opposition](31371711.md), and honored with the [Sakharov Prize](1055590.md) by the [European Parliament](9581.md).[^cite_note-43]

Bialiatski has received honorary citizenship from the cities of [Genoa](47332321.md) (in 2010),[^cite_note-44] [Paris](22989.md) (in 2012),[^cite_note-45] and [Syracuse, Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syracuse,_Sicily) (in 2014).[^cite_note-46]

In 2022, Bialiatski was awarded the [2022 Nobel Peace Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Nobel_Peace_Prize) along with organisations [Memorial](570357.md) and [Centre for Civil Liberties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centre_for_Civil_Liberties_\(Ukrainian_civil_society_organization\)).[^cite_note-\:0-47] Prior to his 2022 award of the Nobel Peace Prize, Bialiatski was nominated five times unsuccessfully,[^cite_note-48] including in 2006 and 2007. In 2012, he was again nominated for the Nobel Peace Prize, but the prize was awarded to the European Union. In February 2013, he was nominated by the Norwegian MP Jan Tore Sanner. In 2014, members of the Polish Parliament nominated Bialiatski for the Nobel Peace Prize. The nomination was signed by 160 Polish MPs.

Following the awarding of the 2022 Nobel Peace Prize, members of the Belarusian opposition celebrated it, with [Sviatlana Tsikhanouskaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatlana_Tsikhanouskaya) saying in a Tweet, "The prize is an important recognition for all Belarusians fighting for freedom & democracy. All political prisoners must be released without delay."[^cite_note-49]

## Personal life

Ales Bialiatski is married to Natallia Pinchuk. They met in 1982 when Ales was a student of Francishak Skaryna Homiel State University and Nataliia studied in the [pedagogical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedagogical) college in [Lojeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyew). The couple married in 1987. He is a practising [Roman Catholic](606848.md).[^cite_note-52]

Bialiatski has a son, Adam. He studied in Poland at [Łódź Film School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%81%C3%B3d%C5%BA_Film_School). In 2018, he was sentenced for 1.5 years in Poland for a "Naked action in [Auschwitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auschwitz_concentration_camp)", which activists called an anti-war performance. During the performance activists slaughtered a lamb.[^cite_note-53] The sentence was not carried out.[^cite_note-54]

During his university years, Bialiatski played bass guitar in a band called *Baski*. He has stated that his two major hobbies now are mushroom hunting and planting flowers. He generally speaks the [Belarusian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_language).
