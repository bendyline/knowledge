[Austria](26964606.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) are geopolitical and commercial trade partners.[^cite_note-y110-1] The [U.S. Embassy in Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_the_United_States_in_Vienna) is located in [Vienna](55866.md). Since 2025, the [United States Ambassador to Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Ambassador_to_Austria) is [Arthur Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Fisher_\(real_estate_broker\)). The [Austrian Embassy in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Austria_in_Washington) is located in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), and is led by Ambassador [Petra Schneebauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_Schneebauer?action=edit\&redlink=1).

Austria first recognized the United States in 1797, and formal diplomatic relations were established between the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) and the U.S. in 1838.[^cite_note-y110-1] Relations were interrupted by [World War I](4764461.md), as [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) fought opposite of the U.S., and again during [World War II](32927.md) following the *[Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss)*.[^cite_note-y110-1] Ties resumed strongly after 1945 as the United States played an essential role in Austria's postwar reconstruction, including through the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan) and [Austrian State Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_State_Treaty).[^cite_note-y110-1] Since then, the two countries have maintained close relations, cooperating on educational exchange, science and technology, trade, and cultural diplomacy.[^cite_note-y110-1]

## History

### 18th and 19th centuries

The [Archduchy of Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduchy_of_Austria) never held any colonies in the Americas. Nevertheless, a few Austrians did settle in what would become the United States prior to the 19th century, including a group of fifty families from [Salzburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg) who were exiled for being [Lutherans](23371382.md) in predominantly [Catholic Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicism_in_Austria). The [Salzburg Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzburg_Protestants) established their own community in [Ebenezer, Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ebenezer,_Georgia), in 1734.[^cite_note-Building-2]

Austria stayed neutral during the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War). It joined the [First League of Armed Neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_League_of_Armed_Neutrality), a league of European states organized by [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great) of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) during the war to protect neutral shipping, which was often under the threat of being seized or interrupted by the [Royal Navy](26061.md). Austria, the epicenter of the [Habsburg monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_monarchy), was initially reluctant to support the American Revolution, given that the goal of the revolution was to liberate a group of colonies from the tyrannical rule of a foreign monarch.[^cite_note-Guide-3] The [Continental Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_Congress) had tried to establish diplomatic relations in 1777 by sending [William Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lee_\(diplomat\)) to [Vienna](55866.md), but the Austrian government did not officially receive him.[^cite_note-Guide-3]

Austria officially recognized the United States as an independent country in 1797 when Conrad Frederick Wagner was accepted as U.S. Consul at [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste).[^cite_note-Guide-3] U.S. diplomats to Austria subsequently served in the Habsburg-held cities of Trieste and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) before an American consulate was established in Vienna on October 10, 1829. This was followed by the establishment of a U.S. legation in Vienna headed by [Henry A.P. Muhlenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_A.P._Muhlenberg) in 1838, with the elevation to embassy status occurring in 1902.[^cite_note-Guide-3] The United States and the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) signed a treaty regarding commerce and navigation in 1829.[^cite_note-Guide-3] An Austrian legation headed by Baron de Mareschal arrived in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), in 1838.[^cite_note-Guide-3]

Serious strains occurred in the relations between the two countries as a result of the [Revolutions of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutions_of_1848). Professor Stephen Tuffnell states:

In its frequent and blundering breaches of etiquette with the Habsburgs, American domestic politics were, as ever, catalytic. Thus, as national-separatist revolutions broke open across the European continent in 1848, ebullient support of [Lajos Kossuth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lajos_Kossuth) and the Hungarian 48ers in the United States drove Washington and Vienna into conflict. Pro-Hungarian fervour in the Senate and Democratic press, stoked by [Lewis Cass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Cass); State Department flirtation with the recognition of Hungarian independence in the Taylor and Fillmore Presidencies; and, finally, the latter's 1851 'rescue' of Kossuth from the Ottoman Empire on board the [USS *Mississippi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Mississippi_\(1841\)) precipitated a breach in relations. Only the death of [Daniel Webster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Webster), a major opponent of reconciliation, averted the crisis.

[^cite_note-4]

From around the 1870s until the outbreak of [World War I](4764461.md) in 1914, Austrians emigrated in large numbers to the U.S.[^cite_note-b321-5][^cite_note-g790-6] Over two million people from [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) immigrated to the United States throughout the 19th century, though because of the empire's multi-ethnic status, it is difficult to determine how many of these immigrants were ethnic Austrians.[^cite_note-Building-2] Over 275,000 Austrian Americans lived in the United States by 1900. Austria-Hungary was also one of six countries to send more than a million immigrants to the U.S. within a single decade, arriving at roughly two million between 1900 and 1909 alone.[^cite_note-g376-7] Austrian Americans settled primarily in [New York](8210131.md), [California](5407.md), [Pennsylvania](23332.md), [Florida](18933066.md), [New Jersey](21648.md), and various [Midwestern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwest) states like [Ohio](22199.md) and [Illinois](14849.md). Over 60% of these immigrants came from [Burgenland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burgenland).[^cite_note-Building-2]

Both [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) and the United States were part of the [Eight-Nation Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eight-Nation_Alliance) that intervened in the [Boxer Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boxer_Rebellion) in [China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) from 1899 to 1901.

### World War I and World War II

In 1917, the United States declared war on the Austro-Hungarian Empire alongside the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire) after being drawn into [World War I](4764461.md). The war caused diplomatic relations between the United States and Austria-Hungary to be terminated on April 8, 1917,[^cite_note-Guide-3] and caused a dramatic decrease in Austrian immigration to the United States.[^cite_note-Building-2]

The [Treaty of Saint-Germain-en-Laye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint-Germain-en-Laye_\(1919\)), negotiated between the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) and Austria following the war, officially dissolved the Austro-Hungarian Empire and created the [First Austrian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Austrian_Republic). The United States never ratified the Treaty of Saint-Germaine-en-Laye. Instead, the United States negotiated [its own peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.-Austrian_Peace_Treaty_\(1921\)) with Austria in 1921. The United States officially recognized the independence of the First Austrian Republic on August 24, 1921.[^cite_note-Guide2-8]

[Nazi Germany](21212.md) annexed the First Austrian Republic in March 1938 in an event known as the *[Anschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anschluss)*. The United States closed its legation to Austria on April 30, 1938.[^cite_note-Guide2-8] During [World War II](32927.md), American prisoners of war were among [Allied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II) POWs held in the Stalag XVII-A, Stalag XVII-B, Stalag 317/XVIII-C and Stalag 398 [German POW camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) operated in German-annexed Austria,[^cite_note-9] and American prisoners were also noted in subcamps of the [Mauthausen concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauthausen_concentration_camp) in [Amstetten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amstetten,_Lower_Austria) and [Melk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melk).[^cite_note-10]

### 1945–present



U.S. President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) and Austrian Chancellor [Werner Faymann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Faymann) in 2009



U.S. Secretary of State [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken) meeting with Austrian President [Alexander Van der Bellen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Van_der_Bellen) in 2024

The U.S. played an important role in Austria's reconstruction after World War II. After the war, the four Allied powers [divided Austria into occupation zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Austria), which were administered jointly through an Allied Council.[^cite_note-y110-1] The United States channeled significant aid to Austria through the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan), which Austria joined in 1948 and which ultimately provided \$962 million in support.[^cite_note-y110-1] This support contributed to the 1955 [Austrian State Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_State_Treaty), which ended the occupation and established the modern Republic of Austria as a free and neutral state.[^cite_note-y110-1]

Vienna has frequently been chosen as the venue of key superpower summit meetings, like the [Vienna Summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vienna_Summit) in June 1961 between U.S. President [John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_F._Kennedy) and Soviet Premier [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev), as well as the [SALT II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Arms_Limitation_Talks) agreement in June 1979 between U.S. President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) and Soviet General Secretary [Leonid Brezhnev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev).

In February 1984, Austrian president [Rudolf Kirchschläger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolf_Kirchschl%C3%A4ger) paid a [state visit](327283.md) to the United States.[^cite_note-11] It was the first state visit of an Austrian president to the United States.[^cite_note-12]

In September 1995, U.S. President [Bill Clinton](3356.md) invited the President of Austria, [Thomas Klestil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Klestil), for a working visit to [Washington, D.C.](108956.md),[^cite_note-13] which took place on October 19.[^cite_note-14] Although Austria is not a part of [NATO](21133.md), U.S. and Austrian troops fought side by side during the [NATO intervention in Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_intervention_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) and in the [Kosovo War](16760.md).[^cite_note-15] Both the United States and Austria were involved in the [War in Afghanistan (2001–2021)](19666611.md).

On June 21, 2006, U.S. President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) held bilateral talks with the President of Austria, [Heinz Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Fischer), at the [Hofburg Imperial Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hofburg_Imperial_Palace) in [Vienna](55866.md), together with U.S. Secretary of State [Condoleezza Rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condoleezza_Rice) and Foreign Minister of Austria [Ursula Plassnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursula_Plassnik), shortly before a summit between the U.S. and the [European Union](9317.md).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] President [Barack Obama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barack_Obama) met Chancellor [Werner Faymann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Faymann) when Obama visited [Prague](23844.md) on April 5, 2009.

President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) met with Chancellor [Sebastian Kurz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Kurz) for a bilateral meeting in February 2019 with the aim of "revitalizing the bilateral relationship between the United States and exploring new avenues for transatlantic cooperation...look\[ing] to address both global conflicts and those in the European neighborhood, promote economic prosperity, and strengthen energy security."[^cite_note-18] On April 2, 2026, a spokesperson for the Austrian defense ministry announced that Austria has turned down U.S. requests for military overflights over its territory since the commencement of the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md), in accordance with its neutrality policy.[^cite_note-19]

According to the 2012 U.S. Global Leadership Report, 31% of [Austrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrians) approve of U.S. leadership, with 40% disapproving and 29% uncertain.[^cite_note-20]
