**Guangdong**[^cite_note-7] is a coastal [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) in [South China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China), on the north shore of the [South China Sea](74209.md)[^cite_note-SNA2008-8] with [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou) as the capital. With a [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_\(human_biology\)) of 128.590 million (as of 2026)[^cite_note-data2022-9] across a total area of about 179,800 km<sup>2</sup> (69,400 sq mi),[^cite_note-mofcom-1] Guangdong is the Chinese province with the [largest GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP), the [largest population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_population) and the [15th-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_area) by area, as well as the [fifth-most populous country subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first-level_administrative_divisions_by_population) and [the 5th largest country subdivision by GDP in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first-level_administrative_divisions_by_GRDP).

Guangdong's economy is the largest of any [provincial-level division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP) in China, with a [GDP](12594.md) of CN¥14.16 trillion (US\$2.0 [trillion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trillion) in GDP nominal) in 2024, contributing approximately 10.5 percent of [mainland China](55079.md)'s economic output.[^cite_note-China_NBS_GDP_data-10] It has a diversified economy, and was known as the starting point of ancient China's [Maritime Silk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Silk_Road).[^cite_note-11] It is home to the production facilities and offices of a wide-ranging set of Chinese and foreign corporations. Guangdong has benefited from its proximity to the financial hub of [Hong Kong](13404.md), which it borders to the south. Guangdong also hosts the largest import and export fair in China, the [Canton Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_Fair), in Guangzhou. The [Pearl River Delta Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta_Economic_Zone), a [Chinese megalopolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megalopolises_in_China), is a core for [high tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_tech), [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing) and [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade). In this zone are two of the [four top Chinese cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_city_tier_system) and the [top two Chinese prefecture-level cities by GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_top_Chinese_cities_by_GDP): Guangzhou and [Shenzhen](83368.md), the first [special economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zones_of_China) in the country. These two are among China's most populous and important cities, and have become two of the world's most populous [megacities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megacities) and [leading financial centres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Financial_Centres_Index) in the [Asia–Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia%E2%80%93Pacific) region.[^cite_note-GFCI-12]

Guangdong surpassed [Henan](166408.md) and [Shandong](105032.md) to become China's [most populous province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_administrative_divisions_by_population) in January 2005, registering 79.1 million [permanent residents](1284324.md) and 31 million migrants who lived in the province for at least six months of the year;[^cite_note-Chinadaily.com-13] the total population was 126,012,510 in the [2020 Chinese census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Chinese_census), accounting for 8.93 percent of mainland China's population.[^cite_note-Census2020-2] This makes it the most populous [first-level administrative subdivision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division) of any country outside [South Asia](21566765.md). The vast majority of the historical province is administered by the People's Republic of China (PRC). [Pratas Island](410487.md) in the South China Sea is part of [Cijin District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cijin_District), [Kaohsiung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaohsiung), Taiwan (ROC); the island was part of Guangdong before the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War).[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

After the unification of [Lingnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingnan) region during the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty), immigrants from the [Central Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhongyuan) moved in and formed a [local culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingnan_culture) with a unique style. With the outward movement of the [Guangdong people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_people), the [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese), [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese) and [Teochew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_Min) [languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Chinese), [music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Guangdong), [cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_cuisine), [opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_opera) and [tea ceremonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gongfu_tea_ceremony) have spread throughout the nation, [Southeast Asia](28741.md), and other countries. Guangdong was also the birthplace of the father of modern China and the founder of the Republic of China, [Sun Yat-sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen). He declared a [military government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Republic_of_China_in_Guangzhou) there in the [Warlord Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warlord_Era). The two [special administrative regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_regions) of Hong Kong and [Macau](19068.md) fall within the scope of Guangdong's cultural influence, and its culture still has profound influences on the [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese) in [Singapore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Singaporeans) and [Malaysia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Chinese), with the vast majority of the Chinese diaspora in the two countries claiming ancestry from Guangdong.

Guangdong is also one of the leading provinces in research and education in China. It hosts 160 [institutions of higher education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_and_colleges_in_Guangdong), ranking first in the [South Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_China) region and second among all Chinese provinces/[municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities), after [Jiangsu](166412.md).[^cite_note-\:02-16] As of 2025, two major cities in the province ranked in the world's top [20 cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_scientific_output) (Guangzhou 6th and Shenzhen 18th) by scientific research output.[^cite_note-17]

## Name

"*Guǎng*" ([traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 廣; [simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 广) means "wide" or "vast" and has been associated with the region since the creation of Guang Prefecture in AD 226.[^cite_note-\:0-18] The name "*Guang*" ultimately came from Guangxin ([廣信](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E5%BB%A3%E4%BF%A1); 广信), an outpost established in [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) near modern [Wuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuzhou), whose name is a reference to an order by [Emperor Wu of Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Wu_of_Han) to "widely bestow favors and sow trust". Together, Guangdong and [Guangxi](47836950.md) are called *[Two Guangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangguang)*.(兩廣; 两广; *liǎng guǎng*) During the Song dynasty, the Two Guangs were formally separated as *Guǎngnán Dōnglù* (廣南東路; 广南东路; 'East [Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_\(administrative_division\)) in Southern Guang') and *Guǎngnán Xīlù* (廣南西路; 广南西路; 'West [Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_\(administrative_division\)) in Southern Guang'), which became abbreviated as *Guǎngdōng Lù* (廣東路; 广东路) and *Guǎngxī Lù* (廣西路; 广西路).

"*Dōng*" ([traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 東; [simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 东) means "east".

In [Iu Mien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iu_Mien), spoken by the [Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao_people_\(Asia\)) ethnic minority, the province is called *_Gongv Dongv_*.

"Canton", though etymologically derived from *_Cantão_* (the Portuguese [transliteration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transliteration) of "Guangdong"), usually by itself refers to the provincial capital [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] Historically, Canton was also used for the province itself,[^cite_note-21] but often either specified as a province (e.g. Canton Province),[^cite_note-22] or written as *Kwangtung* in the [Wade–Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade%E2%80%93Giles) system and now most commonly as *Guangdong* in [Pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin).[^cite_note-23] The local people of the city of [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou) (Canton) and their language are called [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_language) in English. Because of the prestige of [Canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou) and its accent, Cantonese can also be used, in a wider sense, for the [phylogenetically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phylogenetically) related residents and Chinese dialects outside the provincial capital.

## History

### Prehistory

The [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) era began in the [Pearl River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta) (珠江三角洲) 7,000 years before present (BP), with the early period from around 7000 to 5000 BP (c. 5050–3050 BC), and the late period from about 5000 to 3500 BP (c. 3050–1550 BC). In coastal Guangdong, the Neolithic was likely introduced from the middle Yangtze River area (Jiao 2013). In inland Guangdong, the Neolithic appeared in Guangdong 4,600 years before present (BP). The Neolithic in northern inland Guangdong is represented by the Shixia culture (石峽文化), which occurred from 4600 to 4200 BP (c. 2650–2250 BC).[^cite_note-24]

### Imperial

Originally inhabited by a mixture of [tribal groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal_groups) known to the Chinese as the [Baiyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiyue) ("Hundred Yue"), the region first became part of China during the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty). Under the Qin Dynasty, Chinese administration began and along with it, reliable historical records about the region. After establishing the first [unified Chinese empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin's_wars_of_unification), the Qin expanded southwards and set up [Nanhai Commandery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanhai_Commandery) at [Panyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panyu_District), near what is now part of Guangzhou. The region was later controlled by an independent kingdom known as [Nanyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanyue) between the fall of Qin and the reign of [Emperor Wu of Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Wu_of_Han). The [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) administered Guangdong, Guangxi, and northern Vietnam as [Jiaozhi Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhou_\(region\)); southernmost Jiaozhi Province was used as a gateway for traders from the west—as far away as the Roman Empire. Under the [Wu Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Wu) of the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) period, Guangdong was made its own province, the Guang Province, in 226 CE.[^cite_note-\:0-18]

Canton was a prosperous port city along a tropical frontier region beset by disease and wild animals, but rich in oranges, [banyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banyan), bananas, and [lychee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lychee) fruits. They traded slaves, silk and [chinaware](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinaware) with [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persians), [Brahmans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmans) and [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)) in exchange for their renowned medicines and fragrant tropical woods. [Shi'a Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shi'a_Muslims) who had fled persecution in [Khorasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Khorasan) and [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhists) from India lived side by side in the thriving town each erecting their own houses of worship. A foreign quarter sprang up along the [river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_\(China\)) where many traders of diverse backgrounds including Arabs and [Singhalese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhalese_people) took up residence.[^cite_note-University_of_California_Press-25]

The port's importance declined after it was raided by Arabs and Persians in 758 and the foreign residents were at times troubled by the corrupt local officials, sometimes responding violently. During one incident in 684, for example, a merchant vessel's captain murdered a corrupt governor who had used his position to steal from the merchant.[^cite_note-University_of_California_Press-25]

Together with [Guangxi](47836950.md), Guangdong was made part of [Lingnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingnan_culture) Circuit (political division Circuit), or Mountain-South Circuit, in 627 during the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty). The Guangdong part of Lingnan Circuit was renamed [Guangnan East Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangnan_East_Circuit) (廣南東路) in 971 during the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) (960–1279). "Guangnan East" (廣南東) is the source of the name "Guangdong" (廣東; 广东).[^cite_note-26]<sup>: 227 </sup>

As time passed, the demographics of what is now Guangdong gradually shifted to ([Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_people)) Chinese dominance as the populations intermingled due commerce and state-organized colonization from the late 3rd century BCE, which resettled large numbers of northern soldiers, civilians, and convicts/exiles.Wen, Bo; Li, Hui; Lu, Daru; et al. (2004). "Genetic evidence supports demic diffusion of Han culture". *Nature*. **431** (7006): 302–305. [doi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doi_\(identifier\)):[10.1038/nature02878](https://doi.org/10.1038%2Fnature02878)..). From the fall of the Han dynasty onwards, it shifted more abruptly through massive migration from [the north](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_China) during periods of political turmoil and nomadic incursions. For example, internal strife in northern China following [the rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Lushan_Rebellion) of [An Lushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/An_Lushan) resulted in a 75% increase in the population of Guangzhou prefecture between the 740s–750s and 800s–810s.[^cite_note-27] As more migrants arrived, the local population was gradually [assimilated to Han Chinese culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinicization)[^cite_note-28] or displaced.

As [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquest_of_the_Song_dynasty) from the north engaged in their [conquest of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_conquest_of_China) in the 13th century, the [Southern Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Song) court fled southwards from its capital in [Hangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangzhou). The defeat of the Southern Song court by Mongol naval forces in The [Battle of Yamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Yamen) 1279 in Guangdong marked the end of the Southern Song dynasty (960–1279).[^cite_note-29]

During the Mongol [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty), large parts of current Guangdong belonged to [Jiangxi](173817.md).[^cite_note-30] Its present name, "Guangdong" was given in early [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty).

Since the 16th century, Guangdong has had extensive trade links with the rest of the world. European [merchants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merchant) coming northwards via the [Straits of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Straits_of_Malacca) and the [South China Sea](74209.md), particularly the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_people) and [British](19097669.md), traded extensively through Guangzhou. [Macau](19068.md), on the southern coast of Guangdong, was the first European settlement in 1557.

In the late 18th century and early 19th century, coastal Guangdong came to be severely afflicted by pirates, notably those of the [Guangdong Pirate Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong_Pirate_Confederation), around the [Pearl River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River) delta particularly.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] These were suppressed through a joint effort between the [Portuguese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Navy) and [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) forces, however.

Later in the 19th century, the [opium traded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_trade) through Guangzhou triggered the [First Opium War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Opium_War), opening an era of Western [imperialists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperialism)' incursion and intervention in China. In addition to [Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Macau), which was then a [Portuguese colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire), Hong Kong was ceded to the British, and [Kouang-Tchéou-Wan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leased_Territory_of_Guangzhouwan) (modern day area of [Zhanjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhanjiang)) to the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire).

Due to the large number of people that emigrated out of the Guangdong, and in particular the ease of immigration from Hong Kong to other parts of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) (later [British Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Commonwealth)), many overseas Chinese communities have their origins in Guangdong and/or Cantonese culture. In particular, the [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese), [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka), [Teochew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_dialect) dialects have proportionately more speakers among overseas Chinese people than Mandarin-speaking Chinese. Additionally, many Taishanese-speaking Chinese [emigrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_emigration) to Western countries, with the results that many Western versions of Chinese words were derived from the Cantonese dialects rather than through the mainstream Mandarin language, such as "[dim sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dim_sum)". Some Mandarin Chinese words originally of foreign origin also came from the original foreign language by way of Cantonese. For example, the Mandarin word *_níngméng_* ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 柠檬; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 檸檬), meaning "Lemon", came from Cantonese, in which the characters are pronounced as *_lìng mung_*.[^cite_note-33] In the United States, there is a large number of Chinese who are descendants of immigrants from the [county-level city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County-level_city) of [Taishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taishan,_Guangdong) (Toisan in Cantonese), who speak a distinctive dialect related to Cantonese called [Taishanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taishanese) (or Toishanese).

During the 1850s, the [Taiping Heavenly Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Heavenly_Kingdom), whose leader [Hong Xiuquan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hong_Xiuquan) was born in Guangdong and received a pamphlet from a Protestant Christian missionary in Guangdong, was allied with a local Guangdong [Red Turban Rebellion (1854–1856)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Turban_Rebellion_\(1854%E2%80%931856\)). Because of direct contact with the West, Guangdong was the centre of anti-Manchu and anti-imperialist activity. The generally acknowledged founder of modern China, [Sun Yat-sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen), was also from Guangdong.

### 20th century



The Kwangtung Provincial Government in the 1930s

During the early 1920s of the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), Guangdong was the staging area for the [Kuomintang](16903.md) (KMT) to prepare for the [Northern Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Expedition_\(1926%E2%80%931927\)), an effort to bring the various [warlords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warlord_era) of China back under a unified central government. The [Whampoa Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whampoa_Military_Academy) was built near Guangzhou to train military commanders.

At the end of the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War) Guangdong became one of the [Nationalist government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalist_government)'s final footholds in [Mainland China](55079.md), with Guangzhou temporarily serving as the [Kuomintang](16903.md)'s provisional capitol. The [People's Liberation Army](66890.md) seized control of the province after the [retreat of the government of the Republic of China to Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retreat_of_the_government_of_the_Republic_of_China_to_Taiwan).[^cite_note-34]

The new [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) administration issued harsh taxes, requisitioning between 22 and 60 percent of grain annually. However, the local party boss Fang Fang tried to moderate [Chinese land reform policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_Reform_Movement_\(China\)) in order to protect successful businesses in the [Pearl River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta), landholdings by [overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese) seeking to eventually return to the country, and commercial relations with [British Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Hong_Kong). In response [Mao Zedong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mao_Zedong) purged Fang and thousands of cadres from the province in 1952, sending [Tao Zhu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tao_Zhu) to implement a much harsher program under the slogan "Every Village Bleeds, Every Household Fights."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDikötter201376–81-35]

During [Reform and Opening Up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_and_opening_up), Guangdong was supported by the central government to be "one step ahead" of the rest of the country.[^cite_note-\:53-36]<sup>: 43 </sup> Most major cities in Guangdong underwent liberalizing economic reforms in the mid-1980s.[^cite_note-\:53-36]<sup>: 43 </sup> Since Reform and Opening Up, the province has seen extremely rapid economic growth, aided in part by its close trading links with [Hong Kong](13404.md), which borders it. It is now the province with the highest gross domestic product in China.

In 1952, a small section of Guangdong's coastline ([Qinzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinzhou), Lianzhou (now [Hepu County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepu_County)), [Fangchenggang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangchenggang) and [Beihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beihai)) was given to Guangxi, giving it access to the sea. This was reversed in 1955, and then restored in 1965. [Hainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan) Island was originally part of Guangdong, but it was separated into its own province in 1988.

## Geography



A horse in Hongshan village

Guangdong faces the [South China Sea](74209.md) to the south and has a total of 4,300 km (2,700 mi) of coastline. The [Leizhou Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leizhou_Peninsula) is on the southwestern end of the province. There are a few inactive [volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanoes) on Leizhou Peninsula. The [Pearl River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta) is the convergent point of three upstream rivers: the [East River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Jiang), [North River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bei_Jiang), and [West River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_Jiang). The [river delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta) is filled with hundreds of small islands. The province is geographically separated from the north by a few [mountain ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_range) collectively called the [Nan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nan_Mountains) (Nan Ling). The highest peak in the province is [Shikengkong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shikengkong) with an elevation of 6,240 feet (1,900 meters) above sea level. In 2025, researchers confirmed that a [mountaintop crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jinlin_Crater) in [Deqing County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deqing_County,_Guangdong) in Guangdong is the result of an [impact event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_event) estimated to have occurred during the [Holocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene)-era, less than 11,700 years ago.[^cite_note-Chen-37] The crater is the largest known impact crater stemming from an impact during the Holocene era.[^cite_note-38]



Jiangmen beaches, Guangdong



Taishan Xiachuan Island, Guangdong

Guangdong borders [Fujian](88585.md) to the northeast, [Jiangxi](173817.md) and [Hunan](166409.md) to the north, [Guangxi](47836950.md) autonomous region to the west, and [Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Macau](19068.md) [Special Administrative Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_region_\(People's_Republic_of_China\)) to the south. [Hainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainan) is offshore across from the [Leizhou Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leizhou_Peninsula). [Pratas Island](410487.md), which were traditionally governed as part of Guangdong, are part of [Cijin District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cijin_District), [Kaoshiung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaoshiung), Taiwan (ROC).[^cite_note-39] The PRC continues to claim Pratas Island as part of Guangdong under the district of [Chengqu, Shanwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengqu,_Shanwei).[^cite_note-sws-40][^cite_note-chengqu-41]

Cities around the [Pearl River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta) include [Dongguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongguan), [Foshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foshan), [Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou), [Huizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huizhou), [Jiangmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangmen), [Shenzhen](83368.md), [Shunde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shunde), [Taishan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taishan,_Guangdong), [Zhongshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhongshan), and [Zhuhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuhai). Other cities in the province include [Chaozhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaozhou), [Chenghai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chenghai), [Nanhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanhai_District), [Shantou](268030.md), [Shaoguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaoguan), [Zhanjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhanjiang), [Zhaoqing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhaoqing), [Yangjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangjiang), and [Yunfu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunfu).

Guangdong has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cfa* inland, *Cwa* along the coast). Winters are short, mild, and relatively dry, while summers are long, hot, and very wet. Average daily highs in Guangzhou in January and July are 18 and 33 °C (64 and 91 °F), although the humidity makes it feel hotter in summer. Frost is rare on the coast but may happen a few days each winter.

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Guangdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Guangdong)

In 2022, Guangdong's GDP was 13.57 trillion [RMB](66958.md) (\$1.9 trillion in GDP nominal, \$3.78 trillion in [PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity)), with a per capita GDP of CN¥102,465 (US\$15,234 in nominal or US\$25,016 in [PPP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity)).[^cite_note-data2022-9] It is the [richest province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Chinese_provincial-level_divisions_by_GDP_per_capita) in [South Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_China) region and the seventh richest among all provinces by GDP per capita. Guangdong has been the largest province by GDP since 1989 in [Mainland China](55079.md).[^cite_note-42] Its GDP exceeded that of Australia (\$1.70 trillion) and South Korea (\$1.67 trillion), the world's 12th and 13th [largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)), respectively.[^cite_note-IMF-43] If it was a country, Guangdong would be the [12th-largest economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(nominal\)) as of 2022 and the [11th most populous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_population).[^cite_note-data2022-9] Compared to country subdivisions in dollar terms, Guangdong's GDP in nominal is larger than all but four [country subdivisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_country_subdivisions_by_GDP_\(nominal\)): [California](5407.md), [Texas](29810.md), [New York State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_State), and [England](9316.md). Compared to country subdivisions in PPP terms, Guangdong's GDP is larger than all, except California.[^cite_note-IMF-43] By PPP terms, as of 2022, Guangdong's economy ranked between Turkey and Italy with a GDP of \$3.35 trillion and US\$3.06 trillion respectively, the [11th and 12th largest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_GDP_\(PPP\)) respectively.[^cite_note-IMF-43]

After the [communist revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Revolution_\(1949\)) and until the start of the [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) reforms in 1978, Guangdong was an economic backwater, although a large underground, service-based economy has always existed. Economic development policies encouraged industrial development in the interior provinces which were weakly joined to [Guangdong via transportation links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Guangdong). The government policy of economic [autarky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autarky) made Guangdong's access to the ocean irrelevant.

Deng Xiaoping's open door policy radically changed the economy of the province, which was able to take advantage of its access to the sea, proximity to Hong Kong, and historical links to overseas Chinese communities. Guangdong was one of the first provinces to receive permission from the central government to attract foreign investment. Until the 1990s, when China's taxation system was reformed, the province also benefited from preferential fiscal policies and relatively low levels of revenue remittance to the central government because it had historically been classified as an economically less-developed province.[^cite_note-46]



Buildings and tourist attractions in Shenzhen

Guangdong's economic boom began with the early 1990s and has since spread to neighboring provinces, and also pulled their populations inward. The economic growth of Guangdong owes much to the low-value-added manufacturing which characterized (and in many ways still defines) the province's economy following [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping)'s reforms. Guangdong is not only China's largest exporter of goods, it is the country's largest importer as well.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective1-47]

The province is now one of the richest in the nation, with the most billionaires in mainland China,[^cite_note-48] the highest GDP among all the provinces, although wage growth has only recently begun to rise due to a large influx of migrant workers from neighboring provinces. By 2015, the local government of Guangdong hopes that the service industry will account for more than 50 percent of the provinces GDP and high-tech manufacturing another 20 percent.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective1-47]

In 2021, Guangdong's primary, secondary, and tertiary industries were worth 534 billion RMB (US\$79.4 billion), 5.28 trillion RMB (US\$785.6 billion), and 7.09 trillion RMB (US\$1.05 trillion), respectively.[^cite_note-data2022-9] Guangdong contributes approximately 10.6% of the total national economic output.[^cite_note-data2022-9] Now, it has three of the six [Special Economic Zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Economic_Zones_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China): [Shenzhen](83368.md), [Shantou](268030.md) and [Zhuhai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuhai). The affluence of Guangdong, however, remains very concentrated near the [Pearl River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta).

### Urbanization



[Guangzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou) is one of the largest cities in the People's Republic of China.

In 2024, Guangdong's population was 75.9% urban and 24.1% rural.[^cite_note-data2022-9]

### Genealogy

Guangdong is the ancestral home of large numbers of [overseas Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Chinese). Most of the railroad laborers in [Canada](5042916.md), the [Western United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_United_States) and [Panama](22997.md) in the 19th century came from Guangdong, especially the [Siyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siyi) area. Many people from the region also traveled to [California](5407.md) and other parts of the [United States](3434750.md) during the [gold rush of 1849](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Gold_Rush), and also to [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Australia) during its [gold rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_gold_rushes) a decade or so later.

### Languages and ethnicities

The majority of the province's population is [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese), though the Han population is so diverse that the province has been called the "treasure trove of regional languages" (方言寶庫).[^cite_note-64] Within the Han Chinese, the largest subgroup in Guangdong are the [Cantonese people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_people), with significant [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_people) and [Teoswa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_people) populations east of the Pearl River Delta. Guangdong is also home to small [Mien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao_people_\(Asia\)), [She](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/She_people), [Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_people), [Li](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Chinese), and [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people) minorities.

#### Yue Chinese

Main article: [Yue Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yue_Chinese)

Guangdong is the traditional heartland of Yue Chinese ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 粤语; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 粵語; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *yuèyǔ*; [Jyutping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyutping): *jyut<sup>6</sup> jyu<sup>5</sup>*), which has a high degree of internal diversity. The vast majority of these speakers live at or west the [Pearl River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta). A total of 35,810,000 Yue Chinese speakers live in Guangdong.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELi2012160-65]

[Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese) and other Yue varieties spoken at the delta such as [Weitou dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weitou_dialect) and [Shiqi dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiqi_dialect) make up the greatest number of speakers, numbering at around 20,720,000 speakers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELi2012161-66] Due to the large overseas population and cultural impact of [Cantopop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantopop) and Cantonese television shows, Cantonese is a well-known variety of Chinese throughout the world.

[Sze Yap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siyi_Yue) or Siyi Yue, including [Hoisanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoisanese), is spoken in much of [Jiangmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangmen) prefecture, numbering at around 3,880,000 speakers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELi2012161-66] Siyi was once the representative variety of Chinese in many [Chinese American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_American) communities.

#### Hakka Chinese

Main article: [Hakka Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese)

The highlands of the [Jiangxi](173817.md)-[Fujian](88585.md)-Guangdong tripoint are the traditional heartland of the Hakka Chinese ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 客家话; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 客家話; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *kèjiāhuà*; [Jyutping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyutping): *haak<sup>3</sup> gaa<sup>1</sup> waa<sup>6-2</sup>*; [Moiyenese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meizhou_dialect): *hag<sup>5</sup> ga<sup>1</sup> fa<sup>4</sup>*)-speaking people, and [Meizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meizhou) is often dubbed the capital of Hakka culture. Downhill Hakka migrations started in the [early modern period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_modern_period), and due to them being newcomers to the lowlands, they were dubbed "guest families" by the original inhabitants (the [Puntis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punti)). There are around 20,000,000 Neo-Hakka speakers in Guangdong, of which 2,000,000 live significantly west of the traditional Hakka area.

#### Min Chinese

Main articles: [Teochew Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_Min) and [Leizhou Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leizhou_Min)

Teoswa or Chaoshan Min ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 潮汕话; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 潮汕話; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *cháoshànhuà*; [Jyutping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyutping): *ciu<sup>4</sup> saan<sup>3</sup> waa<sup>6-2</sup>*; [Peng'im](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peng'im): *diê<sup>5</sup> suan<sup>1</sup> uê<sup>7</sup>*) is spoken primarily in the [Chaoshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaoshan) area, that is to say, [Chaozhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaozhou), [Jieyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jieyang), [Shantou](268030.md), and [Shanwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanwei) prefectures, by around 14,120,000 speakers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELi2012161-66] It is a [Southern Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Min) branch, but has little mutual intelligibility with [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien).

Leizhou Min ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 黎话; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 黎話; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *líhuà*; [Jyutping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyutping): *leoi<sup>4</sup> waa<sup>6-2</sup>*; Leizhounese: \[lɔi˨ ue˧˥]) is spoken primarily in the Leizhou peninsula of [Zhanjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhanjiang) prefecture by around 4,510,000 speakers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELi2012161-66] It is closely related to [Hainanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hainanese).

#### Other Chinese

Around 500,000 speakers of [Shaozhou Tuhua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaozhou_Tuhua) live in small communities in [Shaoguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaoguan) prefecture, typically surrounded by Hakka speakers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELi2012161-66] These varieties have been observed to be similar to Hakka, and have been dubbed "Paleo-Hakka" by, for instance, [W. South Coblin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/W._South_Coblin).

There are also around 40,000 [Southwestern Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Mandarin) speakers in Guangdong, with around half of them being remnants of Northern *[juntun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juntun?action=edit\&redlink=1)* \[[zh](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E8%BB%8D%E5%B1%AF)] that date back to the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty). These communities largely live in small villages in coastal eastern Guangdong in places such as [Haifeng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haifeng) and [Huidong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huidong_County,_Guangdong) counties. The other half live in parts of [Lechang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lechang) close to [Hunan](166409.md) province, which explains the Mandarin language they use.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELi2012161-66]

### Gender ratio

Guangdong has a highly unbalanced gender ratio that is among the highest of all provinces in China. According to a 2009 study published in [*The British Medical Journal*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_BMJ), in the 1–4 age group, there are over 130 boys for every 100 girls.[^cite_note-67]

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of Guangdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Guangdong)

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of provincial leaders of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_provincial_leaders_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China).

Like all [governing institutions in mainland China](23239.md), Guangdong has a parallel party-government system,[^cite_note-PoliticalSystem2-70] in which the [CCP Guangdong Provincial Committee Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_Secretary_of_Guangdong) outranks the [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Guangdong).[^cite_note-71] The [CCP Guangdong Provincial Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong_Provincial_Committee_of_the_Chinese_Communist_Party) acts as the top policy-formulation body, and has control over the [Guangdong Provincial People's Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong_Provincial_People's_Government).

### Law enforcement and emergency services

Provincial law enforcement in Guangdong is provided by the [Guangdong Provincial Public Security Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong_Public_Security_Department).[^cite_note-72] The [People's Armed Police Guangdong Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Armed_Police_Guangdong_Corps?action=edit\&redlink=1) provides paramilitary law enforcement and disaster relief in the province[^cite_note-73] while the [Guangdong Provincial Fire and Rescue Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong_Provincial_Fire_and_Rescue_Department) provides firefighting and rescue services in the province.[^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75]

The primary domestic intelligence and security agency in Guangdong is the [Guangdong Provincial state security department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong_State_Security_Department).[^cite_note-76]

Corrections facilities in Guangdong are managed by the [Guangdong Prison Administrative Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong_Prison_Administrative_Bureau).[^cite_note-77]

### Dissent

According to [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House)'s China Dissent Monitor, Guangdong accounted for 17% of dissent events in the first quarter of 2024 – over 100 events despite heavy [Censorship in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship_in_China).[^cite_note-78] In 2024, Freedom House rated China as below zero on political rights (−2 out of 40).[^cite_note-79]

### Relations with Hong Kong and Macau

[Hong Kong](13404.md) and [Macau](19068.md), while historically parts of Guangdong before becoming colonies of the United Kingdom and Portugal, respectively, are [special administrative regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_administrative_region_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) (SARs). Furthermore, the [Basic Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basic_Law) of both SARs explicitly forbid [provincial governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China) from intervening in local politics. As a result, many issues with Hong Kong and Macau, such as border policy and water rights, have been settled by negotiations between the SARs' governments and the [Guangdong provincial government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Guangdong).

## Media

Guangdong and the greater Guangzhou area are served by several [Radio Guangdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Guangdong) stations, [Guangdong Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong_Television), [Southern Television Guangdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Television_Guangdong), [Shenzhen Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhen_Television), and [Guangzhou Television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangzhou_Television). There is an English programme produced by [Radio Guangdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Guangdong) which broadcasts information about this region to the entire world through the [WRN Broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WRN_Broadcast).

The [har gow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Har_gow) are classical Cantonese dumplings served as dim sum.

The central region, which is also the political and economic center, is populated predominantly by [Yue Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yue_Chinese) (Cantonese) speakers, though the influx in the last three decades of millions of [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese)-speaking immigrants has slightly diminished Cantonese linguistic dominance. This region is associated with [Cantonese cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_cuisine); [dim sum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dim_sum) is one of these which divides Cantonese food into small portions and is served with in small dishes. [Cantonese opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_opera) is a form of [Chinese opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_opera) popular in Cantonese speaking areas. Related Yue dialects are spoken in most of the western half of the province.

The area comprising the cities of [Chaozhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaozhou), [Shantou](268030.md) and [Jieyang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jieyang) in coastal east Guangdong, known as [Chaoshan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaoshan), forms its own cultural sphere. The [Teochew people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_people) here, along with [Hailufeng Hoklo people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoklo_people) in [Shanwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanwei), speak [Haklau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haklau_Min), which is a [Southern Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Min) variety closely related to mainstream [Hokkien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hokkien) and their cuisine is [Teochew cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_cuisine). [Teochew opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teochew_opera) is also well-known and has a unique form.

The [Hakka people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_people) live in large areas of Guangdong, including [Huizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huizhou), [Meizhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meizhou), [Shenzhen](83368.md), [Heyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heyuan), [Shaoguan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaoguan) and other areas. Much of the Eastern part of Guangdong is populated by the Hakka people except for the Chaozhou and Hailufeng area. Hakka culture include [Hakka cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_cuisine), Han opera ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 汉剧; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 漢劇), Hakka *Hanyue* and *sixian* (traditional instrumental music) and Hakka folk songs (客家山歌).



Jieyang architecture

The outcast [Tanka people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanka_people) traditionally live on boats throughout the coasts and rivers of Guangdong and much of Southern China.

[Zhanjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhanjiang) in southern Guangdong is dominated by [Leizhounese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leizhou_Min), a variety of [Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Chinese); Cantonese and Hakka are also spoken there.

Mandarin is the language used in education and government and in areas where there are migrants from other provinces, above all in Shenzhen. Cantonese maintains a strong and dominant position in common usage and media, even in eastern areas of the province where the local languages and dialects are non-Yue ones.

Guangdong is notable for being the birthplace of many [Xiangqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiangqi) (Chinese chess) grandmasters including [Lü Qin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%C3%BC_Qin), Yang Guanli, Cai Furu and [Xu Yinchuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xu_Yinchuan).

## Education and research

As of 2022, Guangdong hosts 160 institutions of higher education, ranking first in [South Central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_China) region and 2nd among all Chinese provinces/municipalities after [Jiangsu](166412.md) (168).[^cite_note-\:02-16] Guangdong is also the seat of 14 adult higher education institutions.[^cite_note-\:02-16] Many universities and colleges are located in major cities like Shenzhen and Guangzhou. Guangzhou, the capital of Guangdong, hosts 83 institutions of higher education (excluding adult colleges), ranking 1st in [South China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China) region and 2nd (tie) nationwide after [Beijing](18603746.md).[^cite_note-80] [Guangdong Province Department of Education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong_Province_Department_of_Education) is the department of the provincial government that oversees education.

As of 2025, two major cities in the province ranked in the top [20 cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_by_scientific_output) in the world (Guangzhou 6th and Shenzhen 18th) by scientific research output, as tracked by the [Nature Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature_Index).[^cite_note-81]
