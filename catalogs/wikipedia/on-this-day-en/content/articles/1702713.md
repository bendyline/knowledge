**Mysore district**, officially **Mysuru district**, is an administrative district located in the southern part of the state of [Karnataka](16880.md), India. It is the administrative headquarters of [Mysore division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_division).[^cite_note-4] and [Mandya District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandya_district) was carved out of the original larger Mysore district in 1939. During state reorganisation, [Kollegala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kollegal) was transferred from [Coimbatore district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coimbatore_district). Then in 1997 a new district, [Chamarajanagar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamarajanagar_district), was carved out of [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore). Mysore district is bounded by Chamrajanagar district to the southeast, [Mandya district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandya_district) to the east and northeast, [Wayanad district](407429.md) of [Kerala](4349459.md) to the south, [Kodagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodagu) district to the west, and [Hassan district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_district) to the north.[^cite_note-5]

This district has a prominent place in the history of Karnataka; [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore) was ruled by the [Wodeyars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wodeyar) from the year 1399 till the independence of India in the year 1947. It features many tourist destinations, from [Mysore Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Palace) to [Nagarhole National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarhole_National_Park). It is the third-most populous district in Karnataka (out of [31](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_in_Karnataka)), after [Bangalore Urban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore_Urban_district) and [Belgaum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgaum).[^cite_note-districtcensus-6]

## Etymology

Mysore district gets its name from the city of [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore) which is also the headquarters of the district. The original name of this city was Mahishapura derived from a demon named [Mahishasura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahishasura).[^cite_note-7] A statue of Mahishasura and a temple dedicated to [Goddess Chamundeshwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durga) on the top of [Chamundi Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamundi_Hill) near Mysore city, relate to the legend of its origin.

## History



Mysore Palace&#x20;

The earliest known reference of rulers in Mysore district are the [Gangas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gangas) who during the rule of King Avinitha (469-529 CE), moved the capital from [Kolar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolar,_Karnataka) to [Talakadu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talakadu) on the banks of the river [Kaveri](177299.md) in the [Tirumakudalu Narasipura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirumakudal_Narsipur) taluk.[^cite_note-talakad-8] Talakadu remained their regal capital till the end of Ganga rule in the early 11th century. Gangas ruled over a greater part of Mysore district, then known by the name of *Gangavadi*. In the end of the 8th century, the Rashtrakuta king [Dhruva Dharavarsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhruva_Dharavarsha) defeated the Ganga king [Shivamara II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivamara_II) and wrested Gangavadi from him. Gangavadi came under the governorship of [Kambarasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kambarasa), the son of Dhruva Dharavarsha. Gangas who were overthrown from Gangavadi, had to wait till their king Nitimarga Ereganga (853–869 CE) won a victory against the Rashtrakutas at Rajaramudu. Seeing the increasing might of the Gangas, the Rashtrakuta King [Amoghavarsha I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amoghavarsha_I) gave his daughter Revakanimmadi in marriage to the son of Ereganga, [Butuga II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butuga_II) who became the ruler of Gangavadi. Gangas ruled over Gangavadi till the Ganga king, Rakkasa Ganga (985–1024 CE) was defeated by the [Cholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chola).[^cite_note-hist-9]

In the year 1117, [Vishnuvardhana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnuvardhana), the great king of [Hoysala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoysala) dynasty seized Gangavathi and its capital [Talakad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talakad) from the Cholas. To commemorate this achievement, Vishnuvardhana built the Keerthinarayana temple at Talakad.[^cite_note-vishnu-10] Gangavadi was ruled by the Hoysalas till the death of their last ruler, [Veera Ballala III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veera_Ballala_III) after which Gangavadi became a part of the [Vijayanagara Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagar_Empire). In 1399, Yaduraya established the Wodeyar dynasty at Mysore.[^cite_note-hist2-11] It remained as a feudatory to the Vijayanagara Empire owing allegiance to the Vijayanagara kings and the Vijayanagara representative at [Srirangapatna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srirangapatna), till the fall of the [Vijayanagara Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vijayanagar_Empire) in 1565 CE. In the vacuum that was created, Raja Wodeyar I (1578–1617) established control and became the first major ruler of the Wodeyar family. He defeated the Vijayanagara representative in a battle at Kesare near Mysore, shifted his capital from Mysore to Srirangapatna in 1610 AD.[^cite_note-yadu-12]

The Wodeyars continued to rule over Mysore till the reign of Krishnaraja Wodeyar II (1734–1766), when [Hyder Ali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyder_Ali) Khan and his son [Tipu Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipu_Sultan) became the virtual rulers of Mysore.[^cite_note-hist2-11] Though there were Wodeyar kings during the rule of Hyder Ali and Tipu Sultan, they were mere figureheads. With the death of Tipu Sultan in 1799 under the hands of the [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India), the Wodeyars were reinstated to the throne of Mysore and the capital was shifted back to Mysore. Prince Krishnaraja Wodeyar III who was just 5 years old was installed on the throne of Mysore in 1799.[^cite_note-hist2-11] Wodeyars were the subsidiaries of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) and had to pay annual subsidies. During the rule of Krishnaraja Wodeyar III, the British took the kingdom back from Wodeyars in 1831 under the pretext that the Wodeyar king did not pay the annual subsidy.[^cite_note-herald-13] Commissioners were appointed to rule over the Mysore kingdom. Mark Cubbon (Cubbon Road and Cubbon Park in [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore) city are named after him) and L. B. Bowring (Bowring Hospital in Bangalore city is named after him) were the prominent British Commissioners who ruled over Mysore. However, the Wodeyar kings raised a plea against this with the British Parliament who gave a ruling favour of the Wodeyars. In 1881, [Chamaraja Wodeyar IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamarajendra_Wadiyar_X) (son of Krishnaraja Wodeyar III and Wodeyar king since 1868) was given back the reins of the Mysore kingdom from the British.[^cite_note-herald-13] The Wodeyars continued to rule over the Mysore Kingdom, till the rule of [Jayachamaraja Wodeyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayachamaraja_Wodeyar) who, in the year 1947, merged his kingdom into the new dominion of independent India. He remained as a Maharaja till India became a republic in the year 1950 after which he was anointed as a Raja Pramukh (a constitutional position) as the head of Mysore state till 1956. In 1956, after the reorganisation of Indian states, the Mysore state was born and Jayachamaraja Wodeyar was made as the governor of this state – the position he held until 1964.

## Geography



Undulating terrain near [Kabini reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabini_River) in SW Mysore district

Mysore district is located between latitude 11°45' to 12°40' N and longitude 75°57' to 77°15' E. It is bounded by [Mandya district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandya_district) to the northeast, [Chamrajanagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamarajanagar_district) district to the southeast, [Kerala](4349459.md) state to the south, [Kodagu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kodagu) district to the west, and [Hassan district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_district) to the north. It has an area of 6,854 km<sup>2</sup> (ranked 12th in the state). The administrative centre of Mysore District is Mysore City. The district is a part of [Mysore division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_division). Prior to 1998, Mysore district also contained the Chamarajanagar district before that area was separated off.

The district lies on the undulating table land of the southern [Deccan plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau), within the watershed of the [Kaveri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaveri_River), which flows through the northwestern and eastern parts of the district. The [Krishna Raja Sagara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_Raja_Sagara) reservoir, which was formed by building a dam across the Kaveri, lies on the northern edge of the district. [Nagarhole National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarhole_National_Park) lies partly in Mysore district and partly in adjacent Kodagu District.

The district is undulating with only a few isolated peaks. These include [Chamundi Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamundi_Hill) (1,080 m (3,540 ft) above [MSL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_sea_level)) and Bettadapura hill (1,320 m (4,330 ft) above MSL) in Periyapatna Taluk. The latter is the highest mountain peak in Mysore district.

### Climate

The temperature in the district varies from 15 °C in winters to 35 °C in summers. Mysore district receives an average rainfall of 885  mm.[^cite_note-rain-14]

In 2022, Mysore district received an average annual rainfall of 1,318 millimetres (51.9 in) (an excess of 57%). The top three highest rainfall receiving hoblis were:

1. [Mirle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirle) - 1,657 millimetres (65.2 in)

2. [Bannur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bannur) - 1,481 millimetres (58.3 in)

3. [Hosa Agrahara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bherya) - 1,454 millimetres (57.2 in)&#x20;

### Geology

The types of soil found in this district are red soils (red gravelly loam soil, red loam soil, red gravelly clay soil, red clay soil), lateritic soil, deep black soil, saline alluvio-colluvial soil and brown forest soil.[^cite_note-geo-16] Some of the minerals found in this district are [kyanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyanite), [sillimanite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sillimanite), [quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz), [magnesite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnesite), [chromite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromite), [soapstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soapstone), [felsite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felsite), [corundum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corundum), [graphite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graphite), [limestone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limestone), [dolomite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomite_\(rock\)), siliconite and [dunite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunite)[^cite_note-mines-17]

According to the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_census_of_India) Mysore district has a [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_India) of 3,001,127,[^cite_note-districtcensus-6] roughly equal to the nation of [Armenia](10918072.md)[^cite_note-cia-21][^cite_note-renamed_from_1901_on_20200308060728-22] or the US state of [Mississippi](16949861.md).[^cite_note-23] This gives it a ranking of 125th in India (out of a total of [640](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_India)).[^cite_note-districtcensus-6] The district has a population density of 437 inhabitants per square kilometre (1,130/sq mi) .[^cite_note-districtcensus-6] Its [population growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning_in_India) over the decade 2001-2011 was 13.39%.[^cite_note-districtcensus-6] Mysore has a [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio) of 982 [females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_India) for every 1000 males,[^cite_note-districtcensus-6] and a [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_India) of 72.56%. 41.50% of the population lives in urban areas. Scheduled Castes and Scheduled Tribes make up 17.88% and 11.15% of the population respectively.[^cite_note-districtcensus-6] Some of the more ancient forest tribes are [Jenu Kuruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jenu_Kuruba), [Betta Kuruba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betta_Kuruba), [Paniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paniya_people), [Yereva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ravula) and [Soliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soliga_people).[^cite_note-tribe-24]

[Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu) constitute 87.70% of the population with Muslims making up 9.68% of the population; the remaining part of the population is made up by Christians, [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhists) and other religious groups.[^cite_note-religion-19]

At the time of the 2011 census, 80.81% of the population spoke [Kannada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kannada), 9.27% [Urdu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urdu), 2.91% [Telugu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telugu_language), 2.22% [Tamil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamil_language), 1.12% [Marathi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marathi_language) and 0.92% [Hindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindi) as their first language.[^cite_note-languages-20]

## Government and administration

Mysore district is divided into three subdivisions, [Nanjangud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjangud), Mysore and [Hunsur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunsur). The Mysore district administration is headed by the Deputy Commissioner who also has the additional role of a District Magistrate. Assistant Commissioners, Tahsildars, Shirastedars (revenue official at Tahsil level), Revenue inspectors and Village Accountants help the Deputy Commissioner in the administration of the district. Mysore city is the headquarters of the district. It lies on the north eastern part of the district and is well known for its beautiful palaces and also for the festivities that take place during [Dasara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore_Dasara).

### Seat of administration



The old DC office, a heritage building



The new DC office

The seat of the administrative head for 128 years was a heritage building constructed in 1895 on Krishnaraja Boulevard. In order to decongest the old office, a new office was constructed on a 15 acres (6.1 ha) plot in [Siddhartha Nagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddhartha_Nagar) at a cost of Rs. 670 million (67 crores). The three storey building resembles the [Lalitha Mahal Palace Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lalitha_Mahal). It has a built-up area of 13,720 m<sup>2</sup> (147,700 sq ft). In addition, there is basement parking of 9,150 m<sup>2</sup> (98,500 sq ft). The total area of 23,000 m<sup>2</sup> (250,000 sq ft) is 3-4 times larger than the old office. The foundation stone was laid in October 2016 and building was inaugurated on 10 March 2018, when [Siddaramaiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siddaramaiah) was the Chief Minister. However, with the change of government a few weeks later, the office lay vacant for several years. On 8 June 2023, soon after Siddaramaiah became Chief Minister again, the Deputy Commissioner shifted his office to the new building.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## Economy



Banana and coconut cultivated on the bank of the Kabini reservoir

### Agriculture

Agriculture is the backbone of the economy of this district as it is with the rest of India. Though the agriculture is highly dependent on the rainfall, about 45% of the sown area is irrigated. The principal means of irrigation are canals from the rivers Kaveri and [Kabini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabini) rivers and tubewells.[^cite_note-MysDistStats21-22-41]<sup>: pp\:74-76 </sup> According to the 2001 census, about 3,25,823 farmers are involved in cultivation in this district. In the year 2001–2002, Mysore district yielded a food grain production of 608,596 Tonnes which is a contribution of 6.94% of the total food grain production in the state for the year.[^cite_note-agri-42] Some of the important crops grown here are cotton, [grams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chickpea), [groundnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut), [jowar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jowar), maize, [ragi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finger_millet), rice, [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), [sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower) and [tur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tur_\(bean\)).[^cite_note-crops-43] Horticulture is another area contributing significantly to the economy. The principal fruits by area are [banana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana) and [mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango). By production, banana, mango, [papaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya) and [sapota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manilkara_zapota) are the main fruits.[^cite_note-MysDistStats21-22-41]<sup>: pp\:83, 94 </sup>

### Industries



Infosys in Hebbal Industrial Area, Hootagalli, Mysore

Industries in Mysore district are mainly concentrated around the cities of Mysore and Nanjangud. Karnataka Industrial Areas Development Board (KIADB) has established two industrial estates as Mysore and Nanjangud and established six industrial areas in Mysore district to encourage Industrial Development of the district. These are located at Belagola, Belawadi, Hebbal (Electronic City) and Hootagalli of Mysore Industrial estate and the industrial areas Nanjangud and Thandavapura of Nanjangud industrial estate.[^cite_note-indarea-44]

The first major industry to be set up in Mysore with the partnership of the Maharaja of Mysore when Mysore was still an industrially backward district in 1960 was the now defunct [Ideal Jawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ideal_Jawa) India Ltd. motorcycle factory with technical collaboration with [Jawa Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawa_Motors) of [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia).

### Information technology

Mysore is proving to be the next IT hub in [Karnataka](16880.md) after the phenomenal success of Bangalore. The government of India has recognised Mysore as number one among the 20 Tier II cities of India for the promotion of the IT industry.[^cite_note-it-45] Currently, all of the IT related industries are concentrated around Mysore city. The Software Technology Park (STP) in [Mysore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysore) was inaugurated in 1998 by the prime minister of India, [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee). As of August 2006, there are 42 companies registered with the STP. Software exports from Mysore were expected to double to about Rs. 850 crores in the financial year 2006-07 from previous year's exports of Rs. 400 crores.[^cite_note-it-45] Some of the major IT companies located here are:

- [Wipro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wipro) Infotech

- Aris Global Pvt Ltd

- WeP Peripherals Ltd. (earlier known as Wipro ePeripherals Ltd.)

- [Infosys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infosys)

- Software Paradigms (India)

- [Larsen & Toubro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larsen_&_Toubro)

- [Infomaze](https://www.infomazeelite.com/)

### Tourism



Srikanteshwara Temple, Nanjangud

Tourism is another big industry in Mysore. Its importance as a tourist destination was evident when it was selected as the venue for the Karnataka Tourism Expo in 2006.[^cite_note-expo-46] Though Mysore city is well known as a tourist place, other parts the district are yet to see growth in tourism. However, the tourism department plans to develop other areas like Nanjangud, Bettadapura, Hedathali, Kapadi, Mudukuthore Betta, Mugur and Tirumakudalu Narasipura as tourist places.[^cite_note-tour-47]
