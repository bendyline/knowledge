**Pittsburgh** ([/ˈpɪtsbɜːrɡ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*PITS-burg*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a city in [Allegheny County, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_County,_Pennsylvania), United States, and its [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat). Located in [southwestern Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Pennsylvania) where the [Allegheny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_River) and [Monongahela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongahela_River) Rivers meet to form the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River), it had a population of 302,971 at the 2020 census, making it the [second-most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Pennsylvania#Municipalities) in Pennsylvania after [Philadelphia](50585.md). The [Pittsburgh metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Pittsburgh) has over 2.43 million people, making it the largest in the Ohio Valley and [Appalachia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachia), the [second-largest in Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_metropolitan_areas), and the [28th-largest in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area#United_States) The greater [Pittsburgh–Weirton–Steubenville combined statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh%E2%80%93New_Castle%E2%80%93Weirton_combined_statistical_area) includes parts of [Ohio](22199.md) and [West Virginia](32905.md).

Pittsburgh is known as "the Steel City" for its dominant role in the [history of the U.S. steel industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_iron_and_steel_industry_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-Pittsburgh_Names-8] It developed as a vital link of the [Atlantic coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mid-Atlantic_\(United_States\)) and [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States#European_exploration_and_early_settlement), as the mineral-rich [Allegheny Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_Mountains) led to the region being contested by the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_colonial_empire#First_French_colonial_empire) and [British](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire) empires, [Virginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia), [Whiskey Rebels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskey_Rebellion), and [Civil War raiders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_in_the_American_Civil_War).[^cite_note-9] For part of the 20th century, Pittsburgh was behind only [New York City](645042.md) and [Chicago](6886.md) in corporate headquarters employment; it had the most U.S. stockholders per capita.[^cite_note-10] [Deindustrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deindustrialisation_by_country#United_States) in the late 20th century resulted in massive layoffs among [blue-collar workers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-collar_worker) as steel and other heavy industries declined, coinciding with several Pittsburgh-based corporations moving out of the city.[^cite_note-diaspora-11] However, the city divested from steel and, since the 1990s, Pittsburgh has focused its energies on the healthcare, education, and technology industries.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-Lubove-1995-13]

Pittsburgh is home to large medical providers, including the [University of Pittsburgh Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh_Medical_Center) and [Allegheny Health Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_Health_Network), as well as [68 colleges and universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_colleges_and_universities_in_Pittsburgh), including [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), [Duquesne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duquesne_University) and the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh).[^cite_note-edu-14] The area has served as the federal agency headquarters for [cyber defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CERT_Coordination_Center), [software engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_Engineering_Institute), [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Robotics_Engineering_Center), [energy research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Energy_Technology_Laboratory), and the [nuclear navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bettis_Atomic_Power_Laboratory).[^cite_note-15] The city is home to ten [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) companies and seven of the 300 largest U.S. law firms.

Pittsburgh is sometimes called the "City of Bridges" for its [446 bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bridges_of_Pittsburgh).[^cite_note-Pittsburgh_Names-8] Its rich industrial history left the area with renowned cultural institutions, including the [Carnegie Museums of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Museums_of_Pittsburgh), [Pittsburgh Zoo & Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Zoo_&_Aquarium), [Phipps Conservatory and Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phipps_Conservatory_and_Botanical_Gardens), the [National Aviary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aviary), and a diverse [cultural district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_District,_Pittsburgh).[^cite_note-Ritenbaugh-16] The city's major league [professional sports teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Pittsburgh) include the [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers), [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins), and [Pittsburgh Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pirates). [Jehovah's Witnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jehovah's_Witnesses) traces its earliest origins to the city. In 2009, it hosted the [G20 summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_G20_Pittsburgh_summit).

## Etymology

Main article: [Name of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_Pittsburgh)

Pittsburgh was named in 1758, by Scottish General [John Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Forbes_\(British_Army_officer\)), in honor of British statesman [William Pitt, 1st Earl of Chatham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Pitt,_1st_Earl_of_Chatham). As Forbes was a [Scotsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotsman), he probably pronounced the name [/ˈpɪtsbərə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*PITS-bər-ə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key) (similar to [Edinburgh](9602.md)).[^cite_note-spell-17][^cite_note-18]

Pittsburgh was incorporated as a [borough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borough_\(Pennsylvania\)) on April 22, 1794, with the following Act:[^cite_note-19] "Be it enacted by the [Pennsylvania State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_Senate) and [Pennsylvania House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_House_of_Representatives) of the Commonwealth of Pennsylvania ... by the authority of the same, that the said town of Pittsburgh shall be ... erected into a borough, which shall be called the borough of Pittsburgh for ever."[^cite_note-20]

From 1891 to 1911, the city's name was federally recognized as "Pittsburg", though use of the final *h* was retained during this period by the city government and other local organizations.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-spell-17] After a public campaign, the federal decision to drop the *h* was reversed.[^cite_note-spell-17] The *[Pittsburg Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburg_Press)* continued spelling the city without an *h* until 1921.[^cite_note-22]

### Native Americans

The area of the Ohio headwaters was long inhabited by the [Shawnee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawnee) and several other settled groups of [Native Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_the_Americas).[^cite_note-23] The [Seneca people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seneca_people) called the confluence of the rivers *Diondega* or *Jaödeogë’*.[^cite_note-v224-24][^cite_note-Chafe2013-25] [Shannopin's Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shannopin's_Town) was an 18th-century [Lenape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenape) (Delaware) town located roughly from where [Penn Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penn_Avenue) is today, below the mouth of Two Mile Run, from 30th Street to 39th Street. According to [George Croghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Croghan), the town was situated on the south bank of the Allegheny, nearly opposite what is now known as Washington's Landing, formerly [Herr's Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herrs_Island,_Pittsburgh), in what is now the [Lawrenceville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrenceville_\(Pittsburgh\)) neighborhood.[^cite_note-Hanna1-26]<sup>: 289 </sup>

### 18th century

Further information: [Pennsylvania in the American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_in_the_American_Revolution)



[Fort Pitt Block House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Pitt_Block_House), built by the British in 1764, is the oldest extant structure in Pittsburgh.

The first known European to enter the region was the French explorer [Robert de La Salle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_de_La_Salle) from [Quebec](7954867.md) during his 1669 expedition down the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] European pioneers, primarily Dutch, followed in the early 18th century. Michael Bezallion was the first to describe the forks of the Ohio in a 1717 manuscript, and later that year European [fur traders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fur_trade) established area posts and settlements.[^cite_note-chronology-29]

In 1749, French soldiers from Quebec launched an expedition to the forks to unite [Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_\(New_France\)) with [French Louisiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisiana_\(New_France\)) via the rivers.[^cite_note-chronology-29] During 1753–1754, the British hastily built [Fort Prince George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Prince_George) before a larger French force drove them off. The French built [Fort Duquesne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Duquesne) based on LaSalle's 1669 claims. The [French and Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_and_Indian_War), the North American front of the [Seven Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Years'_War), began with the future Pittsburgh as its center. British General [Edward Braddock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Braddock) was dispatched with Major [George Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Washington) as his aide to take Fort Duquesne.[^cite_note-WDL-30] The British and colonial force were defeated at [Braddock's Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Braddock's_Field). General John Forbes finally took the forks in 1758. He began construction on [Fort Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Pitt_\(Pennsylvania\)), named after [William Pitt the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Pitt_the_Elder), while the settlement was named "Pittsborough".[^cite_note-Lorant-31]

During [Pontiac's War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontiac's_War), a loose confederation of Native American tribes [laid siege to Fort Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Fort_Pitt) in 1763; the siege was eventually lifted after Colonel [Henry Bouquet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Bouquet) defeated a portion of the besieging force at the [Battle of Bushy Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bushy_Run). Bouquet strengthened the defenses of Fort Pitt the next year.[^cite_note-EB1911-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

During this period, the powerful nations of the [Iroquois Confederacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroquois_Confederacy), based in New York, had maintained control of much of the Ohio Valley as hunting grounds by right of conquest after defeating other tribes. By the terms of the 1768 [Treaty of Fort Stanwix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fort_Stanwix), the [Penns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Penn) were allowed to purchase the modern region from the [Iroquois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iroquois). A 1769 survey referenced the future city as the "Manor of Pittsburgh".[^cite_note-PCC-36] Both the [Colony of Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_Virginia) and the [Province of Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Pennsylvania) claimed the region under their colonial charters until 1780, when they agreed under a federal initiative to extend the [Mason–Dixon line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mason%E2%80%93Dixon_line) westward, placing Pittsburgh in Pennsylvania. On March 8, 1771, [Bedford County, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bedford_County,_Pennsylvania) was created to govern the frontier.

On April 16, 1771, the city's first civilian local government was created as [Pitt Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitt_Township,_Allegheny_County,_Pennsylvania).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] William Teagarden was the first constable, and William Troop was the first clerk.[^cite_note-39]

Following the [American Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolution), the village of Pittsburgh continued to grow. One of its earliest industries was boat building for settlers of the [Ohio Country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Country). In 1784, Thomas Vickroy completed a town plan which was approved by the Penn family attorney. Pittsburgh became a possession of Pennsylvania in 1785. The following year, the [Pittsburgh Gazette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Post-Gazette) was started, and in 1787, the [Pittsburgh Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Academy) was chartered. Unrest during the [Whiskey Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskey_Rebellion) of 1794 resulted in federal troops being sent to the area. By 1797, glass manufacture began, while the population grew to around 1,400. Settlers arrived after crossing the [Appalachian Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Mountains) or through the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes). [Fort Pitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Pitt_\(Pennsylvania\)) (now Pittsburgh) at the source of the Ohio River became the main base for settlers moving into the [Northwest Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Territory).

### 19th century

Further information: [Pennsylvania in the American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_in_the_American_Civil_War)



The [Monongahela River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongahela_River) and its surroundings in 1857



[Lithograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithograph) of Pittsburgh by [Otto Krebs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Krebs) (before 1874)

The federal government recognizes Pittsburgh as the starting point for the [Lewis and Clark Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_and_Clark_Expedition).[^cite_note-40] Preparations began in Pittsburgh in 1803 when [Meriwether Lewis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meriwether_Lewis) purchased a [keelboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keelboat) that would later be used to ascend the [Missouri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_River).[^cite_note-41]

The [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812) cut off the supply of British goods, stimulating American industry. By 1815, Pittsburgh was producing significant quantities of iron, brass, tin, and glass. On March 18, 1816, the 46-year-old local government became a city. It was served by numerous river steamboats that increased trading traffic on the rivers.

In the 1830s, many [Welsh people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_people) from the [Merthyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merthyr) steelworks immigrated to the city following the aftermath of the [Merthyr Rising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merthyr_Rising). By the 1840s, Pittsburgh was one of the largest cities west of the [Allegheny Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_Mountains). The [Great Fire of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Fire_of_Pittsburgh) destroyed over a thousand buildings in 1845. The city rebuilt with the aid of Irish immigrants who came to escape the [Great Famine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Famine_\(Ireland\)). By 1857, Pittsburgh's 1,000 factories were consuming 22 million coal bushels yearly. Coal mining and iron manufacturing attracted waves of European immigrants to the area, with the most coming from [Germany](11867.md).



Burning of Union Depot during the [Pittsburgh railroad strike of 1877](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_railroad_strike_of_1877)

Because Pennsylvania had been established as a free state after the Revolution, enslaved African Americans sought freedom here through escape as refugees from the South, or occasionally fleeing from travelers they were serving who stayed in the city. There were active stations of the [Underground Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_Railroad) in the city, and numerous refugees were documented as getting help from station agents and African-American workers in city hotels. The Drennen Slave Girl walked out of the Monongahela House in 1850, apparently to freedom.[^cite_note-freedom-42] The Merchant's Hotel was also a place where African-American workers would advise slaves the state was free and aid them in getting to nearby stations of the Underground Railroad.[^cite_note-exhibit-43]

Sometimes refugee slaves from the South stayed in Pittsburgh, but other times they continued North, including into Canada. Many slaves left the city and county for Canada after Congress passed the 1850 [Fugitive Slave Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fugitive_Slave_Act), as it required cooperation from law enforcement even in free states and increased penalties. From 1850 to 1860, the black population in Allegheny County dropped from 3,431 to 2,725 as people headed to safety in Canada.[^cite_note-freedom-42]

The [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) boosted the city's economy with increased iron and armament demand by the Union. [Andrew Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Carnegie) began steel production in 1875 at the [Edgar Thomson Steel Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Thomson_Steel_Works) in [North Braddock, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Braddock,_Pennsylvania), which evolved into the [Carnegie Steel Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Steel_Company). He adopted the [Bessemer process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessemer_process) to increase production. Manufacturing was key to the growth of Pittsburgh and the surrounding region. Railroad lines were built into the city along both rivers, increasing transportation access to important markets.

### 20th century

[^cite_note-44]



Steel mills in the [Strip District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strip_District,_Pittsburgh) in 1906

In 1901, [J. P. Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._P._Morgan) and attorney [Elbert H. Gary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbert_H._Gary) merged [Carnegie Steel Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Steel_Company) and several other companies into [U.S. Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Steel). By 1910, Pittsburgh was the [nation's eighth-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_cities_in_the_United_States_by_population_by_decade), accounting for between one-third and one-half of national steel output.

The [Pittsburgh Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Agreement) was a [memorandum of understanding](1018279.md) completed on 31 May 1918 between members of [Czech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs) and [Slovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovaks) expatriate communities in the U.S. It replaced the [Cleveland Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Agreement) of 22 October 1915.[^cite_note-Sabol_Alzo_2009_p._8-45] It concerned the future foundation of [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) as envisioned by [T. G. Masaryk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T._G._Masaryk).[^cite_note-46]

The city suffered [severe flooding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_flood_of_1936) in March 1936. On March 17 and 18, Pittsburgh sustained its worst flood in history as flood levels rose to a peak of 46 feet. Days of heavy rain and melting snow poured into the city's waterways. At least 62 people died from this tragedy.

The city's population swelled to more than a half million, attracting numerous European immigrants to its industrial jobs. By 1940, non-Hispanic whites were 90.6% of the city's population.[^cite_note-47] Pittsburgh also became a main destination of the African-American [Great Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_\(African_American\)) from the rural South during the first half of the 20th century.[^cite_note-48] Limited initially by discrimination, some 95% percent of the men became unskilled steel workers.[^cite_note-49]

During [World War II](32927.md), demand for steel increased and area mills operated 24 hours a day to produce 95 million tons of steel for the war effort.[^cite_note-Lorant-31] This resulted in the highest levels of air pollution in the city's almost century of industry. The city's reputation as the "arsenal of democracy"[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] was being overshadowed by [James Parton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Parton)'s 1868 observation of Pittsburgh being "hell with the lid off."[^cite_note-52]

Following World War II, the city launched a clean air and civic revitalization project known as the "Renaissance," cleaning up the air and the rivers. The "Renaissance II" project followed in 1977, focused on cultural and neighborhood development. The industrial base continued to expand through the 1970s, but beginning in the early 1980s both the area's steel and electronics industries imploded during national industrial restructuring. There were massive layoffs from mill and plant closures.[^cite_note-diaspora-11]

In the later 20th century, the area shifted its economic base to education, tourism, and services, largely based on healthcare/medicine, finance, and high technology such as robotics. Although Pittsburgh successfully shifted its economy and remained viable, the city's population has never rebounded to its industrial-era highs. While 680,000 people lived in the city proper in 1950, a combination of suburbanization and economic turbulence resulted in a decrease in city population, even as the metropolitan area population increased again.

### 21st century

During the [late 2000s recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_2000s_recession), Pittsburgh was economically strong, adding jobs when most cities were losing them. It was one of the few cities in the United States to see housing property values rise. Between 2006 and 2011, the [Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_metropolitan_area) [metropolitan statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) (MSA) experienced over 10% appreciation in housing prices, the highest appreciation of the largest 25 metropolitan statistical areas in the United States, with 22 of the largest 25 metropolitan statistical areas experiencing depreciations in housing values.[^cite_note-53]

In September 2009, the [2009 G20 Pittsburgh summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_G20_Pittsburgh_summit) was held in Pittsburgh.[^cite_note-54]

In April 2026, Pittsburgh hosted the [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL) draft for the first time.[^cite_note-55] The event hosted over 805,000 people in the city during the 3 day event.[^cite_note-56]

## Geography

See also: [Pittsburgh metropolitan area § Regional identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_metropolitan_area#Regional_identity)



The [Duquesne Incline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duquesne_Incline) from [Mount Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Washington,_Pittsburgh_\(mountain\))

Pittsburgh has an area of 58.3 square miles (151 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 55.6 square miles (144 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 2.8 square miles (7.3 km<sup>2</sup>), or 4.75%, is water. The [80th meridian west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/80th_meridian_west) passes directly through the city's downtown.

The city is located on the [Allegheny Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_Plateau), within the [ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecoregion) of the [Western Allegheny Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Allegheny_Plateau_\(ecoregion\)).[^cite_note-57] The [Downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Pittsburgh) area (also known as the Golden Triangle) sits where the [Allegheny River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_River) flows from the northeast and the [Monongahela River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongahela_River) from the southeast to form the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River). The convergence is at [Point State Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_State_Park) and is referred to as "the Point". The city extends east to include the [Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_\(Pittsburgh\)) and [Shadyside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadyside_\(Pittsburgh\)) sections, which are home to the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh), [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), [Chatham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_University), [Carnegie Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Museums_of_Pittsburgh) and [Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Library_of_Pittsburgh), and many other educational, medical, and cultural institutions. The southern, western, and northern areas of the city are primarily residential.

Many [Pittsburgh neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_neighborhoods) are steeply sloped with two-lane roads. More than a quarter of neighborhood names make reference to "hills", "heights", or similar features.[^cite_note-58]

The [public stairways in Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_stairways_in_Pittsburgh) consist of 800 sets of outdoor public stairways with 44,645 treads and 24,090 vertical feet. They include hundreds of streets composed entirely of stairs, and many other steep streets with stairs for sidewalks.[^cite_note-59] Many provide vistas of the Pittsburgh area while attracting hikers and fitness walkers.[^cite_note-60]

Bike and walking trails have been built to border many of the city's rivers and hollows. The [Great Allegheny Passage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Allegheny_Passage) and [Chesapeake and Ohio Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chesapeake_and_Ohio_Canal) Towpath connect the city directly to downtown Washington, D.C. (some 335 miles \[539 km] away) with a continuous bike/running trail.

The city consists of the Downtown area, called the Golden Triangle,[^cite_note-61] and four main areas surrounding it: North Side, South Side, East End, and West End. These areas are further divided into 90 neighborhoods.[^cite_note-62]

[Downtown Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Pittsburgh) has 30 skyscrapers, nine over 500 feet (150 m), with the [U.S. Steel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Steel_Tower) being the tallest at 841 ft (256 m).[^cite_note-63] The [Cultural District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_District,_Pittsburgh) spans 14 blocks along the Allegheny River and is home to theaters, arts venues, and a growing residential community. The [Firstside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firstside_Historic_District) portion of Downtown borders the Monongahela River, the historic Mon Wharf and hosts the distinctive [PPG Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PPG_Place) Gothic-style glass skyscraper complex. Downtown is served by the [Port Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_of_Allegheny_County)'s [light rail system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_light_rail) and [multiple bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridges_of_Pittsburgh) leading north and south.[^cite_note-64] It is also home to [Point Park University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Park_University) and [Duquesne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duquesne_University) which borders [Uptown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluff_\(Pittsburgh\)).

The [North Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Side_\(Pittsburgh\)), originally the independent [Allegheny City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_City) until being annexed in 1907, is a primarily residential area with well-preserved 19th-century homes. It hosts attractions like [Acrisure Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrisure_Stadium), [PNC Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNC_Park), the [Andy Warhol Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Warhol_Museum), and the [National Aviary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aviary), among others.[^cite_note-65]

The [South Side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Side_\(Pittsburgh\)), once home to railyards and mill workers, has seen revitalization with improvements to East Carson Street and new retail. It is now a vibrant neighborhood with diverse shopping and nightlife.[^cite_note-66] In the 1990s, the [Urban Redevelopment Authority of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Redevelopment_Authority_of_Pittsburgh) purchased the South Side Works steel mill property and redeveloped it into the [SouthSide Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SouthSide_Works) mixed-use development.

The East End includes key institutions including the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh), [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), [Carnegie Museums of Art and Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Museums_of_Pittsburgh), and [Phipps Conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phipps_Conservatory). It features many parks, including [Mellon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellon_Park), [Westinghouse Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westinghouse_Park), [Schenley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schenley_Park), [Frick Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frick_Park), [The Frick Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frick_Pittsburgh), [Bakery Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakery_Square), and the [Pittsburgh Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Zoo), and vibrant neighborhoods like [Shadyside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shadyside_\(Pittsburgh\)). [Squirrel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squirrel_Hill) is also known as the hub of Jewish life in Pittsburgh, home to approximately 20 synagogues.[^cite_note-67] Oakland is home to several universities and the [Petersen Events Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petersen_Events_Center). The [Strip District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strip_District,_Pittsburgh) to the west along the [Allegheny River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_River) is an open-air marketplace by day and a clubbing destination by night. [Bloomfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bloomfield_\(Pittsburgh\)) is Pittsburgh's Little Italy and is known for its Italian restaurants and grocers. [Lawrenceville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrenceville_\(Pittsburgh\)) is a revitalizing rowhouse neighborhood popular with artists and designers. The [Hill District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_District) was home to photographer [Charles Harris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Harris_\(photographer\)) as well as various African-American jazz clubs.[^cite_note-68]

The [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End_\(Pittsburgh\)) includes [Mt. Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Washington,_Pittsburgh_\(neighborhood\)), with its famous view of the downtown skyline, and numerous other residential neighborhoods such as [Sheraden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheraden) and [Elliott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_\(Pittsburgh\)).

Pennsylvania lies within the Northeastern United States as defined by U.S. Census Bureau regional classifications.[^cite_note-71]

Pittsburgh lies within the boundaries of [Appalachia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachia) as designated by the [Appalachian Regional Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appalachian_Regional_Commission), and has long been characterized as a northern urban industrial anchor of Appalachia.[^cite_note-72] In its post-industrial era, Pittsburgh has been described as the "Paris of Appalachia",[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76] in reference to the city's cultural, educational, healthcare, and technological resources. It is the largest city within the Appalachian region.

Geographer Joseph Scarpaci has described Pittsburgh's regional identity as bridging Northeastern and Midwestern influences, characterizing the city as having "one foot in the East... and the other in the Midwest."[^cite_note-77]

Pittsburgh is the principal city of the [Pittsburgh Combined Statistical Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh-New_Castle-Weirton,_PA-OH-WV_Combined_Statistical_Area), a [combined statistical area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_statistical_area) defined by the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census_Bureau).

Under the [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification), Pittsburgh falls within either a [hot-summer humid continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_humid_continental_climate) (*Dfa*) if the 0 °C (32 °F) isotherm is used or a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) (*Cfa*) if the −3 °C (27 °F) isotherm is used. Summers are hot and winters are moderately cold with wide variations in temperature. Despite this, it has one of the most pleasant summer climates between medium and large cities in the U.S.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80] The city lies in the USDA plant hardiness zone 6b except along the rivers where the zone is 7a.[^cite_note-81] The area has four distinct seasons: winters are cold and snowy, springs and falls are mild with moderate levels of sunshine, and summers are warm. As measured by percent possible sunshine, summer is by far the sunniest season, though annual sunshine is low among major US cities at well under 50%.[^cite_note-NOAAsun-82]

The warmest month of the year in Pittsburgh is July, with a 24-hour average of 73.2 °F (22.9 °C). Conditions are often humid, and combined with highs reaching 90 °F (32 °C) on an average 9.5 days a year,[^cite_note-NOAA-83] a considerable [heat index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_index) arises. The coolest month is January, when the 24-hour average is 28.8 °F (−1.8 °C), and lows of 0 °F (−18 °C) or below can be expected on an average 2.6 nights per year.[^cite_note-NOAA-83] Officially, record temperatures range from −22 °F (−30 °C), on [January 19, 1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_North_American_cold_wave) to 103 °F (39 °C), which occurred three times, most recently on July 16, 1988; the record cold daily maximum is −3 °F (−19 °C), which occurred three times, most recently the day of the all-time record low, while, conversely, the record warm daily minimum is 82 °F (28 °C) on July 1, 1901.[^cite_note-NOAA-83][^cite_note-84] Due to elevation and location on the windward side of the Appalachian Mountains, 100 °F (38 °C)+ readings are very rare, and were last seen on July 15, 1995.[^cite_note-NOAA-83]

Average annual precipitation is 39.61 inches (1,006 mm) and precipitation is greatest in May while least in October; annual precipitation has historically ranged from 22.65 in (575 mm) in 1930 to 57.83 in (1,469 mm) in 2018.[^cite_note-85] On average, December and January have the greatest number of precipitation days. Snowfall averages 44.1 inches (112 cm) per season, but has historically ranged from 8.8 in (22 cm) in 1918–19 to 80 in (200 cm) in 1950–51.[^cite_note-86] There is an average of 59 clear days and 103 partly cloudy days per year, while 203 days are cloudy.[^cite_note-87] In terms of annual percent-average possible sunshine received, Pittsburgh (45%) is similar to [Seattle](11388236.md) (49%).

#### Air quality

[United States Environmental Protection Agency](58666.md) (EPA) data from 2021 to 2024 shows that Pittsburgh's air quality was generally good or moderate.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94]

Despite improvements, studies suggest Pittsburgh's air quality still impacts health. A 2014 to 2016 study found that children near pollution sources like industrial sites had asthma rates nearly three times the national average.[^cite_note-Frazier-2020-95] It also revealed that 38% of students lived in areas exceeding EPA's particle pollution standards, and 70% in areas surpassing the WHO's standards.[^cite_note-Frazier-2020-95] Most affected communities were minority populations, leading some residents to believe that the continuing effects of air pollution are a case of environmental racism.[^cite_note-96]

As of 2005, Pittsburgh had 31,000 trees along 900 miles of streets. A 2011 analysis valued the annual benefits of the city's [urban forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_forest) between \$10 and \$13 million, based on contributions to aesthetics, energy use, and air quality. The city invests \$850,000 annually in tree planting and maintenance.[^cite_note-97]

#### Water quality

Local rivers in Pittsburgh continue to exceed EPA pollution limits, primarily due to [frequently overflowing untreated sewage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_County_Sanitary_Authority#Pittsburgh's_sewer_overflow_problem) from the city's outdated infrastructure.[^cite_note-98] Pittsburgh's combined sewer system, built in the early 1900s, carries both stormwater and wastewater, with the treatment plant constructed in 1959.[^cite_note-99] Insufficient upgrades have led to public health concerns, as even a tenth of an inch of rain causes runoff to flow into rivers.[^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] Nine billion gallons of untreated waste and stormwater flow into rivers per year, leading to health hazards and Clean Water Act violations.[^cite_note-102] The [Allegheny County Sanitary Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_County_Sanitary_Authority) (ALCOSAN) is under a Consent Decree from the EPA and proposed a \$2 billion system upgrade in 2017, approved by the EPA in 2019.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104][^cite_note-105]

[Pittsburgh Water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Water), the municipal water and sewer authority that serves most of the city and is responsible for maintaining infrastructure, replacing pipes, and setting water rates, has faced criticism for alleged mismanagement and for high lead levels in the city's drinking water, particularly in 2016.[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-puc-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] While lead levels had risen for years, at the time, many residents blamed the authority's administrative changes for the 2016 spike.[^cite_note-Morrison-2021-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] In response, Pittsburgh Water began adding orthophosphate, a food-grade additive that helps prevent lead pipes from corroding into drinking water, to the water treatment process.\[^cite_note-[www.pgh2o.com-2023-113](http://www.pgh2o.com-2023-113/)] Pittsburgh Water has also been working to replace residential lead service lines, citing a goal of 2027 by which to have replaced every residential lead line in its service area, and continuing to test water for lead.\[^cite_note-\:0-114]\[^cite_note-[www.pgh2o.com-2023-113](http://www.pgh2o.com-2023-113/)] Improvements made since 2016 have resulted in Pittsburgh Water reporting a lead level of just 2 parts per billion in its drinking water in 2025, the lowest amount of lead ever recorded in Pittsburgh's drinking water.\[^cite_note-\:0-114]

There remains concern among residents over the long-term effects of this lead, particularly for children.[^cite_note-Marusic-115] Some people, particularly during and after the 2016 lead crisis, also believe that the high levels of lead reflect environmental racism, as black and Hispanic children in Pittsburgh experience elevated blood-lead levels at 4 times the rate of white children.[^cite_note-Marusic-115][^cite_note-116]

Ethnic origins in Pittsburgh



Map of racial distribution in Pittsburgh, 2010 U.S. census. Each dot is 25 people: ⬤ White ⬤ Black ⬤ Asian ⬤ Hispanic ⬤ Other

At the [2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_United_States_census), there were 305,704 people residing in Pittsburgh, a decrease of 8.6% since 2000; 66.0% of the population was White, 25.8% Black or African American, 0.2% American Indian and Alaska Native, 4.4% Asian, 0.3% Other, and 2.3% mixed; in 2020, 2.3% of Pittsburgh's population was of Hispanic or Latino American origin of any race. [Non-Hispanic whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_whites) were 64.8% of the population in 2010,[^cite_note-census1-120] compared to 78.7% in 1970.[^cite_note-census-121] By the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census), the population slightly declined further to 302,971.[^cite_note-2020_Census-2020-119] Its racial and ethnic makeup in 2020 was 64.7% non-Hispanic white, 23.0% Black or African American, 5.8% Asian, and 3.2% Hispanic or Latino American of any race.

Since the beginning of the 21st century, the five largest European ethnic groups in Pittsburgh were German (19.7%), Irish (15.8%), Italian (11.8%), Polish (8.4%), and English (4.6%), while the metropolitan area is approximately 22% German-American, 15.4% Italian American and 11.6% Irish American. Pittsburgh has one of the largest [Italian-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian-American) communities in the nation,[^cite_note-129] and the fifth-largest [Ukrainian](145144.md) community per the [1990 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_United_States_census).[^cite_note-130] Pittsburgh has one of the most extensive [Croatian communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_Americans) in the United States.[^cite_note-131] Overall, the Pittsburgh metro area has one of the largest populations of [Slavs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) in the country.

Pittsburgh has a sizable Black and African American population, concentrated in various neighborhoods especially in the East End. There is also a small Asian community consisting of [Indian immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Americans), and a small Hispanic community consisting of [Mexicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Americans) and [Puerto Ricans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ricans).[^cite_note-thearda.com-132]

In 2010, there were 143,739 households, out of which 21.9% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 31.2% were married couples living together, 16.5% had a female householder with no husband present, and 48.4% were non-families. 39.4% of all households were made up of individuals, and 13.7% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.17 and the average family size was 2.95. In the city, the population was spread out, with 19.9% under the age of 18, 14.8% from 18 to 24, 28.6% from 25 to 44, 20.3% from 45 to 64, and 16.4% who were 65 years of age or older. The median age was 36 years. For every 100 females, there were 90.7 males. For every 100 females age 18 and over, there were 87.8 males.

The [median income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_income) for a household in the city was \$28,588, and the median income for a family was \$38,795. Males had a median income of \$32,128 versus \$25,500 for females. The [per capita income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Per_capita_income) for the city was \$18,816. About 15.0% of families and 20.4% of the population were below the poverty line, including 27.5% of those under the age of 18 and 13.5% ages 65 or older. By the 2019 [American Community Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Community_Survey), the median income for a household increased to \$53,799.[^cite_note-133] Families had a median income of \$68,922; married-couple families had a median income of \$93,500; and non-family households had a median income of \$34,448. Pittsburgh's wealthiest suburbs within city limits are [Squirrel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squirrel_Hill) and [Point Breeze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Breeze,_Pittsburgh), the only two areas of the city which have average household incomes over \$100,000 a year.[^cite_note-134][^cite_note-135]

As of 2018, much of Pittsburgh's population density was concentrated in the central, southern, and eastern areas. The city limits itself have a [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) of 5,513 people per square mile; its most densely populated parts are [North Oakland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakland_\(Pittsburgh\)) (at 21,200 per square mile) and [Uptown Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uptown_Pittsburgh) (at 19,869 per square mile). Outside of the city limits, [Dormont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormont) and [Mount Oliver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Oliver,_Pennsylvania) were Pittsburgh's most densely populated neighborhoods, with 11,167 and 9,902 people per square mile respectively.[^cite_note-136]

Most of Pittsburgh's immigrants are [from China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Americans), [India](14533.md), [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Americans) and [Italy](14532.md).[^cite_note-137]

### Demographic changes

Since the 1940s, city initiatives for redevelopment have driven demographic changes in Pittsburgh. In the 1950s, the Lower [Hill District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_District) underwent significant upheaval when 1,551 mostly Black residents and 413 businesses were displaced for the construction of the [Civic Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Arena_\(Pittsburgh\)), which opened in 1961.[^cite_note-Lubove-1995-13] This project, part of Pittsburgh's revitalization efforts, led to the neighborhood's population dropping to an estimated 12,000 today.[^cite_note-Klein-2017-138]

In the 1960s, the [Urban Redevelopment Authority of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_Redevelopment_Authority_of_Pittsburgh) (URA) aimed to revitalize [East Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Liberty_\(Pittsburgh\)), resulting in the construction of Penn Center Mall and the displacement of about 3,800 people.[^cite_note-Gillette-2022-139] By the early 2000s, private developers catering to a wealthier demographic introduced businesses like [Home Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Home_Depot), Whole Foods, and Google. This redevelopment, supported by the URA, transformed East Liberty into a desirable area for millennials.[^cite_note-Gillette-2022-139]

These changes have sparked criticism, with some residents arguing that the government's actions were part of a strategy to disperse Black and low-income populations or facilitate [gentrification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gentrification) of neighborhoods.[^cite_note-Klein-2017-138][^cite_note-140] The demolition of housing units like the East Mall public housing complex in 2009, replaced by businesses like Target, is cited as evidence of this process.[^cite_note-141] Besides East Liberty, places like [Lawrenceville (Pittsburgh)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrenceville_\(Pittsburgh\)), [Hill District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_District), and the [South Side Flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Side_Flats) have also experienced immense amounts of gentrification.

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Pittsburgh)

See also: [List of corporations in Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_corporations_in_Pittsburgh)

Pittsburgh has adapted since the collapse of its century-long steel and electronics industries. The region has shifted to high technology, [robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotics), health care, nuclear engineering, tourism, [biomedical technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomedical_technology), finance, education, and services. Annual payroll of the region's technology industries, when taken in aggregate, exceeded \$10.8 billion in 2007,[^cite_note-145] and in 2010 there were 1,600 technology companies.[^cite_note-Bobkoff-146] A [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Bureau_of_Economic_Research) 2014 report named Pittsburgh the second-best U.S. city for intergenerational economic mobility[^cite_note-147] or the [American Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Dream).[^cite_note-148] Reflecting the citywide shift from industry to technology, former factories have been renovated as modern office space. Google has research and technology offices in a refurbished 1918–1998 [Nabisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabisco) factory, a complex known as [Bakery Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakery_Square).[^cite_note-149] Some of the factory's original equipment, such as a large dough mixer, were left standing in homage to the site's industrial roots.[^cite_note-150] Pittsburgh's transition from its [industrial heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_heritage) has earned it praise as "the poster child for managing industrial transition".[^cite_note-151] Other major cities in the northeast and mid-west have increasingly borrowed from Pittsburgh's [model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conceptual_model) in order to renew their industries and economic base.[^cite_note-152]

The largest employer in the city is the [University of Pittsburgh Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh_Medical_Center), with 48,000 employees. All hospitals, outpatient clinics, and doctor's office positions combine for 116,000 jobs, approximately 10% of the jobs in the region. An analyst recently observed of the city's medical sector: "That's both more jobs and a higher share of the region's total employment than the steel industry represented in the 1970s."[^cite_note-153]

The global impact of Pittsburgh technology and business was recently demonstrated in several key components of the [Boeing 787 Dreamliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner) being manufactured and supplied by area companies.[^cite_note-157] Area retail is anchored by over 35 [shopping malls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_shopping_malls) and a healthy downtown retail sector, as well as boutique shops along [Walnut Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walnut_Street_\(Pittsburgh\)), in [Squirrel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squirrel_Hill), [Lawrenceville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawrenceville_\(Pittsburgh\)) and [Station Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Station_Square).

The nonprofit arts and cultural industry in Allegheny County generates \$341 million in economic activity that supports over 10,000 full-time equivalent jobs with nearly \$34 million in local and state taxes raised.[^cite_note-158]

A leader in [environmental design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership_in_Energy_and_Environmental_Design), the city is home to 60 total and 10 of the world's first [green buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_building) while billions have been invested in the area's [Marcellus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcellus_Formation#Economic_impact) natural gas fields.[^cite_note-GrEn-159] Pittsburgh also claims to have the world's first [movie theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickelodeon_\(movie_theater\)) which helped found the long-running [Three Rivers Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Rivers_Film_Festival) in 1982 to an influx of [major television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_shows_shot_in_Pittsburgh) and [movie productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_shot_in_Pittsburgh).

Pittsburgh has hosted many conventions, including [INPEX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/INPEX), the world's largest invention trade show, since 1984;[^cite_note-160] [Tekko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tekko_\(convention\)), a four-day anime convention, since 2003; [Anthrocon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthrocon), a furry convention, since 2006; and the [DUG East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_Unconventional_Gas) energy trade show since 2009.

### Entertainment



East Room of the [Phipps Conservatory and Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phipps_Conservatory_and_Botanical_Gardens)

Pittsburgh boasts a rich arts and culture scene, with a history dating back to 19th-century industrialists who commissioned and donated public works including [Heinz Hall for the Performing Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_Hall_for_the_Performing_Arts) and the [Benedum Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedum_Center), home to the [Pittsburgh Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Symphony_Orchestra) and [Pittsburgh Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Opera). The [Pittsburgh Ballet Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Ballet_Theatre) and [Pittsburgh Dance Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Dance_Council) offer diverse dance events, while polka, folk, square, and round dancing are celebrated by the [Duquesne University Tamburitzans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duquesne_University_Tamburitzans), a multicultural academy dedicated to folk traditions. Pittsburgh is also a popular filming location, with major productions like *[The Dark Knight Rises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Knight_Rises)* filmed in Downtown, Oakland, and the North Shore. The city is also recognized as the birthplace of the modern zombie film genre after [George A. Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_A._Romero)'s 1968 film *[Night of the Living Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_of_the_Living_Dead)*.[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162]



[The Andy Warhol Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Andy_Warhol_Museum) is one of the four [Carnegie Museums of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Museums_of_Pittsburgh).

Major art museums include the [Andy Warhol Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Warhol_Museum), the [Carnegie Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Museum_of_Art), [The Frick Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Frick_Pittsburgh), [Pittsburgh Center for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Center_for_the_Arts), the [Mattress Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattress_Factory), and the [Carnegie Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Museum_of_Natural_History), which holds extensive dinosaur, mineral, and Egyptian collections. The [Soldiers and Sailors Memorial Hall and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldiers_and_Sailors_Memorial_Hall_and_Museum) houses military exhibits, and the [Children's Museum of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_Museum_of_Pittsburgh) features interactive exhibits for kids. The eclectic [Bayernhof Music Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayernhof_Music_Museum) is located six miles (9 km) from downtown, while [The Clemente Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Clemente_Museum) is in Lawrenceville. ### Music

Pittsburgh has a long tradition of [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz), [blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blues), and [bluegrass music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluegrass_music). The [National Negro Opera Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Negro_Opera_Company) was founded in the city as the first all-African American opera company in the U.S., helping launch the careers of African-American opera stars like [Leontyne Price](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leontyne_Price). Pittsburgh also shaped 20th-century music with influential figures like [Billy Strayhorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Strayhorn), who grew up in the city, and pianist-composer [Mary Lou Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Lou_Williams), who honored her hometown with a 1966 album featuring [Leon Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Thomas_\(jazz_singer\)).[^cite_note-164][^cite_note-165]

Recent artists like [Wiz Khalifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wiz_Khalifa) have continued Pittsburgh's musical legacy, with his hit "[Black and Yellow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_and_Yellow)" reaching number one on the [Billboard Hot 100](423161.md) in 2011.[^cite_note-166] Other notable artists from the area include [Perry Como](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry_Como), [Christina Aguilera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christina_Aguilera), and the band [Rusted Root](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusted_Root), which was formed in Pittsburgh. Rusted Root's Liz Berlin owns Mr. Smalls, a popular venue for national touring acts.[^cite_note-167] Hip hop artist [Mac Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mac_Miller), also from Pittsburgh, named his debut album *[Blue Slide Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Slide_Park)* after the local [Frick Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frick_Park).

Pittsburgh has emerged as a leading city in the United States' [heavy metal music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metal_music) scene.[^cite_note-MetalSucks-2016-168][^cite_note-169] Many [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock) and [Hardcore punk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcore_punk) acts, such as [Aus Rotten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aus_Rotten) and [Anti-Flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Flag), originated in Pittsburgh. Pittsburgh has also seen many metal bands gain prominence in recent years, most notably [Code Orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Code_Orange_\(band\)), who were nominated for a Grammy. The city was also home to the [math rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Math_rock) band [Don Caballero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Don_Caballero).

Pittsburgh also had an influential [electronic music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_music) [subculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subculture) in the 1990s, with origins similar to internet [chatroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatroom)-based movements across the United States.[^cite_note-matos-170][^cite_note-kelly-171][^cite_note-pro-172] Pittsburgh [promoters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promoter_\(entertainment\)) and [DJs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DJs) organized [raves](43488.md) in warehouses, [ice rinks](979488.md), barns, and fields which eventually attracted thousands of attendees.[^cite_note-kelly-171][^cite_note-barnes-173][^cite_note-silver-barnes-174] DJs [Adam Beyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Beyer) and [Richie Hawtin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richie_Hawtin) played at local raves.[^cite_note-kelly-171] One notable figure, drum and bass DJ [Dieselboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dieselboy), emerged from this scene.[^cite_note-matos-170][^cite_note-carter-175] Since 2012, the [Hot Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Mass) after-hours electronic music [dance party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dance_party) has been a key part of Pittsburgh's electronic music scene, noted for its European nightclub vibe.[^cite_note-eb-176][^cite_note-stolman-177] Electronic artist [Yaeji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaeji) has credited Hot Mass as a formative influence during her time at Carnegie Mellon University.[^cite_note-kim-178][^cite_note-posner-179]

[Hellbender Vinyl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellbender_Vinyl), the largest vinyl record manufacturing company in Pennsylvania, is located in Pittsburgh.[^cite_note-180]

### Theatre

Main article: [Theatre in Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_in_Pittsburgh)



[Benedum Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedum_Center)

The city's first play was produced at the [old courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_Square,_Pittsburgh) in 1803[^cite_note-chronology-29] and the first theater built in 1812.[^cite_note-chronology-29] Collegiate companies include the University of Pittsburgh's [Repertory Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh_Repertory_Theatre) and [Kuntu Repertory Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuntu_Repertory_Theatre), Point Park University's resident companies at its [Pittsburgh Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Playhouse), and Carnegie Mellon University's School of Drama productions and [Scotch'n'Soda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotch'n'Soda) organization. The Duquesne University Red Masquers, founded in 1912, are the oldest, continuously producing theater company in Pennsylvania. The city's longest-running theater show, [Friday Nite Improvs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friday_Nite_Improvs), is an improv jam that has been performed in the [Cathedral of Learning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_Learning) and other locations for 20 years. The [Pittsburgh New Works Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_New_Works_Festival) utilizes local theater companies to stage productions of original one-act plays by playwrights from all parts of the country. Similarly, [Future Ten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Future_Ten) showcases new ten-minute plays. [Saint Vincent Summer Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Vincent_Summer_Theatre), [Off the Wall Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off_the_Wall_Productions), [Mountain Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Playhouse), The Theatre Factory, and [Stage Right!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stage_Right!) in nearby [Latrobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latrobe,_Pennsylvania), [Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie,_Pennsylvania), [Jennerstown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jennerstown), [Trafford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trafford,_Pennsylvania), and [Greensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greensburg,_Pennsylvania), respectively, employ Pittsburgh actors and contribute to the culture of the region.

Pittsburgh is well known for being home to the late playwright August Wilson.[^cite_note-augustwilsonhouse.org-181] The August Wilson House now remains in Pittsburgh to celebrate the life and work of August Wilson, continue to produce his plays, and serve as an arts center for the Hill District, where Wilson was from.[^cite_note-augustwilsonhouse.org-181]

### Literature

See also: [List of fiction set in Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fiction_set_in_Pittsburgh), [List of films shot in Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_films_shot_in_Pittsburgh), and [List of television shows shot in Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_television_shows_shot_in_Pittsburgh)



[Carnegie Library of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Library_of_Pittsburgh) Main Branch in Oakland

Pittsburgh is the birthplace of notable writers such as [Gertrude Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gertrude_Stein) and [Rachel Carson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rachel_Carson), a [Chatham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_University) graduate from the suburb of [Springdale, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Springdale,_Pennsylvania).[^cite_note-182] Modern writers include [Pulitzer Prize](24230.md)-winning playwright [August Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_Wilson), as well as [Michael Chabon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Chabon), who writes about student and college life in Pittsburgh.[^cite_note-183] Two-time Pulitzer winner and recipient of the [Presidential Medal of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Medal_of_Freedom), [David McCullough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_McCullough), was born and raised in Pittsburgh.[^cite_note-184] Pulitzer Prize-winning author [Annie Dillard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Dillard), whose memoir *An American Childhood* takes place in post-World War II Pittsburgh, also hails from the city.

Award-winning author [John Edgar Wideman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Edgar_Wideman), who grew up in Pittsburgh, has based several books in the city, including *Brothers and Keepers*. Poet [Terrance Hayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrance_Hayes), winner of the 2010 National Book Award and a 2014 MacArthur Foundation Fellow, earned his MFA at the University of Pittsburgh and was a faculty member there. Other local poets include [Michael Simms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Simms_\(publisher\)), founder of [Autumn House Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn_House_Press), and [Samuel John Hazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_John_Hazo), Pennsylvania's first poet laureate. Contemporary writers like [Kathleen Tessaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kathleen_Tessaro), author of novels such as *Elegance*, *The Perfume Collector*, and *Rare Objects*, and new authors including [Chris Kuzneski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Kuzneski) and Brian Celio, who captures Pittsburgh's "Yinzer" dialect, contribute to the city's vibrant literary tradition. Pittsburgh's unique literary style extends to playwrights,[^cite_note-185] as well as local graffiti and hip hop artists.

Pittsburgh's position as the birthplace for community-owned television and networked commercial television helped spawn the modern children's show genres exemplified by *[Mister Rogers' Neighborhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mister_Rogers'_Neighborhood)*, *[Where in the World is Carmen Sandiego?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Where_in_the_World_Is_Carmen_Sandiego%3F_\(TV_series\))*, *[Happy's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy's_Party)*, *[Cappelli & Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cappelli_&_Company)*, and *[The Children's Corner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Children's_Corner)*, all nationally broadcast.

The *[Pittsburgh Dad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Dad)* series has showcased the [Pittsburghese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburghese) genre to a global YouTube audience since 2011.

The modern fantasy, macabre and science fiction genre was popularized by director [George A. Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_A._Romero), television's [Bill Cardille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Cardille) and his *[Chiller Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiller_Theatre_\(Pittsburgh\))*,[^cite_note-186] director and writer [Rusty Cundieff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusty_Cundieff) and makeup effects guru [Tom Savini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Savini).[^cite_note-187] The genre continues today with the PARSEC science fiction organization,[^cite_note-188] The It's Alive Show, the annual "Zombie Fest",[^cite_note-189] and several writer's workshops including Write or Die,[^cite_note-190] Pittsburgh SouthWrites,[^cite_note-191] and Pittsburgh Worldwrights[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193] with [Barton Paul Levenson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barton_Paul_Levenson), [Kenneth Chiacchia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Chiacchia) and [Elizabeth Humphreys Penrose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Humphreys_Penrose).

### Local dialect

Main article: [Western Pennsylvania English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Pennsylvania_English)

The Pittsburgh English dialect, commonly called *[Pittsburghese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburghese)*, was influenced by [Scots-Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scots-Irish_American), German, and [Eastern European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_European) immigrants and African Americans.[^cite_note-198] Locals who speak the dialect are sometimes referred to as "[Yinzers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yinzer)" (from the local word "yinz" \[var. *yunz*], a blended form of "you ones", similar to "y'all" and "you all" in the South). Common Pittsburghese terms are: "slippy" (slippery), "redd up" (clean up), "jagger bush" (thorn bush), and "gum bands" (rubber bands). The dialect is also notable for dropping the verb "to be". In Pittsburghese one would say "the car needs washed" instead of "needs to be washed", "needs washing", or "needs a wash." The dialect has some tonal similarities to other nearby regional dialects of Erie and Baltimore but is noted for its somewhat [staccato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staccato) rhythms. The staccato qualities of the dialect are thought to originate either from Welsh or other European languages. The many local peculiarities have prompted *[The New York Times](30680.md)* to describe Pittsburgh as "the [Galapagos Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galapagos_Islands) of American dialect".[^cite_note-199] The lexicon itself contains notable loans from [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language) and other European languages; examples include *[babushka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Headscarf)*, *[pierogi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierogi)*, and *[halušky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halu%C5%A1ky)*.[^cite_note-200]

### Livability



The Frick Environmental Center at [Frick Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frick_Park)

Pittsburgh has five city parks and several parks managed by the [Pittsburgh Parks Conservancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Parks_Conservancy). The largest, [Frick Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frick_Park), provides 664 acres (269 ha) of woodland park with extensive hiking and biking trails throughout steep valleys and wooded slopes. Birding enthusiasts visit the Clayton Hill area of Frick Park, where over 100 species of birds have been recorded.[^cite_note-201]

Residents living in extremely low-lying areas near the rivers or one of the 1,400 creeks and streams may have occasional floods,[^cite_note-Puko-202] such as those caused when the remnants of [Hurricane Ivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Ivan) hit rainfall records in 2004.[^cite_note-203] River flooding is relatively rare due to federal flood control efforts extensively managing locks, dams, and reservoirs.[^cite_note-Puko-202][^cite_note-204][^cite_note-205] Residents living near smaller tributary streams are less protected from occasional flooding. The cost of a comprehensive flood control program for the region has been estimated at a prohibitive \$50 billion.[^cite_note-Puko-202]

Pittsburgh has the greatest number of bars per capita in the nation.[^cite_note-Ritenbaugh-16]

## Sports

Main article: [Sports in Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Pittsburgh)

Pittsburgh hosted the [first professional football game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_American_football) and the [first World Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1903_World_Series). College sports also have large followings with the University of Pittsburgh in football and sharing Division I basketball fans with Robert Morris University and Duquesne University.

Pittsburgh has a long history with its major professional sports teams—the [Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steelers) of the [National Football League](21211.md), the [Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) of the [National Hockey League](21809.md), and the [Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pirates) of [Major League Baseball](38776.md)—which all share the same team colors, the [official city colors of black and gold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Pittsburgh,_Pennsylvania).[^cite_note-206] Pittsburgh is the only city in the United States where this practice of sharing colors across all city teams takes place.[^cite_note-207] The black-and-gold color scheme has since become widely associated with the city and personified in its famous [Terrible Towel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrible_Towel).[^cite_note-208] Further, the [Pittsburgh Riverhounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Riverhounds) professional soccer team of the [USL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USL_Championship) division wear black and gold colors.

The "[Rails to Trails](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rails_to_Trails)" program has converted miles of former [rail tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_tracks) to recreational trails, including a [Pittsburgh-Washington D.C. bike/walking trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh-Washington_D.C._bike/walking_trail).[^cite_note-209] Several [mountain biking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_biking) trails are within the city and suburbs, [Frick Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frick_Park) has biking trails and [Hartwood Acres Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartwood_Acres_Park) has many miles of [single track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_track_\(mountain_biking\)) trails.[^cite_note-210][^cite_note-211]

### Baseball



[PNC Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNC_Park), home stadium of the [Pittsburgh Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pirates)

> \[t]his is the perfect blend of location, history, design, comfort and baseball ... The best stadium in baseball is in Pittsburgh.

— ESPN

The [Pittsburgh Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pirates) baseball team, often referred to as the Bucs or the Buccos (derived from [buccaneer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buccaneer)), is the city's oldest professional sports franchise. They were founded in 1881, originally called the Pittsburgh Alleghenies, and play in the [Central Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_Central) of the [National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_\(baseball\)). The Pirates are nine-time Pennant winners and five-time [World Series](23489161.md) Champions, were in the first [World Series (1903)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1903_World_Series) and claim two pre-World Series titles in 1901 and 1902. The Pirates play in [PNC Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNC_Park).

Pittsburgh also has a rich [Negro league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Negro_league) history, with the former [Pittsburgh Crawfords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Crawfords) and the [Homestead Grays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homestead_Grays) credited with as many as 14 league titles and 11 Hall of Famers between them in the 1930s and 1940s, while the [Keystones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Keystones_\(baseball\)) fielded teams in the 1920s. In addition, in 1971 the Pirates were the first Major League team to field an all-minority lineup. One sportswriter claimed, "No city is more synonymous with black baseball than Pittsburgh."[^cite_note-217]

Since the late 20th century, the Pirates had three consecutive [National League Championship Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_Championship_Series) appearances (1990–92) (going 6, 7 and 7 games each), followed by setting the MLB record for most consecutive losing seasons, with 20 from 1993 until 2012. This era was followed by three consecutive postseason appearances: the 2013 [National League Division Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_League_Division_Series) and the 2014–2015 Wild Card games. Their [September pennant race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Pittsburgh_Pirates_season) in 1997 featured the franchises' last no-hitter and last award for [Sporting News' Executive of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sporting_News_Executive_of_the_Year_Award).[^cite_note-218]

### Football

Further information: [American football in Western Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_football_in_Western_Pennsylvania)



[Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers) fans waving the [Terrible Towel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrible_Towel), a tradition that dates back to [1975](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Pittsburgh_Steelers_season)

The city's professional football team, [the NFL's](21211.md) [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers), is named after a distribution company: the Pittsburgh Steeling company, established in 1927. The Steelers have been owned by the [Rooney family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rooney_family) since the team's founding in 1933,[^cite_note-219] and has been noted for consistency in coaching; the team has had four head coaches since the 1960s, sharing the same basic philosophy of building through the draft, maintaining a strong defense, and exercising patience, with [Chuck Noll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chuck_Noll), [Bill Cowher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Cowher), [Mike Tomlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Tomlin), and [Mike McCarthy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_McCarthy) leading the team from 1969 through the 2026 season. Possessing a [passionate and loyal fanbase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steeler_Nation), they have sold out every home game since 1972.[^cite_note-220] The team won four [Super Bowls](27718.md) in a six-year span in the 1970s, a [fifth Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XL) in 2006, and a league record at the time [sixth Super Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XLIII) in 2009, tied with the [New England Patriots](21719.md) for the most in league history since [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_LIII).

[College football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football) in the city dates to 1889[^cite_note-221] with the [Division I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_I_\(NCAA\)) (FBS) [Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Panthers_football) of the University of Pittsburgh posting nine [national championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_FBS_National_Football_Championship), qualifying 37 total bowl games, appearing in the [2018 ACC Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_ACC_Championship_Game), and winning the [2021 ACC Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_ACC_Championship_Game) which was the program's first conference title since leaving the [Big East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_East_Conference_\(1979%E2%80%932013\)) for the [ACC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Coast_Conference) between the 2012 and 2013 seasons.[^cite_note-222] Local universities Duquesne and Robert Morris have loyal fan bases that follow their lower [(FCS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_I_Football_Championship) teams.

Acrisure Stadium is home for the Steelers, Panthers, and both the suburban and city high school championships. Playoff franchises [Pittsburgh Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Power) and Pittsburgh Gladiators competed in the [Arena Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_Football_League) in the 1980s and 2010s respectively. The Gladiators hosted [ArenaBowl I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ArenaBowl_I) in the city, competing in two, but losing both before moving to [Tampa, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa,_Florida) and becoming the [Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Storm).[^cite_note-223] The [Pittsburgh Passion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Passion) has been the city's professional women's football team since 2002 and plays its home games at [F.N.B. Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F.N.B._Stadium_\(Pittsburgh\)).

### Hockey

The NHL's [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) have played in Pittsburgh since the team's founding in 1967. The team has won six [Eastern Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Conference_\(NHL\)) titles (1991, 1992, 2008, 2009, 2016 and 2017) and five [Stanley Cup](66968.md) championships (1991, 1992, 2009, 2016 and 2017). [Mario Lemieux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mario_Lemieux) led the Penguins' ownership group from 1999 to 2021 when the Fenway Sports Group purchased the team;[^cite_note-224] in June 2026, the Hoffman Family of Companies acquired the Pens.[^cite_note-225] Until moving into the [PPG Paints Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PPG_Paints_Arena) in 2010 (when it was known as Consol Energy Center), the team played their home games at the world's first retractable domed stadium, the [Civic Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_Arena_\(Pittsburgh\)), or in local parlance "The Igloo".[^cite_note-226]

[Ice hockey](14790.md) has had a regional fan base since the 1890s semi-pro [Keystones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Keystones_\(ice_hockey\)). The city's first ice rink dates back to 1889, when there was an ice rink at the Casino in [Schenley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schenley_Park). From 1896 to 1956, the Exposition Building on the Allegheny River near The Point and Duquesne Gardens in Oakland offered indoor skating.[^cite_note-ice_skating-227]

The NHL awarded one of its first franchises to the city in 1924 on the strength of the back-to-back USAHA championship-winning [Pittsburgh Yellow Jackets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Yellow_Jackets). The NHL's [Pittsburgh Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pirates_\(hockey\)) made several Stanley Cup playoff runs before folding from [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression) financial pressures. Hockey survived with the [Pittsburgh Hornets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Hornets) farm team (1936–1967) and their seven finals appearances and three championships in 18 playoff seasons.

[Robert Morris University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Morris_Colonials_men's_ice_hockey) fields a Division I college hockey team at the Island Sports Center. Pittsburgh has semi-pro and amateur teams such as the Pittsburgh Penguins Elite.[^cite_note-228] Pro-grade ice rinks in the region include the [Rostraver Ice Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rostraver_Ice_Garden) and [Iceoplex at Southpointe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceoplex_at_Southpointe).

### Basketball



A [Pitt Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Panthers_men's_basketball) men's basketball game at the [Petersen Events Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petersen_Events_Center) in 2009

[Professional basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_basketball) in Pittsburgh dates to the 1910s with teams "Monticello" and "Loendi" winning [five national titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Fives#Colored_Basketball_World's_Champions), the [Pirates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pirates_\(NBL\)) (1937–45 in the [NBL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Basketball_League_\(United_States\))), the [Pittsburgh Ironmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Ironmen) (1947–48 [NBA](22093.md) inaugural season), the [Pittsburgh Rens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Rens) (1961–63), the [Pittsburgh Pipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Pipers) (first [American Basketball Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Basketball_Association) championship in 1968) led by Connie Hawkins (team then moved); the Pittsburgh Condors (ABA returned in 1970–72), the [Pittsburgh Piranhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Piranhas) (CBA Finals in 1995), the [Pittsburgh Xplosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Xplosion) (2004–08) and [Phantoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Phantoms_\(ABA\)) (2009–10) both of the [ABA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Basketball_Association_\(2000%E2%80%93present\)).

Three Pittsburgh universities, the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Panthers_men's_basketball), [Duquesne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duquesne_Dukes_men's_basketball), and [Robert Morris University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Morris_Colonials_men's_basketball), compete in NCAA Division I basketball. Pitt and Duquesne are the traditional basketball powers in the city, but all three universities have made multiple appearances in the [National Invitation Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Invitation_Tournament) and [NCAA tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Men's_Division_I_Basketball_Championship). Pitt won two pre-NCAA tournament National Championships in 1928 and 1930[^cite_note-229] while Duquesne won the NIT title in 1955, its second straight trip to the NIT title game. Both Pitt and Duquesne have reached the NCAA tournament Final Four once, Duquesne in 1940 and Pitt in 1941.

[Pittsburgh Panthers women's basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Panthers_women's_basketball) has qualified for 14 post season tournaments (including 4 NCAA tournaments) and boasts of 5 All-Americans selected 6 times with 3 WNBA players. Pitt women began play in 1914 before being reintroduced in 1970. Both Duquesne and Robert Morris also have competitive Division I women's basketball programs.

## Government and politics

### Government

Main article: [Government of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Pittsburgh)



The [Pittsburgh City-County Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_City-County_Building), the seat of government of the City of Pittsburgh

The [Government of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Pittsburgh) is composed of the [Mayor of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Pittsburgh), the [Pittsburgh City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_City_Council), and various boards and commissions. The mayor and the nine-member council each serve four-year terms. Since the 1950s the [Mayor's Chief of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Mayoral_Chief_of_Staff) has assumed a large role in advising, long term planning, and as a "gatekeeper" to the mayor. City council members are chosen by [plurality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_voting_system) elections in each of nine districts. The government's official offices are in the [Pittsburgh City-County Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_City-County_Building).

The [Pennsylvania Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Supreme_Court) holds sessions in Pittsburgh, as well as [Harrisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harrisburg) and Philadelphia. Pittsburgh is represented in the [Pennsylvania General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_General_Assembly) by three [Senate Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_Senate) and nine [House Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_House_of_Representatives). Federally, Pittsburgh is part of [Pennsylvania's 12th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania's_12th_congressional_district).

In 2006, Council President [Luke Ravenstahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luke_Ravenstahl) was sworn in as mayor at age 26, becoming the youngest mayor in the history of any major American city. His successor, [Bill Peduto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Peduto), was sworn in on January 6, 2014. In November 2021, Pittsburgh elected its first African-American mayor, [Ed Gainey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Gainey). In 2026, Corey O'Connor became the 62nd mayor of Pittsburgh.

Prior to the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War), Pittsburgh was strongly abolitionist. It is considered the [birthplace of the national Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_United_States_Republican_Party),[^cite_note-236] as the party held its first convention here in February 1856. From the Civil War to the 1930s, Pittsburgh was a [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Republican_Party) stronghold. The effects of the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), combined with entrenched local GOP scandals, resulted in a shift among voters to the Democratic Party. With the exceptions of the [1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_mayoral_election,_1973) and [1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_mayoral_election,_1977) elections (where lifelong Democrats ran off the party ticket), Democrats have been elected consecutively to the mayor's office since the [1933 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_mayoral_election,_1933). The city's ratio of party registration is 5 to 1 Democrat.[^cite_note-237]

Pittsburgh is represented in the [Pennsylvania General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_General_Assembly) by three [Senate Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_Senate) ([Lindsey Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsey_Williams) (D)-[38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania's_38th_Senatorial_District), [Wayne D. Fontana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_D._Fontana) (D)-[42](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania's_42nd_Senatorial_District), and [Jay Costa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Costa) (D)-[43](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania's_43rd_Senatorial_District)) and nine [House Districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_House_of_Representatives) ([Aerion Abney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerion_Abney)-19, [Emily Kinkead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Kinkead)-20, [Lindsay Powell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsay_Powell)-21, [Dan Frankel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Frankel_\(American_politician\))-23, [La'Tasha Mayes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La'Tasha_Mayes)-24, [Dan Deasy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Deasy)-27, [Abigail Salisbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abigail_Salisbury)-34, [Jessica Benham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jessica_Benham)-36, and John Inglis-38).

Federally, Pittsburgh is part of [Pennsylvania's 12th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania's_12th_congressional_district), represented by [Democrat](5043544.md) [Summer Lee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_Lee) since 2023.

### Law enforcement



A [Ford Taurus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Taurus) and a [Chevrolet Impala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet_Impala) belonging to the [Pittsburgh Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Police)

The area's largest law enforcement agency is the [Pittsburgh Bureau of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Bureau_of_Police), with close to 850 sworn officers. The city also has separate housing and school police departments. Other agencies also provide police protection within the city because of overlapping jurisdictional boundaries. The [Allegheny County Sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_County_Sheriff) focuses on jail and [courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_County_Courthouse) security. The [Allegheny County Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_County_Police) primarily patrols county-owned parks and airports, while providing detective/investigatory functions for smaller suburbs and the [Port Authority police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_of_Allegheny_County) patrols rapid transit. [Pennsylvania State Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_State_Police) Troop B provides patrols for the city and immediate suburbs.

The county's lead law enforcement officer is [Allegheny County District Attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_County_District_Attorney) [Stephen Zappala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Zappala) while the [Allegheny County Medical Examiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_County_Medical_Examiner) heads forensics. Crimes of a federal nature are covered by the [U.S. Attorney for Western Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Western_District_of_Pennsylvania#United_States_Attorneys).

### Crime

Pittsburgh annually ranks as one of America's safest big cities, in 2013 being named the third "most secure" big city by Farmers Insurance.[^cite_note-238] Among [crime rates of the 60 largest U.S. cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_cities_by_crime_rate), 43 had more instances of property crime while 16 had less when compared to Pittsburgh. More instances of violent crime were reported in 21 of the largest cities while 37 had less. The FBI recommends against using data for ranking.[^cite_note-239][^cite_note-240] Per 100,000 persons stats (2012):

|  | Murder | Rape | Robbery | Assault | Burglary | Theft | Motor vehicle | Total violent | Total property |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_cities_by_crime_rate) | 13.1 | 15.1 | 363.3 | 360.4 | 812.8 | 2,438.2 | 174.3 | 752.0 | 3,425.4 |

At the end of 2019, the Pittsburgh Bureau of Police reported 37 murders in the city that year.[^cite_note-241]

In Pittsburgh, the homicide rate for African Americans is seven times the national average.[^cite_note-Byrdsong-2015-242] Some people believe that over-reliance on law enforcement exacerbates homicide rates.[^cite_note-Byrdsong-2015-242] There is also concern regarding the effectiveness of law enforcement in solving these cases, as 97% of unsolved cases involved a black victim.[^cite_note-Benzing-2016-243] This has led certain residents to believe law enforcement to be ineffective or apathetic.[^cite_note-Benzing-2016-243] This is despite an increasing police budget. In 2023, members of the Pittsburgh City Council approved an increase to the police budget by \$6 million.[^cite_note-244] About 6% of this money is expected to go to the Stop the Violence trust fund. This fund goes to improving parks and recreation, various non-profits, and to the office of Community Health and Safety, in effort to holistically improve the social pressures supposedly causing violence in Pittsburgh.[^cite_note-245]

Some people do not believe these efforts to be adequate. In 2023, a study conducted by Police Scorecard rated the Pittsburgh Police Department at 37% quality (with 100% being the best). It rated Pittsburgh below the 50th percentile in 10 out of 15 categories studied: "Police Budget Cost per Person," "Fines/Forfeitures," "Police Presence/Over-Policing (Officers per Population)," "Force Used per Arrest," "Racial Disparities in Deadly Force," "Excessive Force Complaints Upheld," "Discrimination Complaints Upheld," "Criminal Misconduct Complaints Upheld," "Arrest Rate for Low Level Offenses," and "Racial Disparities in Drug Arrests".[^cite_note-246]

Pittsburgh is home to many colleges, universities and research facilities, the most well-known of which are [Carnegie Mellon University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_University), the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh), and [Duquesne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duquesne_University). Carnegie Mellon University (CMU) is a private research university founded by [Andrew Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Carnegie) and [Andrew Mellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Mellon).[^cite_note-usnews_college_rankings-247] CMU contains the [Mellon College of Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mellon_College_of_Science), [School of Computer Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_School_of_Computer_Science), [College of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_College_of_Engineering), [School of Business](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepper_School_of_Business), [Heinz College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_College), [College of Fine Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_Mellon_College_of_Fine_Arts), writing, [Social and Decision Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_and_Decision_Sciences), information systems, statistics, and psychology programs.

The University of Pittsburgh, established in 1787 and popularly referred to as "Pitt", is a [state-related](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-related) school with one of the nation's largest research programs.[^cite_note-edu-14] Pitt is known for the [Kenneth P. Dietrich School of Arts and Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_P._Dietrich_School_of_Arts_and_Sciences), [University of Pittsburgh Graduate School of Public and International Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh_Graduate_School_of_Public_and_International_Affairs), [University of Pittsburgh School of Information Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh_School_of_Information_Sciences), [Swanson School of Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swanson_School_of_Engineering), [University of Pittsburgh College of Business Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh_College_of_Business_Administration), [University of Pittsburgh School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh_School_of_Law), [University of Pittsburgh School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh_School_of_Medicine), [University of Pittsburgh School of Social Work](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh_School_of_Social_Work), and other biomedical and health-related sciences.[^cite_note-usnews_college_rankings-247][^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249][^cite_note-pitt-fall-07-250][^cite_note-251]

[Duquesne University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duquesne_University), a private Catholic university in the [Bluff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluff_\(Pittsburgh\)) neighborhood and is noted for its song and dance troupe, the [Duquesne University Tamburitzans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duquesne_University_Tamburitzans), as well as graduate programs in law, medicine, pharmacy, nursing, engineering, health sciences, business, music, and education. [Carlow University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlow_University) is a small private Catholic university that while coeducational, has traditionally educated women. [Chatham University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatham_University), a liberal arts college that was founded as a woman's college but became fully coeducational in 2015,[^cite_note-252] is in the Shadyside neighborhood, but also maintains a 388-acre (157 ha) Eden Hall Farm campus in the [North Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Hills,_Pennsylvania). [Point Park University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Park_University) was founded in 1961 and is well known for its Conservatory of Performing Arts and its [Pittsburgh Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Playhouse).

### Film

Pittsburgh's 116-year-old film industry accelerated after the 2006 passage of the [Pennsylvania Film Production Tax Credit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Film_Production_Tax_Credit).[^cite_note-260] According to the [Pittsburgh Film Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Film_Office), over 124 major motion pictures have been filmed, in whole or in part, in Pittsburgh, including *[The Mothman Prophecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mothman_Prophecies)*, *[Wonder Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wonder_Boys_\(film\))*,[^cite_note-post-gazette.com-261] *[Dogma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogma_\(film\))*,[^cite_note-post-gazette.com-261] *[Hoffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoffa_\(film\))*, *[The Silence of the Lambs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Silence_of_the_Lambs_\(film\))*,[^cite_note-post-gazette.com-261] *[Sudden Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudden_Death_\(1995_film\))*, *[Flashdance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flashdance)*,[^cite_note-post-gazette.com-261] [*Southpaw*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southpaw_\(film\)), *[Striking Distance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Striking_Distance)*, *[Mrs. Soffel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrs._Soffel)*, [*Jack Reacher*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Reacher_\(film\)), *[Inspector Gadget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_Gadget_\(film\))*, *[The Next Three Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Next_Three_Days)*, *[The Perks of Being a Wallflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Perks_of_Being_a_Wallflower)*,[^cite_note-post-gazette.com-261] *[Zack and Miri Make a Porno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zack_and_Miri_Make_a_Porno)*, and *[Fences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fences_\(film\))*.[^cite_note-post-gazette.com-261][^cite_note-262] Pittsburgh became "Gotham City" in 2011 during filming of *[The Dark Knight Rises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Dark_Knight_Rises)*.[^cite_note-Film-263] [George A. Romero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_A._Romero) shot nearly all his films in the area, including his *[Living Dead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romero's_Dead_series)* series.[^cite_note-264] From 2017 to 2023, Pittsburgh welcomed a series of major film and television productions like *[Fences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fences_\(film\))*, *[Mindhunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mindhunter_\(TV_series\))*, *[Ma Rainey's Black Bottom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma_Rainey's_Black_Bottom)*, *[Sweet Girl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_Girl_\(film\))*, and *[I'm Your Woman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I'm_Your_Woman_\(film\))*, significantly contributing to the local economy.[^cite_note-265][^cite_note-266]

Film production in Pittsburgh has notably impacted the region's economy and job creation, largely due to the 25% tax credit incentive established in 2007.[^cite_note-267][^cite_note-Film_Pittsburgh-268] The Pittsburgh Film Office states that the film and television industry provides employment to over 10,000 people and pays over \$500 million in wages in southwestern Pennsylvania.[^cite_note-269] Furthermore, the industry supports over 345,000 local businesses and contributes over \$41 billion to them.[^cite_note-Film_Pittsburgh-268]

Pittsburgh is home to several film festivals, film schools, and organizations that encourage and promote independent and diverse filmmakers. Notable film festivals include the [Three Rivers Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Rivers_Film_Festival), the Pittsburgh Shorts Film Festival, the JFilm Festival, the ReelAbilities Film Festival, and the Black Bottom Film Festival.[^cite_note-270][^cite_note-271] The local film schools include [Pittsburgh Filmmakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Filmmakers), [Point Park University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Park_University) - Cinema & Digital Arts, and [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh) - Film Studies.[^cite_note-272][^cite_note-273]

Pittsburgh's film studio infrastructure includes several sound stages and production facilities available for hire. ## Health care

See also: [List of hospitals in Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_Pittsburgh)



[UPMC Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPMC_Presbyterian)



[Allegheny General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_General_Hospital)

The two largest area health care providers are the [University of Pittsburgh Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh_Medical_Center) (UPMC) (since 1893) and [Allegheny Health Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allegheny_Health_Network) (since 1882).

The first military hospital in U.S. history and the first west of the Atlantic Plain—General Edward Hand Hospital—served the area from 1777 to 1845.[^cite_note-283] Since 1847, Pittsburgh has hosted the world's first "Mercy Hospital".[^cite_note-284] This was followed by West Penn hospital in 1848, Passavant Hospital in 1849,[^cite_note-chronology-29] the [University of Pittsburgh School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh_School_of_Medicine) in 1883, Children's Hospital in 1887, and [Magee Womens Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magee-Womens_Hospital_of_UPMC) in 1911. In 1954, Allegheny General (AGH) was among the first to administer [Cobalt therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobalt_therapy).[^cite_note-285]

In 1980, UPMC announced a \$250 million (\$1.11 billion today) expansion and also hired transplant pioneer [Thomas Starzl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Starzl).[^cite_note-286] In 1984, Allegheny General surgeons pioneered modern brain surgery. Starzl arranged the 1985 liver transplant of 5-year-old Amie Garrison as a UPMC surgery team flew to [Baylor University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baylor_University), starting its transplant program.[^cite_note-287] Also in 1985, UPMC surgeons Drs. Griffith, Hardesty, and Trento revealed a new device after a heart-lung transplant. In 1986, UPMC announced a \$230 million (\$676 million today) modernization. In 1996, UPMC's planned Sicily [ISMETT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISMETT) branch was approved by the Italian government as transplant surgeons to supervise and deliver the world's third (both earlier ones done at UPMC)--and first public—cross species marrow transplant at [University of California, San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Francisco).[^cite_note-288] UPMC's Thomas Detre founded the [International Society for Bipolar Disorders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Society_for_Bipolar_Disorders) at a world medical conference in Pittsburgh in 1999.[^cite_note-289]

The \$80 million (\$150 million today) [UPMC Sports Performance Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPMC_Sports_Performance_Complex) for the [Pittsburgh Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Panthers) & [Pittsburgh Steelers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Steelers) opened in 2000. In 2002, AGH opened its \$30 million (\$54.5 million today), 5-floor, 100,000 sq. ft., cancer center. The \$130 million (\$233 million today) 350,000 sq. ft. [Hillman Cancer Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillman_Cancer_Center) opened in October 2002,[^cite_note-290] and in 2005, UPMC entered into an 8-year, \$420 million (\$716 million today) agreement with [IBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IBM) to upgrade medical technologies & health information systems.[^cite_note-291]

In 2009, the \$600 million (\$897 million today) [UPMC Children's Hospital of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UPMC_Children's_Hospital_of_Pittsburgh) opened. The campus was featured in world news in 2012 for several unique approaches to patient care.[^cite_note-292] UPMC officially adopted in [Erie, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie,_Pennsylvania)'s [Hamot Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamot_Medical_Center) in 2010. The [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) announced a state of the art training facility with UPMC in 2012.[^cite_note-293] UPMC announced in 2013 it had partnered with [Nazarbayev University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazarbayev_University) to help found its medical school.[^cite_note-294]

### Health discoveries

While he was a professor at the [University of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Pittsburgh), American virologist [Jonas Salk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonas_Salk) developed one of the first successful [polio vaccines](192198.md), which came into use in 1955.

UPMC has pioneered several world firsts including the first known cystic fibrosis heart-lung transplant (1983), the world's first simultaneous liver and heart transplant operation on a child (6-year-old [Stormie Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stormie_Jones) in 1984), the youngest heart-lung transplant (9 years old in 1985), the world's first heart-liver-kidney transplant (1989), the world's first heart-liver transplant on an infant (1997),[^cite_note-295] the first pediatric heart-double lung-liver transplant (1998), the nation's first double hand transplant (2009), and the first total forearm and hand transplant (2010), as well as the state's first heart transplant (1968).[^cite_note-296][^cite_note-297]

*[The Lancet](463899.md)* published a 2012 UPMC study of two 9-year quadriplegics being able to move a robotic arm by thought, to pick up objects, shake hands, and even eat. Wiring the brain around spine damage to restore arm and leg muscle function was successful using robotic arms controlled via an embedded computer to translate signals near a small group of neurons with 200 needles.[^cite_note-298]

## Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Pittsburgh)



Aerial view of some of Pittsburgh's numerous bridges

Pittsburgh is a [city of bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridges_of_Pittsburgh). With 446,[^cite_note-venice-299] it has three bridges more than [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), Italy, which has historically held the title "City of Bridges".[^cite_note-300] Around 40 bridges cross the three rivers near the city. The [Smithfield Street Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smithfield_Street_Bridge) was the world's first lenticular truss bridge. The city's [Three Sisters Bridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Sisters_Bridges) offer a picturesque view of the city from the North. The southwestern "entrance" to Downtown for travelers coming in from [Interstate 79](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_79) and the [Pittsburgh International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_International_Airport) is through the [Fort Pitt Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Pitt_Tunnel) and over the [Fort Pitt Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Pitt_Bridge). The [Fort Duquesne Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Duquesne_Bridge) carrying [Interstate 279](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_279) is the main gateway from Downtown to both [PNC Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNC_Park), [Acrisure Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrisure_Stadium) and the [Rivers Casino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rivers_Casino_\(Pittsburgh\)). The [Panhandle Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panhandle_Bridge) carries Pittsburgh Regional Transit's Blue/Red/Silver subway lines across the [Monongahela River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongahela_River). The renovated [J\&L Steel Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jones_and_Laughlin_Steel_Company) bridge has been a key traffic/running-biking trail conduit connecting the [Southside Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southside_Works) and [Pittsburgh Technology Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Technology_Center). Over 2,000 bridges span the landscape of Allegheny County.[^cite_note-301]

### Public transportation statistics

Pittsburgh is served by [Pittsburgh Regional Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Regional_Transit), the 26th-largest transit agency in the country prior to the [COVID-19 pandemic](63136490.md). The average amount of time people spend commuting with public transit in Pittsburgh, for example to and from work, on a weekday is 73 minutes, while 23% of public transit riders ride for more than two hours every day. The average amount of time people wait at a stop or station for public transit is 17 minutes, while 33% of riders wait for over 20 minutes on average every day. The average distance people usually ride in a single trip with public transit is 3.9 mi (6.3 km), while 11% travel for over 7.5 mi (12 km) in a single direction.[^cite_note-302]

### Regional mass transit

Main articles: [Pittsburgh Light Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Light_Rail) and [Port Authority of Allegheny County § Bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Authority_of_Allegheny_County#Bus_rapid_transit)



[Steel Plaza subway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel_Plaza_\(PAT_station\))

[Pittsburgh Regional Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Regional_Transit), formerly known as the Port Authority of Allegheny County, is the region's mass transit system. While serving only a portion of the Pittsburgh area, the nation's 20th-largest metropolitan area, it is the 11th-largest transit agency in the United States.[^cite_note-304] Pittsburgh Regional Transit runs a network of intracity and intercity bus routes, the [Monongahela Incline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monongahela_Incline) [Funicular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funicular) railway (more commonly known as an "incline") on Mount Washington, a [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Light_Rail) system that runs mostly above-ground in the suburbs and underground as a subway in the city, and one of the nation's largest [busway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) systems.[^cite_note-305] Pittsburgh Regional Transit owns the [Duquesne Incline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duquesne_Incline) but it is operated by a non-profit preservation trust,[^cite_note-306] but accepts Pittsburgh Regional Transit passes and charges PRT fares.

The Bus System lines are [labeled by number and letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bus_routes_in_Pittsburgh). These are the largest portion of Pittsburgh Regional Transit and serve on streets and designated busways. Buses serve most of the county, extending as far as [Pittsburgh International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_International_Airport), [Monroeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monroeville,_Pennsylvania), [McCandless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McCandless,_Pennsylvania), and the borders of [Westmoreland County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmoreland_County,_Pennsylvania) and [Beaver County, Pennsylvania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaver_County,_Pennsylvania). Meanwhile, [the light rail system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Light_Rail) (commonly known as the "T") runs along both new tracks and those refurbished from the streetcar era. The light rail runs from [Acrisure Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acrisure_Stadium) to [South Hills Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Hills_Village) and [Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library,_Pennsylvania), taking commuters through one of two routes: one which serves [Castle Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castle_Shannon), [Mt. Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mt._Lebanon,_Pennsylvania), and [Beechview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beechview), and the other is an express line using railways through [Overbrook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overbrook_\(Pittsburgh\)).

### Freight rail



[Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Pittsburgh\)), built in 1903

Pittsburgh's rail industry dates to 1851 when the [Pennsylvania Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pennsylvania_Railroad) first opened service between the Pittsburgh and [Philadelphia](50585.md). The [Baltimore and Ohio Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore_and_Ohio_Railroad) entered the city in 1871. In 1865, [Andrew Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Carnegie) opened the [Pittsburgh Locomotive and Car Works](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Locomotive_and_Car_Works), which manufactured for the industry until 1919. Carnegie also founded the [Union Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Railroad_\(Pittsburgh\)) in 1894 for heavy freight services and it still serves the area's steel industry, while [George Westinghouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Westinghouse)'s [Wabtec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wabtec) has been a leader in rail engines and switching since 1869.

Pittsburgh is home to one of [Norfolk Southern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norfolk_Southern_Railway)'s busiest freight corridors, the [Pittsburgh Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Line), and operates up to 70 trains per day through the city. The suburban [Conway Rail Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway_Yard), built in 1889, was the largest freight rail center in the world from 1956 until 1980 and is today the nation's second-largest. [CSX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CSX), the other major freight railroad in the eastern U.S., also has [major operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Subdivision) around Pittsburgh.

### Port

The [Port of Pittsburgh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Pittsburgh_Commission) ranks as the [20th-largest port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ports_in_the_United_States) in the United States with almost 34 million short tons of river cargo for 2011. The port ranked ninth-largest in the U.S. when measured in domestic trade.[^cite_note-307]
