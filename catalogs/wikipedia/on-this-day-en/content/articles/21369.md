# Transport in Nicaragua

&#x20; **Transport in Nicaragua** revolves around road, air and water transport modalities.

## Road transport

The road infrastructure is very well spread across the Pacific side, while the Atlantic side has less infrastructure. As of 2009, from a total of 19,137 km 2,033 km are paved and 17,104 km are unpaved.[^cite_note-cia-factbook-1]

### Public transport

[Public transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) in [Nicaragua](21362.md) is mostly served by [buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buses) on both short and wide range distances. There are five different types, based on the size of the [vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vehicle), [target group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Target_group), frequency of stops and distance.[^cite_note-gestern-heute-öffentlicher-verkehr-2]

#### Urban buses



Former school bus at Ferrecalle in [Estelí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estel%C3%AD) used as urban bus on the line from Hospital to Oscar Gamez.



Former school bus at Hospital in [Estelí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estel%C3%AD) used as urban bus on the line from Hospital to Villa Cuba.

[Urban buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_bus) (*Urbanos*) can be found in [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua), [Estelí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estel%C3%AD), [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Nicaragua), [Chinandega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinandega), [Matagalpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matagalpa) and [Bluefields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluefields). In most cases, [passengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passengers) have to pay for each ride on a bus, with the need to pay again when switching to another. The costs differ from 2.50 C\$ in [Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua) to 10 C\$ in [Bluefields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bluefields).[^cite_note-gestern-heute-öffentlicher-verkehr-2]

An urban bus in Nicaragua takes the same road multiple times per day, following a more or less strict [schedule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport_timetable). The organization of the buses in different towns differs heavily as every town is organizing it on their own behalf. In Estelí, every bus driver is assisted by mostly two persons helping them (*Ayudantes*). Bus drivers in Managua have to manage their jobs on their own.[^cite_note-gestern-heute-öffentlicher-verkehr-2]

Another fact that heavily differs is the vehicles used in the different cities. In Managua mostly urban buses sponsored by [Russia](25391.md) are used, in Estelí former school buses from the [United States](3434750.md), in Bluefields [Japanese](15573.md) [light commercial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_commercial_vehicle) [vans](63679.md) and in León [pickup trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pickup_trucks) that got extended with seats and a roof.[^cite_note-gestern-heute-öffentlicher-verkehr-2]



Bus stop sign in [Estelí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estel%C3%AD)

The quality of [bus stops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_stops) also heavily differs. In the center of Managua many proper bus stops exist with roofs or at least signs, in other areas there often isn't any indication of a bus stop. Nevertheless, buses serve a network of established stops with common names known by bus assistants. Passengers need to know or ask where and when which bus stops.[^cite_note-gestern-heute-öffentlicher-verkehr-2]

To improve the accessibility of public transport, in 2016 the [OpenStreetMap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenStreetMap) group in Nicaragua [MapaNica](https://www.mapanica.net/comunidad.html) [crowdsourced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crowdsourcing) with the help of more than 150 citizens of Managua the first bus [transit map](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_map) in the whole of [Central America](6121.md).[^cite_note-3] Later in 2018, they made this data machine-accessible, serving it today in different apps on several platforms.[^cite_note-4]

Urban buses in Managua will use Brazilian [dual-mode bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual-mode_bus) and [hybrid electric bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_electric_bus) that are currently in evaluation process.

#### Suburban buses

Suburban buses (*Suburbanos*) connect larger cities with communities in outer areas. They only stop a few times inside the city, later nearly everywhere where passengers request to get off. Like with urban buses, a team serves a route several times per day and the service is organized by the local government. Prices can vary depending on the distance.[^cite_note-gestern-heute-öffentlicher-verkehr-2]

#### Ruteados



Ruteado at COTRAN Sur, [Estelí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estel%C3%AD)

Connecting two or more cities, Ruteados (also called *Servicio Ordinario*) are the biggest part of bus services in Nicaragua.[^cite_note-gestern-heute-öffentlicher-verkehr-2]

#### Express buses



Express bus in Nicaragua

[Express buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_bus_service) (*Expresos*) connect, like Ruteados and share taxis, two or more cities, but with less stops, resulting in a faster travel time.[^cite_note-gestern-heute-öffentlicher-verkehr-2]

#### Share taxis



Share taxi at COTRAN Norte, [Estelí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estel%C3%AD)

[Share taxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_taxis) are called *Interlocales* in Nicaragua and also connect two or more cities, like Ruteados and express buses, with the main difference that they depart from the bus station once they are filled either mostly or completely with passengers. Like express buses, they nearly don't stop between start and destination.[^cite_note-gestern-heute-öffentlicher-verkehr-2]

## Air transport

Several [airports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) are serving both national and international flights.

### Airports

Main article: [List of airports in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_airports_in_Nicaragua)

As of 2013, 147 airports exist in [Nicaragua](21362.md).[^cite_note-cia-factbook-1] [Nicaragua](21362.md)'s main international [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport) is [Managua International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Managua_International_Airport).

#### Airports - with paved runways

In total, there are 12 airports with paved runways with the following lengths:[^cite_note-cia-factbook-1]

- *2,438 to 3,047 m:* 3

- *1,524 to 2,437 m:* 2

- *914 to 1,523 m:* 3

- *under 914 m:* 4

#### Airports - with unpaved runways

In total, there are 135 airports with unpaved runways with the following lengths:[^cite_note-cia-factbook-1]

- *1,524 to 2,437 m:* 1

- *914 to 1,523 m:* 15

- *under 914 m:* 119

## Water transport

Nicaragua offers 2,220 km of [water transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_transport) roads, including the two large lakes [Lake Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Nicaragua) and [Lake Managua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Managua). A [Nicaragua Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaragua_Canal) was planned but canceled on 21 February 2018.[^cite_note-cancellation-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## Rail transport

Main article: [Rail transport in Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Nicaragua)

Since September 2001, all [rail transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport) has been suspended in [Nicaragua](21362.md).

## Rapid transit

Managua will have [metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) system that is currently in the feasibility study conducted by [Japan International Cooperation Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_International_Cooperation_Agency).
