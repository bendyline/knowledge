The **Italian railway system** is one of the most important parts of the [infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infrastructure) of Italy, with a total length[^cite_note-3] of 24,567 km (15,265 mi) of which active lines are 16,832 km (10,459 mi).[^cite_note-rete_rfi-2] The network has recently grown with the construction of the new [high-speed rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Italy). Italy is a member of the [International Union of Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Union_of_Railways) (UIC). The [UIC Country Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UIC_Country_Code) for Italy is 83. In 2024, the Italian national rail service [Trenitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenitalia) was recognized as the best rail passenger operator in Europe.[^cite_note-4]

## The network

See also: [Narrow-gauge railways in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-gauge_railways_in_Italy)



Map of Italian high-speed and higher speed rail network

*[RFI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rete_Ferroviaria_Italiana)* (*Rete Ferroviaria Italiana*, Italian Rail Network), a state owned [infrastructure manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railway_infrastructure_manager) which administers most of the Italian rail infrastructure. The Italian railway system has a length of 19,394 km (12,051 mi), of which 18,071 km (11,229 mi) [standard gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_gauge). The active lines are 16,723 km (10,391 mi),[^cite_note-rete_rfi-2] of which 7,505 km (4,663 mi) are double tracks.[^cite_note-rete_rfi-2] Italy has 2,507 people and 12.46 km<sup>2</sup> per kilometre of rail track, giving Italy the world's 13th-largest rail network.[^cite_note-network-size-5]

Lines are divided into 3 categories:&#x20;

- *fundamental lines* (*fondamentali*), which have high traffic and good infrastructure quality, comprise all the main lines between major cities throughout the country. Fundamental lines are 6,460 km (4,010 mi) long;

- *complementary lines* (*complementari*), which have less traffic and are responsible for connecting medium or small regional centres. Most of these lines are single track and some are not electrified;

- *node lines* (*di nodo*), which link complementary and fundamental lines near metropolitan areas for a total 950 km (590 mi).

  [^cite_note-rete_rfi-2]

Most of the Italian network is electrified (11,921 km (7,407 mi)). The electric system is 3 kV [DC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_Current) on conventional lines and [25 kV AC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/25_kV_AC_railway_electrification) on high-speed lines.[^cite_note-elet_rfi-6]

A major part of the Italian rail network is managed and operated by *[RFI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rete_Ferroviaria_Italiana)* (*Rete Ferroviaria Italiana*, Italian Rail Network). Other regional agencies, mostly owned by public entities such as [regional governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy), operate on the Italian network.

Travellers who often make use of the railway during their stay in Italy might use [rail passes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_pass), such as the European [Interrail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interrail) / [Eurail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurail) passes or Italy's national and regional passes. These rail passes allow travellers the freedom to use regional trains during the validity period, but all high-speed and intercity trains require up to a 15-euro reservation fee.[^cite_note-7] Regional passes, such as "Io viaggio ovunque Lombardia", offer one-day, multiple-day and monthly periods of validity. There are also saver passes for adults, who travel as a group, with savings up to 20%. Foreign travellers should purchase these passes in advance so that the passes can be delivered by post prior to the trip. When using the rail passes, the date of travel needs to be filled in before boarding the trains.[^cite_note-8]

The first line to be built on the peninsula was the [Naples–Portici line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples%E2%80%93Portici_railway_line), in the [Kingdom of the Two Sicilies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_the_Two_Sicilies), which was 7.64 km (4.75 mi) long and was inaugurated on 3 October 1839, nine years after the world's first "modern" inter-city railway, the [Liverpool and Manchester Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_and_Manchester_Railway).[^cite_note-10] The following year the firm Holzhammer of [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano) was granted the "Imperial-Royal privilege" to build the [Milano–Monza line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan%E2%80%93Monza_railway_line) (12 km (7.5 mi)), the second railway built in Italy, in the then [Kingdom of Lombardy–Venetia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Lombardy%E2%80%93Venetia), a part of the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire).[^cite_note-11]

After the creation of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) in 1861, a project was started to build a network from the [Alps](981.md) to [Sicily](27619.md), in order to connect the country. After unification, construction of new lines was boosted: in 1875, with the completion of the section [Orte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orte)-[Orvieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orvieto), the direct [Florence–Rome line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence%E2%80%93Rome_railway) was completed, reducing the travel time of the former route passing through [Foligno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foligno)-Terontola.[^cite_note-12] Private companies were definitively bought back by the Italian state on 1 July 1905, with the creation of the [Ferrovie dello Stato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovie_dello_Stato) (State Railways), or FFSS, with a total of 10,557 km (6,560 mi) of lines, of which it already owned 9,686 km (6,019 mi). The move was completed the following year with the acquisition of the remaining SFM network: by then FFSS possessed 13,075 km (8,124 mi) of lines, of which 1,917 km (1,191 mi) with double tracks.[^cite_note-13]

The period from 1922 to 1939 was heavy with important construction and modernisation programmes for the Italian railways, which also incorporated 400 km (250 mi) from the *Ferrovie Reali Sarde* of [Sardinia](29376.md). The most important programme was that of the [Rome–Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome%E2%80%93Formia%E2%80%93Naples_railway) and [Bologna–Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna%E2%80%93Florence_railway) *direttissimas* ("very direct lines"): the first reduced the travel time from the two cities by an hour and a half; the second, announced proudly as "constructing [Fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Fascism)", included the second longest tunnel in the world at the time, under the Apennines.[^cite_note-14] Electrification on 3,000 V direct current was introduced, which later supplanted the existing three-phase system. Other improvements included automatic blocks, light signals, construction of numerous main stations ([Milan Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan_Central_railway_station), Napoli Mergellina, Roma Ostiense and others) and other technical modernisations. The first high-speed train was the Italian [ETR 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETR_200), which in July 1939 went from [Milan](36511.md) to [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) at 165 km/h (105 mph), with a top speed of 203 km/h (126 mph).[^cite_note-15] With this service, the railway was able to compete with the upcoming aeroplanes. The [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) stopped these services.

After World War II, Italy started to repair the damaged railways and built nearly 20,000 km (12,000 mi) of new tracks. Entire lines were out of action and much of the rolling stock was destroyed. Thanks to the [Marshall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Plan), in the following years they could be rebuilt, although the possibility of reorganizing the network was missed due to short-sighted policies.[^cite_note-16] The main [Battipaglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battipaglia)-[Reggio Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_Calabria) line (running along the west coast) was doubled, while a program of updating of infrastructures, superstructures, services, colour-light signalling and cars was updated or extended. The three-phase lines were gradually turned into standard 3,000 V dc lines.

Increasing numbers of steam locomotives were replaced by electric or diesel ones; in the 1960s also the first unified passenger cars appeared and the first attempts of interoperability with foreign companies were started, culminating in the creation of [Trans Europe Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans_Europe_Express) services. Nowadays the [rail tracks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_tracks) and infrastructure are managed by [Rete Ferroviaria Italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rete_Ferroviaria_Italiana) (RFI),[^cite_note-17] while the train and the passenger section is managed mostly by [Trenitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenitalia). Both are [Ferrovie dello Stato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovie_dello_Stato) (FS) subsidiaries, once the only train operator in Italy.

The Italian high-speed service began in 1938 with an electric-multiple-unit [ETR 200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETR_200), designed for 200 km/h (120 mph), between Bologna and Naples. It too reached 160 km/h (99 mph) in commercial service, and achieved a world mean speed record of 203 km/h (126 mph) between Florence and Milan in 1938.

High-speed trains were developed during the 1960s. [E444](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FS_Class_E444) locomotives were the first standard locomotives capable of top speed of 200 km/h (125 mph), while an [ALe 601](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALe_601?action=edit\&redlink=1) [electrical multiple unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_multiple_unit) (EMU) reached a speed of 240 km/h (150 mph) during a test. Other EMUs, such as the [ETR 220](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETR_220?action=edit\&redlink=1), [ETR 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETR_250?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [ETR 300](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETR_300), were also updated for speeds up to 200 km/h (125 mph). The braking systems of cars were updated to match the increased travelling speeds.

On 25 June 1970, work was started on the [Rome–Florence *Direttissima*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence%E2%80%93Rome_high-speed_railway_line), the first high-speed line in Italy and in [Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-speed_rail_in_Europe). It included the 5,375-metre-long (3.340 mi) bridge on the [Paglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paglia) river, then the longest in Europe. Works were completed in the early 1990s.

In 1975, a program for a widespread updating of the rolling stock was launched. However, as it was decided to put more emphasis on local traffic, this caused a shifting of resources from the ongoing high-speed projects, with their subsequent slowing or, in some cases, total abandonment. Therefore, 160 [E.656](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FS_Class_E.656) electric and 35 [D.345](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FS_Class_D.345) locomotives for short-medium range traffic were acquired, together with 80 EMUs of the [ALe 801/940](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FS_Class_ALe_801/940) class, 120 [ALn 668](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FS_Class_ALn_668) diesel railcars. Some 1,000 much-needed passenger and 7,000 freight cars were also ordered.

In the 1990s, work started on the [Treno Alta Velocità](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treno_Alta_Velocit%C3%A0) (*TAV*) project, which involved building a new high-speed network on the routes [Milan](36511.md) – (Bologna–Florence–Rome–Naples) – [Salerno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salerno), [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin) – (Milan–Verona–Venice) – [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) and Milan–[Genoa](47332321.md). Most of the planned lines have already been opened, while international links with [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_France), [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Switzerland), [Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Austria) and [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Slovenia) are underway.

Most of the [Rome–Naples line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome%E2%80%93Naples_high-speed_railway_line) opened in December 2005, the [Turin–Milan line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin%E2%80%93Milan_high-speed_railway_line) partially opened in February 2006 and the [Milan–Bologna line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan%E2%80%93Bologna_high-speed_railway_line) opened in December 2008. The remaining sections of the Rome–Naples and the Turin–Milan lines and the [Bologna–Florence line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna%E2%80%93Florence_high-speed_railway_line) were completed in December 2009. All these lines are designed for speeds up to 300 km/h (190 mph). Since then, it is possible to travel from Turin to Salerno (ca. 950 km (590 mi)) in less than 5 hours. More than 100 trains per day are operated.[^cite_note-22]

Other proposed high-speed lines are [Salerno-Reggio Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salerno%E2%80%93Reggio_Calabria_high-speed_railway_line?action=edit\&redlink=1)[^cite_note-23] (connected to Sicily with the future [bridge over the Strait of Messina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Messina_Bridge)[^cite_note-24]), [Palermo-Catania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palermo%E2%80%93Catania_high-speed_railway_line?action=edit\&redlink=1)[^cite_note-25] and [Naples–Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naples%E2%80%93Bari_high-speed_railway_line?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-26]

The main public operator of high-speed trains (*alta velocità AV*, formerly [Eurostar Italia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurostar_Italia)) is [Trenitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenitalia), part of [FSI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovie_dello_Stato_Italiane). Trains are divided into three categories (called "[Le Frecce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Frecce)"): *[Frecciarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frecciarossa)* ("Red arrow") trains operate at a maximum of 300 km/h (185 mph) on dedicated high-speed tracks; *[Frecciargento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frecciargento)* (Silver arrow) trains operate at a maximum of 250 km/h (155 mph) on both high-speed and mainline tracks; *[Frecciabianca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frecciabianca)* (White arrow) trains operate at a maximum of 200 km/h (125 mph) on mainline tracks only.[^cite_note-27]

Since 2012, a new and Italy's first private train operator, [NTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuovo_Trasporto_Viaggiatori) (branded as Italo), run high-speed services in competition with [Trenitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trenitalia). Even nowadays, Italy is the only country in Europe with a private high-speed train operator.

Construction of the [Milan-Venice high-speed line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan%E2%80%93Verona_high-speed_railway) began in 2013 and in December 2016 the Milan-Treviglio-Brescia section has been opened to passenger traffic;[^cite_note-28] the Milan-Genoa high-speed line (Terzo Valico dei Giovi) is also under construction.

Today it is possible to travel from Rome to Milan in less than 3 hours (2h 55' without intermediate stops) with the [Frecciarossa 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frecciarossa_1000), the new high-speed train. As of June 2024, there are 46 Trenitalia [^cite_note-29] and 33 Italo [^cite_note-30] round-trip high-speed trains every weekday that cover this route,

## Night trains

Main article: [Nightjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightjet)



*[Nightjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightjet)* Route Map (2023)

The *[Nightjet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nightjet)* of the [Austrian Federal Railways (ÖBB)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96BB) serves different major cities in Italy like Rome, Venice, Florence and Milano. The trains can be used for rides inside Italy as well as for journeys abroad.

*Nightjet* trains offer beds in [sleeper carriages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sleeper_carriage) (Nightjet's most comfortable service category), [couchette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couchette) carriages, and seated carriages. On certain connections, cars can also be transported on the train. Bikes can be transported in a bike transport bag, or on some connections also in special bike racks.

Trenitalia operates many night trains within Italy, under the brand Intercity Notte.[^cite_note-31] Routes include connections between the Northern Italian cities of Turin, Milan, and Trieste, to Rome and further to Southern Italian cities such as Lecce or Palermo and Syracuse in Sicily. The trains offer standard seats as well as modern couchettes and sleeping compartments.

## Intercity trains

Main articles: [Inter-city rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter-city_rail) and [InterCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCity)



An Italian InterCity train at [Napoli Centrale railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoli_Centrale_railway_station)



An Italian InterCity train at [Roma Termini railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_Termini_railway_station)

With the introduction of high-speed trains, intercity trains are limited to a few services per day on mainline and regional tracks.

The daytime services (*[InterCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCity)* IC), while not frequent and limited to one or two trains per route, are essential in providing access to cities and towns off the railway's mainline network. The main routes are [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) to [Rome](25458.md) (stopping at [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), [Bologna](21069333.md), [Prato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prato), [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) and [Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arezzo)), [Milan](36511.md) to Rome (stopping at [Genoa](47332321.md), [La Spezia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Spezia), [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) and [Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livorno) / stopping at [Parma](24231.md), [Modena](62418.md), Bologna, Prato, Florence and Arezzo), Bologna to [Lecce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecce) (stopping at [Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimini), [Ancona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancona), [Pescara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pescara), [Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari) and [Brindisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brindisi)) and Rome to [Reggio di Calabria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggio_di_Calabria) (stopping at [Latina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latina,_Lazio) and [Naples](55880.md)). In addition, the Intercity trains provide a more economical means of long-distance rail travel within Italy.

The night trains (*Intercity Notte* ICN) have sleeper compartments and washrooms, but no showers on board. The main routes are Rome to [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano)/Bozen (calling at Florence, Bologna, [Verona](263991.md), [Rovereto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rovereto) and [Trento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trento)), Milan to [Lecce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lecce) (calling at Piacenza, Parma, Reggio Emilia, Modena, Bologna, Faenza, Forlì, Cesena, Rimini, Ancona, Pescara, Bari and Brindisi), [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin) to Lecce (calling at [Alessandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alessandria), [Voghera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voghera), [Piacenza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piacenza), Parma, Bologna, Rimini, Pescara, Termoli, San Severo, Foggia, Barletta, Bisceglie, Molfetta, Bari, Monopoli, Fasano, Ostuni and Brindisi) and Reggio di Calabria to Turin (calling at Naples, Rome, [Livorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livorno), La Spezia and Genova). Most portions of these ICN services run during the night; since most services take 10 to 15 hours to complete a one-way journey, their daytime portion provides extra train connections to complement the Intercity services.

- [Emilia-Romagna](162715.md): [Trasporto Passeggeri Emilia-Romagna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trasporto_Passeggeri_Emilia-Romagna) provides vital connections across cities on different mainline networks, including Modena, Parma, Suzzara, Ferrara, Reggio Emilia and Bologna.

2 cities have [tram-train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tram-train) system, [Rome](25458.md) and [Sassari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassari). The [Rome–Giardinetti railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome%E2%80%93Giardinetti_railway) connects [Laziali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Termini-Laziali_\(Rome%E2%80%93Giardinetti_railway\)) (a regional train station some 800 metres (2,625 ft) from [Termini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roma_Termini_railway_station)'s main concourse) with Giardinetti to the east just past the [Grande Raccordo Anulare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Raccordo_Anulare), Rome's orbital motorway.[^cite_note-43] It is run by [ATAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATAC_\(Rome\)), the company responsible for public transportation in the city, which also operates the [Rome Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_Metro).[^cite_note-44] The present railway is the only part of the old and longer [Rome–Fiuggi–Alatri–Frosinone railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome%E2%80%93Fiuggi%E2%80%93Alatri%E2%80%93Frosinone_railway) to be in service. The latest shortening of the line occurred in 2008 with the closing of the Giardinetti–Pantano section, which has now become part of the [Metro Line C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Line_C_\(Rome_Metro\)).[^cite_note-45] The line had been due to be dismantled in 2016 to be replaced with a bus lane along Via Casilina,[^cite_note-46] but in March 2015 it was announced that the line would instead be retained and modernised.[^cite_note-47]

[Metrosassari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrosassari),[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] also called *Sassari tramway*, *Sassari tram-train* or *Sassari metro-tramway* ([Italian](14708.md): *Metrotranvia di Sassari* or Italian: *Metropolitana leggera di Sassari*) is the commercial name of a [tram-train](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tram-train)[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] line in [Sassari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassari), [Sardinia](29376.md), Italy, operated by the regional public transport company *ARST* (*Azienda Regionale Sarda Trasporti*). Despite having been built in the early 2000s, in the urban section the line was built with single track and [narrow gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow_gauge), to connect with the same 950 mm (3 ft 1+3⁄8 in) gauge used in the secondary railway lines in Sardinia. The 2.45 km (1.52 mi) tramway part of the line (*[Stazione](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassari_railway_station)* - *Emiciclo Garibaldi*) opened in October 2006, linking the railway station with the city centre via the hospital district.[^cite_note-53] On 27 September 2009 the line was extended into the peripheral district of Santa Maria di Pisa, running on the electrified portion[^cite_note-54] of the [Sassari–Sorso railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassari%E2%80%93Sorso_railway?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-55] The main part of the network was in 2013 in the advanced development phase. It is under construction is the extension of the line from Santa Maria di Pisa to Li Punti and Baldinca, and the electrification of the railway to [Sorso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorso), 10 km from Sassari. It is also planned to convert and electrify the 28 km Sassari-Alghero railway to allow the trams to reach the village of [Olmedo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olmedo,_Sardinia), [Fertilia Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertilia_Airport) and the town of [Alghero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alghero).

## Rail links to adjacent countries



The [Marseille-Ventimiglia railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Ventimiglia_railway) line in [Ventimiglia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventimiglia,_Italy), near the French border



[Simplon Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplon_Tunnel) is a railway tunnel on the [Simplon railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplon_railway) that connects [Brig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brig,_Switzerland), Switzerland and [Domodossola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domodossola), Italy, through the [Alps](981.md).

Italy has 11 rail border crossings over the [Alpine mountains](981.md) with her neighbouring countries: six are designated as mainline tracks and two are metre-gauge tracks. The six mainline border crossings are: two with [France](5843419.md) (one for Nice and Marseille; the other for Lyon and Dijon), two with [Switzerland](26748.md) (one for Brig, Bern and Geneva; the other for Chiasso, Lugano, Lucerne and Zürich), and two with Austria (one for Innsbruck; the other for Villach, Graz and Vienna). The two-metre-gauge track crossings are located at the border town of [Tirano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirano) (enters Switzerland's Canton [Graubünden/Grisons](169850.md)) and Domodossola (enters Switzerland's canton of [Ticino](84147.md)).

There was a railway line connecting Italy's northeastern port of [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) to [Ljubljana](42374.md) ([Slovenia](27338.md)) and [Vienna](55866.md), built when Trieste was part of the [Austro-Hungarian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary)\:the [Austrian Southern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Southern_Railway). This railway stopped operating at the end of [World War II](32927.md).Direct connections between Trieste and Ljubljana have resumed since September 2018.[^cite_note-56]

- [Italy-France](47329029.md): [Marseille-Ventimiglia railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marseille-Ventimiglia_railway), usually a change of train is required in Ventimiglia (former direct Milan-Marseille services by Thello do not run anymore since 2019)

- [Italy-Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy-Switzerland_border): [Simplon Tunnel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplon_Tunnel), currently [EuroCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroCity) (EC) trains of [SBB CFF FFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Federal_Railways) Milan-Geneva and Milan-Bern

## Heritage railways

See also: [List of heritage railways in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_heritage_railways_in_Italy)



Historic train at the [Cansano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cansano) railway station, along the now tourist [Sulmona–Isernia railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulmona%E2%80%93Isernia_railway?action=edit\&redlink=1) in [Italy](14532.md)

In Italy, the [heritage railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heritage_railways) institute is recognized and protected by law no. 128 of 9 August 2017, which has as its objective the protection and valorisation of disused, suspended or abolished railway lines, of particular cultural, landscape and tourist value, including both railway routes and stations and the related works of art and appurtenances, on which, upon the proposal of the regions to which they belong, tourism-type traffic management is applied (art. 2, paragraph 1).[^cite_note-gazzettaufficiale.it-60] At the same time, the law identified a first list of 18 tourist railways, considered to be of particular value (art. 2, paragraph 2).[^cite_note-gazzettaufficiale.it-60]

The list is periodically updated by decree of the [Ministry of Infrastructure and Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Infrastructure_and_Transport_\(Italy\)), in agreement with the [Ministry of Economy and Finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Economy_and_Finance_\(Italy\)) and the [Ministry of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_\(Italy\)), also taking into account the reports in the State-Regions Conference, a list which in 2022 reached 26 railway lines.[^cite_note-mit.gov.it-61] According to article 1, law 128/2017 has as its purpose: "the protection and valorisation of railway sections of particular cultural, landscape and tourist value, which include railway routes, stations and related works of art and appurtenances, and of the historic and tourist rolling stock authorized to travel along them, as well as the regulation of the use of ferrocycles".[^cite_note-gazzettaufficiale.it-60]

[Bernina railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernina_railway_line) between [Poschiavo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poschiavo), Switzerland, and [Tirano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirano), Italy

The [Bernina railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernina_railway_line) is a [single-track](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single_track_\(rail\)) 1,000 mm (3 ft 3+3⁄8 in) [metre gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_gauge) railway line forming part of the [Rhaetian Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhaetian_Railway) (RhB). It links the spa resort of [St. Moritz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Moritz), in the [canton](180752.md) of [Graubünden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graub%C3%BCnden), [Switzerland](26748.md), with the town of [Tirano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tirano), in the [Province of Sondrio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Sondrio), [Italy](14532.md), via the [Bernina Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernina_Pass). Reaching a height of 2,253 metres (7,392 ft) above sea level, it is the [third highest railway crossing in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_highest_railways_in_Europe). It also ranks as the highest [adhesion railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adhesion_railway) of the continent, and – with [inclines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grade_\(slope\)) of up to 7% – as one of the steepest adhesion railways in the world. The elevation difference on the section between the Bernina Pass and Tirano is 1,824 m (5,984 ft), allowing passengers to view glaciers along the line. On 7 July 2008, the Bernina line and the [Albula railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albula_railway_line), which also forms part of the RhB, were recorded in the list of [UNESCO World Heritage Sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UNESCO_World_Heritage_Sites), under the name *[Rhaetian Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhaetian_Railway) in the Albula / [Bernina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernina_Express) Landscapes*. The whole site is a cross-border joint Swiss-Italian heritage area. Trains operating on the Bernina line include the [Bernina Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernina_Express).

In July 2023, [Ferrovie dello Stato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovie_dello_Stato) established a new company, the "FS Treni Turistici Italiani" (English: FS Italian Tourist Trains), with the mission "to propose an offer of railway services expressly designed and calibrated for quality, sustainable tourism and attentive to rediscovering the riches of the Italian territory. Tourism that can experience the train journey as an integral moment of the holiday, an element of quality in the overall tourist experience".[^cite_note-fsitaliane.it-68] There are three service areas proposed:&#x20;

- *[Luxury trains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luxury_train)*, which includes the circulation of the "[Orient Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orient_Express) - La Dolce Vita" from 2024, and [Venice Simplon Orient Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice_Simplon_Orient_Express), already operating on European routes;

  [^cite_note-fsitaliane.it-68]

- *[Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Express_train) and historic trains*, with the express trains of the 1980s and 1990s being redeveloped and modernized in the railway workshops of [Rimini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rimini), while the historic trains are used for journeys that include stops with guided tours and tastings;

  [^cite_note-fsitaliane.it-68]

- *Regional trains*, also with trips that include experiential tourist stops, which pass through places rich in history, with villages and areas of landscape, naturalistic, food and wine and agri-food interest.

  [^cite_note-fsitaliane.it-68]
