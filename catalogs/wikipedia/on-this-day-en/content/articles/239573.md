**Chaiyaphum** ([Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_language): ชัยภูมิ, pronounced [\[tɕʰāj.jā.pʰūːm\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Thai); [Northeastern Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeastern_Thai_language): ไซยภูมิ, pronounced [\[sa᷇j.jā.pʰu᷇ːm\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Lao)) is one of Thailand's seventy-six [provinces](204637.md) (*changwat*), located in [central northeastern Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isan#Administrative_divisions), also called [Isan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isan). Neighboring provinces are (from north clockwise) [Khon Kaen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khon_Kaen_province), [Nakhon Ratchasima](204656.md), [Lopburi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lopburi_province), and [Phetchabun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phetchabun_province).

## Toponymy

The word *chaiya* originates from the [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) word *jaya* meaning 'victory', and the word *phum* from Sanskrit *bhumi* meaning 'earth' or 'land'. Hence the name of the province literally means 'land of victory'. The Malay/Indonesian/Sanskrit word *jayabumi* is equivalent.

## Geography



The Luak ridge of the Phetchabun Mountains and the Sonthi River valley, from the Sut Phaen Din viewpoint of [Pa Hin Ngam National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pa_Hin_Ngam_National_Park).

The province is bisected by the [Phetchabun mountain range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phetchabun_Mountains), with the highest elevation in the province at 1,222 m. The east of the province is part of the [Khorat Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khorat_Plateau). The total forest area is 3,982 km<sup>2</sup> (1,537 sq mi) or 31.4 percent of provincial area.[^cite_note-AREA-1]

[Tat Ton National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tat_Ton_National_Park) is in the northwest, featuring some scenic waterfalls and dry [dipterocarp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dipterocarp) forests. The biggest attraction of the [Sai Thong National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sai_Thong_National_Park) in the west is the Sai Thong waterfall, but also some fields of the [Siam tulip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siam_tulip). Similar fields can be found in the [Pa Hin Ngam National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pa_Hin_Ngam_National_Park) in the southwest. This park's name ('beautiful rock forest') derives from the strangely shaped rock formations found there. [Phu Laenkha National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phu_Laen_Kha_National_Park) covers 200 km<sup>2</sup> of forested hills northwest of Chaiyaphum city.

## History

Two inscribed *[sema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_sema)* stones, Buddhist boundary markers, are recorded from [Kaset Sombun district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaset_Sombun_district). K 404 was read by Cha-em Kaeokhlai in 1989 as praising Cuḍāmaṇī, a lady or queen of high rank on whose support the power and glory of the king's realm rested. Stephen Murphy rejects the basis of that reading, holding that nothing in the text supports even the proposal that Cuḍāmaṇī was the name of a person.[^cite_note-sema2010-6]<sup>: 268 </sup> A second stone from the district, in [Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit) written in Khmer script, mentions a *sugatapratimavuddhasima*. The term has been read as a Buddha image set up inside a boundary marked by *sema*, rather than a space for monks to assemble in. It is now held at the Phimai National Museum in [Nakhon Ratchasima province](204656.md).[^cite_note-sema2010-6]<sup>: 268 </sup>

The city of Chaiyaphum is often said to date from the 12th century, when it is described as a small place on a route running west from [Angkor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angkor) towards [Prasat Muang Sing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mueang_Sing_historical_park) in what is now [Kanchanaburi province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanchanaburi_province).[^cite_note-9] Seven Sanskrit inscriptions of *śaka* 1108, that is 1186, record hospital foundations of the Angkorian king [Jayavarman VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jayavarman_VII) (r. 1181–1218). They are numbered K.368, K.375, K.386, K.387, K.395, K.402 and K.952, and were found at sites in the [Nakhon Ratchasima](204656.md), [Chaiyaphum](239573.md) and [Surin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surin_province) regions, at Sai Fong on the Mekong, in what is now [Laos](17752.md), and near [Ubon](242147.md). One of the outliers of the group was found in this province.[^cite_note-13th-7]<sup>: 50 </sup> All of Jayavarman VII's hospital inscriptions open by invoking the same three deities, the medicine Buddha Bhaiṣajyaguru with Sūryavairocana and Candravairocana.[^cite_note-maxwell-8]<sup>: 44 </sup>

In 1817 the area was settled by [Lao people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_people) led by Nai Lae, an official of King [Anouvong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anouvong) of [Vientiane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vientiane), which was a tributary state of the Thai monarch. They settled in Baan Nam Khun Nong E Chan in Nakhon Ratchasima province, but soon abandoned it in favor of Ban Luang (today's city of Chaiyaphum). In 1826 Anouvong rebelled against the Thai King [Rama III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama_III), seeking to gain complete independence. Nai Lae, by then made a [chao praya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chao_praya) by the Thai monarch, supported the Siamese troops. Chao Phaya Lae was killed defending his city against Anouvong's army, but Anouvong was defeated by Thai forces weeks later and Anouvong taken in chains to [Bangkok](56615.md). King Rama III remembered Chao Phaya Lae for his loyalty and awarded him the title Phraya Phakdi Chumpon. He is still a local hero and his statue has become a symbol of the province.

## People

Most people in Chaiyaphum province are ethnically [Lao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_people). The first language of most people is the [Lao language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lao_language).

## Economy



Rice, Chaiyaphum province

The principal crops of Chaiyaphum include [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [tapioca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapioca), [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane), and [taro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taro) root. [Chulabhorn Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chulabhorn_Dam), in Thung Lui Lai Subdistrict, [Khon San district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khon_San_district) is a major source of irrigation water. After the drought of 2019 the Royal Irrigation Department proposed three more dams in Chaiyaphum. They are Wang Saphung Dam in Nong Bua Daeng District, Lam Nam Chee Dam in Ban Khwao District, and Prong Khun Petch Dam in Nong Bua Rawe District. The dams would have a combined capacity of 160 million m<sup>3</sup> of water, which could irrigate 127,000 [rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_\(unit\)) of farmland.[^cite_note-10]

## Health

[Chaiyaphum Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaiyaphum_Hospital) is the main hospital of the province, operated by the [Ministry of Public Health](23194319.md).

## Symbols

The provincial seal shows a triangular flag, a symbol of victory in war.

The [provincial tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Thai_provincial_trees) is the Siamese senna (*[Cassia siamea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senna_siamea)*), and the provincial flower the Siam Tulip (*[Curcuma alismatifolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curcuma_alismatifolia)*). The provincial aquatic life is the [bronze featherback](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_featherback) (*Notopterus notopterus*).

The provincial slogan เมืองโบราณ บ้านนักสู้ ภูเสียดฟ้า ป่าช้างหลาย ทุ่งไพรรก น้ำตกใส ผ้าไหมดี สตรีงาม แดนธรรมแดนทอง translates to 'Enchanted city, home to heroes, mountain peaks, elephant forests, waterfalls, beautiful silk, lovely ladies, conscientious Buddhists'.

### Local government

As of 26 November 2019 there are:[^cite_note-11] one Chaiyaphum Provincial Administration Organisation (*_ongkan borihan suan changwat_*) and 36 municipal (*thesaban*) areas in the province. Chaiyaphum has town (*[thesaban mueang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban_mueang)*) status. Further 35 subdistrict municipalities (*[thesaban tambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thesaban_tambon)*). The non-municipal areas are administered by 106 Subdistrict Administrative Organisations - SAO (*ongkan borihan suan tambon*).
