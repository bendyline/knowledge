[Māori Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_Battalion) soldiers performing a [haka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haka) in [Helwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helwan), Egypt for [George II of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_II_of_Greece), July 1941.

The **military history of New Zealand during World War II** began when [New Zealand](4913064.md) entered the [Second World War](32927.md) by declaring war on [Nazi Germany](21212.md) with the [United Kingdom](31717.md) in 1939, and expanded to the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War) when New Zealand declared war on [Imperial Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japan) following the [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) in 1941. New Zealand's forces were soon serving across Europe and beyond, where their reputation was generally very good. In particular, the force of the New Zealanders who were stationed in [Northern Africa](21714.md) and [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe) was known for its strength and determination, and uniquely so on both sides.

New Zealand provided personnel for service in the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) (RAF) and in the [Royal Navy](26061.md) and was prepared to have New Zealanders serving under British command. There were many instances of New Zealanders commanding troops on their own, whether their own or a mixture of theirs and foreign ones; notable commanders include Lieutenant-General [Bernard Freyberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Freyberg,_1st_Baron_Freyberg) and Lieutenant-Colonel [Fred Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Baker_\(soldier\)). Their stationing in North Africa and Greece, specifically during the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_El_Alamein) and [Second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_El_Alamein) [Battles of El Alamein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_El_Alamein) and the Freyburg-commanded [Crete campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Crete), was generally well known on either side for its strength, effectiveness and "spiritual" connection.[^cite_note-pilgrimage-1] [Royal New Zealand Air Force](190903.md) (RNZAF) pilots, many trained in the [Empire Air Training Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Air_Training_Scheme), were sent to Europe but, unlike the other [Dominions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion), New Zealand did not insist on its aircrews serving with RNZAF squadrons, so speeding up the rate at which they entered service. The [Long Range Desert Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Range_Desert_Group) was formed in North Africa in 1940 with New Zealand and Rhodesian as well as British volunteers, but included no Australians for the same reason.

The [New Zealand government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_New_Zealand) placed the [New Zealand Division of the Royal Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_New_Zealand_Navy) at the [Admiralty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Admiralty)'s disposal and made available to the RAF 30 new [Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_Wellington) medium bombers waiting in the United Kingdom for shipping to New Zealand. The [New Zealand Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Army) contributed the 2nd [New Zealand Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Expeditionary_Force) (2NZEF).

## Background

Diplomatically, New Zealand had expressed vocal opposition to [fascism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) in Europe and also to the [appeasement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appeasement) of fascist dictatorships,[^cite_note-2] and national sentiment for a strong show of force met with general support. Economic and defensive considerations also motivated New Zealand involvement—reliance on Britain meant that threats to Britain became threats to New Zealand too in terms of economic and defensive ties.

There was also a strong sentimental link between the former British colony and the United Kingdom, with many seeing Britain as the "mother country" or "Home". This was reinforced by New Zealand's status as a "[white dominion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Dominion)" of the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire). The [New Zealand Prime Minister](106321.md) of the time [Michael Joseph Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Joseph_Savage) summed this up at the outbreak of war with a broadcast on 5 September (largely written by the Solicitor-General [Henry Cornish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Cornish_\(judge\)?action=edit\&redlink=1))[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHensley200921,_22-3][^cite_note-4] that became a popular cry in New Zealand during the war:&#x20;

> It is with gratitude in the past, and with confidence in the future, that we range ourselves without fear beside Britain, where she goes, we go! Where she stands, we stand\![^cite_note-5]

## Declaration of war on Nazi Germany

The state of war with Germany was officially held to have existed since 9\:30 pm on 3 September 1939 (local time), simultaneous with that of Britain, but in fact New Zealand's declaration of war was not made until confirmation had been received from Britain that their ultimatum to Germany had expired. When [Neville Chamberlain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neville_Chamberlain) broadcast Britain's declaration of war, a group of New Zealand politicians (led by [Peter Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Fraser) because Prime Minister [Michael Savage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Joseph_Savage) was terminally ill) listened to it on the shortwave radio in [Carl Berendsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Berendsen)'s room in the Parliament Buildings. Because of static on the radio, they were not certain what Chamberlain had said until a coded telegraph message was received later from London. This message did not arrive until just before midnight because the messenger boy with the telegram in London took shelter due to a (false) air raid warning. The Cabinet acted after hearing the Admiralty's notification to the fleet that war had broken out. The next day the Cabinet approved nearly 30 war regulations as laid down in the War Book, and after completing the formalities with the Executive Council the Governor-General, [Lord Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Monckton-Arundell,_8th_Viscount_Galway), issued the Proclamation of War, backdated to 9.30 pm on 3 September.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHensley200920–21-6][^cite_note-7]

## Home front



Memorandum of 9 December 1941, providing for [mobilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilization) of New Zealand troops.

In total, around 140,000 New Zealand personnel served overseas for the Allied war effort, and an additional 100,000 men were armed for Home Guard duty. At its peak in July 1942, New Zealand had 154,549 men and women under arms (excluding the Home Guard) and by the war's end, a total of 194,000 men and 10,000 women had served in the armed forces at home and overseas.[^cite_note-8]

Auxiliary services were raised for women: the [Women's Auxiliary Army Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Auxiliary_Army_Corps_\(New_Zealand\)) was the largest, followed by the [Women's Auxiliary Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Women's_Auxiliary_Air_Force), and then the [Women's Royal New Zealand Naval Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Royal_New_Zealand_Naval_Service).

[Conscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compulsory_military_training_in_New_Zealand) was introduced in June 1940, and volunteering for Army service ceased from 22 July 1940, although entry to the Air Force and Navy remained voluntary. Difficulties in filling the Second and Third Echelons for overseas service in 1939–1940, the Allied disasters of May 1940 and public demand led to its introduction. Four members of the cabinet including Prime Minister [Peter Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Fraser) had been imprisoned for anti-conscription activities in World War I, the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Labour_Party) was traditionally opposed to it, and some members still demanded "conscription of wealth before men". From January 1942, workers could be "manpowered" or directed to essential industries.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcGibbon2000118–120-9]

Access to imports was hampered and rationing made doing some things very difficult. Fuel and rubber shortages were overcome with novel approaches. In New Zealand, industry switched from civilian needs to making war materials. New Zealand and Australia supplied the bulk of foodstuffs to American forces in the South Pacific, as Reverse [Lend-Lease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lend-Lease).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBaker1965125–127-10] With earlier commitments to supply food to Britain this led to both Britain and America (MacArthur) complaining about food going to the other ally (and Britain commenting on the much more generous ration allocations for American soldiers; General [George Marshall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Marshall) admitted that the meat ration was too large, but he was not going to challenge the ration set by Congress).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHensley2009248-11] By 1943 there was a manpower crisis, and eventually the withdrawal of the Third Division from the Pacific.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHensley2009254-12] To alleviate manpower shortages in the agricultural sector, the [New Zealand Women's Land Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_Women's_Land_Army) was created in 1940; a total of 2,711 women served on farms throughout New Zealand during the war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrookes2016282–283-13][^cite_note-14]

### Axis naval activity in New Zealand waters

Main article: [Axis naval activity in New Zealand waters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_naval_activity_in_New_Zealand_waters)

German and Japanese surface raiders and submarines operated in New Zealand waters on several occasions in 1940, 1941, 1942, 1943 and 1945, sinking a total of four ships while Japanese reconnaissance aircraft flew over [Auckland](18660332.md) and [Wellington](33804.md) preparing for a projected Japanese invasion of New Zealand.

During the winter of 1944, the government hastened work on docks and repair facilities at [Auckland](18660332.md) and [Wellington](33804.md) following a British request, to supplement the bases and repair yards in Australia needed for the [British Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Pacific_Fleet).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEhrman_Volume_VI1956222-15]

## Land forces

### Greek campaign

Main article: [Battle of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Greece)

The New Zealand authorities deployed the 2nd New Zealand Expeditionary Force for combat in three echelons – all originally destined for [Egypt](8087628.md), but one diverted to Scotland (it would arrive there in June 1940) following the [German invasion of France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_France). In April 1941, after a period training in [Egypt](8087628.md), 2NZEF's New Zealand 2nd Division, stationed in Egypt, deployed to take part in the [defence of Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Italian_War) against invasion by Italian troops, and soon German forces too when they [joined the invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Greece). This defence was mounted alongside British and Australian units – the corps-size [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations) contingent under the command of British General [Henry Maitland Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Maitland_Wilson,_1st_Baron_Wilson) known together as W Force, supported a weakened Greek Army.

As German panzers began a swift advance into Greece on 6 April, the British and Commonwealth troops found themselves being outflanked and were forced into retreat. By 9 April, Greece had been forced to surrender and the 40,000 W Force troops began a withdrawal from the country to [Crete](6591.md) and Egypt, the last New Zealand troops leaving by 29 April.

During this brief campaign, the New Zealanders lost 261 men killed, 1,856 captured and 387 wounded.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcClymont1959487-16]

### Crete

Main article: [Battle of Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Crete)

Two of the three brigades of the New Zealand 2nd Division had evacuated to Crete from Greece (the third and division headquarters went to [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria)). New Zealanders bolstered the Crete garrison to a total of 34,000 British and Commonwealth soldiers (25,000 evacuated from Greece) alongside 9,000 Greek troops (see [Crete order of battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crete_order_of_battle#Allied_Forces) for more detail). Evacuated to Crete on 28 April (having disregarded an order to leave on 23 April), the New Zealand General Freyberg became commander of the Allied forces on Crete on the 30th. [Ultra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_\(cryptography\)) intercepts of German signals had already alerted Allied commanders to the German plans to invade Crete with *[Fallschirmjäger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallschirmj%C3%A4ger_\(World_War_II\))* ([Luftwaffe](17885.md) paratroopers). With this knowledge, General Freyberg began to prepare the island's defences, hampered by a lack of modern and heavy equipment, as the troops from Greece had in most cases had to leave only with their personal weapons. Although German plans had underestimated Greek, British and Commonwealth numbers, and incorrectly presumed that the Cretan population would welcome the invasion, Freyberg was still faced with the harsh prospect that even lightly equipped paratroopers could overwhelm the island's defences.

Operation Mercury opened on 20 May when the German Luftwaffe delivered *Fallschirmjäger* around the airfield at [Maleme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maleme) and the [Chania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chania) area, at around 8\:15 pm, by paradrop and [gliders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_glider). Most of the New Zealand forces were deployed around this north-western part of the island and with British and Greek troops they inflicted heavy casualties upon the initial German attacks. Despite near complete defeat for their landing troops east of the airfield and in the [Galatas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatas,_Chania?action=edit\&redlink=1) region, the Germans were able to gain a foothold by mid-morning west of Maleme Airfield (5 Brigade's area) – along the [Tavronitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tavronitis?action=edit\&redlink=1) riverbed and in the [Ayia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agia,_Chania?action=edit\&redlink=1) Valley to the east (10 Brigade's area – dubbed 'Prison Valley').

#### Maleme

Map of the positions of 22 Battalion, New Zealand 2nd Division at [Maleme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maleme) in the [Battle of Crete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Crete), 20 May 1941

Over the course of the morning, the 600-strong New Zealand [22 Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/22nd_Battalion_\(New_Zealand\)) defending Maleme Airfield found its situation rapidly worsening. The battalion had lost telephone contact with the brigade headquarters; the battalion headquarters (in [Pirgos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirgos_Psilonerou?action=edit\&redlink=1)) had lost contact with C and D Companies, stationed on the airstrip and along the Tavronitis-side of Hill 107 (see map) respectively and the battalion commander, Lieutenant-Colonel [Leslie Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Andrew) ([VC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_Cross)) had no idea of the enemy paratrooper strength to his west, as his [observation posts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation_post) lacked wireless sets. While a platoon of C Company situated northwest of the airfield, nearest the sea, was able to repel German attacks along the beach, attacks across the Tavronitis bridge by *Fallschirmjäger* were able to overwhelm weaker positions and take the Royal Air Force camp. Not knowing whether C and D Companies had been overrun, and with German mortars firing from the riverbed, Colonel Andrew (with unreliable wireless contact) ordered the firing of white and green signals – the designated emergency signal for [23 Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/23rd_Battalion_\(New_Zealand\)) (to the south-east of Pirgos), under the command of Colonel Leckie, to counterattack. The signal was not spotted, and further attempts were made to get the message through to no avail. At 5\:00 pm, contact was made with Brigadier [James Hargest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Hargest) at the New Zealand 2nd Division headquarters, but Hargest responded that 23 Battalion was fighting paratroopers in its own area, an untrue and unverified assertion.

Faced with a seemingly desperate situation, Colonel Andrew played his trump card – two [Matilda tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilda_II), which he ordered to counterattack with the reserve infantry platoon and some additional [gunners](2508.md) turned infantrymen. The counterattack failed – one tank had to turn back after suffering technical problems (the turret would not traverse properly) and the second ignored the German positions in the RAF camp and the edge of the airfield, heading straight for the riverbed. This lone tank stranded itself quickly on a boulder and faced with the same technical difficulties as the first Matilda, the crew abandoned the vehicle. The exposed infantry were repelled by the *Fallschirmjäger*. At around 6\:00 pm, the failure was reported to Brigadier Hargest and the prospect of a withdrawal was raised. Colonel Andrew was informed that he could withdraw if he wished, with the famous reply "Well, if you must, you must," but that two companies (A Company, 23 Battalion and B Company, [28 (Māori) Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C4%81ori_Battalion)) were being sent to reinforce 22 Battalion. To Colonel Andrew, the situation seemed bleak; ammunition was running low, the promised reinforcements seemed not to be forthcoming (one got lost, the other simply did not arrive as quickly as expected) and he still had no idea how C and D companies were. The two companies in question were in fact resisting strongly on the airfield and above the Tavronitis riverbed and had inflicted far greater losses on the Germans than they had suffered. At 9\:00 pm, Andrew made the decision to make a limited withdrawal, and once that had been carried out, a full one to the 21 and 23 Battalion positions to the east. By midnight, all of 22 Battalion had left the Maleme area, with the exception of C and D Companies which withdrew in the early morning of the 21st upon discovering that the rest of the battalion had gone.

This allowed German troops to seize the airfield proper without opposition and take nearby positions to reinforce their hold on it. [Junkers Ju 52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junkers_Ju_52) transport aircraft flew in ammunition and supplies, as well as the rest of the *Fallschirmjäger* and troops of the [5th Mountain Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_5th_Mountain_Division). Although the landings were extremely hazardous, with the airstrip under direct British artillery fire, substantial reinforcement was made. On 21 May, the village of Maleme was attacked and captured by the Germans, and a counterattack was made by the 20 Battalion (with reinforcements from the [Australian 2/7 Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2/7th_Battalion_\(Australia\))), 28 (Māori) Battalion and later 21 Battalion. The attack was hampered by communications problems and although the New Zealanders made significant advances in some areas, the overall picture was one of stiff German resistance. 5 Brigade fell back to a new line at [Platanias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platanias), leaving Maleme securely in German hands, allowing them to freely build up their force in this region.

#### Galatas

On the night of 23 May and the morning of 24 May, 5 Brigade withdrew again to the area near [Daratsos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daratsos?action=edit\&redlink=1), forming a new front line running from [Galatas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galatas,_Chania?action=edit\&redlink=1) to the sea. The relatively fresh 18 Battalion replaced the worn troops from Maleme and Platanias, deploying 400 men on a two kilometre front.

Galatas had come under attack on the first day of the battle — *Fallschirmjäger* and gliders had landed around Chania and Galatas, to suffer extremely heavy casualties. They retreated to "Prison Valley," where they rallied around Ayia Prison and repulsed a confused counterattack by two companies of 19 Battalion and three light tanks. Pink Hill (so named for the colour of its soil), a crucial point on the Galatas heights, was attacked several times by the Germans that day and was remarkably held by the Division Petrol Company, with the aid of Greek soldiers, though at a heavy cost to both sides. The Petrol Company comprised poorly armed support troops, primarily drivers and technicians, and by the day's end, all their officers and most of their non-commissioned officers had been wounded. They withdrew around dusk. On the second day, the New Zealanders attacked nearby Cemetery Hill to take pressure off their line, and although they had to withdraw, for it was too exposed, the hill became a [no man's land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No_man's_land) as Pink Hill was, relieving the New Zealand front. Day three, 22 May, saw German soldiers take Pink Hill. The Petrol Company and some infantry reserve prepared a counterattack, but a notable incident pre-empted them – as told by Driver A. Pope:

> Out of the trees came \[Captain] Forrester of the [Buffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Buffs_\(Royal_East_Kent_Regiment\)), clad in shorts, a long yellow army jersey reaching down almost to the bottom of the shorts, brass polished and gleaming, web belt in place and waving his revolver in his right hand \[...] It was a most inspiring sight. Forrester was at the head of a crowd of disorderly Greeks, including women; one Greek had a shotgun with a serrated-edge bread knife tied on like a bayonet, others had ancient weapons—all sorts. Without hesitation, this uncouth group, with Forrester right out in front, went over the top of a parapet and headlong at the crest of the hill. The \[Germans] fled.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDavin1953Chapter:_The_Canea–Galatas_Front-17]

Days four and five featured only skirmishes between the two forces. Luftwaffe air raids targeted Galatas on 25 May at 8\:00 am, 12\:45 pm and 1\:15 pm, and the German ground attack came at around 2\:00 pm. 100 Mountain and 3 Parachute Regiment attacked Galatas and the high ground around it, while two battalions of 85 Mountain Regiment attacked eastwards, with the aim of cutting Chania off. The New Zealand defenders, though prepared, suffered from a disadvantage: 18 Battalion, 400 men, was the only fresh infantry formation on the line – the rest were non-infantry groups like the Petrol Company and the Composite Battalion, consisting of mechanical, supply and artillery troops. The fighting was fierce, especially along the north of the line, and platoons and companies were forced to retreat. Brigadier [Lindsay Inglis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindsay_Merritt_Inglis) called for reinforcement and received 23 Battalion, which, along with an improvised group of reinforcements scraped together at Brigade headquarters (including the brigade band and the [Kiwi Concert Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiwi_Concert_Party)), stabilised the north of the line. South of Galatas, only 18 Battalion and the Petrol Company were defending – 18 Battalion was forced to withdraw, and the Petrol Company on Pink Hill followed suit after eventually becoming aware of this. 19 Battalion was the only formation still in combat on Pink Hill, and they too withdrew. These forces withdrew past Galatas, as no defenders were in the village to link up with.

By nightfall, German troops had occupied Galatas, and Lieutenant-Colonel [Howard Kippenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Kippenberger) prepared a counterattack. Two tanks led two companies of 23 Battalion into Galatas at a running pace – heavy fire was encountered and as the tanks went ahead towards the town square, the infantry cleared each house of German soldiers as they worked inward. When the infantry caught up with the tanks, they found one out of action. With German fire coming primarily from one side of the square, a bayonet charge was mounted and the New Zealanders cleared the German opposition. Patrols quelled resistance elsewhere in Galatas – apart from one small strongpoint, Galatas was back in New Zealand hands.

A conference between Brigadier Inglis and his commanders reached the consensus that Allied forces needed to make a further counterattack urgently – and that without a counterattack Crete would fall to the Germans. Despite hard fighting so far in the battle, the 28 (Māori) Battalion was considered to be the only "fresh" battalion available and the only one capable of carrying out such an attack. Their commander was willing to mount the attack despite the difficulty, but a representative sent from Brigadier [Edward Puttick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Puttick) at New Zealand 2nd Division headquarters recommended against such an attack for fear of being unable to hold the line subsequently. The counter-attack was scrapped, and so too was Galatas, its position being far too vulnerable to hold. Without Galatas the whole line was untenable and so the New Zealanders again retreated, forming a line from the coast to [Perivolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perivolia,_Theriso?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Mournies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mournies), near the Australian 19th Brigade.

New Zealander soldiers recapture a [Matilda tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matilda_II) and take prisoner its German crew during Operation Crusader, 3 December 1941.

While New Zealand soldiers formed the majority of the personnel of the [Long Range Desert Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Range_Desert_Group) when it was formed in 1940, and a small number of New Zealand transport and signals units supported [Operation Compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Compass) in the Western Desert in December 1940, it was not until November 1941 that the [2nd New Zealand Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_New_Zealand_Division) became fully involved in the [North African Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_Campaign). Following its evacuation from Crete, the division regrouped at its camp near [Maadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maadi), at the base of the desert slopes of Wadi Digla and Tel al-Maadi. Reinforcements arrived from New Zealand to bring the division back up to strength and the training, cut short by the deployment to Greece and Crete, was completed.

On 18 November 1941, [Operation Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Crusader) was launched to lift the [Siege of Tobruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Tobruk) (the third such attack), under the command of [Lieutenant-General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant-general_\(United_Kingdom\)) [Alan Cunningham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Cunningham) and the 2nd New Zealand Division (integrated into the [British Eighth Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eighth_Army_\(United_Kingdom\))) took part in the offensive, crossing the [Libyan](17633.md) frontier into [Cyrenaica](75451.md). Operation Crusader was an overall success for the British, although [Erwin Rommel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erwin_Rommel)'s [Afrika Korps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrika_Korps) inflicted heavy armour and infantry losses before its weakened and under-supplied units retreated to [El Agheila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Agheila) and halted the British advance. The New Zealand troops were the ones to relieve [Tobruk](405871.md) after fighting around [Sidi Rezegh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidi_Rezegh), where Axis tanks had inflicted heavy casualties against the several New Zealand infantry battalions, protected by very little of their own armour. In February 1942, with Crusader completed, the [New Zealand government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_New_Zealand) insisted that the division be withdrawn to [Syria](7515849.md) to recover – 879 men were killed and 1,700 wounded during the operation, the most costly battle the 2nd New Zealand Division fought in the Second World War.

On 14 June 1942, the generals recalled the New Zealanders from their occupation duties in Syria, as the Afrika Korps had [broken through Gazala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Gazala) and captured Tobruk. The New Zealanders, put on the defence, found themselves encircled at [Minqar Qa'im](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minqar_Qa'im?action=edit\&redlink=1), but escaped thanks to brutally efficient hand-to-hand fighting by 4 Brigade. The British forces prevented Rommel's advance from reaching [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandria), [Cairo](6293.md) and the [Suez Canal](29323.md) in the [First Battle of El Alamein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_El_Alamein), where New Zealand troops captured [Ruweisat Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruweisat_Ridge) in a successful night attack. They were unable to bring their [anti-tank weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_weapon) forward, and more importantly, British armour did not move forward to support the soldiers. Heavy casualties were suffered by the two New Zealand brigades involved, as they were attacked by German tanks, and several thousand men were taken prisoner. [Charles Upham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Upham) earned a bar for his Victoria Cross in this battle.



New Zealand members of the [Long Range Desert Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Range_Desert_Group) pause for tea in the Western Desert, 27 March 1941

With the Eighth Army now under the new command of Lieutenant-General [Bernard Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Montgomery), the Army launched a new offensive on 23 October against the stalled [Axis forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) in the [Second Battle of El Alamein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_El_Alamein). On the first night, as part of Operation Lightfoot, the New Zealand 2nd Division, with British divisions, moved through the deep Axis minefields while engineers cleared routes for British tanks to follow. The New Zealanders successfully captured their objectives on [Miteiriya Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miteiriya_Ridge?action=edit\&redlink=1). By 2 November, with the attack bogged down, Montgomery launched a new initiative to the south of the battle lines, Operation Supercharge, with the ultimate goal of destroying the Axis army. The experienced 2nd New Zealand Division was called on to carry out the initial thrust – the same sort of attack they had made in *Lightfoot*. As the under-strength division could not achieve this mission alone, two British brigades were attached. The German line was breached by British armour and, on 4 November, the Afrika Korps, faced with the prospect of complete defeat, skillfully withdrew.

The New Zealanders continued to advance with the Eighth Army through the [Tunisia Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunisia_Campaign), driving the Afrika Korps back into [Tunisia](30188.md), and notably fought at [Medenine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medenine), the [Tebaga Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tebaga_Gap) and [Enfidaville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enfidaville). On 13 May 1943, the North African campaign ended with the surrender of the last 275,000 Axis troops in Tunisia. On 15 May, the division began a withdrawal back to Egypt and, by 1 June, the division had returned to Maadi and [Helwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helwan), on standby for use in Europe. Total losses for the 2nd New Zealand Division since November 1941 stood at 2,989 killed, 7,000 wounded and 4,041 taken prisoner.

### Italy

During October and November 1943, New Zealand troops from the 2nd New Zealand Division assembled in [Bari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari) in [Apulia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apulia), weeks after the [Allied invasion of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_invasion_of_Italy). In November, the division crossed the [Sangro River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sangro_River) with a view to breaching the German [Gustav Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustav_Line) and advancing to Rome, capturing the village of [Castelfrentano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castel_Frentano) in the [Abruzzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abruzzo) (part of the Gustav Line) on 2 December. The division attacked Orsogna on the next day but was repulsed by the strong German defence. In January 1944, the 2nd New Zealand was withdrawn from the stalled front line and transferred to the [Cassino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Monte_Cassino) sector, where other Allied troops were bogged down in costly fighting for the position of [Monte Cassino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monte_Cassino). On 17 February, the division attacked [Cassino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassino,_Italy) but it was strongly defended and they withdrew in early April. Cassino was eventually captured on 18 May by British and Polish troops, with the support of New Zealand artillery units. On 16 July, the division captured [Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arezzo) and reached [Florence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence) on 4 August. By the end of October, they had reached the Savio River, and Faenza was captured on 14 December. In [Operation Grapeshot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Grapeshot), the final Allied offensive in Italy, the division crossed the Senio River on 8 April 1945, then began their final push across the Santerno and Gaiana Rivers, finally crossing the [Po River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_River) on [Anzac Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anzac_Day) 1945. The division captured [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua) on 28 April 1945, crossed the Isonzo River on 1 May, and reached [Trieste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trieste) on 2 May, the day of the German unconditional surrender.

### Campaigns in the Pacific



New Zealand troops land on Vella Lavella in the Solomons

Main article: [South West Pacific theatre of World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_West_Pacific_theatre_of_World_War_II)

When [Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_of_Japan) entered the war in December 1941, the New Zealand Government raised another expeditionary force, known as the 2nd N.Z.E.F. In the Pacific, or 2nd N.Z.E.F. (I.P.), for service with the Allied [Pacific Ocean Areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Ocean_Areas) command. This force supplemented existing garrison troops in the South Pacific. The main fighting formation of the 2nd N.Z.E.F. (I.P.) comprised the [New Zealand 3rd Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Zealand_3rd_Division). The 3rd Division never fought as a complete formation; its component brigades became involved in semi-independent actions as part of the Allied forces in the [Solomons](265083.md) at [Vella Lavella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vella_Lavella_\(land\))), [Treasury Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Treasury_Islands) and [Green Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Green_Islands).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrawford2000140–162-18] The War Cabinet had held the division at two rather than three brigades, and this limited its use, although MacArthur had a role for a full division; Halsey was "greatly disappointed that New Zealand could not furnish a division with three full brigades" but his deputy accepted that the division was last in New Zealand's Pacific priorities, after the air force, navy and food production. Preference was given to the Second Division, on the advice of Churchill and Roosevelt.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHensley2009234,_254-19] New Zealand also had 19,000 troops in New Caledonia, Tonga, Norfolk Island and Fiji in 1943; and the 3,000 Air Force personnel would rise to 6,000 when more planes were available. In Australia the reaction of Curtin (but not Evatt) to the withdrawal of the Third Division was hostile.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHensley2009235,237-20]

Eventually, American formations replaced the New Zealand army units in the Pacific, which released personnel for service with the 2nd Division in Italy, or to cover shortages in the civilian labour force.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrawford2000157-21] New Zealand Air Force squadrons and Navy units continued to contribute to the Allied island-hopping campaign, with several RNZAF squadrons supporting Australian ground troops on [Bougainville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bougainville_campaign).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBradley2012395-22]



New Zealand troops land on [Guadalcanal](113645.md) in the Solomons.

In 1945 [Peter Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Fraser) wanted to contribute to a Commonwealth force against Japan, including an army contribution of at least two brigade groups as "from previous experience small units are given the harder jobs or are not properly supported". But during the [Hamilton by-election, 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamilton_by-election,_1945), National had campaigned on withdrawing New Zealand troops from Italy and restricting New Zealand's role in the Pacific War to food supply, Labour wanted to keep New Zealand troops in the Pacific to "have a say" in the peace. So Fraser met the Opposition leaders [Sidney Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Holland) and [Adam Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Hamilton_\(politician\)) before the [Dunedin North by-election, 1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunedin_North_by-election,_1945), noting the divisions in his own caucus. Holland agreed with Fraser not to refer to the matter (which was agitating the whole country) during the by-election campaign, saying it would not be right to divide the House on this. In a (non-broadcast) semi-secret section on 2 August, the House agreed to participate in a force against Japan "within the capacity of our remaining resources of manpower". National's proposal to reduce the total armed forces to 55,000 was accepted.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHensley2009372-23]

The [Commonwealth Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Corps), planned to participate in [Operation Downfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Downfall), the Allied invasion of Japan, would have included New Zealand Army and Air Force units, with Air Force units included in [Tiger Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiger_Force_\(air\)) to bomb Japan.

In 1945, some troops who had recently returned from Europe with the 2nd Division were drafted to form a contribution (known as [J-Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J-Force)) toward the [British Commonwealth Occupation Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Commonwealth_Occupation_Force) (BCOF) in southern Japan. [No. 14 Squadron RNZAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._14_Squadron_RNZAF), equipped with [Corsair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F4U) fighters, and RNZN ships also joined BCOF.

## Naval actions

At the outbreak of war in 1939, New Zealand still contributed to the New Zealand Division of the Royal Navy. Many New Zealanders served alongside other Commonwealth sailors in vessels of the Royal Navy and would continue to do so throughout the war.

[HMNZS *Achilles*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Achilles) took part in the [Battle of the River Plate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_River_Plate) (13 December 1939) as part of a small British force against the German pocket battleship [*Admiral Graf Spee*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_cruiser_Admiral_Graf_Spee). The action resulted in the German ship retiring to neutral [Uruguay](31843.md) and its scuttling a few days later.

Another cruiser, [HMNZS *Leander*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Leander), destroyed the Italian auxiliary cruiser [*Ramb I*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_auxiliary_cruiser_Ramb_I) off the [Maldives](19117.md) on 27 February 1941. The New Zealand Division of the Royal Navy became the Royal New Zealand Navy when King [George VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_VI_of_the_United_Kingdom) granted it the name on 1 October 1941.

### Naval war against Japan



HMNZS *Leander* fires on the Japanese cruiser *[Jintsu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_cruiser_Jintsu)*.

On 8 December 1941, Japan published its declaration of war against the United States and the British Empire, a few hours after the attack on Pearl Harbour. The Prime Minister’s Department then announced that New Zealand was in a state of war with Japan. On 10 December, Japanese troops occupied [Butaritari Atoll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butaritari_Atoll), capturing seven coastwatchers.[^cite_note-24]

The first vessel into action against Japan, the minesweeper [HMNZS *Gale*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Gale), steamed forward to [Fiji](10707.md), arriving on Christmas Day, 1941. HMNZS *Rata* and *Muritai* arrived in January 1942, followed by the corvettes [HMNZS *Moa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Moa_\(T233\)), [HMNZS *Kiwi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Kiwi_\(T102\)) and [HMNZS *Tui*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Tui_\(T234\)), to form a minesweeping flotilla.

The *Achilles*, *Leander*, and [HMNZS *Monowai*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Monowai_\(F59\)) initially served as troop-convoy escorts in the Pacific in early 1942. In January 1942, *Monowai* inconclusively engaged a Japanese submarine off [Fiji](10707.md). On 4 January 1943, a Japanese bomber destroyed the aft gun-house of *Achilles* off [Guadalcanal](113645.md). In January 1943, a morale-boosting episode occurred: the duel *Kiwi* and *Moa* fought with the much larger [*I-1*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_submarine_I-1). Unable to pierce the *I-1*, the *Kiwi* rammed her three times, destroying her ability to dive. *Moa* then hounded *I-1* onto a reef, where she broke up. In April 1943, an aerial attack sunk the *Moa* in [Tulagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulagi) Harbour in the [Solomons](265083.md). The *Tui* participated in the sinking of the 2,200-ton [*I-17*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_submarine_I-17) before joining the *Kiwi* in redeploying to New Guinea, while the corvette [HMNZS *Arabis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Arabis) went to the [Ellice Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ellice_Islands).

The *Leander* helped sink the [*Jintsu*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_cruiser_Jints%C5%AB) in the [Battle of Kolombangara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kolombangara) on the night of 11–12 July 1943. Holed by a Japanese torpedo during the engagement, the *Leander* withdrew to Auckland for repairs. Twelve New Zealand built [Fairmile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairmile_B_motor_launch) launches of the 80th and 81st [Motor Launch Flotillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_Forces_of_the_Royal_New_Zealand_Navy#RNZN_Fairmiles) went forward in early 1944.

The cruiser [HMS *Gambia*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Gambia) bombarded [Sabang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabang,_Indonesia) (in [Sumatra](28679.md)) in July 1944, and with the recommissioned *Achilles* joined the [British Pacific Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Pacific_Fleet), later re-inforced by the corvette [HMNZS *Arbutus*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Arbutus). The Fleet detached *Achilles* to tow the damaged destroyer *Ulster* to the New Zealand Hospital Ship *Maunganui* in the [Philippines](23440.md) (where the was at that time stationed). Both *Gambia* and *Achilles* bombarded Japanese positions in the Sakishima Group in May 1945. They were supported by 100 New Zealanders in the [Fleet Air Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Air_Arm) operating from British carriers. The *Achilles* left the fleet for Manus Island on 10 August. *Gambia* was off Tokyo on VJ day, and was attacked by a Japanese plane while flying the "Cease hostilities" signal – with assistance from surrounding ships, *Gambia* shot down the aircraft but was hit by the debris.

The *Gambia* represented New Zealand at the [surrender ceremonies in Tokyo Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrender_of_Japan) (2 September 1945), and stayed as part of the occupation force. Air Vice-Marshal [Isitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonard_Monk_Isitt_\(aviator\)) signed the surrender document on behalf of New Zealand.[^cite_note-25] By the end of the war the RNZN had 60 vessels, most of them light craft.

## Royal New Zealand Air Force in World War II

### European theatre



Wellingtons of the [RNZAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNZAF) in England, August 1939

On the outbreak of [World War II](32927.md) the [RNZAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNZAF) had as its primary equipment 30 [Vickers Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_Wellington) bombers, which the New Zealand government offered to the United Kingdom in August 1939, together with the crews to fly them. These formed the nucleus of No. 75 Squadron, the first of the Dominion squadrons to serve with the RAF during the war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMcGibbon20008-26]



A restored 485 (NZ) Squadron Spitfire

Many other New Zealanders also served in the [RAF](25679.md). As New Zealand did not require its personnel to serve with RNZAF squadrons, the rate at which they entered service was faster than for other Dominions. About 100 RNZAF pilots had been sent to Europe by the time the [Battle of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Britain) started, and several had a notable role in it.

The RNZAF's primary role took advantage of New Zealand's distance from the conflict by training aircrew as part of the [Empire Air Training Scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire_Air_Training_Scheme), alongside the other major former British colonies, Canada, Australia, and South Africa. Many New Zealanders did their advanced training in Canada. Local enterprises manufactured or assembled large numbers of [De Havilland Tiger Moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Tiger_Moth), [Airspeed Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airspeed_Oxford) and [North American Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Harvard) training aircraft, and the RNZAF also acquired second-hand biplanes such as [Hawker Hinds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Hind) and [Vickers Vincents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_Vincent), as well as other types for specialised training such as [Avro Ansons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Anson) and [Supermarine Walruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Walrus). Only when German surface-raiders became active did the military authorities realise the need for a combat force in New Zealand in addition to the trainers.

Once trained, the majority of RNZAF aircrew served with ordinary units of the RAF or of the [Fleet Air Arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleet_Air_Arm). As in World War I, they served in all theatres. At least 78 became aces. New Zealanders in the RNZAF and RAF included pilots such as the first RAF ace of WW2, Flying Officer [Cobber Kain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cobber_Kain), [Alan Deere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Deere), whose *Nine Lives* was one of the first post war accounts of combat, and leaders such as World War I ace, Air Chief Marshal Sir [Keith Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Park), who commanded 11 Group, responsible for the defence of London in the [Battle of Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Britain), the air defence of [Malta](19137.md) and in the closing stages of the war, the RAF in [South East Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_East_Asia). Through accident or design, several RAF units came to be mostly manned by RNZAF pilots (for example [No. 243 Squadron RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._243_Squadron_RAF) in Singapore, [No. 258 Squadron RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._258_Squadron_RAF) in the UK and several Wildcat and Hellcat units of the FAA – leading some texts to claim these types of aircraft were used by the RNZAF).



Hurricane night fighter pilots of No. 486 Squadron at Wittering in 1942

The Royal Air Force deliberately set aside certain squadrons for pilots from particular countries. The first of these, [75 Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._75_Squadron_RAF), comprised the Wellingtons and pilots lent by New Zealand in August 1939, which later flew [Short Stirlings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Stirling), [Avro Lancasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Lancaster) and [Avro Lincolns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avro_Lincoln). Other New Zealand squadrons within the RAF included [485](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._485_Squadron_RNZAF), which flew [Supermarine Spitfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Spitfire) throughout the war, [486](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._486_Squadron_RNZAF) ([Hawker Hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Hurricane), [Hawker Typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Typhoon) and [Hawker Tempests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Tempest)), [487](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._487_Squadron_RNZAF) ([Lockheed Venturas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Ventura) and [De Havilland Mosquitoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Mosquito)), [488](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._488_Squadron_RNZAF) ([Brewster Buffaloes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewster_Buffalo), Hurricanes, [Bristol Beaufighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Beaufighter) and [De Havilland Mosquitoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Mosquito)), [489](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._489_Squadron_RNZAF) ([Bristol Blenheims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Blenheim), [Bristol Beauforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Beaufort), [Handley Page Hampdens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handley_Page_Hampden), Beaufighters, and Mosquitoes), and [490](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._490_Squadron_RNZAF) ([Consolidated Catalinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_Catalina) and [Short Sunderlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Sunderland)).

### RNZAF in the Pacific

The presence of German raiders led to the formation of New Zealand-based air-combat units – initially using re-armed types like the [Vildebeest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_Vildebeest), and hurriedly converting impressed airliners such as the DH86 to carry bombs. RNZAF obtained [Lockheed Hudsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Hudson) early in 1941 to take over this role. 5 Squadron with [Vickers Vincents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_Vincent) and [Short Singapores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Singapore) was sent to Fiji to protect that colony.



Flying boats of 5 Squadron RNZAF

In December 1941, Japan attacked and rapidly conquered much of the area to the north of New Zealand. New Zealand had perforce to look to her own defence as well as help the ["mother country"](31717.md). Trainers in New Zealand such as the [North American Harvard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-6_Texan), [Hawker Hind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Hind) and even the [de Havilland Tiger Moth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Tiger_Moth) were camouflaged and armed. Hudsons moved forward, while 5 Squadron in Fiji, commenced operations against the Japanese despite its obsolete equipment.

The [Imperial Japanese Navy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Japanese_Navy) demonstrated the vulnerability of New Zealand when submarine-launched Japanese float-planes overflew [Wellington](33804.md) and [Auckland](18660332.md) in 1942. In March a Glen floatplane from [I-25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_submarine_I-25) overflew Wellington on 8 March and Auckland on 13 March, then Suva, Fiji on 17 March. The submarine was not seen by the Wellington-Nelson ferry when navigating Cook Strait on the surface on a full-moon night.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins1992147,_148-27] In May a floatplane from [I-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_submarine_I-21) overflew Suva on 19 May and then Auckland on 24 May. Lost in heavy fog the pilot (Matsumora) was helped by airport staff who heard a plane apparently in trouble and turned on the runway lights so allowing the pilot to find his bearings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJenkins1992164,_165-28] During one March or May 1942 overflight a Tiger Moth apparently gave chase ineffectually.

As few combat-capable aircraft were available at home, and Britain was unable to help, New Zealand benefited from the British-United States [lend-lease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lend-lease) agreement. Gradually, America supplied New Zealand with aircraft for use in the Pacific Theatre. The early [lend-lease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lend-lease) aircraft were obsolescent models like the [Brewster Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brewster_Buffalo) which were no match for the skilled and well-equipped Japanese pilots, although the Brewster was successfully used by [Geoffrey Fisken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoffrey_Fisken) the top New Zealand and Commonwealth ace in the Pacific.



Restored RNZAF Corsair

From mid-1943 at [Guadalcanal](113645.md), starting with [No 15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._15_Squadron_RNZAF) and [No 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No._14_Squadron_RNZAF) squadrons, several [Kittyhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curtiss_P-40) units fought with distinction. Several RNZAF pilots became aces against the Japanese, including [Geoff Fisken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geoff_Fisken), the Commonwealth's leading ace in the Pacific war. Other squadrons flew the elderly but effective [Douglas Dauntless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Dauntless) and later the big, modern [Grumman Avenger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grumman_Avenger) torpedo-bomber.

The RNZAF took on a major part of the maritime reconnaissance task too, with [Catalina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PBY_Catalina) (and later [Sunderland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Sunderland)) flying-boats and Hudson bombers.

The role of the RNZAF changed as the allies moved off the defensive. The Americans, prominent amongst the Allied nations in the Pacific, planned to bypass major Japanese strongholds, but instead to capture a handful of island bases to provide a supply-chain for an eventual attack on Japan itself (see [island hopping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leapfrogging_\(strategy\))). The Allied advance started from the South Pacific. The RNZAF became part of the force tasked with securing the line of advance by incapacitating the bypassed Japanese strongholds.

As the war progressed, more powerful modern aircraft replaced the older types; Kittyhawks gave way to [Corsairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F4U_Corsair) and Hudsons to [Venturas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Ventura). At its peak in the Pacific, the RNZAF had 13 squadrons of Corsair fighters, six of Venturas, two each of Catalinas, Avengers and [C-47 Dakotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-47_Dakotas), one using Dauntless dive bombers, mixed transport and communications squadrons, a flight of [Short Sunderlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Sunderland) and nearly 1,000 training machines. By 1945, the RNZAF had over 41,000 personnel, including just over 10,000 aircrew who served with the RAF in Europe and Africa.

## Intelligence

New Zealand had a "Combined Intelligence Centre" in Wellington. In 1942 papers from the centre to the Commander-in-Chief, [Eastern Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Fleet) in [Colombo](56636.md) and the RN signals intelligence at Anderson outside Colombo were captured on the Australian steamer *Nankin* when she was [intercepted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_naval_activity_in_Australian_waters) in the Indian Ocean by the [German raider *Thor*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_auxiliary_cruiser_Thor).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEElphick1998262-29][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBou2012115-30]

In the 1930s the New Zealand Division of the Royal Navy established a chain of radio direction finding (D/F) stations from [Awarua Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awarua_Radio) on the [Awarua Plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awarua_Plains) in Southland, [Musick Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musick_Point) near Auckland, [Waipapakauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RNZAF_Station_Waipapakauri) in the far north and [Suva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suva), Fiji. There were radio intercept stations at Awarua, Suva, Nairnville in [Khandallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khandallah) near Wellington, and from 1943 [HMNZS *Irirangi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Communications_Station_Irirangi) at [Waiouru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waiouru). Transmissions were sent to the [Far East Combined Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_East_Combined_Bureau) via the Navy Office in Wellington.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBou201218-31]

New Zealand's network of radio intercept and D/F stations sent its material to [Central Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau) in Brisbane despite its main area of responsibility being outside the SWPA.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBou201248-32] The network was supplemented in 1943 by a Radio Finger-Printing (RFP) organisation staffed by members of the [Women's Royal New Zealand Naval Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Royal_New_Zealand_Naval_Service) ("Wrens"). These were valuable for identifying Japanese submarines, and RFP alerted the minesweepers [HMNZS *Kiwi*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Kiwi_\(T102\)) and [HMNZS *Moa*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMNZS_Moa_\(T233\)), who attacked and rammed the [Japanese submarine *I-1*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_submarine_I-1) running supplies to Guadalcanal on 29 January 1943.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEElphick1998387-33]

In 1943 the New Zealand (naval) operation was run by a Lieutenant Philpott assisted by Professor Campbell (Professor of Mathematics at [Victoria University of Wellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_University_of_Wellington)) plus a part-time civilian Japanese interpreter and two women assistants *both of whom were of above average ability and one of whom knew quite a bit of Japanese*. It was said to be *remarkably productive* despite its small size and apparent lack of assistance or direction from [FRUMEL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FRUMEL) in Melbourne (where Rudi Fabian was reluctant to cooperate with the Royal Navy or the US Army's [Central Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESmith2000198-34]
