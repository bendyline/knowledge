**Einstein** is a large [lunar](1621514.md) [impact crater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_crater) that lies along the western limb of the [Moon](19331.md), which makes it difficult to observe from the [Earth](9228.md). The visibility of this formation is subject to [libration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libration) effects,[^cite_note-Whitaker_et_al_1963-3][^cite_note-Grego_2005-4] but even under the best conditions not much detail can be observed except from lunar orbit.[^cite_note-Moore_2001-5] Nearby craters of note include [Moseley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moseley_\(crater\)) just to the north, [Dalton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dalton_\(crater\)) along the eastern rim, [Vasco da Gama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasco_da_Gama_\(crater\)) just to the southeast, and [Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohr_\(crater\)) to the south-southeast.[^cite_note-Byrne_2008-6] The formation [Vallis Bohr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vallis_Bohr) is visible to the south.[^cite_note-Grego_2005-4]

This formation dates to the [Pre-Nectarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Nectarian) period of the [lunar geologic timescale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_geologic_timescale).[^cite_note-Wilhelms_et_al_1987-2] The outer rim of this [walled plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walled_plain) has been strongly degraded by many smaller impacts. Along the northeast rim is an impact with a high albedo skirt. Occupying the center of the interior floor is Einstein A, an impact crater with [terraced](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/terrace) inner walls and a central peak.[^cite_note-Byrne_2008-6] The outer [rampart](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/rampart) of this concentric crater spreads across the interior floor, covering over half the diameter of Einstein. Several smaller craters also lie scattered across the floor, but there are sections of relatively flat surface in the southwest part of the floor.[^cite_note-Bussey_Spudis_2004-7]<sup>: 37 </sup> Two small craters on the west side have [fractured floors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_craters_on_the_moon_with_fractured_floors). These are believed to be [secondary craters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_crater) from the [Orientale impact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mare_Orientale) to the south.[^cite_note-Wilhelms_et_al_1987-2]

The widespread notion that this crater was discovered by [Patrick Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Moore) in 1939[^cite_note-Moore_1967-8][^cite_note-Allen_1966-9][^cite_note-Allen_1969-10] is probably erroneous.[^cite_note-Mobberley_2013-11] In the middle of the 20th century [Hugh Percy Wilkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Percy_Wilkins) designated the crater *Caramuel* to honor [Juan Caramuel y Lobkowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Caramuel_y_Lobkowitz).[^cite_note-Wilkins_300_names-12][^cite_note-Wilkins_unofficial_names-13] The crater was known under this unofficial name for some time,[^cite_note-Allen_1969-10][^cite_note-Longshaw_2014-14] but neither this name, nor almost all of Wilkins' other nomenclature, were adopted by the [International Astronomical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Astronomical_Union) (IAU).[^cite_note-Whitaker_2003-15] In 1963 [E. Whitaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewen_Whitaker) and D.W\.G. Arthur named this crater *Einstein* after German physicist [Albert Einstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert_Einstein) (1879–1955),[^cite_note-Longshaw_2014-14][^cite_note-Menzel_et_al_1971-16] and in 1964 this name was adopted by the IAU.[^cite_note-GPN-1] Wilkins' map also contained a crater called Einstein, but it is a less prominent one[^cite_note-Longshaw_2014-14] – [Simpelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simpelius_\(crater\)) D.[^cite_note-Moore_1967-8]

On August 5, 2026, a discarded [Falcon 9](2647515.md) upper stage was expected to [impact](83838015.md) the Moon near the Einstein crater.[^cite_note-McKenzie_2026-18] The impact site was captured by images from the [Danuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danuri) lunar orbiter on August 7.[^cite_note-Park_2026-19]
