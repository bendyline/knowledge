**North Sumatra** ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Sumatera Utara*,[^cite_note-7] [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_language): ᯘᯮᯔᯖᯩᯒ ᯥᯖᯒ) is a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia) of [Indonesia](14579.md) located in the northern part of the island of [Sumatra](28679.md). Its capital and largest city is [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan) on the east coast of the island. It borders [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh) to the northwest, [Riau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau) to the southeast, [West Sumatra](453590.md) to the south, the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) to the west, and the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca) (with a maritime border with [Malaysia](3607937.md)) to the east.

With a population of around 14.8 million[^cite_note-unstats_2021-8] in 2020 and a mid-2025 estimate of around 15.8 million (and adding about 200,000 people each year),[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2026-1] North Sumatra is Indonesia's fourth most populous province and the most populous province outside of [Java Island](69336.md). At 72,437.755 square kilometres (27,968.374 mi<sup>2</sup>), North Sumatra is the third-largest province in area on the island of Sumatra behind [South Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sumatra) and [Riau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau). Major ethnic groups include the [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Indonesians), native to the east coast; several [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak) groups, indigenous to the west coast and central highlands; the [Nias people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias_people) of [Nias Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias) and its surrounding islets; and [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians), [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), and [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Indonesians) peoples, who first migrated to Sumatra during [Dutch rule](23476997.md).

North Sumatra is home to the Toba [supervolcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supervolcano), located in what is now [Lake Toba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Toba), which [erupted 74,000–75,000 years ago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youngest_Toba_eruption), wiping out nearly all of humanity. The supereruption resulted in the creation of Lake Toba and was rated a [VEI-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VEI-8) eruption.

During Dutch rule, North Sumatra was administered under the *[Gouvernement van Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies#Sumatra),* which governed the entire island of Sumatra out of Medan. In 1948, after Indonesian independence was proclaimed, Sumatra Province was divided into three sub-provinces, each of which had the right to regulate and manage its own affairs. The 15th of April was later designated as the anniversary of the founding of the province of North Sumatra.

## History

### Prehistoric era



*Darodaro*, big stones used as memorials by the [Nias people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias_people), being hauled in [Bawomataluo village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bawomataluo_village) in a thousand-year-old practice

[^cite_note-9]

Archaeological understanding of early North Sumatran peoples is limited compared to that of other nearby regions.[^cite_note-louys-2024-10] Prehistoric relics in North Sumatra show that the oldest population may have been [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) or [Melanesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melanesian_peoples). Archaeological evidence indicates that their dispersal took place in the [Mesolithic era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesolithic) (Middle Stone Age). They spread to the eastern part of Indonesia, to the island of [Papua](15199.md), and to the west, to North Sumatra and the [Malay Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malay_Peninsula). Early peoples in North Sumatra consumed mostly snails and clams, leaving large [shell deposits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shell_midden) sometimes referred to as *kjokkenmoddinger* (kitchen waste), some of which are still found as hills in Saentis, Hinai, Tanjung Beringin, along the Deli-Langkat shore, and on riverbanks.[^cite_note-louys-2024-10]

In the second wave of migration from mainland Southeast Asia c. 1000 BC, the [Young Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)) or Deutero Malays settled on the coast. They mainly lived by fishing and by cultivating the marshy land for agriculture. Their villages were scattered along the big rivers that flow to the east coast of North Sumatra such as Besitang, [Wampu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wampu_River), [Asahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahan_River), and [Barumun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barumun_River). Larger villages grew at the mouths of rivers and became centres of government. Relics of the Mesolithic era have been found in North Sumatra, including finely honed stone axes, bone tools, and painting materials.[^cite_note-Sejarah_daerah_sumatera_utara._Direktorat_Jenderal_Kebudayaan,_Jakarta.-11]



*Situs Hopong* in [Dolok Sanggul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolok_Sanggul), stone statue sites that are possible evidence of prehistoric Batak civilization

Linguistic and archaeological evidence indicates that [Austronesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples) speakers reached Sumatra from [Taiwan](25734.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md) through [Borneo](4518.md) or [Java](69336.md) about 2,500 years ago, and the [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak) probably descended from these settlers.[^cite_note-12]

New genetic research has found that the [Nias people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias_people) also came from the [Austronesian peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austronesian_peoples), though their initial ancestors may have arrived earlier: ancestors of the Nias people are thought to have come from [Taiwan](25734.md) through the [Philippines](23440.md) 4,000 to 5,000 years ago.[^cite_note-taiwan-13] Ten years of research involving blood samples of 440 Nias people in 11 villages on [Nias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias) island show similarities between their Y-chromosome and mitochondrial DNA and that of [Taiwanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_indigenous_peoples) and [Filipino peoples](2298105.md).[^cite_note-dna-14]

Coins founded in *Situs Kota Cina*, [Labuhan Deli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labuhan_Deli), presumably used for trading during the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty), 12th to 13th century AD

The eastern coast of North Sumatra, bordering the Strait of Malacca, has been widely visited by Hindus and by Chinese traders for centuries since the founding of early *Situs Kota Cina* or Chinatown sites in [Hamparan Perak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamparan_Perak) c. 11th–14th centuries AD. [Barus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barus), a trading port on the western coast of Tapanuli, attracted [Middle Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) era traders in search of [camphor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camphor), which was popular in Ancient Egypt. In 1030, [Rajendra Chola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajendra_I) recorded the names of North Sumatran states he defeated in one expedition to conquer [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya). States that he mentioned included Sriwijaya, Malayur, Kendara, and the [Panai Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannai). Furthermore, the [Nagarakretagama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagarakretagama) epic by [Mpu Prapanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mpu_Prapanca) from the 14th century lists states found in North Sumatra such as Pane, Haru, Mandailing, Tamiang, Lawas, and Barus, all of which were mainly defeated by the [Majapahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majapahit).



[Bahal temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahal_temple), also known as *Portibi*, is a [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhists) [candi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Candi_of_Indonesia) complex in [Bahal village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahal,_Indonesia), North Sumatra.

[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMiksic1996105-15]

&#x20;The temple site is linked to the [Pannai Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannai) c. 11th to 13th century AD.

The earliest kingdom that was present on the eastern coast of North Sumatra was the [Aru Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aru_kingdom), which existed from the 13th to the 16th century AD. In its height, the kingdom was a maritime power and controlled the northern part of the [Malacca strait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca).[^cite_note-Archaeology_Highlands_of_Sumatra-Aru-16] The kingdom was initially established as a [Karo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karo_people_\(Indonesia\)) [polity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polity).[^cite_note-Muljana-Haru-17] The indigenous population practiced native [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism) and [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism). Starting in the 13th century, some also practised [Islam](6037917.md).[^cite_note-18] Aru's capital was located close to present-day [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan) and [Deli Serdang](2348469.md). Its people are believed to have been descendants of the Karo people from the hinterland of North Sumatra.[^cite_note-Archaeology_Highlands_of_Sumatra-Aru-16]

An area near [Lake Toba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Toba) called Batakland (or the *Batak Lands*) housed kingdoms of [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak) people. It was first mentioned in [Zhao Rugua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_Rukuo)'s 13th-century *[Description of the Barbarous Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhu_Fan_Zhi)*, which refers to a 'Ba-ta' dependency of [Srivijaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Srivijaya). The *[Suma Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A9_Pires)*, written in the 15th century, also refers to the "Kingdom of Bata" between [Pasai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samudera_Pasai_Sultanate) and the Aru kingdom. The Batak mainly practiced [animism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animism) and [cannibalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_cannibalism) and remained isolated from foreign culture and kingdoms like Srivijaya and Majapahit. The Toba people divided the Batak Lands into several independent kingdoms, which often entered into defensive alliances. Of the many kingdoms, Bakkara and their king or *Sisingamangaraja* held the most leverage due to customs and traditions which consider Bakkara the place of origin of the Batak people.

The Nias people on Nias Island remained isolated during its early era. Its people practised agriculture and cultivation, made art carvings, and adhered to shamanist and pagan practices.

Princes from the Sultanate of Deli, Langkat and Serdang in Sumatra

By c. 1500, there were several kingdoms on the east coast of Sumatra, namely the larger Nagur and [Aru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aru_kingdom) kingdoms and the smaller [Panai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pannai) and Batangiou kingdoms. To the west, in the hinterland of [Tapanuli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Tapanuli_Regency), another kingdom emerged: a Batak kingdom founded by descendants of Sisingamangaraja. This kingdom gradually expanded its influence throughout Tapanuli to Angkola, Mandailing, and Dairi. The three largest kingdoms in North Sumatra in the sixteenth century were Nagur, the Batak kingdom under the rule of King Sisingamangaraja, and Aru.

Wars between these three kingdoms made the region vulnerable to outside influences from [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh_Sultanate), [Melaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca_Sultanate), the [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_maritime_exploration), [Siak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Siak_Sri_Indrapura), and [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pagaruyung_kingdom). The Sultanate of Aceh worked to [spread Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spread_of_Islam) across the eastern coast, and in the [Padri War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padri_Wars), succeeded in spreading Islam into the southern Tapanuli kingdom. As a result of this warfare and cultural shifts, the three big kingdoms split into several small kingdoms and sultanates, including [Deli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Deli), [Serdang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Serdang), [Asahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahan_Sultanate), [Langkat Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Langkat), Maropat and Lingga.



Portrait of [Sisingamangaraja XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisingamangaraja_XII), painted by Augustin Sibarani, from the obverse of the 1,000 rupiah banknote (1987 series)

Malacca fell to the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company) (the VOC) in 1641. Coastal areas of North Sumatra felt economic impacts as the VOC subsequently reduced the presence of trade in [Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malacca) and shifted resources towards [Batavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batavia,_Dutch_East_Indies). Nevertheless, North Sumatra also saw an increase in Arab, Chinese and Indian traders.

After Britain gained a position on the nearby island of [Penang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penang) by establishing a trading post for the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company) (Britain's first such post in the Far East) in 1786, the British traded heavily with the east coast of Sumatra.[^cite_note-British_trade_history_with_Penang_&_Sumatra-19] Prior to the nineteenth century, Dutch power was concentrated on the island of Java and parts of the Moluccas, the Dutch East India Company competed with the British East India Company for trade in the area, especially for pepper, with the two nations trying to obstruct the other's trade efforts.[^cite_note-British_and_Dutch_trade_in_18th_century_Sumatra-20] In the nineteenth century, the Dutch began to focus more on areas outside Java, including North Sumatra, driving out British influence. Dutch control was formalized with the handover of most of Indonesia in the [1814 Treaty of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Dutch_Treaty_of_1814), which was renewed in the [1824 Treaty of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Dutch_Treaty_of_1824). However, the UK maintained a presence in several places that were considered important for trade, including parts of Sumatra and Kalimantan.



Simalungun headchiefs of East Sumatra residency, Dutch-East Indies

During the [Padri War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padri_Wars), in the years prior to 1860, Dutch forces arrived in South Tapanuki at the request of a local leader to provide protection. The [Siak sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Siak_Sri_Indrapura) subsequently signed a treaty with the [Dutch East Indies](23476997.md) government recognizing Dutch authority over it and the sultanates of [Asahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asahan_Sultanate), [Serdang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Serdang), [Deli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Deli) and [Langkat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Langkat). The Dutch proceeded further into Batakland and North Sumatra, invading Toba, Karo Highland, Nias and Silindung with the help of Christian missionaries, such as [Ludwig Ingwer Nommensen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludwig_Ingwer_Nommensen), who worked to [evangelise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization) the area. The Dutch invasion into Batakland met resistance by [Sisingamangaraja XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisingamangaraja_XII), causing a guerilla war lasting 30 years until and Sisingmangaraja XII's death in battle and a subsequent Dutch victory. Meanwhile, particularly after 1869, Dutch tobacco plantation activities expanded on the east coast, including the establishment of [Deli Maatschappij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deli_Company) and *London Sumatra*, using land leased from the Malay sultans. As there was a worker shortage, the Dutch began importing labourers from [Java](69336.md), [Southern China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China) and [Southern India](678583.md). This first big wave of migration established substantial [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesian), and [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Indonesians) populations in North Sumatra that remain to this day. By 1874, the sultanates had been formally included within the Dutch East Indies, and in Deli, the sultan of Deli granted the Dutch sole rights over taxation and leases, in exchange for an annual pension. That year, the [East Coast Residency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatra's_East_Coast_Residency) was established.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

On March 12, 1942, Japanese forces landed in Palubuhan Ruku on the east coast, and advanced on Medan. The advance force reported it had captured the town the next day. The main force followed on bicycles. There was some resistance from the Dutch forces, particularly around [Pematangsiantar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pematangsiantar), but the last major town, [Sibolga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sibolga), fell on 15 March. During the Japanese occupation of North Sumatra, the leader of the Japanese Armed Forces was centred in [Bukittinggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukittinggi), moving the de facto capital out of the Dutch centre of Medan. The occupation lasted 3 years. In 1945 the Japanese occupation officially ended with [Japan's surrender in the Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_over_Japan_Day) and two days later [Sukarno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarno) declared [Indonesian Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_Indonesian_Independence), beginning the four-year [Indonesian War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution) against the Dutch.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### Independence and contemporary era

Main articles: [East Sumatra revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Sumatra_revolution) and [State of East Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_East_Sumatra)



[East Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_East_Sumatra) rally in [Pematangsiantar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pematangsiantar), between 1945 and 1950

Beginning on 3 March 1946, there was a [social revolution in East Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Sumatra_revolution). Across 25 "native states", many sultanates were overthrown. Armed *pergerakan* groups (Indonesian nationalists) conducted mass killings of the members of aristocratic families.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaid1973-26] To opportunistic *pergerakan* militants (including [Communist Party of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Indonesia) members Xarim MS and Luat Siregar), the revolutionary movement was seen as a means for East Sumatra to be freed from colonial rule and to join the larger [Indonesian National Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_National_Revolution).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKahin1970180-27] Participants of the revolution were believed to be provoked by leaders to kill aristocrats and create violence with three prime objectives: to eliminate the sultans and aristocrats (who were seen as Dutch allies), to seize their wealth (to fund the Indonesian independence campaign), and to eliminate the region's feudal social structure.[^cite_note-28]

The Dutch authorities, working with the East Sumatra Union (*Persatuan Sumatra Timur*), which had been formed in 1938 by westernized Malay elites to reassert [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)) and [Simalungun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simalungun_people) ethnic interests, established the [State of East Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_East_Sumatra) ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Negara Sumatra Timur (NST)*) as part of the planned [United States of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Indonesia). The state lasted from December 1947 to August 1950.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELangenberg19821–30-29] In addition to the Dutch, the NST state was supported by Malay aristocrats, most of the Simalungun rajas, some [Karo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karo_people_\(Indonesia\)) chieftains, and Chinese groups who felt that the revolution threatened their interests. Dr Tengku Mansu, a member of the Asahan royal family, was selected as head of state for the NST. While the Dutch wanted the NST to be seen as an orderly and progressive alternative to the republic, western-educated aristocrats saw it as a bastion for their own ethnic interests.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEReid2014-30] Following the [Dutch-Indonesian Round Table Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch%E2%80%93Indonesian_Round_Table_Conference) in late 1949, the Dutch withdrew military support for the State of East Sumatra and its local authority began to collapse. The short-lived state was viewed with suspicion, and Dr Tengku Mansur entered into negotiations with [Mohammad Hatta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Hatta) to reunify East Sumatra with the Republic of Indonesia in May 1950. Mansur surrendered authority to the republic and East Sumatra merged with Tapanuli to become the province of North Sumatra on August 15, 1950.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

After Indonesian independence, at the first session of the National Committee of Regions (KND), Sumatra was divided into three sub-provinces: North Sumatra, [Central Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Sumatra) and [South Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Sumatra). North Sumatra was an amalgamation of three administrative regions called Residencies: the Residency of Aceh, the East Sumatra Residency, and the Residency of Tapanuli. The publication of the *Law of the Republic of Indonesia (R.I.) No. 10, Year 1948* on April 15, 1948, formalized the division of these three provinces. The date of 15 April 1948 has been subsequently celebrated as the anniversary of North Sumatra Province.

Act R.I. No. 24 of 1956, promulgated on 7 December 1956, established a separate Aceh Province, independent of the province of North Sumatra.[^cite_note-33]

## Geography



[Lake Toba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Toba), world's largest volcanic lake

The province of North Sumatra stretches across the island of [Sumatra](28679.md), between the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) and the [Strait of Malacca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Malacca), with an area of 72,437.755 km<sup>2</sup>. It borders the province of [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aceh) to the northwest and [Riau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau) and [West Sumatra](453590.md) to the southeast. The province contains a broad, low plain along the Strait of Malacca on which the provincial capital, Medan, is located. In the south and west, the land rises to the [Bukit Barisan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barisan_Mountains) mountain range that runs the length of Sumatra; the mountains here surround [Lake Toba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Toba), which was formed from the [caldera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera) of an ancient volcano. Several large islands in the Indian Ocean off the west coast of Sumatra are currently part of North Sumatra, most notably [Nias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias) Island and the [Batu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batu_Islands).



Satellite photo of North Sumatra and Nias Island with Lake Toba in the centre

There are 419 islands in North Sumatra. The outer islands include the island of Simuk (the most westerly of the Batu Islands) and the island of [Berhala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berhala_Island_\(Sumatra\)) in the Strait of Malacca. The Nias archipelago, located off the coast of western Sumatra in the Indian Ocean, consists of Nias Island and other much smaller islands in its vicinity. Its administrative centre is located at the city of [Gunungsitoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunungsitoli) on the northeast coast of Nias. The Batu Islands, just southeast of Nias, consist of 51 islands including three major islands: Pini, Tanahbala and Tanahmasa. Pulau Telo is their administrative centre on the small island of Situasi. Other [islands in North Sumatra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Indonesia#North_Sumatra) include Imanna, Pasu, Bawa, Hamutaia, Batu Makelele, Masa, Bau, Simaleh, Makole, Jake, and [Wunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wunga_Island?action=edit\&redlink=1).

Half of the province is located on a high plateau that runs along the Bukit Barisan mountains, from central North Sumatra to the western coast. The tallest mountain in the province is [Mount Sinabung](8494859.md) in [Karo Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karo_Regency), at elevation around 2,460 metres (8,070 ft), the most active volcano in the region. Volcanic activities (cracks where steam, gas, and lava are emitted) were observed at the summit in 1912; recent documented events include an eruption in the early hours of 29 August 2010 and eruptions in September and November 2013, January, February and October 2014.[^cite_note-JPost280910-34] Another volcano nearby is [Mount Sibayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Sibayak), also located in the Karo highland, sitting at an elevation of 2,181 metres (7,156 ft). Crystalline [sulfur](27127.md) deposits on Mount Sibayak have historically been mined on a small scale, and seepage of sulfurous gases has caused acidic discolouration of its small [crater lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_crater_lake).



Mount Sinabung emitting [vog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vog), as seen from the village of Kandibata, Karo Regency, 2017.

Lake Toba is the site of a supervolcanic eruption that occurred 69,000 to 77,000 years ago, estimated at [VEI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_explosivity_index) 8, that formed a climate-changing event.[^cite_note-35] Recent advances in dating methods suggest more precise dating at 74,000 years ago.[^cite_note-36] It is the largest-known explosive eruption on Earth in the last 25 million years. It has been accepted that the eruption of the Toba Caldera led to a [volcanic winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_winter) with a worldwide decrease in temperatures between 3 and 5 °C (5.4 and 9.0 °F), and up to 15 °C (27 °F) in higher latitudes. Additional studies in [Lake Malawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Malawi) in East Africa show significant amounts of ash deposited by the Toba Caldera eruptions, despite a great distance from the area, but these studies offer little indication of a significant climatic effect in East Africa.[^cite_note-37] According to the [Toba catastrophe theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youngest_Toba_eruption), the eruption killed most humans living at that time and is believed to have created a [population bottleneck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_bottleneck) in central East Africa and India, affecting the human genome to the present day.[^cite_note-38] More recent studies have cast doubt on this theory and found no evidence of substantial changes in population.[^cite_note-39]



Mount Leuser National Park

In North Sumatra, there are currently two national parks: the [Gunung Leuser National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunung_Leuser_National_Park) and [Batang Gadis National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batang_Gadis_National_Park). According to the Ministerial Decree No. 44 of 2005, forest area in North Sumatra today covers 3,742,120 hectares (9,247,000 acres), consisting of a 477,070-hectare (ha) Natural Reserve Area/Natural Conservation Area, 1,297,330 ha of protected forest, 879,270 ha of limited production forest, 1,035,690 ha of permanent production forest, and 52,7600 ha of production forest that can be converted to another status.

Map of North Sumatra showing tree-cover loss years, with forest remaining since 2000 in green and loss years shaded yellow through purple.

Tree-cover loss year in North Sumatra, 2001-2024, from the [Global Forest Change dataset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Forest_Change_dataset).

However, the above figure is only *[de jure](43594.md)*. In reality, the forests are not as large as the figures suggest. A lot of the forests are damaged, due to encroachment and [illegal logging](465780.md). So far, over 206,000 ha of forests in Sumatra have experienced changes in function. As many as 163,000 ha were converted to plantations and 42,900 ha were transmigration areas.

## Governance



North Sumatra governor main office, Medan

The administrative centre of North Sumatra is located in the city of [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan), governed by a [governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_Indonesian_governors). Earlier governments ruled all of Sumatra (1945–1950); and a North Sumatra Province that included Aceh (1950–1956). In 1956, Aceh split off to form a separate Aceh Province.

### Administrative divisions

North Sumatra is currently subdivided into 25 [regencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regency_\(Indonesia\)) (*kabupaten*) and 8 autonomous [cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cities_of_Indonesia) (formerly municipalities). When originally created as a province with its current boundaries, it was composed of 10 regencies and 6 cities, but an 11th regency ([Dairi Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairi_Regency)) was created on 23 September 1964. Fourteen more new regencies and two new cities were created between 1998 and 2008. All these are listed below with their areas and their populations at the 2010 census[^cite_note-Biro_Pusat_Statistik_2011-40] and the 2020 census,[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2021-41] together with their official estimates for mid-2025.[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2026-1] Regencies and cities are sub-divided into 455 [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Indonesia), which in turn are further sub-divided into 6,110 [villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villages_of_Indonesia) (as of early 2024). Proposals have been under consideration since 2013 by the People's Representative Council (*Dewan Perwakilan Rakyat* or DPR) to create three new provinces from parts of the present North Sumatra: Nias Islands, Tapanuli, and East Sumatra; however since 2013 the Indonesian Government have maintained a moratorium on the intended creation of new provinces, regencies and cities. #### Tapanuli Region



A photo of *Bagas Godang* in Panyabungan, Mandailing Natal



Old Batak Toba Village, or known as [Bolon House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolon_House) (*Rumah Bolon*) in Simanindo

This region comprises the southwestern part of the province on the island of [Sumatra](28679.md), and equates to the former [Tapanoeli Residency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tapanuli_Residency) which existed at the time of independence (except for Nias Island). When the province was created, the region comprised three regencies (North Tapanuli, Central Tapanuli and South Tapanuli) and the city of Sibolga. A fourth regency - Dairi - was created on 23 September 1964 from part of North Tapanuli Regency. Two more new regencies were formed on 23 November 1998 - Mandailing Natal from part of South Tapanuli Regency, and Toba Samosir from another part of North Tapanuli Regency. The city of Padang Sidempuan was split off from South Tapanuli Regency on 21 June 2001. Two further regencies were formed on 25 February 2003 - Humbang Hasundutan from part of North Tapanuli Regency, and Pakpak Bharat from part of Dairi Regency. A new Samosir Regency was created from part of Toba Samosir Regency on 18 December 2003 (more recently, the remaining part of this regency was renamed Toba Regency). Another two regencies were created on 17 July 2007 - Padang Lawas and North Padang Lawas, both from parts of South Tapanuli Regency.

### National Electoral Districts

The province comprises three of Indonesia's 84 national electoral districts to elect members to the [People's Representative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Consultative_Assembly). Each of the three districts elects 10 members to the People's Representative Council (for a total of 30 members from the province).[^cite_note-44]

- The **North Sumatra I Electoral District** consists of 2 of the regencies in the province (Deli Serdang and Serdang Bedagai), together with the cities of Medan and Tebingtinggi.

- The **North Sumatra II Electoral District** consists of 16 regencies (Nias, South Nias, North Nias and West Nias; Samosir, Toba, North Tapanuli, Humbang Hasundutan, Central Tapanuli, South Tapanuli, North Padang Lawas, Padang Lawas and Mandailing Natal; Labuhan Batu, South Labuhan Batu and North Labuhan Batu), together with the cities of Sibolga, Padangsidempuan and Gunungsitoli.

- The **North Sumatra III Electoral District** consists of the remaining 7 regencies (Asahan, Simalungun, Dairi, Karo, Langkat, Pakpak Bharat and Batubara), along with the cities of Tanjungbalai, Pematangsiantar and Binjai.

### Population

North Sumatra recorded a population of 12,985,075 in the 2010 national census,[^cite_note-Biro_Pusat_Statistik_2011-40] making it the 4th most populous province in Indonesia, with a [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_sex_ratio) of 99.59 men per 100 women.[^cite_note-45] The 2015 Intermediate census gave a total of 13,923,262, while the 2020 census gave a total of 14,799,361,[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2021-41] and the official estimate for mid-2025 was 15,785,839[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2026-1] The mid-2025 total comprised 7,922,846 males and 7,862,993 females, giving a sex ratio of 100.76 men per 100 women.

### Ethnic groups

Ethnic groups in North Sumatra (census 2010).

North Sumatra is a multi-ethnic province. The Malay people are regarded as the majority people of the east coast of the province, while the west coast of the province is mainly inhabited by the Batak (*Pakpak*, *Angkola* and *Mandailing* groups). The central region around Lake Toba to the northern Karo highland is predominantly inhabited by other Batak groups (*Toba*, *Simalungun* and *Karo*). The Nias people are indigenous to Nias Island and its surrounding islets. With the opening of tobacco plantations in East Sumatra during the colonial era, the colonial government employed many contract labourers for plantations, they were mainly Chinese, Javanese and Indian migrants, who were majority does not returned after end contract and decided to stay in North Sumatra. The rapid urbanisation in the province also attract neighbouring people from Aceh, Riau and West Sumatra, which is the Aceh and Minangkabau people presents.

Bataks make up 44.95% of the population, including the Batak Karo and Mandailing. The Javanese come second with 30.62%, the Malays add up to 5.92% and the ethnic Chinese comprise 5.75%. The Nias people make up around 4.10% and the rest are Minangkabau (2.66%), Acehnese (1.07%), ethnic Indian (1.00%) and other ethnic groups (1.15%)

North Sumatra hosts various ethnicities, religions and traditions, influenced by the [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak), [Malay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)) and [Nias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias_people) peoples, and other cultures such as [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians), [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Indonesians) and [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people).

### Music

The music that is usually played depends on the traditional ceremonies held, but is more dominant with the drums. As in the Coastal Ethnic ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Orang Pesisir*), there are musical instruments called *Sikambang*.



Bataknese *Gondang* [percussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percussion) during a local ceremony in North Sumatra

The Batak Toba, Pakpak and Simalungun tribes have a musical instrument called *Gondang* which is usually played during traditional ceremonies in marriage, death, and so on. Meanwhile, the Mandailing and Angkola Batak tribes have musical instruments similar to the gondang, namely *Gordang Sambilan*. The Malays in the East Coast have the same musical instruments as the Malays in general, such as the [accordion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accordion), the Malay drum ([kompang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kompang)) and the [violin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violin). Meanwhile, in Tanah Karo, there are the [Kulcapi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kacapi) and *Gendang* musical instruments which are commonly used to accompany the *Landek* or *Guro Guro Aron* dance.

### Architecture

Main article: [Batak architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_architecture)

In the field of [fine arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_arts), what stands out is the traditional house architecture which is a combination of the results of sculpture and carving as well as the results of handicrafts. Traditional house architecture is found in various forms of ornament. In general, the shape of the traditional house building in the Batak traditional group symbolizes a "buffalo standing upright" ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Kerbau berdiri tegak*). This is even clearer by decorating the top of the roof with buffalo heads.

The traditional house of the Batak Toba ethnic, [Ruma Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_architecture), is still commonly found in Samosir and Lake Toba area.The Batak Karo traditional house looks big and taller than other traditional houses. The roof is made of palm fiber and is usually supplemented with smaller triangular roofs called *ayo-ayo rumah*, *Jambur* and *tersek*. With multi-layered roofs, the Karo house has a distinctive shape compared to other traditional houses that only have one roof in North Sumatra; there are still several villages in Karo highland that still reserve traditional house and buildings, like in *Lingga*. The shape of the traditional house in the Batak Simalungun area is quite attractive. The traditional house complex in *Pematang Purba* village consists of several buildings, namely the *Rumah Bolon*, *Balai Bolon*, drying rack, taboo hall of need, and mortar. The prominent Mandailing buildings are called *Bagas Gadang* (house of Namora Natoras) and *Sopo Godang* (customary consultation hall).

Malay traditional houses in North Sumatra are not much different from Malay houses in other provinces, only the green and yellow colour is more dominant.

### Dances



[Batak-Karonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karo_people_\(Indonesia\)) couple doing traditional dance called *landek*

The traditional dance repertoire includes various types. Some are in the form of sacred dances and some are solely for entertainment. In addition to traditional dances which are part of traditional ceremonies, sacred dances are usually danced by *dayu-datu*. Included in this type of dance are teacher dances and stick dances. Datu danced while swinging a magic stick called Tunggal Panaluan.

*Profane* dance is usually a young social dance that is danced at a happy party. Tortor is danced at the wedding ceremony. These are usually danced by the audience including the bride and young people. These youth dances, for example *morah-morah, parakut, sipaj ok, patam-patam* and *kebangkiung*. Magical dances, such as the *Nasiraan Tortor Dance, Tunggal Panaluan Tor Tor Dance*. This dance is usually performed with solemnity.

Besides Batak dances, there are also Malay dances such as *Serampang XII, Gundala-Gundala and Landek* dance from the Karo Highlands, *Moyo* and *Maena* dance from Nias.

### Handicraft



Local craftswomen weaving [Ulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulos) in *Huta Raja* village, Ulos is Bataknese traditional [Tenun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenun) which is popular exported as garment from North Sumatra

In addition to architecture, [weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weaving) is another craft art from the Batak tribe. Examples of this weave are [ulos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulos) cloth and [songket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Songket) cloth. Ulos is a traditional Batak cloth used in wedding ceremonies, death, building houses, arts, etc. Ulos cloth is made of cotton or hemp yarn. Ulos colors are usually black, white, and red which have certain meanings. In the Pakpak tribe there is a weave known as *oles*. Usually the base color of the ointment is black-brown or white. In the Karo tribe there is a weave known as *uis*. Usually uis base colors are dark blue and reddish. In the west coast community there is a woven fabric known as *Songket Barus*. The basic color of this craft is Dark Red or Yellow Gold.

*Batubara Malay Songket* is one of the typical crafts of the East Coast that has been worldwide. The process of making *songket* still uses wooden looms in the traditional way, but still has good quality, thus this *songket* is not inferior to *songket* produced with today's sophisticated machines. The Batubara *songket* also has a variety of unique motifs such as bamboo shoots, [mangosteen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangosteen) flowers, cempaka flowers, Caul buds, *Tolak Betikam*, and Fighting Dragons. The Batubara songket woven has an attractive design and a high cultural artistic value.

### Culinary

Main article: [Batak cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak_cuisine)



Bataknese cuisine such as *Saksang*, *Babi panggang Karo* and *Sayur Daun ubi tumbuk*

There is a wide variety of food in North Sumatra, depending on the region. This includes [Saksang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saksang) and [Babi panggang karo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babi_panggang), and in the Pakpak Dairi area, *Pelleng* is a very spicy popular typical food. The characteristic of Batak cuisine is its preference for [andaliman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanthoxylum_acanthopodium) (*Zanthoxylum acanthopodium*) as the main spice. That is why andaliman in Indonesia is sometimes dubbed as *Batak pepper*.[^cite_note-Kompas.com-48] In the Batak land itself there is *dengke nani arsik* which is fish that is fried without using coconut. *Pasiyak Natonggi* or money to buy sweet palm wine is a very familiar term there, describing how close palm wine or *nira* is to their lives. Batak people are majority [Christian](18337522.md) — unlike neighboring Muslim-majority ethnic groups such as [Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acehnese_people) and [Minang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people) — Christian Batak people are not restricted to Islamic [halal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halal) dietary law.[^cite_note-JakartaGlobe-49]



*Lapo Tuak*, Bataknese [warung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warung) which served *Tuak*, an alcoholic [palm wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_wine)

Many of the Batak popular meals contain [pork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pork) as well as dishes made from unusual ingredients, such as [dog meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog_meat) or [blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blood_as_food), however there are also halal batak dishes, mostly chicken, beef, lamb, mutton, and freshwater fishes. With a large population of Batak being Muslim, especially among [Mandailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandailing_people) people, they have their own dishes such as *Sayur daun ubi tumbuk* or mashed-cassava leaf soup, *Pora-pora*, *Salai ikan*, *Pakkat* and others. Batak culinary centres are located in towns of Batak highlands, such as the town of [Kabanjahe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabanjahe) and [Berastagi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berastagi) in Tanah Karo area. Some towns around the Lake Toba offer freshwater fish dishes such as [carp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carp) [arsik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsik). The Northern Sumatra capital of Medan is also a Batak cuisine hotspot where numerous of *Lapo* (Batak restaurants) can be found anywhere across the province, it even expanded into neighbouring provinces such as [Riau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riau), [Riau Islands](453745.md), even capital [Jakarta](16275.md) and neighbouring countries like in [Malaysia](3607937.md) and [Singapore](27318.md).

The North Sumatran Malay cuisine is similar to another Malay region, meals such as [Nasi lemak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasi_lemak) (called as *Nasi Gurih*), [Bubur pedas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubur_pedas), [Lemang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lemang) and [dodol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodol) are the most known dishes from Medan and the east coast. The Chinese have influenced the province's cuisine, examples are [Cha Sio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Char_siu),*Tau Kua He Ci*, [Popia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popiah), [Bakpao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baozi), *Teng-Teng*, *Chai Pao*, *Roti Kacang* and [Bika ambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bika_ambon) which are dishes also popular elsewhere in Indonesia. Indian influences can also be seen in dishes such as [Martabak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtabak), [Roti canai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roti_canai), [Putu bambu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kue_putu) and [Mie rebus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mee_rebus). The [Minangkabau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people) and [Acehnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acehnese_people) peoples brought [Nasi padang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasi_padang) and [Mie Aceh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mie_aceh) dishes.

### Energy

North Sumatra is rich in natural resources such as natural gas in the area of Tandem, Binjai and [petroleum](23195.md) in Pangkalan Brandan, Langkat which has been explored since the days of the Dutch East Indies. Besides that, in Kuala Tanjung, Asahan, there is the company named PT Inalum (abbreviated from *Indonesia Aluminium*) that is engaged in ore mining and smelting of aluminium which is the only one in Southeast Asia.

The rivers that disgorge from the mountains around Lake Toba are also a natural resource that has enough potential to become an exploited resource for hydroelectric power plants. *Asahan hydropower*, which is the largest hydroelectric power plant on Sumatra Island, is located at Porsea in Toba Samosir Regency.

Moreover, in the mountains there are many geothermal hot spots which are very likely to be developed as a source of thermal energy or steam that can then be transformed into electrical energy.

### Agriculture and farming



Birdview of Palm Oil Estate of Asahan Regency

The province is famous for its plantations. The plantations are managed by private companies and the state. SOE Plantation area is located in North Sumatra, among others PT Perkebunan Nusantara II (PTPN II), PTPN IV and PTPN III.

North Sumatra produces rubber, cocoa, tea, palm oil, coffee, cloves, coconut, cinnamon, and tobacco. These commodities have been exported to many countries and contribute a large part to the foreign exchange for Indonesia. In addition to commodities, North Sumatra is also known as a producer of horticultural commodities (vegetables and fruits); e.g. Medanese Orange, Deli Guava, Cabbage Vegetable, Tomato, Potato and Carrot generated by Karo, Simalungun and North Tapanuli. The horticultural products have been exported to Malaysia and Singapore.

The plantation is spread in East coast area such as Deli Serdang, Serdang Bedagai Langkat, Simalungun, Asahan, Labuhan Batu, and also around west coast: Central and South Tapanuli. Plantations, especially palm oil, are important for sourcing reticulated and blood pythons, the skins of which are a major export product.[^cite_note-51]

The size of technical irrigation entirely in North Sumatra is 132 ha that covers an area of 174 irrigation site.

Sumatra Mandheling and Sumatra Lintong coffee beans are grown in North Sumatra and largely exported to the United States. [Mandheling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandailing_people) is named after the similarly spelt Mandailing people located in North Sumatra, Indonesia. The name is the result of a misunderstanding by the first foreign purchaser of the variety, and no coffee is actually produced in the "Mandailing region". Lintong on the other hand, is named after the Lintong district, also located in North Sumatra.

### Tourism

North Sumatra has lots of travel destinations. According to [Ministry of Tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Tourism_\(Indonesia\)), North Sumatra is ranked amongst the top 10 most visited province in Indonesia. There are various kinds of tourist destinations that could be found throughout the province, Berastagi is best known as a hilly place with a cooler temperature as the whole province is in tropical region. Lake Toba is also a popular travel destination. Toba is a large [volcanic lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanogenic_lake) which has an island inside of it, [Samosir Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samosir). [Nias Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nias) and several other islands nearby named [Batu Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batu_Islands) are a popular spot for surfing. The capital itself, Medan, has many places of interest, mainly for its historical sites. The city is also known for its variety of cuisine. The list below is the most known places of interest in North Sumatra:



[Sipisopiso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sipisopiso) waterfall, Tongging



Surfing in Tello Island, Nias

- [Medan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medan) is the capital of North Sumatra province with a diverse crowd population of tribes and religions in unity. There are many historical tourist monuments to spot and various great cuisines to taste in Medan.

- [Lake Toba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Toba) is the largest volcanic lake in the world. Located in the centre of North Sumatra, the lake can be reached via [Parapat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parapat) (Simalungun regency), Tongging (Karo regency), and [Balige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balige) (Toba Samosir regency).

- [Samosir Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samosir) is a volcanic island in middle of Lake Toba, It is a popular tourist destination due to its exotic [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak) history and the vistas it offers. - [Bukit Lawang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Lawang) known for the largest [animal sanctuary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_sanctuary) of [Sumatran orangutan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumatran_orangutan) (around 5,000 orangutans occupy the area), and also the main access point to the [Gunung Leuser National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunung_Leuser_National_Park) from the east side.
