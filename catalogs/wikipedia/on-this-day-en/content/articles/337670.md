**Victory Day**[^cite_note-NameUkrRusetcVD-1] is a holiday that commemorates the victory of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) over [Nazi Germany](21212.md) in 1945. It was first inaugurated in the 15 [republics of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union)[^cite_note-Karelo-FinnishDay-2] following the signing of the [German Instrument of Surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Instrument_of_Surrender) late in the evening on 8 May 1945 (9 May [Moscow Time](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Time)). The Soviet government announced the victory early on 9 May after the [signing ceremony in Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Instrument_of_Surrender#Definitive_surrender_document_in_Berlin).[^cite_note-3] Although the official inauguration occurred in 1945, the holiday became a non-labor day only in 1965.

In [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), 8 May was observed as **Liberation Day** from 1950 to 1966, and was celebrated again on the 40th anniversary in 1985. In 1967, a Soviet-style "Victory Day" was celebrated on 8 May.

The [Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Federation) has officially recognized 9 May since its formation in 1991 and considers it a non-working holiday even if it falls on a weekend (in which case any following Monday will be a non-working holiday). The holiday was similarly celebrated while the country was part of the Soviet Union. Most other countries in Europe observe [Victory in Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_in_Europe_Day) (often abbreviated to VE Day, or V-E Day) on 8 May, and [Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_Day)[^cite_note-euDay-5] on 9 May as national remembrance or victory days.

The [German Instrument of Surrender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Instrument_of_Surrender) was signed twice. An initial document was signed in [Reims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reims), France on 7 May 1945 by [Alfred Jodl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Jodl) (chief of staff of the German [OKW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OKW)) for Germany, [Walter Bedell Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Bedell_Smith) (USA), on behalf of the [Supreme Commander of the Allied Expeditionary Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Headquarters_Allied_Expeditionary_Force), and [Ivan Susloparov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Susloparov), on behalf of the [Soviet High Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavka), in the presence of Free French Major-General [François Sevez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Sevez) as the official witness. Since the Soviet High Command had not agreed to the text of the surrender, and because Susloparov, a relatively low-ranking officer, was not authorized to sign this document, the Soviet Union requested that a second, revised, instrument of surrender be signed in Berlin. [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) declared that the Soviet Union considered the Reims surrender a preliminary document, and [Dwight D. Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwight_D._Eisenhower) immediately agreed with that. Another argument was that some German troops considered the Reims instrument of surrender as a surrender to the Western Allies only, and fighting continued in the East, [especially in Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prague_offensive).[^cite_note-6]

> \[Quoting Stalin:] Today, in Reims, Germans signed the preliminary act on an unconditional surrender. The main contribution, however, was done by Soviet people and not by the Allies, therefore the capitulation must be signed in front of the Supreme Command of *all countries* of the anti-Hitler coalition, and not only in front of the Supreme Command of Allied Forces. Moreover, I disagree that the surrender was not signed in Berlin, which was the center of Nazi aggression. We agreed with the Allies to consider the Reims protocol as preliminary.

A second surrender ceremony was organized in the outskirts of [Berlin](3354.md) late on 8 May, when it was already 9 May in [Moscow](19004.md) due to the difference in time zones. Field-Marshal [Wilhelm Keitel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Keitel), chief of OKW, signed a final German Instrument of Surrender, which was also signed by Marshal [Georgy Zhukov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgy_Zhukov), on behalf of the Supreme High Command of the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army), and Air Chief Marshal [Arthur Tedder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Tedder,_1st_Baron_Tedder) (Britain), on behalf of the Allied Expeditionary Force, in the presence of General [Carl Spaatz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Spaatz) (USA) and General [Jean de Lattre de Tassigny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_de_Lattre_de_Tassigny) (Free French), as witnesses. The surrender was signed in the Soviet Army headquarters in [Berlin-Karlshorst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin-Karlshorst). Both [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language) versions of the instrument of surrender signed in Berlin were considered authentic texts.

The revised Berlin text of the instrument of surrender differed from the preliminary text signed in Reims in explicitly stipulating the [complete disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_disarmament) of all [German military forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht), handing over their weapons to local Allied military commanders.

Both the Reims and Berlin instruments of surrender stipulated that forces under German control to cease active operations at 23\:01 hours [CET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_European_Time) on 8 May 1945. However, due to the difference in Central European and Moscow time zones, the end of war is celebrated on 9 May in the Soviet Union and most post-Soviet countries.

To commemorate the victory in the war, the ceremonial [Moscow Victory Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Victory_Parade_of_1945) was held in the Soviet capital on 24 June 1945.

### In Russia

During the Soviet Union's existence, 9 May was celebrated throughout it and in the [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc). Though the holiday was introduced in many Soviet republics between 1946 and 1950, it became a non-working day only in the [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) in 1963 and the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic) in 1965.[^cite_note-7] In the Russian SFSR, a weekday off (usually a Monday) was given if 9 May fell on a Saturday or Sunday.

The celebration of Victory Day continued during subsequent years. The war became a topic of great importance in cinema, literature, history lessons at school, the mass media, and the arts. The ritual of the celebration gradually obtained a distinctive character with a number of similar elements: ceremonial meetings, speeches, lectures, receptions and fireworks.[^cite_note-szf.lu.lv-8]

In Russia during the 1990s, the 9 May holiday was not celebrated with large Soviet-style mass demonstrations due to the policies of successive Russian governments. Following [Vladimir Putin](32817.md)'s rise to power, the Russian government began promoting the prestige of the governing regime and history, and national holidays and commemorations became a source of national self-esteem. Victory Day in Russia has become a celebration in which popular culture plays a central role. The 60th and 70th anniversaries of Victory Day in Russia (2005 and 2015) became the largest popular holidays since the collapse of the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-szf.lu.lv-8]

In 1995, as the world celebrated the 50th anniversary of the end of the war, many world leaders converged on [Moscow](19004.md) to attend the city's first state sponsored ceremonies since the end of the Soviet Union. In 2015 around 30 leaders, including those of China and India, attended the 2015 celebration, while Western leaders boycotted the ceremonies because of the [Russian military intervention in Ukraine](42085878.md) that started in 2014.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] The [2020 edition of the parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Moscow_Victory_Day_Parade), marking the [75th anniversary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diamond_jubilee) of the victory over Nazi Germany, was postponed due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

[Russophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russophone) populations in many countries celebrate the holiday regardless of its local status,[^cite_note-13] organize public gatherings and even parades on this day.[^cite_note-14] Some multilanguage broadcasting television networks translate the "Victory speech" of the Russian president and the parade on [Red Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Square) for telecasts for viewers all over the globe, making the parade one of the world's most watched events of the year.[^cite_note-15] [RT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT_\(TV_network\)) also broadcasts the parade featuring live commentary, and also airs yet another highlight of the day – the [Minute of Silence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minute_of_Silence) at 6\:55 pm MST, a tradition dating back to 1965.

Because of [massive losses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_II_casualties_of_the_Soviet_Union) among both military and civilians during [World War II](32927.md), Victory Day is one of the most important and emotional dates in Russia.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

### Other countries currently celebrating 9 May

- [Armenia](10918072.md) [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Armenia) has officially recognized 9 May since [its independence](10918072.md#Restoration_of_independence) in 1991. The holiday was similarly celebrated there while the country was [part of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).

  [^cite_note-18]

  &#x20;[Shushi Liberation Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shushi_Liberation_Day) is also commemorated with the holiday.

- [Azerbaijan](746.md) [Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Azerbaijan) has officially recognized 9 May since 1994. Upon [its independence](746.md#Republic_era) in 1991, the [Azerbaijani Popular Front Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_Popular_Front_Party) and later the government of [Abulfaz Elchibey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abulfaz_Elchibey) deliberately erased the holiday from the calendar, with veterans being subjected to a targeted anti-Russian/Soviet media campaign. Upon coming to power, [Heydar Aliyev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heydar_Aliyev) restored the holiday to the national calendar.

  [^cite_note-19]

  &#x20;The holiday is similarly celebrated as it was while the country was [part of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).

  [^cite_note-20]

- [Belarus](3457.md) [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Belarus) has officially recognized 9 May since [its independence](3457.md#Independence) in 1991 and considers it a non-working day. The holiday was similarly celebrated there while the country was [part of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). Belarus has had seven Victory Day Parades, on [Independence Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Square,_Minsk) and [Masherov Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victors_Avenue) (1995, 2000, 2005, 2010, 2015, 2020 and 2025) and has had an annual ceremony on [Victory Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Square,_Minsk) since independence.

- [Bosnia and Herzegovina](3463.md) [Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Bosnia_and_Herzegovina) has officially recognized 9 May since its [independence](3463.md). However, it is not a working day only in [Republic of Srpska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Srpska).

- [Croatia](5573.md) [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Croatia) has officially recognized 9 May as Victory Day over Fascism as a memorial day since 2019, which is a legally-recognised day, but is not a public holiday.

- [Georgia (country)](48768.md) [Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Georgia) has officially recognized 9 May since [its independence](48768.md#After_restoration_of_independence) in 1991. The holiday was similarly celebrated there while the country was [part of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). It is particularly celebrated in this way with the [Russian community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Georgia) in the country.

- [Israel](9282173.md) In Israel, Victory Day on 9 May has historically been celebrated as an unofficial [national remembrance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_memory) day. However, in 2017, [Victory in Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_in_Europe_Day_\(Israel\)) was upgraded to the status of an official [national holiday day of commemoration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday) by the [Knesset](74656.md), with schools and businesses operating as usual.

  [^cite_note-21]

  &#x20;As a result of immigration of many Red Army veterans, Israel now hosts the largest and most extensive Victory Day celebrations outside the former Soviet Union. - [Kazakhstan](16642.md) [Kazakhstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Kazakhstan) has officially recognized 9 May since [its independence](16642.md#Independence) in 1991 as a national holiday. The holiday is sometimes celebrated in connection with the [Defender of the Fatherland Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defender_of_the_Fatherland_Day_\(Kazakhstan\)) holiday on 7 May. From 1947 the holiday was similarly celebrated there while the country was [part of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kazakh_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).

- [Kyrgyzstan](170131.md) [Kyrgyzstan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Kyrgyzstan) has officially recognised 9 May since [its independence](170131.md#Independence) in 1991. The holiday was similarly celebrated there while the country was [part of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirghiz_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).

- [Mongolia](19271.md) [Mongolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Mongolia) had officially recognized 9 May during its existence as a [satellite state of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongolian_People's_Republic). The holiday continues to be celebrated unofficially throughout the country.

  [^cite_note-22]

- [Montenegro](20760.md) [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Montenegro) officially recognised 9 May as Victory Day over Fascism as an official holiday.

- [Serbia](29265.md) [Serbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Serbia) celebrates 9 May as Victory Day over Fascism but it is a working holiday. Still many people gather to mark the anniversary with the war veterans, including the President, Minister of Defense and the highest officers of the [Serbian Armed Forces](381170.md).

- [Tajikistan](30108.md) [Tajikistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Tajikistan) has officially recognised 9 May since [its independence](30108.md#Independence) following the dissolution of the Soviet Union. The holiday was similarly celebrated there while the country was [part of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajik_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).

  [^cite_note-23]

- [Turkmenistan](198149.md) [Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Turkmenistan) has officially recognised 9 May since [its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Turkmenistan#Independence_and_Turkmenbashi) in 1991. It officially known as the Day of Remembrance of National Heroes of Turkmenistan in the 1941–1945 World War. It was established by a special decree of [President of Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Turkmenistan) [Saparmurat Niyazov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saparmurat_Niyazov) in 2000.

  [^cite_note-24]

  &#x20;The holiday was similarly celebrated there while the country was [part of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkmen_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).

  [^cite_note-26]

  [^cite_note-27]

  &#x20;In recent years, Turkmen prisoners have received pardons from the [President of Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Turkmenistan) on Victory Day.

  [^cite_note-28]

- [Uzbekistan](31853.md) [Uzbekistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Uzbekistan) has officially recognised 9 May from 2 March 1999, where the holiday was introduced as the "Day of Remembrance and Honour" (*Xotira va Qadirlash Kuni*).

  [^cite_note-Their_memory_lives_on-29]

  &#x20;It is the only country in the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md) to not officially recognize the 9 May holiday as Victory Day. Under President [Islam Karimov](335230.md), the holiday was toned down, with many veterans being told not to wear their Soviet-era decorations or uniforms on the holiday.

  [^cite_note-30]

  &#x20;Since Karimov's death in 2016, the holiday has been celebrated there similarly to how it was celebrated while the country was [part of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uzbek_Soviet_Socialist_Republic).

### Countries formerly celebrating 9 May

- [Bulgaria](3415.md) [Bulgaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Bulgaria) officially recognized 9 May during its existence as a satellite state of the Soviet Union. Since 1989, all official celebrations of 9 May have been cancelled. As in other EU countries, Victory Day in Bulgaria is 8 May, whereas 9 May is [Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_Day). However, many still gather unofficially to celebrate Victory Day on 9 May.

  [^cite_note-31]

  &#x20;Flowers are generally laid at the [Monument to the Soviet Army in Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_the_Soviet_Army,_Sofia).

- [Moldova](19260.md) [Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Moldova) officially recognized 9 May as [a public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Moldova) from [its independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_of_Moldova) in 1991 to 2023. From 1951 to 1991, the holiday had also been celebrated during the country's rule by the Soviet Union as the [Moldavian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavian_SSR). It is now officially known as the "Victory Day and Commemoration of the Fallen Heroes for the Independence of the Fatherland" ([Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Ziua Victoriei și a Comemorării Eroilor Căzuți pentru Independența Patriei*).

  [^cite_note-32]

  &#x20;Victory Day was a major national holiday, particularly due to Moldova's [Russian community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Moldova) and also due to the [Party of Socialists of the Republic of Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_of_Socialists_of_the_Republic_of_Moldova) (PSRM) and its state-sponsored Victory Day rallies of 2017 on the [Great National Assembly Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_National_Assembly_Square,_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u) at Chișinău. Wreath-laying ceremonies were commonly held at the [Eternity Memorial Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eternity_Memorial_Complex,_Chi%C8%99in%C4%83u) of Chișinău during the day.

- [Poland](22936.md) [Poland](22936.md) officially recognised 9 May as [a state holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Poland), where it was known as *Narodowe Święto Zwycięstwa i Wolności* ("National Victory and Freedom Day") from 1945 until 2015. Until 1950 it was also a day free from work. The holiday served both as a celebration of victory against Nazi Germany as well as to highlight the union between the [Polish People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Republic) and the USSR. It was a key celebration that was observed with parades and major events throughout the entire country. Since the fall of the Eastern Bloc, the holiday had become controversial in Poland. Since 24 April 2015, Poland officially recognised *Narodowy Dzień Zwycięstwa* ("National Victory Day") on 8 May (like Victory in Europe Day) in place of the previous holiday.

  [^cite_note-33]

- [Romania](25445.md) [Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Romania) officially recognized Victory Day as [a public holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Romania) during the [communist era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania). Since Romania's [1877 independence day from the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_War_of_Independence) also coincides with Victory Day on 9 May, the holiday was made a double celebration, especially under [Nicolae Ceaușescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Ceau%C8%99escu) who sought to portray [communism in Romania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_communism_in_Romania) as a continuation of Romania's independence struggles from previous centuries. Victory Day in Romania is now celebrated on 8 May, whereas 9 May is celebrated as [Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_Day). However, some Russophiles, Eurosceptics and leftists gather at informal meetings to celebrate Victory Day on 9 May. Thus, it may mark a triple celebration in the country.

- [Ukraine](31750.md) [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Ukraine) officially recognised 9 May from [its independence](31750.md#Independence) in 1991 until 2013, where it was a non-working day. If it fell on a weekend the following Monday was non-working.

  [^cite_note-LvivDay-35]

  &#x20;The holiday was similarly celebrated there while the country was [part of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). According to opinion polling by [Kyiv International Institute of Sociology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_International_Institute_of_Sociology), in 2010 almost 60% of Ukrainians considered Victory Day one of the biggest holidays of Ukraine.

### Holiday traditions

#### Victory Day Parades

See also: [Moscow Victory Day Parade](49466651.md)



The [2005 Victory Day parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Moscow_Victory_Day_Parade) on [Moscow](19004.md)'s [Red Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Square)



[2015 Victory Day parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Moscow_Victory_Day_Parade) on [Moscow](19004.md)'s [Red Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Square)



2021 [Moscow Victory Day Parade](49466651.md). [Military parades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_parade) and Soviet military symbolism play an important role in the 9 May celebrations across Russia.

[Victory Day Parades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Day_Parades) are [military parades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_parades) that are held on 9 May, particularly in various post-soviet nations such as [Russia](25391.md), [Kazakhstan](16642.md), [Belarus](3457.md), and until 2015, [Ukraine](31750.md). Outside of the former Soviet Union, military victory parades have also been held in [Serbia](29265.md), [Poland](22936.md) and the [Czech Republic](5321.md). The first victory day parade on [Red Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Square) took place with the participation of the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) and a small detachment from the [First Polish Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Polish_Army) on 24 June 1945. After a 20-year hiatus, the parade was held again and became a regular tradition among [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc) countries and Soviet allies. Countries that had this tradition included [Yugoslavia](34244.md) and [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia), both of which had their last parades in 1985.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] After the [fall of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Soviet_Union), they quickly fell out of style in [Europe](9239.md) and soon became a practice among [post-Soviet nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_nations), many of which have large Russian populations. In 1995, Russia, [Kazakhstan](16642.md), [Belarus](3457.md), and [Ukraine](31750.md) held parades for the first time since 1991.

#### Mass processions

In Belarus on non-jubilee years, a procession is held from [October Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Square,_Minsk), which ends with the laying of wreaths on [Victory Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Square,_Minsk).[^cite_note-61] In 2015, a parade of young people, cadets of military lyceums, young athletes took place on Bishkek's [Ala-Too Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ala-Too_Square), attended by President [Almazbek Atambayev](7837715.md) and Prime Minister [Temir Sariev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temir_Sariev).[^cite_note-62] The [Immortal Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immortal_Regiment) ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Бессмертный полк; Bessmertniy Polk) is a massive civil event staged in major cities in Russia and around the world every 9 May. Since it was introduced in 2012, it has been conducted in cities such as [Moscow](19004.md), [Washington D.C.](108956.md), [Dushanbe](56658.md), [Berlin](3354.md), and [Yekaterinburg](66524.md). Participants carry pictures of relatives or family members who served during the Second World War. The front line of the procession carries a banner with the words *Bessmertniy Polk* written on it.[^cite_note-63] Up to 12 million Russians have participated in the march nationwide in recent years. Since 2015, the President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) and senior Russian officials have participated in the procession in Moscow.[^cite_note-rferl.org-64] It has come under criticism by those who charge that participants are carrying photographs and discarding them after the event.[^cite_note-rferl.org-64][^cite_note-ImmortalRegimentDay-65]

#### Gatherings at monuments

Members of government usually take part in a wreath-laying ceremony at their national war memorial, usually dedicated to the specific war victory. Wreaths are often laid at memorials such as the [Tomb of the Unknown Soldier (Moscow)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomb_of_the_Unknown_Soldier_\(Moscow\)), the [Monument to the Unknown Sailor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_to_the_Unknown_Sailor) ([Odesa](55545.md)), and the Monument to [Hazi Aslanov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazi_Aslanov) ([Baku](4566.md)). Although [Latvia](17514.md) does not officially recognize 9 May, most of the large [Russian community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Latvia) informally celebrates the holiday, with trips to the [Victory Memorial to Soviet Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Memorial_to_Soviet_Army) being common in [Riga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riga), with some diplomats ([ambassadors of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassadors_of_Russia), Kazakhstan, and Belarus) as well as some politicians ([Nils Ušakovs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nils_U%C5%A1akovs), [Alfrēds Rubiks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfr%C4%93ds_Rubiks)) also taking part.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] On 20 April 2023 the [Latvian Parliament](499765.md) passed a bill to ban all public celebrations on May 9, the only exception being Europe Day.[^cite_note-20230420LatvianSaeima-68] The law was meant to stop the "glorification of warfare and to stem the propagandist distortions of World War II history often implicit in Victory Day celebrations."[^cite_note-20230420LatvianSaeima-68]

#### Religious commemorations

In the Easter message of 1945, the [Patriarch Alexy I of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_Alexy_I_of_Moscow) wrote:[^cite_note-69]

> The Easter joy of the Resurrection of Christ is now combined with the bright hope of an imminent victory of truth and light over the untruth and darkness of German fascism, which before our eyes is crushed by the combined force of our valiant troops and the troops of our allies. The dark forces of fascism were not able to resist the light and power of Christ, and God's omnipotence appeared over the imaginary power of man.

Every 26 April ([Old Style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style), O.S.; 9 May, New Style or N.S.), the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church) commemorates the dead, being the only special remembrance day for the dead with a fixed date. After the [liturgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgy), a memorial service for the fallen soldiers is served in all churches and monasteries of the Orthodox Church. The annual commemoration on Victory Day "of the soldiers who for faith, the Fatherland and the people laid down their lives and all those who died in the Great Patriotic War of 1941–1945" was established by the [Bishops' Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishops'_Council_of_the_Russian_Orthodox_Church) of the Russian Orthodox Church in 1994.[^cite_note-70] On the eve of the 65th anniversary in 2010, [Patriarch Kirill of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_Kirill_of_Moscow) gave his blessing for all the churches of the Russian Orthodox Church to perform a "prayer service in memory of the deliverance of our people from a terrible, mortal enemy, from a danger that our Fatherland has not known in all history". The patriarch composed a special prayer for this rite, taking as a basis the prayer of [Philaret Drozdov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philaret_Drozdov), written in honor of the victory of the [Imperial Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army) over the [French Grande Armee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grande_Arm%C3%A9e) during the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars).[^cite_note-kirill-71] The completion of the [Main Cathedral of the Russian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Cathedral_of_the_Russian_Armed_Forces) was timed to Victory Day in 2020.

#### Other events

Traditions such as the Victory Relay Race are held on jubilee anniversaries.[^cite_note-72] In 2013, Turkmenistan conducted live-fire military exercises "Galkan-2013" (Shield-2013) dedicated to the 68th anniversary of the Victory, observed by President [Gurbanguly Berdymukhamedov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gurbanguly_Berdymukhamedov) at the Kelyata Training Center of the [Ministry of Defence of Turkmenistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defense_\(Turkmenistan\)) in the [Bäherden District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A4herden) of the [Ahal Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahal_Region).[^cite_note-73] In 2016, Moldovan Defence Minister [Anatol Șalaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatol_%C8%98alaru) attended a display of vehicles from the [Moldovan National Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldovan_National_Army) and the [United States Army](32087.md) in the central park of [Chișinău](56635.md).[^cite_note-74] In April 2020, an official in the [Western Military District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Military_District) of Russia announced that an air show would be held at [Kubinka air base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kubinka_\(air_base\)) in Victory Day.[^cite_note-75]

On Victory Day, many books on topics such as the war such as [Panfilov's Twenty-Eight Guardsmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panfilov's_Twenty-Eight_Guardsmen)[^cite_note-76] are published. On the eve of the diamond jubilee, President Vladimir Putin, at the request of Chancellor [Sebastian Kurz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastian_Kurz), gave a live address broadcast Austrian TV channel [ORF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ORF_\(broadcaster\)).[^cite_note-77]

### Banner of Victory

Main article: [Victory Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Banner)

The [Victory Banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victory_Banner) refers to the Soviet military [banner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banner) raised by the Soviet [soldiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldier) on the [Reichstag building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichstag_building) in [Berlin](3354.md) on 1 May 1945. Made during the [Battle of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin) by soldiers who created it while under [battlefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle) conditions, it has historically been the official symbol of the Victory of the Soviet people against Nazi Germany. Being the 5th banner to be created, it was the only army flag that was prepared to be raised in Berlin to survive the battle. The [Cyrillic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrillic_script) inscription on the banner reads: "[150th Rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/150th_Rifle_Division), [Order of Kutuzov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Kutuzov) 2nd class, [Idritsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idritsa) Division, [79th Rifle Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/79th_Rifle_Corps), [3rd Shock Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Shock_Army), [1st Belorussian Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Belorussian_Front)", representing the unit that soldiers who raised the banner were from. On 9 May, a specially made replica of the Victory Banner is carried by a [color guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Color_guard) of the [154th Preobrazhensky Independent Commandant's Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/154th_Preobrazhensky_Independent_Commandant's_Regiment) through [Red Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Square).[^cite_note-78] The Victory Banner was brought to [Kyiv](585629.md) from [Moscow](19004.md) in October 2004 to take part in the parade in honor of the [60th Anniversary of the Liberation of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60th_Anniversary_of_the_Liberation_of_Ukraine). In 2015, the banner was brought to [Astana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astana) to participate in the [Defender of the Fatherland Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defender_of_the_Fatherland_Day_\(Kazakhstan\)) parade on 7 May.[^cite_note-79]

### Saint George's Ribbon

Main article: [Ribbon of Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbon_of_Saint_George)



Residents of [Donetsk](562951.md) carry the ribbon and portraits of ancestors who fought in [World War II](32927.md), 9 May 2015

The [Ribbon of Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ribbon_of_Saint_George) is a military symbol that dates back to the era of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). It consists of a black and orange bicolour pattern, with three black and two orange stripes. In the early 21st century, it became an [awareness ribbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awareness_ribbon) to commemorate the veterans of the war, being recognized as a [patriotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriotism) symbol.[^cite_note-OD150501-80] In countries such as Ukraine and the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states), it has been associated recently with [Russophilia](2541825.md) and [Russian irredentism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_irredentism).[^cite_note-RFE150507-81] It has become especially associated with Russian support for the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md). In Ukraine the government chose to replace it with the [remembrance poppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remembrance_poppy) which is associated with the [Remembrance Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remembrance_Day) commemorations in the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and the [British Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Commonwealth).[^cite_note-kp5-82][^cite_note-83]

On 5 May 2014, the [Belarusian Republican Youth Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_Republican_Youth_Union) encouraged activists not to use the ribbon due to the situation in Ukraine.[^cite_note-84] In time for Victory Day 2015, the ribbon's colors were replaced there by the red, green and white from the [Flag of Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Belarus).[^cite_note-85]
