**Chatsworth** is a suburban neighborhood in [Los Angeles, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles,_California), in the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley). The area around the town was home to Native Americans, who left caves containing [rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art). Chatsworth was discovered and settled by the [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) beginning in the [18th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/18th_century_in_the_United_States). The land was part of a Spanish land grant, [Rancho Ex-Mission San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Ex-Mission_San_Fernando). After the United States purchased the land following the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War), it was the largest such grant in [California](5407.md). Chatsworth has seven public and eight private schools. There are large open-space and smaller recreational parks as well as a public library and a transportation center. Landmarks in the town include the former Chatsworth Reservoir and the Santa Susana Field Laboratory. The neighborhood has one of the lowest population densities in Los Angeles and a relatively high income level. Chatsworth is the home of the [Iverson Movie Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iverson_Movie_Ranch), a 500-acre (202-hectare) area which was the most filmed movie ranch in history, as more than 2,000 productions used it as a filming location.

## Population

The 2000 U.S. census counted 35,073 residents in the 15.2 square miles (39 km<sup>2</sup>) Chatsworth neighborhood, or 2,301 people per square mile, among the lowest [population densities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) for both the city and [the county](79734.md). In 2008, the city estimated that the population had increased to 37,102. In 2000, the median age for residents was 40, considered old for city and county neighborhoods; the percentages of residents aged 35 and older were among the county's highest.[^cite_note-MappingLAChatsworth-2]

The neighborhood was considered to be ethnically "moderately diverse" for both the city of Los Angeles and its county, with a relatively high percentage of whites and Asian people, and a sizable Hispanic/Latino community. The population was 65.7% [Non-Hispanic White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_White), 14.4% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_American), 13.5% [Hispanic or Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans), 2.2% [Black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_American), and 4.2% of other origins. Among the 25.2% of residents born abroad - a low figure for Los Angeles - [Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Korea) (10.4%) and the [Philippines](23440.md) (9.3%) were the most common places of birth.[^cite_note-MappingLAChatsworth-2]

The median yearly household income in 2008 dollars was \$84,456, considered to be a high income for the city. The percentages of families that earned more than \$40,000 was considered high for the county. Renters occupied 28.9% of the housing stock, and house- or apartment-owners held 71.1%. The average household size of 2.6 people was considered average for Los Angeles.[^cite_note-MappingLAChatsworth-2]

In 2000, there were 2,933 military veterans, or 10.8% of the population, a high percentage compared to the rest of the city. The percentage of married people was among the county's highest. The rate of 10% of families headed by single parents was low for the city.[^cite_note-MappingLAChatsworth-2]

## Geography

Chatsworth is flanked by the [Santa Susana Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Mountains) on the north, [Porter Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter_Ranch,_Los_Angeles) and [Northridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northridge,_Los_Angeles) on the east, [Winnetka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winnetka,_Los_Angeles), [Canoga Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canoga_Park,_Los_Angeles), [West Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Hills,_Los_Angeles) on the south, the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills), and unincorporated Los Angeles County and [Ventura County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County,_California) on the west, and Twin Lakes, a community founded by San Francisco's George Haight in the early 20th century and unincorporated Los Angeles County which includes a 1,600-acre park with equestrian trails, to the north.[^cite_note-MappingLAColoredMap-3][^cite_note-4]

## History

### Indigenous

Chatsworth was inhabited by the [Tongva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongva_people), [Chumash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumash_\(tribe\)), and [Tataviam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tataviam_people). They inhabited the valley for an estimated 8,000 years. [Stoney Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoney_Point_\(California\)) is the site of the [Tongva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongva_people) village of *[Momonga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momonga_\(village\))*, which was also a trading place with the neighboring [Tataviam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tataviam_people) and [Chumash people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chumash_people).[^cite_note-\:22-6] The nearby [Burro Flats Painted Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burro_Flats_Painted_Cave) remains a legacy of the Chumash culture's [rock art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art) and [solstice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solstice) ceremony spirituality.[^cite_note-Lubas-7]

### Spain and Mexico

The first European explorers came into the Chatsworth area on August 5, 1769, led by the Spanish military leader [Gaspar de Portolà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspar_de_Portol%C3%A0). With its establishment in 1797 and subsequent Spanish Land Grant by the King of Spain, [Mission San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Fernando_Rey_de_Espa%C3%B1a) (Mission San Fernando Rey de España) gained dominion over the San Fernando Valley's lands, including future Chatsworth.



[Old Santa Susana Stage Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Santa_Susana_Stage_Road) trail up scenic [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills) in Chatsworth

The Native American trail that had existed from the Tongva-Tatavium village called rancheria Santa Susana (Chatsworth) to another village, replaced by [Mission San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Fernando_Rey_de_Espa%C3%B1a), became the route for missionaries and other Spanish travel up and down California. It was part of the *El Camino del Santa Susana y Simi* trail that connected the Valley's Mission, Los Angeles pueblo (town), and the southern missions with the [Mission San Buenaventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Buenaventura), the [Presidio of Monterey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidio_of_Monterey,_California), and the northward missions. The trail crossed over the [Santa Susana Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Pass) to the [Simi Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Valley), through present day city park Chatsworth Park South and the [Santa Susana Pass State Historic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Pass_State_Historic_Park). In 1795, the Spanish land grant had been issued for [Rancho Simi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Simi), reconfirmed in 1842 by the Mexican governor.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] Its lands included part of current Chatsworth, westward from Andora Avenue.

In 1821, after the [Mexican War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_War_of_Independence) from [Spain](26667.md), the Mission San Fernando became part of [Alta California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California), Mexico. In 1834, the Mexican government began redistributing the mission lands. In 1846, the Mexican land grant for [Rancho Ex-Mission San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Ex-Mission_San_Fernando) was issued by Governor [Pío Pico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%ADo_Pico). It was bounded on the north by [Rancho San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_San_Francisco) and the [Santa Susana Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Mountains), on the west by the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills), on the east by [Rancho Tujunga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Tujunga), and on the south by the Montañas de Portesuelo ([Santa Monica Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Mountains)). The Rancho Ex-Mission San Fernando received a Federal land patent to retain ownership by the United States [Public Land Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Land_Commission) in 1873 and was the single largest land grant in California.[^cite_note-11]

### Chatsworth

&#x20;The Chatsworth Historical Society was formed in 1963 in an effort to successfully save the historic Pioneer Church from demolition. The society and Chatsworth Museum are located in Chatsworth Park South at Los Angeles Cultural Historical Monument No. 133, the Homestead Acre . In addition to preserving Chatsworth history and educating the public, the society acts as conservator of the Homestead Acre.[^cite_note-12]



The center of Chatsworth, 1911, on what is now Topanga Canyon Boulevard

In 1869, the grantee's son, Eulogio F. de Celis, returned from Spain to Los Angeles.[^cite_note-13] In 1874, the family sold their northern half of [Rancho Ex-Mission San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Ex-Mission_San_Fernando) to northern Californians, [California State Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Senate) [Charles Maclay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Maclay) and his partners George K. Porter, a San Francisco shoe manufacturer, and his cousin Benjamin F. Porter. The Porters' land was west of present-day [Sepulveda Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepulveda_Boulevard) including most of Chatsworth, and the Maclay land was east of Sepulveda Boulevard.[^cite_note-14]

The [Old Santa Susana Stage Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Santa_Susana_Stage_Road) or Santa Susana Wagon Road continued in use as an alternative to the route along El Camino Viejo from 1861 to 1875, replacing the older road as the main route between Los Angeles and San Francisco. The downgrade from the Santa Susana Pass into Chatsworth was known as "The Devil's Slide". A new wagon route bypassing the deteriorating Devil's Slide was opened in 1895. Initially called *El Camino Nuevo* (the New Road), it was later named the Chatsworth Grade Road, which continued in use until Santa Susana Pass Road (now Old Santa Susana Pass Road) was built in 1917. In 1876, the [Southern Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Pacific_Railroad) opened a tunnel through the [Newhall Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newhall_Pass), enabling rail connections from Los Angeles north to San Francisco, and rail travel soon replaced travel by stagecoach between Los Angeles and San Francisco. From this time, the stagecoach traffic to Santa Barbara once again used the coast route, and the Santa Susana Pass road was relegated to local traffic.[^cite_note-SSP-15] Construction of the 118 Ronald Reagan Freeway through the Santa Susana Pass began in 1968 with major traffic now bypassing Santa Susana Pass Road.[^cite_note-16]

The Chatsworth name, which hails from the stately [Chatsworth House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatsworth_House) in [Derbyshire](71070.md), England, was first recorded in 1888, when George R. Crow filed with the Los Angeles County Recorder's Office a map of the San Fernando Valley with a subdivision he called "Chatsworth Park". William Booth Barber, the president of the San Fernando Valley Improvement Company, was born in England 17 miles from the Duke of Devonshire's palatial estate, Chatsworth House. In 1893 W\.B. Barber filed an additional map with the Recorder's Office called the "Plat of Chatsworth Park Townsite".[^cite_note-17]

## Government and infrastructure

The Chatsworth Branch Library, operated by the [Los Angeles Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Public_Library), is located at 21052 [Devonshire Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonshire_Street_\(Los_Angeles\)).[^cite_note-18] It was rebuilt in a modern style in 2002.[^cite_note-19]

The Chatsworth Post Office, of the [United States Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postal_Service), is located at 21606 [Devonshire Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonshire_Street_\(Los_Angeles\)).[^cite_note-20]

The [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Census_Bureau) operates the Los Angeles Regional Census Center in Chatsworth.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22]

### Transportation

The [Chatsworth Transportation Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatsworth_station), a major transportation hub for the town and west valley, is served by 20 daily trains on the [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)) [Ventura County Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura_County_Line) from [Ventura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ventura,_California) to [Union Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Station_\(Los_Angeles\)) in [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles). [Amtrak California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_California) *[Pacific Surfliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Surfliner)* has 10 trains daily traveling through here[^cite_note-23] on the [Coast Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Line_\(Union_Pacific_Railroad\)) which also handles freight transport by [Union Pacific Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Pacific_Railroad) and was involved in the [2008 Chatsworth train collision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Chatsworth_train_collision) near here.

The [Orange Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)) of the [Los Angeles Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Metropolitan_Transportation_Authority) system was extended to Chatsworth Station in 2012, with a dedicated right-of-way across the Valley to [North Hollywood Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Hollywood_\(Los_Angeles_Metro_station\)) of the [Red Line subway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)).[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

The Chatsworth Transportation Center also offers connections to several Los Angeles Metro, [Simi Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_small_Southern_California_transit_agencies#Simi_Valley_Transit), and [Santa Clarita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_Santa_Clarita_Transit) bus routes. It is located at 10046 Old Depot Plaza Road, between [Devonshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devonshire_Street_\(Los_Angeles\)) and Lassen streets.[^cite_note-27]

Chatsworth is serviced by the [118](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Reagan_Freeway) freeway in the northern end of the neighborhood.

### Chatsworth Reservoir



Chatsworth Reservoir, 1921

A distinctive feature in Chatsworth is the Chatsworth Dam and reservoir. Built in 1918 as part of the [Los Angeles Aqueduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Aqueduct) system, the property belongs to the [Los Angeles Department of Water and Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Department_of_Water_and_Power). Due to increasing concerns of the water quality because of algae plumes and storm water inflow, it was taken out of service in August 1969. After the [1971 San Fernando earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_San_Fernando_earthquake) additional concerns for seismic safety led to its abandonment by the LADWP as a storage facility.[^cite_note-49] Chatsworth Oaks Park and the [Chatsworth Nature Preserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chatsworth_Nature_Preserve) are located here giving views of migrating birds and other animals, and their sounds such as the coyotes calling in the evening. The views of the rocky and dramatic [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills) surround the open space.[^cite_note-50]

Chatsworth Reservoir is classified by the *Los Angeles Times* as a city neighborhood, but "because there are relatively few homes in this area," the *Times* does not provide separate statistics for it, but adds them to Chatsworth.[^cite_note-MappingLAChatsworthReservoir-51] The Devonshire and Topanga stations of the Los Angeles Police Department provide services in the area.[^cite_note-MappingLAChatsworthCrime-52]

## Economy

Chatsworth is the home of the [National Notary Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Notary_Association) and the headquarters for [Premier America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_America) (formerly Litton Federal Credit Union) and Matadors Community Credit Union.

The [Santa Susana Field Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Susana_Field_Laboratory) (SSFL), once busy with [Rocketdyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pratt_&_Whitney_Rocketdyne) testing rocket engines and [Atomics International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomics_International) doing nuclear research, is high in the [Simi Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simi_Hills) west of Chatsworth.[^cite_note-53] It has been closed and will undergo an extensive environmental cleanup, andsoon become an open-space park.[^cite_note-54] The park will permanently add to Chatsworth's scenic backdrop, [greenway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenway_\(landscape\)), and [hiking](147313.md) opportunities.[^cite_note-55]

Some of the current large employers based in Chatsworth are [Capstone Turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capstone_Turbine), [Natel Engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natel_Engineering), and [Hydraulics International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulics_International,_INC.). [Lamps Plus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamps_Plus) has production and distribution facilities in Chatsworth.

[Titus Software](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titus_Software)'s United States subsidiary once had its headquarters in Chatsworth.[^cite_note-Contact2004-56]

Seltzer Motor Industries, of Chatsworth, in 1979 produced the [Willow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willow_Sports_Car), a two-seat sports car kit. It is the first transverse, inline four-cylinder, mid-engined kit car ever offered to the public.[^cite_note-57]
