The **National Police** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Police nationale*, pronounced [\[pɔlis nɑsjɔnal\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)), formerly known as the *[Sûreté nationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BBret%C3%A9)*, is one of two national [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police) forces of [France](5843419.md), the other being the [National Gendarmerie](6330819.md). The National Police is the country's main civil [law enforcement agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_agency), with primary jurisdiction in cities and large towns. By contrast, the National Gendarmerie has primary jurisdiction in smaller towns, as well as in rural and border areas. The National Police comes under the jurisdiction of the [Ministry of the Interior](871703.md) and has about 145,200 employees (as of 2015). Young French citizens can fulfill their optional [national service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_conscription) (*[Service national universel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_national_universel)*) in the national police force.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

The National Police operates mostly in cities and large towns. In that context, it conducts security operations such as [patrols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrol), traffic control and identity checks. Under the orders and supervision of [investigating magistrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Investigating_magistrate) of the judiciary, it conducts criminal inquiries and serves search warrants. It also maintains specific services ('judicial police') for these inquiries.[^cite_note-ESD-6]

## History

The national police force was created on 14 August 1941, under the [Vichy regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vichy_France), by a decree signed by the head of government, [Philippe Pétain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_P%C3%A9tain). This decree implements the law of 23 April 1941, creating the Police nationale: the forces of the Sûreté nationale (with the former services of the Sûreté générale, which became the Sûreté nationale in 1934, and the municipal police units, which became "étatisées" for the police forces of towns with more than 10,000 inhabitants) and the police services of the Préfecture de police in Paris are thus united. It was dissolved after the [Liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Paris), by order of the Provisional Government of the French Republic on 16 November 1944. It was revived by Law no. 66-492 of 9 July 1966, on the organization of the police in France. This law unified the Sûreté Nationale and the Préfecture de Police.[^cite_note-ESD-6]

## Organization

The National Police is commanded by the Director-General (*directeur général de la police nationale*), who is currently [Louis Laugier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Laugier). The Director-General is personally in command of the General Directorate of the National Police ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Direction Générale de la Police nationale*) (DGPN) and responsible to the Minister of the Interior.[^cite_note-7]

The Prefect of Police manages the *[Préfecture de Police de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Police)* that includes all police and security services in [Paris](22989.md), the three neighbouring departments of the *'la petite couronne'* region ([Hauts-de-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauts-de-Seine), [Seine-Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seine-Saint-Denis) and [Val-de-Marne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Val-de-Marne)), and the airports of [Roissy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_de_Gaulle_Airport), [Orly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orly_Airport) and [Le Bourget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris%E2%80%93Le_Bourget_Airport). The Paris Police Prefecture is a separate law enforcement jurisdiction. While its officers belong to the National Police, their chief (the Police Prefect) acts completely independently from the Director-General of the National Police, reporting directly to the French Minister of the Interior. The elevated status of the Paris Police Prefect is also underlined by the fact that he/she is also head of the [Île-de-France](1716427.md) Defence and Security Zone (*Zone de défense et de sécurité Île-de-France*). The police forces in the other departments of the [Île-de-France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%8Ele-de-France_\(region\)) region are under the direct command of a *[Préfet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pr%C3%A9fet_de_d%C3%A9partement)* (Department Prefect), being himself under the supervision of the *Préfet de Police* as far as the active on-the-field police work is concerned, and under the control of the Director-General for the rest.[^cite_note-8]

The National Police is subdivided into (central) directorates, which are further composed of sub-directorates:

- *Direction centrale du recrutement et de la formation de la Police nationale* (Central Directorate for Recruitment and Training of the National Police; DCRFPN):

  [^cite_note-9]

  &#x20;formed from the fusion of the former *Direction de la formation de police nationale* (Directorate of Training of the National Police; DFPN) and *Direction de l'administration de la police nationale* (Directorate of Administration of the National Police; DAPN). It was established on 1 September 2010 and employs approximately 3 000 people.

  [^cite_note-10]

- *Direction Nationale de la Police judiciaire* (National Directorate of the Judicial Police; DNPJ): charged with all criminal investigations under direction of magistrates. This mission is fulfilled in the Paris area by the *[Direction Régionale de Police Judiciaire de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direction_R%C3%A9gionale_de_Police_Judiciaire_de_Paris)* which is nicknamed after its address "*36 Quai des Orfèvres*" (often without the number), and is a metonym for the Police generally; the national headquarters of the *PJ*, as it is usually called in French, are actually located at 11 rue des Saussaies, within the Ministry of the Interior). The main Sub-Directorates of the Judicial Police are:

  [^cite_note-Sub_directorates-11]

  - *L’office anti-stupéfiants* "OFAST" (Anti-Narcotics Office): Responsible investigating drug trafficking networks in the country and coordinating national drug control efforts with other agencies such as police and customs.

  - *Sous-direction de lutte contre la criminalité organisée et de la délinquance spécialisée* "SDLCODS" (Sub-Directorate for Combating Organized Crime and Specialized Delinquency). Includes all the National Investigation Offices specializing in Organized and Financial Crime, except for the National Itinerant Criminality Struggle Office (which falls under the *Gendarmerie Nationale*):&#x20;

    - *L’office central de lutte contre le crime organisé* "OCLCO" (Central Office for Combating Organized Crime): Specializes in investigating armed [banditry](658200.md) ([kidnapping](169376.md), [murder](20171.md), [robbery](54849.md) and [rape](7980471.md)) by [organized criminal groups](22625.md).

    - *L’office central pour la répression de la traite des êtres humains* "OCRTEH" (Central Office for the Repression of Human Trafficking): Combats sexual exploitation crimes like [sex trafficking](2101268.md) and [pimping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procuring_\(prostitution\)).

    - *L’office central pour la répression du faux monnayage* "OCRFM" (Central Office for the Suppression of Counterfeiting): Investigates [counterfeiting of currency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterfeit_money) and industrial goods (tobacco, branded products).

    - *L’office central de lutte contre le trafic de biens culturels* "OCBC" (Central Office for Combating Trafficking in Cultural Goods): Specializes in investigating the theft and trafficking of art and artifacts from foreign archaeological sites.

    - *L’office central pour la répression des violences aux personnes* "OCRVP" (Central Office for the Repression of Violence Against Persons): Handles particularly complex cases involving crimes against persons including cold cases, [hate crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hate_crimes), [sectarian violence](648554.md), and crimes committed against French nationals abroad.

    - *L’office mineurs* "OFMIN" (Office for Minors): Specializes in combating violence against children including school harassment, [child pornography](25211885.md), and family [sexual violence](731637.md).

  - *Sous-direction de lutte contre la criminalité financière* "SDLCF" (Sub-Directorate for Combating Financial Crime): Consists of two sub-directorates:&#x20;

    - *L’office central pour la répression de la grande délinquance financière* "OCRGDF" (Central Office for the Repression of Major Financial Crime): Specializes in investigating major [money laundering](19390.md) cases.

    - *L’office central de lutte contre la corruption et les infractions financières et fiscales* "OCLCIFF" (Central Office for Combating Corruption and Financial and Tax Offenses)

  - *Sous-direction anti-terroriste* "SDAT" (Anti-Terrorist Sub-Directorate): elite counter-terrorist task-force.

  - *Sous-Direction de la police technique et scientifique* "SDPTS" (Sub-directorate of forensics and crime scene investigation)

  - *L’office anti-cybercriminalité* "OFAC" (The Office for the Fight Against Cybercrime): Investigates major instances of cyberattacks against computer systems and illegal activities on the dark web.

  - *Le service central des courses et jeux* "SCCJ" (Central Racing and Gaming Service): Responsible for enforcing national laws on gambling and handles litigation related to racing and gaming.

  - *La sous-direction de la stratégie et du pilotage territorial* (Sub-directorate for Strategy and Territorial Management): The newest sub-directorate created on July 1, 2023 whose objective is to promote and implement the policies created by the central and territorial judicial police services, with the goal of improving inter-agency cooperation, coordinating victim support services, and conduct criminological research.

    [^cite_note-Sub_directorates-11]

- *Direction nationale de la sécurité publique* (National Directorate of Public Security; DNSP): Patrol and response duties, misdemeanour investigations, emergency help; this Directorate comprises approximately 80% of the workforce. The DNSP is the National Police's equivalent of the [Departmental Gendarmerie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departmental_Gendarmerie).

## Requirements

Admission requirements for the competition:[^cite_note-23]

- 35 years old maximum on January 1st of the competition year

* Bachelor's degree or equivalent

- [French nationality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_nationality_law)

* Clean [criminal record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_record)

- Good physical condition, good moral standing

The entrance exam has 3 stages:

To pass the entrance exam, a candidate must pass several tests: the eligibility test, the pre-admission test and the admission test.&#x20;

The eligibility test consists of written exams (general knowledge, solving a practical case, law and criminal procedure tests). If this test is passed, you must then take the pre-admission tests (physical exercise tests), and if this stage is completed, the competition will end with the admission test (psychotechnical tests, interview with the jury).&#x20;

Once accepted, the paid training for police officers at the police academy lasts 18 months.

### Weapons

Intervention in 2020 of a police team in [Courbevoie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courbevoie). Policemen are equipped with [tasers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasers).

Prior to the Second World War and the formation of the Police Nationale, the French police used a variety of side arms, both revolvers and semi-automatic pistols, notably comprising the [MAS 1873](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAS_1873_revolver), the [MAS 1892](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mod%C3%A8le_1892_revolver), the [FN M1900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN_M1900), [Ruby pistols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_pistol), and a variety of privately purchased weapons.

Immediately after the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), a variety of military side arms were issued, often captured weapons provided by the Army or French-produced German-designed weapons, such as the [Mauser HSc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauser_HSc) or the [Walther P38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walther_P38) for sidearms, and the [Karabiner 98k](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabiner_98k) rifle, to the now unified national force.

In 1951, a standardisation was performed on the RR 51 pistol[^cite_note-24] in [7.65×17mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7.65%C3%9717mm) and on the [MAS-38](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAS-38) and [MAT-49](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAT-49) for submachine guns. From 1953, in the context of heightening violence of the [Algeria War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria_War), CRS units were upgraded to the [9×19mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9%C3%9719mm) [MAC Mle 1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MAC_Mle_1950).

In the early 1960s, large-caliber revolvers were introduced, culminating with the introduction of the [Manurhin MR 73](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manurhin_MR_73) and the [Ruger SP101](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruger_SP101). In the 80s, a process to standardize revolvers was initiated. The 1970s also saw the introduction of automatic rifles and carbines (such as the [SIG SG 543](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIG_SG_543)) to fend off heavily armed organised crime and terrorism.

In the 2000s, the police started switching to semi-automatic pistols and to the [9×19mm Parabellum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9%C3%9719mm_Parabellum) cartridge. For some years, the standard sidearm in the National Police and the [Gendarmerie Nationale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gendarmerie_Nationale_\(France\)) was the [PAMAS G1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beretta_92), which was French licensed and made. In 2003 both agencies made the biggest small arms contract since the Second World War[^cite_note-Ayoob-25] for about 250,000 [SIG Sauer Pro SP 2022s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIG_Pro), a custom-tailored variant of the SIG Pro, replacing the PAMAS-G1 and several other pistols in service. The weapons are planned to stay in service until the year 2022, hence the weapon name. The police purchased more pistols in late 2018 possibly indicating they intend them to be used beyond 2022.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

For greater threats the police use slightly modified [Ruger Mini-14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruger_Mini-14) [semi-automatic rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-automatic_rifles) purchased in the 1970s.[^cite_note-28] More modern long guns like the [Remington 870](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remington_870) [shotgun](26838.md), [H\&K UMP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_UMP) [submachine gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Submachine_gun) and the [FAMAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAMAS) & [H\&K G36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_G36) [select fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Select_fire) [assault rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assault_rifles) are also issued to certain tactical units.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] Some of the elite Gendarme and National Police units like RAID and SDAT have adopted [short-barreled rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short-barreled_rifle) (SBR) versions of the [French Army](611740.md)'s newest assault rifle, the [HK416F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heckler_&_Koch_HK416).[^cite_note-31]

## Injured and killed

### In the line of duty

According to data from the Ministry of the Interior released in 2021, the number of police officers killed in the line of duty has significantly decreased since the 1980s. Indeed, 36 police officers died while on duty in the 2010s, compared with 88 in the 1980s[^cite_note-34].

### Deaths caused by the French National Police

In France, the [Inspectorate General of the National Police](https://fr.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspection%20g%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale%20de%20la%20Police%20nationale) has recorded individuals killed or injured during police operations since 2018[^cite_note-35].

The collective "Désarmons-les" maintains a list of people killed by law enforcement since 2012. They record 437 deaths.[^cite_note-36] Most of the victims are [racialized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racialization), particularly Black and Arab[^cite_note-37], which some analysts perceive as institutionalized [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism)[^cite_note-38]. The number of deaths is increasing, with 2024 being a particularly deadly year[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]. This phenomenon is exacerbated by police impunity.[^cite_note-41]
