The **Quds Force** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): نیروی قدس, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *niru-ye qods*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'The Jerusalem Force') is one of five [branches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch) of the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC) of Iran.[^cite_note-3] Considered an elite unit within the IRGC,[^cite_note-4] it specializes in [unconventional warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unconventional_warfare) and [military intelligence](146678.md) operations. U.S. Army General [Stanley McChrystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_A._McChrystal) describes the Quds Force as being analogous to a combination of the [CIA](5183633.md) and the [Joint Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Special_Operations_Command) (JSOC).[^cite_note-5]

Responsible for [extraterritorial operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterritorial_operations),[^cite_note-army-6] the Quds Force has been described as the IRGC’s *de facto* external affairs branch.[^cite_note-7] It supports [non-state actors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violent_non-state_actor) in many countries, including [Hezbollah](13919.md), [Hamas](13913.md), [Palestinian Islamic Jihad](13093253.md), the [Houthis](17240332.md), and [Shia](26961.md) militias in [Iraq](7515928.md), [Syria](7515849.md), and [Afghanistan](737.md);[^cite_note-army-6] it has also deployed personnel in foreign conflicts such as the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) and the [Iraqi offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Tikrit) against the [Islamic State](9087364.md).[^cite_note-8]

The Quds Force reports directly to the [supreme leader of Iran](36814344.md), currently [Mojtaba Khamenei](23324096.md) since 8 March 2026.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDionisi20067-9][^cite_note-10] After Quds Force commander [Qasem Soleimani](16805217.md) was [assassinated in a U.S. drone strike](62726668.md) in January 2020, his deputy, [Esmail Qaani](62727035.md), replaced him.[^cite_note-11]

In 2019, the U.S. secretary of state designated the Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps and Quds Force as a [Foreign Terrorist Organization](421835.md) (FTO) based on the IRGC's "continued support to and [engagement in terrorist activity around the world](80345339.md)."; this was the first time that the U.S. designated a government entity as an FTO.[^cite_note-12]

While the formation's official name is Quds Force (lit. 'Jerusalem Force'), it has also been referred to as the 'Quds Corps' ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): سپاه قدس, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Persian): *sepah-e qods*) in Persian media.[^cite_note-13] In Arabic, [Jerusalem](16043.md) is most commonly known as القُدس *al-Quds*, meaning "The Holy" or "The Holy Sanctuary", cognate with [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language): הקדש, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Hebrew): *Ha-Qodesh*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'The Holy'.

It was originally titled Corps but changed to Force by Khamenei.[^cite_note-14]

## History and mission

The predecessor of the Quds Force, known as 'Department 900', was created during the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) as a special intelligence unit, while the IRGC was allegedly active abroad in Afghanistan before the war.[^cite_note-Uskowi-15] The department was later merged into 'Special External Operations Department'.[^cite_note-Uskowi-15] After the Iran–Iraq War ended in 1988, the IRGC was reorganized and the Quds Force was established as an independent [service branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_branch).[^cite_note-Uskowi-15] It has the mission of liberating "Muslim land", especially *[al-Quds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Quds)*, from which it takes its name—"Jerusalem Force" in English.[^cite_note-16]

Both during and after the war, it provided support to the [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurd) fighting [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein). In 1982, a Quds unit was deployed to [Lebanon](17771.md), where it assisted in the genesis of [Hezbollah](13919.md).[^cite_note-17] The Force also expanded its operations into neighboring Afghanistan, including assistance for [Abdul Ali Mazari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Ali_Mazari)'s Shi'a [Hezbe Wahdat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbe_Wahdat) in the 1980s against the government of [Mohammad Najibullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Najibullah). It then began funding and supporting [Ahmad Shah Massoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Massoud)'s [Northern Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Alliance) against the [Taliban](30635.md).[^cite_note-18] However, in recent years, the Quds Force is alleged to have been helping and guiding the [Taliban insurgents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_insurgency) against the NATO-backed [Karzai administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Hamid_Karzai).[^cite_note-telegraph-19][^cite_note-PAN-20][^cite_note-CNN-21][^cite_note-Concern_in_US_over_increasing_Iranian_activity_in_Afghanistan-22][^cite_note-rferl-23][^cite_note-Afghans_find_tons_of_explosive_devices_transferred_from_Iran-24][^cite_note-jamestown.org-25] There were also reports of the unit lending support to [Bosnian Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_Muslims) fighting the [Bosnian Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_Serbs) during the [Bosnian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnian_War).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

According to the Egyptian newspaper *[Al-Ahram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Ahram)*, former Iranian President [Mahmoud Ahmadinejad](7907317.md) helped fund the Quds Force while he was stationed at the Ramazan garrison near Iraq, during the late 1980s.[^cite_note-29]

In January 2010, according to the [Washington Institute for Near East Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Washington_Institute_for_Near_East_Policy), the mission of the Quds Force was expanded and the Force along with Hezbollah started a new campaign of attacks targeting not only the US and Israel but also other Western bodies.[^cite_note-30]

In January 2020, Quds Force commander Major General [Qasem Soleimani](16805217.md) was killed by a [US airstrike on his convoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Baghdad_International_Airport_airstrike) outside [Baghdad International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad_International_Airport).[^cite_note-31]

The Quds Force is run from Tehran and has ties with armed groups in Afghanistan, Iraq, Lebanon, Syria and the Palestinian territories.[^cite_note-secretive-32]

### Predecessors

#### Liberation Movements Unit

The LMU of the IRGC was established in 1981 by [Mohammad Montazeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Montazeri), son of [Grand Ayatollah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ayatollah) [Hussein-Ali Montazeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussein-Ali_Montazeri), and [Mehdi Hashemi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehdi_Hashemi), then a member of the IRGC Command Council and brother of Ayatollah Montazeri’s son-in-law.[^cite_note-Alf2013-33] This unit was tasked with providing military assistance to "Islamic liberation movements" abroad, especially in [Shia](26961.md)-majority countries ruled by [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) minorities, including [Bahrain](18933277.md), [Iraq](7515928.md) and Lebanon. Both Montazeri and Hashemi had themselves received [irregular warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irregular_warfare) training in [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization)-run training camps in Southern Lebanon before the [1979 Iranian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_Iranian_Revolution).[^cite_note-auto-34]

#### 9th Badr Brigade of the IRGC

During the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), Major-General [Mohsen Rezaee](1862765.md), then the Commander-in-Chief of the Revolutionary Guards, ordered the formation of the [9th Badr Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Badr_Organization), which consisted of Iraqi Shia fugitives who had fled from Saddam Hussein's persecution and were fighting for Iran. The Badr Brigade was manned by Iraqis but led by Iranian officers. Among the Badr Brigade's earliest Iraqi members were [Abu Mahdi al-Muhandis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Mahdi_al-Muhandis), Deputy Chief of the [Popular Mobilization Forces](45671374.md) who was assassinated together with Soleimani in January 2020, and Brigadier-General [Hadi al-Amiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadi_al-Amiri), later Interior Minister of Iraq. Muhandis and Amiri took part in the 1986 Iranian [Siege of Basra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Basra) under the command of IRGC General [Hassan Danaeifar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Danaeifar). After the fall of Saddam Hussein, Danaeifar became the Iranian ambassador in Iraq between 2006 and 2010. Muhandis had fled Iraq to Kuwait in the early 1980s and allegedly collaborated with Lebanese Hezbollah's Chief of Military Operations [Imad Mughniyeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imad_Mughniyeh) in [bombing the US embassy in Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%931988_Kuwait_terror_attacks) in 1983, after which he fled to Iran.[^cite_note-35]

#### Ramadan Headquarters

In 1986 the Ramadan Headquarters of External Operations was created within the IRGC. This headquarters was responsible for Iran's links to Iraqi Kurdish groups, including the forces of the [Kurdistan Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Democratic_Party) and the [Patriotic Union of Kurdistan](70789.md), led by [Massoud Barzani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massoud_Barzani) and [Jalal Talabani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalal_Talabani). One of the Ramadan Headquarters' senior commanders and its chief of staff in the 1980s was the IRGC Brigadier-General [Iraj Masjedi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraj_Masjedi), who from 2017 to 2022 served as Iran’s ambassador to Iraq. At the time, another commander of the Ramadan Headquarters was the IRGC Brigadier-General [Mohammad Reza Naqdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Reza_Naqdi), who was later appointed as commander of the [Basij](2892082.md) militia.[^cite_note-36]

#### Lebanon Corps

The "Lebanon Corps" of the IRGC was established in June 1982 when Iran sent 1,500 Revolutionary Guard commandos to the [Syrian](7515849.md)-controlled [Beqaa Valley](2162385.md) of Eastern Lebanon to fight against [Israel's invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_Lebanon_War). This force was led by IRGC Brigadier-General [Hossein Dehghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hossein_Dehghan) (later Defense Minister) and was tasked with training members of Hezbollah. Two other people who were influential in guiding and communicating with Hezbollah after Dehghan were IRGC Brigadier-General [Ahmad Vahidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Vahidi), the IRGC’s intelligence chief at the time and later the first commander of the Quds Force from its establishment in 1988 to 1998, and Fereydoun Vardinejad, later the Political Deputy of the Office of Iranian President [Hassan Rouhani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hassan_Rouhani). Both Vahidi and Vardinejad were tasked in 1985 by [Ali Akbar Hashemi Rafsanjani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Akbar_Hashemi_Rafsanjani) and the IRGC to negotiate with [Robert McFarlane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_McFarlane_\(American_government_official\)), U.S. President [Ronald Reagan](25433.md)'s special envoy to Iran, on the issue of the [Lebanon hostage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanon_hostage_crisis).[^cite_note-37]

## Organization

The force is described as "active in dozens of countries."[^cite_note-wright-38] According to former [U.S. Army intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Military_Intelligence) officer David Dionisi, the Quds Force is organized into eight different directorates based on geographic location:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDionisi20068-39]

- [Western countries](21208200.md) (excluding Turkey, including the former [Eastern Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Bloc))

- [Former](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states) [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union)

- Iraq

- Afghanistan, Pakistan, and India

- Israel, Lebanon, and Jordan

- Turkey

- North Africa

- [Arabian Peninsula](47858.md)

According to journalist [Dexter Filkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dexter_Filkins), the force's members are "divided between combatants and those who train and oversee foreign assets," and the force is divided into branches focusing on "intelligence, finance, foreign languages, politics, sabotage, and special operations." Members are chosen both for their skill and "allegiance to the doctrine of the Islamic Revolution."[^cite_note-filkins1-40]

In *American Hiroshima:* *The Reasons Why and a Call to Strengthen America's Democracy,* Dionisi asserts that the Iranian Quds Force headquarters for operations in Iraq was moved in 2004 to the Iran–Iraq border in order to better supervise activities in Iraq. The Quds Force also operates a base in the former compound of the U.S. embassy, which [was overrun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_hostage_crisis) in 1979.[^cite_note-filkins1-40]

According to Filkins and American General [Stanley A. McChrystal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_A._McChrystal), it was the Quds Force that "flooded" Iraq with "[explosively formed projectiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosively_formed_projectile)" which fire a molten copper slug able to penetrate armor, and which accounted for "nearly 20%" of American combat deaths in Iraq (i.e. hundreds of soldiers).[^cite_note-filkins1-40] In September 2007, a few years after the publication of *American Hiroshima*, General [David Petraeus](3223944.md) reported to Congress that the Quds Force had left Iraq. Petraeus said, "The Quds Force itself, we believe, by and large, those individuals have been pulled out of the country, as have the Lebanese Hezbollah trainers that were being used to augment that activity."[^cite_note-41]

On 7 July 2008, journalist [Seymour Hersh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seymour_Hersh) wrote an article in *[The New Yorker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Yorker)* revealing that President Bush had signed a [Presidential Finding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Finding) authorizing the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA) and [Joint Special Operations Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Special_Operations_Command) to conduct cross-border paramilitary operations from Iraq and Afghanistan into Iran. These operations would be against the Quds Force and "high-value targets."[^cite_note-newyorker.com-42] "The Finding was focused on undermining Iran's nuclear ambitions and trying to undermine the government through regime change," a person familiar with its contents said, and involved "working with opposition groups."[^cite_note-newyorker.com-42]

### Size

The size of the Quds Force is [classified](252857.md) and unknown. In 2007, Mahan Abedin of Center for the Study of Terrorism said that Quds Force numbers no more than 2,000 people, with 800 core operatives.[^cite_note-LA_Times-51][^cite_note-52] [Scott Shane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Shane), who interviewed several American scholars later that year, wrote that estimates range from 3,000 to 50,000.[^cite_note-53] In 2013, Dexter Filkins wrote that the Quds Force has 10,000–20,000 members, "divided between combatants and those who train and oversee foreign assets".[^cite_note-filkins1-40] The 2020 edition of *The Military Balance*, published by the [International Institute of Strategic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_of_Strategic_Studies) (IISS), estimated that the force has about 5,000 personnel.[^cite_note-IISS2020-54]

In 2020, Jack Watling of the [Royal United Services Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_United_Services_Institute) estimated the Quds Force had the "[divisional strength military formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Division_\(military\))" of approximately 17,000 to 21,000 members, split regionally.[^cite_note-secretive-32]

### Financing

Companies controlled by the Quds Force maintain banking relationships with the Bank of Kunlun, a subsidiary of the [China National Petroleum Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_National_Petroleum_Corporation).[^cite_note-55]

The Quds Force has been involved in lucrative drug smuggling operations between Afghanistan and Iran, leveraging connections with South American drug cartels.[^cite_note-56] It has also been involved in [smuggling to Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_smuggling_to_Lebanon).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

The Quds Force Corps budget was leaked in a hack in February 2024 to be \$7,700 billion toman.[^cite_note-59]

### Outside analysis

While it reports directly to the [supreme leader](36814344.md), there are debates over how independently Quds Force operates.[^cite_note-LA_Times-51]

Mahan Abedin, director of research at the London-based Center for the Study of Terrorism (and editor of *Islamism Digest*), believes the unit is not independent: "Quds Force, although it's a highly specialized department, it is subject to strict, iron-clad military discipline. It's completely controlled by the military hierarchy of the IRGC, and the IRGC is very tightly controlled by the highest levels of the administration in Iran."[^cite_note-rfe216-60]

According to a *[Los Angeles Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Times)* report,[^cite_note-LA_Times-51] in Abedin's view, "\[I]t's a very capable force—their people are extremely talented, \[and] they tend to be the best people in the IRGC".[^cite_note-rfe216-60]

## Activities

See also: [List of wars and battles involving the Quds Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_wars_and_battles_involving_the_Quds_Force)

The Quds Force trains and equips foreign Islamic revolutionary groups around the Middle East. [Paramilitary](146730.md) instruction typically occurs in [Iran](14653.md) or [Sudan](27421.md). Foreign recruits are transported from their home countries to Iran to receive training. The Quds Force sometimes plays a more direct role in the military operations of the forces it trains, including pre-attack planning and other operation-specific military advice.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDionisi20068-39]

### Afghanistan

Since 1979, Iran had supported the Shi'a [Hezbe Wahdat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbe_Wahdat) forces against the Afghan government of [Mohammad Najibullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Najibullah). When Najibullah stepped down as President in 1992, Iran continued supporting Hezbe Wahdat against other Afghan militia groups. When the [Taliban](30635.md) took over Afghanistan in 1996, Hezbe Wahdat had lost its founder and main leader, [Abdul Ali Mazari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Ali_Mazari), so the group joined [Ahmad Shah Massoud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Massoud)'s [Northern Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Alliance). Iran began supporting the Northern Alliance against the Taliban, who were backed by Pakistan and the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world).[^cite_note-61] In 1999, after several Iranian diplomats were killed by the Taliban in [Mazar-e Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazar-e_Sharif), Iran nearly got into a war with the Taliban.[^cite_note-TimeQuds-62][^cite_note-63] The Quds Force reportedly fought alongside the United States and the Northern Alliance in the [Battle for Herat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_uprising_in_Herat). However, in recent years Iran is accused of helping and training the [Taliban insurgents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_insurgency) against the NATO-backed [Karzai administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Hamid_Karzai).[^cite_note-telegraph-19][^cite_note-PAN-20] Iranian-made weapons, including powerful explosive devices are often found inside Afghanistan.[^cite_note-CNN-21][^cite_note-rferl-23][^cite_note-Afghans_find_tons_of_explosive_devices_transferred_from_Iran-24][^cite_note-jamestown.org-25]

> We did interdict a shipment, without question the [Revolutionary Guard](1285472.md)'s core Quds Force, through a known Taliban facilitator. Three of the individuals were killed... Iranians certainly view as making life more difficult for us if Afghanistan is unstable. We don't have that kind of relationship with the Iranians. That's why I am particularly troubled by the interception of weapons coming from Iran. But we know that it's more than weapons; it's money; it's also according to some reports, training at Iranian camps as well.[^cite_note-Concern_in_US_over_increasing_Iranian_activity_in_Afghanistan-22]

— [General](2965165.md) [David Petraeus](3223944.md)

In March 2012, Najibullah Kabuli, leader of the *National Participation Front* (NPF) of Afghanistan, accused three senior leaders of Iran's Revolutionary Guards of plotting to [assassinate](2963.md) him.[^cite_note-64] Some members of the [Afghan Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Afghanistan) accuses Iran of setting up Taliban bases in several Iranian cities, and that "Iran is directly involved in fanning [ethnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic), [linguistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linguistic) and [sectarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sectarian) tensions in Afghanistan."[^cite_note-65] There are reports about Iran's Revolutionary Guards training Afghans inside Iran to carry out terrorist attacks in Afghanistan.[^cite_note-Kamal-66]

> Currently, the Revolutionary Guards recruit young people for terrorist activities in Afghanistan and try to revive the [Hezb-i-Islami Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezbi_Islami) led by [Gulbadin Hekmatyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulbuddin_Hekmatyar) and Taliban groups[^cite_note-Kamal-66]

— Syed Kamal, a self-confessed agent for Iran's Revolutionary Guards and member of Sipah-i-Mohmmad

### India

Following an attack on an Israeli diplomat in India in February 2012, [Delhi Police](8808263.md) contended that the IRGC had some involvement.[^cite_note-TOI-67] This was subsequently confirmed in July 2012, after a report by the Delhi Police found evidence that members of IRGC had been involved in the 13 February bomb attack in the capital.[^cite_note-TOI-67]

### United States

On 11 October 2011, the [Obama Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Barack_Obama) revealed the [United States Government's](195149.md) allegations that the Quds Force was involved with the [plot to assassinate Saudi Arabia's Ambassador to the United States Adel al-Jubeir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adel_al-Jubeir_assassination_plot), which also entailed plans to bomb the [Israeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Israel_in_Washington,_D.C.) and [Saudi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Saudi_Arabia_in_Washington,_D.C.) embassies located in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md)[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

In August 2022, plans to assassinate former US government officials [John Bolton](997107.md) and [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo) were uncovered by US federal prosecutors, likely in retaliation for the January 2020 death of Soleimani.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72]

### Iraq

The Quds Force has been described as the Iranian "unit deployed to challenge the United States presence" in Iraq following the [U.S. invasion](201936.md) of that country, which put "165,000 American troops along [Iran](14653.md)'s western border," adding to the American troops already in Iran's eastern neighbor [Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Afghanistan_\(2001%E2%80%93present\)).[^cite_note-WrightRobin-75]

The force established [Unit 3800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_3800?action=edit\&redlink=1) with the aid of [Hezbollah](13919.md)[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77] and "operated throughout Iraq, arming, aiding, and abetting [Shiite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiite) militias"—i.e., the [Supreme Council for Islamic Revolution in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Council_for_Islamic_Revolution_in_Iraq), [Dawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Dawa_Party), and the [Mahdi Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdi_Army)—"all" of which "had close ties to Iran, some dating back decades" as part of their struggle against [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein)'s oppressive Arab nationalist regime.[^cite_note-WrightRobin-75] The Quds Force trained the Shiite militias in the use of roadside bombs, known as [IEDs](265112.md), that were the largest cause of U.S. military deaths in Iraq.[^cite_note-78]

In November 2006, with sectarian violence in Iraq increasing, U.S. General [John Abizaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Abizaid) accused the Quds Force of supporting "Shi'a death squads", while the government of Iran was pledging support in stabilization.[^cite_note-79] Similarly, in July 2007, [Major General](36301471.md) Kevin Bergner of the U.S. Army alleged that members of the Quds Force aided in the planning of [a raid](9129813.md) on U.S. forces in the Iraqi city of [Karbala](210957.md) in January 2007.[^cite_note-80]

Former CIA officer [Robert Baer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Baer) asserts the Quds Force uses couriers for all sensitive communications.[^cite_note-81]

#### 2006 detainment in Iraq

On 24 December 2006, *[The New York Times](30680.md)* reported that at least four Iranians had been captured by American troops in Iraq in the previous few days. According to the article, the U.S. government suspected that two of them were members of Quds Force, which would be some of the first physical proof of Quds Force activity in Iraq.[^cite_note-82] According to [The Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon), the alleged Quds Force members were "involved in the transfer of [IED](265112.md) technologies from Iran to Iraq."[^cite_note-Lekic-83] The two men had entered Iraq legally, although they were not accredited diplomats. Iraqi officials believed that the evidence against the men was only circumstantial, but on 29 December, and under U.S. pressure, the Iraqi government ordered the men to leave Iraq. They were driven back to Iran that day.[^cite_note-84] In mid-January 2007 it was reported that the two alleged Quds force officers seized by American forces were Brig. Gen. Mohsen Chizari and Col. Abu Amad Davari. According to *[The Washington Post](102226.md)*. Chizari is the third highest officer of Quds Force, making him the allegedly highest-ranked Iranian to ever be held by the United States.[^cite_note-85]

#### *New York Sun* report

*[The New York Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_York_Sun)* reported that the documents described the Quds Force as not only cooperating with Shi'a death squads, but also with fighters related to [al-Qaeda](1921.md) and [Ansar al-Sunna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ansar_al-Sunna). It said that the Quds Force had studied the Iraq situation in a similar manner to the U.S. [Iraq Study Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Study_Group), and had concluded that they must increase efforts with [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) and Shiite groups in order to counter the influence of Sunni states.[^cite_note-86]

#### U.S. raid on Iranian liaison office

Main article: [U.S. raid on the Iranian Liaison Office in Erbil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._raid_on_the_Iranian_Liaison_Office_in_Erbil)

Further information: [U.S. kill or capture strategy in Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._kill_or_capture_strategy_in_Iraq)

On 11 January 2007, U.S. forces raided and detained five employees of the Iranian liaison office in [Erbil](24534207.md), Iraq. The U.S. military said the five detainees were connected to the Quds Force.[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] The operation drew protests from the regional Kurdish government while the Russian government called the detainments unacceptable.[^cite_note-89]

[Alireza Nourizadeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alireza_Nourizadeh), a political analyst at [Voice of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_America), stated that their arrests were causing concern in Iranian intelligence because the five alleged officials were knowledgeable of a wide range of Quds Force and Iranian activities in Iraq.[^cite_note-90] According to American ambassador [Zalmay Khalilzad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zalmay_Khalilzad), one of the men in custody was Quds Force's director of operations.[^cite_note-91]

Iranian and Iraqi officials maintained that the detained men were part of a diplomatic mission in the city of Erbil, [Iraq](7515928.md).[^cite_note-IHT-20070406-92] The five Iranian detainees were still being held at a U.S. prison in Iraq as of 8 July 2007.[^cite_note-first-93] The U.S. said they were "still being interrogated" and that it had "no plans to free them while they are seen as a security risk in Iraq."[^cite_note-Times-20070327-94] Iran said that the detainees were "kidnapped diplomats" and that they were "held as hostages."[^cite_note-95]

On 9 July 2009, the five detainees were released from U.S. custody to Iraqi officials.[^cite_note-96]

#### Allegations of involvement in Karbala attack

Main article: [Karbala provincial headquarters raid](9129813.md)

On 20 January 2007, a group of gunmen attacked the Karbala Provincial Joint Coordination Center in [Karbala](210957.md), captured four American soldiers, and subsequently killed them. The attackers passed through an Iraqi checkpoint at around 5 pm, a total of five black [GMC Suburbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GMC_Suburban), similar to those driven by U.S. security and diplomatic officials. They were also wearing American military uniforms and spoke fluent English. Because of the sophistication of the attack, some analysts have suggested that only a group like the Quds Force would be able to plan and carry out such an action.[^cite_note-97] Former [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA) officer [Robert Baer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Baer) also suggested that the five Americans were killed by the Quds Force in revenge for the Americans holding five Iranians since the 11 January raid in Irbil.[^cite_note-98] It was reported that the U.S. military is investigating whether or not the attackers were trained by Iranian officials; however, no evidence besides the sophistication of the attack has yet been presented.[^cite_note-99]

On 2 July 2007, the U.S. military said that information from captured [Hezbollah](13919.md) fighter [Ali Musa Daqduq](12079041.md) established a link between the Quds Force and the Karbala raid. The U.S. military claims Daqduq worked as a liaison between Quds force operatives and the Shia group that carried out the raid. According to the United States, Daqduq said that the Shia group "could not have conducted this complex operation without the support and direction of the Quds force".[^cite_note-100]

#### Allegations of support for Iraqi militants

In June 2007, U.S. General [Ray Odierno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Odierno) asserted that Iranian support for these Shia militia increased as the United States itself implemented the 2007 "[troop surge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_War_troop_surge_of_2007)".[^cite_note-101] Two different studies have maintained that approximately half of all foreign insurgents entering Iraq come from [Saudi Arabia](349303.md).[^cite_note-102]

In December 2009 evidence uncovered during an investigation by *The Guardian* newspaper and Guardian Films linked the Quds Force to the kidnappings of five Britons from a government ministry building in [Baghdad](4492.md) in 2007. Four of the hostages, Jason Creswell, Jason Swindlehurst, Alec Maclachlan, and Alan McMenemy, were killed. Peter Moore was released on 30 December 2009. The investigation uncovered evidence that Moore, 37, a computer expert from Lincoln was targeted because he was installing a system for the Iraqi Government that would show how a vast amount of international aid was diverted to Iran's militia groups in Iraq. One of the alleged groups funded by the Quds force directly is the Righteous League, which emerged in 2006 and has stayed largely in the shadows as a proxy of the Quds Force. Shia cleric and leading figure of the Righteous League, Qais al-Khazali, was handed over by the U.S. military for release by the Iraqi government on 29 December 2009 as part of the deal that led to the release of Moore.[^cite_note-103]

#### Combat against Islamic State



The Quds Force's commander, General [Qasem Soleimani](16805217.md), was involved with both the planning as well as the execution of the operation to expel ISIL from Tikrit.

In 2014, Quds Force was [deployed into Iraq to lead Iranian action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian-led_intervention_in_Iraq) against [ISIL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_State_in_Iraq_and_the_Levant). Iran sent three Quds Force battalions to help the Iraqi government repel ISIL's [2014 Northern Iraq offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Iraq_offensive_\(June_2014\)).[^cite_note-116] Over 40 officers participated in the [Second Battle of Tikrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Battle_of_Tikrit), including the commander of the force, Gen. Qasem Soleimani who took a leading role in the operation.

#### 2020 drone strike on Qasem Soleimani in Iraq

On 3 January 2020, a drone strike approved by United States President Donald Trump at Baghdad International Airport killed General [Qasem Soleimani](16805217.md), the head of the Quds Force. He was replaced by General Esmail Qaani [^cite_note-117]

In November 2021 the Commander of the corps went to Iraq for a visit.[^cite_note-118]

### Lebanon

In Lebanon, Quads Force plays an important and ongoing role, mostly by supporting Hezbollah and other groups that are friendly to Iran. Its actions in Lebanon are part of a larger strategy that involves unconventional warfare, intelligence operations, and support for armed groups throughout the Middle East.[^cite_note-\:1-119][^cite_note-120]

One of the main ways the Quds Force is involved in Lebanon is through its close partnership with Hezbollah. It provides Hezbollah with weapons, training, money, and strategic advice through [Unit 190](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_190?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] [Unit 700](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_700?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] and [Unit 18000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unit_18000?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-126][^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] This support has helped Hezbollah grow into a powerful military and political group in Lebanon, and a central part of Iran’s efforts to challenge Israel in the region.[^cite_note-\:1-119] The Quds Force also provides training for fighters, both in Lebanon and Iran. They help build up military infrastructure, including weapons storage sites in southern Lebanon. These are sometimes located near civilian areas or [UN](31769.md) facilities, which increases risks during conflicts.[^cite_note-129]

The Quds Force is also directly involved in planning attacks. For example, a man named Hassan Ali Mahmoud Badir—who worked with both Hezbollah and the Quds Force—was recently killed in Beirut. Reports say he was helping to coordinate attacks between Hamas and Hezbollah, targeting Israeli civilians. This shows how the Quds Force is actively involved in organizing operations, not just offering support from a distance.[^cite_note-\:2-130]

Esmail Qaani, the leader of the Quds Force, has visited Lebanon often, especially after the 7 October Hamas attack on Israel.[^cite_note-\:3-131] His visits are meant to strengthen cooperation between Iran, Hezbollah, and Palestinian groups. However, even with his involvement, Hezbollah still runs most operations in Lebanon on its own.[^cite_note-\:3-131] Lebanon is just one part of the Quds Force’s broader mission. It also supports armed groups in Syria, Iraq, Yemen, and the Palestinian territories. These efforts are part of Iran’s regional strategy to build influence and challenge rival powers, especially Israel and the United States.[^cite_note-132][^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

In recent months, Israel has carried out strikes to kill Quds Force members and their allies in Lebanon, saying these individuals were planning attacks on civilians. The killing of people like Hassan Badir highlights how directly involved the Quds Force is in planning military operations.[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136] The presence of the Quds Force in Lebanon also contributes to regular flare-ups in violence between Hezbollah and Israel. Even when ceasefires are in place, the Quds Force’s actions often lead to violations and renewed clashes, making peace in the region more difficult to achieve.[^cite_note-\:2-130][^cite_note-137]

### Syria

Further information: [Syrian civil war](30741795.md) and [Iranian involvement in the Syrian civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_involvement_in_the_Syrian_civil_war)

In 2011, the Quds Force deployed to Syria.[^cite_note-138] IRGC Commander Jafari announced on 16 September 2012 that Quds Force "were present" in Syria.[^cite_note-139]

In order to support Assad, the IRGC's Quds Force recruited, funded, and trained two key militias which are led by Quds Force commanders and operate from Iran. These are:

-  [Liwa Fatemiyoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_Fatemiyoun) (a Shia Afghan militia founded in 2013)

  [^cite_note-Soleimani-140]

-  [Liwa Zainabiyoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liwa_Zainabiyoun) (a Shia Pakistani militia founded in 2015)

  [^cite_note-141]

Coinciding with the [Geneva II Conference on Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_II_Conference_on_Syria) in 2014, Iran boosted its presence in Syria with several "hundred" military specialists, including senior commanders from the Quds Force, according to Iranian sources and security experts. While recently retired senior IRGC commander told that there were at least 60 to 70 Quds force commanders on the ground in Syria at any given time.[^cite_note-Y4-142] The primary role of these forces is to gather intelligence and manage the logistics of the battle for the Syrian Government.[^cite_note-Y4-142][^cite_note-iranian-strategy-143]

In November 2015, the Quds Force conducted a successful rescue mission of a Russian bomber pilot who was shot down by a Turkish fighter jet.

In May 2018, Quds forces on the Syrian-held side of the [Golan Heights](7515865.md) allegedly [fired around 20 projectiles towards Israeli army positions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_House_of_Cards) without causing damage or casualties.[^cite_note-144] Israel responded with airstrikes against Iranian bases in Syria.[^cite_note-145] At least twenty-three fighters, among them 18 foreigners, were reportedly killed in the strikes.[^cite_note-146]

In January 2019, the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) confirmed that it had carried out strikes against Iranian military targets in Syria several hours after a rocket was intercepted over the Golan Heights. The Israeli military claimed in a statement that Quds Force positions were targeted and included a warning to the [Syrian military](27479.md) against "attempting to harm Israeli forces or territory."[^cite_note-NYT-147]

In April 2021, prominent Syria-based Quds operative Brigadier General Mohammad Reza Fallahzadeh became Quds Deputy Commander.[^cite_note-syriaquds-2]

## Designation as a terrorist organization

The [United States Department of the Treasury](53667.md) designated the Quds Force under [Executive Order 13224](1296167.md) for providing material support to U.S.-designated terrorist organizations on 25 October 2007, prohibiting transactions between the group and U.S. citizens, and freezing any assets under U.S. jurisdiction.[^cite_note-us-2007-10-25-156] [Canada](5042916.md) designated the Quds Force as a [terrorist organization](63578.md) on 17 December 2012.[^cite_note-canada-2014-01-22-157] [Israel](9282173.md) designated the Quds Force as a terrorist organization in March 2015.[^cite_note-158]

On 23 October 2018, the kingdoms of [Saudi Arabia](349303.md) and [Bahrain](18933277.md), both involved in [Saudi Arabian-led intervention in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saudi_Arabian-led_intervention_in_Yemen) against Quds Force-backed [Houthis](17240332.md), designated the IRGC as a terrorist organization. The designation also included former commander [Qasem Soleimani](16805217.md).[^cite_note-159]

In April 2019, the U.S. made the decision to designate the [Islamic Revolutionary Guard Corps](1285472.md) (IRGC), a foreign military, as a foreign terrorist organization by the [State Department](31975.md) under an immigration statute and their maximum pressure campaign.[^cite_note-160] This designation was done over the opposition of the [Central Intelligence Agency](5183633.md) (CIA) and the [Department of Defense](7279897.md) (DoD).[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162][^cite_note-163]

On 28 August 2019, when Israel's foreign minister [Israel Katz](19041378.md) made a visit to the United Kingdom, he asked the UK's foreign minister [Dominic Raab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominic_Raab) to designate the Quds Force as a terrorist organization.[^cite_note-164] The U.S. government's [Rewards for Justice Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rewards_for_Justice_Program) offers \$15 million for information on QF financing.[^cite_note-165]

On 17 January 2026, [Argentina](18951905.md) designated the Quds Force as a terrorist organization.[^cite_note-Argentina-166]

### Sanctions

The Quds Force circumvents international [sanctions against Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctions_against_Iran) by forming fake businesses and institutions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEUstiashvili20234936-167]

On 1 May 2020, the U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) designated Iranian-Iraqi national Amir Dianat, associate of Revolutionary Guards Quds Force officials. Dianat, who also known as Amir Abdulazeez Jaafar, has been involved in the Quds Force's efforts to generate revenue and smuggle weapons abroad. The U.S. Department of the Treasury's Office of Foreign Assets Control (OFAC) also designating "Taif" Mineral Mining Services Company, a company owned, controlled, or directed by Dianat.[^cite_note-168]
