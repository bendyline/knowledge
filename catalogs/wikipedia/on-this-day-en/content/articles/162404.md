# Stratovolcano



[Mount Rainier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Rainier), a 4,390-metre-tall (14,410 ft) stratovolcano, the highest mountain in the US state of Washington



Exposed internal structure of alternating layers of [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) and [pyroclastic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_rock) in the [eroded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erosion) [Broken Top](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broken_Top) stratovolcano in [Oregon](26811621.md)

A **stratovolcano**, also known as a **composite volcano**, is a typically [conical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_cone) [volcano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcano) built up by many alternating layers ([strata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratum)) of hardened [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) and [tephra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tephra).[^cite_note-1] Unlike [shield volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shield_volcano), stratovolcanoes are characterized by a steep profile with a summit crater and explosive eruptions.[^cite_note-2] Some have collapsed summit craters called [calderas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caldera).[^cite_note-3] The lava flowing from stratovolcanoes typically cools and solidifies before spreading far, due to high [viscosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity). The [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma) forming this lava is often [felsic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felsic), having high to intermediate levels of [silica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silica) (as in [rhyolite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyolite), [dacite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dacite), or [andesite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andesite)), with lesser amounts of less viscous [mafic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafic) [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma).[^cite_note-4] Extensive felsic lava flows are uncommon, but can travel as far as 8 kilometres (5 miles).[^cite_note-5]

The term *composite volcano* is used because strata are usually mixed and uneven instead of neat layers.[^cite_note-6] They are among the most common types of volcanoes;[^cite_note-\:0-7] more than 700 stratovolcanoes have erupted lava during the [Holocene Epoch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene_Epoch) (the last 11,700 years),[^cite_note-GVP_Holocene_Composite-8] and many older, now extinct, stratovolcanoes erupted lava as far back as [Archean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archean) times.[^cite_note-GVP_Pleistocene_Composite-9][^cite_note-10] Stratovolcanoes are typically found in [subduction zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction) but they also occur in other geological settings. Two examples of stratovolcanoes famous for catastrophic eruptions are [Krakatoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krakatoa) in [Indonesia](14579.md) (which erupted in [1883](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1883_eruption_of_Krakatoa) claiming 36,000 lives)[^cite_note-11] and Mount [Vesuvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vesuvius) in [Italy](14532.md) (which erupted in [79](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eruption_of_Mount_Vesuvius_in_79) A.D killing an estimated 2,000 people).[^cite_note-12] In modern times, [Mount St. Helens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_St._Helens) (1980) in [Washington State](13015878.md), US, and [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo) (1991) in the [Philippines](23440.md) have erupted catastrophically, but with fewer deaths.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

The existence of stratovolcanoes on other bodies of the [Solar System](26903.md) has not been conclusively demonstrated.[^cite_note-13] [Zephyria Tholus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zephyria_Tholus) is one of two mountains in the [Aeolis region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aeolis_quadrangle) of Mars that have been proposed as possible stratovolcanoes.[^cite_note-mars-14]

## Distribution



Cross-section of subduction zone and associated stratovolcanoes

Stratovolcanoes are common at [subduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction) zones, forming chains and clusters along [plate tectonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plate_tectonics) boundaries where an [oceanic crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_crust) plate is drawn under a [continental crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_crust) plate (continental arc volcanism, e.g. [Cascade Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cascade_Range), [Andes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andes), [Campania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campanian_volcanic_arc)) or another [oceanic crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_crust) plate ([island arc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Island_arc) volcanism, e.g. [Japan](15573.md), [Philippines](23440.md), [Aleutian Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleutian_Islands)).[^cite_note-15]

Stratovolcanoes also occur in some other geological settings, for example as a result of [intraplate volcanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intraplate_volcanism) on oceanic islands far from plate boundaries. Examples are [Teide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teide) in the [Canary Islands](5717.md),[^cite_note-Cas_etal_2022-16] and [Pico do Fogo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pico_do_Fogo) in [Cape Verde](18962637.md).[^cite_note-Carvalho_etal_2022-17] Stratovolcanoes have formed in continental [rifts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rift). Examples in the [East African Rift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_African_Rift) are [Ol Doinyo Lengai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ol_Doinyo_Lengai) in Tanzania,[^cite_note-OlDoinyoGVP-18] and [Longonot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longonot) in Kenya.[^cite_note-LongonotGVP-19]

## Formation

[Subduction zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction) volcanoes form when [hydrous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrous) [minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) are pulled down into the mantle on the slab. These hydrous minerals, such as [chlorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chlorite_group) and [serpentine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpentine_subgroup), release their water into the [mantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_mantle) which decreases its [melting point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melting_point) by 60 to 100 °C (110 to 180 °F). The release of water from [hydrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrate) minerals is termed "[dewatering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dewatering)", and occurs at specific pressures and temperatures for each mineral, as the plate descends to greater depths.[^cite_note-20] This allows the [mantle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_mantle) to partially melt and generate [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma). This is called [flux melting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flux_melting). The magma then rises through the [crust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crust_\(geology\)), incorporating silica-rich crustal rock, leading to a final [intermediate composition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate_composition). When the magma nears the top surface, it pools in a [magma chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma_chamber) within the crust below the stratovolcano.[^cite_note-21]

The processes that trigger the final eruption remain a question for further research. Possible mechanisms include:[^cite_note-\:3-22]

- [Magma differentiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous_differentiation), in which the lightest, most silica-rich magma and volatiles such as water, [halogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halogen), and [sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) accumulate in the uppermost part of the magma chamber. This can dramatically increase pressures.

  [^cite_note-23]

- [Fractional crystallization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_crystallization_\(geology\)) of the magma. When [anhydrous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anhydrous) [minerals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mineral) such as [feldspar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feldspar) crystallize out of the magma, this concentrates volatiles in the remaining liquid, which can lead to a second boiling that causes a [gas phase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phase_\(matter\)) (carbon dioxide or water) to separate from the liquid magma and raise magma chamber pressures.

  [^cite_note-24]

  [^cite_note-25]

- Injection of fresh magma into the magma chamber, which mixes and heats the cooler magma already present. This could force volatiles out of solution and lower the density of the cooler magma, both of which increase pressure. There is considerable evidence for magma mixing just before many eruptions, including magnesium-rich [olivine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olivine) crystals in freshly erupted silicic [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) that show no reaction rim. This is possible only if the lava erupted immediately after mixing since olivine rapidly reacts with silicic magma to form a rim of [pyroxene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroxene).

  [^cite_note-26]

- Progressive melting of the surrounding [country rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country_rock_\(geology\)).

  [^cite_note-\:3-22]

These internal triggers may be modified by external triggers such as [sector collapse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sector_collapse), [earthquakes](10106.md), or [interactions with groundwater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phreatomagmatic_eruption). Some of these triggers operate only under limited conditions. For example, sector collapse (where part of the flank of a volcano collapses in a massive [landslide](37208.md)) can only trigger the eruption of a very shallow magma chamber. Magma differentiation and [thermal expansion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_expansion) also are ineffective as triggers for eruptions from deep magma chambers.[^cite_note-\:3-22]

## Hazards

Main article: [Volcanic hazard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_hazard)



Mount Etna on the island of [Sicily](27619.md), in southern Italy





[Mount Fuji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Fuji) on [Honshu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honshu) (top) and [Mount Unzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Unzen) on [Kyushu](16914.md) (bottom), two of [Japan](15573.md)'s stratovolcanoes

In [recorded history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recorded_history), explosive eruptions at [subduction zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction_zone) ([convergent-boundary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convergent_boundary)) volcanoes have posed the greatest hazard to civilizations.[^cite_note-dynearth-27] [Subduction-zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subduction) stratovolcanoes, such as [Mount St. Helens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_St._Helens), [Mount Etna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Etna) and [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo), typically erupt with explosive force because the [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma) is too [viscous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity) to allow easy escape of [volcanic gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_gas).[^cite_note-28] As a consequence, the tremendous internal pressures of the trapped volcanic gases remain and intermingle in the pasty [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma). Following the breaching of the [vent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_vent) and the opening of the crater, the [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma) degasses explosively. The magma and gases blast out with high speed and full force.[^cite_note-dynearth-27]

Since 1600 [CE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era), nearly 300,000 people have been killed by [volcanic eruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_eruptions). Most deaths were caused by [pyroclastic flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_flow) and [lahars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahar), deadly hazards that often accompany explosive eruptions of subduction-zone stratovolcanoes.[^cite_note-dynearth-27] Pyroclastic flows are swift, avalanche-like, ground-sweeping, incandescent mixtures of hot volcanic debris, fine [ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash), fragmented [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava), and superheated gases that can travel at speeds over 150 km/h (90 mph).[^cite_note-dynearth-27] Around 30,000 people were killed by pyroclastic flows during the [1902 eruption of Mount Pelée](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1902_eruption_of_Mount_Pel%C3%A9e) on the island of [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique) in the [Caribbean](18956035.md).[^cite_note-dynearth-27] During March and April 1982, [El Chichón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Chich%C3%B3n) in the State of [Chiapas](6787.md) in southeastern [Mexico](3966054.md), erupted 3 times, causing the worst volcanic disaster in Mexico's history and killing more than 2,000 people in pyroclastic flows.[^cite_note-dynearth-27]

Two [Decade Volcanoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decade_Volcanoes) that erupted in 1991 provide examples of stratovolcano hazards. On 15 June, Mount Pinatubo erupted and caused an [ash cloud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash) to shoot 40 km (25 mi) into the air. It produced large [pyroclastic surges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_surge) and lahar floods that caused a lot of damage to the surrounding area.[^cite_note-dynearth-27] Mount Pinatubo, located in [Central Luzon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Luzon) 90 km (56 mi) west-northwest of [Manila](184334.md), had been dormant for six centuries before an eruption in 1991. This eruption was the second largest in the 20th century.[^cite_note-\:1-29] It produced a large cloud of [volcanic ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash) that affected global temperatures, lowering them as much as 0.5 °C.[^cite_note-\:1-29] The cloud consisted of 22 million tons of [sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) which combined with water droplets to create [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid).[^cite_note-dynearth-27] In 1991 Japan's [Mount Unzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Unzen) also erupted, after 200 years of inactivity. It's located on the island of [Kyushu](16914.md) about 40 km (25 mi) east of [Nagasaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagasaki).[^cite_note-dynearth-27] Beginning in June, a newly formed lava dome repeatedly collapsed. This generated a pyroclastic flow that flowed down the mountain's slopes at speeds as high as 200 km/h (120 mph).[^cite_note-dynearth-27] The [1991 eruption of Mount Unzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_eruption_of_Mount_Unzen) caused 43 deaths. In 1792, Mount Unzen was responsible for one of the worst volcanic disasters in Japan's history, killing more than 15,000 people.[^cite_note-30]

The [eruption of Mount Vesuvius in 79 AD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eruption_of_Mount_Vesuvius_in_79_AD) is the most famous example of a hazardous stratovolcano eruption. Pyroclastic surges completely smothered the nearby ancient cities of [Pompeii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pompeii) and [Herculaneum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herculaneum) with thick deposits of ash and [pumice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumice) ranging from 6–7 meters deep. Pompeii had 10,000–20,000 inhabitants at the time of eruption.[^cite_note-31] [Mount Vesuvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Vesuvius) is recognized as one of the most dangerous of the world's volcanoes, due to its capacity for [powerful explosive eruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plinian_eruption) coupled with the high population density of the surrounding [Metropolitan Naples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_City_of_Naples) area (totaling about 3.6 million inhabitants).[^cite_note-32]

### Ash

Main article: [Volcanic ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash)



Snow-like blanket of [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo)'s ashfall deposits in a parking lot on [Clark Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_Air_Base) (15 June 1991)

In addition to potentially affecting the climate, [volcanic ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash) clouds from [explosive eruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_eruption) pose a serious hazard to [aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation).[^cite_note-dynearth-27] Volcanic ash clouds consist of silt- or sand-sized pieces of rock, mineral, [volcanic glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_glass). Volcanic ash grains are jagged, abrasive, and insoluble in water.[^cite_note-\:22-33] For example, during the 1982 eruption of [Galunggung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galunggung) in [Java](69336.md), [British Airways Flight 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Airways_Flight_9) flew into the ash cloud, causing it to sustain temporary engine failure and structural damage.[^cite_note-34] Although no crashes have happened due to ash, more than 60, mostly [commercial aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_aircraft), have been damaged. Some of these incidents resulted in emergency landings.[^cite_note-\:05-35][^cite_note-dynearth-27] [Ashfalls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash) are a threat to health when inhaled and are also a threat to property. A square yard (0.84 m²) of a 4-inch (10 cm) thick volcanic ash layer can weigh 120–200 pounds (54–91 kg) and can get twice as heavy when wet. Wet ash also poses a risk to electronics due to its [conductive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_conductor) nature.[^cite_note-\:22-33] Dense clouds of hot volcanic ash can be expelled due to the collapse of an [eruptive column](1533051.md), or laterally due to the partial collapse of a [volcanic edifice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_edifice) or [lava dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_dome) during [explosive eruptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Explosive_eruption). These clouds are known as [pyroclastic surges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_surge) and in addition to volcanic ash, they contain hot [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava), [pumice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pumice), [rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_rock), and volcanic gas. Pyroclastic surges flow at speeds over 50 mph (80 km/h) and are at temperatures between 200 °C – 700 °C. These surges can cause major damage to property and people in their path.[^cite_note-36]

### Lava

Main article: [Lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava)



[Mayon Volcano](256485.md) in [Philippines](23440.md) extruding lava flows during its eruption on 29 December 2009

[Lava flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) from stratovolcanoes are generally not a significant threat to humans or animals because the highly [viscous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity) [lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava) moves slowly enough for everyone to evacuate. Most deaths attributed to lava are due to related causes such as [explosions](18993857.md) and [asphyxiation](98534.md) from [toxic gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toxic_gas).[^cite_note-\:12-37] Lava flows can bury homes and farms in thick [volcanic rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_rock) which greatly reduces property value.[^cite_note-\:12-37] However, not all stratovolcanoes erupt viscous and sticky lava. [Nyiragongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nyiragongo), near [Lake Kivu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Kivu) in [central Africa](539606.md), is very dangerous because its [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma) has an unusually low [silica content](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicate), making it much less viscous than other stratovolcanoes. Low [viscosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity) lava can generate massive [lava fountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lava_fountain), while lava of thicker viscosity can solidify within the vent, creating a [volcanic plug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_plug). Volcanic plugs can trap [volcanic gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_gas) and create pressure in the magma chamber, resulting in violent eruptions.[^cite_note-38] Lava is typically between 700 and 1,200 °C (1,300–2,200 °F).[^cite_note-39]

### Volcanic bombs

Main article: [Volcanic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_bomb)

[Volcanic bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_bomb) are masses of unconsolidated rock and lava that are ejected during an eruption. Volcanic bombs are classified as larger than 64 mm (2.5 in). Anything from 2 to 64 mm is classified as [lapilli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapilli).[^cite_note-\:03-40] When erupted, volcanic bombs are still molten and partially cool and solidify on their descent. They can form ribbon or oval shapes that can also flatten on impact with the ground.[^cite_note-41] Volcanic bombs are associated with [Strombolian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strombolian_eruption) and [Vulcanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcanian_eruption) eruptions and [basaltic lava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basaltic_lava). Ejection velocities ranging from 200 to 400 m/s have been recorded causing volcanic bombs to be destructive.[^cite_note-\:03-40]

### Lahar

Main article: [Lahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahar)

[Lahars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahar) (from a [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_language) term for volcanic mudflows) are a mixture of volcanic debris and water. Lahars can result from heavy rainfall during or before the eruption or interaction with ice and snow. Meltwater mixes with volcanic debris causing a fast moving mudflow. Lahars are typically about 60% sediment and 40% water.[^cite_note-\:04-42] Depending on the abundance of volcanic debris the lahar can be fluid or thick like concrete.[^cite_note-\:13-43] Lahars have the strength and speed to flatten structures and cause great bodily harm, gaining speeds up to dozens of kilometers per hour.[^cite_note-\:04-42] In the [1985 eruption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armero_tragedy) of [Nevado del Ruiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nevado_del_Ruiz) in [Colombia](5222.md), [pyroclastic surges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyroclastic_surge) melted snow and ice atop the 5,321 m (17,457 ft) high Andean volcano. The ensuing lahar killed 25,000 people and flooded the city of [Armero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armero) and nearby settlements.[^cite_note-\:13-43]

### Volcanic gas

Main article: [Volcanic gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_gas)

As a volcano forms, several different [gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_gas) mix with [magma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magma) in the volcanic chamber. During an eruption the gases are then released into the [atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere) which can lead to toxic human exposure. The most abundant of these gases is [H<sub>2</sub>O](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water) ([water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water)) followed by [CO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) ([carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide)), [SO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) ([sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide)), [H<sub>2</sub>S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_sulfide) ([hydrogen sulfide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_sulfide)), and [HF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_fluoride) ([hydrogen fluoride](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_fluoride)).[^cite_note-\:04-42] If at concentrations of more than 3% in the air, when breathed in [CO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) can cause dizziness and difficulty breathing. At more than 15% concentration [CO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) causes death. CO<sub>2</sub> can settle into depressions in the land, leading to deadly, odorless pockets of gas.[^cite_note-\:06-44] [SO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) classified as a respiratory, skin, and eye irritant. It is known for its pungent egg smell and role in ozone depletion and has the potential to cause acid rain downwind of an eruption.[^cite_note-\:06-44] [H<sub>2</sub>S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_sulfide) has an even stronger odor than [SO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) as well as being even more toxic. Exposure for less than an hour at concentrations of over 500 ppm causes death.[^cite_note-\:06-44] [HF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrogen_fluoride) and similar species can coat [ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash) particles and once deposited can poison soil and water.[^cite_note-\:06-44] Gases are also emitted during volcanic degassing, which is a passive release of [gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas) during periods of dormancy.[^cite_note-\:06-44]

## Eruptions that affected global climate



Mount Pinatubo's 1991 eruption ash cloud seen from Clark Airbase. 12 June 1991

While eruptions like [Mount Unzen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Unzen) have caused deaths and local damage, the impact of the June 1991 eruption of [Mount Pinatubo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pinatubo) was seen globally.[^cite_note-\:05-35] The eruptive columns reached heights of 40 km (25 mi) and dumped 17 megatons of SO<sub>2</sub> into the lower [stratosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stratosphere).[^cite_note-\:14-45] The [aerosols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosol) that formed from the [sulfur dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) (SO<sub>2</sub>), [carbon dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) (CO<sub>2</sub>), and other [volcanic gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_gas) dispersed around the world. The [SO<sub>2</sub>](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfur_dioxide) in this cloud combined with water (both of volcanic and atmospheric origin) and formed [sulfuric acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulfuric_acid), blocking a portion of the sunlight from reaching the [troposphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troposphere).[^cite_note-\:05-35] This caused the global temperature to decrease by about 0.4 °C (0.72 °F) from 1992 to 1993. These [aerosols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerosol) caused the [ozone layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone_layer) to reach the lowest concentrations recorded at that time.[^cite_note-\:14-45] An eruption the size of Mount Pinatubo affected the weather for a few years; with warmer winters and cooler summers observed.[^cite_note-\:14-45]

A similar phenomenon occurred in the April 1815, the eruption of [Mount Tambora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tambora) on [Sumbawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumbawa) island in [Indonesia](14579.md). This eruption is recognized as the most powerful eruption in recorded history.[^cite_note-\:05-35] Its eruption cloud lowered global temperatures as much as 0.4 to 0.7 °C (0.72 to 1.26 °F).[^cite_note-\:2-46] In the year following the eruption, most of the Northern Hemisphere experienced cooler temperatures during the summer. In the [northern hemisphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere), 1816 was known as the "[Year Without a Summer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year_Without_a_Summer)". The eruption caused crop failures, food shortages, and floods that killed over 100,000 people across Europe, Asia, and North America.[^cite_note-\:2-46]
