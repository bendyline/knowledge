The **prime minister of Hungary** ([Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language): *Magyarország miniszterelnöke*, lit. 'minister-president of Hungary') is the [head of government](37570.md) of [Hungary](13275.md). The [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister) and the [Cabinet](17048864.md) are collectively [accountable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accountability) for their policies and actions to the [National Assembly](585416.md), which is directly elected by universal suffrage at least once every four years. The [current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Hungary) holder of the office is [Péter Magyar](76453946.md), who has served since 9 May 2026.[^cite_note-4]

According to the [Fundamental Law of Hungary](5602839.md), the prime minister is nominated by the [president of Hungary](380393.md) and formally elected by the National Assembly. Constitutionally, the president is required to nominate the leader of the political party that wins a [majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority#Majority_vote) of seats in the National Assembly as prime minister.[^cite_note-Flaw-5] When there is no party with a majority, the president holds an audience with the leaders of all parliamentary parties and nominates the person most likely to command a majority in the Assembly; the Assembly then elects this person with a simple majority. Historically, when this situation has occurred, the prime minister has been the leader of the party that won a [plurality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_\(voting\)) of votes in the election or the leader of the governing coalition's senior partner party.&#x20;

Since 2026, the Fundamental Law limits the premiership to a maximum of eight years, equivalent to two parliamentary terms in office, regardless of consecution.[^cite_note-6]

## Official title

The title of Hungary's head of government in Hungarian is *miniszterelnök*. Translated, this means "[minister-president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister-president)". However, since "[prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_minister)" or "[premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier)" is the more usual title in a parliamentary system for a head of government in English-speaking nations, the title is translated as "prime minister" by most English sources.

## History

Further information: [List of prime ministers of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Hungary)



Portrait of [Count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count) [Lajos Batthyány](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lajos_Batthy%C3%A1ny) by [Miklós Barabás](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikl%C3%B3s_Barab%C3%A1s), 1848. He was appointed as Hungary's first Prime Minister.

The longest-serving prime minister is [Viktor Orbán](750582.md), who held the position from 6 July 1998 to 27 May 2002 and from 29 May 2010 to 9 May 2026. He surpassed [Kálmán Tisza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A1lm%C3%A1n_Tisza) as the longest-serving prime minister on 30 November 2020, with (if the years are counted cumulatively) a total of 20 years of service.

### Palatine of Hungary

Main article: [Palatine (Kingdom of Hungary)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palatine_\(Kingdom_of_Hungary\))

The **palatine** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *comes palatii, comes palatinus*, later *_palatinus (regni)_*, [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language): *nádorispán/nádor*, [Slovak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_language): *nádvorný župan / nádvorný špán*, later: *_palatín/nádvorník_*, [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Palatin*) was the highest dignitary in the [Kingdom of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Hungary) after the [king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch) (a kind of powerful [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister) and supreme judge) from the kingdom's rise up to 1848/1918.

Initially, he was the representative of the king; later, the vice-regent ([viceroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroy)). In the early centuries of the kingdom, he was appointed by the king, later elected by the [Diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_\(assembly\)) of the Kingdom of Hungary. After the [Habsburgs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg) solidified their hold of Hungary, the dignity became an appointed position once again. Finally, it became hereditary in a cadet (junior) branch of the Habsburg dynasty after [King Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) appointed his brother [Joseph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archduke_Joseph,_Palatine_of_Hungary).

### Creation of the position

Main article: [Hungarian Revolution of 1848](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Revolution_of_1848)

During the Hungarian Revolution of 1848, the revolutionaries wanted the creation of a Hungarian cabinet which would be independent from the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) and the Buda Chancellery (which was an office of the imperial governor-general). One of the [12 points](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_points_of_the_Hungarian_Revolutionaries_of_1848) said: *2. A responsible government in [Buda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buda)-[Pest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pest,_Hungary).*

[Ferdinand V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_I_of_Austria) appointed Count [Lajos Batthyány](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lajos_Batthy%C3%A1ny) for the position of prime minister of Hungary on 17 March 1848. The government was called *ministry*, different from the current acceptance. The ministries were called *departments*. The position was vacant after the defeat of the freedom fight.
