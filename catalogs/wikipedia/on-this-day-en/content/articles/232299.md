The **Society of Saint Pius X** (**SSPX**; [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Fraternitas Sacerdotalis Sancti Pii X*, [lit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literal_translation) 'Priestly Fraternity of Saint Pius X', **FSSPX**) is a [traditionalist Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholic) organization founded in 1970 by Archbishop [Marcel Lefebvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Lefebvre). Considered [schismatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schism_in_Christianity) by the [Catholic Church](606848.md), the society comprises [priests](8655005.md) and [seminarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary) and is associated with a worldwide network of chapels, schools, [religious communities](6896845.md), and [lay supporters](31488305.md). It is known for its opposition to several reforms associated with the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEsspx.org2018-5] Adherents to the SSPX are called **Lefebvrists**,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDouthat2009-6][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPongratz-Lippitt2021-7] **Lefebvrians**,[^cite_note-O'Connell-2026-8] or **Lefebvrites**,[^cite_note-9] terms SSPX rejects.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEdu_Chalard1993-10]

The [Holy See](13393.md) formally declared the SSPX and its adherents[^cite_note-SSPXlaity-12] to be in schism and [excommunicated](28394423.md) them [in 1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_%C3%89c%C3%B4ne_consecrations) and [in 2026](83588828.md), each time following [illicit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Validity_and_liceity) consecrations of bishops by the SSPX. The SSPX considers itself never to have been excommunicated nor to be or have been in schism.

Named after [Pope Pius X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_X) for his anti-[modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism_in_the_Catholic_Church) stance,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnittel2004-13] the society retains the [Tridentine Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tridentine_Mass) and pre-[Vatican II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vatican_II) [liturgical books](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgical_books_of_the_Roman_Rite) in [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin). It has more than 700 priests among 1,482 total members.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEsspx.org2022-4] Estimates of the number of laypeople who attend SSPX Masses vary significantly.

## History



Lefebvre, the society's founder, celebrating the [Traditional Latin Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Latin_Mass)

Like [Traditionalist Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditionalist_Catholicism) in general, SSPX was born out of an attachment to many elements that they sought to preserve despite the changes in the Catholic Church that followed the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) (1962–1965).[^cite_note-14] The founder and central figure of the society was the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_people) [Archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop) [Marcel Lefebvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Lefebvre), who had served the Catholic Church as [Apostolic Delegate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Delegate) for French-speaking Africa, [Archbishop of Dakar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Dakar), and Superior General of the [Congregation of the Holy Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_of_the_Holy_Spirit), a missionary order of priests.[^cite_note-15]

### Foundation of the Society

In September 1970, shortly after his retirement as Superior General of the Congregation of the Holy Spirit, Lefebvre was approached by eleven members of the [Pontifical French Seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_French_Seminary) in [Rome](25458.md), who asked him to help them find a seminary with a more traditional formation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWord_on_Fire2026-16] They sought Lefebvre's advice on a conservative seminary where they could complete their studies.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGagnon2003-17] He directed them to the [University of Fribourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Fribourg), in [Switzerland](26748.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBritannica2026-18]

In late 1970, Lefebvre was urged by the [abbot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbot) of [Hauterive Abbey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauterive_Abbey) and the [Dominican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Order) theologian [Marie-Dominique Philippe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie-Dominique_Philippe) to teach the seminarians personally but he felt he was too old to undertake such a large project, and told them he would visit [François Charrière](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran%C3%A7ois_Charri%C3%A8re), Bishop of [Lausanne, Geneva and Fribourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Lausanne,_Geneva_and_Fribourg), with a request to set up a religious society. He told them that if he agreed to go through with it, he would see in it a sign of [Divine Providence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divine_Providence). Charrière granted Lefebvre's request. With a document predated six days to 1 November 1970, he established the Pius Union under the official title of the Apostles of Jesus and Mary. It was also given the civil title, the Society of Saint Pius X..[^cite_note-19] It was a *pia unio* (Latin, for "pius union"/"[holy union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_the_Christian_faithful)") on a *ad experimentum* ("provisional"/"experimental") basis for six years.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFSSPX_District_of_Great_Britain2026-20] The Society of Saint Pius X was formally founded, adhered to all canonical norms, and received the episcopal blessing and encouragement of the [local ordinary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ordinary_\(Catholic_Church\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFSSPX_District_of_Great_Britain2026-20] Some Swiss laymen offered the [International Seminary of Saint Pius X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Seminary_of_Saint_Pius_X) at [Écône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89c%C3%B4ne) to the newly formed group, and in 1971 the first 24 candidates entered, followed by a further 32 in October 1972.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoly_Cross_Seminary2003-21]

### 1974: canonical visitation of the seminary, Lefebvre's declaration

On 11 November 1974, two apostolic visitors from the Holy See came to the Seminary of St. Pius X in Écône. The SPPX claims that the seminarians and staff at Écône were shocked by some alleged "scandalous opinions" expressed by the two priests, such as "the ordination of married men will soon be a normal thing, truth changes with the times, and the traditional conception of the [Resurrection of our Lord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resurrection_of_Jesus) is open to discussion". In response to this, in November 1974, Lefebvre wrote a declaration denouncing what he considered liberal trends "clearly evident" in the council and in the subsequent reforms.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELefebvre1974-22]

### Canonical suppression of the SSPX and its aftermath

On 24 January 1975, Bishop [Pierre Mamie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Mamie), Charrière's successor as bishop of Fribourg, petitioned the Sacred Congregation for Religious in Rome to suppress SSPX's status as a *pia unio*, even before the end of its six-year experimental period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWord_on_Fire2026-16] The reason given was the November 1974 declaration of Lefebvre.[^cite_note-23] The congregation's prefect, Cardinal Arturo Tabera, replied on 25 April 1975, stating that Mamie had the authority to withdraw his predecessor's approval and that the congregation agreed SSPX should be suppressed immediately.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWord_on_Fire2026-16] In the same period, Lefebvre was summoned to the Vatican, meeting with the cardinals on 13 February and 3 March.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHoly_Cross_Seminary2003-21]

On 6 May 1975, with the cardinals' approval, Bishop Mamie formally withdrew SSPX's pious union status.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWord_on_Fire2026-16] On the same day, a commission of cardinals that had interviewed Lefebvre in January issued a separate letter, approved by Pope Paul VI, detailing the conditions of the suppression.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWord_on_Fire2026-16]

SSPX continued to operate in spite its canonical suppression.

In the [consistory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_consistory) of 24 May 1976, [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) rebuked Archbishop Lefebvre by name, and appealed to him and his followers to change their minds.[^cite_note-24]

On 1 July 1976, the Press Office of the Holy See declared that following [canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canon_\(canon_law\)) 2373 of [the then-Code of Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_Code_of_Canon_Law), Lefebvre was automatically suspended for one year from conferring ordination and that those whom he had ordained were automatically suspended from exercising the order received. It was also announced that the Holy See was examining Lefebvre's disobedience to the Pope's orders.[^cite_note-25]

On 11 July 1976, Lefebvre signed a certificate of receipt of a letter from Cardinal [Sebastiano Baggio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebastiano_Baggio), Prefect of the [Congregation for Bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_Bishops), intimating that further penalties would be imposed per canon 2331 §1 of the then Code of Canon Law concerning obstinate disobedience to legitimate precepts or prohibitions of the Roman Pontiff. He was enjoined, within ten days of receipt of the letter, to take steps "to repair the scandal caused." In a letter of 17 July to Pope Paul VI, Lefebvre declared that he judged his action of 29 June to be legitimate. The Pope considered this response inadequate, and on his instructions, the Congregation for Bishops, on 22 July 1976, suspended Lefebvre for an indefinite time from all exercise of holy orders: he could not confer any of the Sacraments, save Reconciliation or Baptism in an emergency ([suspension *a divinis*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspension_a_divinis)).[^cite_note-26]

### Écône consecrations of 1988

Main article: [1988 Écône consecrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_%C3%89c%C3%B4ne_consecrations)

A central controversy surrounding SSPX concerns the [consecration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecration) by Archbishop Lefebvre and Brazilian bishop [Antônio de Castro Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B4nio_de_Castro_Mayer) of four SSPX priests as bishops in 1988, in violation of the orders of [Pope John Paul II](23805.md).

By 1987, Archbishop Lefebvre was 81. If he had died at that point, SSPX could have had their members ordained to the priesthood only at the hands of non-SSPX bishops, regarded by Lefebvre as unreliable and unorthodox. In June 1987, Lefebvre announced his intention to consecrate a successor to the episcopacy. He implied that he intended to do this with or without the approval of the [Holy See](13393.md).[^cite_note-27] In the Catholic Church, a bishop requires the mandate of the Pope if he is to consecrate a bishop,[^cite_note-28] and an unauthorized consecration automatically incurs [excommunication](28394423.md).[^cite_note-29]

Earlier, [Pope Pius XII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_XII), in his encyclical *[Ad Apostolorum principis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ad_Apostolorum_principis)*, had described the sacramental activity of bishops who had been consecrated without papal approval as "gravely illicit, that is, criminal and sacrilegious".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPope_Pius_XII195841-30]

On 5 May 1988, a skeleton agreement between Lefebvre and Cardinal Joseph Ratzinger, the prefect of the [Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Doctrine_of_the_Faith) (and future [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI)) was signed.[^cite_note-31]

On Pope John Paul II's instructions, Cardinal Ratzinger replied to Lefebvre on 27 May, insisting on the observance of the 5 May agreement and adding that, if Lefebvre carried out unauthorized consecrations on 30 June, the promised authorization for the ordination to the episcopacy would be withdrawn.[^cite_note-32]

On 2 June, Lefebvre wrote to Pope John Paul II that he intended to proceed with his consecrations.[^cite_note-33] On 9 June, the Pope replied with a personal letter, appealing to him to abandon a design that "would be seen as nothing other than a [schismatic act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schism_\(religion\)), the theological and canonical consequences of which are known to you".[^cite_note-34]

On 30 June 1988, Archbishop Lefebvre proceeded to ordain to the episcopate four priests of SSPX. [Antônio de Castro Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B4nio_de_Castro_Mayer), the retired [Bishop of Campos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Campos) in Brazil, assisted in the ceremony. Those consecrated as Bishops were: [Bernard Fellay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Fellay), [Bernard Tissier de Mallerais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Tissier_de_Mallerais), [Alfonso de Galarreta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_de_Galarreta), and [Richard Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Williamson).

&#x20;The following day, the [Congregation for Bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_Bishops) issued a decree declaring that Archbishop Lefebvre and the four newly ordained bishops had incurred the automatic canonical penalty of excommunication reserved to the Holy See.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECard._Gantin1988-35] On the following day, 2 July, Pope John Paul II issued an apostolic letter known as *[Ecclesia Dei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesia_Dei)* in which he condemned the Archbishop's action.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPope_John_Paul_II1988-36] The Pope stated that, since schism is defined in the [1983 Code of Canon Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_Code_of_Canon_Law) as "withdrawal of submission to the Supreme Pontiff or from communion with the members of the Church subject to him" (canon 751),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECode_of_Canon_Law1983a751-37] the consecration "constitute\[d] a schismatic act", and that, by virtue of canon 1382 of the Code,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECode_of_Canon_Law1983c1382-38] it entailed *[ipso facto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latae_sententiae)* excommunication for all the bishops involved.



[Veldhoven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veldhoven), Archbishop Lefebvre giving Communion assisted by [Franz Schmidberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Schmidberger)

Some members of SSPX disassociated themselves from the society as a result of Lefebvre's actions and, with the approval of the Holy See, formed a separate society called the [Priestly Fraternity of Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priestly_Fraternity_of_Saint_Peter).

Bishop [Antônio de Castro Mayer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B4nio_de_Castro_Mayer) resigned as [Bishop of Campos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Campos) on 20 August 1981; he later participated in the 1988 Écône consecrations, declaring "my presence here at this ceremony is a matter of conscience: It is the duty of a profession of the Catholic Faith before the entire Church."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEde_Castro_Mayer1988-39] After his retirement, Bishop Mayer founded the [Priestly Union of Saint John Mary Vianney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_Apostolic_Administration_of_Saint_John_Mary_Vianney) (SSJV). Under the leadership of his successor, Bishop [Licínio Rangel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lic%C3%ADnio_Rangel), the SSJV was reconstituted in January 2002 by Pope John Paul II as the [Personal Apostolic Administration of Saint John Mary Vianney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_Apostolic_Administration_of_Saint_John_Mary_Vianney), with the same territory as the [Diocese of Campos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Campos).

### Discussions with the Holy See (1988–2017)

Main article: [Dialogue between the Holy See and the Society of Saint Pius X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dialogue_between_the_Holy_See_and_the_Society_of_Saint_Pius_X)

Archbishop Lefebvre died in 1991.

At the express request of the 4 bishops consecrated in 1988, in 2009 the Holy See remitted the excommunications of the SSPX's bishops that it had declared at the time of the 1988 consecrations, and expressed the hope that all members of the society would follow this up by speedily returning to full communion with the church.[^cite_note-40]

Discussions since then have been complex, stemming from the society's insistence that the teachings of the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) on [ecumenism](146656.md), [religious liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_liberty), and [collegiality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiality) are inconsistent with Catholic teaching and doctrine, a claim that the Holy See views as unacceptable, but recent discussions have indicated the possibility of an understanding. In an interview on 4 March 2017 with DICI, the official news organ of the society, Bishop [Bernard Fellay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Fellay) stated:

> Whether it is a question of religious liberty, collegiality, ecumenism, the new Mass, or even the new rites of the sacraments \[...] And now all of a sudden, on these points that have been stumbling blocks, the emissaries from Rome tell us that they are open questions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEsspx.org2016-41]

In the same month of March 2017, Archbishop Guido Pozzo, the prelate in charge of the [Pontifical Commission *Ecclesia Dei*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Commission_Ecclesia_Dei), the [Roman Curia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Curia)'s organ for traditionalist societies, stated that the Holy See and the society were close to an agreement regularizing the society's status.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECatholic_Culture2017a-42] In a letter of the same month, it was announced that Pope Francis authorized diocesan bishops to grant to SSPX priests faculties to officiate at a marriage valid in the Catholic Church in cases where no priest in good standing could do so.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWells2017-43]

In July 2017 Bishop Fellay signed a document along with a number of other clergy and academics labelled as [a "Filial Correction" of Pope Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correctio_filialis_de_haeresibus_propagatis). In the twenty-five-page document, which was made public in September after receiving no reply from the Holy See, they criticized the Pope for allegedly promoting heresy through various words, actions and omissions during his pontificate.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAssociated_Press2017-44]

### Expulsion of Bishop Williamson

In August 2012, Bishop [Richard Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Williamson), one of those illicitly consecrated by Lefebvre, administered the sacrament of [confirmation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confirmation) to about 100 [laypeople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laity) at the Benedictine Monastery of the Holy Cross in [Nova Friburgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nova_Friburgo), Brazil, during a visit to the [State of Rio de Janeiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_Rio_de_Janeiro) which had not been authorized by the Society's local leadership. The Society's South American district superior, Christian Bouchacourt, protested against his action on the Society's website, saying that it was "a serious act against the virtue of obedience."[^cite_note-45]

In early October 2012, the leadership of the Society gave Williamson a deadline to declare his submission; instead, he published an "open letter" asking for the resignation of Bishop [Bernard Fellay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Fellay) as [Superior General of the Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_General_of_the_Society_of_Saint_Pius_X).[^cite_note-46] On 4 October 2012, the Society expelled Williamson in what it described as a "painful decision" citing the failures "to show respect and obedience deserved by his legitimate superiors".[^cite_note-47]

### Bishop Vitus Huonder

&#x20;In May 2019, SSPX announced that [Vitus Huonder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitus_Huonder), [Bishop emeritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishop_emeritus) of [Chur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese_of_Chur), Switzerland, as per a long-stated intention, had retired to one of the Society's houses in order "to dedicate himself to prayer and silence, to celebrate the traditional Mass exclusively, and to work for Tradition, the only way of renewing the Church." In 2015, Bishop Huonder was sent by then-prefect of the Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith [Gerhard Ludwig Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Ludwig_M%C3%BCller) to dialogue with SSPX. After four years Bishop Huonder was permitted by Pope Francis to go and live one of the Society's houses. He became a major supporter of the Society, reporting back positive things to Pope Francis and even producing a video titled "My journey to the SSPX – with Bishop Vitus Huonder" on YouTube. In that video, Huonder claimed that Pope Francis personally told him that SSPX is not in schism. The SSPX stated:

> The Society of Saint Pius X appreciates Bishop Huonder's courageous decision and rejoices to be able to provide him with the spiritual and priestly surroundings that he desires so deeply. May this example be followed by others, so as to "restore everything in Christ".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEsspx.org2019-48]

Bishop Huonder died on 3 April 2024 at the age of 81, following serious illness.[^cite_note-49] His funeral Mass on 17 April was celebrated by Bishop Fellay, and Huonder was buried at the [International Seminary of Saint Pius X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Seminary_of_Saint_Pius_X) in [Écône](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89c%C3%B4ne) next to the tomb of Archbishop [Marcel Lefebvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcel_Lefebvre). Huonder's successor, Bishop [Joseph Maria Bonnemain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Maria_Bonnemain), attended the funeral, but did not take part in the celebration, due to the irregular canonical situation of SSPX.[^cite_note-50]

### Death of Bishop Tissier de Mallerais

On 28 September 2024, Bishop [Bernard Tissier de Mallerais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Tissier_de_Mallerais), another of the Bishops illicitely consecrated by Lefebvre, fell on the stairs of the Écône seminary, causing a skull fracture and an internal haemorrhage; he was promptly brought to the Hospital at Martigny and placed into an induced coma. He died on 8 October 2024, after 10 days of coma; his funeral was held at the International Seminary of Saint Pius X on 18 October.[^cite_note-Lasserre-2024-51]

The deaths of Bishops Huonder and Tissier de Mallerais, coupled with the expulsion and later death of Bishop Williamson, left [Bernard Fellay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Fellay) and [Alfonso de Galarreta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_de_Galarreta) the only remaining bishops affiliated with SSPX, prompting Catholic media to speculate whether the Society would proceed to new consecrations.[^cite_note-Lasserre-2024-51] In November 2024, [Davide Pagliarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Pagliarani), General Superior of the Fraternity, explicitly mentioned this possibility for the future of FSSPX.[^cite_note-52]

### Écône consecrations of 2026

Main article: [2026 Écône consecrations](83588828.md)

On 2 February 2026, the Superior General announced their plans to proceed with consecrating bishops for the Society on 1 July 2026.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] The decision has been taken in harmony with the unanimous advice of his Council and has been publicly announced on occasion of the feast of the [Purification of the ever Blessed Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purification_of_the_Blessed_Virgin), during a ceremony he presided over at the International Seminary of Saint-Curé d'Ars in [Flavigny-sur-Ozerain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flavigny-sur-Ozerain), France.[^cite_note-55]

On 12 February 2026, [Davide Pagliarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Pagliarani) met with Cardinal [Víctor Manuel Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Manuel_Fern%C3%A1ndez), the head of the [Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Doctrine_of_the_Faith). The dicastery stated its openness to begin talks with SSPX, while warning against the planned consecrations.[^cite_note-56]

On 18 February 2026, the FSSPX published a letter addressed by Don Pagliarani to Cardinal Fernandez, in which 1 July was confirmed as the starting point for the new episcopal consecrations.[^cite_note-57]

On 13 May 2026 Cardinal [Víctor Manuel Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%ADctor_Manuel_Fern%C3%A1ndez), prefect of the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith, explicitly warned the Society that the planned episcopal consecrations in July would be considered an act of formal [schism](6288753.md), and result in the [excommunication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Excommunication) of the new bishops and all involved in the consecrations.[^cite_note-NCR-May13-58]

On 16 June 2026, [Pope Leo XIV](54520372.md) urged the Society not to proceed with the announced consecrations.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] On 30 June 2026, Pope Leo XIV addressed an open letter to the Superior General of the Fraternity in which he asked to renounce consecrations.[^cite_note-61]

On 1 July 2026, Bishop [Alfonso de Galarreta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_de_Galarreta), proceeded with the consecrations to the episcopate of the four aforementioned priests of the SSPX, with [Bernard Fellay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Fellay) acting as co-consecrator. This resulted in the automatic [excommunication](28394423.md) of the SSPX.[^cite_note-O'Connell-2026-8] The Holy See confirmed the excommunication of the entire society[^cite_note-SSPXlaity-12] the following day, and the [Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Doctrine_of_the_Faith) rescinded the confession and marriage faculties previously granted to SSPX priests, on the grounds that schismatics lack the jurisdiction necessary for such sacraments to be valid.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63] The 2017 provisions granted SSPX priests this faculty and delegation; the 2026 decree withdrew them.[^cite_note-\:0-64][^cite_note-decree+explanatory_note-67]

## Canonical situation

Main article: [Canonical situation of the Society of Saint Pius X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canonical_situation_of_the_Society_of_Saint_Pius_X)



[Saint-Nicolas-du-Chardonnet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Nicolas-du-Chardonnet), Paris, occupied by SSPX since 1977



[St. Willibrord's Church, Utrecht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Willibrord's_Church,_Utrecht), Netherlands, operated by SSPX since 2015



[Minoritenkirche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minoritenkirche_\(Vienna\)), Vienna, Austria, received by SSPX as a gift in 2021

SSPX was founded as a Pious Union in 1970, but was suppressed in 1975 by [Bishop Pierre Mamie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Mamie) with the approval of the Roman Curia. In 1988, [Pope John Paul II](23805.md) excommunicated Lefebvre and the four bishops consecrated by him, as well as "those who adhered to the schism". In 1996, the [Pontifical Council for Legislative Texts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Council_for_Legislative_Texts) declared that "the whole Lefebvrian movement was to be considered schismatic".

In 2009, [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) remitted the excommunications of the four bishops consecrated by Lefebvre, but reiterated that SSPX had no canonical status.

In 2026, the whole fraternity was declared schismatic by the [Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Doctrine_of_the_Faith), excommunicating all clergy associated with the society and forbidding them from licitly celebrating any sacraments.[^cite_note-\:0-64][^cite_note-decree+explanatory_note-67]

### Marriage

On 27 March 2017, the [Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Doctrine_of_the_Faith) communicated that Pope Francis granted local ordinaries the right to give to a priest in good standing the faculty to preside at the marriage of the society's followers, immediately after which they will participate in a Mass celebrated by an SSPX priest, or, if no priest in good standing can receive the couple's consent, to give the faculty instead to an SSPX priest. This faculty was revoked by the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith after the illicit consecrations of four bishops in 2026.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEsteves2017-68]

### Confession

On 20 November 2016, Pope Francis personally and indefinitely extended an allowance he created during the Holy Year of 2015[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPope_Francis2015-69] for penitents confessing to priests affiliated with SSPX:

> For the Jubilee Year I had also granted that those faithful who, for various reasons, attend churches officiated by the priests of the Priestly Fraternity of Saint Pius X, can validly and licitly receive the sacramental absolution of their sins.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPope_Francis2016-70][^cite_note-FOOTNOTECNA2016-71]

As with the faculties to validly witness marriages, this was revoked by the Dicastery for the Doctrine of the Faith in July 2026.

### Denying the validity of the suppression and the excommunications

The SSPX considers that its suppression as a canonical organization by the Catholic Church is null and void.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

The SSPX also considers its 1988[^cite_note-75] and 2026[^cite_note-76] excommunications as null and void.

The SSPX considers itself not to be in schism with the Catholic Church.[^cite_note-77]

## Organisational structure



A liturgy held at the society's St. Jude's Chapel, Philadelphia, U.S.

As of January 2026, the society had 2 bishops and 733 priests residing in 77 countries, 760 Mass centers, 159 priories, 144 religious brothers, 88 sister oblates, 264 [seminarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminarian) in five [seminaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminaries).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFSSPX_News2026-1]

In 2025, the Congregation of the Sisters of the Society Saint Pius X numbered 245 professed sisters in 30 communities throughout 10 countries: France, Belgium, Switzerland, Germany, Italy, the United States, Argentina, Gabon, the Dominican Republic, and Australia.[^cite_note-78]

The society is divided into two classes of territorial units called districts and autonomous houses, each headed by a superior. Over 120 (>20%) of the society's priests are stationed in the District of France.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELPL2021-80]

### Laity

Properly speaking, no members of the laity belong to the SSPX. Indeed, as a priestly fraternity, its members are limited to priests, religious, and seminarians.[^cite_note-Coppen-2026-82]

It is difficult to estimate how many laity attend Mass regularly at the SSPX chapels; the SSPX itself does not have worldwide attendance counts.[^cite_note-Coppen-2026-82]

Cardinal [Darío Castrillón Hoyos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dar%C3%ADo_Castrill%C3%B3n_Hoyos), at the time president of the [Pontifical Commission *Ecclesia Dei*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Commission_Ecclesia_Dei), estimated in 2007 that the number who attended SSPX chapels was around 600,000; this is the number that the SSPX cites, and which many news sources, such as the [BBC](19344654.md), republish.[^cite_note-Coppen-2026-82] However, *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* estimates closer to 150,000 to 200,000.[^cite_note-Coppen-2026-82][^cite_note-Giuffrida-2026-83]

Catholic news publisher *[The Pillar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pillar)* reported that traditionalist blog *Rorate Caeli* estimated the number to be much lower, around 100,000. It arrived at this number by taking the SSPX's published attendance of 25,000 in its District of the USA, its largest district, comparing it to the similarly-sized District of France, and estimating the remaining districts to number only several tens of thousands.[^cite_note-Coppen-2026-82] French Catholic magazine *[La Nef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Nef_\(revue\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)* estimated the attendance in the French district to be 35,000 in 2021.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-Coppen-2026-82] Using that number and erring on the side of generosity to assume 20,000 for each of the other thirteen districts, *The Pillar* estimated that the total worldwide attendance of SSPX could not exceed 320,000.[^cite_note-Coppen-2026-82]

## Splits from the SSPX

The group has a long history of numerous splits and defections. There have been two major kinds of splits from the SSPX. Two notable splits of the first kind involved priests who viewed SSPX as too liberal and who use the form that the Mass had before [Pope John XXIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_XXIII). The other kind involved groups who have reconciled with the Holy See and who, like SSPX, use the 1962 edition of the Roman Missal.

Groups which broke with the SSPX which are not recognized by the Holy See include:

- [Society of Saint Pius V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Saint_Pius_V): in 1983, nine U.S. SSPX priests broke with or were forced to leave SSPX's Northeast USA District partly because they were opposed to Lefebvre's instructions that Mass be celebrated according to the 1962 edition of the Roman Missal issued by Pope John XXIII.

- [Istituto Mater Boni Consilii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Istituto_Mater_Boni_Consilii) (English: "Institute of the Mother of Good Counsel"), a traditionalist congregation of priests that follows the [sedeprivationist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedeprivationist) school of thought. The founders of the institute seceded in 1985 from the Society of St. Pius X under the leadership of Fr. Francesco Ricossa, a one-time faculty member of the Écône seminary. In contrast to the North American-based SSPV, this Institute is based in Europe.

- [SSPX Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSPX_Resistance) and Priestly Union Marcel Lefebvre: a traditionalist Catholic apostolic association founded on 15 July 2014 by Bishop Richard Williamson after his expulsion from the Priestly Fraternity of Saint Pius X.

- [Sons of the Most Holy Redeemer](2514235.md) (Filii Sanctissimi Redemptoris, FSSR): founded as a religious community affiliated with SSPX in 1988. In 2008 the community petitioned to be reconciled with the Holy See, which was accepted by then Pope Benedict XVI. The Bishop of Christchurch on 13 July 2024 disallowed priests of the Sons of the Most Holy Redeemer to minister in the Diocese of Christchurch.

  [^cite_note-85]

  &#x20;In May 2026, the religious community published a document, signed by all its members, in which they rejected the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council) and declared the [Sedevancantist](27930.md) belief.

  [^cite_note-86]

Groups which broke with the SSPX but reconciled with Rome include:

- [Priestly Fraternity of Saint Peter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priestly_Fraternity_of_Saint_Peter) (FSSP): established in 1988 after the Écône consecrations. Responding to the Holy See's declaration that these constituted a schismatic act and that those involved were thereby automatically excommunicated, twelve priests left SSPX and established the FSSP, in full communion with the Holy See.

  [^cite_note-87]

- [Institute of the Good Shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_the_Good_Shepherd) (*Institut du Bon-Pasteur*, IBP), established as a papally-recognized society of apostolic life on 8 September 2006 for a group of the SSPX members who maintained it was time for the society to accept reconciliation with Pope Benedict XVI.

  [^cite_note-88]

## Lifestyle and dress code

In the Society of Saint Pius X, a [complementarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Complementarian) position for gender roles is upheld; "In [St. Mary's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Marys,_Kansas), few married women work, especially once they have children."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGreen2020-89]

[Richard Williamson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Williamson), a now deceased former bishop of the SSPX who later went on to align with the [SSPX Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSPX_Resistance), wrote a pastoral letter, in which he stated that "women's trousers, as worn today, short or long, modest or immodest, tight or loose, open or disguised (like the '[culottes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culottes)'), are an assault upon woman's womanhood and so they represent a deep-lying revolt against the order willed by God."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWilliamson1991-90][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBarber2009-91] *[The Atlantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Atlantic)*, in covering SSPX, described their female adherents as "women in long, modest skirts loading vans that had enough seats to accommodate eight or nine kids."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGreen2020-89]

During [Catholic Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Mass), though there is no strict dress code, the SSPX advises "[Sunday best](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_clothing#Clothing_worn_during_religious_services)" attire.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEsspx.org2023-92] Women are recommended to wear skirts that fall at least below the knee, and no tight-fitting clothing.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEsspx.org2023-92] It is customary for women to wear a [veil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_head_covering) during prayer and worship.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEsspx.org2023-92] Men are encouraged to wear suits and ties.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEsspx.org2023-92]

## Controversies

Main article: [Controversies surrounding the Society of Saint Pius X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controversies_surrounding_the_Society_of_Saint_Pius_X)

### Political

After Bishop Richard Williamson, the subject of the complaint by the [Anti-Defamation League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Defamation_League), denied the use of [Nazi gas chambers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_chamber) to massacre Jews in a 2009 interview, the superior-general of the society said that if he repeated his denial, he would be expelled.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECatholic_Culture2009-98] His expulsion in 2012 was for refusing to show due respect and obedience to the SSPX authorities and calling on the superior-general to resign.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECatholic_Culture2012-99] A German court convicted Williamson of [Holocaust denial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocaust_denial).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAssociated_Press2013-100]

French [Nazi collaborator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_collaborator) and [war criminal](48626.md) [Paul Touvier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Touvier) was arrested in an SSPX priory. The priory's superiors claimed that they had granted him asylum as "an act of charity to a homeless man". They claimed no knowledge about the man's background when he first appeared in the priory. On his death, in 1996, an SSPX [Requiem Mass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Requiem_Mass) was offered for his soul, upon his request.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFox2009-101]

On 16 October 2013, the society offered to perform a funeral for Nazi war criminal [Erich Priebke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erich_Priebke). Priebke, a baptized Protestant, converted post-war with his wife to a form of Catholicism and had his children baptized. He rejected the cult of race as a "mistake that led down a path of no return."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPriebkeGiachini2003150,_160–161,_170-102] The ceremony did not take place due to protests by some 500 people outside the society's Italian district house in [Albano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albano_Laziale), near Rome. The local authorities of the Catholic Church refused him a public funeral, citing a rule of canon law that, unless they gave some signs of repentance before death, a public funeral must be refused to manifest sinners to whom it cannot be granted without public scandal of the faithful.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWooden2013-103]

Despite controversies surrounding Nazism, the society mentioned that Archbishop Lefebvre's father, René Lefebvre, met his death in the [concentration camp at Sonnenburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonnenburg_concentration_camp) in February 1944, three years after his arrest by the [Gestapo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestapo); he died, "his rosary in hand, a victim of Nazi insanity."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEfsspx.org2024-104]

The SSPX is also known for supporting the [far-right](221220.md), [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_nationalism), ultra-conservative political party [Civitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civitas_\(movement\)) in [France](5843419.md) (dissolved in 2023).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESchreiber2019-105][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBunderson2012-106]

### Sexual abuse allegations

On 5 April 2017, *[Uppdrag granskning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uppdrag_granskning)*, a Swedish television program, alleged that four members of SSPX (three priests and a former seminarian) had molested at least a dozen young people in several countries.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECatholic_Culture2017b-107][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFeganSvenssonAndersson2017a-108][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFeganSvenssonAndersson2017b-109] The program also stated that evidence of abuse was kept secret by SSPX and that the priests were allowed to continue in ministry.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECatholic_Culture2017b-107][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMartinierePérisseGastaldiFegan2017-110]

Kevin Gerard Sloniker, the former seminarian and the only person accused by name in the program, was expelled from the society's St. Thomas Aquinas Seminary in 2005. In 2016, Sloniker pled guilty for molesting seven boys with ages ranging from 8 to 14 over a period of about 10 years. He was sentenced to [life imprisonment](15549479.md) with the possibility of parole after 35 years.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKramer2016-111] The remaining three accusations regard priests whose names have been withheld (referred to in the program as Fathers P, S, and M); their accusers have likewise remained anonymous. Nonetheless, P was the subject of a canonical trial presided over by Bishop Fellay, authorized by the [Congregation for the Doctrine of the Faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congregation_for_the_Doctrine_of_the_Faith) in 2013; he was found guilty, and subsequently ordered to retire to a monastery.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESan_Martín2017-112] *Crux* reports that "P refused to go and, according to officials of SSPX, joined \[Bishop] [Williamson's Resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SSPX_Resistance)."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESan_Martín2017-112]

In May 2020, the Kansas Bureau of Investigation stated that, as part of its investigation into the four Catholic dioceses in the state, it was also investigating accusations that SSPX members were either perpetrating or covering up clerical sex abuse in the state.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHadro2020-113] SSPX St. Mary's Rectory in Kansas faced sex abuse allegations, although both the U.S. district of the society and the St. Mary's Rectory denied the allegations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEThomas2020-114]
