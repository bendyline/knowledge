**Smartwings, a.s.** (formerly *Travel Service, a.s.*)[^cite_note-register-2] is a [Czech](5321.md) [airline](1942.md) with its head office on the property of [Václav Havel Airport Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel_Airport_Prague).[^cite_note-5] It is the biggest airline in the [Czech Republic](5321.md), and it operates scheduled and chartered flights, mainly to leisure destinations. The airline also provides aircraft on [wet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_lease) and [dry leases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dry_lease) to other airlines, and operates charter flights through several subsidiaries: [Smartwings Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartwings_Poland), [Smartwings Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartwings_Hungary) and [Smartwings Slovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartwings_Slovakia).

Although Smartwings previously acquired [Czech Airlines](335703.md) in 2017,[^cite_note-6] following the latter's bankruptcy and a major corporate restructuring in October 2024, Czech Airlines was transformed into a holding company, and Smartwings became its wholly owned operating subsidiary.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-justice1-4] In December 2025, it was announced that [Pegasus Airlines](1597891.md) had reached an agreement to acquire the holding company.[^cite_note-Pegasus-8]

## History

### Foundation and early years



A Travel Service [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_Next_Generation) in 2013



A Travel Service [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_Next_Generation) promoting Prague as destination

Smartwings was founded in September 1997 under the name **Travel Service Airlines a.s.** and focused on charter flights mainly for Czech tour operators. The first airliner was a [Tupolev Tu-154M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-154) but in 2000, the air carrier received its first [Boeing 737-400](15103676.md) with which own operations were started.

In 2004, Travel Service launched the brand *Smart Wings* for low-cost flights. Travel Service leased two [Boeing 737-500s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-500) from [Lufthansa](97733.md) which were primary intended for Smartwings flights. Both aircraft were painted in the new Smartwings livery. Former Czech president [Václav Klaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Klaus) attended the opening ceremony, which was held on May 1, 2004.[^cite_note-9] In 2007, the company carried 2.2 million passengers, and a year later about 2.3 million passengers. In 2014, it carried 4.3 million passengers, about 1.2 of them on regular flights under the brand name Smartwings.[^cite_note-10]



A Travel Service [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_Next_Generation) in repainted [O2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O2) livery, with Travel Service titles attached

On 18 September 2007, the [Icelandair Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icelandair_Group) acquired 50% stake in the carrier, and purchased further shares to bring its holding up to 80% by April 2008. In December 2008, it reduced its holding to 66% by selling shares to the other shareholders, Unimex.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] In 2009, their stake was further diluted to 50.1% through a new share issue; they also sold a portion of their stake to fellow owners, reducing their holding to 30%.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In 2009, Icelandair spun its 30% stake in Travel Service into a new company, which was taken over by Icelandair's creditors. Chinese investment group [CEFC China Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CEFC_China_Energy) then held a 49.9% stake in Travel Service.[^cite_note-15]

In 2013, Travel Service cancelled its order for a Boeing 787 Dreamliner and purchased a [Boeing 737 MAX 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_MAX_8) instead.

### Development since 2017



A [Boeing 737-500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-500) in special Smartwings 2004 colors

Travel Service owned 98 percent of [Czech Airlines](335703.md); it bought 64% from Prisko and [Korean Air](315411.md) in 2017. Before, it owned 34%, bought in 2014. After Czech Airlines went through business restructuring, Smartwings retained 30% of the company.[^cite_note-16]

In October 2017, it was announced that Travel Service planned to transfer its branding from an airline to a holding company, and would move all of its operations under the Smartwings brand.[^cite_note-17] In 2018, it was announced that the Travel Service [livery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_livery) would be replaced by the Smartwings livery.[^cite_note-18] Also in that year, deliveries of the new [Boeing 737 MAX](32924090.md) began.

&#x20;Travel Service was renamed **Smartwings**, the name of its former low-cost subsidiary, on December 10, 2018.[^cite_note-19]



Headquarters in [Prague](23844.md)

In March 2019, Smartwings announced plans to create a [German](11867.md) subsidiary by late 2019, and to transition its [Czech Airlines](335703.md) subsidiary to an all-[Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) fleet.[^cite_note-aanet-20] However, these plans have not materialized, and Czech Airlines maintains its [Airbus](26220236.md) fleet.[^cite_note-21] In February 2021, Smartwings announced the return of their stored [Boeing 737 MAX](32924090.md) to service by the end of the month, the first European airline to resume operations following the flying ban lasting over 20 months due to the COVID-19 pandemic.[^cite_note-737maxreturn-22]

In February 2024, then owner CITIC – which bought its shares from CEFC – sold all of its shares in Smartwings to Czech-owned Prague City Air, which also owns the majority in [Czech Airlines](335703.md).[^cite_note-2024ownership-23] In October 2024, Smartwings took over all flight operations of Czech Airlines, continuing to operate them under the Czech Airlines brand, while Czech Airlines was restructured as a [holding company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holding_company).[^cite_note-24]

In December 2025, it was announced the [Turkish](11125639.md) low-cost airline, [Pegasus Airlines](1597891.md) has signed an agreement to acquire both Czech Airlines and Smartwings in a deal worth €154 million (3,8 billion CZK).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Incidents

- On 22 August 2019, a Smartwings [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737-800) (Registration: OK-TVO) operated flight QS-1125 departed from [Samos, Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samos_International_Airport) to [Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A1clav_Havel_Airport_Prague), [Czech Republic](5321.md) with 170 passengers on board. The aircraft was flying at 36,000 feet (11,000 m) over the [Aegean Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegean_Sea) about 100 nautical miles (120 mi; 190 km) northeast of Athens, Greece when it had an engine failure. The crew drifted the aircraft down to 24,000 feet (7,300 m) without informing ATC of the emergency, only informing them of a "technical malfunction". Instead of landing at the nearest suitable airport, as per engine failure procedure, the flight crew carried on to Prague at that level for a landing without further incident about 2 hours 20 minutes later. The Czech Civil Aviation Authority (CAA) confirmed that an investigation had been launched into the incident: "we have decided to launch an investigation as the procedure followed doesn't seem standard."

  [^cite_note-42]

  [^cite_note-43]

  &#x20;Following the incident, Prague police launched an investigation into the flight on suspicion of endangering the public due to negligence, and a Smartwings internal investigation demoted the captain from his post as head of flight operations.

  [^cite_note-AirlineGeeks-44]

  &#x20;In July 2020, the CAA released the final report by the [Air Accidents Investigation Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Accidents_Investigation_Institute) (AAII), which blamed the incident on the captain failing to follow mandatory procedures, including landing at the nearest possible diversion airport after an engine failure.

  [^cite_note-AirlineGeeks-44]

  [^cite_note-45]

  &#x20;As of July 2020, the captain who piloted the plane during the incident continued to pilot Smartwings flights.

  [^cite_note-46]
