**Wim Wenders**[^cite_note-spotify-1] (German: [\[ˈvɪm ˈvɛndɐs\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German); born **Wilhelm Ernst Wenders**; 14 August 1945[^cite_note-spotify-1]) is a German filmmaker and photographer. Regarded as a major figure of [New German Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_German_Cinema)[^cite_note-2] and widely considered an [auteur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auteur) director,[^cite_note-3] he has received various accolades, including a [BAFTA Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award), a [Palme d'Or](612946.md), a [Golden Lion](2386128.md), and an [Honorary Golden Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Golden_Bear), in addition to nominations for four [Academy Awards](324.md), an [Emmy Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emmy_Award), and a [Grammy Award](44635.md).

Wenders made his feature film debut with *[Summer in the City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_in_the_City_\(film\))* (1970). He earned critical acclaim for directing the films *[Alice in the Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_in_the_Cities)* (1974), *[The Wrong Move](8863374.md)* (1975), and *[Kings of the Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_of_the_Road)* (1976), later known as the *[Road Movie trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_Movie_trilogy)*. Wenders won the [BAFTA Award for Best Direction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAFTA_Award_for_Best_Direction) and the [Palme d'Or](612946.md) for *[Paris, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris,_Texas_\(film\))* (1984) and the [Cannes Film Festival Best Director Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Best_Director_Award_\(Cannes_Film_Festival\)) for *[Wings of Desire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wings_of_Desire)* (1987). His other notable films include *[The American Friend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Friend)* (1977), *[Faraway, So Close!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraway,_So_Close!)* (1993), and *[Perfect Days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfect_Days)* (2023).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Wenders has received three nominations for the [Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Documentary_Feature): for *[Buena Vista Social Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buena_Vista_Social_Club_\(film\))* (1999), *[Pina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pina_\(film\))* (2011), and *[The Salt of the Earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Salt_of_the_Earth_\(2014_film\))* (2014). He received a nomination for the [Grammy Award for Best Long Form Music Video](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grammy_Award_for_Best_Long_Form_Music_Video) for *[Willie Nelson at the Teatro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson_at_the_Teatro)* (1998). He is also known for directing the documentaries *[Tokyo-Ga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokyo-Ga)* (1985), *[The Soul of a Man](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Soul_of_a_Man)* (2003), *[Pope Francis: A Man of His Word](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Francis:_A_Man_of_His_Word)* (2018), and *[Anselm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anselm_\(film\))* (2023).

Wenders formerly served as the president of the [European Film Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Film_Academy) from 1996–2020. He also earned an [Honorary Golden Bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honorary_Golden_Bear) in 2015. He is an active photographer, emphasizing images of desolate landscapes.[^cite_note-theguardian1-6][^cite_note-telegraph-7]

## Early life and education

Wenders was born in [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf) into a traditionally Catholic family. His father, Heinrich Wenders, was a surgeon. The Dutch name "Wim" is a shortened version of the baptismal name "Wilhelm". As a boy, Wenders took unaccompanied trips to [Amsterdam](844.md) to visit the [Rijksmuseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rijksmuseum). He graduated from high school in [Oberhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberhausen) in the [Ruhr area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruhr_area). He then studied medicine at the [University of Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Freiburg) (1963–64) and philosophy at the [University of Dusseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Dusseldorf) (1964–65), but dropped out and moved to Paris in October 1966 in order to become a painter.[^cite_note-Biography-8] He failed his entry test at France's national film school, [IDHEC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IDHEC) (now [La Fémis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_F%C3%A9mis)), and instead became an engraver at [Johnny Friedlaender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Friedlaender)'s studio in [Montparnasse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montparnasse).[^cite_note-Biography-8] During this time he became fascinated with cinema, and saw up to five movies a day at the local movie theater.

Set on making his obsession his life's work, he returned to Germany in 1967 to work in the Düsseldorf office of [United Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Artists). That fall, he entered the [University of Television and Film Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Television_and_Film_Munich) (HFF).[^cite_note-Biography-8] Between 1967 and 1970, while at the HFF, he also worked as a film critic for *FilmKritik*, the Munich daily newspaper *[Süddeutsche Zeitung](30873120.md)*, *[Twen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twen)* magazine, and *[Der Spiegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Spiegel)*.[^cite_note-Biography-8]

Wenders completed several short films before graduating from the Hochschule with a [16mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16mm) black-and-white film, *[Summer in the City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_in_the_City_\(film\))* (1970), his feature directorial debut.

## Career

### 1970–1976: Film debut and early work

Wenders's career began in the late 1960s, the [New German Cinema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_German_Cinema) era.[^cite_note-9] Much of the distinctive [cinematography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinematography) in his movies is the result of a long-term collaboration with Dutch cinematographer [Robby Müller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robby_M%C3%BCller).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Wenders made his directorial film debut with *[Summer in the City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_in_the_City_\(film\))* (1970), his graduation project at the [University of Television and Film Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Television_and_Film_Munich), which he attended from 1967 to 1970. Shot in [16 mm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16_mm) black-and-white by Müller, the movie exhibited many of Wenders's later trademark themes of aimless searching, running from invisible demons, and persistent wandering toward an indeterminate goal. Protagonist Hans (Zischler) is released from prison, and after searching through seedy [West German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_German) streets and bars, he visits an old friend in Berlin.

Wenders then directed *[The Goalkeeper's Fear of the Penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Goalkeeper's_Fear_of_the_Penalty)*, titled *The Goalie's Anxiety at the Penalty Kick* in the United States.[^cite_note-14] The film was adapted from [Peter Handke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Handke)'s [1970 short novel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Goalie's_Anxiety_at_the_Penalty_Kick).[^cite_note-15] He then directed the period drama *[The Scarlet Letter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scarlet_Letter_\(1973_film\))* (1973),[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] adapted from [Nathaniel Hawthorne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nathaniel_Hawthorne)'s [1850 novel of the same name](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Scarlet_Letter). From 1974 to 1976 Wender directed the [Road Movie trilogy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_Movie_trilogy). The first film in the trilogy was *[Alice in the Cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alice_in_the_Cities)* (1974), which was shot in 16mm. The last two films are *[The Wrong Move](8863374.md)* (1975) and *[Kings of the Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kings_of_the_Road)* (1976),[^cite_note-18] the latter of which won the [FIPRESCI Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIPRESCI_Prize) at the [1976 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Cannes_Film_Festival).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

### 1977–1987: Breakthrough and acclaim



Wenders with [Carrie Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrie_Fisher) in 1978

In 1977 Wenders gained prominence for directing the neo-noir *[The American Friend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_American_Friend)*, starring [Dennis Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Hopper) and [Bruno Ganz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Ganz). The film is adapted from the [Patricia Highsmith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patricia_Highsmith) 1974 novel *[Ripley's Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ripley's_Game)*. J. Hoberman of *[The New York Times](30680.md)* has compared the film to [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese)'s *[Taxi Driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taxi_Driver)*, writing, "Like *Taxi Driver*, *The American Friend* was a new sort of movie-movie—sleekly brooding, voluptuously alienated and saturated with cinephilia."[^cite_note-21]

Wenders earned critical acclaim for his road drama *[Paris, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris,_Texas_\(film\))* (1984), starring [Harry Dean Stanton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Dean_Stanton), [Nastassja Kinski](21873.md) and [Dean Stockwell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Stockwell). The film premiered at the [1984 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Cannes_Film_Festival), where it won the [Palme d'Or](612946.md). Critic [Roger Ebert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Ebert) wrote of the film, "\[it's] a movie with the kind of passion and willingness to experiment that was more common fifteen years ago than it is now. It has more links with films like *[Five Easy Pieces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five_Easy_Pieces)* and *[Easy Rider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easy_Rider)* and *[Midnight Cowboy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midnight_Cowboy)* than with the slick arcade games that are the box-office winners of the 1980s. It is true, deep, and brilliant".[^cite_note-22]

Wenders then directed the romance fantasy *[Wings of Desire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wings_of_Desire)* (1987), starring [Bruno Ganz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Ganz) and [Peter Falk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Falk). It premiered at the [1987 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Cannes_Film_Festival), where Wenders won the [Cannes Film Festival Award for Best Director](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannes_Film_Festival_Award_for_Best_Director). [Peter Handke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Handke) co-wrote the screenplay. West Germany submitted *Wings of Desire* for consideration for the [Academy Award for Best Foreign Language Film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Foreign_Language_Film), a bid supported by its distribution company. It was not nominated; the academy seldom recognized West German cinema.[^cite_note-23] The film was one of the most acclaimed films of the year, with many critics adding it on their top 10 lists.

### 1991–2010: Career fluctuations



Wenders at the [2002 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Cannes_Film_Festival)

In 1991 Wenders directed the science fiction adventure drama *[Until the End of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Until_the_End_of_the_World)*, starring [William Hurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hurt), [Solveig Dommartin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solveig_Dommartin), [Max Von Sydow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Von_Sydow) and [Jeanne Moreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeanne_Moreau). The film has been released in several editions, ranging in length from 158 to 287 minutes, with the longer versions receiving mixed reviews. In 1993 he directed *[Faraway, So Close!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faraway,_So_Close!)*, a sequel to *Wings of Desire*. Actors [Otto Sander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_Sander), [Bruno Ganz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Ganz) and [Peter Falk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Falk) reprised their roles as angels who have become human. The film also stars [Nastassja Kinski](21873.md), [Willem Dafoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willem_Dafoe) and [Heinz Rühmann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinz_R%C3%BChmann), in his last film role. It received critical acclaim, premiering at the [1993 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_Cannes_Film_Festival), where it earned the [Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_\(Cannes_Film_Festival\)). The next year, he directed *[Lisbon Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_Story_\(1994_film\))*, which screened at [Un Certain Regard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Un_Certain_Regard) at the [1994 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Cannes_Film_Festival). In 1995 he directed both *[A Trick of Light](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Trick_of_Light)* and the anthology film *[Lumière and Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumi%C3%A8re_and_Company)*.

In 1997, Wenders directed the American drama film *[The End of Violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_End_of_Violence)*, starring [Bill Pullman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Pullman), [Andie MacDowell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andie_MacDowell), and [Gabriel Byrne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Byrne). The film received negative reviews and performed poorly at the box office after its debut at the [1997 Cannes Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Cannes_Film_Festival). Like many other of Wenders's American movies, it was shot in multiple locations, including the [Griffith Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffith_Observatory) and the [Santa Monica Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica_Pier). Wenders has directed several highly acclaimed documentaries, including *[Willie Nelson at the Teatro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Nelson_at_the_Teatro)*, a documentary about the recording sessions of *[Teatro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_\(Willie_Nelson_album\))* (1998). The next year he directed *[Buena Vista Social Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buena_Vista_Social_Club_\(film\))*, about the [music of Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Cuba). It was nominated for the [Academy Award for Best Documentary Feature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Academy_Award_for_Best_Documentary_Feature). In 2002, he directed a documentary about the German rock group [BAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAP_\(German_band\)) called *_Viel passiert_* (A lot has happened).

### 2011–present: Resurgence with documentaries



Wenders in 2008

Wenders has directed music videos for groups such as [U2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U2) and [Talking Heads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talking_Heads), including "[Stay (Faraway, So Close!)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stay_\(Faraway,_So_Close!\))" and "[Sax and Violins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sax_and_Violins)".[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] His television commercials include a UK advertisement for [Carling Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carling_Premier) Canadian beer.[^cite_note-26] Wenders's book *Emotion Pictures*, a collection of diary essays written as a film student, was adapted and broadcast as a series of plays on [BBC Radio 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_3), featuring Peter Capaldi as Wenders, with [Gina McKee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gina_McKee), [Saskia Reeves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saskia_Reeves), [Dennis Hopper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Hopper), [Harry Dean Stanton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Dean_Stanton) and [Ricky Tomlinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricky_Tomlinson), dramatized by Neil Cargill.

Wenders also directed a documentary-style film on the [Skladanowsky brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Max_Skladanowsky), known in English as *A Trick of the Light*.[^cite_note-27] The Skladanowsky brothers were inventing "moving pictures" when several others like the [Lumière brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumi%C3%A8re_brothers) and [William Friese-Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Friese-Greene) were doing the same. In 2011, Wenders was selected to stage the 2013 cycle of [Richard Wagner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Wagner)'s *[Der Ring des Nibelungen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Ring_des_Nibelungen)* at the [Bayreuth Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayreuth_Festival).[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29] The project fell through when he insisted on filming in [3-D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stereoscopy), which the Wagner family found too costly and disruptive.[^cite_note-30] In 2012, while promoting his 3-D dance film *[Pina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pina_\(film\))*, Wenders told the [Documentary channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DOC:_The_Documentary_Channel) blog that he had begun work on a new 3-D documentary about architecture.[^cite_note-31] He also said he would only work in 3-D from then on.[^cite_note-32] Wenders had admired the dance choreographer [Pina Bausch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pina_Bausch) since 1985, but only with the advent of digital 3-D cinema did he decide that he could sufficiently capture her work on screen.[^cite_note-33]

In 2015, Wenders collaborated with artist/journalist and longtime friend [Melinda Camber Porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melinda_Camber_Porter) on a documentary feature about his body of work, *Wim Wenders – Visions on Film*. Porter died before it was finished, and the film remains incomplete.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Wenders is a member of the advisory board of [World Cinema Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Cinema_Foundation). The project was founded by [Martin Scorsese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Scorsese) and aims to find and reconstruct world cinema films that have been neglected. As of 2015 he served as a Jury Member for the digital studio [Filmaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmaka), a platform for undiscovered filmmakers to show their work to industry professionals.[^cite_note-36]



Wenders with wife Donata at [Berlinale 2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlinale_2017)

In June 2017, Wenders stage-directed [Georges Bizet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Bizet)'s opera *[Les Pêcheurs de perles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Les_P%C3%AAcheurs_de_perles)*, starring [Olga Peretyatko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_Peretyatko) and [Francesco Demuro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Demuro) and conducted by [Daniel Barenboim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Barenboim) at the [Berlin State Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin_State_Opera) (Staatsoper). In a 2018 interview, he said his favorite movie of all time was his film about [Pope Francis](1687680.md), and that his entire career had been building up to it. His admiration for Francis is profound; he said he felt Francis is doing his best in a world full of calamities. He also said that, though raised Catholic, he had converted to Protestantism years earlier.[^cite_note-37]

In 2019 Wenders acted as executive producer for his former assistant director Luca Lucchesi's documentary *A Black Jesus*, which has similar themes to *Pope Francis: A Man of His Word.* The film explores the role of religion in communal identity and how this can create or dissolve differences in a small Sicilian town during the height of the refugee crisis.[^cite_note-38] Lucchesi noted that Wenders pushed the film to be more symbolic and philosophical, saying that Wenders wanted the film to have a "universal fairy-tale aspect" and to represent "Europe in a nutshell".[^cite_note-39]

In 2025, Wenders made a short film called "The Keys to Freedom" in [Reims, France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reims,_France). In the film, Wenders tours the Museum of the Surrender, where General [Eisenhower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisenhower) gave the liberated city's keys to the mayor in 1945, and comments "These are the keys to the freedom of the world". Wenders told *The New York Times* that Europeans can no longer assume American protection, adding that young people take freedom for granted, not realizing they may soon need to defend it.[^cite_note-40]

## Photography

Wenders has worked with photographic images of desolate landscapes and themes of memory, time, loss, nostalgia and movement.[^cite_note-theguardian1-6][^cite_note-telegraph-7] He began his long-running project "Pictures from the Surface of the Earth" in the early 1980s and pursued it for 20 years. The initial photographic series was titled "Written in the West" and was produced while Wenders criss-crossed the American West in preparation for his film *[Paris, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris,_Texas_\(film\))* (1984).[^cite_note-Biography-8] It became the starting point for a nomadic journey across the globe, including Germany, Australia, Cuba, Israel and Japan, to take photographs capturing the essence of a moment, place or space.[^cite_note-41]

## Personal life

Wenders lives and works in Berlin with his wife, Donata.[^cite_note-Biography-8] He has lived in Berlin since the mid-1970s.[^cite_note-42] He is an [ecumenical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical) Christian; as a teenager he wished to become a Catholic priest.[^cite_note-43] He supports the football club [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund).[^cite_note-44]

In 2009, Wenders signed a petition in support of director [Roman Polanski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Polanski), who had been detained while traveling to a film festival in relation to his 1977 [sexual abuse charges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Polanski_sexual_abuse_case), which the petition argued would undermine the tradition of film festivals as a place for works to be shown "freely and safely" and argued that arresting filmmakers traveling to neutral countries could open the door to "actions of which no-one can know the effects."[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

From 1979 to 1981, Wenders was married to the American actress and singer-songwriter [Ronee Blakley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronee_Blakley).

Wenders has been awarded honorary doctorates by the [Sorbonne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Paris) in Paris in 1989, the [University of Fribourg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Fribourg) (Switzerland) in 1995, and the [Université Catholique de Louvain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universit%C3%A9_Catholique_de_Louvain), Belgium, in 2005. The Wim Wenders Foundation was established in Düsseldorf in 2012. It provides a framework to bring together his cinematic, photographic, artistic and literary works in his native country and make them permanently accessible to the public.[^cite_note-105] In 2016, he received the Großer Kulturpreis of the Sparkassen Culture-Foundation Rhineland.[^cite_note-106] Wim-Wenders-Gymnasium was founded in [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf)-[Oberbilk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberbilk) in 2017 and bears his name since the 1 August 2018.[^cite_note-107]
