Elections for the [Bangsamoro Parliament](42303279.md) were held on September 14, 2026, in the [Bangsamoro Autonomous Region in Muslim Mindanao](37260549.md) (BARMM) of the Philippines. All 80 [members of parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Members_of_parliament) (MPs) were elected, comprising 40 party representatives, 32 [district representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_districts_of_Bangsamoro), and eight sectoral representatives, including two seats reserved for [Non-Moro Indigenous Peoples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Moro_Indigenous_Peoples_in_the_Bangsamoro_Parliament) (NMIP) were filled through a separate traditional selection process. The election was held in accordance with the [Bangsamoro Organic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Organic_Law), which serves as the charter of the [autonomous region](45522517.md). It was the first regular election for the Bangsamoro Parliament, succeeding the interim [Bangsamoro Transition Authority Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Transition_Authority#Interim_Parliament).

Originally scheduled for May 2022, the election was postponed four times. It was first postponed to May 2025 due to the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) and the absence of an electoral code. It was moved again to October 2025 after the [Supreme Court](1335534.md) ruled that [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu) was not part of Bangsamoro, leaving seven seats unallocated. The election was moved for a third time to no later than March 31, 2026, after the Supreme Court declared two districting laws unconstitutional. It was postponed for the final time to September 14, 2026, after President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) signed Republic Act No. 12317 on March 25, 2026, officially resetting the election date.

The elections resulted in a [hung parliament](652214.md), as no party or coalition was able to secure the 41 seats needed for a majority. The [Bangsamoro Federalist Party](83436978.md) (BFP), the party of incumbent Chief Minister [Abdulraof Macacua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulraof_Macacua), holds a one-seat [plurality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_\(voting\)), having won 31 seats, followed by the [United Bangsamoro Justice Party](46652799.md) (UBJP) with 30. The [Bangsamoro Grand Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BARMM_Grand_Coalition) (BGC), a coalition of four parties, won 15 seats, while the [Raayat Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raayat_Democratic_Party) and [Progresibong Bangsamoro Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progresibong_Bangsamoro_Party) each won a single seat.

## Background

### Interim parliament

When Bangsamoro was formed in 2019, the [Bangsamoro Transition Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Transition_Authority) (BTA) served as an interim government of the autonomous region, and also acted as its [interim parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Parliament_of_Bangsamoro). The interim government is not officially divided by political party affiliation but instead into two groups according to the nominating entity; the majority are nominees of the [Moro Islamic Liberation Front](51673.md) (MILF), while the rest are nominees of the Philippine national government.[^cite_note-rep-mangudadatu-9]

Under the law which postponed the elections to 2025, the [President of the Philippines](271126.md) was allowed to appoint a new set of members for the interim parliament whose terms would run until June 30, 2025.[^cite_note-10]

### Initial postponement

Originally scheduled to be held on May 9, 2022, the Bangsamoro Parliament election was postponed. The impact of the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Bangsamoro) in the region was cited as justification, along with the resulting non-passage of a Bangsamoro Electoral Code. The electoral code would be the regional legislation that would define the [parliamentary districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_districts_of_Bangsamoro) for the purpose of the regional election. The electoral code would be legislated using data from the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Philippine_census) which was likewise affected by the pandemic.[^cite_note-senate-okays-11] In order for the election to be postponed, the [Bangsamoro Organic Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Organic_Law) had to be amended.

The Bangsamoro interim government and some advocacy groups in Mindanao campaigned for the postponement of the polls.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Interim Chief Minister [Murad Ebrahim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murad_Ebrahim) reasoned that three years was not enough to restructure the region's government, and that the COVID-19 pandemic and delays in the release of the region's budget delayed the transition process. The postponement also received opposition. The [Kusug Tausug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kusug_Tausug) party in the [House of Representatives](293605.md) believed that postponing the elections was tantamount to justifying the interim Bangsamoro government's performance, which it found inadequate. [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu) Governor [Abdusakur Mahail Tan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdusakur_Mahail_Tan) also opposed the extension since he wanted elected officials to lead the region by 2022.[^cite_note-houseconsiders-14]

In the [Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_the_Philippines), there was debate about whether it would be necessary to hold a plebiscite for the potential postponement of the Bangsamoro election.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-houseconsiders-14] The [National Citizens' Movement for Free Elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Citizens'_Movement_for_Free_Elections), amid talks on the possible postponement of the election, wanted the polls to be "desynchronized" or held on a different date from the national elections since a new electoral system would be used for the regional elections.[^cite_note-16]

On September 6, 2021, the [Senate](299521.md) approved Senate Bill No. 2214, postponing the election to 2025.[^cite_note-17] A counterpart bill in the [House of Representatives](293605.md) was approved on September 15.[^cite_note-18] As the two bills were different, they had to be reconciled in a [conference committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_Committee) before being submitted for the president's signature to become law, and to actually postpone the election. While both bills gave the president of the Philippines the power to appoint parliament members for the 2019–2022 term, the House bill gave the incumbent president [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) that power, while the Senate bill gave it to the winner of the [2022 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Philippine_presidential_election).[^cite_note-19] In late September, both chambers ratified the conference committee's version of the bill, giving the winning president in the 2022 election the power to appoint the members of the next transitional parliament.[^cite_note-20]

On October 28, 2021, President Duterte signed into law the bill postponing the elections to 2025.[^cite_note-21] However Duterte's successor was given the ability to change the parliament's entire composition once he or she assumed office.[^cite_note-22]

### Second interim parliament

Duterte's successor President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) appointed new members for the interim parliament. Forty-nine were reappointed while 31 were new members. The composition of 41 MILF nominees and 39 government nominees was still retained.[^cite_note-marcos-no-extension-23]

### Exclusion of Sulu from Bangsamoro and second postponement

In September 2024, [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu) province was excluded from Bangsamoro by a [Supreme Court](1335534.md) ruling, consequentially excluding it from the 2025 election. The ruling stated that its inclusion in the BARMM was unconstitutional as a majority of its residents voted against joining the region in the [2019 Bangsamoro autonomy plebiscite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Bangsamoro_autonomy_plebiscite).[^cite_note-24] The ruling led to uncertainty regarding the fate of seven seats allotted to Sulu in the Bangsamoro Parliament.[^cite_note-25]

On October 22, 2024, the BTA adopted a resolution requesting the national Congress to move the election to 2028 on account of Sulu's exclusion from the BARMM.[^cite_note-26] On November 4, 2024, Senate President [Chiz Escudero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiz_Escudero) filed [Senate](299521.md) bill 2862, which called for the election to be postponed to May 11, 2026, in order for the BARMM "to reconfigure its jurisdictions as well as reallocate" its parliamentary seats following the exclusion of Sulu.[^cite_note-27] A separate bill was also filed by Speaker [Martin Romualdez](45505193.md) and [Lanao del Sur](297370.md) representative [Zia Alonto Adiong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zia_Alonto_Adiong) in the [House of Representatives](293605.md) to postpone the election until 2026.[^cite_note-28]

A group of 35 civil society organizations in Mindanao opposed the postponement of the election and were also critical on the progress of decommissioning of the MILF as an armed rebel group.[^cite_note-29] [Maguindanao del Sur](67871248.md) governor [Mariam Mangudadatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariam_Mangudadatu) said that there had been fatal skirmishes involving the MILF since 2022. The governor questioned MILF's legitimacy since their leadership in the transition government would be extended if the election was postponed. She also brought up a pledge by president [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) that the election would be held as planned in 2025.[^cite_note-30]

The Bangsamoro regional government expressed openness to the postponement, stating that it was deferring to the "sound discretion" of the national Congress. Concerns were largely on the disenfranchisement of voters in Sulu, especially in the event the Supreme Court reversed its decision.[^cite_note-31] [Mohagher Iqbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohagher_Iqbal) of the MILF and also a parliament member was surprised by the initiative of the national congress. He believed that it would be advantageous to the [United Bangsamoro Justice Party](46652799.md), the MILF's party, if the election was held as planned in 2025.[^cite_note-iqbal-interview-32] On November 21, Basilan governor [Hadjiman Hataman Salliman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadjiman_Hataman_Salliman), Maguindanao del Norte Governor [Abdulraof Macacua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulraof_Macacua), and Tawi-Tawi Governor [Yshmael Sali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yshmael_Sali) issued a joint statement supporting the postponement, citing the need to address the distribution of parliamentary seats following the exclusion of Sulu.[^cite_note-33]

On February 16, 2025, COMELEC announced the suspension of ballot-printing for the election after a bicameral conference committee in Congress approved the postponement of the vote.[^cite_note-34] On February 19, President [Bongbong Marcos](572460.md) signed into law Republic Act No. 12123, officially moving the date of the election to October 13, 2025.[^cite_note-35]

The COMELEC gave the Bangsamoro Parliament the ability to reallocate the seven parliamentary districts originally allocated for Sulu. The Parliament missed two deadlines; on May 30 and on June 15, 2025. COMELEC announced that only 73 out of 80 parliamentary seats would be filled.[^cite_note-36]

### Third postponement

On September 15, 2025, the Supreme Court, acting on an petition filed by Bangsamoro MP Lanang Ali Jr., League of Bangsamoro Organizations Secretary General Samsodin Amella, and peace advocate Datuan Magon Jr., issued a temporary restraining order against the Bangsomoro Autonomy Act (BAA) No. 77, or the Bangsamoro Parliamentary Redistricting Act of 2025, which sought to implement the reallocation of parliamentary districts,[^cite_note-37] prompting the COMELEC to suspend its preparations for the election on September 17.[^cite_note-38] On September 25, COMELEC Chair [George Garcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Garcia) said it was "legally and factually impossible" to hold the election as scheduled, citing the TRO by the Supreme Court.[^cite_note-39]

Supreme Court of the Philippines invalidates the Bangsamoro Parliament Districting

On October 1, 2025, the Supreme Court declared the BAA 77 as well as the preceding BAA 58 unconstitutional, leaving Bangsamoro without a law defining its parliamentary districts and effectively postponing the elections again. The high court nevertheless directed the COMELEC to proceed with preparations and conduct of the election not later than March 31, 2026. The court ordered the parliament to determine the distribution of district parliamentary seats by October 30, 2025.[^cite_note-40]

After the Bangsamoro Parliament failed to comply with the first COMELEC deadline, the election body warned that the parliament should pass a new districting law by November 30, 2025. As per the interpretation of the COMELEC, the parliament should pass the districting law by this date to comply the Voters' Registration Act which states that no law modifying electoral districts shall be passed 120 days before the elections – one of the cited reasons why the Supreme Court declared BAA 77 as unconstitutional.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42]

On November 25, 2025, the COMELEC set the election date for March 30, 2026.[^cite_note-43]

The Bangsamoro Parliament still failed to pass a districting law after it held its last regular session for the year on December 18, 2025.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] On December 22, the COMELEC has already urged Congress to pass a law to set the elections for November 2026.[^cite_note-46]

Chief Minister [Abdulraof Macacua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulraof_Macacua) called for a special session to be held from December 29 to 31, 2025 in a bid to pass a districting law.[^cite_note-47] The special session took place as [Mohammad Yacob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Yacob) decided against convening the parliament as its members were expected to be unavailable due to the Christmas holiday.[^cite_note-48]

A special session began on January 12, 2026,[^cite_note-49] with Parliament Bill 415 being passed minutes after midnight of the following day.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] However the Institute for Autonomy and Governance and COMELEC raised concerns that the new districting law had already missed the deadline to be compliant with the 120-days requirement set by the Voters' Registration Act.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-faces-legal-53]

### Fourth postponement

On January 28, 2026, the COMELEC announced the deferment of the election, citing the 120-day deadline.[^cite_note-54] Senator [Migz Zubiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migz_Zubiri) subsequently filed Senate Bill 1823, which sought to hold the election on September 14, 2026.[^cite_note-55] On March 25, 2026, President Bongbong Marcos signed the bill into law. Signed as Republic Act No. 12317, the law resets the date of the first regular Bangsamoro Parliament election to September 14, 2026. It ensures that future elections will be synchronized with the Philippines' national and local election schedules. The law amended Section 13, Article XVI of the Bangsamoro Organic Law to reflect the new election date after multiple postponements.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57]

## Electoral system

1. Political parties (40 seats) (50.0%)

2. Single member districts (32 seats) (40.0%)

3. Sectoral representatives (8 seats) (10.0%)



Sample ballot paper for voters in the Cotabato City 1st district which shows the logos of the parties for the proportional representation section, and photos of candidates for the district representative candidates.

A total of 80 seats were contested in the 2026 Bangsamoro elections. The final composition of the parliament after the elections should satisfy the following:[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-electoral29-59]

- One half (40 seats) shall be representatives of political parties elected through a system of [proportional representation](23298.md).

- Not more than 40 percent (32 seats) of the members of the parliament shall be elected from [single member districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Single-member_district).

- [Reserved seats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reserved_political_positions) and sectoral representatives shall constitute at least 10 percent, which in any case should be no less than 8 seats.

This means a voter has three votes of [parallel voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parallel_voting); the vote on one does not affect the other two. A party may opt to participate in one, two or all three votes. Independents can only participate in district elections.

Ballots for the election included candidates' photos and the official logos of political parties.[^cite_note-60] This is the first time that such visual aids were added on ballots for an elections in the Philippines.[^cite_note-61]

### Proportional representation

A voter may opt to vote in the proportional representation ballot question. This is voted upon by the region [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large). The [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) is at 2.5%.

### Districts

The [Bangsamoro parliamentary districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_districts_of_Bangsamoro) were initially defined on February 28, 2024, via Bangsamoro Act No. 58, and on August 19, 2025, via Bangsamoro Act No. 77.[^cite_note-minda-creates-62] The two acts were then declared unconstitutional by the Supreme Court, and a replacement law, which was later known as the Bangsamoro Act No. 86, provided for the current scope of the regional parliamentary districts.

The parliamentary districts exist independently from the [congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_districts_of_the_Philippines) used to determine representation in the national [House of Representatives](293605.md).[^cite_note-63]

The NMIPs, Traditional leaders, and *'Ulama* representatives were supposed be elected in their own convention/assembly separate from the parliamentary elections. The names of the elected representatives should be submitted to the Commission on Elections seven days prior to the parliamentary elections and would be proclaimed simultaneously with the rest of the elected members of parliament.[^cite_note-electoral29-59]

Ultimately, the entire region voted [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large) for sectoral seats, except for the NMIP seat, which was determined via convention a few weeks prior to the election. Voting system is [first-past-the-post voting](261709.md) for all other sectors, except for settler communities, which is via [plurality block voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_block_voting). A voter may opt to vote for all sectors, even on ones the voter is not a part of (i.e., a Christian male settler senior citizen may opt to vote for the ballot question for women, traditional leaders, youth and Ulama).

## Parties

### Background

When the [United Bangsamoro Justice Party](46652799.md) (UBJP) was asked in May 2021 if they would participate in the not-yet-postponed Bangsamoro elections, they said that they were "ready" but expressed preference that "there would be no election yet", so that they would keep focused on "the delivery of basic services" to residents of Bangsamoro. The UBJP is an affiliate of the [Moro Islamic Liberation Front](51673.md), whose nominees form the majority of the [Bangsamoro Transition Authority Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Transition_Authority_Parliament).[^cite_note-72]

On April 24, 2024, the [Bangsamoro Peoples Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangsamoro_Peoples_Party) (BPP), the [Al Ittihad–UKB Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Ittihad%E2%80%93UKB_Party) and the [Serbisyong Inklusibo–Alyansang Progresibo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbisyong_Inklusibo%E2%80%93Alyansang_Progresibo) (SIAP) formed an [electoral alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_alliance) for the 2025 election.[^cite_note-73]

Eligible parties should have at least 10,000 members with chapters throughout the Bangsamoro region.[^cite_note-74] This prerequisite remains despite Sulu's exclusion from Bangsamoro in September 2024.[^cite_note-75]

### Contesting parties

Eight parties have registered with the [Commission on Elections](1847922.md) as of March 2024.[^cite_note-barmmvows-76] On May 18, 2024, [Sulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu) governor [Abdusakur Mahail Tan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdusakur_Mahail_Tan) announced that he would forego another term as governor in the 2025 local election and challenge Murad Ebrahim as Chief Minister during a rally in [Maimbung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maimbung). He received support from the [BARMM Grand Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BARMM_Grand_Coalition) (BGC).[^cite_note-77] However, Tan became ineligible to run for chief minister following the Supreme Court decision excluding Sulu from the BARMM on September 9, 2024.[^cite_note-78]

Sixteen political groups sought accreditation for the purpose of parliamentary election.[^cite_note-79] The BGC registered as a regional party and not as a coalition, although two of its members, Al Ittihad–UKB and SIAP also sought separate accreditations.[^cite_note-80] As of November 5, 2024, there are eight accredited regional parties, including the BGC.[^cite_note-81][^cite_note-pcij-accred-82]

In March 2026, the COMELEC asked all accredited parties to register again after their certifications were nullified by Bangsamoro Autonomy Act No. 88.[^cite_note-ask-83] Sixteen political groups has filed applications by the April 6, 2026 deadline.[^cite_note-84] 13 parties were part of the tentative list of parties published on May 13, 2026.[^cite_note-tentativelist-85][^cite_note-86]

COMELEC cancelled the accreditation of [Moro Ako Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moro_Ako_Party) as a regional party over alleged misrepresentation on August 4, 2026. The petition alleged that Moro Ako declared non-existent chapters but the party insisted that the offices do exist and there were only "typographical errors". COMELEC denied the motion for reconsideration by the party of August 28.[^cite_note-87] COMELEC also disqualified Anwar Alamada, the UBJP's candidate for the fifth parliamentary district of Maguindanao del Sur, after the [Bangsamoro Federalist Party](83436978.md) (BFP) candidate Rafsanjani Ali accused Alamada of falsely declaring that he was a long-time resident of the area.[^cite_note-88]

### Sectoral representatives

Additionally, eight seats are reserved for sectoral representatives namely women, youth, non-Moro indigenous peoples (NMIP), [settler communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlers_of_Bangsamoro), [Ulama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulama), and traditional leaders[^cite_note-vera-newfiles-97] Originally accredited organizations representing each group were to hold assemblies to determine their respective representatives.[^cite_note-98]

However, Bangsamoro Act No. 88 was passed amending the Bangsamoro Election Code.[^cite_note-vera-newfiles-97] The representatives for each sectoral group except the NMIPs should be held on the same election day through a plurality of valid votes by the electorate.[^cite_note-99] Eligible voters do not have to belong to a certain sector. This means that both men and women can vote for the women sector representative.[^cite_note-vera-newfiles-97]

#### Non-Moro Indigenous Peoples

The COMELEC set July 27 to August 9, 2026, as the period where [Non-Moro Indigenous People (NMIP) communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Moro_Indigenous_Peoples_in_the_Bangsamoro_Parliament) were to conduct their tribal assemblies and August 24 to September 4 as the period for Regional Inter-Tribal Convention to select the two representatives.[^cite_note-100]

On August 26, 2026, during the Regional Inter-Tribal Convention at the Pagana Convention Center in Cotabato City, the NMIPs chose two representatives for the Bangsamoro Parliament.[^cite_note-101] Six tribes were present during the Convention, namely the Limbubungan [Higaonon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higaonon_people), Erumanen ne Menuvu, Blaan, [Dulangan Manobo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manobo), Lambiangan, and [Teduray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teduray_people). Each of the six groups proposed two potential members of parliaments and nominated ten members to the convention[^cite_note-nmip-elect-102]

The delegates have decided the order of representation for the six tribes. For the 2026 elections, they agree that the representatives should come from the Teduray and the Lambiangan. The next representatives in the immediate elections after 2026 will be selected from the Manobo Dulangan and Erumanen ne Menuvu, and then after the Blaan and Higaonon. The Teduray and the Lambiangan will then get their turn again.[^cite_note-103]

The Bangsamoro Electoral Code requires half of the NMIP representatives with two allocated seats to be women. The male nominee was Leticio Datuwata of the Lambiangan while the female nominee was Jo Genna Jover of the Teduray.[^cite_note-nmip-elect-102]

#### Traditional leaders

The traditional leaders sector represents the royal [sultanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanates) and [houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_houses) in Bangsamoro. The following institutions were explicitly named in Section 31 of the Bangsamoro Electoral Code.[^cite_note-104]

- [Sulu Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulu_Sultanate)

  [^cite_note-106]

- [Maguindanao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_Sultanate), Kabuntalan, and [Buayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Buayan)

- [Royal House of Ranao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Confederate_States_of_Lanao)

- Royal House of Iranun

The Intra-Sultanate Assembly was originally intended to determine the rotational order for the four groups from which the traditional leader sectoral representative would be selected. Under the original guidelines, the Sultanates of Maguindanao, Kabuntalan, and Buayan were treated as a single cluster and were to determine their own internal rotation.[^cite_note-supplemental-105] However, Bangsamoro Autonomy Act No. 88 subsequently scrapped this arrangement, providing instead for the traditional leader sectoral representative to be elected by the electorate through plurality voting on election day.

## Awareness

The Institute for Autonomy and Governance (IAG) conducted a survey between January 27 and February 15, 2025, asking 2,688 likely voters. Almost half or 47.3% admitted they were completely unaware of how Bangsamoro region's voting process actually works.[^cite_note-107]

## Security concerns

### Gun ban and other measures

A gun ban went into effect in Bangsamoro on August 14, 2025, coinciding with the election period, and will last until October 28, 2025.[^cite_note-108] This also applies to [Isabela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabela,_Basilan) in [Basilan](297003.md), despite the city itself not being part of Bangsamoro.[^cite_note-109] On September 11, 2025, COMELEC placed 12 municipalities in the Bangsamoro under its red areas of concern due to a history of conflict and violence. These included six towns in Lanao del Sur ([Masiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masiu), [Lumba-Bayabao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lumba-Bayabao), [Poona Bayabao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poona_Bayabao), [Tamparan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamparan), [Taraka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taraka,_Lanao_del_Sur) and [Mulondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulondo,_Lanao_del_Sur)), five in Maguindanao del Sur ([Paglat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paglat), [Pandag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandag), [Buluan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buluan), [Datu Paglas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu_Paglas) and [Mangudadatu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangudadatu,_Maguindanao_del_Sur)), and [Al-Barka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Barka) in Basilan.[^cite_note-110] Following the Supreme Court's ruling voiding Bangsamoro Autonomy Acts No. 77 and 58, COMELEC said that it would lift all election-related security measures.[^cite_note-111]

After the election was rescheduled to September 2026, a new gun ban went into effect on July 16, 2026, and will last until September 29, 2026.[^cite_note-112] By August 24, at least 15 people were arrested for violating the ban, while 20 firearms were seized. More than 24,000 security checkpoints were set up across the region,[^cite_note-113] while around 30,000 security personnel were deployed.[^cite_note-114]

At least 108 international observers from 16 organizations and diplomatic delegations have attended to watch the proceedings of the Bangsamoro election.[^cite_note-115]

### Incidents

On August 14, a convoy carrying interim BARMM Chief Minister [Abdulraof Macacua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdulraof_Macacua) was fired upon by unidentified assailants in [Datu Odin Sinsuat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Datu_Odin_Sinsuat), [Maguindanao del Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maguindanao_del_Norte) as he was returning from [Kabuntalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabuntalan), where he had assumed the leadership of the Bangsamoro Federalist Party. No injuries were recorded.[^cite_note-116]

On September 11, gunmen opened fire on the residence of Rima Hassan, the BFP candidate for the [Lamitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamitan) constituency in Basilan, injuring two people including her son-in-law.[^cite_note-117]

Main article: [Datu Usman Mampen Elementary School shooting](84224214.md)

Overnight on September 13-14, clashes broke out among rival supporters of the UBJP and BFP queuing in advance at a school being used as a polling precinct in [Cotabato City](298955.md), leaving three dead and 15 injured. In another incident in the city, gunmen opened fire on a group of UBJP supporters, with no injuries reported, while in a third incident, one person was critically injured in an explosion.[^cite_note-118][^cite_note-119] The incidents prompted the imposition of COMELEC control in the city as well as in [Maluso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluso) in Basilan, following the discovery of 16,000 pre-shaded ballot papers that affected 36 of 38 polling stations in the municipality.[^cite_note-120][^cite_note-121] Voting in affected areas in Maluso began on September 15 after the compromised ballots were replaced.[^cite_note-122]

On election day, clashes broke out again between voters at another polling station in Cotabato City, leaving three people injured including a police officer.[^cite_note-123] In the town of [Shariff Saydona Mustapha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shariff_Saydona_Mustapha) in Maguindanao del Sur, clashes broke out again between supporters of rival parties at a polling station which escalated into a shootout that involved a unit of the MILF and damaged the municipal hall. Two people, including a member of the PNP [Special Action Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Action_Force), were injured, while 10 others were arrested.[^cite_note-124]

### Proclamation

All thirty-two parliamentary district representatives were formally proclaimed on September 15.[^cite_note-129]

After midnight on September 16, the COMELEC Regional Board of Canvassers formally proclaimed all the elected members of the parliament. The [BFP](83436978.md), [UBJP](46652799.md), [BGC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BARMM_Grand_Coalition), and [Raayat Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raayat_Democratic_Party) secured all forty seats for political party representation. Moreover, the UBJP likewise secured all six sectoral seats.[^cite_note-130] Since none of the contesting parties managed to win the required 41-seat majority, this may result to a possible [hung parliament](652214.md).[^cite_note-131]
