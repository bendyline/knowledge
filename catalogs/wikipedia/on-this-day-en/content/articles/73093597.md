**Sabastian Kimaru Sawe** (born 16 March 1995) is a Kenyan [long-distance runner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long-distance_runner) who holds the world record in the [marathon](49132.md). Sawe made his debut at the [2024 Valencia Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Valencia_Marathon), winning in 2\:02\:05. At the [2026 London Marathon](83030339.md), he became the first person to run a [sub-two-hour marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-two-hour_marathon) in a record-eligible race.

## Early life and background

Sawe was born in the village of Barsombe in the [Rift Valley Province](72874.md) of Kenya (today in [Uasin Gishu County](1152280.md)) to a father who worked as a maize farmer.[^cite_note-\:0-2][^cite_note-bbc-3] He was primarily raised by his grandmother.[^cite_note-bbc-3] Sawe grew up in a house "with mud walls and no electricity."[^cite_note-4] He attended [St Patrick's High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Patrick's_High_School_\(Iten,_Kenya\)) in [Iten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iten). He is married and has a son.[^cite_note-\:0-2] He was raised as a Catholic and continues to practice the religion.[^cite_note-americahumblefaith-5]

## Career

Sawe's uncle [Abraham Chepkirwok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abraham_Chepkirwok) was the national record holder in Uganda over 800m.[^cite_note-bbc-3] Sawe himself initially ran middle-distances, only attempting the 5000 metres for the first time in 2019 under serendipitous circumstances: having arrived late to an athletics meet, it was the only race still available. He won it in 13\:56. Through his uncle's contacts, he began training with [Abel Mutai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel_Mutai), a former peer of Chepkirwok and an assistant coach at the 2Running Club, based in Nandi County and founded by Claudio Berardelli. In March 2020, however, he ruptured a tendon, limiting his racing, and later missed his international debut — pacing teammate [Amos Kipruto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amos_Kipruto) at the 2020 Valencia Marathon — after testing positive for COVID-19.[^cite_note-6]

### Breakthrough

In January 2022, Sawe finished his first half marathon in Seville in 59\:02, despite initially starting the race as a pacemaker.[^cite_note-\:0-2] He won the run and improved on the previous course record of 1\:00\:44. His time was the sixth fastest half marathon time ever.[^cite_note-7] In March 2022, Sawe set a course record at the [Rome-Ostia Half Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome-Ostia_Half_Marathon) in a time of 58\:02 (a non-record eligible course).[^cite_note-\:1-8] He set a then half marathon best of 58\:58 from his win at the 2022 Bahrain Royal Night Half Marathon.[^cite_note-9]

In September 2022, Sawe set a Kenyan national record in the [one hour run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_hour_run) at the [Memorial Van Damme](1111662.md) with a distance of 21,250 metres, narrowly missing the world record.[^cite_note-10] Sawe finished seventh in the senior men's race at the [2023 World Cross Country Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Athletics_Cross_Country_Championships).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] In December 2023, he won the 15 kilometres road race [Montferland Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montferland_Run) held in ['s-Heerenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'s-Heerenberg) in the Netherlands with a time of 42\:35.[^cite_note-13] In September 2024, Sawe won the [Copenhagen Half Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copenhagen_Half_Marathon) in a new personal best of 58\:05.[^cite_note-14]

### 2024 Valencia Marathon debut

On 1 December 2024, he won the [Valencia Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencia_Marathon) in a world-leading time of 2\:02\:05.[^cite_note-15] Sawe's mark was the 5th-fastest marathon ever run at the time, although still slower than the course record of 2\:01\:48 set by Sisay Lemma in the previous year.[^cite_note-worldathletics-16] Sawe's time was the second-fastest marathon debut in history, 12 seconds slower than [Kelvin Kiptum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin_Kiptum).[^cite_note-worldathletics-16] His mark stood as the fastest marathon time run in 2024.

On 27 April 2025, Sawe won the [London Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Marathon) in a world-leading time of 2\:02\:27.[^cite_note-17] This was followed in September 2025 with a win at the [Berlin Marathon](1663911.md), where he lowered the world-leading time of the year to 2\:02\:16.[^cite_note-WA25-18] Sawe was following a group of 7 pacemakers, with the last one dropping out at the 23k mark.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] In response to doping violations from other Kenyan athletes, Sawe had undergone an unprecedented 25 out-of-competition drug tests from the AIU to combat suspicions of doping in his buildup to Berlin.[^cite_note-21]

On 26 April 2026, Sawe became the first person to complete a competitive [marathon in under two hours](83044214.md) when he won the [London Marathon](83030339.md) in a world-record time of 1\:59\:30.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] A previous sub-two-hour run by [Eliud Kipchoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliud_Kipchoge) in the [Ineos 1\:59 Challenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ineos_1\:59_Challenge) in 2019 was not recognised as a valid record as it was not achieved under race conditions.[^cite_note-25] Sawe's run was ten seconds faster than Kipchoge's and 65 seconds faster than the previous official record held by [Kelvin Kiptum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin_Kiptum).[^cite_note-26] Sawe's former personal best was set in his debut marathon in [Valencia](63861.md) in 2024 with a time of 2\:02\:05.[^cite_note-27] In his first marathon, [Yomif Kejelcha](46932262.md) of Ethopia finished second behind Sawe in a time of 1\:59\:41.[^cite_note-28]

The women's former world record holder [Paula Radcliffe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paula_Radcliffe) stated: "We've witnessed history being made, but it is more than that. It is an iconic barrier that there has been this discussion over for a long time about whether it is even possible."[^cite_note-bbc-3] Kipchoge stated: "My deepest congratulations to both Sabastian Sawe and [Yomif Kejelcha](46932262.md). Breaking the sub-two-hour barrier in the marathon has long been a dream for runners everywhere, and today you've made that dream come true."[^cite_note-bbc-3]

The race saw 800,000 spectators. The last time a men's world record was set in London was in 2002. Kelvin Kiptum had formerly set the course record with 2\:01\:25. Sawe ran an average of 200 km a week in the 6 weeks leading up to the run. Sawe's race shoes were a lightweight model of [super shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_shoe), the Pro Evo 3 from [Adidas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adidas).[^cite_note-29] The prize money for Sawe's win was £740,000.[^cite_note-30]
