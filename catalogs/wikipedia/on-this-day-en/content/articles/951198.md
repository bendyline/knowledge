The **Men's FIH Hockey World Cup** is an international [field hockey](10886.md) competition organised by the [International Hockey Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Hockey_Federation) (FIH). The tournament was [started in 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Men's_Hockey_World_Cup). It is held every four years, bridging the four years between the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_hockey_at_the_Summer_Olympics). As of the 2026 edition, only 6 nations have won the World Cup, with [Germany](7032540.md) and [Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_men's_national_field_hockey_team) being the most successful teams, both having won the tournament four times. The [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_men's_national_field_hockey_team) and [Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australia_men's_national_field_hockey_team) have each won three titles, while [Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgium_men's_national_field_hockey_team) and [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India_men's_national_field_hockey_team) have both won the tournament once.

The [2026 tournament](71081638.md) took place in the [Netherlands](21148.md) and [Belgium](3343.md) from 15 to 30 of August. [Germany](7032540.md) defeated [Spain](7091174.md) in the final 1-0 to win their 4th title and 2nd in a row.

## History

The Hockey World Cup was first conceived by [Pakistan](23235.md)'s Air Marshal [Nur Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_Khan). He proposed his idea to the [FIH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Hockey_Federation) through Patrick Rowley, the first editor of *World Hockey* magazine. Their idea was approved on 26 October 1969 and adopted by the FIH Council at a meeting in Brussels on 12 April 1970. The FIH decided that the inaugural World Cup would be held in October 1971, in Pakistan.

However, political issues would prevent that first competition from being played in Pakistan. The FIH had inadvertently scheduled the first World Cup to be played in Pakistan during the [Bangladesh Liberation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Liberation_War). Furthermore, Pakistan and India had been at war with each other only six years earlier. When Pakistan invited India to compete in the tournament, a crisis arose. Pakistanis, led by cricketer [Abdul Hafeez Kardar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hafeez_Kardar), protested against India's participation in the Hockey World Cup.

Given the intense political climate between Pakistan and India, the FIH decided to move the tournament elsewhere. In March 1971, coincidentally in the same month [Bangladesh declared independence from Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladeshi_Declaration_of_Independence), the FIH decided to move the first Hockey World Cup to the [Real Club de Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Club_de_Polo) grounds in [Barcelona](4443.md), [Spain](26667.md), which was considered a neutral and peaceful European site.[^cite_note-1]

The FIH has set no requirements or limitations on the size of the competition. The 1971 Cup included only ten nations, the smallest World Cup. The 1978 Cup featured fourteen nations. The 2002, 2018 and 2023 Cups featured sixteen nations. The remaining 10 World Cups have featured 12 nations.

The first three tournaments were held every two years. The 1978 Cup was the only tournament held three years from the previous one. It was halfway between the [Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Hockey_at_the_Summer_Olympics) hockey competition and has continued that way. In other words, the tournament has been held every four years ever since.

## Trophy

The Hockey World Cup trophy was designed by Bashir Moojid and created by the [Pakistani Army](1965925.md). On 27 March 1971, in [Brussels](3708.md), the trophy was formally handed to FIH President Rene Frank by Mr Muhammad Masood, the Pakistani Ambassador to Belgium. The trophy consists of a [silver](27119.md) cup with an intricate floral design, surmounted by a globe of the world in silver and [gold](12240.md), placed on a high blade base inlaid with [ivory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivory). At its peak is a model hockey [stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_stick) and ball. Without its base, the trophy stands 120.85 mm (4.758 in) high. Including the base, the trophy stands 650 mm (26 in). It weighs 11,560 [g](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gram) (408 oz), including 895 g (31.6 oz) of gold, 6,815 g (240.4 oz) of silver, 350 g (12 oz) of ivory and 3,500 g (120 oz) of [teak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teak).[^cite_note-2]

## Format

The Hockey World Cup consists of a qualification stage and a final tournament stage. The format for each stage is the same.

### Qualification

The qualification stage has been a part of the Hockey World Cup since 1977. All participating teams play in the qualification round. The teams divide into two or more pools and compete for a berth in the final tournament. The top two teams are automatically qualified and the rest of the berths are decided in playoffs.

### Final tournament

The final tournament features the continental champions and other qualified teams. Sometimes it also features the winners of the Summer Olympics' hockey competition or the continental runners-up. The teams divide into pools once more and play a [round robin tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Round_robin_tournament). The composition of the pools is determined using the current [FIH World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIH_World_Rankings).

### Successful national teams

Twenty seven teams have qualified for a Hockey World Cup. Of these, thirteen teams have made it to the semifinals. Eight teams have made it through to the finals.

To date, the most successful teams are Germany and Pakistan with four titles each, followed by the Netherlands and Australia with three titles each, while India and Belgium won their lone titles in 1975 and 2018 respectively.

### Performance by host nations

Nine nations have hosted the Hockey World Cup. Only the Netherlands ([1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Men's_Hockey_World_Cup) and [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Men's_Hockey_World_Cup)) and Germany ([2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Men's_Hockey_World_Cup)) have won the tournament as hosts. Spain, England, and Pakistan emerged as host runners-up in the [1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971_Men's_Hockey_World_Cup), [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Men's_Hockey_World_Cup) and [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Men's_Hockey_World_Cup) tournaments. Australia placed third when it hosted the [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Men's_Hockey_World_Cup) tournament in [Sydney](27862.md).

### Performance by continental zones

To date, the finals of the Hockey World Cup have been contested by [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Hockey_Federation), [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Hockey_Federation) and [Oceania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceania_Hockey_Federation) continental teams. European teams have won the most with eight titles, followed by Asian teams with five titles. Australia is the only team from Oceania to win the tournament. Neither [the Americas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_Hockey_Federation) nor [Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_Hockey_Federation) have ever won the title.
