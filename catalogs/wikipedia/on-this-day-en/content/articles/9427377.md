The **Maine Democratic Party** is the affiliate of the [Democratic Party](5043544.md) in the [U.S. state](18618239.md) of [Maine](19977.md).

After the Civil War, Democrats were a minor player in a political scene dominated by the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Republican_Party). However, during the 1950s, [Edmund Muskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Muskie) led an expansive political insurgency culminating in his election as [Governor of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine) and successive Democratic elections to both state and national offices.[^cite_note-\:0-3]

It is currently the state's favored party, controlling both houses of the [state legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Legislature), [governorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine), and both of Maine's [U.S. House](19468510.md) seats. One of Maine's [U.S. Senate](24909346.md) seats is currently held by [Angus King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angus_King), an [Independent](2172378.md) who caucuses with the Democrats.

## Current Democratic officeholders

### Members of Congress

#### U.S. Senate

- None

Maine's [U.S. Senate](24909346.md) Class I seat has been held by [Independent](2172378.md) [Angus King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angus_King) since [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_United_States_Senate_elections) (though King does caucus with the Senate Democrats) while the Class II seat has been held by [Republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_Republican_Party) [Susan Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Collins_\(politician\)) since [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_United_States_Senate_elections). Senate Majority Leader [George J. Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_J._Mitchell) was the last Democrat to represent Maine in the U.S. Senate, leaving office at the conclusion of his term on January 3, 1995. Mitchell had been appointed to the seat in May 1980 to fill the vacancy left by [Edmund Muskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Muskie) after Muskie's appointment as the [U.S. Secretary of State](32293.md). Mitchell was subsequently elected to a full term in [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_United_States_Senate_elections#Maine). After winning a second term in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_United_States_Senate_election_in_Maine), Mitchell opted to retire instead of seeking a third term. Congressman [Thomas Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Andrews_\(American_politician\)) ran as the Democratic nominee in the [1994 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_United_States_Senate_election_in_Maine) and was subsequently defeated by Republican challenger [Olympia Snowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympia_Snowe).

## History



The Maine Democratic Party was revitalized by [Edmund Muskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Muskie) during the 1950s and 1960s. Muskie served as governor, senator and finally secretary of state under President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter).

The Democratic Party has history dating back to the 1800s. Maine entered the Union in 1820 as an [Anti-Federalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Federalist) state. Soon after, in 1834, the Anti-Federalists adopted the Democratic Party name.[^cite_note-\:0-3]

The Democrats had limited success for nearly a hundred years, in part due to slavery. The Northeast was predominantly anti-slavery whereas the South was pro-slavery. When the [Whig Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whig_Party_\(United_States\)) split in 1856, most of its northern wing formed the [Republican Party](32070.md), which dominated Maine due to its anti-slavery stance. Maine was predominantly Republican until 1954.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-\:1-4] Between 1855 and 1955, only five Democrats — [Samuel Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Wells) (1855–1856), [Alonzo Garcelon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonzo_Garcelon) (1878–1879), [Frederick W. Plaisted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_W._Plaisted) (1911–1913), [Oakley C. Curtis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oakley_C._Curtis) (1915–1917), and [Louis J. Brann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_J._Brann) (1933–1937) — served as governor of Maine, and in that same time only two Democrats — [Charles F. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_F._Johnson) and [Obadiah Gardner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obadiah_Gardner) — represented Maine in the United States Senate.[^cite_note-5]

In the [1954 Maine gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Maine_gubernatorial_election), Democratic state representative [Edmund Muskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_Muskie) became only the fourth Democrat to win the governorship in the 20th century. He won the state over in part due to his stance on economic growth through industrial development, accessible politics on television and being acceptable to diverse population growth. Muskie was re-elected in [1956](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1956_Maine_gubernatorial_election) and elected to the U.S. Senate in [1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1958_United_States_Senate_election_in_Maine), the first Democrat to be popularly elected to the U.S. Senate in Maine history.

Between 1954 and 1974, the number of registered Democrats more than doubled from 99,000 to 212,000.[^cite_note-\:1-4][^cite_note-\:433-6][^cite_note-\:043-7]

In more recent years, Maine has been roughly evenly split between Democrats, Republicans, and independents. For instance in 1990, independent registered voters constituted 37% of the electorate, Democrats 33%, and Republicans 30%. This is a stark contrast to the first hundred years of Maine politics.[^cite_note-Palmer,_Kenneth_T_1992-8][^cite_note-9]

From 2012 to 2019, despite having a Republican governor in [Paul LePage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_LePage), the [party remained strong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_party_strength_in_Maine), holding key offices in the state government and the U.S. Congress and maintaining a majority in the [Maine House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maine_House_of_Representatives) for six of LePage's eight years in office.

Democrat [Janet Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Mills) was elected [Governor of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_Maine) in the [2018 gubernatorial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Maine_gubernatorial_election), and was re-elected in [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Maine_gubernatorial_election).[^cite_note-10] Democrats have held a [government trifecta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_trifecta) in Maine since the 2018 election.

## Party platform



[George J. Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_J._Mitchell), Senate Majority leader (1989–1995)

Maine Democrats promote economic opportunity, ethical government, freedom, safety, and national security. They also promote voting rights, environmental protection, strong public education, access to health care, civil rights, transportation infrastructure, and help for those in serious poverty.[^cite_note-Palmer,_Kenneth_T_1992-8]

The party advocates economic security through strong support of [Social Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_\(United_States\)), [Medicare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicare_\(United_States\)), and [Medicaid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medicaid).

Maine Democrats believe in fair workplace opportunities and support Maine workers' right to form unions through the Employee Free Choice Act, which gives employees the chance to strike without being punished by employers and also keeps the workplace free of hazards, harassment, and discrimination.

The platform also supports environmental protection through an energy plan to curb pollution and increase productivity.[^cite_note-11]
