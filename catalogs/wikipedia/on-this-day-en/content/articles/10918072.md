**Armenia**,[^cite_note-11] officially the **Republic of Armenia**,[^cite_note-12] is a [landlocked country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked_country) in the [Armenian highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_highlands) of [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia).[^cite_note-CIA-5][^cite_note-13][^cite_note-15] It is a part of the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) region and is bordered by [Turkey](11125639.md) to the west, [Georgia](48768.md) to the north, [Azerbaijan](746.md) to the east, and [Iran](14653.md) and the Azerbaijani exclave of [Nakhchivan](385358.md) to the south.[^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press-16] [Yerevan](34352.md) is the capital, largest city and financial center.

The Armenian highlands have been home to the [Hayasa-Azzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayasa-Azzi), [Shupria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shupria), and [Nairi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairi) peoples. By at least 600 BC, an archaic form of [Proto-Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Armenian_language), an [Indo-European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-European_languages) language, had diffused into the Armenian highlands.[^cite_note-EIEC-17][^cite_note-Drews2017-18] The first Armenian state of [Urartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu) was established in 860 BC, and by the 6th century BC it was replaced by the [Satrapy of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrapy_of_Armenia). The [Kingdom of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_\(antiquity\)) reached its height under [Tigranes the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigranes_the_Great) in the 1st century BC and in AD 301 became the first state in the world to adopt [Christianity](5211.md) as its [official religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-stringer-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] Armenia still recognises the [Armenian Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church), the world's oldest [national church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_church), as the country's primary religious establishment.[^cite_note-Article_18-23][^cite_note-24] The ancient Armenian kingdom was split between the [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire) and [Sasanian Empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire) around the early 5th century. Under the [Bagratuni dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagratuni_dynasty), the [Bagratid Kingdom of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagratid_Armenia) was restored in the 9th century before falling in 1045. [Cilician Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Kingdom_of_Cilicia), an Armenian principality and later a kingdom, was located on the coast of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) between the 11th and 14th centuries.

Between the 16th and 19th centuries, the traditional Armenian homeland composed of [Eastern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia) and [Western Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Armenia) was divided between the [Ottoman](22278.md) and [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Armenia_\(1502%E2%80%931828\)) empires, with control of various regions shifting between the two powers over time. By the 19th century, Eastern Armenia had been conquered by the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) while most of Western Armenia remained under Ottoman rule. During [World War I](4764461.md), up to 1.5 million Armenians were systematically exterminated in the [Armenian genocide](140376.md). Following the [Russian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Revolution), the [First Republic of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) declared independence in 1918. By 1920, the state was incorporated into the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) as the [Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic). The Republic of Armenia became independent in 1991 during the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union).

Modern Armenia is a [unitary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state), [multi-party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-party_system), democratic [nation-state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation_state). It is a [developing country](78449.md) and ranks 69th on the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) as of 2023.[^cite_note-HDI-9] [Its economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Armenia) is primarily based on industrial output and mineral extraction. While Armenia is geographically located in the [South Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Caucasus), it views itself as part of Europe[^cite_note-CIA-5] and is generally considered [geopolitically](163225.md) European. The country is a member of numerous European organisations including the [Organization for Security and Co-Operation in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_for_Security_and_Co-Operation_in_Europe), the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), the [Eastern Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Partnership), [Eurocontrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurocontrol), the [Assembly of European Regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_European_Regions), and the [European Bank for Reconstruction and Development](37963.md). Armenia is a member of certain regional groups throughout Eurasia, including the [Asian Development Bank](2512.md), the [Collective Security Treaty Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Security_Treaty_Organization),[^cite_note-25] the [Eurasian Economic Union](27290106.md), and the [Eurasian Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_Development_Bank). Armenia supported the once *de facto* independent [Republic of Artsakh](1000530.md) (Nagorno-Karabakh), which had [seceded from Azerbaijan in 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Nagorno-Karabakh_independence_referendum), until Azerbaijan reincorporated the region through a [siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_Nagorno-Karabakh) and [military offensive in 2023](74857564.md).

## Etymology

Main article: [Name of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Name_of_Armenia)

The original native [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language) name for the country was Հայք ([*Hayk'*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D5%80%D5%A1%D5%B5%D6%84)) after the legendary founder [**Hayk Nahapet**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayk); however, it is currently rarely used. The contemporary name Հայաստան (*[Hayastan](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%D5%80%D5%A1%D5%B5%D5%A1%D5%BD%D5%BF%D5%A1%D5%B6)*) became popular in the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) by addition of the [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) suffix *[-stan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/-stan)* (place).[^cite_note-26] However the origins of the name Hayastan trace back to much earlier dates and were first attested in c. 5th century in the works of [Agathangelos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agathangelos),[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28] [Faustus of Byzantium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faustus_of_Byzantium),[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] [Ghazar Parpetsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazar_Parpetsi),[^cite_note-31] [Koryun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koryun),[^cite_note-32] and [Sebeos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebeos).[^cite_note-33]

The name has traditionally been derived from [Hayk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayk) (Հայկ), the legendary patriarch of the [Armenians](387816.md) and a great-great-grandson of [Noah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah), who, according to the 5th-century AD author [Movsis Khorenatsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Movses_Khorenatsi), defeated the [Babylonian king](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Kings_of_Babylon) Bel in 2492 BC and established his nation in the [Ararat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ararat,_Armenia) region.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPanossian2006106-34] Further origin of the name is uncertain. It is postulated[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-Bauer-36] that the name *Hay* comes from one of the two confederated, Hittite vassal states – the [Ḫayaša](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayasa)-Azzi (1600–1200 BC).

The [exonym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exonym_and_endonym) *Armenia* is attested in the [Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian) [Behistun Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun_Inscription) (515 BC) as *[Armina](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%F0%90%8E%A0%F0%90%8E%BC%F0%90%8E%B7%F0%90%8E%A1%F0%90%8E%B4#Old_Persian)* (𐎠𐎼𐎷𐎡𐎴). The [Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) terms [Ἀρμενία](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BC%88%CF%81%CE%BC%CE%B5%CE%BD%CE%AF%CE%B1#Ancient_Greek) (*Armenía*) and [Ἀρμένιοι](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E1%BC%88%CF%81%CE%BC%CE%AD%CE%BD%CE%B9%CE%BF%CF%82#Ancient_Greek) (*Arménioi*, "Armenians") are first mentioned by [Hecataeus of Miletus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hecataeus_of_Miletus) (c. 550 BC – c. 476 BC).[^cite_note-37] [Xenophon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xenophon), a Greek general serving in some of the Persian expeditions, describes many aspects of Armenian village life and hospitality in around 401 BC.[^cite_note-38]

Some scholars have linked the name *Armenia* with the Early Bronze Age state of *[Armani (Armanum, Armi)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armani_\(kingdom\))* or the Late Bronze Age state of *[Arme (Shupria)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shupria)*.[^cite_note-39] These connections are inconclusive as it is not known what languages were spoken in these kingdoms. Additionally, while it is agreed that Arme was located to the immediate west of [Lake Van](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Van) (probably in the vicinity of [Sason](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sason), and therefore in the greater Armenia region), the location of the older site of Armani is a matter of debate. Some modern researchers have placed it near modern [Samsat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samsat),[^cite_note-40] and have suggested it was populated, at least partially, by an early Indo-European-speaking people.[^cite_note-41] It is possible that the name *Armenia* originates in *Armini*, Urartian for "inhabitant of Arme" or "Armean country".[^cite_note-42] The Arme tribe of Urartian texts may have been the Urumu, who in the 12th century BC attempted to invade Assyria from the north with their allies the [Mushki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mushki) and the [Kaskians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaskians). The Urumu apparently settled in the vicinity of Sason, lending their name to the regions of Arme and the nearby lands of Urme and Inner Urumu.[^cite_note-43]

&#x20;According to the histories of both Movsis Khorenatsi and Michael Chamchian, *Armenia* derives from the name of [Aram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aram_\(Nahapet\)), a lineal descendant of Hayk.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-battles-45] In the [Hebrew Bible](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_Bible), the [Table of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Generations_of_Noah) lists [Aram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aram,_son_of_Shem) as the son of [Shem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shem), to whom the [Book of Jubilees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Book_of_Jubilees) attests,&#x20;

> And for Aram there came forth the fourth portion, all the land of Mesopotamia between the Tigris and the Euphrates to the north of the Chaldees to the border of the mountains of Asshur and the land of 'Arara'.[^cite_note-pseudepigrapha-46][^cite_note-digitalcommons.andrews.edu-47]

&#x20;Jubilees 8\:21 also apportions the Mountains of Ararat to Shem, which Jubilees 9\:5 expounds to be apportioned to Aram.[^cite_note-pseudepigrapha-46][^cite_note-digitalcommons.andrews.edu-47]

Jewish historian Flavius [Josephus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josephus) states in his *[Antiquities of the Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiquities_of_the_Jews)*,

> Aram had the Aramites, which the Greeks called [Syrians](8862873.md);... Of the four sons of Aram, Uz founded [Trachonitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trachonitis) and [Damascus](8914.md): this country lies between [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)) and [Celesyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celesyria). Ul founded Armenia; and Gather the [Bactrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bactrians); and Mesa the Mesaneans; it is now called [Charax Spasini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charax_Spasini).[^cite_note-48]

## History

Main article: [History of Armenia](87368.md)

### Prehistoric

Main articles: [Prehistoric Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prehistoric_Armenia), [Origin of the Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Origin_of_the_Armenians), and [Archaeology of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeology_of_Armenia)



Bronze Age burial site [Zorats Karer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorats_Karer) (also known as [Karahunj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zorats_Karer))

The first human traces are supported by the presence of [Acheulean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acheulean) tools, generally close to the [obsidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsidian) outcrops more than 1 million years ago.[^cite_note-49] The most recent and important excavation is at the [Nor Geghi 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nor_Geghi) [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age) site in the [Hrazdan river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrazdan_\(river\)) valley.[^cite_note-50] Thousands of 325,000 year-old artifacts may indicate that this stage of human technological innovation occurred intermittently throughout the Old World, rather than spreading from a single point of origin (usually hypothesized to be Africa), as was previously thought.[^cite_note-51]



[Petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyph) with images of various animals on Mount [Ughtasar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ughtasar_Petroglyphs)

Many early [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) settlements were built in Armenia (Valley of Ararat, Shengavit, Harich, Karaz, Amiranisgora, Margahovit, Garni, etc.). One of the important sites of the Early Bronze Age is [Shengavit settlement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shengavit_\(site\)).[^cite_note-o766-52] It was located on the site of today's capital of Armenia, [Yerevan](34352.md).

### Antiquity

Main articles: [Lesser Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_Armenia), [Satrapy of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrapy_of_Armenia), [Kingdom of Armenia (antiquity)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Armenia_\(antiquity\)), [Roman Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Armenia), and [Sasanian Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Armenia)



Historical Armenia, 150 BC

Armenia lies in the highlands surrounding the mountains of [Ararat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ararat). There is evidence of an early civilisation in Armenia in the Bronze Age and earlier, dating to about 4000 BC. Archaeological surveys in 2010 and 2011 at the [Areni-1 cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areni-1_cave) complex have resulted in the discovery of [the world's earliest known leather shoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areni-1_shoe),[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-CNNc-54] skirt,[^cite_note-strawskirt-55] and [wine-producing facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Areni-1_winery).[^cite_note-National_Geographic-56]

Several Bronze Age cultures and states flourished in the area of Greater Armenia, including the [Trialeti-Vanadzor culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trialeti_culture), [Hayasa-Azzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hayasa-Azzi), and [Mitanni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitanni) (located in southwestern historical Armenia), all of which are believed to have had Indo-European populations.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] The [Nairi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nairi) confederation and its successor, [Urartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu), successively established their sovereignty over the [Armenian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Highlands). Each of the aforementioned nations and confederacies participated in the ethnogenesis of the [Armenians](387816.md).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] A large cuneiform lapidary inscription found in [Yerevan](34352.md) established that the modern capital of Armenia was founded in the summer of 782 BC by King [Argishti I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argishti_I_of_Urartu). Yerevan is one of the world's [oldest continuously inhabited cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_oldest_continuously_inhabited_cities).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBournoutian200612-67]



[Behistun Inscription of Darius I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Behistun_Inscription) mentioning Armenia. 6th century BC.

After the fall of the state of [Urartu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urartu) at the beginning of the 6th century BC, the [Armenian highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_highlands) were for some time under the hegemony of the [Medes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medes), and after that they were part of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire). Armenia was part of the Achaemenid state from the second half of the 6th century BC until the second half of the 4th century BC divided into two satrapies: XIII (western part, with the capital in [Melitene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melitene)) and XVIII (northeastern part).[^cite_note-ИВ-68] During the late 6th century BC, the [first geographical entity that was called Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrapy_of_Armenia) by neighbouring populations was established under the [Orontid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orontid_dynasty).[^cite_note-c210-69]



Coin of [Tigranes the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigranes_the_Great)

The kingdom became fully sovereign from the sphere of influence of the [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) in 190 BC under King [Artaxias I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artaxias_I) and begun the rule of the [Artaxiad dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artaxiad_dynasty).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewsen200134-70] Armenia reached its height between 95 and 66 BC under [Tigranes the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigranes_the_Great), becoming the most powerful kingdom of its time east of the [Roman Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Republic).[^cite_note-RydénRosenqvist1993-71] In the next centuries, Armenia was in the [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Empire)'s sphere of influence during the reign of [Tiridates I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiridates_I_of_Armenia), the founder of the [Arsacid dynasty of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsacid_dynasty_of_Armenia), which was a branch of the [Parthian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parthian_Empire). Throughout its history, the kingdom of Armenia enjoyed both periods of independence and periods of autonomy subject to contemporary empires. Its strategic location between two continents has subjected it to invasions by many peoples, including [Assyria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria) (under [Ashurbanipal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashurbanipal), at around 669–627 BC, the boundaries of Assyria reached as far as Armenia and the [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains)),[^cite_note-72] Medes, Achaemenid Empire, [Greeks](42056.md), Parthians, [Romans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Rome), [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire), [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire), [Arabs](2185.md), [Seljuk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire), [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols), [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), the successive [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty), [Afsharid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_dynasty), and [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) dynasties of Iran, and the Russians.



The pagan [Garni Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garni_Temple), probably built in the first century, is the only "Greco-Roman [colonnaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonnade) building" in the [post-Soviet space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states).

[^cite_note-73]

Religion in ancient Armenia was historically related to a set of beliefs that, in Persia, led to the emergence of [Zoroastrianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrianism). It particularly focused on the worship of [Mithra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mithra) and also included a pantheon of gods such as [Aramazd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramazd), [Vahagn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vahagn), [Anahit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anahit), and [Astghik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astghik). The country used the solar [Armenian calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_calendar), which consisted of 12 months.[^cite_note-j409-74]

Christianity first appeared in Armenia in the 1st century AD, with the arrival of two of [Jesus](1095706.md)'s twelve [apostles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostles_in_the_New_Testament) – [Thaddaeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jude_the_Apostle) and [Bartholomew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomew_the_Apostle).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHacikyanBasmajianFranchukOuzounian200075–76-75][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalker199024-76] Under the influence of [Gregory the Illuminator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_the_Illuminator), King [Tiridates III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiridates_III_of_Armenia) proclaimed Christianity as the [state religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion) in 301,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHacikyanBasmajianFranchukOuzounian200077–79-77] partly in defiance of the [Sasanian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Empire).[^cite_note-boyce-78] Armenia thus became the first officially Christian state.[^cite_note-stringer-20] Prior to this, during the latter part of the Parthian era, Armenia was a predominantly Zoroastrian country.[^cite_note-boyce-78]

Another major cornerstone of Armenian identity – the [Armenian alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_alphabet) – was invented a century later by [Mesrop Mashtots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesrop_Mashtots) c. 405.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHacikyanBasmajianFranchukOuzounian200087-79] After the fall of the Armenian Arsacids in 428, most of Armenia was incorporated as a [*marzpanate* within Sasanian Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sasanian_Armenia).[^cite_note-80] Following the [Battle of Avarayr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Avarayr) in 451, Christian Armenians maintained their religion, and Armenia gained autonomy in the [Treaty of Nvarsak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Nvarsak) in 484.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPanossian200647–48-81]

### Middle Ages

Main articles: [Medieval Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medieval_Armenia), [Zakarid Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarid_Armenia), and [Mongol Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Armenia)



The [Etchmiadzin Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etchmiadzin_Cathedral), Armenia's Mother Church traditionally dated 303 AD, is considered the oldest cathedral in the world.

[^cite_note-82]

[^cite_note-83]

[^cite_note-84]

The Sassanid Empire was conquered by the [Rashidun Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_Caliphate) in the mid 7th century, reuniting Armenian lands previously taken by the Byzantine Empire, and Armenia subsequently emerged as [Arminiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arminiya), an autonomous principality under the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate). The principality was ruled by the Prince of Armenia, and recognised by the [Caliph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate) and the [Byzantine Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Byzantine_emperors). It was part of the administrative division/emirate *Arminiya* created by the Arabs, which also included parts of Georgia and [Caucasian Albania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasian_Albania), and had its centre in the Armenian city [Dvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dvin_\(ancient_city\)). Arminiya lasted until 884, when it regained its independence from the weakened [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) under [Ashot I of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashot_I_of_Armenia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECanardCahen1960-85]

The reemergent Armenian kingdom was ruled by the [Bagratuni dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagratuni_dynasty) and lasted until 1045. In time, several areas of the [Bagratid Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagratid_Armenia) separated as independent kingdoms and principalities such as the Kingdom of [Vaspurakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaspurakan) ruled by the House of [Artsruni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artsruni) in the south, Kingdom of [Syunik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syunik_Province) in the east, or [Kingdom of Artsakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Artsakh) on the territory of modern [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md), while still recognising the supremacy of the Bagratid kings.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPayaslian200752-86]

In 1045, the Byzantine Empire conquered Bagratid Armenia. Soon, the other Armenian states fell under Byzantine control as well. The Byzantine rule was short-lived, as in 1071 the [Seljuk Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuk_Empire) defeated the Byzantines and conquered Armenia at the [Battle of Manzikert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Manzikert).[^cite_note-87] [Ruben I, Prince of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruben_I,_Prince_of_Armenia), went with some of his countrymen into the gorges of the [Taurus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taurus_Mountains) and then into [Tarsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarsus,_Mersin) of [Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cilicia); he was escaping death or servitude at the hands of those who had killed [Gagik II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gagik_II_of_Armenia), King of [Ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani), his relative. The Byzantine governor of the palace gave them shelter where the [Armenian Kingdom of Cilicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Kingdom_of_Cilicia) was eventually established on 6 January 1198 under [Leo I, King of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_I,_King_of_Armenia), a descendant of Prince Ruben.[^cite_note-EvansWixomWixom1997-88]

Cilicia was a strong ally of the [European Crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) and saw itself as a bastion of [Christendom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christendom) in the East. Cilicia's significance in Armenian history and statehood is also attested by the transfer of the seat of the [Catholicos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicos) of the [Armenian Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church), the spiritual leader of the Armenian people, to the region.[^cite_note-g429-89]

In the early 12th century, Armenian princes of the [Zakarid family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarid_dynasty) drove out the Seljuk Turks and established a semi-independent principality in northern and eastern Armenia known as [Zakarid Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zakarid_Armenia), which lasted under the patronage of the [Georgian Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Georgia_\(country\)). The [Orbelian dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbelian_dynasty) shared control with the Zakarids in various parts of the country, especially in [Syunik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syunik_Province) and [Vayots Dzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vayots_Dzor_Province), while the [House of Hasan-Jalalyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Hasan-Jalalyan) controlled provinces of [Artsakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artsakh_\(historical_province\)) and [Utik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utik) as the [Kingdom of Artsakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Artsakh).[^cite_note-Hovannisian2004-90]

### Early modern era

Further information: [Iranian Armenia (1502–1828)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_Armenia_\(1502%E2%80%931828\)), [Armenians in the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_the_Ottoman_Empire), and [Russian Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Armenia)



In 1501–02, most of the [Eastern Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia) territories, including Yerevan, were conquered by the emerging [Safavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) of Iran led by Shah [Ismail I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismail_I).

During the 1230s, the [Mongol Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_Empire) conquered Zakarid Armenia and then the remainder of Armenia. The Mongolian invasions were soon followed by those of other Central Asian tribes, such as the [Kara Koyunlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kara_Koyunlu), [Timurid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_dynasty) and [Ağ Qoyunlu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C4%9F_Qoyunlu), which continued from the 13th century until the 15th century. After incessant invasions, each bringing destruction to the country, with time Armenia became weakened.[^cite_note-Hovannisian2004b-91]

In the 16th century, the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) and the [Safavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) of Iran divided Armenia. From the early 16th century, both [Western Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Armenia) and [Eastern Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Armenia) fell to the Safavids.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-Ward2014-93] Owing to the century long [Turco-Iranian geopolitical rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Safavid_relations) that would last in West Asia, significant parts of the region were frequently fought over between the two rivalling empires during the [Ottoman–Persian Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Persian_Wars). From the mid 16th century with the [Peace of Amasya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_of_Amasya), and decisively from the first half of the 17th century with the [Treaty of Zuhab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Zuhab) until the first half of the 19th century,[^cite_note-HerzigKurkchiyan2004-94] Eastern Armenia was ruled by the successive Safavid, [Afsharid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afsharid_dynasty) and [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) dynasties, while Western Armenia remained under Ottoman rule. From 1604, [Abbas I of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_I_of_Persia) implemented a "[scorched earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorched_earth)" policy in the region to protect his north-western frontier against any [invading Ottoman forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman%E2%80%93Safavid_War_\(1603%E2%80%931618\)), a policy that involved a [forced resettlement](87368.md#Persian_Armenia) of masses of Armenians outside of their homelands.[^cite_note-95]



[Capture of Erivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Erivan) fortress by Russian troops in 1827 during the [Russo-Persian War (1826–28)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1826%E2%80%9328\)) by [Franz Roubaud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Roubaud)

The 18th century saw the first foray of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) into the region, with [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great) and his alliance with the Armenian [meliks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melik) of [Syunik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syunik_\(historical_province\)) and [Artsakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melikdoms_of_Karabakh) during the [Russo-Persian War (1722–1723)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1722%E2%80%931723\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewsen2001163–165-96][^cite_note-97] Despite the setbacks of Peter's campaign, this initial move encouraged the meliks in their [resistance struggle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_liberation_struggle_\(1722%E2%80%931730\)) against the Safavids and the Ottomans.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHewsen2001163–165-96] However, it was not until the early 19th century that Eastern Armenia formally passed to Russian control, following the [Russo-Persian War (1804–1813)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1804%E2%80%931813\)) and the [Russo-Persian War (1826–1828)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1826%E2%80%931828\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBadalyan_Riegg202055,_91-98] In the 1813 [Treaty of Gulistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Gulistan) and the 1828 [Treaty of Turkmenchay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Turkmenchay), Qajar Iran ceded to Russia the [Karabakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh_Khanate), [Erivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erivan_Khanate), and [Nakhichevan Khanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nakhichevan_Khanate).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBournoutian20186–7-99][^cite_note-FOOTNOTETsutsiev201415–17-100] The [Russo-Turkish War (1877–1878)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1877%E2%80%931878\)) resulted in further gains for [Russian Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Armenia), notably the regions of [Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kars) and [Ardahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardahan).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPanossian2006120-101][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBadalyan_Riegg2020164-102]

Most of Western Armenia, however, remained under Ottoman rule. Within the Ottoman *[millet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millet_\(Ottoman_Empire\))* system, Armenians lived in relative harmony with other groups, including the ruling Turks. However, as Christians under a strict Muslim [social structure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_structure), Armenians faced pervasive discrimination. In response to the [1894 Sasun rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1894_Sasun_rebellion), Sultan [Abdul Hamid II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_II) organised state-sponsored massacres against the Armenians between 1894 and 1896, resulting in close to 200,000 deaths.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKévorkian201111-103] The [Hamidian massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamidian_massacres), as they came to be known, gave Abdul Hamid international infamy as the "Bloody Sultan"[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuny199399-104] or the "Red Sultan".[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKévorkian201167-105]



The [Armenian Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Oblast) (1828–1840) included the territory of the former Erivan (marked in yellow) and Nakhichevan (marked in light green) khanates.

During the 1890s, the [Armenian Revolutionary Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Revolutionary_Federation), commonly known as the *Dashnaktsutyun*, became active within the Ottoman Empire with the aim of unifying the various small groups in the empire that were advocating for reform and defending Armenian villages from massacres that were widespread in some of the Armenian-populated areas of the empire. Dashnaktsutyun members also formed [Armenian fedayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_fedayi) groups that defended Armenian civilians through armed resistance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalker1990177-106] The Dashnaks also worked for the wider goal of creating a "free, independent and unified" Armenia, although they sometimes set aside this goal in favour of a more realistic approach, such as advocating autonomy.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuny1993100-107][^cite_note-108]

The Ottoman Empire began to collapse, and in 1908, the [Young Turk Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Turk_Revolution) overthrew the government of Sultan Hamid. In April 1909, the [Adana massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adana_massacre) occurred in the [Adana Vilayet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adana_Vilayet) resulting in the deaths of as many as 20,000–30,000 Armenians. The Armenians living in the empire hoped that the [Committee of Union and Progress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_of_Union_and_Progress) would change their second-class status. The [Armenian reform package](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_reform_package) (1914) was presented as a solution by appointing an [inspector general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inspector_general) over Armenian issues.[^cite_note-109]

### World War I and the Armenian genocide



[Armenian genocide](140376.md) victims in 1915

The outbreak of World War I led to confrontation between the Ottoman Empire and the Russian Empire in the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_campaign) and [Persian campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_campaign_\(World_War_I\)). The new government in [Istanbul](3391396.md) began to look on the Armenians with distrust and suspicion because the [Imperial Russian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russian_Army) contained a contingent of [Armenian volunteers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_volunteer_units). On 24 April 1915, [Armenian intellectuals were arrested by Ottoman authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deportation_of_Armenian_intellectuals_on_24_April_1915) and, with the [Tehcir Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_Law_of_Deportation) (29 May 1915), eventually a large proportion of Armenians living in [Anatolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatolia) perished in what has become known as the [Armenian genocide](140376.md).[^cite_note-a354-110][^cite_note-k407-111]

The genocide was implemented in two phases: the wholesale killing of the able-bodied male population through massacre and subjection of army conscripts to forced labour, followed by the deportation of women, children, the elderly and infirm on [death marches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_march) leading to the [Syrian desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_desert). Driven forward by military escorts, the deportees were deprived of food and water and subjected to periodic robbery, rape, and massacre.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalker1990200–203-112][^cite_note-113] There was local [Armenian resistance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_resistance_during_the_Armenian_genocide) in the region, developed against the activities of the Ottoman Empire. The events of 1915 to 1917 are regarded by Armenians and the vast majority of Western historians to have been state-sponsored mass killings, or genocide.[^cite_note-114]

Turkish authorities deny the genocide took place to this day. The Armenian genocide is acknowledged to have been one of the first modern genocides.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuny1993106-115][^cite_note-24.04.1998-116] According to the research conducted by [Arnold J. Toynbee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arnold_J._Toynbee), an estimated 600,000 Armenians died during deportation from 1915 to 1916. This figure, however, accounts for solely the first year of the genocide and does not take into account those who died or were killed after the report was compiled on 24 May 1916.[^cite_note-117] The [International Association of Genocide Scholars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Association_of_Genocide_Scholars) places the death toll at "more than a million".[^cite_note-118] The total number of people killed has been most widely estimated at between 1 and 1.5 million.[^cite_note-119] Armenia and the Armenian diaspora have been campaigning for [official recognition](12001341.md) of the events as genocide for over 30 years. These events are traditionally commemorated yearly on 24 April, the Armenian Martyr Day, or the Day of the Armenian genocide.[^cite_note-Matiossian2021-120]

### First Republic of Armenia



&#x20; Territory held by [Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) and the [Karabakh Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh_Council) at some point

&#x20; Area given to Armenia by the [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres), which was never enforced

[^cite_note-121]

The [Imperial Russian Caucasus Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Army_\(Russian_Empire,_1914%E2%80%931917\)), commanded by [Nikolai Yudenich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Yudenich), and the Armenian volunteer units, led by [Andranik Ozanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andranik_Ozanian) and [Tovmas Nazarbekian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tovmas_Nazarbekian), succeeded in gaining much of Western Armenia during World War I. However, their gains were lost with the [October Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution) of 1917.[^cite_note-c446-122] At the time, Russian-controlled Eastern Armenia, Georgia, and Azerbaijan attempted to bond together in the [Transcaucasian Democratic Federative Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasian_Democratic_Federative_Republic). This federation, however, lasted from only February to May 1918, when all three parties decided to dissolve it. As a result, the Dashnak-dominated government of Eastern Armenia declared its independence on 28 May as the [First Republic of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) under the leadership of [Aram Manukian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aram_Manukian).[^cite_note-a193-123]



The Government house of the [First Republic of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_Armenia) (1918–1920)

The First Republic's short-lived independence was fraught with war, territorial disputes, [ethnic unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_uprisings_in_Kars_and_Sharur%E2%80%93Nakhichevan), [Bolshevik rebellions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Uprising), and a mass influx of refugees from Western Armenia, bringing with them disease and starvation. The [Entente Powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_I) sought to help the newly founded Armenian state through relief funds and other forms of support.[^cite_note-h780-124]

At the end of the war, the victorious powers sought to divide up the Ottoman Empire. Signed between the Allied Powers and the Ottoman Empire at [Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A8vres) on 10 August 1920, the [Treaty of Sèvres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_S%C3%A8vres) promised to maintain the existence of the Armenian republic and to attach the former territories of Western Armenia to it.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPanossian2006247–248-125] Because the new borders of Armenia were to be drawn by United States President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson), Western Armenia was also referred to as "[Wilsonian Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilsonian_Armenia)". In addition, just days prior on 5 August, [Mihran Damadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihran_Damadian) of the Armenian National Union, the de facto Armenian administration in Cilicia, declared the independence of Cilicia as an Armenian autonomous republic under French protectorate.[^cite_note-126] There was even consideration of making Armenia a [mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations_mandate) under the protection of the United States. The treaty, however, was rejected by the [Turkish National Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_National_Movement) and never came into effect.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPanossian2006247–248-125]

In 1920, Turkish nationalist forces [invaded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_invasion_of_Armenia) the fledgling Armenian republic from the east. Under the command of [Kâzım Karabekir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%A2z%C4%B1m_Karabekir), they captured the Armenian territories that Imperial Russia had annexed in 1878 and occupied Alexandropol (today [Gyumri](1529357.md)). Hostilities finally concluded with the [Treaty of Alexandropol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Alexandropol) on 2 December 1920. The treaty forced Armenia to disarm most of its military forces and to cede all former Ottoman territory granted to it by the Treaty of Sèvres. Simultaneously, the [11th Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/11th_Army_\(RSFSR\)), led by [Sergo Ordzhonikidze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergo_Ordzhonikidze), invaded Armenia at Karavansarai (today [Ijevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ijevan)) on 29 November. By early December, Ordzhonikidze's forces entered Yerevan and the short-lived Armenian republic collapsed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHovannisian1996b389–390-127]

After the fall of the First Republic, the [war communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_communism) policies of the new [Soviet Armenian government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) led to the [February Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_Uprising) of 1921.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHovannisian1996b405–406-128] Following the defeat of that revolt, Armenian forces under the command of [Garegin Nzhdeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garegin_Nzhdeh) established the [Republic of Mountainous Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Mountainous_Armenia) and continued to fight Soviet forces in the southern region of [Zangezur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zangezur).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaparov2015104–106-129] To end the rebellion, Soviet authorities pledged to include the [Syunik Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syunik_Province) within Armenia's borders.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPanossian2006259-130] The Red Army soon took control of the region, driving the leaders of the revolt out of Armenia, across the [Araks River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aras_\(river\)) into Iran on 15 July 1921.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaparov2015104–106-129]

### Soviet Armenia

Main article: [Armenian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic)



The [state emblem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emblem_of_the_Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) of [Soviet Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) depicting [Mount Ararat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ararat)

#### Sovietization to Stalinism

Following Armenia's [Sovietization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Armenia), the Treaty of Alexandropol was superseded by the [Treaty of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Moscow_\(1921\)) and the [Treaty of Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kars). In these agreements, Turkey renounced its claims on [Batumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batumi) to [Soviet Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) in exchange for the provinces of [Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kars_oblast), [Ardahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardahan_okrug), and [Surmalu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surmalu_uezd), all of which had been under Turkish military control since the Turkish invasion of Armenia in 1920. The territory ceded to Turkey included the medieval Armenian capital [Ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani) and [Mount Ararat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ararat), the national symbol of the Armenian people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETsutsiev201474–76,_79-131] Together with Soviet Georgia and [Soviet Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijan_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), Soviet Armenia officially entered the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) as part of the [Transcaucasian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasian_SFSR) (TSFSR) in March 1922.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPanossian2006246-132] The TSFSR later split into three separate republics in December 1936 – the Armenian SSR, the Azerbaijan SSR, and the Georgian SSR.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPanossian2006266-133]

[Vladimir Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin), who "favored a moderate policy in Armenia,"[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShakarian202549-134] appointed [Alexander Miasnikian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Miasnikian), an experienced administrator, to oversee affairs in the republic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaparov2015103-135] During the era of the [New Economic Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Economic_Policy) (NEP), Armenia enjoyed a period of stability and cultural revival within the USSR, in contrast to the turbulent years of the First Republic.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuny1993140-136] However, after the [death of Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_and_state_funeral_of_Vladimir_Lenin) and the [rise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin's_rise_to_power) of [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) to the Soviet leadership, the situation changed significantly. Armenians suffered greatly during [Stalin's Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_victims_of_the_Great_Purge), with [Lavrentiy Beria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavrentiy_Beria) playing an especially conspicuous role, beginning with the death of the popular Soviet Armenian leader [Aghasi Khanjian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghasi_Khanjian) in 1936.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShakarian202514–15-137][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuny1993156–157-138] The repressions claimed the lives of major Armenian intellectual figures like [Yeghishe Charents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yeghishe_Charents), [Axel Bakunts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axel_Bakunts), and [Vahan Totovents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vahan_Totovents).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuny1993155-139] The Armenian Church also suffered in this period, culminating in the murder of the Armenian Catholicos [Khoren I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoren_I_of_Armenia), an act for which Beria is usually held responsible.[^cite_note-140]

#### Great Patriotic War and post-war period



Armenian [Marshals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshal_of_the_Soviet_Union) and [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_of_the_Fleet_of_the_Soviet_Union) of World War II on stamps: [Bagramyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Bagramyan), [Isakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Isakov), [Babadzhanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamazasp_Babadzhanian), [Khudyakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Khudyakov)

Armenia was not the scene of any battles in the [Great Patriotic War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_II\)) of [World War II](32927.md). Nevertheless, an estimated 500,000 Armenians (nearly a third of the population) served in the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) during the war, and 175,000 died.[^cite_note-141] A total of 117 citizens of Soviet Armenia, including 10 non-Armenians, were awarded [Hero of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hero_of_the_Soviet_Union). Six special military divisions were formed in Armenia in 1941–42. Five of them, the [89th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/89th_Rifle_Division), [409th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/409th_Rifle_Division), [408th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/408th_Rifle_Division_\(Soviet_Union\)), [390th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/390th_Rifle_Division), and [76th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/76th_Rifle_Division_\(Soviet_Union\)) Divisions, would have a distinguished war record, while the sixth was ordered to stay in Armenia to guard the republic's western borders against a possible incursion by neighboring Turkey. The 89th Tamanyan Division, composed of ethnic Armenians, fought in the [Battle of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Berlin). Over 60 Armenians were promoted to the rank of general, while one attained the rank of [Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Admiral_of_the_Fleet_of_the_Soviet_Union) ([Ivan Isakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Isakov)) and three achieved the rank of [Marshal of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshal_of_the_Soviet_Union) ([Ivan Bagramyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Bagramyan), [Hamazasp Babadzhanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamazasp_Babadzhanian), and [Sergei Khudyakov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Khudyakov)).[^cite_note-142]

After the [death of Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_funeral_of_Joseph_Stalin) in 1953, Armenia experienced a new period of liberalization under [Nikita Khrushchev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Khrushchev)'s [Thaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khrushchev_Thaw). In March 1954, two years before Khrushchev [denounced Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On_the_Cult_of_Personality_and_Its_Consequences), his close ally, the Armenian statesman [Anastas Mikoyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastas_Mikoyan), flew to Yerevan and called for the [rehabilitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rehabilitation_\(Soviet\)) of Charents and the republication of the writers [Raphael Patkanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Patkanian) and [Raffi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffi_\(novelist\)).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEShakarian202534–35-143] During the Thaw, life in Soviet Armenia soon began to see rapid improvement with greater investment in housing and the consumer economy. The Armenian Church, which was limited under Stalin, was revived when [Catholicos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholicos_of_All_Armenians) [Vazgen I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vazgen_I) assumed the duties of his office in 1955.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPanossian2006352-144] In 1962, the massive statue of Stalin that towered over Yerevan was pulled down from its pedestal by troops and replaced with that of [Mother Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mother_Armenia) in 1967.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPanossian2006349-145] That same year, a memorial to the victims of the Armenian genocide was built at the [Tsitsernakaberd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsitsernakaberd) hill above the Hrazdan gorge in [Yerevan](34352.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPanossian2006349-145] This occurred after [mass demonstrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Yerevan_demonstrations) took place on the tragic event's fiftieth anniversary in 1965.[^cite_note-146]

#### Glasnost and perestroika



Armenians gather at [Theater Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Square,_Yerevan) in central [Yerevan](34352.md) to claim unification of [Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh_Autonomous_Oblast) with the [Armenian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_SSR), 1988.

Following the [Brezhnev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Brezhnev) era, [Mikhail Gorbachev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Gorbachev)'s reforms of *[glasnost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasnost)* and *[perestroika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perestroika)* witnessed a major national revival in Armenia with the rise of the [Karabakh movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karabakh_movement). Taking advantage of the new political climate, the Armenians of the [Nagorno-Karabakh Autonomous Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nagorno-Karabakh_Autonomous_Oblast) (NKAO) of Soviet Azerbaijan began to demand unification with Soviet Armenia, citing [discrimination by Baku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forced_assimilation_in_Azerbaijan).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESuny1993192–194-147] Peaceful protests in Armenia supporting the Karabakh Armenians became common. By the beginning of 1988, nearly one million Armenians from several regions of the republic engaged in these demonstrations, centered on Yerevan's Theater Square (today [Freedom Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_Square,_Yerevan)).[^cite_note-148] However, in neighboring Azerbaijan, [violence against Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumgait_pogrom) erupted in the city of [Sumgait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumgait).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESaparov2015166-149] Ethnic rioting soon broke out between Armenians and Azeris, preventing any peaceful resolution from taking place.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETsutsiev2014113-150] Compounding Armenia's problems was a [devastating earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Armenian_earthquake) in 1988 with a [surface-wave magnitude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface-wave_magnitude) of 6.8.[^cite_note-151] Another major [pogrom against Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baku_pogrom) in [Baku](4566.md) in January 1990 forced almost all of the 200,000 Armenians in the Azerbaijani capital to flee to Armenia.[^cite_note-152]

Gorbachev's inability to alleviate any of Armenia's problems created disillusionment among the Armenians and fed a growing hunger for independence. On 23 August 1990, Armenia declared its sovereignty on its territory. On 17 March 1991, Armenia, along with the [Baltic states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_states), Georgia and [Moldova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moldavian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), boycotted a nationwide [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Soviet_Union_referendum) in which 78% of all voters voted for the retention of the Soviet Union in a reformed form.[^cite_note-NewsBrief-153]

### Post-Soviet independence

Main article: [History of Armenia § Independent Armenia (1991-today)](87368.md#Independent_Armenia_\(1991-today\))



Armenian soldiers in 2008, during [Nagorno-Karabakh conflict](4695860.md)

On 21 September 1991, Armenia officially declared its [statehood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statehood) after the [failed August coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Soviet_coup_d'%C3%A9tat_attempt) in [Moscow](19004.md) in the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic). [Levon Ter-Petrosyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levon_Ter-Petrosyan), who rose to prominence during the Karabakh movement, was [popularly elected](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Armenian_presidential_election) the first president of the newly independent Republic of Armenia in October 1991.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPayaslian2007198-154] The Soviet Union formally [dissolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) following the [Belovezha Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belovezha_Accords) in December 1991 and Armenia's independence was recognised.[^cite_note-155]

Ter-Petrosyan led Armenia alongside defence minister [Vazgen Sargsyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vazgen_Sargsyan) through the [First Nagorno-Karabakh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nagorno-Karabakh_War) with neighbouring Azerbaijan. The initial post-Soviet years were marred by economic difficulties and a [severe energy crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990s_Armenian_energy_crisis). The latter was rooted in the early years of the Karabakh conflict when the [Azerbaijani Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijani_Popular_Front_Party) managed to pressure the Azerbaijan SSR to instigate a railway and air [blockade against Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish%E2%80%93Azeri_blockade_of_Armenia). This move effectively debilitated Armenia's economy as 85% of its cargo and goods arrived through rail traffic.[^cite_note-156] In 1993, Turkey joined the blockade against Armenia in support of Azerbaijan.[^cite_note-GHF-157]

The first Karabakh war ended after a Russian-brokered [ceasefire](592591.md) was concluded in 1994. The war was a success for the Karabakh Armenian forces who managed to capture practically the entire territory of the former NKAO as well as most of [seven adjacent districts of Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian-occupied_territories_surrounding_Nagorno-Karabakh).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETsutsiev2014114-158] The Armenian backed forces remained in control of almost all of that territory until 2020. By the time both Azerbaijan and Armenia had finally agreed to a ceasefire in 1994, an estimated 30,000 people had been killed and over a million had been displaced.[^cite_note-159]

### 21st century

In the 21st century, Armenia continued to face many hardships. The [2018 Armenian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Armenian_revolution) was a series of anti-government protests in Armenia from April to May 2018 staged by various political and civil groups led by a member of the Armenian parliament—[Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) (head of the [Civil Contract](43200633.md) party). Protests and marches took place initially in response to [Serzh Sargsyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serzh_Sargsyan)'s third consecutive term as President of Armenia and later against the [Republican Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republican_Party_of_Armenia) controlled government in general. Pashinyan declared the movement, which led to Sargsyan's resignation, a "[velvet revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velvet_revolution)".[^cite_note-160]

In March 2018, the Armenian parliament elected [Armen Sarkissian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armen_Sarkissian) as the new President of Armenia. The controversial constitutional reform to reduce presidential power was implemented, while the authority of the prime minister was strengthened.[^cite_note-161] In May 2018, parliament elected opposition leader Nikol Pashinyan as the new prime minister. His predecessor Serzh Sargsyan resigned two weeks earlier following [widespread anti-government demonstrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Armenian_revolution).[^cite_note-162]

On 27 September 2020, a full-scale war erupted due to the unresolved [Nagorno-Karabakh conflict](4695860.md).[^cite_note-163] Both the armed forces of Armenia and Azerbaijan reported military and civilian casualties.[^cite_note-164] The [Nagorno-Karabakh ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement) to end the six-week [war between Armenia and Azerbaijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_war) was seen by many as Armenia's defeat and capitulation.[^cite_note-165] The year-long [March of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%932021_Armenian_protests) protests forced early elections.

On 20 June 2021, Pashinyan's Civil Contract party won an early parliamentary [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Armenian_parliamentary_election). Acting Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan was officially appointed to the post of prime minister by Armenia's President Armen Sarkissian.[^cite_note-166] In January 2022, Armenian President Armen Sarkissian resigned from office, stating that the constitution no longer gives the president sufficient powers or influence.[^cite_note-167] On 3 March 2022, [Vahagn Khachaturyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vahagn_Khachaturyan) was elected as the fifth president of Armenia in the second round of parliamentary vote.[^cite_note-168] The next month [yet more protests broke out](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Armenian_protests).[^cite_note-169]

#### 2023 Azerbaijani offensive in Nagorno-Karabakh

Main article: [2023 Azerbaijani offensive in Nagorno-Karabakh](74857564.md)

See also: [Flight of Nagorno-Karabakh Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_of_Nagorno-Karabakh_Armenians)

Between 19 and 20 September 2023, [Azerbaijan](746.md) launched a large-scale military offensive against the [self-declared](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Nagorno-Karabakh) breakaway state of [Artsakh](1000530.md), a move seen by the European Parliament as a violation of the [2020 ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement).[^cite_note-170][^cite_note-171] The offensive took place in the disputed region of [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md), which is internationally recognized as part of Azerbaijan, but populated by [Armenians](387816.md).[^cite_note-172][^cite_note-173] The attacks occurred in the midst of an escalating crisis caused by Azerbaijan [blockading Artsakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blockade_of_the_Republic_of_Artsakh_\(2022%E2%80%93present\)), which resulted in significant scarcities of essential supplies such as food, medicine, and other goods in the affected region.[^cite_note-Nagorno-Karabakh-174]

One day after the offensive started, on 20 September, a [ceasefire agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Nagorno-Karabakh_ceasefire_agreement) was reached at the mediation of the [Russian peacekeeping command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peacekeeping_operations_in_Nagorno-Karabakh) in Nagorno-Karabakh.[^cite_note-175] Azerbaijan held a meeting with representatives of the Nagorno-Karabakh Armenians on 21 September in [Yevlakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevlakh), to be followed by another meeting in October.[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] Ceasefire violations by Azerbaijan were nonetheless reported by both Artsakhi residents and officials.[^cite_note-ceasefirevio1-178][^cite_note-ceasefirevio2-179]

Human rights organisations and experts in genocide prevention issued multiple alerts, stating that the region's Armenian population was at risk or actively [being subjected to ethnic cleansing and genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_of_Nagorno-Karabakh_Armenians). [Luis Moreno Ocampo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Moreno_Ocampo), a former [prosecutor of the International Criminal Court](33903993.md), warned that another [Armenian genocide](140376.md) could take place, and attributed the inaction of the international community to encouraging Azerbaijan that it would face no serious consequences.[^cite_note-washingtonpost-genocide-180]

On 8 August 2025, Armenian Prime Minister [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) and Azerbaijani President [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md) signed a joint declaration at the [White House](33057.md),[^cite_note-181] committing to a [peace deal](79518584.md) facilitated by the [Trump administration](78298108.md) that would end nearly four decades of conflict between the two countries.[^cite_note-182]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Armenia)



Satellite image of the territory of Armenia (2003)

Armenia is a [landlocked country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked_country) in the [geopolitical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geopolitical) [Transcaucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasus) (South [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus)) region, that is located in the Southern [Caucasus Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Mountains) and their lowlands between the [Black Sea](3386.md) and [Caspian Sea](19653787.md), and northeast of the [Armenian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Highland). Armenia is widely accepted as part of [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia) geographically, although other definitions place it either fully in Europe or across Europe and Asia.[^cite_note-britannica-14][^cite_note-BBC-183] Located on the [Armenian Highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Highlands), it is bordered by Turkey to the west, [Georgia](48768.md) to the north, the [Lachin corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachin_corridor) which is a part of [Lachin District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lachin_District) that is under the control of a Russian peacekeeping force and [Azerbaijan](746.md) proper to the east, and [Iran](14653.md) and Azerbaijan's [exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave) of [Nakhchivan](385358.md) to the south.[^cite_note-Oxford_University_Press-16] Armenia lies between latitudes [38°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/38th_parallel_north) and [42° N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/42nd_parallel_north), and meridians [43°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/43rd_meridian_east) and [47° E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/47th_meridian_east). It contains two terrestrial ecoregions: [Caucasus mixed forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_mixed_forests) and [Eastern Anatolian montane steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Anatolian_montane_steppe).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-184]

### Topography



Armenia's mountainous and volcanic [topography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topography)

Armenia has a territorial area of 29,743 square kilometres (11,484 sq mi). The terrain is mostly mountainous, with fast flowing rivers, and few forests. The land rises to 4,090 metres (13,419 feet) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metres_above_sea_level) at [Mount Aragats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Aragats), and no point is below 390 metres (1,280 ft) above sea level.[^cite_note-185] [Average elevation of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_average_elevation) area is tenth highest in the world and it has 85.9% mountain area, more than Switzerland or [Nepal](171166.md).[^cite_note-186]

[Mount Ararat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ararat), which was historically part of Armenia, is the highest mountain in the region at 5,137 meters (16,854 feet). Now located in Turkey, but clearly visible from Armenia, it is regarded by the Armenians as a symbol of their land. Because of this, the mountain is present on the [Armenian national emblem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_Armenia) today.[^cite_note-Azarian2007-187][^cite_note-Adalian2010-188][^cite_note-Minahan1998-189]

### Climate

Main article: [Climate of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_of_Armenia)



[Köppen-Geiger climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen-Geiger_climate_classification) map for Armenia

[^cite_note-190]

The climate in Armenia is markedly highland [continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate). Summers are hot, dry and sunny, lasting from June to mid-September. The temperature fluctuates between 22 and 36 °C (72 and 97 °F). However, the low humidity level mitigates the effect of high temperatures. Evening breezes blowing down the mountains provide a welcome refreshing and cooling effect. Springs are short, while autumns are long. Autumns are known for their vibrant and colourful foliage.

Winters are quite cold with plenty of snow, with temperatures ranging between −10 and −5 °C (14 and 23 °F). [Winter sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_sport) enthusiasts enjoy skiing down the hills of [Tsaghkadzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsaghkadzor), located thirty minutes outside Yerevan. [Lake Sevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Sevan), nestled up in the Armenian highlands, is the second largest lake in the world relative to its altitude, at 1,900 metres (6,234 ft) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metres_above_sea_level).

### Environment



Carbon dioxide emissions in metric tons per capita in Armenia, Azerbaijan, Georgia, Russia, Germany, Italy, USA in 2000–2012. World Bank data.

Armenia ranked 63rd out of 180 countries on [Environmental Performance Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_Performance_Index) (EPI) in 2018. Its rank on subindex Environmental Health (which is weighted at 40% in EPI) is 109, while Armenia's rank on subindex of Ecosystem Vitality (weighted at 60% in EPI) is 27th best in the world.[^cite_note-191] This suggests that main environmental issues in Armenia are with [population health](#Health_care), while environment vitality is of lesser concern. Out of sub-subindices contributing to Environmental Health subindex ranking on Air Quality to which population is exposed is particularly unsatisfying.

In Armenia [forest cover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_cover) is around 12% of the total land area, equivalent to 328,470 hectares (ha) of forest in 2020, down from 334,730 hectares (ha) in 1990. In 2020, naturally regenerating forest covered 310,000 hectares (ha) and planted forest covered 18,470 hectares (ha). Of the naturally regenerating forest 5% was reported to be [primary forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_forest) (consisting of native tree species with no clearly visible indications of human activity) and around 0% of the forest area was found within protected areas. For the year 2015, 100% of the forest area was reported to be under [public ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_ownership).[^cite_note-192][^cite_note-193]

[Waste management in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waste_management_in_Armenia) is underdeveloped, as no waste sorting or recycling takes place at Armenia's 60 landfills. A waste processing plant is scheduled for construction near [Hrazdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrazdan) city, which will allow for closure of 10 waste dumps.[^cite_note-194]

Despite the availability of abundant renewable energy sources in Armenia (especially [hydroelectric](381399.md) and [wind power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_power)) and calls from EU officials to shut down the [nuclear power plant at Metsamor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metsamor_Nuclear_Power_Plant),[^cite_note-195] the Armenian Government is exploring the possibilities of installing new small modular nuclear reactors. In 2018 existing nuclear plant is scheduled for modernisation to enhance its safety and increase power production by about 10%.[^cite_note-196][^cite_note-197]

Armenia is a [representative parliamentary democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) republic. The Armenian constitution adhered to the model of a [semi-presidential republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-Presidential_Republic) until April 2018.

According to the current [Constitution of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Armenia), the [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Armenia) is the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) holding largely representational functions, while the [Prime Minister](483914.md) is the [head of government](37570.md) and exercises [executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)).

Since 1995, [legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislature) is vested in the [Azgayin Zhoghov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Assembly_of_Armenia) or National Assembly, which is a [unicameral parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unicameral_parliament) consisting of 105 members.[^cite_note-Fischer-2018-198]

Since its first report in 2006 until its most recent in 2019, the [Fragile States Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fragile_States_Index) has consistently ranked Armenia better than all its neighboring countries (with one exception in 2011).[^cite_note-199]

Armenia has universal [suffrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffrage) from the age of 18.[^cite_note-200][^cite_note-201]

Within each province there are [communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_of_Armenia) (*hamaynkner*, singular *hamaynk*). As of 2007, Armenia includes 915 communities, of which 49 are considered urban and 866 are considered rural. The capital, Yerevan, also has the status of a community.[^cite_note-202] Additionally, Yerevan is divided into twelve semi-autonomous districts.

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Armenia)

See also: [Armenia–European Union relations](11949053.md), [Armenia in the Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia_in_the_Council_of_Europe), [Armenia–BSEC relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93BSEC_relations), [Armenia–CSTO relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93CSTO_relations), [Armenia–NATO relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93NATO_relations), [Armenia–OSCE relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93OSCE_relations), [Armenia and the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia_and_the_United_Nations), [Armenia–Russia relations](13875647.md), [Armenia–Turkey relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93Turkey_relations), and [Armenia–United States relations](15961619.md)



Joint Declaration signed on [meeting](79518584.md) between Armenian Prime Minister [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) and Azerbaijani President [Ilham Aliyev](22765442.md) held in Washington, D.C., 8 August 2025

Armenia became a member of the [United Nations](31769.md) on 2 March 1992, and is a signatory to a number of its organisations and other international agreements. Armenia is also a member of international organisations such as the [Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Europe), the [Asian Development Bank](2512.md), the [European Bank for Reconstruction and Development](37963.md), the [European Political Community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Political_Community), the [Commonwealth of Independent States](36870.md), the [Organization for Security and Cooperation in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_for_Security_and_Cooperation_in_Europe), the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization), the [World Customs Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Customs_Organization), the [Organization of the Black Sea Economic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_the_Black_Sea_Economic_Cooperation) and [La Francophonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organisation_internationale_de_la_Francophonie). It is a member of the [CSTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Security_Treaty_Organisation) military alliance, and also participates in [NATO](21133.md)'s [Partnership for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_for_Peace) programme and the [Euro-Atlantic Partnership Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro-Atlantic_Partnership_Council). In 2004, its forces joined [KFOR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosovo_Force), a NATO-led international force in [Kosovo](17391.md). Armenia is also an observer member of the [Arab League](52625.md),[^cite_note-Armenia-205] the [Organization of American States](22788.md), the [Pacific Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Alliance), the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement), and a dialogue partner in the [Shanghai Cooperation Organisation](152422.md). As a result of its historical ties to France, Armenia was selected to host the biennial Francophonie summit in 2018.[^cite_note-206]

Armenia has a difficult relation with neighbouring countries [Azerbaijan](11716506.md) and [Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia%E2%80%93Turkey_relations). Tensions were running high between Armenians and Azerbaijanis during the final years of the Soviet Union. The [Nagorno-Karabakh conflict](4695860.md) dominated the region's politics throughout the 1990s.[^cite_note-CFR-9148-207] To this day, Armenia's borders with Turkey and Azerbaijan are under [a severe blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish%E2%80%93Azeri_blockade_of_Armenia_\(1989%E2%80%93ongoing\)). In addition, a permanent solution for the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict has not been reached despite the mediation provided by organisations such as the [OSCE](22796.md).

Turkey also has a long history of poor relations with Armenia over its refusal to acknowledge the [Armenian genocide](140376.md), even though it was one of the first countries to recognize the Republic of Armenia (the third republic) after its independence from the USSR in 1991. Despite this, for most of the 20th century and early 21st century, relations remain tense and there are no formal diplomatic relations between the two countries due to Turkey's refusal to establish them for numerous reasons. During the first Nagorno-Karabakh War, and citing it as the reason, Turkey closed its border with Armenia in 1993. It has not lifted its [blockade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish%E2%80%93Azeri_blockade_of_Armenia_\(1989%E2%80%93ongoing\)) despite pressure from the powerful Turkish business lobby interested in Armenian markets.[^cite_note-CFR-9148-207]



Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) shakes hands with Armenian prime minister [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md), 2018

On 10 October 2009, Armenia and Turkey [signed protocols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Protocols) on the normalisation of relations, which set a timetable for restoring diplomatic ties and reopening their joint border.[^cite_note-208] The ratification of those had to be made in the national parliaments. In Armenia, before sending the protocols to the parliament, it was sent to the Constitutional Court to have their [constitutionality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionality) to be approved. The Constitutional Court made references to the preamble of the protocols underlying three main issues.[^cite_note-iaireport-209] One of them stated that the implementation of the protocols did not imply Armenia's official recognition of the existing Turkish-Armenian border established by the [Treaty of Kars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Kars). By doing so, the Constitutional Court rejected one of the main premises of the protocols, i.e. "the mutual recognition of the existing border between the two countries as defined by relevant treaties of international law".[^cite_note-iaireport-209][^cite_note-210] This was for the Turkish Government the reason to back down from the Protocols.[^cite_note-211] The Armenian President had made multiple public announcements, both in Armenia and abroad, that, as the leader of the political majority of Armenia, he assured the parliamentary ratification of the protocols if Turkey also ratified them. Despite this, the process stopped, as Turkey continuously added more preconditions to its ratification and also "delayed it beyond any reasonable time-period".

Armenia maintains close cultural, economic, and security ties with Russia. Due to the republic's position between two hostile neighbours, the security component is especially important. At the request of the Armenian government, Russia maintains a [military base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_102nd_Military_Base) in the city of [Gyumri](1529357.md) in northwestern Armenia as a deterrent against NATO member Turkey.[^cite_note-212] At the same time, Armenia has friendly relations with the United States, which is home to the second largest [Armenian diaspora](214413.md) community in the world after Russia. According to the [US Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census), there are 427,822 [Armenian Americans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Americans) in the country.[^cite_note-213]



Russian President [Dmitry Medvedev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmitry_Medvedev) at the [Armenian Genocide memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsitsernakaberd) in Yerevan

Because of the illicit [border blockades by Azerbaijan and Turkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish%E2%80%93Azeri_blockade_of_Armenia_\(1989%E2%80%93ongoing\)), Armenia also maintains strong relations with its southern neighbour, Iran, especially in the economic sector. Economic projects are being developed between the two nations, including a gas pipeline going from Iran to Armenia.

Armenia is a member of the Council of Europe and maintains close relations with the [European Union](9317.md); especially with its [member states](10890716.md) France and Greece. In January 2002, the [European Parliament](9581.md) noted that Armenia may enter the EU in the future.[^cite_note-214] A 2005 survey reported that 64% of Armenians favoured joining the EU,[^cite_note-215] a move multiple Armenian officials have voiced support for.[^cite_note-216]

A former Soviet republic and an [emerging democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerging_democracy), Armenia was negotiating to become an associate EU partner and had completed negotiations to sign an [Association Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Union_Association_Agreement) with a [Deep and Comprehensive Free Trade Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_and_Comprehensive_Free_Trade_Area) with the EU in 2013. However, the government opted not to finalize [the agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EU%E2%80%93Armenia_Partnership_and_Cooperation_Agreement#Cancellation_of_Association_Agreement), and instead joined the [Eurasian Economic Union](27290106.md).[^cite_note-217][^cite_note-218][^cite_note-219] Despite this, Armenia and the EU finalized the [Armenia-EU Comprehensive and Enhanced Partnership Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia-EU_Comprehensive_and_Enhanced_Partnership_Agreement) (CEPA) on 24 November 2017. The agreement enhances the relationship between Armenia and the EU to a new partnership level, further develops cooperation in economic, trade and political areas, aims to improve investment climate, and is designed to bring [Armenian law](43824388.md) gradually closer to the [EU acquis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acquis_communautaire).[^cite_note-220][^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222]

Legally speaking, Armenia has the right to be considered as a [prospective EU member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potential_enlargement_of_the_European_Union) provided it meets necessary standards and criteria, though officially such a plan does not exist in Brussels.[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225][^cite_note-226] Armenia is included in the EU's [European Neighbourhood Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Neighbourhood_Policy) (ENP) and participates in both the [Eastern Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Partnership) and the [Euronest Parliamentary Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euronest_Parliamentary_Assembly), which aims at bringing the EU and its neighbours closer.

Following the [2023 Azerbaijani offensive in Nagorno-Karabakh](74857564.md), Armenia's relations with a long-term ally Russia started to deteriorate. In February 2024, Armenian Prime Minister [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md) said that the CSTO "hasn't fulfilled its security obligations towards Armenia" and that "in practice we have basically frozen our participation in the CSTO".[^cite_note-227] On 28 February 2024, during a speech made in the [National Assembly](4014302.md), Pashinyan further stated that the CSTO is "a threat to the national security of Armenia".[^cite_note-228] In March 2024, Armenia officially expelled Russian border guards from the [Zvartnots International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvartnots_International_Airport) in Yerevan.[^cite_note-229]

On 2 March 2024, Armenian Prime Minister Nikol Pashinyan advised that Armenia would officially "apply to become a candidate for EU membership in the coming days, within a month at most".[^cite_note-230][^cite_note-231] On 5 March, Pashinyan stated that Armenia would apply for EU candidacy by Autumn 2024 at the latest.[^cite_note-232] On 8 March 2024, Armenian Foreign Minister [Ararat Mirzoyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ararat_Mirzoyan) stated, "Armenia is seeking to get closer to the West amid worsening relations with Russia" and "New opportunities are largely being discussed in Armenia nowadays, that includes membership in the European Union".[^cite_note-233][^cite_note-234]

On 12 February 2025, Armenia's parliament approved a bill officially endorsing [Armenia's EU accession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_Armenia_to_the_European_Union). The decision for the government to pass the bill was reported to be the first step of "the beginning of the accession process of Armenia to the European Union".[^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236]

### Military

Main article: [Armed Forces of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_Armenia)

See also: [Military history of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Armenia)



[Armenian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Air_Force) [Su-25s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Su-25) during a military parade

The [Armenian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Army) and [Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Air_Force) are the two branches of the Armed Forces of Armenia. The Armenian military was formed after the [collapse of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union_\(1985%E2%80%9391\)) in 1991 and with the establishment of the Ministry of Defence in 1992. The [Commander-in-Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commander-in-chief) of the military is the [Prime Minister of Armenia](483914.md), [Nikol Pashinyan](17897961.md). The Ministry of Defence is in charge of political leadership, headed by [Davit Tonoyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Tonoyan), while military command remains in the hands of the general staff, headed by the [Chief of Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_General_Staff_\(Armenia\)), who is Lieutenant-General [Onik Gasparyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onik_Gasparyan).

Active forces number about 81,000 soldiers, with an additional [reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_reserve_force) of 32,000 troops. Armenian border guards are in charge of patrolling the country's borders with [Georgia](48768.md) and [Azerbaijan](746.md), while Russian troops continue to monitor its borders with Iran and Turkey. In the case of an attack, Armenia is able to mobilize every able-bodied man between the age of 15 and 59, with military preparedness.

The [Treaty on Conventional Armed Forces in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_Conventional_Armed_Forces_in_Europe), which establishes comprehensive limits on key categories of military equipment, was ratified by the Armenian parliament in July 1992. In March 1993, Armenia signed the multilateral [Chemical Weapons Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_Weapons_Convention), which calls for the eventual elimination of chemical weapons. Armenia acceded to the [Nuclear Non-Proliferation Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Non-Proliferation_Treaty) (NPT) as a non-nuclear weapons state in July 1993. Armenia is a member of the [Collective Security Treaty Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collective_Security_Treaty_Organisation) (CSTO). Armenia also has an [Individual Partnership Action Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_Partnership_Action_Plan) with [NATO](21133.md) and it participates in NATO's [Partnership for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_for_Peace) (PiP) programme and the [Euro-Atlantic Partnership Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro-Atlantic_Partnership_Council) (EAPC).

### Human rights and freedom

Main article: [Human rights in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Armenia)



In April 2018, a quasi-authoritarian [regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serzh_Sargsyan) collapsed as a result of a nationwide [protest movement in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Armenian_revolution).

Armenia scored 5.63 on [The Economist Democracy Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist_Democracy_Index), published in January 2023 (data for 2022). Although still classified as "[hybrid regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hybrid_regime)", Armenia recorded the strongest improvement among European countries and reached its ever-best score since calculation began in 2006.[^cite_note-237]

Armenia is classified as "partly free" in the 2019 report (with data from 2018) by [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House), which gives it a score of 51 out of 100,[^cite_note-238] which is 6 points ahead of the previous estimate.[^cite_note-239]

Armenia recorded unprecedented progress in the 2019 [World Press Freedom Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Freedom_Index) published by [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md), improving its position by 19 points and ranking 61st on the list. The publication also confirms the absence of cases of killed journalists, citizen journalists or media assistants.[^cite_note-240][^cite_note-241]

Armenia ranks 26th in the 2022 report of The Human Freedom Index published by the American [CATO Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CATO_Institute) and Canada's [Fraser Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Institute).[^cite_note-242]

Armenia ranked 29th for economic freedom and 76th for personal freedom among 159 countries in the 2017 [Human Freedom Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_freedom_indices) published by the [Cato Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cato_Institute).[^cite_note-243][^cite_note-244]

In October 2023 Armenia ratified signing the [Rome statute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rome_statute), whereby Armenia will become a full member of the [International Criminal Court](14880.md).[^cite_note-245]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Armenia)

The economy relies heavily on investment and support from Armenians abroad.[^cite_note-246] Before independence, Armenia's economy was largely industry-based – [chemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_substance), [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), machinery, [processed food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), [synthetic rubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Synthetic_rubber), and textile – and highly dependent on outside resources. The republic had developed a modern [industrial sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy), supplying [machine tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_tool), textiles, and other [manufactured goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Final_goods) to sister republics in exchange for raw materials and energy.[^cite_note-CIA-5]

Agriculture accounted for less than 20% of both [net material product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_material_product) and [total employment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full_employment) before the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Soviet_Union_\(1985%E2%80%931991\)) in 1991. After independence, the importance of agriculture in the economy increased markedly, its share at the end of the 1990s rising to more than 30% of GDP and more than 40% of total employment.[^cite_note-agri-247] This increase in the importance of agriculture was attributable to food security needs of the population in the face of uncertainty during the first phases of [transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transition_economy) and the collapse of the non-agricultural sectors of the economy in the early 1990s. As the economic situation stabilised and growth resumed, the share of agriculture in GDP dropped to slightly over 20% (2006 data), although the share of agriculture in employment remained more than 40%.[^cite_note-yb2007-248]



[Yerevan](34352.md) is the economic and cultural centre of Armenia.

Armenian mines produce copper, zinc, gold, and lead. The vast majority of energy is produced with fuel imported from Russia, including gas and nuclear fuel (for its one nuclear power plant); the main domestic energy source is hydroelectric. Small deposits of coal, gas, and petroleum exist but have not yet been developed.

Access to [biocapacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biocapacity) in Armenia is lower than world average. In 2016, Armenia had 0.8 global hectares [^cite_note-footprintdata-249] of biocapacity per person within its territory, much less than the world average of 1.6 global hectares per person.[^cite_note-250] In 2016 Armenia used 1.9 global hectares of biocapacity per person—their [ecological footprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecological_footprint) of consumption. This means they use double as much biocapacity as Armenia contains. As a result, Armenia is running a biocapacity deficit.[^cite_note-footprintdata-249]

Like other [newly independent states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states) of the former Soviet Union, Armenia's economy suffers from the breakdown of former Soviet trading patterns. Soviet investment in and support of Armenian industry has virtually disappeared, so that few major enterprises are still able to function. In addition, the effects of the [1988 Spitak earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Armenian_earthquake), which killed more than 25,000 people and made 500,000 homeless, are still being felt. The conflict with Azerbaijan over Nagorno-Karabakh has not been resolved. Shutdown of the nuclear power plant in 1989 led to the [Armenian energy crisis of 1990s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_energy_crisis_of_1990s). The GDP fell nearly 60% between 1989 and 1993, but then resumed robust growth after the power plant was reopened in 1995.[^cite_note-agri-247] The national currency, the dram, suffered hyperinflation for the first years after its introduction in 1993.

Nevertheless, the government was able to make wide-ranging economic reforms that paid off in dramatically lower inflation and steady growth. The 1994 ceasefire in the Nagorno-Karabakh conflict has also helped the economy. Armenia has had strong economic growth since 1995, building on the turnaround that began the previous year, and inflation has been negligible for the past several years. New sectors, such as precious-stone processing and jewelry making, information and communication technology and [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Armenia) are beginning to supplement more traditional sectors of the economy, such as agriculture.[^cite_note-251]

This steady economic progress has earned Armenia increasing support from international institutions. The [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) (IMF), [World Bank](36752.md), [European Bank for Reconstruction and Development](37963.md) (EBRD), and other [international financial institutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_financial_system) (IFIs) and foreign countries are extending considerable grants and loans. Loans to Armenia since 1993 exceed \$1.1 billion. These loans are targeted at reducing the budget deficit and stabilising the currency; developing private businesses; energy; agriculture; food processing; transportation; the health and education sectors; and ongoing rehabilitation in the earthquake zone. The government joined the WTO on 5 February 2003. But one of the main sources of foreign direct investments remains the Armenian diaspora, which finances major parts of the reconstruction of infrastructure and other public projects. Being a growing democratic state, Armenia also hopes to get more financial aid from the Western World.

A liberal foreign investment law was approved in June 1994, and a law on privatisation was adopted in 1997, as well as a programme of state property privatisation. Continued progress will depend on the ability of the government to strengthen its macroeconomic management, including increasing revenue collection, improving the investment climate, and making strides against corruption. However, unemployment, which was 18.5% in 2015,[^cite_note-252] still remains a major problem due to the influx of thousands of refugees from the Karabakh conflict.

In 2017, the economy grew by 7.5% due to rising copper prices.[^cite_note-Fischer-2018-198]

In 2022, Armenia's GDP stood at \$39.4 billion, and enjoyed an economic freedom index of 65.3, according to Heritage Organisation.[^cite_note-253]

The Armenian economy is predicted to grow by 13% in 2022 due to a huge influx of Russian citizens.[^cite_note-254] The IMF's preliminary forecast as of March 2022 predicted growth of 1.5% for the year.[^cite_note-255]

### Science and technology

Main article: [Science and technology in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_in_Armenia)

Research spending is low in Armenia, averaging 0.25% of GDP over 2010–2013. However, the statistical record of research expenditure is incomplete, as expenditure by privately owned business enterprises is not surveyed in Armenia. The world average for domestic expenditure on research was 1.7% of GDP in 2013.[^cite_note-Erocal-2015-256]



Gross domestic expenditure on R\&D (GERD) to GDP ratio for the Black Sea countries, 2001–2013. Source: UNESCO Science Report: towards 2030 (2015), Figure 12.3.

The country's *Strategy for the Development of Science 2011–2020* envisions that 'by 2020, Armenia is a country with a knowledge-based economy and is competitive within the European Research Area with its level of basic and applied research.' It fixes the following targets:[^cite_note-Erocal-2015-256]

- Creation of a system capable of sustaining the development of science and technology;

- Development of scientific potential, modernisation of scientific infrastructure;

- Promotion of basic and applied research;

- Creation of a synergistic system of education, science and innovation; and

- Becoming a prime location for scientific specialisation in the European Research Area.

Based on this strategy, the accompanying *Action Plan* was approved by the government in June 2011. It defines the following targets:[^cite_note-Erocal-2015-256]

- Improve the management system for science and technology and create the requisite conditions for sustainable development;

- Involve more young, talented people in education and research, while upgrading research infrastructure;

- Create the requisite conditions for the development of an integrated national innovation system; and

- Enhance international co-operation in research and development.



GERD in the Black Sea region by sector of performance, 2005 and 2013. Source: UNESCO Science Report: towards 2030 (2015), Figure 12.5.

Although the *Strategy* clearly pursues a 'science push' approach, with public research institutes serving as the key policy target, it nevertheless mentions the goal of establishing an innovation system. However, the main driver of innovation, the business sector, is not mentioned. In between publishing the *Strategy* and *Action Plan*, the government issued a resolution in May 2010 on *Science and Technology Development Priorities for 2010–2014*. These priorities are:[^cite_note-Erocal-2015-256]

- Armenian studies, humanities and social sciences;

- Life sciences;

- Renewable energy, new energy sources;

- Advanced technologies, information technologies;

- Space, Earth sciences, sustainable use of natural resources; and

- Basic research promoting essential applied research.

The Law on the National Academy of Sciences was adopted in May 2011. This law is expected to play a key role in shaping the Armenian innovation system. It allows the National Academy of Sciences to extend its business activities to the commercialisation of research results and the creation of spin-offs; it also makes provision for restructuring the National Academy of Sciences by combining institutes involved in closely related research areas into a single body. Three of these new centres are particularly relevant: the Centre for Biotechnology, the Centre for Zoology and Hydro-ecology and the Centre for Organic and Pharmaceutical Chemistry.[^cite_note-Erocal-2015-256]

The government is focusing its support on selected industrial sectors. More than 20 projects have been cofunded by the State Committee of Science in targeted branches: pharmaceuticals, medicine and biotechnology, agricultural mechanisation and machine building, electronics, engineering, chemistry and, in particular, the sphere of information technology.[^cite_note-Erocal-2015-256]

Over the past decade, the government has made an effort to encourage science–industry linkages. The Armenian information technology sector has been particularly active: a number of public–private partnerships have been established between companies and universities, in order to give students marketable skills and generate innovative ideas at the interface of science and business. Examples are Synopsys Inc. and the Enterprise Incubator Foundation.[^cite_note-Erocal-2015-256] Armenia was ranked 59th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025.[^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258]

## Demographics

Main articles: [Demographics of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Armenia) and [Armenians](387816.md)



Population pyramid 2016

Armenia has a population of 3,081,100 as of 2025.[^cite_note-259] It is the third most densely populated of the former Soviet republics.[^cite_note-260] The country has faced [population decline](720599.md) since the breakup of the [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) due to high levels of [emigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emigration);[^cite_note-261] the population peaked at 3.6 million in 1991. However, since 2012, the emigration rate has slowed, and some population growth has been observed.[^cite_note-262]



The [Armenian population around the world](214413.md)

Armenia has [a relatively large external diaspora](214413.md)—8 million by some estimates, greatly exceeding the 3 million people in Armenia itself—which is dispersed across the globe. The largest Armenian communities outside of Armenia are in Russia (1.1 million–2.9 million), the United States (1 million–1.5 million), and France (250,000–750,000); sizable numbers are also found in Georgia, Syria, Lebanon, Australia, Canada, Greece, Cyprus, Israel, Poland, Ukraine, and Brazil. Roughly 40,000 to 70,000 Armenians still live in Turkey (mostly in and around [Istanbul](3391396.md)),[^cite_note-263] not including between 500,000 and 3 million Turkish citizens who have ["hidden" Armenian ancestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hidden_Armenians).

About 1,000 Armenians reside in the [Armenian Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Quarter) in the [Old City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Jerusalem\)) of [Jerusalem](16043.md), a remnant of a once-larger community.[^cite_note-264] Italy is home to the [San Lazzaro degli Armeni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Lazzaro_degli_Armeni), an island located in the [Venetian Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_Lagoon), which is completely occupied by a monastery run by the [Mechitarists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechitarists), an [Armenian Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church) congregation.[^cite_note-265]

Approximately 139,000 Armenians lived in the *de facto* independent country [Republic of Artsakh](1000530.md), where they formed a majority before 1 October 2023, when [almost the entire population](74922032.md) fled to Armenia.[^cite_note-266][^cite_note-267]

At the beginning of the 21st century ethnic [Armenians](387816.md) made up 98.1% of the population. [Yazidis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidi) 1.1%, and Russians 0.5%. Other minorities include [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyrian_people), [Ukrainians](145144.md), [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontic_Greeks) (usually called [Caucasus Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Greeks)), [Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_people), [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgians), [Belarusians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusians), and [Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Armenia). There are also smaller communities of [Vlachs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlachs), [Mordvins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mordvins), [Ossetians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ossetians), [Udis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Udi_people), and [Tats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tat_people_\(Caucasus\)). Minorities of [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) and [Caucasus Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus_Germans) also exist though they are heavily [Russified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification).[^cite_note-minorities-268] As of 2022, there are 31,077 Yazidis in Armenia.[^cite_note-Guardian-269]

During the [Soviet era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_era), [Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis) were historically the second largest population in the country, numbering 76,550 in 1922,[^cite_note-270] and forming about 2.5% in 1989.[^cite_note-1989census-271] However, due to the conflict over [Nagorno-Karabakh](65220.md), virtually all of them emigrated from Armenia to Azerbaijan. Conversely, Armenia received a large influx of Armenian refugees from Azerbaijan, thus giving Armenia a more homogeneous character.

According to Gallup research conducted in 2017 Armenia has one of the highest migrant acceptance (welcoming) rates in eastern Europe.[^cite_note-272]

### Languages

Main article: [Languages of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Armenia)



Armenian-language writing

Armenians have their own distinct [alphabet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_alphabet) and [language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language),[^cite_note-273] which is the only official language. The alphabet, invented by Mesrop Mashtots, consists of thirty-nine letters, three of which were added during the [Cilician period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Cilicia).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHacikyanBasmajianFranchukOuzounian200087-79] The main foreign languages that Armenians know are Russian and English. Due to its Soviet past, most of the old population can speak Russian quite well. According to a 2013 survey, 95% of Armenians said they had some knowledge of Russian (24% advanced, 59% intermediate) compared to 40% who said they knew some English (4% advanced, 16% intermediate and 20% beginner). However, more adults (50%) think that English should be taught in public secondary schools than those who prefer Russian (44%).[^cite_note-2012_knowledge-274]

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Armenia)



The 7th-century [Khor Virap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khor_Virap) monastery in the shadow of [Mount Ararat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Ararat), the peak on which [Noah's Ark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noah's_Ark) is said to have landed during the [biblical flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genesis_flood_narrative).



Portal to the Holy See at Etchmiadzin, the seat of the Catholicos

The predominant [religion of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Armenia) is Christianity. Its roots in the country go back to the 1st century AD, when it arrived by way of the apostles Thaddaeus and Bartholomew.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHacikyanBasmajianFranchukOuzounian200075–76-75][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWalker199024-76] Armenia was the first nation to adopt [Christianity](5211.md) as a [state religion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_religion),[^cite_note-stringer-20] an event traditionally dated to AD 301.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHacikyanBasmajianFranchukOuzounian200077–79-77] Over 93% of Christians in Armenia belong to the [Armenian Apostolic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Apostolic_Church),[^cite_note-census1-275][^cite_note-CIA-5] which is in communion only with the churches comprising [Oriental Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy#Oriental_Orthodox_Communion)—of which it is itself a member.[^cite_note-276]

The [Catholic Church](606848.md) maintains jurisdictions of both the [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) and [Armenian Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Catholic_Church) in Armenia. Of note are the [Mechitarists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechitarists) (also spelled "Mekhitarists" [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language): Մխիթարեան), a congregation of [Benedictine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Saint_Benedict) monks in the Armenian Catholic Church, founded in 1712 by [Mekhitar of Sebaste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mekhitar_of_Sebaste). They are best known for their series of scholarly publications of ancient [Armenian](387816.md) versions of otherwise lost ancient Greek texts.

Other Christian denominations include the [Armenian Evangelical Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Evangelical_Church) and the Pentecostal branches of the Protestant community. These include the Word of Life, the [Armenian Brotherhood Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Brotherhood_Church),[^cite_note-277] the [Baptists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptists) (which are known as one of the oldest existing denominations in Armenia, and were permitted by the authorities of the Soviet Union),[^cite_note-278][^cite_note-279] and [Presbyterians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterians).[^cite_note-280]

Armenia is also home to a [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Armenia) community of [Molokans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molokan) which practice a form of Spiritual Christianity originated from the [Russian Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church).[^cite_note-281]

The Yazidis, who live in the western part of the country, practice [Yazidism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yazidism).[^cite_note-282] The world's largest Yazidi temple, [Quba Mêrê Dîwanê](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quba_M%C3%AAr%C3%AA_D%C3%AEwan%C3%AA), was completed in 2019[^cite_note-Al_Jazeera-283] in the village of [Aknalich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aknalich).[^cite_note-Guardian-269]

There is a [Jewish community in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Armenia) of approximately 750 people since independence with most emigrants leaving for Israel. There are currently two synagogues in Armenia – one in the capital, Yerevan, and the other in the city of [Sevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sevan_\(city\)) located near [Lake Sevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Sevan).

### Education

Main articles: [Education in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Armenia) and [List of universities in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Armenia)



[Yerevan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerevan_State_University) building

In medieval times, the [University of Gladzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Gladzor) and [University of Tatev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatev_Monastery#Tatev_University) took an important role for Armenian education.

A literacy rate of 100% was reported as early as 1960.[^cite_note-loc-284] [In the communist era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), Armenian education followed the standard Soviet model of complete state control (from Moscow) of [curricula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curriculum) and teaching methods and close integration of education activities with other aspects of society, such as politics, culture, and the economy.[^cite_note-loc-284]

In the 1988–89 school year, 301 students per 10,000 were in specialized secondary or higher education, a figure slightly lower than the Soviet average.[^cite_note-loc-284] In 1989, some 58% of Armenians over age fifteen had completed their secondary education, and 14% had a higher education.[^cite_note-loc-284] In the 1990–91 school year, the estimated 1,307 primary and secondary schools were attended by 608,800 students.[^cite_note-loc-284] Another seventy specialised secondary institutions had 45,900 students, and 68,400 students were enrolled in a total of ten postsecondary institutions that included universities.[^cite_note-loc-284] In addition, 35% of eligible children attended [preschools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preschool_education).[^cite_note-loc-284] In 1992 Armenia's largest institution of higher learning, [Yerevan State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerevan_State_University), had eighteen departments, including ones for social sciences, sciences, and law.[^cite_note-loc-284] Its faculty numbered about 1,300 teachers and its student population about 10,000 students.[^cite_note-loc-284] The [National Polytechnic University of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Polytechnic_University_of_Armenia) is operating since 1933.[^cite_note-loc-284]

In the early 1990s, Armenia made substantial changes to the centralised and regimented Soviet system.[^cite_note-loc-284] Because at least 98% of students in higher education were Armenian, curricula began to emphasise [Armenian history](87368.md) and culture.[^cite_note-loc-284] [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_language) became the dominant language of instruction, and many schools that had taught in Russian closed by the end of 1991.[^cite_note-loc-284] Russian was still widely taught, however, as a second language.[^cite_note-loc-284]

In 2014, the National Programme for Educational Excellence embarked on creating an internationally competitive and academically rigorous alternative educational programme (the Araratian Baccalaureate) for Armenian schools and increasing the importance and status of the teacher's role in society.[^cite_note-285][^cite_note-286]

The Ministry of Education and Science is responsible for regulation of the sector. Primary and secondary education in Armenia is free, and completion of secondary school is compulsory.[^cite_note-loc-284] Higher education in Armenia is harmonized with the [Bologna process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologna_Process) and the [European Higher Education Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Higher_Education_Area). The [Armenian National Academy of Sciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_National_Academy_of_Sciences) plays an important role in postgraduate education.

Schooling takes 12 years in Armenia and breaks down into primary (4 years), middle (5 years) and high school (3 years). Schools engage a 10-grade mark system. The government also supports [Armenian schools outside of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Armenian_schools#Schools_outside_Armenia).

Gross enrollment in [tertiary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education) at 44% in 2015 surpassed peer countries of the South Caucasus but remained below the average for Europe and Central Asia.[^cite_note-287] However, public spending per student in tertiary education in GDP-ratio terms is one of the lowest for post-USSR countries (for which data was available).[^cite_note-288]

### Health

Main article: [Health in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Armenia)

Healthcare in Armenia has undergone significant changes since independence in 1991. Initially, the Soviet healthcare system was highly centralized and provided free medical assistance to all citizens. After independence, the healthcare system underwent reform and primary care services have been free of charge since 2006. Despite improvements in accessibility and the implementation of an Open Enrollment programme, out-of-pocket health expenditures remain high and corruption among healthcare professionals remains a concern.[^cite_note-289] In 2019, healthcare became free for all citizens under the age of 18 and the number of people receiving free or subsidized care under the Basic Benefits Package was increased.[^cite_note-290][^cite_note-291]

After a significant decline in earlier decades, crude[^cite_note-292] birth rates in Armenia slightly increased from 13.0 (per 1000 people) in the year 1998 to 14.2 in 2015;[^cite_note-293] this timeframe also showed a similar trajectory in the crude death rate, which grew from 8.6 to 9.3.[^cite_note-294] [Life expectancy at birth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life_expectancy) at 74.8 years was the 4th-highest among the [Post-Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states) in 2014.[^cite_note-295]

### Architecture

Main article: [Armenian architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_architecture)



[Zvartnots Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zvartnots_Cathedral), 643–652, [UNESCO World Heritage Site](44940.md)

Armenian architecture, as it originates in an earthquake-prone region, tends to be built with this hazard in mind. Armenian buildings tend to be rather low-slung and thick-walled in design. Armenia has abundant resources of stone, and relatively few forests, so stone was nearly always used throughout for large buildings. Small buildings and most residential buildings were normally constructed of lighter materials, and hardly any early examples survive, as at the abandoned medieval capital of [Ani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ani).[^cite_note-296]

### Art

Main article: [Armenian art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_art)

See also: [List of museums in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Armenia)



Ancient Armenian [Khachkars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khachkar) (cross-stones)

[Yerevan Vernissage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerevan_Vernissage) (arts and crafts market), close to Republic Square, bustles with hundreds of vendors selling a variety of crafts on weekends and Wednesdays (though the selection is much reduced mid-week). The market offers woodcarving, antiques, fine lace, and the hand-knotted wool carpets and kilims that are a Caucasus speciality. [Obsidian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obsidian), which is found locally, is crafted into assortment of jewellery and ornamental objects. Armenian gold smithery enjoys a long tradition, populating one corner of the market with a selection of gold items. Soviet relics and souvenirs of recent Russian manufacture – nesting dolls, watches, enamel boxes and so on – are also available at the Vernissage.

Across from the Opera House, a popular art market fills another city park on the weekends. Armenia's long history as a crossroads of the [ancient world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_history) has resulted in a landscape with numerous [archaeological sites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site) to explore. [Medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age), [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) and even [Stone Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone_Age) sites are all within a few hours' drive from the city. All but the most spectacular remain virtually undiscovered, allowing visitors to view churches and fortresses in their original settings.



*[Queen Zabel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isabella,_Queen_of_Armenia)'s Return to the Palace*, [Vardges Sureniants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vardges_Sureniants) (1909)

The National Art Gallery in Yerevan has more than 16,000 works that date back to the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), which indicate Armenia's rich tales and stories of the times. It houses paintings by many [European](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Europe) masters as well. The Modern Art Museum, the Children's Picture Gallery, and the [Martiros Saryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martiros_Saryan) Museum are only a few of the other noteworthy collections of fine art on display in Yerevan. Moreover, many private galleries are in operation, with many more opening every year, featuring rotating exhibitions and sales.

On 13 April 2013, the Armenian government announced a change in law to allow [freedom of panorama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_panorama) for 3D works of art.[^cite_note-297]

### Media

Main article: [Media of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_Armenia)

Television, magazines, and newspapers are all operated by both state-owned and for-profit corporations which depend on [advertising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advertising), [subscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subscription), and other sales-related revenues. The [Constitution of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Armenia) guarantees freedom of speech and Armenia ranks 61st in the 2020 [Press Freedom Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_Freedom_Index) report compiled by [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md), between [Georgia](48768.md) and Poland.[^cite_note-FH-298] Armenia's press freedom rose considerably following the 2018 Velvet Revolution.[^cite_note-299]

As of 2020, the biggest issue facing press freedom in Armenia is judicial harassment of journalists, specifically defamation suits and attacks on journalists' right to protect sources,[^cite_note-300] as well as excessive responses to combat disinformation spread by social media users. Reporters Without Borders also cites continued concerns about lack of transparency regarding ownership of media outlets.[^cite_note-FH-298]

### Cinema

Main article: [Cinema of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Armenia)

[Cinema in Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Armenia) was officially established on 16 April 1923, with the founding of the Armenian State Committee of Cinema by a decree of the [Soviet Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) government.[^cite_note-301]

Prior to this, the first Armenian film, titled "Haykakan Sinema", was produced in 1912 in [Cairo](6293.md) by [Armenian-Egyptian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenians_in_Egypt) publisher Vahan Zartarian and premiered on 13 March 1913.[^cite_note-302]

In March 1924 the first Armenian film studio, [Armenfilm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenfilm), was established in [Yerevan](34352.md), beginning with the documentary film "Soviet Armenia".

"[Namus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namus_\(film\))" directed by [Hamo Beknazarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamo_Beknazarian) in 1925, was the first Armenian silent [black-and-white film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-and-white).[^cite_note-303] Based on [Alexander Shirvanzade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Shirvanzade)'s 1885 novel, it portrays the tragic fate of two lovers whose engagement is thwarted due to violations of "[namus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namus)" (a tradition of honor), leading the girl's father to marry her to another man.[^cite_note-304]

The first Armenian sound film, "[Pepo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepo_\(film\))," was directed by Hamo Beknazarian in 1935.[^cite_note-305][^cite_note-306]

Chess Grandmaster [Levon Aronian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levon_Aronian) is a former [FIDE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIDE) No. 2 rated player and the [fourth-highest rated player in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_top_chess_players_throughout_history#Elo_system).



[Tigran Petrosyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigran_Petrosyan), 9th [World Chess Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Chess_Champion)

Competitively, Armenia has been successful in chess, weightlifting and wrestling at the international level. Armenia is also an active member of the international sports community, with full membership in the Union of European Football Associations ([UEFA](32332.md)) and International Ice Hockey Federation ([IIHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IIHF)). It also hosts the [Pan-Armenian Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Armenian_Games).

Prior to 1992, Armenians participated in the Olympics representing the USSR. As part of the Soviet Union, Armenia was very successful, winning plenty of medals and helping the USSR win the medal standings at the Olympics on numerous occasions. The first medal won by an Armenian in modern Olympic history was by [Hrant Shahinyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hrant_Shahinyan) (sometimes spelled as Grant Shaginyan), who won two golds and two silvers in gymnastics at the [1952 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1952_Summer_Olympics) in Helsinki. To highlight the level of success of Armenians in the Olympics, Shahinyan was quoted as saying:

"Armenian sportsmen had to outdo their opponents by several notches for the shot at being accepted into any Soviet team. But those difficulties notwithstanding, 90 percent of Armenian athletes on Soviet Olympic teams came back with medals."[^cite_note-ArmeniaNow\.com-307]

Armenia first participated at the [1992 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics) in Barcelona under a unified CIS team, where it was very successful, winning three golds and one silver in weightlifting, wrestling and sharp shooting, despite only having five athletes. Since the [1994 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Winter_Olympics) in [Lillehammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lillehammer), Armenia has participated as an independent nation.

Armenia participates in the Summer Olympic Games in boxing, wrestling, weightlifting, judo, gymnastics, track and field, diving, swimming and sharp shooting. It also participates in the Winter Olympic Games in alpine skiing, cross-country skiing and figure skating.

[Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Armenia) is also popular in Armenia. The most successful team was the [FC Ararat Yerevan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Ararat_Yerevan) team of the 1970s who won the [Soviet Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Cup) in 1973 and 1975 and the [Soviet Top League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Top_League_1973) in 1973. The latter achievement saw FC Ararat gain entry to the [European Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_European_Cup) where – despite a home victory in the second leg – they lost on aggregate at the quarter-final stage to eventual winner [FC Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich). Armenia competed internationally as part of the [USSR national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR_national_football_team) until the [Armenian national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_national_football_team) was formed in 1992 after the split of the Soviet Union. Armenia have never qualified for a major tournament although recent improvements saw the team to achieve 44th position in the [FIFA World Rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_World_Rankings) in September 2011. The national team is controlled by the [Football Federation of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_Federation_of_Armenia). The [Armenian Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Premier_League) is the highest level football competition in Armenia, and has been dominated by [FC Pyunik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Pyunik) in recent seasons. The league currently consists of eight teams and relegates to the [Armenian First League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_First_League).

Armenia and the Armenian diaspora have produced many successful footballers, including [Henrikh Mkhitaryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrikh_Mkhitaryan), [Youri Djorkaeff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youri_Djorkaeff), [Alain Boghossian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alain_Boghossian), [Andranik Eskandarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andranik_Eskandarian), [Andranik Teymourian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andranik_Teymourian), [Edgar Manucharyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Manucharyan), [Khoren Oganesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khoren_Oganesian) and [Nikita Simonyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikita_Simonyan). Djokaeff and Boghossian won the [1998 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_FIFA_World_Cup) with [France](250197.md), Teymourian competed in the [2006 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup) for [Iran](272865.md) and Manucharyan played in the Dutch [Eredivisie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eredivisie) for [Ajax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Ajax). Mkhitaryan has been one of the most successful Armenian footballers in recent years, playing for international clubs such as [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund), [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.), [Arsenal](2174.md), [A.S. Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A.S._Roma) and currently for [Inter Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan).[^cite_note-308]

[Wrestling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrestling) has been a successful sport in the Olympics for Armenia. At the [1996 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenia_at_the_1996_Summer_Olympics) in [Atlanta](3138.md), [Armen Nazaryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armen_Nazaryan) won the gold in the Men's [Greco-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_wrestling) Flyweight (52 kg) category and [Armen Mkrtchyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armen_Mkrtchyan) won the silver in Men's [Freestyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freestyle_wrestling) Paperweight (48 kg) category, securing Armenia's first two medals in its Olympic history.

Traditional Armenian wrestling is called Kokh and practised in traditional garb; it was one of the influences included in the Soviet combat sport of [Sambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambo_\(martial_art\)), which is also very popular.[^cite_note-309]

The [government of Armenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Armenia) budgets about \$2.8 million annually for sports and gives it to the National Committee of Physical Education and Sports, the body that determines which programmes should benefit from the funds.[^cite_note-ArmeniaNow\.com-307]

Due to the lack of success lately on the international level, in recent years, Armenia has rebuilt 16 Soviet-era sports schools and furnished them with new equipment for a total cost of \$1.9 million. The rebuilding of the regional schools was financed by the Armenian government. \$9.3 million has been invested in the resort town of [Tsaghkadzor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsaghkadzor) to improve the [winter sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_sport) infrastructure because of dismal performances at recent winter sports events. In 2005, a cycling centre was opened in [Yerevan](34352.md) with the aim of helping produce world class Armenian cyclists. The government has also promised a cash reward of \$700,000 to Armenians who win a gold medal at the Olympics.[^cite_note-ArmeniaNow\.com-307]

Armenia has also been very successful in chess, winning the [World Champion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Team_Chess_Championship) in 2011 and the [World Chess Olympiad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_Olympiad) on three occasions.[^cite_note-310]
