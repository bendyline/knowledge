# Chinese space program













From left to right, top to bottom: [Fei Junlong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fei_Junlong) conducting a [spacewalk](9792.md) from the [*Tiangong* space station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_Space_Station), [*Shenzhou* 14](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_14) and [15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_15) crews aboard *Tiangong*, [Long March 5B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_5B) launching *[Wentian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wentian_module)*, *[Shenzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_\(spacecraft\))* crewed spacecraft, [*Yutu* lunar rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yutu_\(rover\)), [*Zhurong* rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhurong_rover) on [Mars](14640471.md).

[China](5405.md) has one of the most active [space programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_program) in the world. With [launch vehicles](1306157.md) of the [Long March rocket family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_rocket_family) and four [spaceports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceport) ([Jiuquan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuquan_Satellite_Launch_Center), [Taiyuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiyuan_Satellite_Launch_Center), [Xichang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xichang_Satellite_Launch_Center), [Wenchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenchang_Spacecraft_Launch_Site)), China conducts [the most or second most orbital launches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_spaceflight#Orbital_launches_by_year) each year, and is one of three countries with a [human spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight) capability.[^cite_note-1] China's fleet of over 1,300 [Earth orbit](568972.md) [satellites](27683.md)[^cite_note-x177-2] serves [communication](45207.md), [navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_navigation), [reconnaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconnaissance_satellite) and scientific research.[^cite_note-3] [China Manned Space Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Manned_Space_Program) operates [*Tiangong*](2481401.md), one of two active [space stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_station) alongside the [International Space Station](15043.md) (ISS). Most space operations are conducted by civilian [China National Space Administration](344618.md) (CNSA) and the military [People's Liberation Army Aerospace Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Army_Aerospace_Force) (PLAASF). CNSA has achieved robotic rover, lander, and orbiter missions to the [Moon](19331.md) and [Mars](14640471.md).[^cite_note-\:1-4] PLAASF directs the [Astronaut Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Army_Astronaut_Corps) and its [Deep Space Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Deep_Space_Network).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

From the 1950s, [aided by](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Soviet_Treaty_of_Friendship,_Alliance_and_Mutual_Assistance) the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), development began on [China's nuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_weapons_of_China), [*Dongfeng*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dongfeng_\(missile\)) [ballistic missiles](38025.md), and Long March rockets, honored as the [Two Bombs, One Satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Bombs,_One_Satellite) projects. Despite setbacks from the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution) and [Sino-Soviet split](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Soviet_split), China became the [fifth country to independently launch a satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_first_satellites_by_country), *[Dong Fang Hong 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_Fang_Hong_1)* in 1970. China developed the [*Shenzhou*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_\(spacecraft\)) crewed craft from Russia's [Soyuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_\(spacecraft\)) after a 1995 treaty; [*Shenzhou* 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_5) carried the [first Chinese astronaut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Liwei) in 2003. China [commercially launched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_\(rocket_family\)#Entry_into_commercial_launch_market) Western satellites from 1988, but failures, especially [Intelsat 708](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelsat_708), caused the US to [embargo satellite export](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Traffic_in_Arms_Regulations#Satellite_components) in 1998. After the US [vetoed its joining the ISS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Amendment), China began the [*Tiangong* program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_program), operating prototypes [*Tiangong*-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong-1) and [-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong-2) before the current *Tiangong*, the world's third [modular space station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modular_space_station).[^cite_note-7] Since the 2020s, the program has been contrasted against Western space programs as the "[New Space Race](80436266.md)".

[China's Moon missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Lunar_Exploration_Program) achieved the world's first [far side](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_side_of_the_Moon) landing ([*Chang'e* 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_4)), far side [sample return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample-return_mission) ([*Chang'e* 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_6)), and longest-lived [rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_rover) (*[Yutu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yutu-2)*[-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yutu-2)). [China's interplanetary missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_Exploration_of_China) deployed [*Tianwen*-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-1), a [Mars](14640471.md) [orbiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mars_orbiters), [lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_landing), and the [rover](421366.md) [*Zhurong*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhurong_\(rover\)); [*Tianwen*-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-2) visited [an asteroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/469219_Kamo%CA%BBoalewa) in 2026 and will visit [a comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/311P/PanSTARRS) in 2035. Its [satellite constellations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_constellation) include [BeiDou Navigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beidou), [Gaofen Observation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaofen), and the [Guowang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guowang) and [Qianfan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qianfan) [internet constellations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_internet_constellation). [Long March 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_5) is the [most powerful rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comparison_of_orbital_launch_systems) operating outside of the US, and first Long March to not use [hypergolic propellant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypergolic).

Since 2019, China's private and [state enterprises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned_enterprises_of_China) have made over a hundred launches with new rocket designs, such as the [*Kuaizhou* family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuaizhou) and [Ceres-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceres-1). China is developing [reusable spacecraft](336014.md), including the [Long March 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10) rocket family and [a military spaceplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_reusable_experimental_spacecraft). China [plans a crewed lunar landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Lunar_Exploration_Program#Crewed_mission_phase) by 2030, using the under-development [*Mengzhou*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengzhou_\(spacecraft\)) craft, *[Lanyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanyue)* lander, and [Long March 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10) rocket. After scouting with [*Chang'e* 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_7) and [8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_8), China aims to construct the [International Lunar Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Research_Station) at the [lunar south pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_south_pole) from 2031, collaborating with [Roscosmos](406573.md) and 11 other countries; China previously cooperated with the [European Space Agency](10363.md) on [deep space tracking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESTRACK) and [space science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_science). The [*Tianwen*-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-3) robotic [Mars sample-return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_sample-return_mission) is scheduled for dual launches in 2028, and the [*Tianwen*-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-4) [Jupiter orbiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_of_Jupiter) and [Uranus flyby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_of_Uranus) in 2029; the *[Shensuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shensuo)* probes aim to [flyby Neptune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_of_Neptune).

## International cooperation

### Belt and Road Initiative

One of China's priorities in its [Belt and Road Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_and_Road_Initiative) is to improve satellite information pathways.[^cite_note-\:32-10]<sup>: 300 </sup>

### Bilateral space cooperation

CBERS-4 satellite of the China–Brazil Earth Resources Satellite program

[CBERS-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBERS-4) satellite of the [China–Brazil Earth Resources Satellite program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Brazil_Earth_Resources_Satellite_program)

China is an attractive partner for space cooperation for other developing countries because it launches their satellites at a reduced cost and often provides financing in the form of policy loans.[^cite_note-\:32-10]<sup>: 301 </sup>

With respect to the African countries, the 2022-2024 action plan for the [Forum on China-Africa Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forum_on_China%E2%80%93Africa_Cooperation) commits China to using space technology to enhance cooperation with African countries and to create centers for Africa-China cooperation on satellite remote sensing application.[^cite_note-\:32-10]<sup>: 300 </sup> African countries are increasingly cooperating with China on satellite launches and specialized training.[^cite_note-\:32-10]<sup>: 301 </sup> As of 2022, China has launched two satellites for Ethiopia, two for Nigeria, one for Algeria, one for Sudan, and one for Egypt.[^cite_note-\:32-10]<sup>: 301–302 </sup>

[China and Namibia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Namibia_relations) jointly operate the China Telemetry, Tracking, and Command Station which was established in 2001 in [Swakopmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swakopmund), Namibia.[^cite_note-\:32-10]<sup>: 304 </sup> This station tracks Chinese satellites and space missions.[^cite_note-\:32-10]<sup>: 304 </sup>

[China and Brazil](25412031.md) have successfully cooperated in the field of space.[^cite_note-\:03-11]<sup>: 202 </sup> Among the most successful space cooperation projects were the development and launch of earth monitoring satellites.[^cite_note-\:03-11]<sup>: 202 </sup> As of 2023, the two countries have jointly developed six [China-Brazil Earth Resource Satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China%E2%80%93Brazil_Earth_Resources_Satellite_program).[^cite_note-\:03-11]<sup>: 202 </sup> These projects have helped both Brazil and China develop their access to satellite imagery and promoted remote sending research.[^cite_note-\:03-11]<sup>: 202 </sup> Brazil and China's cooperation is a unique example of [South-South cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South%E2%80%93South_cooperation) between two developing countries in the field of space.[^cite_note-\:03-11]<sup>: 202 </sup>

#### With the European Space Agency

See also: [China–European Union relations](2016720.md)

China has cooperated extensively with the [European Space Agency](10363.md). [Double Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Double_Star_\(satellite\)) was a twin satellite mission the latter called "the first Sino-European satellite", that studied [Earth's magnetosphere](20479.md).[^cite_note-12] ESA's [ESTRACK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESTRACK), including the French [Kourou Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kourou_Station) and Spanish [Maspalomas Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maspalomas_Station), was used to communicate with [Chang'e 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_5) and [Chang'e 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_6), the latter carried a French, an Italian, and a Swedish scientific instrument.[^cite_note-\:6-13] ESA's [Mars Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Express) served as a backup communications satellite for the [*Zhurong*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhurong_\(rover\)) Mars rover if the [Tianwen-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-1) orbiter could transmit commands.[^cite_note-14]

China and Russia initially hoped to attract ESA and its member nations to the [International Lunar Research Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Lunar_Research_Station). In September 2021, Roscosmos and CNSA held private talks in Dubai with representatives of the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_space_program), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_space_programme), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Space_Agency), and [Dutch space efforts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_Space_Agency), as well as Malaysia, Thailand, and the [United Nations Office for Outer Space Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Office_for_Outer_Space_Affairs).[^cite_note-15] This effort was considered dead following the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-\:6-13] In January 2023, ESA stated it had ceased pursuing sending astronauts to the [Tiangong space station](2481401.md).[^cite_note-16] Between 2020 and 2026, all ESA members became party to the US [Artemis Accords](63968963.md), seen as a competing international lunar exploration framework. ESA is not planned to participate in [Chang'e 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_7), which will carry a [Bahrain Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahrain_Space_Agency)-[Egyptian Space Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian_Space_Agency) [hyperspectral imager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperspectral_imager).[^cite_note-\:6-13]

### Dual-use technologies and outer space

The PRC is a member of the [United Nations Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Committee_on_the_Peaceful_Uses_of_Outer_Space) and a signatory to all [United Nations](31769.md) treaties and conventions on space, with the exception of the 1979 [Moon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Treaty).[^cite_note-17] The United States government has long been resistant to the use of PRC launch services by American industry due to concerns over alleged civilian technology transfer that could have dual-use military applications to countries such as [North Korea](21255.md), [Iran](14653.md) or [Syria](7515849.md). Thus, financial retaliatory measures have been taken on many occasions against several Chinese space companies.[^cite_note-18]

### NASA's policy excluding Chinese state affiliates

Further information: [Wolf Amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf_Amendment)

The [Cox Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox_Report), released in 1999, alleged that following decades of intelligence operations against U.S. weapons laboratories conducted by the [Ministry of State Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_State_Security_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China), [China stole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_espionage_in_the_United_States) design information regarding advanced [thermonuclear weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermonuclear_weapon). In 2011, Congress passed a law prohibiting [NASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Aeronautics_and_Space_Administration) researchers from working with [Chinese](5405.md) citizens affiliated with a Chinese state enterprise or entity without FBI certification or using NASA funds to host Chinese visitors.[^cite_note-justice1-19] In March 2013, the U.S. Congress passed legislation barring Chinese nationals from entering NASA facilities without a waiver from NASA.[^cite_note-\:5-20]

The history of the U.S. exclusion policy can be traced back to the [Cox Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cox_Report)'s allegations that the technical information that American companies provided China for its commercial satellite ended up improving Chinese intercontinental ballistic missile technology.[^cite_note-\:3-21] This was further aggravated in 2007 when China blew up a defunct meteorological satellite in [low Earth orbit](47568.md) to test a ground-based [anti-satellite (ASAT) missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-satellite_weapon). The debris created by the explosion contributed to the [space junk](266344.md) that litter Earth's orbit, exposing other nations' space assets to the risk of accidental collision.[^cite_note-\:3-21] The United States also fears the Chinese application of dual-use space technology for nefarious purposes.[^cite_note-\:4-22]

The Chinese response to the exclusion policy involved its own space policy of opening up its space station to the outside world, welcoming scientists coming from all countries.[^cite_note-\:4-22] American scientists have also boycotted NASA conferences due to its rejection of Chinese nationals in these events.[^cite_note-\:5-20] In September 2025, NASA prohibited Chinese nationals from working with its programs.[^cite_note-23]

## Organization

Initially, the space program of the PRC was organized under the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md), particularly the [Second Artillery Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Artillery_Corps) (now the [PLA Rocket Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Army_Rocket_Force), PLARF). In the 1990s, the PRC reorganized the space program as part of a general reorganization of the defense industry to make it resemble Western defense procurement.

The China National Space Administration, an agency within the [State Administration of Science, Technology and Industry for National Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Administration_of_Science,_Technology_and_Industry_for_National_Defense), is now responsible for launches. The Long March rocket is produced by the China Academy of Launch Vehicle Technology, and satellites are produced by the China Aerospace Science and Technology Corporation. The latter organizations are [state-owned enterprises](204768.md); however, it is the intent of the PRC government that they should not be actively state-managed and that they should behave as independent design bureaus.

## Notable spaceflight programs

### Project 714

Main article: [Shuguang (spacecraft)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuguang_\(spacecraft\))

As the [Space Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Race) between the two superpowers reached its climax with humans landing on the Moon, Mao Zedong and Zhou Enlai decided on July 14, 1967, that the PRC should not be left behind, and therefore initiated China's own crewed space program. The top-secret Project 714 aimed to put two people into space by 1973 with the [Shuguang spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuguang_spacecraft). Nineteen [PLAAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PLAAF) pilots were selected for this goal in March 1971. The Shuguang-1 spacecraft to be launched with the [CZ-2A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CZ-2A) rocket was designed to carry a crew of two. The program was officially cancelled on May 13, 1972, for economic reasons, though the internal politics of the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution) likely motivated the closure.

The short-lived second crewed program was based on the successful implementation of landing technology (third in the World after [USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSR) and United States) by [FSW satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanhui_Shi_Weixing). It was announced a few times in 1978 with the open publishing of some details including photos, but then was abruptly canceled in 1980. It has been argued that the second crewed program was created solely for propaganda purposes, and was never intended to produce results.[^cite_note-Astronautix-37]

### Project 863

A new crewed space program was proposed by the Chinese Academy of Sciences in March 1986, as *Astronautics plan 863-2*. This consisted of a crewed spacecraft (Project 863–204) used to ferry astronaut crews to a space station (Project 863–205). In September of that year, astronauts in training were presented by the Chinese media. The various proposed crewed spacecraft were mostly spaceplanes. Project 863 ultimately evolved into the 1992 *Project 921*.

### China Manned Space Program (Project 921)

Main article: [China Manned Space Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Manned_Space_Program)

#### Spacecraft

Return capsule and parachute of Shenzhou spacecraft

Return capsule and parachute of [Shenzhou spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_spacecraft)

Exhibition of China Manned Space Program at the National Museum of China in 2023

Exhibition of [China Manned Space Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Manned_Space_Program) at the [National Museum of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_China) in 2023

In 1992, authorization and funding were given for the first phase of Project 921, which was a plan to launch a crewed spacecraft. The Shenzhou program had four uncrewed test flights and two crewed missions. The first one was [Shenzhou 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_1) on November 20, 1999. On January 9, 2001 [Shenzhou 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_2) launched carrying test animals. [Shenzhou 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_3) and [Shenzhou 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_4) were launched in 2002, carrying test dummies. Following these was the successful [Shenzhou 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_5), China's first crewed mission in space on October 15, 2003, which carried [Yang Liwei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yang_Liwei) in orbit for 21 hours and made China the third nation to launch a human into orbit. [Shenzhou 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_6) followed two years later ending the first phase of Project 921. Missions are launched on the [Long March 2F](340934.md) rocket from the Jiuquan Satellite Launch Center. The China Manned Space Agency (CMSA) of the [Equipment Development Department of the Central Military Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equipment_Development_Department_of_the_Central_Military_Commission) provides engineering and administrative support for the crewed Shenzhou missions.[^cite_note-38]

#### Space laboratory

Main article: [Project 921-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_921-2)

The second phase of the Project 921 started with [Shenzhou 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_7), China's first spacewalk mission. Then, two crewed missions were planned to the first Chinese space laboratory. The PRC initially designed the Shenzhou spacecraft with docking technologies imported from Russia, therefore compatible with the [International Space Station](15043.md) (ISS). On September 29, 2011, China launched [Tiangong 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_1). This target module is intended to be the first step to testing the technology required for a planned space station.

On October 31, 2011, a Long March 2F rocket lifted the [Shenzhou 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_8) uncrewed spacecraft which docked twice with the Tiangong 1 module. The [Shenzhou 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_9) craft took off on 16 June 2012 with a crew of 3. It successfully docked with the Tiangong-1 laboratory on 18 June 2012, at 06\:07 UTC, marking China's first crewed spacecraft docking.[^cite_note-39] Another crewed mission, [Shenzhou 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_10), launched on 11 June 2013. The Tiangong 1 target module is then expected to be deorbited.[^cite_note-40]

A second space lab, [Tiangong 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_2), launched on 15 September 2016, 22\:04\:09 (UTC+8).[^cite_note-41] The launch mass was 8,600 kg, with a length of 10.4m and a width of 3.35m, much like the Tiangong 1.[^cite_note-Rui_Barbosa-42] [Shenzhou 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_11) launched and rendezvoused with Tiangong 2 in October 2016, with an unconfirmed further mission Shenzhou 12 in the future. The Tiangong 2 brings with it the POLAR gamma ray burst detector, a space-Earth quantum key distribution, and laser communications experiment to be used in conjunction with the Mozi 'Quantum Science Satellite', a liquid bridge thermocapillary convection experiment, and a space material experiment. Also included is a stereoscopic microwave altimeter, a space plant growth experiment, and a multi-angle wide-spectral imager and multi-spectral limb imaging spectrometer. Onboard TG-2 there will also be the world's first-ever in-space cold atomic fountain clock.[^cite_note-Rui_Barbosa-42]

#### Space station

Main articles: [Tiangong Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_Space_Station) and [Tiangong program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_program)

A larger basic permanent space station (基本型空间站) would be the third and last phase of Project 921. This will be a modular design with an eventual weight of around 60 tons, to be completed sometime before 2022. The first section, designated [Tiangong 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_3), was scheduled for launch after Tiangong 2,[^cite_note-sdc20110307-43] but ultimately not ordered after its goals were merged with Tiangong 2.[^cite_note-44]

This could also be the beginning of China's crewed international cooperation, the existence of which was officially disclosed for the first time after the launch of Shenzhou 7.[^cite_note-45]

The first module of [Tiangong space station](2481401.md), [*Tianhe* core module](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianhe_\(space_station_module\)), was launched on 29 April 2021, from Wenchang Space Launch Site.[^cite_note-Tianhe-launch-46] It was first visited by [Shenzhou 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_12) crew on 17 June 2021. The Chinese space station is scheduled to be completed in 2022[^cite_note-47] and fully operational by 2023.

### Lunar exploration

Main article: [Chinese Lunar Exploration Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Lunar_Exploration_Program)



Annotated image of the approximate landing site of the Chinese Chang'e-3 lander. It was launched at 17\:30 UTC on 1 December 2013 and reached the Moon's surface on 14 December 2013. The lunar coordinates are: 44.12°N 19.51°W.

Chang'e 4 lander on the far side of the Moon

[Chang'e 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_4) lander on the far side of the Moon

In January 2004, the PRC formally started the implementation phase of its uncrewed [Moon](19331.md) exploration project. According to [Sun Laiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Laiyan), administrator of the China National Space Administration, the project will involve three phases: orbiting the Moon; landing; and returning samples.

On December 14, 2005, it was reported "an effort to launch lunar orbiting satellites will be supplanted in 2007 by a program aimed at accomplishing an uncrewed lunar landing. A program to return uncrewed space vehicles from the Moon will begin in 2012 and last for five years, until the crewed program gets underway" in 2017, with a crewed Moon landing planned after that.[^cite_note-48]

The decision to develop a new Moon rocket in the 1962 Soviet [UR-700M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Rocket#UR=700)-class ([Project Aelita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Aelita?action=edit\&redlink=1)) able to launch a 500-ton payload in [LTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_transfer_orbit#Application_to_interplanetary_travel) and a more modest 50 tons LTO payload LV has been discussed in a 2006 conference by academician [Zhang Guitian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhang_Guitian?action=edit\&redlink=1) (张贵田), a liquid propellant rocket engine specialist, who developed the [CZ-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CZ-2) and [CZ-4A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CZ-4A) rockets engines.[^cite_note-war-sky-49][^cite_note-ChineseCrewedLunarRocket-50][^cite_note-zhangguitian-51]

On June 22, 2006, [Long Lehao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Lehao), deputy chief architect of the lunar probe project, laid out a schedule for China's lunar exploration. He set 2024 as the date of China's first moonwalk.[^cite_note-52]

In September 2010, it was announced that the country is planning to carry out explorations in deep space by sending a man to the Moon by 2025. China also hoped to bring a Moon rock sample back to Earth in 2017, and subsequently build an observatory on the Moon's surface. [Ye Peijian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ye_Peijian), Commander in Chief of the Chang'e program and an academic at the Chinese Academy of Sciences, added that China has the "full capacity to accomplish Mars exploration by 2013."[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54]

On December 14, 2013[^cite_note-55] China's Chang'e 3 became the first object to [soft-land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_landing_\(rocketry\)) on the Moon since [Luna 24](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luna_24) in 1976.[^cite_note-56]

On 20 May 2018, several months before the Chang'e 4 mission, the Queqiao was launched from Xichang Satellite Launch Center in China, on a [Long March 4C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_4C) rocket.[^cite_note-nasaspaceflifgt-launch-57] The spacecraft took 24 days to reach L<sub>2</sub>, using a [gravity assist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravity_assist) at the Moon to save propellant.[^cite_note-P_Society_Luyuan_Xu-58] On 14 June 2018, Queqiao finished its final adjustment burn and entered the mission orbit, about 65,000 kilometres (40,000 mi) from the Moon. This is the first lunar relay satellite ever placed in this location.[^cite_note-P_Society_Luyuan_Xu-58]

On January 3, 2019, Chang'e 4, the China National Space Administration's lunar rover, made the first-ever soft landing on the Moon's far side. The rover was able to transmit data back to Earth despite the lack of radio frequencies on the far side, via a dedicated satellite sent earlier to orbit the Moon. Landing and data transmission are considered landmark achievements for human space exploration.[^cite_note-59]

Yang Liwei declared at the 16th Human in Space Symposium of International Academy of Astronautics (IAA) in Beijing, on May 22, 2007, that building a lunar base was a crucial step to realize a flight to Mars and farther planets.[^cite_note-60]

According to practice, since the whole project is only at a very early preparatory research phase, no official crewed Moon program has been announced yet by the authorities. But its existence is nonetheless revealed by regular intentional leaks in the media.[^cite_note-61] A typical example is the Lunar Roving Vehicle (月球车) that was shown on a Chinese TV channel (东方卫视) during the 2008 [May Day](39353050.md) celebrations.

On 23 November 2020, China launched the new Moon mission Chang'e 5, which returned to Earth carrying lunar samples on 16 December 2020. Only two nations, the United States and the former Soviet Union have ever returned materials from the Moon, thus making China the third country to have ever achieved the feat.[^cite_note-62]

China sent [Chang'e 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_6) on 3 May, which conducted the first lunar sample return from the [far side of the Moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_side_of_the_Moon).[^cite_note-AJ_FI-20230425-63] This is China's second lunar sample return mission, the first was achieved by [Chang'e 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_5) from the lunar near side 4 years ago.[^cite_note-auto-64]

### Mission to Mars and beyond

See also: [Planetary Exploration of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_Exploration_of_China)

Tianwen-1 probe in Mars orbit.

[Tianwen-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-1) probe in Mars orbit.

Zhurong rover and lander captured by HiRISE from NASA's MRO on June 6, 2021

[*Zhurong* rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhurong_\(rover\)) and lander on Martian surface. Image captured by [HiRISE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiRISE) from [NASA](18426568.md)'s *[Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Reconnaissance_Orbiter)* on 6 June 2021

In 2006, the Chief Designer of the Shenzhou spacecraft stated in an interview that:

> 搞航天工程不是要达成升空之旅, 而是要让人可以正常在太空中工作, 为将来探索火星、土星等作好准备。 Space programs are not aimed at sending humans into space *per se*, but instead at enabling humans to work normally in space, and prepare for the future exploration of Mars, Saturn, and beyond.

— *[CAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Academy_of_Sciences) Academician [Qi Faren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qi_Faren)*[^cite_note-65]

[Sun Laiyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Laiyan), administrator of the China National Space Administration, said on July 20, 2006, that China would start deep space exploration focusing on [Mars](14640471.md) over the next five years, during the [Eleventh Five-Year Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleventh_Five-Year_Plan_\(People's_Republic_of_China\)) (2006–2010) Program period.[^cite_note-66] In April 2020, the [Planetary Exploration of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_Exploration_of_China) program was announced. The program aims to explore planets of the Solar System, starting with Mars, then expanded to include [asteroids and comets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZhengHe_\(spacecraft\)), [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Jupiter_probe) and more in the future.[^cite_note-Tianwen-announcement-en-67]

The first mission of the program, [Tianwen-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-1) Mars exploration mission, began on July 23, 2020. A spacecraft, which consisted of an orbiter, a lander, a [rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhurong_\(rover\)), a remote and a deployable camera, was launched by a [Long March 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_5) rocket from [Wenchang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenchang_Spacecraft_Launch_Site).[^cite_note-Tianwen-launch-68] The Tianwen-1 was inserted into Mars orbit in February 2021 after a seven-month journey, followed by a successful soft landing of the lander and Zhurong rover on May 14, 2021.[^cite_note-69]

### Space-based solar power

According to the China Academy of Space Technology (CAST) presentation at the 2015 International Space Development Congress in Toronto, Canada, Chinese interest in [space-based solar power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-based_solar_power) began in the period 1990–1995. By 2013, there was a national goal, that "the state has decided that power coming from outside of the earth, such as solar power and development of other space energy resources, is to be China's future direction" and the following roadmap was identified: "In 2010, CAST will finish the concept design; in 2020, we will finish the industrial level testing of in-orbit construction and wireless transmissions. In 2025, we will complete the first 100kW SPS demonstration at LEO; and in 2035, the 100MW SPS will have an electric generating capacity. Finally in 2050, the first commercial level SPS system will be in operation at GEO."[^cite_note-\:2-70] The article went on to state that "Since SPS development will be a huge project, it will be considered the equivalent of an Apollo program for energy. In the last century, America's leading position in science and technology worldwide was inextricably linked with technological advances associated with the implementation of the Apollo program. Likewise, as China's current achievements in aerospace technology are built upon with its successive generations of satellite projects in space, China will use its capabilities in space science to assure sustainable development of energy from space."[^cite_note-\:2-70]

In 2015, the CAST team won the [International SunSat Design Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_SunSat_Design_Competition?action=edit\&redlink=1) with their video of a Multi-Rotary Joint concept.[^cite_note-71] The design was presented in detail in a paper for the Online Journal of Space Communication.[^cite_note-72]

In 2016, Lt Gen. Zhang Yulin, deputy chief of the [PLA armament development department of the Central Military Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equipment_Development_Department_of_the_Central_Military_Commission), suggested that China would next begin to exploit Earth-Moon space for industrial development. The goal would be the construction of space-based solar power satellites that would beam energy back to Earth.[^cite_note-\:0-73]

In June 2021, Chinese officials confirmed the continuation of plans for a geostationary solar power station by 2050. The updated schedule anticipates a small-scale electricity generation test in 2022, followed by a megawatt-level orbital power station by 2030. The gigawatt-level geostationary station will require over 10,000 tonnes of infrastructure, delivered using over 100 Long March 9 launches.[^cite_note-74]

### Space exploration

#### Crewed LEO Program

- [Project 921-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_921-1) – [Shenzhou spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenzhou_spacecraft).

- [*Tiangong*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong_program) - first three crewed Chinese Space Laboratories.

- [Project 921-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_921-2) – permanent crewed modular [Chinese Space Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Space_Station)

  [^cite_note-87]

  [^cite_note-88]

- [*Tianzhou*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianzhou_\(spacecraft\)) – robotic cargo vessel to resupply the Chinese Space Station, based on the design of [Tiangong-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiangong-1), not meant for reentry, but usable for garbage disposal.

  [^cite_note-89]

  [^cite_note-90]

- [Next-generation crewed spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next-generation_crewed_spacecraft) (货运飞船) – upgrade version of the Shenzhou spacecraft to resupply the Chinese Space Station and return cargo back to Earth.

- [Project 921-11 – X-11 reusable spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next-generation_crewed_spacecraft) for Project 921-2 Space Station.

- Tianjiao-1 or Chang Cheng-1 (Great Wall-1) - winged spaceplane orbiters of [Project 869](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_921-3#869_Project) reusable shuttle system. Project of 1980s-1990s.

- [*Shenlong*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenlong_\(spacecraft\)) - winged spaceplane orbiter of current [Project 921-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_921-3) reusable shuttle system.

- [*Tengyun*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_921-3#Tengyun) - winged spaceplane orbiter in another current project of two wing-staged reusable shuttle system.

- [HTS Maglev Launch Assist Space Shuttle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_921-3#HTS_Maglev_Launch_Assist_Technology) - winged spaceplane orbiter in another current shuttle project.

#### Chinese Lunar Exploration Program

- **First phase**, [*Chang'e* 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_1) and [*Chang'e* 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_2) – launched in 2007 and 2010

- **Second phase**, [*Chang'e* 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_3) and [*Chang'e* 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_4) – launched in 2013 and 2018

- **Third phase**, [*Chang'e* 5-T1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_5-T1) (completed in 2014) and [*Chang'e* 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_5) – (completed in 2020)

- **Fourth phase**, [*Chang'e* 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_6) (sample-return from lunar far-side in May–June 2024), [*Chang'e* 7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_7) and [*Chang'e* 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chang'e_8) – will explore the south pole for natural resources; may 3D-print a structure using regolith.

- **Crewed mission**: by the year 2030, – [crewed lunar missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Lunar_Exploration_Program#Crewed_mission_phase) employing the [*Mengzhou*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mengzhou_\(spacecraft\)) orbiter, *[Lanyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lanyue)* lander, and [Long March 10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_10) rocket.

#### Deep space exploration program

See also: [Planetary Exploration of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_Exploration_of_China)

China's first deep space probe, the [*Yinghuo*-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yinghuo-1) orbiter, was launched in November 2011 along with the joint [Fobos-Grunt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fobos-Grunt) mission with Russia, but the rocket failed to leave Earth orbit and both probes underwent destructive re-entry on 15 January 2012.[^cite_note-91]

In 2018, Chinese researchers proposed a deep space exploration roadmap to explore Mars, an asteroid, Jupiter, and further targets, within the 2020–2030 timeframe.[^cite_note-Planning2030-92][^cite_note-2018UNOOSA-93] Current and upcoming robotic missions include:

- **[Chinese Deep Space Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Deep_Space_Network)** relay satellites, for deep-space communication and exploration support network.

- **[*Tianwen*-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-1)**, launched on 23 July 2020 with arrival at Mars on 10 February 2021. Mission includes an orbiter, a deployable and remote camera, a lander, and the [*Zhurong* rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhurong_\(rover\)).

  [^cite_note-Planning2030-92]

- **[*Tianwen*-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-2)**, formerly *ZhengHe*, launched on 29 May 2025. Mission goals include asteroid [flyby observations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_flyby), [global remote sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_sensing), [robotic landing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lander_\(spacecraft\)), and [sample return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sample-return_mission).

  [^cite_note-Planning2030-92]

  [^cite_note-94]

- ***[Shensuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shensuo)***, formerly Interstellar Express, targeting for launch around 2024–2025 for *Interstellar Heliosphere Probe-1* (IHP-1) and around 2025–2026 for *Interstellar Heliosphere Probe-2* (IHP-2). Mission objectives include exploration of the heliosphere and interstellar space.

  [^cite_note-95]

  &#x20;Also to become the first non-NASA probes to [leave the Solar System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_artificial_objects_leaving_the_Solar_System).

  [^cite_note-96]

- **[*Tianwen*-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-3)**, [Mars sample return mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_sample-return_mission) planned for launch around 2028–2030.

  [^cite_note-2018UNOOSA-93]

  [^cite_note-gbtimes20170714-97]

  &#x20;Mission goals include [in-situ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_situ#Space-related) topography and soil composition analysis, deep interior investigations to probe the planet's origins and geologic evolution, and sample return.

  [^cite_note-Planning2030-92]

  &#x20;As of December 2019, the plan is for two launches to be conducted during the November 2028 Earth-to-Mars launch window: a sample collection lander with Mars ascent vehicle on a [Long March 3B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_3B), and an Earth Return Orbiter on a [Long March 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_5), with samples returning to Earth in September 2031. Earlier plans implemented the mission in a single launch using the [Long March 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_March_9).

  [^cite_note-98]

- **[*Tianwen*-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tianwen-4)**, 2029–2030, orbital [exploration of Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_of_Jupiter) and its [four largest moons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galilean_moons) (with a focus on [Callisto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callisto_\(moon\)) by orbiting this Jovian moon), study of the [magnetohydrodynamics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetohydrodynamics) in the Jupiter system, and investigation of the internal composition of Jupiter's atmosphere and moons,

  [^cite_note-Planning2030-92]

  [^cite_note-gbtimes20170714-97]

  &#x20;A [Uranus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uranus) fly-by probe will detach from the Jupiter orbiter while in interplanetary space for encounter during the 2040s.
