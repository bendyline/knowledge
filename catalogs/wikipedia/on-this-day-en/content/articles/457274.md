**Paktia** (also spelled *Paktiya* and *Paktya*, [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): د پکتيا ولايت, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Pashto): *Da Paktiā Wilāyat* and [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language): ولایت پکتیا, romanized: *Wilāyat-e Paktiā*) is one of the southeastern [provinces of Afghanistan](412343.md) and is widely regarded as a region of major strategic, cultural, and tribal significance. It borders [Khost](457249.md) to the south, [Paktika](457294.md) to the east, [Logar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logar_Province) and [Wardak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maidan_Wardak_Province) to the west, and [Nangarhar](457263.md) to the north, while also sharing a long international border with [Pakistan](23235.md)’s [Kurram](5053207.md) and [North Waziristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Waziristan) regions. The provincial capital is [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez), which serves as the main administrative, economic, and military center of the region called *[Loya Paktia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loya_Paktia)*.

Covering an area of approximately 6,400 square kilometers and having an estimated population of around 640,000 inhabitants as of 2023, Paktia is characterized by a rugged mountainous landscape that forms part of the southeastern extensions of the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) range. Deep valleys, high passes, and forested uplands shape the province's geography, while seasonal rivers and limited arable land define rural settlement patterns. Its location along key mountain corridors connecting central Afghanistan with the Pakistan borderlands gives Paktia enduring geopolitical and military relevance.

Historically, Paktia has been a core region of the [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) tribal world and has played a central role in the political and military history of Afghanistan. The province is traditionally associated with powerful Pashtun tribal confederations, particularly the [Zadran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zadran_\(Pashtun_tribe\)) and related tribes, whose social structures have long shaped local governance and security dynamics. Due to its border position, Paktia has frequently been a frontline region during major conflicts, including the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), the [civil wars of the 1990s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1992%E2%80%931996\)), and the [post-2001 conflict](19666611.md).

Today, Paktia is characterized by its strategic border position, strong tribal identity, limited economic development, and persistent security challenges. Much of the population depends on subsistence agriculture, livestock herding, small-scale trade, and cross-border commerce. Despite gradual improvements in infrastructure and services, large rural areas remain affected by difficult terrain, poverty, and restricted connectivity. At the same time, Paktia retains a powerful regional identity rooted in tribal traditions, resistance history, and its role as a gateway between central Afghanistan and the [Pashtun belt of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_belt).

## Etymology

The name *Paktia* is derived from the ancient ethnonym *[Paktha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakhtas)*, which is believed to designate one of the earliest known [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns)-related tribes mentioned by [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) in the 5th century BCE among the peoples of eastern [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) Iran. The Paktha are generally identified with the ancient inhabitants of the southeastern Hindu Kush region, forming part of the broader [Indo-Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Iranian_peoples) tribal sphere. Linguistically, the name is linked to the [Old Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Iranian) root *Pakta* ([Old Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Persian_language): 𐬞𐬀𐬐𐬙𐬀, romanized: *\*pakta-*), referring to a tribal or ethnic grouping associated with the mountainous frontier zone between [Central Asia](6742.md) and the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent).[^cite_note-Iranica-Paktia-3] Some historians also associate the name with the wider historical region of [Pakhtunkhwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakhtunkhwa), suggesting a shared etymological and ethnocultural origin tied to the early Pashtun homeland in southeastern Afghanistan and northwestern Pakistan.

## History

Further information: [History of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Afghanistan)

### Antiquity



5th century marble of the Hindu god [Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha) found in [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez)

The territory of present-day Paktia has been inhabited since antiquity and formed part of the eastern highland zone connecting the Iranian plateau with the northwestern regions of the [Indian subcontinent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_subcontinent). In classical sources, the area is associated with the *[Paktha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paktha)*, a tribal group mentioned by [Herodotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herodotus) in the 5th century BCE, widely regarded as an early ancestor of modern [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) populations. During the period of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) (c. 550–330 BCE), the region formed part of the empire's eastern frontier and was integrated into military supply routes linking [Bactria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bactria) with the Indus basin.

After the conquests of [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) in the late 4th century BCE, Paktia lay on the margins of the [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) and later came under the influence of successive Hellenistic and South Asian political formations, including the [Indo-Greek Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Greek_Kingdom) and the [Maurya Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire). Under the [Kushan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire) (1st–3rd centuries CE), southeastern Afghanistan became part of major transregional trade and cultural networks connecting [Central Asia](6742.md), South Asia, and the Iranian world. While major urban centers developed mainly in surrounding lowland regions, Paktia remained predominantly tribal and rural, with limited urbanization due to its mountainous geography.

### Medieval period

With the expansion of Arab-Islamic power in the 7th and 8th centuries, Islam gradually spread into the mountainous regions of southeastern Afghanistan. The initial presence of the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) was followed by more sustained control under the [Abbasid Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate), though the effective administration of Paktia remained heavily dependent on local tribal authorities. By the 9th and 10th centuries, the province was incorporated into the domains of the [Saffarids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffarid_dynasty) and subsequently the [Ghaznavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids). Under the Ghaznavids, especially during the reign of [Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni), Paktia functioned as a strategically important hinterland supplying manpower for campaigns into [India](14533.md) and serving as a corridor linking [Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazni) with the eastern Afghan frontier. Control later passed to the [Ghurid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurid_dynasty) in the 12th century.

The [Mongol invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasions) of the 13th century caused widespread devastation in much of Afghanistan, though the rugged terrain of the region limited the permanent establishment of Mongol administration, allowing many tribal structures to survive with relative continuity. During the [Timurid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire) period (14th–15th centuries), Paktia remained loosely integrated into imperial systems through indirect rule. Political authority was exercised primarily through tribal leaders, while the region maintained trade connections with [Kabul](16826.md), [Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazni), and the eastern Afghan lowlands.

### Early modern period

From the 16th century onward, Paktia lay on the shifting frontier between the [Safavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) and the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire). While both powers periodically sought influence over southeastern Afghanistan, direct imperial administration in Paktia remained limited. The province functioned primarily as a Pashtun tribal zone whose inhabitants frequently participated in the military struggles between the two empires as auxiliary forces.

The early 18th century marked a major turning point with the rise of Pashtun political authority under the [Hotak dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotak_dynasty). Although the Hotak center of power lay in Kandahar, the political transformation strengthened Pashtun autonomy across southeastern Afghanistan. Following the defeat of the Hotaks by [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah) in 1738 and his subsequent death, [Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani) founded the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire) in 1747. Paktia became firmly incorporated into the emerging Afghan state and served as a key recruitment base for tribal forces supporting the Durrani rulers.

Throughout the 19th century, Paktia remained one of the strongholds of tribal power during the reigns of Afghan emirs, particularly under the centralization efforts of [Abdur Rahman Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdur_Rahman_Khan). During the [Anglo-Afghan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Afghan_Wars), the province was not permanently occupied by British forces but played an important supporting role in resistance efforts and in maintaining Afghanistan's eastern frontier.

### During war times (1979–2021)

The [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_invasion_of_Afghanistan) in 1979 transformed Paktia into one of the main battlegrounds of the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War). Due to its direct access to the [Pakistan](23235.md) border, particularly through routes leading to the [Kurram District](5053207.md) and [North Waziristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Waziristan), the province became a crucial logistics corridor for *[mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen)* groups. Among the most influential commanders operating from Paktia was [Jalaluddin Haqqani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalaluddin_Haqqani), whose network played a central role in coordinating resistance in eastern Afghanistan. Throughout the 1980s, the province was subjected to repeated bombardment, ground offensives, and forced displacement of civilian populations.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-Oliynik-6] In 1985, [Khost](457249.md) and [Paktika](457294.md) separated from Paktia and became distinct provinces.[^cite_note-7]

After the [Soviet withdrawal from Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_withdrawal_from_Afghanistan) in 1989, power fragmented among rival mujahideen factions. During the [Afghan civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1992%E2%80%931996\)), control over Paktia shifted between competing commanders aligned either with the government in [Kabul](16826.md) or with regional power centers.[^cite_note-8] By the late 1990s, the [Taliban](30635.md) had extended their control over most of eastern Afghanistan, including Paktia. The province was administered under the [Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\)), with strict enforcement of their interpretation of [Sharia law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharia_law).



[U.S. military](32212.md) personnel during a fire mission (2013)

Following the [United States invasion of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Afghanistan) in late 2001, Taliban forces were expelled from the province with the support of local militias and U.S. airpower. Paktia subsequently became a persistent center of insurgent activity throughout the [war in Afghanistan](19666611.md), with frequent attacks on Afghan government installations and international forces.[^cite_note-9] Despite years of international military presence and development programs, the province remained affected by insecurity, underdevelopment, and cross-border militancy throughout the 2000s and 2010s.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

### Today (2021–)

During the nationwide collapse of the Afghan government in the summer of 2021, Taliban forces rapidly seized control of Paktia as part of the [2021 Taliban offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive). The provincial capital [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez) fell without prolonged resistance, and the province returned to Taliban rule under the re-established [Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan). Since 2021, the Taliban have governed Paktia through appointed provincial officials and security structures. New social and political restrictions have been introduced, particularly affecting women's education, employment, and public participation. Economic contraction, limited humanitarian access, and reduced cross-border formal trade have further strained local livelihoods, though large-scale armed conflict has subsided.

## Geography

### Landscape



Snow-covered mountains in Paktia province

Paktia is situated in southeastern Afghanistan and shares borders with [Khost](457249.md) to the south, [Paktika](457294.md) to the east, [Logar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logar_Province) and [Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazni_Province) to the west, and [Nangarhar](457263.md) to the north. The province is dominated by rugged [mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain) terrain forming part of the southeastern extensions of the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush) mountain system. The [Khost-Gardez mountain pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khost-Gardez_Pass) links Paktia with Khost to the south. Deep valleys, narrow passes, and elevated plateaus characterize much of the landscape, creating natural barriers between districts and shaping settlement patterns.

Unlike the lowland agricultural regions of southern Afghanistan, Paktia's terrain is largely composed of steep hills, forested uplands, and rocky ridges. Arable land is mostly confined to valley floors and basin areas around the provincial capital [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez), as well as in smaller intermontane plains. Seasonal rivers and mountain streams provide limited irrigation, supporting subsistence farming and livestock grazing. The mountainous terrain has historically contributed to the relative isolation of Paktia and its long tradition of tribal autonomy.

### Flora and fauna



A forest in Paktia

The flora of Paktia reflects its transitional position between the dry lowlands of eastern Afghanistan and the cooler highland zones of the central mountains. Higher elevations support scattered [conifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conifer) forests, particularly of [juniper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juniper) and [pine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pine), while lower slopes are covered with shrubs, wild grasses, and drought-resistant vegetation. Cultivated areas in valley bottoms produce crops such as [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), and various vegetables, alongside small orchards of [apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple), [apricots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apricot), and [walnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walnut) trees.

Wildlife in the province includes species adapted to mountainous environments. Common mammals include [wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf), [foxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox), [jackals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackal), [wild goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_goat), and [porcupines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porcupine), while higher elevations may still host limited populations of [ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibex) and [snow leopard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_leopard). Birdlife includes [eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle), [hawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawk), [partridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partridge), and migratory species that pass through river valleys during seasonal movements. Due to habitat loss, overgrazing, and hunting, several native species have experienced population decline in recent decades.

### Climate



Paktia often experiences heavy [snowfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowfall) during winters

Paktia experiences a predominantly [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) influenced by altitude, with cold winters and mild to warm summers. Winter temperatures in higher elevations frequently fall below freezing, with regular [snowfall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowfall) in mountainous areas, while summers are comparatively moderate compared with Afghanistan's lowland regions. Average summer temperatures generally range between 20 °C and 30 °C, though lower valleys can experience higher heat.

[Precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) is higher than in much of southern Afghanistan and occurs mainly during late winter and spring, contributing to seasonal river flow and groundwater recharge. Snowmelt from surrounding mountains plays a critical role in water availability for agriculture. The province is occasionally affected by strong winds and localized storms, which can disrupt transportation through mountain passes. Overall, the cooler climate and higher precipitation distinguish Paktia environmentally from the arid provinces of southern Afghanistan.

## Government and politics

Further information: [Politics of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Afghanistan)

### Local governance

Further information: [List of governors of Paktia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Paktia)

Paktia's governance has historically been shaped by the strong presence of tribal structures, local councils, and periodic central oversight. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, the province was administered through a combination of [tribal leaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal_elder), appointed provincial officials from [Kabul](16826.md), and influential *maliks* who exercised authority over local communities. These traditional leaders mediated disputes, organized local defense, and coordinated tax collection while recognizing the sovereignty of the Afghan state.

During the conflicts from 1979 onwards—including the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), subsequent [civil wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1989-2001\)), and the [first Taliban regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\))—formal governance structures were often supplanted by armed factions and tribal militias. After the [2001 intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Afghanistan), the provincial administration was re-established through appointed officials, although tribal councils and religious leaders continued to influence decision-making and local dispute resolution.

Since the [Taliban return to power in 2021](68481047.md), Paktia has been governed under the [Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan), with provincial and district officials appointed centrally. Tribal networks, religious figures, and local shuras remain significant in mediating conflicts and guiding community decisions.

As of December 2025, the governor of Paktia is [Mullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullah) [Muhrullah Hamad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhrullah_Hamad?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-mei-1]

### Administrative divisions



Map of the districts of Paktia as of January 2004, prior to the redrawing of provincial and district boundaries later that year

Paktia is divided into 13 official and two unofficial districts, each administered by a district chief responsible for governance, coordination with the provincial government, and basic public services. The provincial capital, [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez), serves as the main administrative, economic, and cultural hub, while other important districts include [Lazha Mangal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lazha_Mangal_District), [Said Karam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Said_Karam_District), and [Zazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zazi_District). District centers are typically located along valley floors, near water sources, or on accessible mountain routes. Local administrations manage civil registration, dispute resolution, and oversight of education, health services, and infrastructure maintenance.

### Security



[Afghan National Police](13327465.md) recruits (2007)

Paktia's security situation is shaped by its mountainous terrain, tribal networks, and proximity to the [Pakistan](23235.md) border. During the [Islamic Republic of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) period, the province was a focal point for counterinsurgency operations against [Taliban](30635.md) and other militant groups. Since 2021, the Taliban maintain primary control over the province, establishing administrative and security mechanisms in both urban and rural areas. While major population centers such as [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez) are relatively stable, remote districts remain prone to localized insurgent activity, cross-border smuggling, and tribal disputes. Traditional structures, including shuras and maliks, continue to influence security arrangements and dispute mediation throughout the province.

## Economy

Further information: [Transport in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Afghanistan) and [Economy of Afghanistan](9895.md)

The economy of Paktia is primarily based on [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), livestock farming, and cross-border trade with [Pakistan](23235.md). Subsistence farming and small-scale commerce remain the backbone of local livelihoods, while public employment and aid-dependent income have fluctuated due to political instability. Remittances from family members working elsewhere also contribute significantly to household incomes.

### Agriculture and animal husbandry



A local farmer stands in front of a plot of apple trees

Agriculture is central to Paktia's economy. Fertile valleys and irrigated plots support the cultivation of [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [barley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley), [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetables), and [fruits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fruit) such as [apples](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apples) and [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grapes). Farmers also grow high-value crops like [pomegranates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranates) and [nuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nut_\(fruit\)) in suitable areas. Livestock farming is widespread, with [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep), [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goats), [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle), and [poultry](23197.md) providing meat, dairy products, wool, and hides for both domestic use and local trade. Seasonal fluctuations and limited irrigation infrastructure constrain agricultural productivity in some districts.

### Mining and industry

Industrial activity in Paktia is limited and mostly artisanal. Small-scale operations include stone quarrying, brick-making, and traditional handicrafts. Mining focuses on construction materials such as [gravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel), [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand), and [stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone), which are used locally.[^cite_note-15] The lack of large-scale industry is largely due to limited infrastructure, rugged terrain, and restricted access to investment and energy.

### Trade



Two local men unload coal used for heating

Paktia's economy benefits from its location along border trade routes with [Pakistan](23235.md). Informal and formal cross-border commerce involves [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food), [construction materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_materials), [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textiles), and consumer goods. Local markets in [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez) and other towns serve as trade centers for surrounding districts. Trade is often seasonal and affected by security conditions and infrastructure constraints.

### Energy and irrigation



Burning wood is commonly used to supply energy and warmth

Energy supply in Paktia is limited, relying on [woodburning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodburning), small-scale [diesel generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_generator), solar installations, and sporadic grid connections. Irrigation is essential for agriculture, supported by traditional *[karez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karez)* systems and small canals drawing water from rivers and mountain streams. Maintenance challenges, drought cycles, and limited technical support affect crop yields and water availability.

### Tourism

Security concerns and underdeveloped infrastructure limit large-scale tourism in Paktia, though local and domestic visitors occasionally travel to prominent religious, cultural, and recreational sites.[^cite_note-16]

### Communication

Telecommunications have expanded gradually, with mobile networks and limited internet access available in urban areas. Rural districts still face connectivity issues due to difficult terrain, electricity shortages, and economic constraints. Internet penetration remains low.

### Transportation and infrastructure



U.S. military cars driving on the [Khost-Gardez Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khost-Gardez_Pass)

Paktia is connected to surrounding provinces and [Pakistan](23235.md) by a network of highways and mountain roads. Roads vary in quality, with some paved arterial routes linking [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez) to major towns, while secondary and rural roads are often unpaved or in poor condition. The province lacks operational railway infrastructure. Ground transport relies on trucks, buses, cars, and motorcycles, supporting both local commerce and cross-border trade. Maintenance and development of transportation infrastructure remain key challenges for economic growth.

## Demographics

Further information: [Demographics of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Afghanistan)

### Population



Local men from Paktia

Paktia province has an estimated population of approximately 650,000 people as of 2023, distributed across the provincial capital [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez), several small district centers, and a large number of mountain and valley villages.[^cite_note-NSIA-2] The majority of residents live in rural environments and depend on subsistence-based livelihoods. Poverty, limited infrastructure, and weak access to public services continue to significantly affect living conditions, especially in remote districts, with a [multidimensional poverty index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multidimensional_Poverty_Index) of 0.261 and 19.0% of the population living in [severe poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_poverty) as of 2023.[^cite_note-ophi-17]. Many households rely on agriculture, cross-border trade, informal labor, and remittances from relatives working outside the province or abroad.

### Ethnicity, languages, and religion

Paktia is ethnically and linguistically one of the most homogeneous provinces of Afghanistan. The population is overwhelmingly [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns), with the dominant tribal confederations belonging mainly to the [Zadran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zadran_\(Pashtun_tribe\)), [Mangal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangal), [Ahmadzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadzai_\(Ghilji_clan\)), and [Tanai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanai) tribes.[^cite_note-understandingwar-18] Tribal identity continues to play a central role in social organization, dispute resolution, and political influence.[^cite_note-nps-19]

[Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto) is the dominant and nearly universal language of daily life, administration, and education. [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari) is spoken by a small minority, largely in the district center of [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez), mainly for official purposes or trade. The population is almost entirely [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam), with religious life centered on local mosques, *madrasas*, and community religious leaders. Sectarian minorities are extremely limited, and non-Muslim communities are practically absent in the province.

### Education

Further information: [List of universities in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Afghanistan) and [List of schools in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Afghanistan)



Schoolgirls in the district of [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez_District), Paktia

Educational access in Paktia has historically been constrained by geography, insecurity, and poverty. The province has a network of primary and secondary schools concentrated mainly in Gardez and district centers, while many rural villages still face shortages of school buildings, qualified teachers, and learning materials. [Paktia University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paktia_University) in Gardez serves as the main institution of higher education and offers programs in education, agriculture, Islamic studies, and social sciences.

Overall literacy rates remain low, particularly among women, with the most recent estimates from 2011 indicating an overall literacy rate of 27% and an overall net enrolment rate for school-age children of approximately 24%.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-31] Since 2021, the education system has undergone major structural changes under Taliban administration. While boys schooling continues at most levels, access to secondary and higher education for girls is largely restricted. *Madrasas* have expanded across the province, becoming a significant alternative form of education, especially in rural areas.

### Health

Further information: [Health in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Afghanistan) and [Healthcare in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Afghanistan)



An injured boy receives medical care at the Paktia Regional Medical Hospital

Healthcare services in Paktia are centered primarily in Gardez, where the main provincial hospital and several smaller clinics provide basic and emergency care. Outside the provincial capital, access to healthcare is limited, with many districts reliant on small health posts or mobile medical teams. Travel distance, poor roads, and economic hardship often delay or prevent treatment.

Common health challenges include maternal and infant mortality, malnutrition, respiratory diseases, and limited access to clean drinking water, with the most recent available estimates from 2011 indicating that 36% of households had access to clean drinking water and 3% of births were attended by a skilled birth attendant.[^cite_note-cimicweb.org-31]. [NGOs](46539.md) and humanitarian agencies play a crucial role in providing vaccinations, maternal care, and basic health services, particularly in underserved rural areas. Despite gradual improvements over the past two decades, Paktia's health system remains fragile and heavily dependent on external support.

## Culture

### Music and dances

Traditional music and dance in Paktia are deeply rooted in eastern [Pashtun tribal culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_tribal_culture). The *[attan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attan)* is the most important communal dance and is performed at weddings, tribal gatherings, and religious festivities. Compared to southern styles, the *Paktia attan* is often faster in rhythm and accompanied by strong drum patterns. Music is traditionally played using instruments such as the *[dhol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhol)*, *[rubab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubab_\(instrument\))*, and sometimes the *[sitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitar)*. Folk songs often focus on themes of bravery, mountain life, clan loyalty, migration, and borderland identity. Religious recitations and *[nasheed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasheed)* chants are widespread, especially during [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan), funerals, and important religious gatherings. Public music performances have declined since 2021, but private family events continue to preserve musical traditions.

### Dresses and attire



Paktiawal man wearing a typical *[turban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turban)*

Traditional dress in Paktia reflects both Pashtun customs and the mountainous environment. Men commonly wear *[perahan o tunban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perahan_o_tunban)*, often combined with woolen *[shawls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawl)* and tightly wrapped *turbans* adapted for cold winters. In rural areas, embroidered waistcoats and leather belts remain common. Women traditionally wear long dresses with colorful embroidery, mirror work, and layered scarves or veils. Jewelry is often modest, with silver ornaments used more frequently than gold. Clothing styles may vary between tribes such as the [Zadran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zadran_\(Pashtun_tribe\)), [Mangal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangal), and [Ahmadzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadzai_\(Ghilji_clan\)), but conservative dress norms remain widespread across the province.

### Cuisine

Paktia's cuisine is shaped by its mountain agriculture and livestock economy. [Wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat) bread, [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_and_mutton), and [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_product) form the core of daily nutrition. Popular dishes include *[qabeli palaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qabeli_palaw)*, *[qormas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korma)* with beans and potatoes, and grilled [kababs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kababs). Yogurt, *[qurut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurut)*, and fresh milk are commonly consumed. Wild herbs and mountain greens are used seasonally in soups and side dishes. Tea, especially [green tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_tea), is served throughout the day in homes, guest rooms, and tribal gatherings. Compared to southern Afghanistan, fruit cultivation is less prominent, but apples, walnuts, and mulberries are locally important.

### Architecture, art, and literature

Traditional architecture in Paktia is adapted to mountainous terrain and cold winters. Homes are commonly built from stone, mudbrick, and timber, with flat or gently sloped roofs. Many villages feature compact housing clusters for defensive and climatic reasons. Mosques serve as both religious and community centers. Local crafts include carpet weaving, embroidery, wool processing, and wood carving. Oral literature remains highly important, with [Pashto poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_poetry), epic storytelling, and tribal history recounted through recitations during gatherings. Poets, religious scholars, and tribal elders play a central role in preserving cultural memory.

### Media, entertainment, and festivities

Radio remains the most important media source in rural Paktia, with broadcasts from national Pashto stations and regional outlets. Television and mobile internet are accessible mainly in [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez) and district centers. Major cultural celebrations include [Eid al-Fitr](154944.md), [Eid al-Adha](358226.md), weddings, and circumcision ceremonies, which traditionally involve communal meals, music, and dance. Since 2021, public entertainment has been restricted under Taliban rule, leading to fewer public celebrations, while private family gatherings continue cultural practices with limited visibility.

### Places of interest

Paktia contains a range of cultural and natural landmarks, although most remain locally known rather than nationally prominent. The historic core of [Gardez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardez) includes old mosques, *bazaars*, and traditional neighborhoods. Several tribal shrines and burial sites of local religious figures attract visitors from surrounding districts. The mountainous landscapes of eastern Paktia, with forested valleys and highland pastures, also hold cultural significance for seasonal migration and pastoral traditions. Many sites remain undocumented due to decades of conflict and limited archaeological research.

### Sports



Paktiawal men playing [cricket](25675557.md)

Traditional sports in Paktia have included *[buzkashi](169471.md)* in open plains and valleys, as well as local forms of wrestling and strength competitions during festivals. Horse riding remains associated with tribal prestige. In recent decades, [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_in_Afghanistan) and [football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Afghanistan) have become the most popular modern sports, especially among youth. Informal football pitches and cricket grounds exist in Gardez and district centers. [Volleyball](32558.md) is also widely played due to low equipment requirements. Private gyms support [boxing](4243.md), [taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taekwondo), and basic fitness training, mainly in urban areas.

In national cricket competitions such as the [Shpageeza Cricket League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shpageeza_Cricket_League), Paktia is represented by the [Mis Ainak Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mis_Ainak_Knights) franchise, which covers the southeastern provinces. During the period of the [Islamic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan), Paktia was represented in national football competitions through [De Abasin Sape FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Abasin_Sape_FC), which served as the regional team for Paktia together with [Khost](457249.md), [Paktika](457294.md) and [Logar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logar_Province) in the [Afghan Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Premier_League). In the successor competition, the [Afghanistan Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_Champions_League), Paktia is represented through [Wahidy FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahidy_FC?action=edit\&redlink=1).
