On 9 February 2025, [Kosovo](17391.md) held [parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2025_Kosovan_parliamentary_election) to choose the 120 members of its [Assembly](1068656.md). The largest party in parliament after the election was [Vetëvendosje](3225595.md) (LVV), who won 42.3% of the vote and was granted 48 seats, 13 short of a [parliamentary majority](465640.md). The largest party is required by law to appoint the new [Speaker of the Assembly](1260708.md) within 30 days after the results are certified, needing to pass the threshold of 61 votes (an absolute majority).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

## Deadlocks

### Februrary 2025 parliament

#### Speaker deadlock

LVV's proposition for Assembly Speaker is the outgoing Minister of Justice, [Albulena Haxhiu](43297113.md). The voting process began on 15 April 2025, and was defeated by a vote of 52–48, with 11 abstentions.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] The meeting was delayed by 48 hours as per Kosovo's legal framework, and the members reconvened again on 19 April. The members did not reach a consensus once more.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] The meeting was delayed more than 40 times as a result of the opposition parties categorically refusing the proposed candidate, which has been called a "deadlock" or "blockade" by all parties. On 26 August, [Dimal Basha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimal_Basha) from LVV was voted as Assembly Speaker on his second proposal with a majority vote of 73 to 30.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

All parties have accused each other of trying to block the process, and have refused several options, such as the option for a [secret ballot](479210.md) which was proposed by lawmakers.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-RL_May-10] Haxhiu has said that if a secret ballot goes ahead and she does not receive the required 61 votes, she would withdraw.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-BI_Jun-12] Neither opposition party has proposed its own candidate for Assembly Speaker. The Assembly has also not approved a budget for 2026, increasing tensions.[^cite_note-13]

On 26 June, the [Constitutional Court](26764195.md) ruled that the deadlock must be ended within 30 days (by 26 July).[^cite_note-BI_Jun2-14][^cite_note-\:2-15] Following the court's decision, [President](1260820.md) [Vjosa Osmani](61979540.md) began to meet with party leaders to find an agreement.[^cite_note-16]

The deadlock has been met with symbolic civil protests.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] In one protest on 3 July, the lawyer Arianit Koci gained a lot of media attention after he brought four donkeys in front of the doors of the Assembly, to symbolize "the stubbornness that the Assembly members are showing".[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] By 27 July, there were 54 votes in total before the deadline set by the Constitutional Court.[^cite_note-\:2-15][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-BTA_54-22]

On 8 August, the Court gave MPs another 30 days to elect a speaker. It however stipulated that no candidate could be proposed more than three times, and that MPs must be present to vote during the sessions. The Court also found that the acting speaker had violated the constitution by continuing to call for a commission to establish a secret ballot.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

#### Serb deputy speaker deadlock

From 26 August to 10 October 2025, Kosovo entered another institutional crisis, this time related to the election of the Serbian Deputy Speaker of the Assembly.[^cite_note-32] Slavko Simić was initially proposed as the Serb deputy speaker by the [Serb List](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serb_List_\(Kosovo\)) on 26 August 2025, but the nomination was rejected three times. Basha did not allow Simić to be proposed a fourth time, interpreting that the Constitutional Court ruling that stated a candidate can only be nominated three times for speakership also extended to the procedure to elect deputy speakers. After the Serb List refused to nominate another candidate, Basha began drawing lots among the Serb List MPs, and three candidates called from the lots were rejected.[^cite_note-33]

The deadlock was temporarily resolved after [ZSPO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/For_Freedom,_Justice_and_Survival) MP [Nenad Rašić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nenad_Ra%C5%A1i%C4%87) was elected in the position on 10 October, and the Assembly was declared constituted. Basha stated that the procedure was correct, as all nine MPs from the Serb List had been proposed and exhausted. Basha also explained that Kosovo’s constitution specified the candidate must be a member of the Serb community but did not have to be from a particular political party.[^cite_note-34] The election of Rašić to the position resulted in the Serb List filing a complaint to the Constitutional Court.[^cite_note-35]

The Constitutional Court later declared Rašić's election as Serb deputy speaker unconstitutional on 28 January 2026, finding that Rašić hadn't been proposed by a majority of the Serb community, while also noting that the Serb List played a blocking role in the constitution of the Assembly.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Basha stated that the ruling by the Constitutional Court was "unfounded", stating that Rašić was elected only after the majority of Serbian community MPs failed to propose an alternative candidate.[^cite_note-38] The full ruling was later released on 4 February 2026.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-FutureCases-40]

The ruling of procedures relating to the election of the Serb deputy speaker would later be applied after the Assembly met following the [December 2025 elections](81646979.md) and elected [Albulena Haxhiu](43297113.md) as Assembly Speaker on 11 February 2026.[^cite_note-FutureCases-40] The election of the deputy speakers was conducted as a single package, with Ardian Gola becoming [Vetëvendosje](3225595.md)'s deputy speaker, [Vlora Çitaku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vlora_%C3%87itaku) becoming [PDK](1260606.md)'s deputy speaker, [Kujtim Shala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kujtim_Shala_\(politician\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) becoming [LDK](1256321.md)'s deputy speaker, [Emilija Rexhepi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emilija_Rexhepi) becoming the minorities' deputy speaker, and Slavko Simić becoming the Serb deputy speaker.[^cite_note-41]

#### Cabinet deadlock

On 26 October 2025, LVV nominated [Albin Kurti](4774301.md) as prime minister-designate and presented a proposed cabinet; however, it failed to secure the required 61 votes, receiving only 56 in favor, while 52 deputies voted against and 4 abstained.[^cite_note-42] According to the [Constitution of Kosovo](9516144.md), if the proposed composition of the new government does not obtain the necessary majority in the Assembly, the president must appoint a second prime minister-designate. Accordingly, on 19 November 2025, LVV nominated [Glauk Konjufca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glauk_Konjufca) as prime minister-designate and presented a proposed cabinet, but again failed to obtain the required 61 votes, receiving 56 votes in favor, 53 against, and 4 abstentions.[^cite_note-43]

### December 2025 parliament

See also: [December 2025 Kosovan parliamentary election](81646979.md)

A [fresh election](81646979.md) was held on 28 December in which [Vetëvendosje](3225595.md) won nearly a majority in parliament, effectively ending the deadlock as [Albulena Haxhiu](43297113.md) was voted as the next Assembly Speaker by a majority of 66 lawmakers on 11 February 2026.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Later that day, Albin Kurti's third cabinet was approved, including nine nominees from the previous failed attempts.[^cite_note-New-44]

#### Presidential election deadlock

See also: [2026 Kosovan presidential election](67301945.md)

Under Article 86 of the Constitution of Kosovo, the Assembly must elect a president no later than 30 days before the end of the incumbent's term.[^cite_note-56] The 2026 election is the sixth presidential election held in Kosovo since independence in 2008. An initial attempt on 5 March 2026 failed because of a lack of quorum, triggering the 60-day constitutional period for electing a president.[^cite_note-57] On 6 March, President Vjosa Osmani dissolved the Assembly and moved toward snap parliamentary elections, but the decree was challenged by Vetëvendosje.[^cite_note-58] On 9 March, the Constitutional Court suspended the decree pending review.[^cite_note-59] On 25 March, the Court ruled that the dissolution decree had no legal effect and gave the Assembly 34 more days, until 28 April, to elect a president.[^cite_note-60] After Osmani's term expired on 4 April, Speaker of the Assembly Albulena Haxhiu became acting president until a new president is elected, for up to six months.[^cite_note-61]

A second attempt to elect the president is scheduled for 27 April 2026.[^cite_note-62] However, the three main opposition parties—the [Democratic Party of Kosovo](1260606.md) (PDK), [Democratic League of Kosovo](1256321.md) (LDK), and [Alliance for the Future of Kosovo](1260747.md) (AAK)—announced they would boycott the session, labeling the proceedings a "propaganda show" and "not serious".[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

On 28 April 2026, after the Assembly failed to elect a president within the deadline set by the Constitutional Court, it was dissolved in accordance with the Court's ruling. Acting president Albulena Haxhiu subsequently called snap [parlimentary elections](82589105.md), which were scheduled for 7 June 2026.[^cite_note-66]

### 2026 election

On 13 September 2026, the Assembly of Kosovo approved a new government led by [Albin Kurti](4774301.md), following the June 2026 parliamentary election. Kurti's government received 62 votes in the 120-seat Assembly, while opposition parties boycotted the vote and challenged the legality of the proceedings.[^cite_note-69]

The formation of the government ended one part of the institutional deadlock, but the broader political crisis remained unresolved because the Assembly had still not elected a president. The failure to reach the required two-thirds majority raised the possibility of another snap parliamentary election if the presidential deadlock could not be resolved.[^cite_note-70]
