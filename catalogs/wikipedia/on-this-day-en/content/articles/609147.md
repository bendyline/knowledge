# Transmission (mechanical device)

For other uses of the word "transmission", see [Transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_\(disambiguation\)).

"Gearbox" redirects here. For other uses, see [Gearbox (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gearbox_\(disambiguation\)).



Plan of sprocket-and-chain "gearbox" in Benz Patent-Motorwagen, 1886 - 1888. Normal transmission is from fixed pulley 34 to counter-shaft 22. Low gear is from loose pulley 31 through the gears and clutch 36.



Transmission internals for a 2009 [Volkswagen Golf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volkswagen_Golf)

A **transmission** (also called a **gearbox**) is a mechanical device which uses a [gear set](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_set)—two or more [gears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gears) working together—to change the speed, direction of rotation, or torque multiplication or reduction, in a [machine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine).[^cite_note-\:0-1][^cite_note-2]

It was invented by [Louis Renault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Renault_\(industrialist\)) (the founder of [Renault](162292.md)). It had been anticipated by [Carl Benz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Benz), who in 1886 used sprockets and chains to and from an auxiliary shaft and a clutch to provide a second, low gear in the first practical car, his belt-driven [Patent-Motorwagen Nr. 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benz_Patent-Motorwagen#Patent-Motorwagen_Nr._2).&#x20;

A transmission can have a single, or *fixed*, [gear ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_ratio) or it can have *variable* ratios; a *variable-ratio* transmission can have multiple *discrete* gear ratios or be *continuously variable*. Variable-ratio transmissions are used in many kinds of machinery, especially vehicles.

## Applications

### Early uses

Early transmissions included the right-angle drives and other gearing in [windmills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windmill), [horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse)-powered devices, and [steam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine)-powered devices. Applications of these devices included [pumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pump), [mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mill_\(grinding\)) and [hoists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hoist_\(device\)).

### Bicycles

Main article: [Bicycle gearing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle_gearing)

[Bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle) conventionally have used [hub gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hub_gear) or [derailleur gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derailleur_gear) transmissions, but there are other more recent design innovations.

### Automobiles



Power and torque curves for two example car engines

Since the [torque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torque) and [power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horsepower) output of an [internal combustion engine](41228673.md) (ICE) varies with its [(rotational) speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_speed), [automobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Car) powered by ICEs require multiple gear ratios to keep the engine within its [power band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power_band) to produce optimal power, [fuel efficiency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuel_efficiency), and smooth operation. Multiple gear ratios are also needed to provide sufficient acceleration and velocity for safe and reliable operation at modern highway speeds. ICEs typically operate over a range of approximately 600–7000 rpm, while the vehicle's speeds requires the wheels to [rotate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_speed) in the range of 0–1800 rpm.[^cite_note-3]

In the early mass-produced automobiles, the standard transmission design was [manual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manual_transmission): the combination of gears was selected by the driver through a lever (the [gear stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_stick)) that displaced gears and gear groups along their axes. Starting in 1939, cars using various types of [automatic transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_transmission) became available in the US market. These vehicles used the engine's own power to change the effective gear ratio depending on the load so as to keep the engine running close to its optimal rotation speed. Automatic transmissions now are used in more than two thirds of cars globally, and on almost all new cars in the US.

Most currently produced passenger cars with gasoline or diesel engines use transmissions with 4–10 forward gear ratios (also called *speeds*) and one reverse gear ratio. [Electric vehicles](279350.md) typically use fixed-ratio or two-speed transmissions with no *reverse*, as electric motors can operate efficiently across wider ranges of speed, all the way down to stopped, and change directions electrically. Electrical vehicles with more than one driven axle often have separate front and rear motors and drive trains rather than longitudinal drive shafts.

### Motorcycles

Main article: [Motorcycle transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorcycle_transmission)

## Fixed-ratio

The simplest transmissions used a fixed ratio to provide either a gear reduction or increase in speed, sometimes in conjunction with a change in the orientation of the output shaft. Examples of such transmissions are used in [helicopters](8286923.md) and [wind turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_turbine). In the case of a wind turbine, the first stage of the gearbox is usually a planetary gear, to minimize the size while withstanding the high torque inputs from the turbine.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

## Multi-ratio

Many transmissions – especially for transportation applications – have multiple gears that are used to change the ratio of input speed (e.g. engine speed) to the output speed (e.g. the speed of a car) as required for a given situation. Gear (ratio) selection can be manual, semi-automatic, or automatic.

### Manual

Main article: [Manual transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manual_transmission)

A manual transmission requires the driver to manually select the gears[^cite_note-6] by operating a [gear stick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_stick) and [clutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clutch) (which is usually a foot pedal for cars or a hand lever for motorcycles).

Early manual transmissions were non-synchronized and used straight cut gears, the latter means that while the gears move they make a distinct and loud whining sound, nowadays making them applicable only to heavy machinery like trucks, buses and tractors since they can withstand higher forces, or motorcycles and racing cars for the simple design and reductions in weight compared to common manual transmissions.

Most transmissions in modern cars use the [synchromesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manual_transmission#Synchromesh) design to synchronise the speeds of the input and output shafts. However, prior to the 1950s, most cars used [non-synchronous transmissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-synchronous_transmission).

#### Sequential manual

Main article: [Sequential manual transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sequential_manual_transmission)

A sequential manual transmission is a type of non-synchronous transmission used mostly for motorcycles and racing cars. It produces faster shift times than synchronized manual transmissions, through the use of [dog clutches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog_clutch) rather than synchromesh.[^cite_note-7] Sequential manual transmissions also restrict the driver to selecting either the next or previous gear, in a successive order.

Due to sequential transmissions being designed for reduction in complexity, weight and size for their use cases, unlike common manual transmissions, which are designed for simplicity of use and quiet operation, they use straight cut gears ([spur gears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spur_gears)), which produce a very characteristic whining sound while spinning. Sequential transmissions can produce more than one distinct whining sound depending on the specific design, but usually the most audible whine changes in pitch with (corresponds to) the speed of the wheels (output shaft), and gets more audible the faster the gears spin.

#### Non-synchronized manual (dog box)

Nicknamed a "dog box", it is a modern variant of a non-synchronized manual transmission which is constant mesh and used in performance applications, for example, racing. Manual transmissions like this lack the synchronizing part of the synchromesh design and use only the [dog clutches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dog_clutch) specifically made for dog boxes to engage gears. For engagement, the selector fork slides a dog clutch with teeth on its side directly onto the side of a constant mesh gear with a similar set of teeth or slots for them, which engages the dog teeth and the gear. This design allows for faster and also clutchless shifts, unlike a synchromesh design, but has the advantage of constant mesh, unlike older non-synchronized transmissions used in first automobiles.

Since first gear is mostly used only for taking off, and having 2nd and 3rd gear across each other gives a time advantage with the more common shift from second to third or backwards when racing, majority of dog box shifters use a shift pattern called "dog leg", which puts 1st gear into the left-down position, meaning 2nd gear is center-up, 3rd is center-down and so on. Reverse is usually, but not always, in the left-up position.

### Semi-automatic

A [semi-automatic transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-automatic_transmission) is where some of the operation is automated (often the actuation of the clutch), but the driver's input is required to move off from a standstill or to change gears.

#### Automated manual / clutchless manual

Main articles: [Automated manual transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automated_manual_transmission) and [Clutchless manual transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clutchless_manual_transmission)

An automated manual transmission (AMT) is essentially a conventional manual transmission that uses automatic actuation to operate the clutch and/or shift between gears.

Many early versions of these transmissions were semi-automatic in operation, such as *[Autostick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostick#Volkswagen_Autostick)*, which automatically control only the [clutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clutch), but still require the driver's input to initiate gear changes. Some of these systems are also referred to as *clutchless manual* systems.[^cite_note-motorbiscuit.com_clutchless_manual-8] Modern versions of these systems that are fully automatic in operation, such as *[Selespeed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selespeed)* and *[Easytronic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easytronic)*, can control both the clutch operation and the gear shifts automatically, without any input from the driver.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-tranzmile.com-10]

### Automatic

An [automatic transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_transmission) does not require any input from the driver to change forward gears under normal driving conditions.

#### Hydraulic automatic

Main article: [Hydraulic automatic transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_automatic_transmission)

The most common design of automatic transmissions is the hydraulic automatic, which typically uses [planetary gearsets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epicyclic_gearing) that are operated using [hydraulics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_machinery).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] The transmission is connected to the engine via a [torque converter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torque_converter) (or a [fluid coupling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluid_coupling) prior to the 1960s), instead of the [friction clutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clutch#Friction_clutches) used by most manual transmissions and dual-clutch transmissions.[^cite_note-awnc-13]

#### Dual-clutch (DCT)

Main article: [Dual-clutch transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual-clutch_transmission)

A dual-clutch transmission (DCT) uses two separate [clutches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clutch) for odd and even [gear sets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_train).[^cite_note-AMS-14] The design is often similar to two separate [manual transmissions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manual_transmission) with their respective clutches contained within one housing, and working as one unit.[^cite_note-AEI-15][^cite_note-PorschePDK_Glossary-16] In car and truck applications, the DCT functions as an automatic transmission, requiring no driver input to change gears.

## Continuously-variable ratio

Main article: [Continuously variable transmission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuously_variable_transmission)

A continuously variable transmission (CVT) can change seamlessly through a continuous range of [gear ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_ratio). This contrasts with other transmissions that provide a limited number of gear ratios in fixed steps. The flexibility of a CVT with suitable control may allow the engine to operate at a constant [speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotational_speed) while the vehicle moves at varying speeds.

CVTs are used in cars, tractors, [side-by-sides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Side-by-side_\(vehicle\)), motor scooters, [snowmobiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmobile), bicycles, and [earthmoving equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_equipment).

The most common type of CVT uses two [pulleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulley) connected by a [belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belt_\(mechanical\)) or [chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_drive); however, several other designs have also been used at times.

## Noise and vibration

Gearboxes are often a major source of [noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roadway_noise) and vibration in vehicles and stationary machinery. Higher [sound levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_intensity_level) are generally emitted when the vehicle is engaged in lower gears. The design life of the lower ratio gears is shorter, so cheaper gears may be used, which tend to generate more noise due to smaller overlap ratio and a lower mesh stiffness etc. than the helical gears used for the high ratios. This fact has been used to analyze vehicle-generated sound since the late 1960s, and has been incorporated into the simulation of urban roadway noise and corresponding design of urban [noise barriers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise_barrier) along roadways.[^cite_note-17]

## Gear design

Main article: [Gear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear)

Gears are machine elements aimed to transmit power, their proper design is defined by standard.

The standard ISO 6336[^cite_note-\:02-18] and AGMA 2001[^cite_note-\:1-19] provide informations regarding the design procedure and define calculation method to verify if gears are safe with respect to different failure mechanism.&#x20;

Without a correct design and [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gear_manufacturing) of the gears the transmission could present low efficiency, high [NVH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noise,_vibration,_and_harshness) and low lifetime.

Gears present several failure mechanisms, which are: [wear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wear), scuffing, pitting, [micro-pitting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micro_pitting), tooth flank fracture and tooth root fatigue fracture.

These mechanisms are due to several phenomena: [friction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friction), [contact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contact_mechanics) (Hertzian pressure, sliding/rolling), bending [fatigue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatigue_\(material\)) and lack of [lubrication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubrication). All these phenomena can happen simultaneously and they lead to the failure of the gearbox.
