[Nicaragua](21362.md) and [Spain](26667.md) maintain diplomatic relations. Both nations are members of the [Association of Academies of the Spanish Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_Academies_of_the_Spanish_Language) and the [Organization of Ibero-American States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organization_of_Ibero-American_States).

## History

### Spanish colonization



Spanish [Fortress of the Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortress_of_the_Immaculate_Conception) built in 1673 in [El Castillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Castillo_\(village\)), Nicaragua.

Main article: [Spanish conquest of Nicaragua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_Nicaragua)

In September 1502, explorer [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus) arrived to eastern Nicaragua on his fourth voyage to the Americas. In 1522, Spanish conquistador [Gil González Dávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gil_Gonz%C3%A1lez_D%C3%A1vila) arrived to Nicaragua and claimed the territory for Spain. Gil González Dávila named the territory after a chief named [Nicarao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicarao_\(cacique\)) and combined the name for the Spanish word for water (*Agua*).[^cite_note-BBC-1] In 1524, Spanish conquistador [Francisco Hernández de Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Hern%C3%A1ndez_de_C%C3%B3rdoba_\(founder_of_Nicaragua\)) founded the first Spanish settlements of [Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granada,_Nicaragua) and [León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le%C3%B3n,_Nicaragua).[^cite_note-History-2]

As a result of the Spanish invasion, the native population drastically fell. Within three decades an estimated native population of one million plummeted to a few tens of thousands, as approximately half of the natives died of contagious diseases originally from [Europe](9239.md), and most of the rest were made to work in forced labor in other New World Spanish colonies.[^cite_note-History-2]

The territory of Nicaragua soon officially became part of the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) under the [Viceroy of New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Spain) based in [Mexico City](18987.md) and administered by the [Captaincy General of Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_Guatemala) based in [Santiago de Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigua_Guatemala). For most of Spanish colonization, the territory was mostly neglected. In 1740, [Great Britain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Britain) took over the [Mosquito Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast) of Nicaragua and held it until 1786.[^cite_note-History-2]

### Independence

Main article: [Act of Independence of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Act_of_Independence_of_Central_America)

In 1808, [Joseph Bonaparte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Bonaparte) was installed as King of Spain and several [Spanish American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_America) colonies began to declare their independence from Spain. As Nicaragua and most [Central American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American) nations were governed by Mexico City; New Spain declared its independence from Spain in 1810. In 1821, the [Plan of Iguala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_of_Iguala) declared Mexico as a constitutional monarchy. Nicaragua declared its own independence from Spain on 15 September 1821 and chose to join the [Mexican Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mexican_Empire) under Emperor [Agustín de Iturbide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agust%C3%ADn_de_Iturbide).[^cite_note-BBC-1]

In March 1823, Iturbide resigned as Emperor and Mexico became a republic. Nicaragua decided to separate from Mexico on 1 July 1823. Nicaragua, along with [Costa Rica](5551.md), [El Salvador](9356.md), [Guatemala](17238567.md) and [Honduras](13394.md) formed the [Federal Republic of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Central_America). In 1839 the Central American Federation dissolved and Nicaragua became an independent nation.[^cite_note-History-2]

### Post-Independence

On 20 March 1851, Nicaragua and Spain officially established diplomatic relations and signed a *Treaty of Peace and Friendship*.[^cite_note-3] During the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War), Nicaragua officially recognized the government of [Francisco Franco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Franco).[^cite_note-4]

During the [Nicaraguan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Revolution) (1961-1990), Spain maintained diplomatic relations with the [Junta of National Reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junta_of_National_Reconstruction) which was led by [Daniel Ortega](8778.md). As a condition of relations, Nicaragua promised that the [Basque separatist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basque_separatist) group, [ETA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ETA_\(separatist_group\)), would not be allowed to operate on Nicaraguan soil even though they openly supported the [Sandinistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front).[^cite_note-5]

In April 1991, King [Juan Carlos I of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Carlos_I_of_Spain) paid his first and only visit to Nicaragua to celebrate the end of the [Nicaraguan Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Revolution). During his visit, he met with Nicaraguan President [Violeta Chamorro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violeta_Chamorro).[^cite_note-6]

In February 2023, the Spanish government extended an offer to grant [Spanish citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_citizenship) to any [Nicaraguan citizen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_nationality_law) that may be declared [stateless](789786.md) by the Ortega government, building upon an initial proposal extended to 222 specific Nicaraguan individuals expelled to the United States earlier in the month.[^cite_note-7]

Relations were later reset with new ambassadorial appointments in 2023. In 2026, Nicaragua and Spain again ordered the expulsion of each others' ambassadors.[^cite_note-8]

## Agreements

Both nations have signed several bilateral agreements such as an Agreement on Dual-Nationality (1961); Agreement on Technical Cooperation (1975); Extradition Treaty (1977); Agreement on Cultural, Educational and Scientific Cooperation (1991); Agreement on Air Transportation (1992); Agreement on the reciprocal Promotion and Protection of Investments (1994) and an Agreement on homologation and exchange of Driving Licenses (2012).[^cite_note-Bilateral-19]

## Trade

In 2024, trade between Nicaragua and Spain totaled €119 million Euros.[^cite_note-Bilateral-19] Nicaragua's main exports to Spain include: fish, crustaceans and mussels. Spain's main exports to Nicaragua include: machinery, pharmaceuticals, electronic equipment, clothing and food.[^cite_note-Bilateral-19] Spanish multinational companies such as [Mapfre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mapfre) and [Zara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zara_\(retailer\)) operate in Nicaragua.
