**Winterthur** (Swiss Standard German pronunciation: [\[ˈvɪntərtuːr\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German); [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Winterthour* [\[vintəʁtuʁ, vintɛʁ-\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)) is a city and municipality in the [canton of Zurich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_of_Zurich) in northern [Switzerland](26748.md). With over 120,000 residents, it is the country's [sixth-largest city by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Switzerland), as well as its ninth-largest agglomeration with about 140,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-2] Located about 20 kilometres (12 mi) northeast of [Zurich](40334603.md), Winterthur is a service and high-tech industrial [satellite city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_city) within [Zurich Metropolitan Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Metropolitan_Area). Winterthur is usually abbreviated as *Winti* in the local dialect.

Winterthur is connected to Germany by direct trains and has links to [Zurich Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Airport). It is also a regional transport hub: the A1 motorway from [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva) through to [St. Margrethen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Margrethen) connects in Winterthur with the A4 motorway heading north toward [Schaffhausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schaffhausen) and the A7 motorway heading close to the Swiss-German border at [Kreuzlingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kreuzlingen). There are also roads leading to other places such as [Turbenthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbenthal). The [railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterthur_railway_station) is the fourth busiest railway station in Switzerland,[^cite_note-3] and is 20 minutes away by train from Zurich.

## History

*[Vitudurum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitudurum)* was a *[vicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicus_\(Rome\))* in what is now [Oberwinterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberwinterthur) during the [Roman era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_in_the_Roman_era) (first century BC to third century AD). It was fortified into a *[castrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castrum)* at the end of the third century, apparently in reaction to the incipient [Alamannic invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alemanni#Conflicts_with_the_Roman_Empire).

There was an [Alamannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamanni) settlement on the site in the seventh century.

In a [battle near Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Winterthur_\(919\)) in 919, [Burchard II of Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burchard_II,_Duke_of_Swabia) asserted his control over the [Thurgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turgowe) within the [Duchy of Swabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Swabia) against the claims of [Rudolph II of Burgundy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudolph_II_of_Burgundy).

The [counts of Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counts_of_Winterthur), a cadet branch of the family of the [counts of Bregenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counts_of_Bregenz), built [Kyburg castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyburg_castle) in the tenth century. With the extinction of the counts of Winterthur in 1053, the castle passed to the [counts of Dillingen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counts_of_Dillingen). Winterthur as a city (presumably on the site of a pre-existing village) was founded by Hartmann III of Dillingen in 1180, shortly before his death in the same year. From 1180 to 1263, Winterthur was ruled by the cadet line of the [House of Kyburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyburg_family).

When the counts of Kyburg became extinct in the male line in 1263, Winterthur passed to the [House of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg), who established a [comital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Count_\(nobility\)) line of *Neu-Kyburg* in 1264 and granted [city rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_rights) to Winterthur in the same year.[^cite_note-4] From 1415 until 1442 Winterthur was *[reichsfrei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_immediacy)* (subject only to the [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor)). However, in the [Old Zürich War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Z%C3%BCrich_War) they lost this freedom and came back under the control of the Austrian Habsburgs. Needing money, in 1467, the Habsburgs sold Winterthur to the city of [Zurich](40334603.md).

While it was under the leadership of Zurich, Winterthur's economic freedom was restricted. It lost many of its market rights and the right to trade in some goods. This ended in 1798, when [Napoleonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleon) troops took the town. On 27 May 1799, it was the site of the [Battle of Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Winterthur) between elements of the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_First_Republic) [Army of the Danube](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Danube) and elements of the Habsburg army, commanded by [Friedrich, Baron von Hotze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friedrich_Freiherr_von_Hotze) during the [War of the Second Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_the_Second_Coalition), in the [French Revolutionary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolutionary_Wars). Because Winterthur lies near Zurich and at the junction of seven roads, the army that held the town held the access to most of Switzerland and points crossing the [Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhine) into southern Germany. Although the forces involved were small, the ability of the Austrians to sustain an 11-hour assault against the French line, on the plateau north of Zurich, resulted in the consolidation of three Austrian forces. This led to the French [defeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Zurich) a few days later.

In the 19th century, Winterthur became an industrial town when companies, like [Sulzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulzer_\(manufacturer\)), [Rieter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rieter) and [SLM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Locomotive_and_Machine_Works), built large industrial plants. Winterthur suffered severely from its investments in and guarantee of loans to the National Railway of Switzerland (a private enterprise). In 1878, Winterthur had to sell its shares in the line, and from 1881 to 1885 it was in great difficulties due to a loan of nine million francs guaranteed in 1874 by the town, together with three others in Aargau, to the enterprise. As the three co-guarantor towns were unable to pay their shares, the whole burden fell on Winterthur, which struggled to meet its liabilities. But it was assisted by large loans from the cantonal and federal governments.[^cite_note-5]

The [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression), in the 1930s, hit Winterthur extremely hard. Sixty percent of the total employees in town worked in the [machine industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machine_industry). Jobs became extremely hard to find. However, with the outbreak of [World War II](32927.md), industry grew again in the city.

In 2008, Winterthur reached 100,000 inhabitants.

## Geography

### Topography

Winterthur is located at an elevation of 439 meters (1,440 ft). The city is located in a basin south and east of the river [Töss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%B6ss_\(river\)) before it meets the [High Rhine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Rhine) after 10 kilometers. The [Eulach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eulach_\(river\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), a small river, flows from the town's east end through the middle of the town to meet the Töss at the west exit of the city. Because of this the town is colloquially also called "Eulachstadt". Zurich lies about 20 km (12 mi) southwest of Winterthur.

### Area

As of 2004-2009, Winterthur has an area of 68.1 km<sup>2</sup> (26.3 sq mi); 24.8% is used for agricultural purposes, 40.4% is forested, 33.6% is settled (buildings or roads) and 1.1% is non-productive (rivers, glaciers or mountains).[^cite_note-SFSO-6] In 1996 housing and buildings made up 21.9% of the total area, while transportation infrastructure made up the rest (9%).[^cite_note-StatZürich-7] Of the total unproductive area, water (streams and lakes) made up 0.6% of the area. As of 2007, 27.6% of the total municipal area was undergoing some type of construction.[^cite_note-StatZürich-7]

### Government

The City Council (**Stadtrat**) constitutes the executive government of the City of Winterthur and operates as a [collegiate authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collegiate_authority?action=edit\&redlink=1). It is composed of seven councilors (German: *Stadtrat/ Stadträtin*), each presiding over a department. Departmental tasks, coordination measures and implementation of laws decreed by the Large Municipal Council are carried by the City Council. In the mandate period 2018–2022 (*Legislatur*) the City Council is presided by *Stadtpräsident* [Michael Künzle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_K%C3%BCnzle?action=edit\&redlink=1). The regular election of the City Council by any inhabitant valid to vote is held every four years. Any resident of Winterthur allowed to vote can be elected as a member of the City Council. The mayor is elected as such by public election by means of a system of [Majorz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majorz?action=edit\&redlink=1), while the heads of the other directorates are assigned by the collegiate. The delegates are elected by means of a system of Majorz.[^cite_note-8]

As of 2018, Winterthur's City Council is made up of three representatives of the SP ([Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland)), one member of the FDP ([FDP.The Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDP.The_Liberals)), one of the GPS ([Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Switzerland)), one of the GLP ([Green Liberal Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Liberal_Party_of_Switzerland)), and one of the CVP ([Christian Democratic People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_People's_Party_of_Switzerland), who is also the mayor), giving the left parties a combined four out of seven seats. The last regular election was held on 5 March and 15 April 2018,[^cite_note-wahlen18-9] the last special election on 23 August 2020.[^cite_note-10]

The Large Municipal Council (**Grosser Gemeinderat**) holds [legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power). It is made up of 60 members, with elections held every four years. The Large Municipal Council decrees regulations and by-laws that are executed by the City Council and the administration. The delegates are selected by means of a system of [proportional representation](23298.md) (Proporz).

The sessions of the Large Municipal Council are public. Unlike members of the City Council, members of the Large Municipal Council are not politicians by profession, and they are paid a fee based on their attendance. Any resident of Winterthur allowed to vote can be elected as a member of the Large Municipal Council. The parliament holds its meetings in the *Rathaus* once a month on Mondays.[^cite_note-13]

The last regular election of the Large Municipal Council was held on 8 March 2018 for the mandate period (German: *Legislatur*) from May 2018 to April 2022. Currently the Large Municipal Council consists of 18 members of the [Social Democratic Party (SP/PS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland), 10 [Swiss People's Party (SVP/UDC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_People's_Party), 8 [The Liberals (FDP/PLR)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDP.The_Liberals), 7 [Green Liberal Party (GLP/PVL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Liberal_Party_of_Switzerland), 5 [Green Party (GPS/PES)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Switzerland), 4 [Evangelical People's Party (EVP)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_People's_Party_of_Switzerland), 3 [Christian Democratic People's Party (CVP/PDC)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_People's_Party_of_Switzerland), 2 [Alternative List (AL)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_List), one representative each of the [Conservative Democratic Party (BDP/PBD)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland), [Federal Democratic Union (EDU/UDF)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Democratic_Union_of_Switzerland), and the [Pirate Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pirate_Party_Switzerland).[^cite_note-wahlen18-9]

#### National Council

The next six most popular parties were the [SVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_People's_Party) (17.8%, -5.6), the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Switzerland) (17.8%, +9), the [glp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Liberal_Party_of_Switzerland) (14.3%, +5.5), [FDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDP.The_Liberals) (10.6%, -1.5), the [EVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_People's_Party_of_Switzerland) (5.0%, 0), and the [CVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_People's_Party_of_Switzerland) (4.2%, +0.2).[^cite_note-14] In the federal election a total of 32,907 votes were cast, and the [voter turnout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voter_turnout) was 47.0%.[^cite_note-15]

In the 2015 election for the [Swiss National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_\(Switzerland\)) the most popular party was the [SPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland) which received 26.1% of the vote. The next most popular parties were the [SVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_People's_Party) (23.4%), the [FDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FDP.The_Liberals) (12.1%), the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_of_Switzerland) (8.8%), the [glp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Liberal_Party_of_Switzerland) (8.8%), the [EVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_People's_Party_of_Switzerland) (5.0%), the [CVP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Democratic_People's_Party_of_Switzerland) (4.0%), and [BDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Democratic_Party_of_Switzerland) (3.5%). In the federal election, a total of 33,426 voters were cast, and the voter turnout was 49.3%.[^cite_note-16] In the 2011, [federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elections_in_Switzerland) the most popular party was the SP which received 22.5% of the vote. The next three most popular parties were the SVP (21.8%), the [Green Liberals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Liberal_Party_of_Switzerland) (11.1%) and the Green Party (10.1%).

As of July 2008 the population of Winterthur is 100,000.[^cite_note-21] More recently (as of 31 December 2020) the population was 114,220.[^cite_note-Stat2020_ZH-22] As of 2007 23.6% of the population was made up of foreign nationals.[^cite_note-Winterthur_stat-23] As of 2008 the gender distribution of the population was 48.6% male and 51.4% female. Over the last 10 years the population has grown at a rate of 10.4%. Most of the population (as of 2000) speaks German (83.0%), with Italian being second-most common (4.9%) and Albanian being third (2.0%).

The age distribution of the population (as of 2018) is children and teenagers (0–19 years old) make up 19.9% of the population, while adults (20–64 years old) make up 63.9% and seniors (over 64 years old) make up 16.2%.[^cite_note-SFSO-6] There are 42,028 households in Winterthur.[^cite_note-StatZürich-7]

As of 2008 there were 37,327 members of the [Swiss Reformed Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Reformed_Church) (37.1% of the population) and 26,995 Catholics (26.7% of the population) in Winterthur. Of the other Christian faiths, 326 (0.3%) were [Lutheran](23371382.md), 203 (0.2%) were [Christian Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Catholic_Church_of_Switzerland), 3,141 (3.1%) are some type of [Christian Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodoxy) and 3,132 (3.1%) are another Christian faith. Of the rest of the population, 11,608 (11.5%) were Muslim, 108 (0.1%) were Jewish, 1,359 (1.3%) belonged to another non-Christian faith and 16,779 (16.6%) were atheist or agnostic or did not belong to any organized faith.[^cite_note-Winterthur_stat-23]

## Economy



Axa Winterthur headquarters

Historically, Winterthur was one of the homes of [Switzerland's rail industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_rail_transport_in_Switzerland) and an industrial centre, however the rail industry and other heavy industry have mostly shut down. Amongst the most significant companies was Sulzer Brothers, today's [Sulzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulzer_\(manufacturer\)). Textile production declined even earlier on. The [Rieter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rieter) textile machinery company is based in Winterthur.

Switzerland's largest bank, and one of the world's large banks, [Union Bank of Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Bank_of_Switzerland) (UBS, since 1998 [UBS AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UBS_AG)), was founded in Winterthur.

The *[Landbote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Der_Landbote)* newspaper is situated in Winterthur, and also serves as *Winterthurer Stadtanzeiger*, the official publication organ of the city of Winterthur.

[Peraves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peraves), the manufacturer of the fully enclosed "cabin motorcycle" named the Monotracer, predated by an earlier model named the [Ecomobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecomobile), has been manufacturing these vehicles since the early 1980s. In 2010, Peraves won the [Progressive Insurance Automotive X-Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automotive_X_Prize) with an electric powered version of the Monotracer.

Among other commercial organizations, Winterthur was home to Switzerland's largest insurance business [Winterthur Insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterthur_Group). Until its acquisition, the company was the largest in Switzerland and was in Europe's top 10. On 1 January 2007 the Winterthur company was acquired by the French [Axa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axa) group and is now known as AXA Winterthur.

As of 2011, Winterthur had an unemployment rate of 3.53%.[^cite_note-SFSO2012-24] As of 2017, there were 185 people employed in the [primary economic sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_sector_of_the_economy) and about 60 businesses involved in this sector; 11,880 people are employed in the [secondary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_sector_of_the_economy) and there are 884 businesses in this sector; 59,767 people are employed in the [tertiary sector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy), with 6,983 businesses in this sector.[^cite_note-SFSO-6] As of 2007 47.9% of the working population were employed full-time, and 52.1% were employed part-time.[^cite_note-StatZürich-7]

## Education



[ZHAW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_University_of_Applied_Sciences) Campus and [Roter Turm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Tower_\(Winterthur\))

In Winterthur, about 70.7% of the population (between ages 25 and 64) have completed either non-mandatory [upper secondary education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Switzerland#Secondary) or additional higher education (either university or a *[Fachhochschule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fachhochschule)*).[^cite_note-SFSO2012-24]

The town is renowned for its institute of higher education *Technikum*, which is the largest school of technology in Switzerland. The institute has recently teamed up with schools from Zurich and is now known as [Zürcher Hochschule für Angewandte Wissenschaften](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_University_of_Applied_Sciences/ZHAW) (ZHAW).

The headquarters of the [Club of Rome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_of_Rome) are located in Winterthur.

[SIS Swiss International School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIS_Swiss_International_School) maintains a campus in Winterthur. [International School Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_School_Winterthur), formerly located in Winterthur, closed in 2015.

## Transport



Winterthur railway station

[Winterthur railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterthur_railway_station) (*Bahnhof Winterthur*), the city's principal [railway station](26161590.md), is the fifth-busiest railway station on the [Swiss Railway Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Switzerland), with 110,900 passengers per day (as of 2023).[^cite_note-25] As Winterthur is close to [Zurich](40334603.md), there are frequent rail services between the two cities. Winterthur is served by trains of the regional [Zurich S-Bahn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_S-Bahn) network (operated by [Zürcher Verkehrsverbund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Z%C3%BCrcher_Verkehrsverbund), ZVV), but also several long-distance services, both national and international, such as the [EuroCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EuroCity) (EC) service to [Munich](19058.md), [InterCity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterCity_\(Switzerland\)) (IC) services to [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), [Bern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bern), [St. Gallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Gallen) and [Romanshorn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanshorn), and [InterRegio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InterRegio) (IR) services to [Konstanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstanz), St. Gallen, [Chur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chur) and [Lucerne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucerne).

## Culture

### Architecture



Street in the old town

The church of *St. Laurenz* in the city centre dates from 1264, the town hall was built in 1781, the assembly hall in 1865.

The city hall *[Stadthaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadthaus_\(Winterthur\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)*, in which the concerts of the Musikkollegium take place, was built by [Gottfried Semper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Semper).

In 1989, Winterthur received the [Wakker Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakker_Prize) for the development and preservation of its architectural heritage.

### Music

Winterthur's chamber orchestra [Orchester Musikkollegium Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchester_Musikkollegium_Winterthur) is the oldest orchestra in Switzerland, and also plays at the [Zurich Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zurich_Opera). Between 1922 and 1950, the philanthropist [Werner Reinhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Reinhart) and the conductor [Hermann Scherchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hermann_Scherchen) played a leading role in shaping the musical life of Winterthur, with numerous premiere performances emphasizing contemporary music.[^cite_note-31]

Musikfestwochen, in late August and early September, sees Winterthur's Old Town taken over for live music of all kinds, in the street and bars as well as in concert venues.

The "Albanifest", the largest annual festival in a historic town in Switzerland, is named after [Saint Alban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Alban), one of the city's four saints, is held here, over three days in late June every year. Although a recent creation, the festival celebrates the granting of a charter to the town in 1264 by Rudolf of [Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburgh?action=edit\&redlink=1) on 22 June of that year, which happened to be the saint's day.

The Swiss [folk metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_metal) band [Eluveitie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eluveitie) was formed in Winterthur, and both the [Punkabilly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychobilly) band The Peacocks and the technical death metal band [Punish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punish_\(band\)) come from here.

### Arts

#### Open Doors

[Open Doors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Doors_Winterthur?action=edit\&redlink=1) is an artist supported platform for artists with art studios in Winterthur, Switzerland. The platform was established to bridge arts and the community as well as provide the artists with means to independently promote their art in any way they choose. Open Doors takes place annually during the last weekend of September. Participating artists open their studios to the public and present their art to the public. Oftentimes it is possible to view the artists while they are working. Among the approximate 60 artists who participate there are local, international, autodidacts and art academy graduates. Open Doors Winterthur was founded in 2008 by San Francisco-born artist Michelle Bird and resident of Winterthur. Open Doors Winterthur publishes the annual MAP Magazine Artist Professionals which is available on line and in print form. *MAP Magazine* features articles about local art initiatives and profiles local artists and their art studios. The event is supported by a map that indicates the location of each artist's studio on a map.

## Sport

[EHC Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EHC_Winterthur) is the city's main hockey team which currently plays in the [Swiss League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_League), the second-highest ice hockey league in Switzerland. Their arena is the 3,000-seat [Deutweg Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutweg_Arena?action=edit\&redlink=1). The arena held in April 2011 the [2011 IIHF Women's World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_IIHF_Women's_World_Championship) top division, sharing the hosting with the [Hallenstadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallenstadion) in [Zurich](40334603.md).

[FC Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Winterthur) are the city's [football](10568.md) club and currently play in the [Swiss Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_Super_League). They play at the [Stadion Schützenwiese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadion_Sch%C3%BCtzenwiese).

[Pfadi Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pfadi_Winterthur) is the professional [handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_handball) club, former multiple national champions and still playing in the [Swiss First League of Handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swiss_First_League_of_Handball). They play at the [Winterthur Central Sports Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterthur_Central_Sports_Hall) which they share with top [floorball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floorball) club [HC Rychenberg Winterthur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HC_Rychenberg_Winterthur).

[Winterthur Lions AFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winterthur_Lions_AFC?action=edit\&redlink=1), founded in 2017, have been playing [Australian rules football](2403.md) in the [AFL Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL_Switzerland) league since 2019.
