The **Ministry of National Defence** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Ministerstwo Obrony Narodowej* [\[mi.ɲisˈtɛr.stfɔ ɔˈbrɔ.nɘ na.rɔˈdɔ.vɛj\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish)*, MON* [\[ˈmɔn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b6/Pl-MON.ogg/Pl-MON.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/b/b6/Pl-MON.ogg/Pl-MON.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is an [office of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Poland) in [Poland](22936.md) headed by the Minister of National Defence. It is responsible for the organisation and management of the [Polish Armed Forces](40153898.md). During the [Second Polish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Polish_Republic) and [World War II](32927.md) it was called the **Ministry of Military Affairs** (*Ministerstwo Spraw Wojskowych*). Its budget for 2025 was 186,6 billion [PLN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_z%C5%82oty).[^cite_note-2]

## History

The beginning of the Ministry of Defence's operations is connected with the 1775 establishment of the Military Department within the [Permanent Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Council). In 1789, the Military Commission of the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth) was established, and from the [Constitution of 3 May 1791](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_3_May_1791) was under the [Guardians of the Laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guardians_of_the_Laws). Between 1793 and 1794, the department was restored in the Supreme National Council. When [Warsaw](32908.md) became part of the [Kingdom of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Prussia) after the [Third Partition of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Partition_of_Poland) in 1795, the Prussian Ministry of War headquarters was moved into the local [Copper-Roof Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper-Roof_Palace).[^cite_note-wiezowce-3] Another War Ministry was established in the [Duchy of Warsaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Warsaw). After the establishment of the [Stanisław Małachowski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanis%C5%82aw_Ma%C5%82achowski) government on 5 October 1807, the War Directorate became the Minister of War.

From 1807 to 1810, the number of ministry officials increased from a dozen to over one hundred. The ministry's activities ceased on 4 May 1813. In 1814, the Military Organizing Committee was established in [Paris](22989.md) to regulate the military affairs of the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland) in 1815.[^cite_note-kp-4][^cite_note-5] After the [November Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/November_Uprising) and the introduction of the [Organic Statute of the Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_Statute_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) in the early 1830s, the distinctiveness of the Polish defence establishment from the Ministry of War of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) ceased to exist. On 30 January 1917, the [Provisional Council of State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Council_of_State) created an acting military commission, which was to deal with Polish military matters until a war office was organised.



The main gate to the Ministry of National Defence complex on Klonowa Street.

On 2 November 1918, the commission was transformed into the Ministry of Military Affairs, based at the [Copper-Roof Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper-Roof_Palace).[^cite_note-caw-6] During the London emigration of Polish power during World War II, on November 30, 1942, the name of the Ministry of Military Affairs was changed to the Ministry of National Defence. In 1944, under the [Polish Committee of National Liberation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Committee_of_National_Liberation) under the [communists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) controlled a National Defence Centre to manage the war front. After the war, the [Provisional Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_Government_of_National_Unity) (TRJN) reestablished the Military Affairs Ministry, which would be replaced by the Ministry of National Defence in 1979 and was under the [Polish People's Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_People's_Army) (LWP) in the [People's Republic of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Republic_of_Poland). The ministry would be transferred from the LWP to the Polish Army in 1990.
