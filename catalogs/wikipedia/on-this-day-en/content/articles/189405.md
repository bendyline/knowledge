**Cundinamarca&#x20;**&#x20;(Spanish pronunciation: [\[kundinaˈmaɾka\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is a [department of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Colombia). Its area covers 22,623 square kilometres (8,735 sq mi) (not including the Capital District) and it has a population of 2,919,060 as of 2018 (3,445,300, DANE 2023 projections). It was created on August 5, 1886, under the constitutional terms presented on the same year. Cundinamarca is located in the center of Colombia.

Cundinamarca's capital city is [Bogotá](211271.md), the capital of Colombia. This is a special case among Colombian departments, since [Bogotá is not legally a part of Cundinamarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_capitals_outside_the_territories_they_serve), yet it is the only department that has its capital designated by the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Constitution_of_1991) (if the capital were to be ever moved, it would take a constitutional reform to do so, instead of a simple ordinance passed by the Cundinamarca Assembly). In [censuses](6889.md), the populations for Bogotá and Cundinamarca are tabulated separately; otherwise, Cundinamarca's population would total over 11 million.

## Etymology

According to Colombia’s National Cultural Information System (*Sistema Nacional de Información Cultural de Colombia,* SINIC), the territory that now comprises the department of Cundinamarca was inhabited in [pre-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Columbian_era) times by various Indigenous societies, most notably the [Muisca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muisca). This presence has led some historians to associate the origin of the department’s name with terms belonging to this community. However, there is no consensus regarding its exact origin, resulting in various interpretations of its meaning.[^cite_note-\:1-6]

One of the most widely circulated theories was proposed by historian [Liborio Zerda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liborio_Zerda), who suggests that *Cundinamarca*, *Cundurcunca*, *Cutirimarca*, or *Cundurrumarca* translates as “land or height where the [condor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condor) lives.” According to SINIC, several scholars of linguistics agree that Cundinamarca has its roots in a combination of words from the [Chibchan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibchan_languages) spoken by the Muisca.[^cite_note-\:1-6]

On the other hand, the official website of the Government of Cundinamarca proposes that the name may derive from the words *Cundi-ramarca* and *Cuntinamarca*, which would have their origins in [Aymara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aymara_language), the language spoken by ancient inhabitants of [Peru](170691.md) and [Bolivia](3462.md).[^cite_note-\:2-7]

A notable aspect of the name Cundinamarca is its longevity, as it has been used to refer to the region since pre-Hispanic times. Despite the variations it has undergone throughout history, it has maintained its connection to Indigenous heritage, reflecting the cultural continuity of the area.[^cite_note-\:1-6]

## History

### Prehistoric period



Goldwork representation of the ancient tradition of the Zipa, who, covered in gold, made offerings to the goddess of [Lake Guatavita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Guatavita) by throwing gold objects into the lake. This tradition is the origin of the legend of El Dorado.

The territory of Cundinamarca was inhabited as early as around 12,000 BC, as demonstrated by fossil records found in [El Abra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Abra), [Zipaquirá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipaquir%C3%A1), [Nemocón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nemoc%C3%B3n), [Soacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soacha), and [Tequendama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tequendama). Like [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica) and the [Andean region of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andean_civilizations), it underwent periods of cultural development, progressing from nomadism to the establishment of an advanced culture and passing through the Preclassic, Classic, and Postclassic periods. However, the pre-Muisca civilizations left few traces of their development.

The [Muisca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muisca), belonging to the [Chibchan language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibchan_languages) family, are believed to have arrived sometime between 1000 BC and AD 1000, during the Classic period. The cultivation of maize, potatoes, tomatoes, and other crops emerged around 1200 BC, when a moderate degree of sedentary settlement developed. Intensive agriculture and industrial processes enabled the development in Cundinamarca of the Muisca civilization, one of the three most advanced pre-Hispanic cultures in the Americas. Probably during the 14th century, peoples of [Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pijao_people) origin invaded the [Magdalena Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_Valley) and the provinces of [Sumapaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumapaz_Province), [Tequendama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tequendama_Province), [Gualivá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gualiv%C3%A1_Province), and [Rionegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rionegro_Province). These peoples became known as the [Panches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panche_people), while some smaller tribes of [Arawak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawak) origin were displaced toward the eastern foothills.[^cite_note-\:3-8]

### Spanish period



Territory of the [Province of Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogot%C3%A1_Province) in 1810, which proclaimed itself the [Free and Independent State of Cundinamarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_Independent_State_of_Cundinamarca) in 1811.

When the Spanish arrived in the region, they found that the Muisca inhabited the high plateau and were governed by the [Zipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muisca_rulers) and a number of lesser *[caciques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cacique)*. Two major confrontations in Zipaquirá and on the banks of the [Bogotá River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogot%C3%A1_River) defeated the Chibcha, and the Zipa Tisquesusa retreated. He was located in [Facatativá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facatativ%C3%A1), where he was killed by the Spanish. An alliance with his successor, [Sagipa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sagipa), enabled the Spanish to defeat and subjugate the [Panches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panche_people) at the [Battle of Tocarema](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tocarema). Subsequently, through an agreement at [Tocaima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocaima), the remaining Panche nation was brought under Spanish control. In this way, the entire territory of Cundinamarca came under the authority of the [Spanish Crown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy_of_Spain).[^cite_note-\:3-8]

The [Bogotá savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogot%C3%A1_savanna) was the site of the meeting of the conquistadors [Gonzalo Jiménez de Quesada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzalo_Jim%C3%A9nez_de_Quesada), [Nicolás de Federmán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolaus_Federmann), and [Sebastián de Belalcázar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_de_Belalc%C3%A1zar) in 1538. Although Jiménez de Quesada named the territories the [New Kingdom of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Granada), the region was initially placed under the jurisdiction of the Governorate of [Cartagena](63927.md). A [Royal Audiencia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Kingdom_of_Granada) was established in 1548 as part of the [Viceroyalty of Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_Peru), and beginning in 1564, presidents were appointed to govern it. Throughout these years, the territories expanded until they encompassed what is now Colombia.

On March 9, 1687, in the city of [Santafé](211271.md) and its surrounding areas, a loud noise accompanied by a sulfurous odor caused panic among the population. Its origin could not be determined. The expression “*en el tiempo del ruido*” (“in the time of the noise”) subsequently emerged as a way of referring to events from a very distant past. In 1717, the territory was established as a viceroyalty, which was suspended in 1724 and restored in 1739. It continued until the [independence movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_War_of_Independence) of 1810, was restored for the final time in 1816 during the [Spanish reconquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_reconquest_of_New_Granada), and was definitively abolished in 1819 following independence. Cundinamarca was also the site of episodes of the [Comuneros' Revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolt_of_the_Comuneros_\(New_Granada\)) in [La Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Mesa,_Cundinamarca), [Villeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villeta,_Cundinamarca), [Guaduas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaduas), and [Bituima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bituima).

### Independence



The Sovereign State of Cundinamarca

The struggle for independence began in Cundinamarca on July 16, 1813. It was there that the name “Cundinamarca” came to prominence when the centralists, under the leadership of [Antonio Nariño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Nari%C3%B1o), sought to create [a unified and centralized state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_and_Independent_State_of_Cundinamarca) under that name. Meanwhile, the federalists led by [Camilo Torres](866661.md) fought to establish the [United Provinces of New Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_New_Granada). This [period of civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Republic_of_New_Granada) facilitated the [Spanish reconquest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_reconquest_of_New_Granada) in 1816. Along with [Boyacá](185665.md), [Casanare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casanare_Province), [Norte de Santander](14691168.md), and [Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander,_Colombia), Cundinamarca became a center of resistance in New Granada, with the Guerrilla of the Almeyda Brothers and other smaller groups operating in the region. In the [Tocaima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocaima) area, these groups were led by José Antonio Olaya Bolívar and José Ignacio Rodríguez Obregón. Following the [Battle of Boyacá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Boyac%C3%A1), independence was achieved, and the name [Cundinamarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cundinamarca_Department_\(1819\)) was extended to all of [New Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_New_Granada).[^cite_note-9]

In 1831, [New Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_New_Granada) separated from [Gran Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gran_Colombia), and the territory became one of its departments. Due to various political and administrative changes, this territorial entity was divided into several provinces, including Bogotá. Bogotá subsequently became the [Sovereign State of Cundinamarca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cundinamarca_State) (*Estado Soberana de Cundinamarca*) on June 15, 1857, through the union of the New Granadan provinces of [Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogot%C3%A1_Province), [Mariquita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariquita_Province), [Neiva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neiva_Province), [Tequendama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tequendama_Province), [Zipaquirá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipaquir%C3%A1_Province), and the [Territory of San Martín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Mart%C3%ADn_Territory). This state existed during the period of the [Granadine Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granadine_Confederation) and later the [United States of Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_of_Colombia), under the authority of federal constitutions.[^cite_note-\:2-7]

In 1886, following the adoption of a new political constitution, Cundinamarca became a department with the establishment of the new [Republic of Colombia](5222.md).[^cite_note-10] Interestingly, President [Manuel Antonio Sanclemente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Antonio_Sanclemente) governed from [Villeta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villeta,_Cundinamarca) because he could not tolerate the cold climate of the republic's capital.

### 20th and 21st centuries

In 1905, Cundinamarca was divided for the final time into the department of the same name, with its capital in [Facatativá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facatativ%C3%A1); the Department of Quesada, with its capital in [Zipaquirá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipaquir%C3%A1); and the Capital District of Bogotá. These changes resulted from the political reforms of [Rafael Reyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Reyes) and remained in effect until 1910.

During the dictatorship of [Gustavo Rojas Pinilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavo_Rojas_Pinilla), [Bogotá](211271.md) was declared a Special District, definitively separating it from the department along with the then-municipalities of [Bosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosa,_Bogot%C3%A1), [Engativá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engativ%C3%A1), [Fontibón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fontib%C3%B3n), [Suba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suba,_Bogot%C3%A1), [Usaquén](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usaqu%C3%A9n), and [Usme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usme). This separation was confirmed by the [1991 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Constitution_of_1991), which reestablished Bogotá as a Capital District. As a result, alternative proposals have been made to designate various municipalities—including [Zipaquirá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipaquir%C3%A1), [Girardot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girardot,_Cundinamarca), [Chía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%ADa,_Cundinamarca), [Fusagasugá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusagasug%C3%A1), [Facatativá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facatativ%C3%A1), and [Soacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soacha)—as the departmental capital. The proposal to make [Soacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soacha) the capital was rejected by the [Congress of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Colombia) in October 2022.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## Geography

Most of Cundinamarca is on the [Eastern Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Oriental,_Colombia) (*Cordillera Oriental*), just south of [Boyacá](185665.md), bordered by the [Magdalena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_River) on the west, reaching down into the [Orinoco River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoco_River) [basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orinoqu%C3%ADa_Region_of_Colombia) on the east, and bordering on [Tolima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolima_Department) to the south. The capital district of [Bogotá](211271.md) is nearly completely surrounded by Cundinamarca territory and was formed by carving up Cundinamarca. Because of this and other border changes, the present department of Cundinamarca is much smaller than the original state.

#### Important cities

Cundinamarca is made up of 116 municipalities, six of which recorded a population of over 100,000 and could be considered as cities: Soacha, Fusagasugá, Girardot, Facatativá, Zipaquirá and Chia, while Bogotá District is in the category of capital.



Chisaca Lake on the [Sumapaz Paramo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumapaz_Paramo)

- **[Metropolitan Area of Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Area_of_Bogot%C3%A1)**: Comprises the towns of Soacha, Facatativá, Chia, Madrid, Funza, and Mosquera, among others. Its activities are centered in the industrial sector. Estate activity is important especially in the suburbs closer to Bogotá: Chía, La Calera and [Tocancipá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocancip%C3%A1).

- **[Girardot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Girardot,_Cundinamarca)**: In the far southwest and bordering department of [Tolima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tolima_Department), is the capital of the [Upper Magdalena Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_Magdalena_Province). Its main economic activity is trade, as a result of a major tourist dynamics and its proximity to major agricultural areas of Tolima. It has a college and important trade fairs and events.

- **[Fusagasugá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusagasug%C3%A1)** is located between Bogotá and Girardot, and it is an hour of each city. The capital city of the Province of [Sumapaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumapaz), it is an important focus of agricultural marketing and regional services, standing out as an educational city with a large university and an increasing population trend. Its economy is mainly focused on trade and agricultural marketing, with a significant production of ornamental plants and flowers for export, for which it is known as the garden city of Colombia.

- **[Zipaquirá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipaquir%C3%A1)**: Despite being part of the metropolitan area of Bogotá, it has managed to position itself as one of the most important centers of Colombia's [salt mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_mining) industry. Its economy is focused on commerce and services.

Other major towns are Ubaté due to high livestock and dairy production. Guaduas, is an important cultural center. Chocontá and Fred are agricultural centers.
