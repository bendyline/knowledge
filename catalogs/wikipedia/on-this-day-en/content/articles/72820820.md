The **3rd Separate Assault Brigade** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): 3-тя окрема штурмова бригада, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *3-tia okrema shturmova bryhada*) is a brigade of the [Ukrainian Ground Forces](7433327.md) formed in 2023.[^cite_note-4] It is considered one of Ukraine's most elite military units, and is mostly composed of combat veteran volunteers from other Ukrainian units.[^cite_note-\:3-5] In 2025, it formed the foundation for the newly-formed [3rd Army Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Army_Corps_\(Ukraine\)), which the brigade is now a part of.[^cite_note-\:3-5][^cite_note-\:4-6]

The brigade was established by a merger of the Azov SSO ([Special Operations Forces](55773764.md)) units that had been created by former [Azov Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Battalion) veterans and local volunteers in [Kyiv](585629.md), [Sumy](561837.md) and [Kharkiv](2788558.md) in reaction the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-\:1-7][^cite_note-\:2-8] The brigade was commanded by [Andriy Biletsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriy_Biletsky), founder and former commander of the Azov Battalion and former [People's Deputy of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Deputy_of_Ukraine).

The 3rd Brigade gained broader public attention through the [Mstyslav Chernov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mstyslav_Chernov) documentary *[2000 Meters to Andriivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Meters_to_Andriivka)*, which follows its operations during the fighting near [Andriivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriivka,_Bakhmut_Raion,_Donetsk_Oblast) during the [2023 Ukrainian counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Ukrainian_counteroffensive).[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## History

### Background and founding

With the start of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) in 2022, most units of the [Azov Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Regiment) were based in [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol) where they soon [would be besieged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Mariupol). Outside of Mariupol, many former veterans of Azov outside the city began forming new units, in particular in [Kyiv](585629.md), [Sumy](561837.md) and [Kharkiv](2788558.md). These units were initially part of the [Territorial Defense Forces of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_Defense_Forces_\(Ukraine\)) (TDF). Veterans of the Azov Regiment formed the "backbone" of these units.[^cite_note-Вот_Так-11] The Azov TDF units proved themselves to be particularly effective in combat, and thus they were soon turned into regiments and reassigned as part of the [Special Operations Forces of Ukraine](55773764.md) (SSO) of the [Armed Forces of Ukraine](42799055.md), where they received special training and equipment, and became collectively known as "**Azov SSO**".[^cite_note-\:1-7][^cite_note-Ball-2022-12] In addition, at [Dnipro](989795.md), former Azov veterans established the [98th Territorial Defence Battalion 'Azov-Dnipro'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/98th_Territorial_Defence_Battalion_'Azov-Dnipro') of the TDF.[^cite_note-Azov1-13]

In January 2023, all the former Azov SSO units, in addition to the 98th TDF Battalion, were merged into a single fully operational combat unit within the [Ukrainian Ground Forces](7433327.md) of the Armed Forces of Ukraine.[^cite_note-\:0-14][^cite_note-\:2-8] The formation of this brigade was strategically designed to create a highly mobile, well-equipped, and extensively trained force capable of engaging in both defensive and offensive operations.[^cite_note-\:1-7][^cite_note-\:2-8]

### 2023

An important milestone for the brigade occurred on 24 February 2023, when President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) personally presented the Regimental Colour during a ceremonial event.[^cite_note-15] The colors of the brigade were said to symbolize "the tenacity of the traditions of Ukrainian statehood: from princely times \[of [Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus')], the [Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks) period, the [first liberation struggles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence) \[in the early 20th century] to modern times".[^cite_note-16]



3rd Brigade soldiers in the [Battle of Bakhmut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bakhmut)

The brigade's soldiers took part in the [liberation of Kherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Kherson) and parts of the [Kherson Oblast](1273370.md), as well as in the [battle of Bakhmut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bakhmut).[^cite_note-17] On 12 April 2023, the brigade's official media resources announced the start of recruitment efforts.[^cite_note-18] In May 2023, it was one of the first to report successful offensives in the Bakhmut district against the Russian Ground Forces.[^cite_note-19]

On 15 September 2023, after [months of fighting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Ukrainian_counteroffensive), the brigade liberated [Andriivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriivka,_Bakhmut_Raion,_Donetsk_Oblast),[^cite_note-DW_2023-09-15-20] and held onto it despite a Russian counterattack on 17 September.[^cite_note-Institute_for_the_Study_of_War-21] Ukrainian ground forces commander General Syrskyi called Klishchiivka and Andriivka "important elements of the Russian Bakhmut-Horlivka defensive line", and ISW assessed that the capture of these settlements defending a key Russian [ground line of communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground_line_of_communication) will make it difficult for the already weary defending forces to replenish and continue defending.[^cite_note-Institute_for_the_Study_of_War-21]

### 2024



[Australian](4689264.md) [M113AS4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M113AS4) in service of the 3rd Separate Assault Brigade

In February 2024, shortly after the dismissal of [Zaluzhnyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaluzhnyi), the brigade was transferred into [Avdiivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avdiivka) to relieve the potential encirclement of the city.[^cite_note-22] After the fall of Avdiivka on February 17, the brigade took new defensive lines west of Avdiivka around [Lastochkyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lastochkyne).[^cite_note-23] On 17 February, the brigade's press officer, Oleksandr Borodin, claimed that during its relocation to Avdiivka, his unit had killed 1,500 Russians, wounded over 3,500, and destroyed 20 armoured vehicles. He also said that the brigade has "no reports" of large numbers of their soldiers being taken prisoner.[^cite_note-24]

On 27 May 2024, the brigade claimed to have killed, wounded or taken prisoner "close to" 600 Russian troops in [Kharkiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Kharkiv_offensive). One Russian soldier captured by the brigade claimed in an interview that he was the only survivor of his 15-man section following an attack.[^cite_note-25] On 19 June, the brigade claimed that their 1st Mechanised Battalion had killed 250 Russians in Kharkiv Oblast, publishing a video that purportedly proved their claims.[^cite_note-26]

On 22 August 2024, the brigade claimed to have taken 2 square kilometres (0.77 mi<sup>2</sup>) in a counterattack against Russia's [20th Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Combined_Arms_Army) in Kharkiv Oblast. Despite being outnumbered 2.5 to 1, they claimed to have defeated a battalion, including "platoon and company strong points", killing 300 personnel in three days and diverting Russian forces away from [Makiivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Makiivka,_Luhansk_Oblast).[^cite_note-27]

In November 2024, it was reported that certain units of the brigade's 2nd Assault Battalion were operating in the Kharkiv Oblast.[^cite_note-28]

### 2025

Plans to transform this elite formation to become the [III Army Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Army_Corps_\(Ukraine\)) began in March 2025, with Andriy Biletskyi expected to retain his billet of commanding officer upon the conclusion of the reorganization process.[^cite_note-\:4-6]

In late March 2025, it was reported that units of the brigade, had recaptured the village of [Nadiia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadiia,_Luhansk_Oblast) in the [Luhansk Oblast](494904.md). Biletskyi claimed that this resulted in the destruction of two regiments of Russia's [20th Combined Arms Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/20th_Combined_Arms_Army): the [752nd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/752nd_Guards_Motor_Rifle_Regiment) and [254th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/144th_Guards_Motor_Rifle_Division) Motor Rifle Regiments, as well as the recapture of 3 square kilometres (1.2 mi<sup>2</sup>) of territory.[^cite_note-23march-29] The operation to recapture Nadiia was mainly conducted by the brigade's 1st Assault Battalion,[^cite_note-23march-29][^cite_note-30] though other units were involved, including the brigade's 2nd Mechanized Battalion[^cite_note-31] and its tank battalion.[^cite_note-32] The next month, combat continued near the village of Nadiia; the brigade's 1st Assault Battalion, 1st Mechanized Battalion, and 2nd Mechanized Battalion were reportedly involved.[^cite_note-33] Around the same time, units of the 3rd Brigade recaptured the village of [Nevske](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balka_Zhuravka), according to deputy commander [Rodion Kudriashov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodion_Kudriashov?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-34]

In a late March 2025 interview, Biletskyi said that the brigade was defending the town of [Borova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borova,_Izium_Raion,_Kharkiv_Oblast) in the [Kharkiv Oblast](494901.md), which he said was a Russian target. The 3rd Assault Brigade was opposed by units of the [1st Guards Tank Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Guards_Tank_Army) and the 20th Combined Arms Army.[^cite_note-35]

In April and May 2025, it was reported that the 3rd Assault Brigade was holding over 50 kilometres (31 mi) of the front line on the [Kupiansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kupiansk_front) and [Lyman fronts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyman_front?action=edit\&redlink=1), which was reportedly the longest portion of the front held by any Ukrainian brigade across the entire country, and that it had done so for nearly a year.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38] Ukrainian military journalist [Yurii Butusov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yurii_Butusov?action=edit\&redlink=1) and brigade deputy commander [Dmytro Kukharchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dmytro_Kukharchuk) both said the brigade was holding a 60-kilometre (37 mi) front in June and July 2025, respectively.[^cite_note-butusovjuly-39][^cite_note-40] Butusov said this represented 5% of the entire active front line in Ukraine.[^cite_note-butusovjuly-39]

During an undated operation, the 3rd Assault Brigade claimed the world's first surrender of enemy soldiers to unmanned drones in the Kharkiv Oblast. Fpv and ground drones were used to attack the Russian positions. As more ground drones approached the position, the Russians opted to surrender. The Ukrainian ground drones then guided the Russians soldiers to the Ukrainian position where they surrendered. The press release read: "For the first time in history: Russian soldiers surrendered to the 3rd Assault Brigade's ground drones,".[^cite_note-41]

On 22 December 2025, the 3rd Assault Brigade said that a Ukrainian [UGV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UGV), equipped with a [.50 cal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.50_BMG) machine gun, held off Russian attacks for 45 days while being operated either remotely or using AI. The first time a position was held solely by an UGV without direct human support.[^cite_note-42]

## Controversies



A backpack with three patches: [Azov SSO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_SSO), the ["National Idea" symbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Idea_\(symbol\)) (a *[Wolfsangel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolfsangel)* variation) and the 3rd Assault Brigade

The 3rd Assault Brigade has attracted controversy over its origins in the [Azov Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Battalion), and its continued association with the [Azov Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Movement), particularly because of the presence of personnel accused of holding [far-right](221220.md) or [neo-Nazi](54361.md) views and the use of symbols associated with the far right.

According to the independent French investigative online newspaper *[Mediapart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediapart)*, the French army trained soldiers from the 3rd Assault Brigade at the La Courtine camp, in the [Creuse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creuse_\(d%C3%A9partement\)) in 2023. Several of them displayed [neo-Nazi](54361.md) symbols and on their social network profiles, including "[Hitlerian salutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_salute), [Celtic crosses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_cross), emblems of several [SS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schutzstaffel) divisions, [skulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totenkopf), pictures of [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md)". Mediapart asked the [French Ministry of the Armed Forces](37707412.md) to comment on the matter. The response was "It is the Ukrainian armed forces that organise the flow and selection of Ukrainian soldiers sent to France and Europe. We therefore have no comment to make on this organisation."[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] The Politician [Catherine Couturier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Couturier) protested against the Ministry of the Armed Forces.[^cite_note-lejdd-46]

In 2022, the Canadian Army was also criticised for training soldiers of the 3rd Assault Brigade.[^cite_note-lejdd-46]

In July 2024, the brigade organised a tour through Europe to recruit volunteers and promote their image. After protests from left-wing groups, especially in Germany, events in Berlin, Hamburg, Rotterdam, Cologne and Brussels were cancelled.[^cite_note-berliner-zeitung-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

The NGO [Promote Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promote_Ukraine?action=edit\&redlink=1) responded to this kind of criticism: "Among the soldiers of the brigade are representatives of different nations—Georgians, Americans, Jews, Armenians, Tatars, Belarusians, Swedes, etc.—who are fighting side by side with Ukrainians to stop the genocide started by the Russian Federation".[^cite_note-51] Indeed, there are reports on Jews serving within the 3rd Assault Brigade, including Hungarian volunteer Benjamin Aser who got killed in the Kharkiv region in May 2025.[^cite_note-52] Platoon commander Igor Tish who also served in the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) stated in an interview with Ukrainian Jewish Encounter: "The Third Assault Brigade, which separated from Azov, has a very respectful attitude to Jews and other ethnic minorities; they have punishment for saying bad words about nationalities".[^cite_note-53]
