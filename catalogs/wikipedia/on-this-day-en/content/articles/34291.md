# Yacht

For other uses, see [Yacht (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yacht_\(disambiguation\)).



A 45-foot cruising yacht in 2010



The [superyacht](3652673.md) *[Azzam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azzam_\(2013_yacht\))*, the longest private yacht, as of 2018.

[^cite_note-boat-1]

A **yacht** ([/jɒt/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is a [sail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sail)- or [motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_propulsion)-propelled [watercraft](301664.md) made for pleasure, cruising, or racing.[^cite_note-definition-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] There is no standard definition, though the term generally applies to vessels with a cabin intended for overnight use.

The Commercial Yacht Code classifies yachts 79 ft (24 m) and over as *large*.[^cite_note-law-5] Such yachts typically require a hired crew[^cite_note-boat-1] and have higher construction standards.[^cite_note-law-5] Further classifications for large yachts are *commercial*: carrying no more than 12 passengers; *private*: solely for the pleasure of the owner and guests, or by [flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag#At_sea), the country under which it is registered.[^cite_note-classification-6] A [superyacht](3652673.md) (sometimes *megayacht*) generally refers to any yacht (sail or power) longer than 131 ft (40 m).[^cite_note-stewardess-7]

Racing yachts are designed to emphasize performance over comfort.[^cite_note-8] Charter yachts are run as a business for profit.[^cite_note-stewardess-7] As of 2020, there were more than 15,000 yachts of sufficient size to require a professional crew.[^cite_note-stewardess-7]

## Etymology



An 18th-century Dutch *jacht*

The term, *yacht*, originates from the [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_language) word *jacht* (pl. *jachten*), which means "hunt", and originally referred to light, fast sailing vessels used by the [Dutch Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_Republic) navy and the [Dutch East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_East_India_Company). Typically, a *jacht* would scout ahead of larger vessels in a fleet.[^cite_note-definition-2][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]

## History

The history of pleasure boats begins with rowed craft in Pharaonic Egyptian times, and other vessels in the waters of Myanmar, India, [Mindanao](20546.md) and Japan. Anglo-Saxon royal *pleg-scips* (play ships) of the 8th-century featured ornamented bows and sterns and had the capability of cooking on board.[^cite_note-11]

### Sail



1893 [America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Cup) match between *[Vigilant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vigilant_\(yacht\))* and *[Valkyrie II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valkyrie_II)*

The history of sailing yachts begins in Europe in the beginning of the 1600s with the building of a pleasure vessel for the son of [King James I of England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_VI_and_I). While other monarchs used naval ships for transportation and conquest, James I was the first English monarch to commission the construction of a yacht—for his son [Henry, Prince of Wales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Frederick,_Prince_of_Wales) in the early 1600s.[^cite_note-12] Pleasure vessels acquired the name *yacht* after the time of [Charles II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_II_of_England), who spent time exiled in Europe and visited the Netherlands, where a variety of *jachten* were already well developed as pleasure boats for the elite classes since the beginning of the 17th century. Upon his restoration to the English crown, Charles was presented with [a yacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMY_Mary) and later commissioned a series of royal yachts, which included at least one experimental [catamaran](195952.md). The first recorded [yacht race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yacht_racing) between two vessels occurred in 1661, followed by the first open sailing competition in 1663 in English waters.[^cite_note-golden-13]

Starting in 1739, England found itself in a series of wars—a period that saw a decline in yachting. In Ireland, however, the gentry enjoyed yachting and founded the first yacht club in Cork as the [Cork Harbour Water Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Cork_Yacht_Club) in 1720. English yacht racing continued among the English gentry who founded England's oldest yacht club, the [Cumberland Fleet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland_Fleet), in 1775. With maritime peace, starting in 1815, came a resurgence of interest in yachting. Boatbuilders, who had been making fast vessels both for smugglers and the government [revenue cutters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutter_\(boat\)), turned their skills again to yachts.[^cite_note-golden-13]

The fast yachts of the early 19th century were [fore-and-aft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fore-and-aft_rig) [luggers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lugger), [schooners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schooner), and [sloops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloop). By the 1850s, yachts featured large sail areas, a narrow beam, and a deeper draft than was customary until then. Racing between yachts owned by wealthy patrons was common, with large wagers at stake. The [America's Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America's_Cup) arose out of a contest between the yacht, *[America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/America_\(yacht\))*, and its English competitors. Both countries had rules by which to rate yachts, the English by tonnage and the American by length.[^cite_note-golden-13]

In the late 19th century, yacht owners would base their choice of vessel upon preferred lifestyle and budget, which would determine the size and type of vessel, which would most likely be a fore-and aft, two-masted sailing vessel. A treatise on the subject, *A Manual of Yacht and Boat Sailing*, provided detailed information on selecting, equipping, sailing, seamanship, management of the paid crew, and racing such vessels. It included a brief section on steam yachts, the recirculating coil steam engine just having made such yachts efficient enough for leisure travel on the water.[^cite_note-14]

### Power



Steam yacht, *[Gunilda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunilda)*, c. 1910



Turkish state yacht, *[Savarona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savarona)* in 2014, a steam-turbine yacht re-engined with diesels

While sailing yachts continued to exist, the development of reliable power plants created a new category of pleasure craft. The power plants started with the [steam engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine) and transitioned to the [internal combustion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_combustion) engine. Whereas sailing yachts continued to be steered from the after portion of the vessel, power yachts adopted the [bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_\(nautical\)) in a forward cabin structure that afforded better forward and sideways visibility.[^cite_note-golden-13]

#### Steam

The history of steam yachts starts with large sailing yachts with a [steam auxiliary engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_engine). Early examples, driven with paddle wheels, had a railed platform from which the person conning the vessel could walk across the vessel above the main deck, the origin of the [bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridge_\(nautical\)). In the late 18th century, steam engines became more efficient, spars were removed and [screw propellers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propeller) became standard.[^cite_note-steam-15] Steam yachts evolved with the [development of the steam engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_steam_engine). Ultimately, engines employed pistons driven by steam within cylinders, connected to a crank shaft, which drove a propeller.[^cite_note-steam-15]

Near the end of the 19th century, [compound engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compound_engine) came into widespread use.[^cite_note-steam-15] Compound engines exhausted steam into successively larger cylinders to accommodate the higher volumes at reduced pressures, giving improved efficiency. These stages were called expansions, with double- and triple-expansion engines being common, especially in shipping where efficiency was important to reduce the weight of coal carried.[^cite_note-Hunter-16] Steam engines remained the dominant source of power until the early 20th century, when advances in the design of the [steam turbine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_turbine), [electric motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_motor) and [internal combustion engines](41228673.md) gradually resulted in the replacement of reciprocating (piston) steam engines.[^cite_note-Hunter-16][^cite_note-Wiser-17]

Large steam yachts were luxurious; their staff included a [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain), [engineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engine_officer), and stewards, as well as deck hands.[^cite_note-golden-13]

#### Internal combustion

[Nicolaus Otto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Otto) and [Gottlieb Daimler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottlieb_Daimler) developed practical [four-stroke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four-stroke_engine) gasoline engines, starting in 1876. Beginning in 1898 engines increased in horsepower from 25 horsepower (19 kW) to 500 brake horsepower (370 kW) by 1906. Some were destined for [speedboats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorboat), other for motor yachts.[^cite_note-rudder-18] [Diesel power plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine) for boats were demonstrated in 1903.[^cite_note-19] Diesels became a more prevalent type of power plant in the 20th century thanks to their low cost of operation and reliability.[^cite_note-rudder-18]

## Classification



*Maltese Falcon* sailing yacht in 2008

The [Recreational Craft Directive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recreational_Craft_Directive) requires that all vessels sold in the [European Union](9317.md) and United Kingdom satisfy one of four design categories, based on the [wind force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaufort_scale) and seas that they are designed to encounter:[^cite_note-20]

- *A* yachts are fit for conditions that exceed wind force 8—40 knots (21 m/s)—and 13-foot (4 m) maximum wave heights, encountered in ocean passages and extended voyages.

- *B* yachts are fit for conditions that are less than wind force 8—40 knots (21 m/s)—and 13-foot (4 m) maximum wave heights, encountered in ocean passages and extended voyages.

- *C* yachts are fit for wind force 6—27 knots (14 m/s)—and 6.6-foot (2 m) maximum wave heights, encountered in exposed coastal waters, bays inlets, lakes and rivers.

- *D* yachts are fit for wind force 4—16 knots (8.2 m/s)—and 1.6-foot (0.5 m) maximum wave heights, encountered in sheltered coastal waters, bays inlets, lakes and rivers.

The *Large Commercial Yacht Code (LY2)* of Great Britain and its dominions defines a *large yacht* as one that is 24 metres (79 ft) or more at the waterline and is in commercial use for sport or pleasure, while not carrying cargo or more than 12 passengers and carrying a professional crew. The code regulates the equipping of such vessels, both at sea and in port—including such matters as crew duty times and the presence of a helicopter on board. The code has different levels of standard for vessels above and below 500 [gross tons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_tonnage).[^cite_note-21] Such yachts may be considered *superyachts* and are more commonly at 40 metres (130 ft) or more in length.[^cite_note-22] Other countries have standards similar to LY2.[^cite_note-classification-6]

Whereas *commercial* large yachts may carry no more than 12 passengers, *private* yachts are solely for the pleasure of the owner and guests do not carry the passenger restriction. Yachts may be identified by flag—the country under which a yacht is registered.[^cite_note-classification-6] An industry publication categorizes superyachts by size,[^cite_note-23] by speed,[^cite_note-24] as "explorer" yachts,[^cite_note-explorer-25] as sailing yachts,[^cite_note-26] and as classic yachts.[^cite_note-27]

## Construction

Originally, all yachts were made of wood, using a wooden keel and ribs, clad with planks. These materials were supplanted with iron or steel in steam yachts.[^cite_note-golden-13] In the 1960s [fiberglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiberglass) became a prevalent material. These materials and others continue in use.[^cite_note-offshore-28] Whereas yachts of 79 feet (24 m) and below may be constructed of [fiberglass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiberglass), larger yachts are more likely to be constructed of steel, aluminum or composite [fiber-reinforced plastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibre-reinforced_plastic).[^cite_note-29]

- *Wood* construction, using conventional planks over ribs continues. [Hard-chined](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chine_\(boating\)) boats made with plywood is an infrequent technique, whereas yachts made with the WEST system—plies of wood strips, soaked in epoxy and applied over the boat frame—provide a durable, lightweight and robust hull.

- *Metal* hulls from steel or aluminum offer the opportunity for welding components to a completely watertight hull. Both metals are vulnerable to damage due to [electrolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrolysis). Steel is easy to repair in boatyards around the world, whereas aluminum is a much lighter material.

- *Fiberglass* construction is best suited for [mass-produced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_production) yachts, using a mold and is therefore the most prevalent material. Fiberglass skins comprise plies of roving (glass fabric) and matting, soaked in resin for the hull. [Decks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deck_\(ship\)) typically have a core of balsa, or [PVC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyvinyl_chloride) foam between layers of glass mat. Both elements of construction are vulnerable to intrusion of water and the development of blisters below the waterline.

## Transport



M/V *Yacht Servant*, with a cargo of yachts, is a purpose-built semi-submersible vessel.

As an alternative to the traditional passaging (sailing or motoring), yachts can also be shipped, usually when the destination and [cruising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruising_\(maritime\)) is more important than the passaging, as it generally eliminates costly and time-consuming ocean crossings.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

[Trailers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailer_\(vehicle\)), whether private or commercial, are often used to transport small yachts for short distances. As the yacht size and the over-land distances increase, yacht owners typically employ commercial trailer services to move vessels.[^cite_note-33]



Semi-submersible ship DYT Yacht Express at Port Everglades, Fort Lauderdale, Florida, USA.

Yacht owners sometimes employ [semi-submersible ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy-lift_ship) to relocate their craft to distant cruising grounds. Such ships submerge sufficiently to allow a yacht to be floated on or off a pre-prepared cradle, which shall be certified for marine transport by one of the IACS member [^cite_note-IACSMembers-34], and then re-float, lifting the yacht out of the water for transport. These ships travel among North American, Caribbean, and Pacific-Ocean destinations.[^cite_note-35]



M/V *Statengracht*, with a cargo of yachts in the port of West Palm Beach, Florida.

Yachts can be shipped as deck and/or underdeck cargo mostly on [Heavy-lift ships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy-lift_ship) or [Multi-purpose vessels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-purpose_vessel) and that is mostly limited to those equipped with their own cranes, which makes a transport less costly comparing to options requiring usage of shore or floating cranes but opens up geographical that have no shore or floating cranes available, and allows the loading and discharging at the protected anchorages without using costly port infrastructure.[^cite_note-36]

Yachts are also transported by [container ships](262084.md), arranged through [freight forwarders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freight_forwarder). Container shipment costs are almost independent of distance shipped and depend more on imbalances in container locations. It is significantly less expensive and has greater flexibility with respect to timing and destinations. The drawback to container cruising is that there are a limited number of yachts that have the necessary sizes that fit in a standard container.

## Accommodations



Sailing yacht interior with fold-down table in main salon, galley (kitchen) on left, and navigation station on right and forward cabin visible beyond.

Depending on size, a cruising yacht is likely to have at least two [cabins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_\(ship\)), a main salon and a forward [stateroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_room). In smaller yachts, the salon is likely to have convertible [berths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berth_\(sleeping\)) for its crew or passengers. Typically the salon includes a dining area, which may have a folding, built-in table. The salon is typically contiguous to the [galley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galley_\(kitchen\)). A cruising yacht is likely to have a [head](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_\(watercraft\)) (bathroom) with a marine toilet that discharges waste into a holding tank. Larger yachts may have additional staterooms and heads. There is typically a navigation station that allows planning the route.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-manual-38]

## Systems



Wheelhouse of motor yacht, *[Taransay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taransay_\(yacht\))*, in 2015 with navigation and systems displays

Onboard systems include:[^cite_note-manual-38]

- Electrical power, provided by batteries recharged by a motor-driven alternator (sail) or by a generator set (motor)

- Water, stored in on-board tanks, refilled on shore or replenished with a [desalination water maker](156787.md)

- Sanitation, provided by toilets using seawater and discharged into holding tanks.

- Refrigeration by ice or an engine-driven mechanical system.

Modern yachts employ a suite of electronics for communication, measurement of surroundings, and navigation.[^cite_note-39]

- *Communications* equipment includes radios in a variety of bandwidths, specifically for maritime use.

- *Instrumentation* also provides information on depth of water under the vessel ([depth sounder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Echo_sounding)), windspeed ([anemometer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemometer)), and directional orientation ([compass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compass)).

- *Navigation* electronics include units that identify a vessel's location (e.g. [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System)) and display the vessel's location ([chartplotter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chartplotter)) and other vessels and nearby shore ([radar](25676.md)).

## Sailing yachts



Small sailing yacht with [outboard motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outboard_motor) in 2017

[Sailing yachts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailing_yacht) for *cruising* versus *racing* embody different tradeoffs between comfort and performance. Cruising yachts emphasize comfort over performance. Racing yachts are designed to compete against others in their class, while providing adequate comfort to their crews.

### Cruising

Cruising yachts may be designed for near-shore use or for passage-making. They may also be raced, but they are designed and built with the comfort and amenities necessary for overnight voyages. Qualities considered in cruising yachts include: performance, comfort under way, ease of handling, stability, living comfort, durability, ease of maintenance, affordability of ownership.[^cite_note-RYA-40]

#### Categories

Cruising sailboats share the common attribute of providing overnight accommodations. They may be classified as *small* (easy to haul behind a trailer),[^cite_note-small-41] *near-shore*[^cite_note-definition-2] and *off-shore*.[^cite_note-42] [Multihull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multihull) sailing yachts are a category, apart.[^cite_note-cruisingworld-43]

- *Small yachts* are typically shorter than 33 feet (10 m) length overall.

  [^cite_note-cruisingworld-43]

  &#x20;[Trailer sailers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trailer_sailer) that are readily towed by a car are generally shorter than 25 feet (7.6 m) length overall and weigh less than 5,000 pounds (2,300 kg).

  [^cite_note-small-41]

- *Near-shore yachts* typically range in size from 33–45 feet (10–14 m) length overall.

  [^cite_note-law-5]

- *Offshore yachts* typically exceed 45 feet (14 m) length overall.

  [^cite_note-cruisingworld-43]

#### Design



Cruising *catamaran* in 2012



Cruising *trimaran* with folding [amas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ama_\(sailing\)) in 2005

Design considerations for a cruising yacht include seaworthiness, performance, sea kindliness, and cost of construction, as follows:[^cite_note-RYA-40]

- *Seaworthiness* addresses the integrity of the vessel and its ability to stay afloat and shelter its crew in the conditions encountered.

- *Performance* hinges on a number of factors, including the waterline length (longer means faster), drag in the water (narrower hull with smooth appendages), hull shape, and sail shape and area.

- *Sea kindliness* is an indicator of steering ease, directional stability and quelling of motion induced by wind and waves.

Multihulls offer tradeoffs as cruising sailboats, compared with [monohulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monohull). They may be catamarans or trimarans. They rely on form stability—having separate hulls far apart—for their resistance to capsize.[^cite_note-RYA-40] Their advantages include greater: stability, speed, (for catamarans) living space, and shallower draft. Their drawbacks include: greater expenses, greater [windage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windage), more difficult tacking under sail, less load capacity, and more maneuvering room required because of their broad beam. They come with a variety of sleeping accommodations and (for catamarans) bridge-deck configurations.[^cite_note-44]

#### Rigs

[Gaff rigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaff_rig) have been uncommon in the construction of cruising boats, since the mid 20th century. More common rigs are [Bermuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bermuda_rig), [fractional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_rig), [cutter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cutter_\(boat\)), and [ketch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ketch). Occasionally employed rigs since then have been the [yawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yawl), schooner, [wishbone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wishbone_rig), [catboat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catboat).[^cite_note-offshore-28]

#### Gear

Sailboats employ [standing rigging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standing_rigging) to support the rig, [running rigging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_rigging) to raise and adjust sails, cleats to secure lines, winches to work the sheets, and more than one anchor to secure the boat in harbor. A cruising yacht's deck usually has safety line to protect the crew from falling overboard and a bow pulpit to facilitate handling the jib and the anchor. In temperate climates, the cockpit may have a canvas windshield with see-through panels, called a "dodger". Steering may be either by tiller or wheel.[^cite_note-offshore-28]

#### Engine

Cruising yachts have an auxiliary propulsion power unit to supplement the use of sails. Such [power is inboard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_propulsion) on the vessel and diesel, except for the smallest cruising boats, which may have an [outboard gasoline motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outboard_motor). A 31-foot (9.4 m) sailboat might have a 13-horsepower (9.7 kW) engine,[^cite_note-45] whereas a 55-foot (17 m) sailboat might have a 110-horsepower (82 kW) engine.[^cite_note-46]

### Racing



Racing yacht, *Comanche*, beginning a 2015 transatlantic race

Racing yachts emphasize performance over comfort. [World Sailing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Sailing) recognizes eleven classes of racing yacht.[^cite_note-Chogath_Kog'sante-47]

#### Design features

High-performance rigs provide aerodynamic efficiency and [hydrodynamically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrodynamical) efficient hulls minimize drag through the water and sideways motion.

Racing yachts have a wide selection of weights and shapes of sail to accommodate different wind strengths and points of sail. A suite of sails on a racing yachts would include several weights of [jib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jib) and spinnaker, plus a specialized storm jib and [trysail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trysail) (in place of the [mainsail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainsail)). Performance yachts are likely to have full-battened kevlar or carbon-fiber mainsails.[^cite_note-manual-38]

Underwater foils can become more specialized, starting with a higher-aspect ratio fin keel with hydrodynamically efficient bulbs for ballast.[^cite_note-48] On some racing yachts, a [canting keel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canting_keel) shifts angle from side to side to promote sailing with less heeling angle (sideway tilt), while other underwater foils take care of [leeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeway) (sideways motion).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-manual-38]

*An example for a [full-rigged ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-rigged_ship) yacht which is also one of the world's most complex and largest sailing yachts at 88 m (289 ft) is the [Maltese Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_Falcon_\(yacht\)).*

## Motor yachts



Lazzara 80-foot (24 m) planing-hull, *sports-cruiser* motor yacht in 2014



*Nordhavn* 47-foot (14 m) displacement-hull, *expedition-style* motor yacht in 2005



Grand Banks 42-foot (13 m) displacement-hull, *trawler-style* motor yacht in 2018

Motor yachts range in length from 33–130 feet (10–40 m) before they are considered [superyachts](3652673.md), which are 130 feet (40 m) and longer. They also vary by use, by style, and by hull type. As of April 2020 a 600-foot (180 m) yacht, *REV Ocean*, was under construction,[^cite_note-50] which when launched would replace the 590-foot (180 m) *[Azzam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azzam_\(2013_yacht\))* as the longest superyacht.[^cite_note-boat-1]

### Classification

The [United States Coast Guard](32223.md) classifies motorboats—any vessel less than 65 feet (20 m), propelled by machinery—in four classes by length:[^cite_note-51]

- Class A: motorboats less than 16 feet (4.9 m)

- Class 1: motorboats 16–26 feet (4.9–7.9 m)

- Class 2: motorboats 26–40 feet (7.9–12.2 m)

- Class 3: motorboats 40–65 feet (12–20 m)

### Style

A motor yacht's style can both be functional and evoke an aesthetic—trending towards the modern or the traditional. Among the styles, mentioned in the literature, are:[^cite_note-powerboat-52]

- *Cruiser* – A [cruiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabin_cruiser) has a displacement hull for economical, long-distance passage-making.

- *Sports cruiser* – A sports cruiser has a semi-displacement or [planing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planing_\(boat\)) hull for fast trips.

- *Sports fisherman* – A sports fisherman has a semi-displacement or planing hull for fast trips and carries gear for recreational catching of large fish.

- *Expedition* – An expedition yacht has a displacement hull for economical, long-distance passage-making to remote destinations. These are also called *explorer* yachts.

  [^cite_note-explorer-25]

- *Lobster* – A lobster yacht is styled like a Maine lobster boat and has a semi-displacement or planing hull for fast trips.

- *Trawler* – A trawler has conservative, traditional styling and has a displacement hull for economical, medium-distance passage-making.

### Hulls

There are three basic types of motor yacht hull: *full-displacement*, *semi-displacement*, and *planing*, which have progressively higher cruise speeds and hourly fuel consumption with increased engine power:[^cite_note-powerandmotoryacht.com-53]

- *Full-displacement hulls* move the water up and out of the way of the vessel, making a wave. They are limited in speed by the square root of the waterline length multiplied by a factor, depending on the units used. Added horsepower cannot increase the maximum speed, only the size of the waves produced.

- *Semi-displacement hulls* allow speeds that are faster than the [hull speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hull_speed) of a displacement vessel because they rise somewhat out of the water and create smaller waves. They also provide greater comfort than planing hulls.

- *Planing hulls* require sufficient power for the boat to [plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planing_\(boat\)) on the surface, which avoids the need to use power to lift water out of the way of the vessel. Such vessels have flat surfaces on the undersides.

A typical semi-displacement yacht has a wedge-shaped bow, which promotes penetrating waves, that transitions to flatter, wider surfaces aft, which promotes lifting the vessel out of the water—the "deep vee" hull, designed by Ray Hunt, found in approximately 75% of modern power boats.[^cite_note-54]

Cruising motor yachts are available in a range of styles as two-engine *catamarans*, ranging in length from 40–150 feet (12–46 m) with top speeds ranging from 20–60 knots (37–111 km/h).[^cite_note-55]

### Engines



260-horsepower (190 kW) diesel [marine engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inboard_motor)

Motor yachts typically have one or more [diesel engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_engine). Gasoline-powered motors and engines are the provenance of [outboard motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outboard_motor) and [racing boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boat_racing), due to their [power-to-weight ratios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Power-to-weight_ratio).[^cite_note-powerboat-52] Two engines add expense, but provide reliability and maneuverability over a single engine.[^cite_note-56]

Motor yachts in the 42-foot (13 m) range might have the following hull, horsepower, cruise speed, and hourly fuel consumption characteristics:

- *Full-displacement hull* – two 135-horsepower (101 kW) diesels to cruise at 9 knots (17 km/h), consuming 5 US gallons per hour (19 L/h).

  [^cite_note-grandbanks42-57]

- *Semi-displacement hull* – two 350-horsepower (260 kW) diesels to cruise at 17 knots (31 km/h), consuming 12 US gallons per hour (45 L/h).

  [^cite_note-58]

  [^cite_note-59]

- *Planing hull* – two 400-horsepower (300 kW) diesels to cruise at 30 knots (56 km/h), consuming 24 US gallons per hour (90 L/h).

  [^cite_note-60]

  [^cite_note-grandbanks42-57]

Superyachts may employ multiple 9,000-horsepower (6,700 kW) diesels[^cite_note-61] or a combination of diesels and [gas turbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_turbine) with a combined 47,000 horsepower (35,000 kW).[^cite_note-SYTimes-NautaComments-62]
