The **Amazon Theatre** ([Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_language): *Teatro Amazonas*) is an [opera house](155040.md) located in [Manaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manaus), [Brazil](3383.md), in the heart of the [Amazon rainforest](48139.md). It is the location of the annual *[Festival Amazonas de Ópera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Festival_Amazonas_de_%C3%93pera)* (Amazonas Opera Festival) and the home of the [Amazonas Philharmonic Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazonas_Philharmonic) which regularly rehearses and performs at the Amazon Theatre along with choirs, musical [concerts](236918.md) and other performances.

More than 126 years old, it represents the city's heyday during the [rubber boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_rubber_boom).[^cite_note-2] It was chosen by the magazine *[Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_\(magazine\))* as one of the most beautiful [opera houses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opera_houses) in the world.[^cite_note-3] In July 2026, it was designated by [UNESCO](21786641.md) as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md) along with [Teatro da Paz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_da_Paz).[^cite_note-4]

## History

The Amazonas Theatre was built during the [Belle Époque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belle_%C3%89poque) at a time when fortunes were made in the [rubber boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubber_boom). Construction of the Amazon Theatre was first proposed in 1881 by a member of the local House of Representatives, Antonio Jose Fernandes Júnior, who envisioned a "jewel" in the heart of the Amazon rainforest.[^cite_note-5]

In 1882, the State legislature approved some limited financing, but this was considered insufficient. Later that year, the president of the Province, [José Lustosa Paranaguá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Lustosa_Paranagu%C3%A1?action=edit\&redlink=1), approved a larger budget and initiated a competition for the presentation of plans. The chosen project was made by the *Gabinete Português de Engenharia e Arquitectura*, an engineering and architecture office from [Lisbon](18091.md). By 1884, construction was ready to begin under the Italian architect [Celestial Sacardim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_Sacardim?action=edit\&redlink=1). Work proceeded slowly over the following fifteen years with some stops and restarts from 1885 to 1892.

By 1895, when the masonry work and exterior were completed, the decoration of the interior and the installation of electric lighting could begin more rapidly. The theatre was inaugurated on December 31, 1896, with the first performance occurring on January 7, 1897, with the [Italian opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_opera), *[La Gioconda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Gioconda_\(opera\))*, by [Amilcare Ponchielli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amilcare_Ponchielli).

## Architecture and style



Amazon Theatre and its surrounding area in 2017

The theatre's architectural style is considered typically [Renaissance Revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Renaissance_Revival_architecture). The roofing tiles were imported from [Alsace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alsace), the steel walls from [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow), Scotland and the [Carrara marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrara_marble) for the stairs, statues and columns, from Italy. The [dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome) is covered with 36,000 decorated [ceramic tiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ceramic_tile) painted in the colors of the national flag of Brazil. The interior furnishing came from France in the [Louis Quinze style](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Quinze). Italian artist Domenico de Angelis the Younger painted the panels that decorate the ceilings of the auditorium and of the audience chamber. The curtain, with its painting "Meeting of the Waters", was originally created in Paris by [Crispim do Amaral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crispim_do_Amaral). The theatre's 198 [chandeliers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandelier) were imported from Italy, including 32 of [Murano glass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murano_glass).

## Internal structure

The Auditorium seats 701 persons. The ground floor (stalls, in British usage) seats 266; the stall boxes, 100; the first-tier boxes seat 110; the 25 second-tier boxes seat 125; and the 20 third-tier boxes seat 100. The Main Stage is 10.50 m (34.4 ft) wide, 6.40 m (21.0 ft) high and 11.97 m (39.3 ft) deep and the principal stage is 14 m (46 ft) high for a total area of 123.29 m<sup>2</sup> (1,327.1 sq ft). The orchestra pit has a height of 2 m (6 ft 7 in); width: 6 ft 11 ins; and a length of 7 m (23 ft).

## In popular culture

- The theatre is featured in the film *[Fitzcarraldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitzcarraldo)*

  [^cite_note-6]

  &#x20;directed by the German director [Werner Herzog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Werner_Herzog) in 1982. At the beginning of the film, the opera-obsessed character Brian Sweeney "Fitzcarraldo" Fitzgerald makes his way by paddling his own boat to the opera house to hear [Enrico Caruso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enrico_Caruso) sing in [Verdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giuseppe_Verdi)'s *[Ernani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernani)*,

  [^cite_note-7]

  &#x20;which also features aging [Sarah Bernhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_Bernhardt) whose role is sung by an off-stage soprano.

- It is featured twice in novels by [Eva Ibbotson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eva_Ibbotson): *[Journey to the River Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journey_to_the_River_Sea)*

  [^cite_note-8]

  [^cite_note-9]

  &#x20;and *[A Company of Swans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Company_of_Swans)*. Both are adventure stories set principally in the city of [Manaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manaus) (where the theatre is situated) and surroundings in 1912. In the former (children's) book a visiting acting group performs the play, [Little Lord Fauntleroy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Lord_Fauntleroy) at the theatre, which is briefly described. In the latter (young adult) novel a visiting ballet troupe performs [Swan Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Lake), [Giselle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giselle), [The Nutcracker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Nutcracker) and [La Fille Mal Gardee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Fille_Mal_Gardee) at the theatre.

- The theatre was featured in [Ann Patchett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Patchett)'s 2011 novel, *State of Wonder*; the characters attend a performance of *Orfeo ed Euridice* by [Christoph Willibald Gluck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christoph_Willibald_Gluck).

- The film [Pavarotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavarotti_\(film\)) opens with [Luciano Pavarotti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luciano_Pavarotti) traveling to the theatre in 1995 to replicate Caruso's performance there.

- The theatre is mentioned in Daniel Catán's 1996 opera "[Florencia en el Amazonas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florencia_en_el_Amazonas)" as the location where the titular opera singer Florencia Grimaldi is traveling to give a concert.

- The White Stripes performed in the theater on June 1, 2005. The show was later released on vinyl and DVD formats as Under Amazonian Lights. It was reported to be the first rock show at the theater.
