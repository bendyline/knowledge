Geographically, the country of [Iran](14653.md) is predominantly located on the [Iranian plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_plateau) at the crossroads of [West Asia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asia) and [South Asia](21566765.md). Topographically, it borders the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md), [Persian Gulf](24761.md), and [Gulf of Oman](12896.md). Its mountains have impacted both the political and the economic history of the country for several centuries. The mountains enclose several broad [basins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drainage_basin), on which major agricultural and urban settlements are located. Until the 20th century, when major [highways and railroads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Iran) were constructed through the mountains to connect the population centers, these basins tended to be relatively isolated from one another.

Typically, one major town has dominated each basin, and there were complex economic relationships between the town and the hundreds of villages that surrounded it. In the higher elevations of the mountains rimming the basins, tribally organized groups practiced [transhumance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transhumance), moving with their herds of sheep and goats between traditionally established summer and winter [pastures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasture). There are no major river systems in the country, and historically transportation was by means of caravans that followed routes traversing gaps and passes in the mountains. The mountains also impeded easy access to the Persian Gulf and the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md).

With an area of 1,648,195 square kilometres (636,372 sq mi), Iran ranks [seventeenth in size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area) among the countries of the world. Iran shares [its northern borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borders_of_Iran) with several [post-Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states): [Armenia](10918072.md), [Azerbaijan](746.md), and [Turkmenistan](198149.md). These borders extend for more than 2,000 kilometres (1,200 mi), including nearly 650 kilometres (400 mi) of water along the southern shore of the Caspian Sea. Iran's western borders are with [Turkey](11125639.md) in the north and [Iraq](7515928.md) in the south, terminating at the [Arvand Rud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arvand_Rud).

The [Persian Gulf](24761.md) and Gulf of Oman [littorals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littoral) form the entire 1,770-kilometre (1,100 mi) southern border. To the east lie [Afghanistan](737.md) on the north and [Pakistan](23235.md) on the far south. Iran's diagonal distance from the [province of West Azerbaijan](47567329.md) in the northwest to [Sistan and Baluchestan province](448105.md) in the southeast is approximately 2,333 kilometres (1,450 mi).

## Topography

See also: [List of mountains in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mountains_in_Iran), [List of islands of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_islands_of_Iran), [Transport in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Iran), and [Communications in Iran](64706.md)



Iran at night



Topography of Iran

The topography of Iran consists of rugged, mountainous rims surrounding high interior basins. The main mountain chain is the [Zagros Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros_Mountains), a series of parallel ridges interspersed with plains that bisect the country from northwest to southeast. Many peaks in the Zagros exceed 3,000 metres (9,843 ft) above sea level, and in the south-central region of the country there are at least five peaks that are over 4,000 metres (13,123 ft).

As the Zagros continue into southeastern Iran, the average elevation of the peaks declines dramatically to under 1,500 metres (4,921 ft). Rimming the Caspian Sea littoral is another chain of mountains, the narrow but high [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz) Mountains. Volcanic [Mount Damavand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Damavand), 5,610 metres (18,406 ft), located in the center of the Alborz, is not only the country's highest peak but also the second highest mountain on the Eurasian landmass west of the [Hindu Kush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Kush), after [Mount Elbrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Elbrus).

The center of Iran consists of several closed basins that collectively are referred to as the Central Plateau. The average elevation of this plateau is about 900 metres (2,953 ft), but several of the mountains that tower over the plateau exceed 3,000 metres (9,843 ft). The eastern part of the plateau is covered by two salt deserts, the [Dasht-e Kavir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dasht-e_Kavir) (Great Salt Desert) and the [Dasht-e Lut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lut_Desert). Except for some scattered [oases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis), these deserts are uninhabited.

Parts of northwestern Iran are part of the [Armenian highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_highlands), which adjoins it topographically with other parts of neighbouring [Turkey](11125639.md), [Armenia](10918072.md), [Azerbaijan](746.md), and [Georgia](48768.md).[^cite_note-4]

Iran has only two expanses of lowlands: the [Khuzestan Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuzestan_Plain) in the southwest and the Caspian Sea coastal plain in the north. The former is a roughly triangular-shaped extension of the Mesopotamia plain and averages about 160 kilometres (99 mi) in width. It extends for about 120 kilometres (75 mi) inland, barely rising a few meters above sea level, then meets abruptly with the first foothills of the Zagros. Much of the Khuzestan plain is covered with marshes.

The Caspian plain is both longer and narrower. It extends for some 640 kilometres (400 mi) along the Caspian shore, but its widest point is less than 50 kilometres (31 mi), while at some places less than 2 kilometres (1.2 mi) separate the shore from the Alborz foothills. The Persian Gulf coast south of Khuzestan and the Gulf of Oman coast have no real plains because the Zagros in these areas come right down to the shore.

There are no major rivers in the country. Of the small rivers and streams, the only one that is navigable is the 830 kilometres (520 mi)-long [Karun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karun), which shallow-draft boats can negotiate from [Khorramshahr](464970.md) to [Ahvaz](599079.md), a distance of about 180 kilometres (110 mi). Other notable rivers include the [Karkheh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karkheh_River), spanning 700 kilometres (430 mi) and joining the [Tigris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tigris); and the [Zayandeh River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zayandeh_River), which is 300 kilometres (190 mi) long. Several other permanent rivers and streams also drain into the Persian Gulf, while a number of small rivers that originate in the northwestern Zagros or Alborz drain into the Caspian Sea.

On the Central Plateau, numerous rivers—most of which have dry beds for the greater part of the year—form from snow melting in the mountains during the spring and flow through permanent channels, draining eventually into salt lakes that also tend to dry up during the summer months. There is a permanent salt lake, [Lake Urmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Urmia) (the traditional name, also cited as Lake Urmiyeh, to which it has reverted after being called Lake Rezaiyeh under [Mohammad Reza Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Reza_Shah)), in the northwest, whose brine content is too high to support fish or most other forms of aquatic life. There are also several connected salt lakes along the Iran-Afghanistan border in the province of Baluchestan va Sistan.

Iran's highlands are home to some of the world's most unexpected glaciers. Their appearance in the dry environment is advantageous for those who depend on glacial ice as a supply of fresh water. In an expedition, [Klaus Thymann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klaus_Thymann) together with the environmental charity [Project Pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Pressure) produced a series of archive and expedition photographs that depict the urgency of the situation surrounding climate change and the individuals who rely on such natural ice forms to sustain life in remote areas.[^cite_note-5]

A recent global [remote sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_sensing) analysis suggested that there were 1,481 km<sup>2</sup> of tidal flats in Iran, making it the 22nd ranked country in terms of tidal flat area.[^cite_note-6]

## Climate

See also: [Climate](14653.md#Climate) and [Climate change in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Iran)

Iran has a diverse climate. In the northwest, winters are cold with heavy snowfalls and sub-freezing temperatures. Spring and fall are relatively mild, while summers are dry and hot. In the south, winters are mild and summers are very hot, with average daily maximum temperature exceeding 38 °C (100.4 °F) in the month of July. On the [Khuzestan Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khuzestan_Plain), summer heat is accompanied by high humidity.

In general, Iran has a temperate climate in which most of the relatively scant annual precipitation falls from October through April. In most of the country, yearly precipitation averages 400 millimetres (15.7 in) or less. The major exceptions are the higher mountain valleys of the Zagros and the Caspian coastal plain, where precipitation averages at least 600 millimetres (24 in) and is in the form of snow at high altitudes. In the western part of the Caspian, rainfall exceeds 1,500 millimetres (59 in) annually and is distributed relatively evenly throughout the year. This contrasts with some basins of the Central Plateau that receive 100 millimetres (4 in) or less of precipitation. Iran is considered colder than neighboring countries such as Iraq and Turkmenistan, due to its higher elevation.

7% of the country is forested. The most extensive growths are found on the mountain slopes rising from the Caspian Sea, with stands of [oak](39696.md), [ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ash_tree), [elm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elm), [cypress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupressaceae), and other valuable trees. On the plateau proper, areas of scrub oak appear on the best-watered mountain slopes, and villagers cultivate orchards and grow the plane tree, poplar, [willow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willow), [walnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walnut), [beech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beech), [maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple), and [mulberry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulberry). Wild plants and [shrubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrubs) spring from the barren land in the spring and afford pasturage, but the summer sun burns them away. According to [FAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAO) reports,[^cite_note-65] the major types of forests that exist in Iran and their respective areas are:

[Wildlife of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Iran) is diverse and composed of several animal species including bears, gazelles, wild pigs, wolves, jackals, panthers, Eurasian lynx, and foxes. Domestic animals include, sheep, goats, cattle, horses, water buffalo, donkeys, and camels. The pheasant, partridge, stork, eagles and falcon are also native to Iran.

As of 2001, 20 of Iran's [mammal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal) species and 14 bird [species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Species) are [endangered](20110668.md). Among them are the [Baluchistan bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluchistan_bear) (*Ursus thibetanus gedrosianus*), a [subspecies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subspecies) of [Asian black bear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_black_bear), [Persian fallow deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_fallow_deer), [Siberian crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_crane), [hawksbill turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawksbill_turtle), [green turtle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_turtle), [Oxus cobra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxus_cobra), [Latifi's viper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latifi's_viper), [dugong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dugong) and dolphins. The [Asiatic cheetah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatic_cheetah) is a [critically endangered species](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critically_endangered_species) which is [extinct elsewhere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_extinction) and now can only be found in central to northeastern parts of Iran.

Iran lost all its [Asiatic lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatic_lion) and [Caspian tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_tiger) by the earlier part of the 20th century. The [Syrian wild ass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_wild_ass) has become extinct. [Syrian brown bears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_brown_bear) in the mountains, wild sheep and goats, [gazelles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gazelle), [Persian onagers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_onager), [wild pigs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sus_\(genus\)), [Persian leopards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_leopard), and foxes abound. Domestic animals include sheep, goats, cattle, horses, [water buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_Asian_water_buffalo), donkeys, and camels. The [pheasant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pheasant), [partridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partridge), [stork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stork), and [falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falcon) are native to Iran.

The Persian leopard is said to be the largest of all the subspecies of leopards in the world. The main range of this species in Iran closely overlaps with that of [bezoar ibex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bezoar_ibex). Hence, it is found throughout [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz) and [Zagros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zagros_Mountains) mountain ranges, as well as smaller ranges within the Iranian plateau. The leopard population is very sparse, due to loss of habitat, loss of natural prey, and population fragmentation. Apart from bezoar ibex, wild sheep, [boar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boar), deer, (either [Caspian red deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_red_deer) or [roe deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roe_deer)), and domestic animals constitute leopards' diet in Iran.

Iran's [bio-diversity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bio-diversity) ranks 13th in the world. ## Environmental concerns

Main article: [Environmental issues in Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_issues_in_Iran)

**Natural hazards:** periodic droughts, floods; dust storms, sandstorms; [earthquakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_earthquakes_in_Iran) along western border and in the northeast

**Environment – current issues:** air pollution, especially in urban areas, from vehicle emissions, refinery operations, and industrial effluents; [deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation); [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification); [oil pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_pollution) in the [Persian Gulf](24761.md); wetland losses from drought; [soil degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_degradation) ([salination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salination)); inadequate supplies of [potable water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potable_water) in some areas; [water pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_pollution) from raw [sewage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sewage) and industrial waste; urbanization.

## International territorial disputes

Main article: [Foreign relations of Iran](64709.md)

Iran is currently engaged in international territorial disputes with several neighbouring countries.

The country protests [Afghanistan](737.md)'s limiting flow of dammed tributaries to the [Helmand River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmand_River) in periods of drought. The lack of a [maritime boundary](29613061.md) in the Persian Gulf with [Iraq](7515928.md) also prompts jurisdictional disputes beyond the mouth of the Arvand Rud. Iran and the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) have a [territorial dispute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_dispute) over the [Greater and Lesser Tunbs](931816.md) and [Abu Musa](931858.md) islands, which are administered by Iran. Iran currently insists on dividing the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) resources equally among the five littoral states, after the Russian-backed former soviet breakaway republics refused to respect the 50-50 agreements between Iran and the Soviet Union (despite their international obligation). Russia, Azerbaijan, Kazakhstan and Turkmenistan continue to claim territorial waters thus regarding the Caspian Sea as open international body of water, dismissing its geographically lake nature.[^cite_note-74]
