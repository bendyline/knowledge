[Syria](7515849.md) is a [unitary state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_state), but for administrative purposes, it is divided into fourteen **governorates**,[^cite_note-1] also called **provinces** or **counties** in English ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مُحَافَظَة [\[muˈħaːfaðˤa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic); pl. مُحَافَظَات *muḥāfaẓāt* [\[muħafaˈðˤaːt\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic)). The [governorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate) are divided into sixty-five [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Districts_of_Syria) ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مناطق, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *manāṭiq*, singular *[minṭaqah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mintaqah)*), which are further divided into subdistricts ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): نَاحِيَة [\[ˈnaːħijah\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic), plural نَوَاحِي, *nawāḥī* [\[naˈwaːħiː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Arabic)). The *nawāḥī* contain villages, which are the smallest administrative units.&#x20;

Each governorate is headed by a governor, appointed by the [president](516106.md), subject to cabinet approval. The governor is responsible for administration, health, social services, education, tourism, public works, transportation, domestic trade, agriculture, industry, civil defense, and maintenance of law and order in the governorate. The minister of each local administration works closely with each governor to coordinate and supervise local development projects. The governor is assisted by a provincial council, all of whose members are popularly elected for four-year terms. In addition, each council elects from among its members an executive bureau which administers the day to day issues between provincial council sessions. Each executive officer is charged with specific functions.&#x20;

Districts and subdistricts are administered by officials appointed by the governor. These officials work on local matters with elected district councils and serve as intermediaries between the central government and traditional local leaders, such as village chiefs, clan leaders, and councils of elders.&#x20;

## History

Upon achieving independence from the French Mandate on April 17, 1946, Syria retained much of the administrative framework inherited from the mandate period and organized its territory into nine top-level districts (later governorates): Aleppo, Damascus, Euphrates (Al-Furat), Hama, Hauran, Homs, Jabal al-Druze, Al Jazira, and Latakia.&#x20;

During the 1950s, several governorates names were standardized: [Hauran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauran) became [Daraa](5317030.md), Jabal al-Druze was renamed [Sweida](3096933.md), and [Al-Jazira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Jazira_Province) became [Al-Hasakah](1429110.md).&#x20;

In the early 1960s, further territorial adjustments were introduced: the Euphrates Governorate was divided into Al-Rashid and [Deir ez-Zor](1429146.md), while [Idlib](7445043.md) Governorate was separated from [Aleppo](7444618.md). In 1962, Damascus was reorganized into two administrative units, Damascus Governorate and the City of Damascus (Madīnat Dimashq).&#x20;

In 1970, Al-Rashid Governorate was renamed [Raqqa](5317143.md).[^cite_note-2]&#x20;

On August 27, 1964, [Quneitra Governorate](7445647.md) was created from parts of Damascus Governorate. Additional changes occurred in the 1970s, when [Tartus Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tartus_Governorate) was separated from the [Latakia Governorate](7445289.md). In 1987, the City of Damascus was formally designated as an independent governorate, and Damascus Governorate was reorganized as [Rif Dimashq Governorate](4521946.md), the rural governorate surrounding the capital, completing the basic structure of Syria's contemporary administrative system.[^cite_note-3]&#x20;
