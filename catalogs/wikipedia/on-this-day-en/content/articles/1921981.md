**Sohar** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): صُحَار, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Ṣuḥār*), officially: Suhar, is the capital and largest city of the [Al Batinah North Governorate](34513101.md) in [Oman](22316.md). An ancient capital of the country that once served as an important [Islamic](6037917.md) port town on the [Gulf of Oman](12896.md),[^cite_note-Agius-4] Sohar has also been credited as the mythical birthplace of [Sinbad the Sailor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinbad_the_Sailor).[^cite_note-5] It was historically known as **Mazūn** (مَزُوْن).[^cite_note-Wilkinson1964-1][^cite_note-Williamson1974-2][^cite_note-Duturaeva2022-3]

At the 2010 census, Sohar's population was 140,006, making it Oman's fifth most-populated settlement.[^cite_note-6] Described as an industrial town,[^cite_note-7] the development of the [Sohar Industrial Port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohar_Industrial_Port) during the 2000s has transformed it into a major Omani industrial hub.

## History

See also: [History of Oman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Oman) and [Tawam (region)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawam_\(region\))



The [16th-century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_century) [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire) fortress, in a drawing of the [17th-century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_century) *Book of Fortress*

As the largest town in the region, it has been argued that Sohar is to be identified with the ancient town called 'Omanah' ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): عُمَانَة) mentioned by [Pliny the Elder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) in his *[Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_History_\(Pliny\))*. This settlement is believed to have given [Oman](22316.md) its name.[^cite_note-EJ-8]

According to [Al-Tabari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Tabari), in 893 or 894 [C.E.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era), during the [Abbasid era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate), there was a dispute about who should rule Oman amongst local factions. A faction that approached the [Abbasids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid) was the Bani Sama, who were based in [Buraimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Buraimi_Governorate), before moving to Sohar. The Bani Sama also referred to themselves as the [Wajihid Dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wajihids), and assumed leadership over the region. The present-day town of [Buraimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Buraimi) is part of a historical region that Tabari referred to as '[Tawam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawam_\(region\))', which is now believed to have encapsulated much of the present day United Arab Emirates, and to have been named for [St Thomas the Apostle of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_the_Apostle).[^cite_note-TheNational_10-2015-9]

By the [10th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10th_century), Sohar was one of the richest ports of the [Persian Gulf](24761.md). It was praised by [Ibn Hawqal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Hawqal) and [Al-Maqdisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Maqdisi), who called it "flourishing, populous, beautiful, pleasant and delightful", and compared it favourably with the ports of [China](5405.md). [Aloes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agarwood), wood, bamboo, sandalwood and spices were brought from [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Subcontinent) and frankincense from [Dhofar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dhofar). The city was sacked by the [Buwayhids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buwayhids) in 971 and its trade went into decline. It also suffered from attacks by the merchants of [Kish](847277.md) across the gulf, who briefly took control of the city around 1100. Sohar then became the initial seat of the [Sultanate of Sohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nabhani_dynasty), under the rule of the Bani Nabhan dynasty. They achieved ascendancy after being appointed governors of Sohar by their [Buyid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyid_dynasty) suzerains.[^cite_note-\:0-10] The city's famous fort was constructed in the late 13th century by the "[Princes of Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ormus)" while the city remained under Nabhani control.[^cite_note-11] The two entities maintained an agreement over maritime and internal control of Oman.[^cite_note-\:0-10] After a period of ruin, Sohar was re-established in the [15th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_century) as a minor port under the Hormuzi-[Qalhati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalhat) sphere of influence.[^cite_note-\:0-10] It was still an international port until at least the 16th century.[^cite_note-12]

### Portuguese occupation

In the early [16th century](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_century), Sohar was conquered by the [Portuguese Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Empire), which used the city to control the entrances to the Persian Gulf and trade in the region. It was part of a web of fortresses controlled by the Portuguese, from [Bahrain](18933277.md) to [Hormuz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormuz_Island), and was depicted in António Bocarro's 17th-century *Book of Fortresses*.[^cite_note-13]

### Recent history

Starting on 26 February 2011, Sohar was the site of [mass protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Omani_protests), part of the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) uprisings. Although protests also occurred in [Muscat](57053.md) and [Salalah](1518956.md), the Sohar protests were the biggest in the country, attracting an estimated 2,000 people at times.[^cite_note-aljazeera2-14] The protesters' initial demands were for more job opportunities and greater political participation; later demonstrations also called for the release of detained protesters and an inquiry into the use of violence by security forces.[^cite_note-aljazeera-15] The protests centered on Sohar's [Globe Roundabout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globe_Roundabout). In response, [Sultan Qaboos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaboos_bin_Said) announced reforms and a jobs program, and reshuffled the Omani cabinet.[^cite_note-aljazeera2-14]

## Infrastructure

### Industry

A number of enterprises and investment projects in Sohar are centred around the [Port of Sohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Sohar). Established in 2002, the port has a strategic importance due to its nearness to the [Strait of Hormuz](29005.md). It is operated by the Sohar Industrial Port Company (SIPC). With current investments exceeding \$12 billion, it is one of the world's largest port development projects. The port's container terminal, managed by [Hutchison Ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hutchison_Port_Holdings)[^cite_note-16] handles the majority of the container cargo of North Al Batinah region of the Sultanate.

The industrial development of Sohar was prioritised in the Omani Government's [economic development plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Oman) in 2020. Investments include more than \$5 billion in the [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel) industry in which Oman aims to be one of the [Gulf Cooperation Council](220950.md)'s leading producers. In addition to the steel industry, there is also the industry of [aluminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminium) in Sohar industrial area. The [Sohar Aluminium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohar_Aluminium) Company was established in 2004[^cite_note-17] and it is considered one of the leading projects that play a major role in the sultanate's economic diversification strategy.

### Parks

Sohar has four main parks. The first is Sohar Park, located in Al Humbar. The second is the Silver Jubilee Park which is located in Sallan. The third is the Entertainment Park in Sanaiyyah. The fourth is in falaj alqabail. There are many other parks, like Alminyal, Alsanqar, Alsuwaihra, Al Ons, Corniche park and Aluwaynat park. The city also has the [Sohar Regional Sports Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohar_Regional_Sports_Complex).

## Climate

Sohar has a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_desert_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *BWh*) with very hot summers and mild winters. Precipitation is low; more than half of the year's total rainfall falls in February, and summers are almost completely dry.
