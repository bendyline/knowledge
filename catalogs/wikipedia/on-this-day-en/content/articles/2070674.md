The **Larry O’Brien Championship Trophy** is the championship [trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trophy) awarded annually by the [National Basketball Association](22093.md) (NBA) to the [winner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_NBA_champions) of the [NBA Finals](890793.md) since its introduction in 1977. The trophy depicts a basketball over a hoop and basket. It is named after [Larry O'Brien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_O'Brien), who served as [NBA commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioner_of_the_NBA) from 1975 to 1984, and as [United States Postmaster General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Postmaster_General) under President [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) from 1965 to 1968.[^cite_note-1]

## History

A new trophy was created for the [1977 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_NBA_Finals), the first following the [ABA–NBA merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABA%E2%80%93NBA_merger). The inaugural winners were the [Portland Trail Blazers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976%E2%80%9377_Portland_Trail_Blazers_season), who defeated the [Philadelphia 76ers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976%E2%80%9377_Philadelphia_76ers_season) in six games. Unlike the [original championship trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_A._Brown_Trophy), the new trophy was given permanently to the winning team and a new one was made every year, similar to the [Vince Lombardi Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vince_Lombardi_Trophy), awarded annually to the winning team of the [National Football League](21211.md)'s [Super Bowl](27718.md) and the [Commissioner's Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioner's_Trophy_\(MLB\)), awarded annually to the winning team of [Major League Baseball](38776.md)'s [World Series](23489161.md).

The trophy was initially called the "World Championship Trophy".[^cite_note-2] In 1984, it was renamed to the Larry O'Brien Championship Trophy, in honor of Larry O'Brien, who served as NBA commissioner from 1975 to 1984.[^cite_note-larry-3] The [Boston Celtics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_Boston_Celtics_season) were the inaugural winners of the renamed trophy, defeating the [Los Angeles Lakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983%E2%80%9384_Los_Angeles_Lakers_season) in seven games in the [1984 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_NBA_Finals).

As part of a series of redesigned awards as part of its 75th anniversary season, the trophy design was once again changed. The most notable change is two discs replacing the square foundation of the previous design. Victor Solomon, the designer of the new trophy, had designed it with a round base as he thought the former square base awkward to hold and prop the trophy upon in the hand. The two discs also function as the metal bands of the [National Hockey League](21809.md)'s [Stanley Cup](66968.md) do. The top disc displays the league's first 75 championship teams from [1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_BAA_Finals) to [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_NBA_Finals), while the bottom disc will contain the next 25 championship teams from [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_NBA_Finals) to 2046, in time for the association's 100th anniversary. The redesigned trophy's ball and net are also slanted to the right, or forward, to represent the league's continued desire to be progressive.[^cite_note-4]

### Use in NBA Finals



Game 3 of the [2006 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_NBA_Finals), with the painted O'Brien trophy seen at center court behind the [Miami Heat](72850.md) logo.

From the [2004–05](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NBA_season) to [2008–09 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008%E2%80%9309_NBA_season), the NBA used a painted version of the O'Brien trophy on the center of the court during the NBA Finals. The trophy was featured for the [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_NBA_Finals), [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_NBA_Finals), [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NBA_Finals), [2008](4516528.md), and [2009 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_NBA_Finals) before being removed, with players citing the slipperiness of the trophy and other decals.[^cite_note-\:0-5] In 2025, after significant public backlash for the O'Brien Trophy missing at center court during the [2025 NBA Finals](76692117.md) and use of a digital hologram of the trophy, NBA commissioner [Adam Silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Silver) announced the return of the O'Brien trophy at center court, alongside the Finals script logo, with its official return being the [2026 NBA Finals](82427430.md).[^cite_note-\:0-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Starting in the [1996 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_NBA_Finals), both participating teams wore a jersey patch featuring a heavily stylized Larry O'Brien Trophy.[^cite_note-\:1-9] From the [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NBA_Finals) through the [2001 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_NBA_Finals), the trophy was featured as part of an oval patch which read "NBA Finals" and indicated the year.[^cite_note-\:1-9] For the [2002 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_NBA_Finals), the patch featured the O'Brien Trophy and the NBA logo, without the oval. From the [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_NBA_Finals) to 2009 NBA Finals, the participating teams wore just the trophy design as a jersey patch, without the NBA logo.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Since the [2010 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_NBA_Finals), the trophy design has not been featured on the NBA Finals jersey patch.[^cite_note-\:1-9]

## Description



The original Larry O'Brien Trophy on the right that was awarded from 1977 to 2021

The trophy is two feet tall and is made of 15.5 pounds of [sterling silver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling_silver) and [vermeil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vermeil) with a 24 [karat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carat_\(purity\)) [gold](12240.md) overlay. The basketball depicted on top is the same size as a real basketball. The trophy was designed by artist Victor Solomon for the NBA's 75th anniversary season and is manufactured by [Tiffany & Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiffany_&_Co.)[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The championship team maintains permanent possession of the trophy (although one exception exists, as described below). The year and winning team names are engraved on the trophies, and are often prominently displayed in the winning team's arena.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-tour-16] After the sale of the [Houston Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Rockets) from [Leslie Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leslie_Alexander_\(businessman\)) to [Tilman Fertitta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilman_Fertitta) in late 2017, Alexander maintained the ownership of the team's [1993–94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_NBA_Finals) and [1994–95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_NBA_Finals) trophies as mementos of his ownership. Thus, the team commissioned Tiffany to create replica versions of both Larry O'Brien trophies (and replacing the 1993–94 trophy, which was unexpectedly dropped and dented by reserve center [Richard Petruška](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Petru%C5%A1ka) during the celebration), which were publicly unveiled on September 20, 2018.[^cite_note-17]

The trophy's design was updated in 2021, with changes including the netting being more visible than on the original design, and the base of the trophy being changed from a square to a circular shape.[^cite_note-18]

## Promotion



The O'Brien trophy displayed for the 2005 NBA Legends Tour in the New York City [NBA Store](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_Store)

Although the Larry O'Brien Trophy has been compared with the National Hockey League's Stanley Cup, it has never been as prominent as the NHL trophy.[^cite_note-19] To reduce this discrepancy, the NBA has been actively promoting the O'Brien Trophy in recent years to generate more recognition and an iconic status for the trophy.[^cite_note-china-20] The trophy appeared on logos for the [NBA Finals](890793.md) for the most part. After the [Detroit Pistons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Pistons) won the NBA Finals in 2004, the trophy was toured around the state of [Michigan](18859.md), marking the first time the trophy toured around the state of the winning team.[^cite_note-21] In 2005, the NBA Legends Tour was launched in New York City. As part of the tour, the O’Brien Trophy was showcased in various cities—including those that were hosting the playoffs—for fans' autograph and photo sessions. It was escorted by many former players, including [Julius Erving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julius_Erving), [Kareem Abdul-Jabbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kareem_Abdul-Jabbar) and [Bill Russell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Russell).[^cite_note-tour-16][^cite_note-china-20] In May 2007, the NBA unveiled the NBA Headquarters on [Second Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Life), an Internet-based virtual reality environment. With this launch, fans could take pictures with the championship trophy in the virtual Toyota Larry O'Brien Trophy Room.[^cite_note-22] In August 2007, the trophy traveled to [Hong Kong](13404.md) for the first time as part of the NBA Madness Asia Tour.[^cite_note-23] Leading up to the [2023 NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_NBA_Finals), the trophy went on a "bucket list", traveling to various locations across sports and entertainment, as well as meeting numerous celebrities. This coincided with the launch of new social media accounts made specifically for the trophy itself.[^cite_note-24]
