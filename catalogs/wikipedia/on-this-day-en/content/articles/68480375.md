**Amir Khan Muttaqi** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): امیر خان متقی [\[ˈamɪr xɑn mʊtaˈqi\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Pashto); born 26 February 1971) is an Afghan [Taliban](30635.md) politician serving as the [minister of foreign affairs](8980088.md) of the [Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan) since 2021.[^cite_note-\:0-3] He was also a member of the Taliban negotiation team in the [Qatar office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_activities_in_Qatar).[^cite_note-4]

## Early life and education

Khan was born on 26 February 1971 in Zarghoon village of [Nad Ali District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nad_Ali_District), in [Helmand Province](457235.md). His family originates from [Paktia Province](457274.md), and Khan initially received his primary education in a local school and mosque. Due to the communist [Saur Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saur_Revolution), he had to move with his family to neighboring Pakistan, where he was enrolled in a refugees' [madrasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasa) and studied subjects such as [Arabic grammar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_grammar), [logic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logic_in_Islamic_philosophy), rhetoric, [jurisprudence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiqh), [hadith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith) and [Tafsir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tafsir).[^cite_note-5]

He continued his higher Islamic studies at the [Darul Uloom Haqqania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darul_Uloom_Haqqania), a seminary in Pakistan's [Khyber Pakhtunkhwa](21950.md) province from which many other influential Taliban members graduated.[^cite_note-6]

He reportedly lived in [Banaras Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banaras_Colony) in Karachi, Pakistan during his stay and still has properties, real estate and many other business interests in Pakistan.[^cite_note-\:1-7]

## Political career

Further information: [List of international trips made by Amir Khan Muttaqi as Minister of Foreign Affairs of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_international_trips_made_by_Amir_Khan_Muttaqi_as_Minister_of_Foreign_Affairs_of_Afghanistan)

### Afghan Jihad

He was initially part of [Maulvi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maulvi) [Mohammad Nabi Mohammadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Nabi_Mohammadi)'s group during the Afghan jihad but later joined the [Taliban](30635.md) movement when it emerged.[^cite_note-8]

### Different Taliban high positions (1999–2021)

He has held a series of senior positions within the Taliban movement. In 1999, he served as a member of the Taliban High Council and was also placed in charge of Kandahar Radio.[^cite_note-\:1-7] That same year he became Chief of Staff of the Ministry of Foreign Affairs. Khan served as [Minister of Information and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Information_and_Culture_\(Afghanistan\)) and as a representative of the [1996–2001 Taliban government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\)) in [United Nations](31769.md)-led talks.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] During that time, a pro-Taliban source says that his "innovative activities" led to "a systematic jihadist publication apparatus against the enemy's widespread media aggression."[^cite_note-11]

On 17 August 2021, just after the [fall of Kabul](68481047.md) to the Taliban, he was reported to be in [Kabul](16826.md) talking to non-Taliban politicians such as [Abdullah Abdullah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_Abdullah) and [Hamid Karzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Karzai) about forming a government.[^cite_note-12] Taliban forces took control of Afghanistan's capital city of Kabul on 15 August 2021 during a [military offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive) against the Afghan government that had begun in May 2021.

### Minister of Foreign Affairs (2021–present)

On 7 September 2021, the Taliban announced the first members of a new "acting" government, three weeks after coming to full power with the takeover of Kabul on 15 August. Amir Khan Muttaqi was appointed as Afghanistan's acting [foreign minister](317153.md).[^cite_note-\:0-3] Muttaqi was reappointed on a permanent basis with the rest of the cabinet on 15 August 2025.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

In December 2021, Amir Khan Muttaqi attended a session of the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md) Council of foreign ministers as Afghanistan delegate.[^cite_note-The_U.S._failed_in_Afghanistan_by_trying_to_moralize_with_bullets_and_bombs-15] The session was attended by delegations from 57 nations with China, Russia, and the United States as guest delegations.[^cite_note-OIC_Pimpin_Pertemuan_Bahas_Krisis_Kemanusiaan,_Ekonomi_Afghanistan-16] Amir Khan discussed with Pakistan prime minister, [Imran Khan](413184.md), regarding the threat of ISIS in the [Afghanistan–Pakistan border](203570.md) region.[^cite_note-Amir_Khan_Muttaqi_downplays_Imran_Khan’s_remarks_at_OIC_summit-17]

#### Visit to Darul Uloom Deoband



Muttaqi with Indian minister of External Affairs [S. Jaishankar](40435868.md) in [New Delhi](51585.md), India, 9 October 2025

On 11 October 2025, Muttaqi visited the Islamic seminary [Darul Uloom Deoband](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darul_Uloom_Deoband), located in [Saharanpur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharanpur_district) of [Uttar Pradesh](231623.md), India. The visit was part of his first diplomatic trip to India.[^cite_note-\:2-18]

Muttaqi held discussions with senior scholars and students of the [Deobandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deobandi_movement) seminary, and expressed hopes for strengthening ties between India and Afghanistan.[^cite_note-\:2-18] Muttaqi was conferred the honorary title of *Qasmi* and was presented with a certificate granting him the *Sanad-e-Hadith* (authorization to teach Hadith). A public event was initially planned, but it was cancelled due to security and crowd management concerns.[^cite_note-19]

## Writings

In 2004 he wrote a book which was banned by the Taliban leadership, which thought some of its information could not be aired publicly.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]
