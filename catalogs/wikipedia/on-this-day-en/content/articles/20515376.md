The **Region of Murcia** ([/ˈmʊərsiə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English), [US](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_English) also [/ˈmɜːrʃ(i)ə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English);[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] [Spanish](26825.md): *Región de Murcia* [\[reˈxjon de ˈmuɾθja\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA); [Valencian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_language): *Regió de Múrcia*) is an [autonomous community](55002.md) of Spain located in the southeastern part of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula), on the [Mediterranean](19006.md) coast. The region has a population of 1,586,989 in an area of 11,316.17 km<sup>2</sup> (4,369.20 sq mi) as of 2025.[^cite_note-population-2][^cite_note-area-1] About a third of its population lives in the capital, [Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia). Its Mediterranean coastline extends along 258 kilometres (160 mi), alternating beaches, cliffs and rocky shores, in addition to the 73 kilometres (45 mi) of shoreline of the coastal lagoon of [Mar Menor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar_Menor).[^cite_note-8] At 2,014 m (6,608 ft), the region's highest point is Los Obispos Peak in the [Revolcadores Massif](https://es.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macizo%20de%20Revolcadores).[^cite_note-vertice-9]

A jurisdiction of the [Crown of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Castile) since the Middle Ages, the [Kingdom of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Murcia) was replaced in the 19th century by territory primarily belonging to the [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Spain) of [Albacete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Albacete) and Murcia (and subsidiarily to those of Jaén and Alicante).[^cite_note-10] The former two were henceforth attached to a 'historical region' also named after Murcia. The province of Murcia constituted as the full-fledged single-province autonomous community of the Region of Murcia in 1982.

The region is bordered by [Andalusia](2736.md) (the provinces of [Almería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almer%C3%ADa_\(province\)) and [Granada](143442.md)), [Castilla-La Mancha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castilla-La_Mancha) (the province of Albacete), the [Valencian Community](20514168.md) (province of [Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Alicante)), and the Mediterranean Sea. The autonomous community is a single [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Spain). The city of [Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia) is the capital of the region and the seat of the regional government, but the legislature, known as the [Regional Assembly of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Assembly_of_Murcia), is located in [Cartagena](38101530.md). The region is subdivided into [municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_Murcia).[^cite_note-11]

The region is among Europe's largest producers of fruits, vegetables, and flowers, with important vineyards in the municipalities of [Jumilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumilla), [Bullas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullas), and [Yecla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yecla) that produce wines of *Denominación de origen*. It also has an important tourism sector concentrated on its Mediterranean coastline, which features the [Mar Menor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar_Menor) saltwater lagoon. Industries include the petrochemical and energy sector (centered in Cartagena) and food production. Because of Murcia's warm climate, the region's long growing season is suitable for agriculture; however, rainfall is low. As a result, in addition to the water needed for crops, there are increasing pressures related to the booming tourist industry. Water is supplied by the [Segura River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segura_River) and, since the 1970s, by the [Tagus-Segura Water Transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagus-Segura_Water_Transfer), a major civil-engineering project that brings water from the [Tagus River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajo) into the Segura under environmental and sustainability restraints.

Notable features of the region's extensive cultural heritage include 72 [cave art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art_of_the_Iberian_Mediterranean_Basin) ensembles, which are part of the [rock art of the Iberian Mediterranean Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_art_of_the_Iberian_Mediterranean_Basin), a [World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-12] Other culturally significant features include the [Council of Wise Men of the plain of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Wise_Men_of_the_plain_of_Murcia) and the *tamboradas* (drumming processions) of [Moratalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moratalla,_Murcia) and [Mula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mula,_Spain), which were declared [intangible cultural heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intangible_cultural_heritage) by [UNESCO](21786641.md).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The region is also the home of [Caravaca de la Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravaca_de_la_Cruz), a holy city in the Catholic Church that celebrates the Perpetual Jubilee every seven years in the [Santuario de la Vera Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Vera_Cruz).[^cite_note-15]

## Etymology and denomination

The [toponym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toponymy) (place name) *Murcia* is of uncertain origin. According to [Francisco Cascales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Cascales), it could refer to the Roman goddess [Venus Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia_\(deity\)), from the [myrtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrtus_communis) on the banks of the Segura River. Historical studies conclude that, like the deity, Murcia is of Latin origin deriving most likely from *Myrtea* or *Murtea* ('place of myrtles' or 'place where myrtles grow'). Furthermore, *Mursiya* (already documented in the Islamic period as the name of the city of Murcia), was the adaptation in the Arabic of the pre-existing Latin.[^cite_note-16] According to Bienvenido Mascaray, it is also possible that the name originates from the [Iberian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_language) in the form *m-ur-zia*, meaning 'the water that empowers or moistens.'[^cite_note-17]



Map of the [Kingdom of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Murcia_\(Crown_of_Castile\)) in 1795

The use of "Murcia" to define the present region has its origin in the [Taifa of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa_of_Murcia), an Arab kingdom that existed at different times in the 11th, 12th, and 13th centuries.[^cite_note-18] After the Christian [conquest of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Murcia_\(1265%E2%80%9366\)) between 1243 and 1266, the [Kingdom of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Murcia_\(Crown_of_Castile\)) emerged, a territorial jurisdiction that formed its own institutions until its demise in 1833.[^cite_note-19]

After the provincial administrative reform of 1833, the first [Region of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_of_Murcia_\(1833-1978\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) was formed from the provinces of Albacete and Murcia. In the first attempt at decentralization, during the [First Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Spanish_Republic), this region was one of the 17 member states that were contemplated by the [Spanish Draft Constitution of 1873](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Draft_Constitution_of_1873),[^cite_note-20] proclaiming during that year the so-called [Cantón Murciano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cant%C3%B3n_Murciano), as an attempt to form a regional [canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_\(country_subdivision\)) in the context of the [Cantonal rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonal_rebellion).[^cite_note-21]

In 1978, the [Regional Council of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Murcia) was created as a pre-autonomous body, in effect until 1982, when the [Statute of Autonomy of the Region of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Autonomy_of_the_Region_of_Murcia?action=edit\&redlink=1) was approved. The province of Murcia was then granted autonomy under the official name of the Autonomous Community of the Region of Murcia in the framework of the political process in place during the Spanish transition to democracy.

## History

Main article: [History of the region of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_region_of_Murcia)

### Prehistory and Ancient Era

Since the [Lower Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Paleolithic) era, the Region of Murcia has been inhabited by humans,[^cite_note-22] with [Neanderthal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal) remains found in the [Sima de las Palomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sima_de_las_Palomas) site in the south east of the region. The [Argaric culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Argaric_culture) one of the most developed cultures of the [Metal Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal_Ages), flourished into the early [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age), with the site of [La Bastida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Bastida,_Totana) in the southwest of the region being a prominent example.[^cite_note-23] Later, the [Iberians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberians) were present in this territory during the Middle and Late Bronze Age and remained until very early in ancient history, before the Romans conquered a large part of the Iberian Peninsula.[^cite_note-24]



Roman Theatre, Cartagena

In 227 BC, [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Carthage) settled in what is now [Cartagena](38101530.md) with the mountainous territory merely the hinterland of their seacoast [empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire) although in 209 BC it was conquered by the Romans and was incorporated into the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_province) of [Hispania Carthaginensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania_Carthaginensis).[^cite_note-25] During the Roman era, Carthago Nova was the most important place in the region, and there are still remains of ancient villas in the Campo de Cartagena.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] The Romans built a salt factory and settled in a little town called Ficaria, in the current municipality of [Mazarrón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazarr%C3%B3n).[^cite_note-28] [Altiplano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comarcas_of_Region_of_Murcia#Altiplano) and Noroeste *[comarcas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comarca)* (a kind of region) both contain surviving dwellings of the Romans.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30]

In the early 5th century, the [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandals) together with the [Suebi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suebi) and [Alans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alans) invaded the Iberian Peninsula with the Vandals settling in the region. The Roman Empire successfully proposed an alliance with the [Visigoths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visigoths) to recover its lost Spanish land in return for goods and territory. The Vandals fled to North Africa and the Visigoths federated to the Roman Empire in a kingdom that stretched from Gibraltar to the [Loire River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loire). The Visigothic kingdom became independent of the Roman Empire in 476.[^cite_note-31]

In 555 AD, the Byzantines, under the emperor [Justinian the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_the_Great), conquered the southeastern coast of the Iberian Peninsula and established the province of [Spania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spania). Part of the current Region of Murcia belonged to the province and therefore to the Eastern Roman or [Byzantine Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Empire). The current of [Campo Cartagena-Mar Menor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_de_Cartagena) (Cartagena, La Unión, Fuente Álamo, Torre-Pacheco, Los Alcázares, Mazarrón) and [Alto Guadalentín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comarcas_of_Region_of_Murcia#Alto_Guadalent%C3%ADn_of_Comarca_de_Lorca) (Lorca, Águilas, San Javier and Santiago de la Ribera, and Puerto Lumbreras) also belonged to the province.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33]

### Moorish Middle Ages

In the early 8th century there was a disputed succession to the Visigothic throne. The king [Wittiza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wittiza) wanted his son Agila to be his successor, but the nobles of the court elected [Roderic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roderic), duke of [Baetica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispania_Baetica), as king. The people in favour of Agila conspired to overthrow Roderic. They asked the [Moors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moors) for help and promised spoils of war in return.[^cite_note-\:0-34]

The Moors began conquering the Iberian Peninsula in 711. Roderic was killed, and the Visigothic kingdom disappeared. Consequently, the Moors quickly conquered much of the peninsula.[^cite_note-\:0-34]

[Theodemir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodemir_\(Visigoth\)) led a nucleus of resistance in almost all the current region and the south of Alicante province. In 713, he signed the [Treaty of Orihuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Orihuela), because the resistance could no longer endure. The territory came under Muslim rule, but the conquerors granted it political autonomy.[^cite_note-\:0-34]

Under the Moors, who introduced the large-scale [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) upon which Murcian agriculture relies, the province was known as *Todmir*. According to [Idrisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Idrisi), the 12th century Arab cartographer based in [Sicily](27619.md), it included the cities of [Orihuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orihuela), [Lorca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorca,_Spain), [Mula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mula_city,_Spain), and [Chinchilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinchilla_de_Montearag%C3%B3n).



[Ibn Hud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Hud) as depicted in the *[Cantigas de Santa Maria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantigas_de_Santa_Maria)*

In the early 11th century, after the fall of the Umayyad [Caliphate of Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliphate_of_C%C3%B3rdoba), a territory centered on the city of Murcia became an independent principality, or *[taifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taifa)*. At one point, the taifa included parts of the present-day provinces of [Albacete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Albacete) and [Almería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Almer%C3%ADa), as well.

After the 1086 [Battle of Sagrajas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sagrajas), the [Almoravid emirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almoravid) swallowed up the *taifas*. When Almoravid rule ultimately declined, [Abu ʿAbd Allāh Muḥammad ibn Saʿd ibn Mardanīš](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_%CA%BFAbd_All%C4%81h_Mu%E1%B8%A5ammad_ibn_Sa%CA%BFd_ibn_Mardan%C4%AB%C5%A1) established a taifa—including the cities of Murcia, Valencia, and Dénia—that opposed for a time the spread of the [Almohads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad), but ultimately succumbed to the latter's advance in the 1170s. Conversely, when the Almohads receded after their defeat in the 1212 [Battle of Las Navas de Tolosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Las_Navas_de_Tolosa), another taifa-prince based in Murcia, [Ibn Hud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Hud), rebelled against Almohad rule and briefly controlled most of [Al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus).

### Christian Middle Ages and early modern period

[Ferdinand III of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_III_of_Castile) received the submission of the Moorish king of Murcia under the terms of the 1243 [Treaty of Alcaraz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Alcaraz) and made the territory a protectorate of the Crown of Castile. There were towns that rejected compliance with the treaty, such as Qartayanna-Al halfa (Cartagena), Lurqa (Lorca) and Mula. There were also towns where governors accepted the treaty but the inhabitants did not, such as Aledo, Ricote, Uruyla (Orihuela), and Medina La-Quant (Alicante), (although the two last do not belong to the present-day Region of Murcia; they were part of the Taifa of Murcia). In 1245, a Castilian army and a fleet from the [Cantabrian Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantabrian_Sea) conquered Qartayanna. Consequently, the rest of the rebellious towns were also taken by the Castilians.[^cite_note-35] Following the support of local Muslims for the [Mudéjar revolt of 1264–1266](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud%C3%A9jar_revolt_of_1264%E2%80%931266), in 1266 [Alfonso X of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfonso_X_of_Castile) [annexed the territory outright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_Murcia_\(1265%E2%80%9366\)) with critical military support from his uncle [Jaime I of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_I_of_Aragon).[^cite_note-36]

The Castilian conquest of Murcia marked the end of the [Aragon's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_Aragon) southward expansion along the Iberian Mediterranean coast. The kingdom of Murcia was repopulated with people from Christian territories by giving them land.[^cite_note-37]

[James II of Aragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_II_of_Aragon) broke an agreement between the Castile and Aragon regarding the division of territory between the two kingdoms and, from 1296 to 1302, conquered Alicante, Elche, Orihuela, Murcia, Cartagena, and Lorca. In consequence of those victories, James II and [Ferdinand IV of Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_IV_of_Castile) agreed to the [Treaty of Torrellas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Torrellas), which stipulated the return of the conquered territory to Castile, save for the towns of Cartagena, Orihuela, Elche, and Alicante. In 1305, Cartagena was returned to Castile. The kingdom of Murcia lost the territory of the current province of Alicante.[^cite_note-38]

The Castilian monarchs proceeded to delegate power over the whole Kingdom of Murcia (then a borderland of the Crown of Castile, near Granada and Aragon) to a senior officer called the [Adelantado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adelantado). The kingdom of Murcia was divided into religious manors, nobility manors, and *señoríos de* *[realengo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Realengo)* (a type of [manorialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manorialism) in which the noble had the property, but the king had the authority to administer justice). There were two noble lineages during the [Late Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Middle_Ages) and the modern period: Los Manueles and Los Fajardos.[^cite_note-39]

The Kingdom of Murcia was adjacent to the [Emirate of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emirate_of_Granada), which provoked several Muslim raids and wars that occurred mainly during the 15th century.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41]



Map of the Kingdom of Murcia in 1590

In the early 16th century, the population increased in the Kingdom of Murcia. There were three plague epidemics during the century, but they did not severely affect the region. In the first third of the century, the [Revolt of the Comuneros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolt_of_the_Comuneros) occurred. Some places that supported the revolt were towns in the present-day Castile and León and Castilla-La Mancha regions. In the Kingdom of Murcia, the revolutionary towns were Murcia, Cartagena, Lorca, Caravaca, Cehegín, and Totana. The castle of [Aledo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aledo,_Spain) defended the monarchy. In 1521, the Revolt of the Comuneros was defeated.[^cite_note-42]

In the early 17th century, King [Philip III of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philip_III_of_Spain) [expelled all the *Moriscos*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expulsion_of_the_Moriscos) (descendants of Muslims) from Valencia, Aragon, and Castille. During this century, two [plague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bubonic_plague) epidemics also occurred.[^cite_note-43]

During the 18th century, [Francisco Salzillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Salzillo) was a notable Baroque artist in the Kingdom of Murcia. He made carvings with religious imagery.[^cite_note-44]

### Napoleonic wars

In 1807, Napoleon signed the [Treaty of Fontainebleau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Fontainebleau_\(October_1807\)) with Spain, in order for French armies to cross the peninsula to conquer Portugal. In early 1808, Napoleon betrayed Spain and invaded Pamplona, San Sebastián, Barcelona, Burgos, and Salamanca. In 1808, the people of Madrid rebelled, and all of Spain was summoned to fight the French invaders. The people of the country established for each province political organisations, or *[juntas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junta_\(Peninsular_War\))*, as alternatives to the official administrations. Since the French were not much present in the Kingdom of Murcia, battles were rare in the region. Nevertheless, Spaniards from the region battled the French in other areas of Spain. In addition, the region became a staging area for the movement of troops, guns, and supplies destined for the eastern Iberian Peninsula, or Andalucía. In 1810, French troops did attack the Kingdom of Murcia. Most local officials escaped. The French, coming from Lorca, invaded the town of Murcia on 23 April, and looted it on the 26th. The troops returned to the town in August, but defensive measures had been taken and the French attack was repelled. The French army occupied Murcia again in January 1812, looting Águilas, Lorca, Caravaca, Cehegín, Jumilla, Yecla, Mula, Alhama de Murcia, and the Ricote Valley. Cartagena withstood a French siege, owing to its rampart and the help of an English fleet. In 1813, the French were decisively defeated in the north at the [Battle of Vitoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Vitoria).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46]

### 20th century

In 1936, under the [Second Spanish Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Spanish_Republic), there was an uprising. The North African territories of Spain were taken on 17 July. The uprising was successful in [some areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War#Beginning_of_the_coup) of Spain. The partial success of the uprising brought on the [Spanish Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Civil_War). The province of Murcia supported the [Popular Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Front_\(Spain\)) (the governing party in that era). The port of Cartagena became the main base of the Republican navy and was home to destroyer, cruiser, and submarine fleets. Thus, the Region of Murcia was of geostrategic importance during the war. To defend Cartagena, there were anti-aircraft bases throughout the region. The region was not near the frontlines and overall it was not attacked, except from the air against Cartagena and Águilas. Large factories, basic services, and some other properties were seized by trade unions. There was an impoverishment among the inhabitants and a lack of food supplies. Consequently, rationing was established in the region.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

Under [Francoist Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francoist_Spain), wine agriculture and economic activities increased in the Altiplano *comarca* (north of the region).[^cite_note-49] An oil refinery infrastructure was established in Cartagena in 1942, and power refineries, supply refineries, and factories were constructed in the same area during the 1950s and 1960s.[^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51]

Murcia became an autonomous region in 1982.

Massive riots erupted in Cartagena in 1992 protesting against the closing down of shipbuilding, mining and chemical companies and the regional legislature building was set on fire.[^cite_note-52]

## Geography



Satellite view of the Region of Murcia.

The Region of Murcia is located in the southeast of the [Iberian Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_Peninsula), on the coast of the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md). It ranges from 38º 45' in the north to 37º 23' in the south, and from 0º 41' in the east to 2º 21' in the west. With an area of 11,313 square kilometres (4,368 sq mi), it is the ninth-largest region of Spain by area and constitutes 2.9% of the national area. It extends over the greater part of the hydrographic basin of the [Segura River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segura_River), thus constituting a well-defined geographical unit, except for the *[comarcas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comarca)* of the [Sierra de Segura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mancomunidad_de_Municipios_de_la_Sierra_del_Segura) and the [Campos de Hellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campos_de_Hell%C3%ADn) which were in the province of Albacete, Los Vélez in Almería and [La Vega Baja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Baix_Segura_/_La_Vega_Baja) in the province of Alicante, all belonging to the same basin.[^cite_note-53]

### Terrain



The [Massif of Revolcadores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif_of_Revolcadores?action=edit\&redlink=1) is the highest point of the Region of Murcia, its highest peak is Los Obispos Peak at 2,014 metres (6,608 ft).

Approximately 27% of the Murcian territory consists of mountainous reliefs, 38% intermountain depressions and corridor valleys, and the remaining 35% of plains and high plateaus. The region is located at the eastern end of the [Baetic System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baetic_System), being affected climatologically by an orography that isolates it from the Atlantic influence. These mountain ranges are divided in turn from north to south into:[^cite_note-54]

- the [Cordillera Prebética](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prebaetic_System): the northernmost, where the Sierra del Carche stands out from the others.

  [^cite_note-55]

- the [Cordillera Subbética](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subbaetic_System): it consists of numerous dipping faults superimposed on each other or on the materials of the Prebaetic. The Massif of Revolcadores, the highest in the region at 2,015 metres (6,611 ft), belongs to this system.

- the [Cordillera Penibética](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penibaetic_System): with three distinct lithological complexes from north to south (Nevado-Filabride, Alpujárride and Maláguide). They are very fractured, although there is a predominance of dipping faults and inverse faults between these complexes. [Sierra Espuña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Espu%C3%B1a) is one of the fundamental penibaetic mountains.

Among the high plateaus are the Campo de San Juan and the Altiplano murciano.[^cite_note-ATLAS-56]

Some of the valleys and plains are the [coastal depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_plain) of the [Campo de Cartagena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campo_de_Cartagena)-Mar Menor; a little farther inland is the Valle del Guadalentín (also called the *Murcian pre-coastal depression*), which crosses the region from southwest to northeast.[^cite_note-ATLAS-56] The fertile plains lie along the Segura River (among the most famous ones the so-called Valle de Ricote), and tributaries of the Segura, such as the Mula basin.

To explain this complex relief, it is important to highlight the existence of significant [faults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)) throughout the area—such as Alhama de Murcia, [Bullas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullas)-[Archena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archena), or the Cicatriz Nor-Bética—which, along with intersections with other minor faults, generate numerous earth movements, such as the [2011 Lorca earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Lorca_earthquake).

The most widely present soil types are the calcaric fluvisol, the calcaric regosol, and the calcic xerosol.[^cite_note-57] [Regosol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regosol) soils form about a quarter of the region's surface;[^cite_note-58] and calcic horizons (B [horizons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horizon_\(geology\)) \[third layers of the soil] being formed by calcium carbonate deposits and 15 cm thick at least, and containing a minimum 15% of CaCO<sub>3</sub> besides more features) occur in almost half of the surface.[^cite_note-59]

### Climate

Murcia predominantly has a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BSh*) and a [cold semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-arid_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BSk*). However, parts of the southern coast of Murcia have a [hot desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *BWh*). In addition, there are small areas in the interior that have a [cold desert climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_climate) (*BWk*), a [hot summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) (Csa) and, to a lesser extent, a [warm summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) (*Csb*).[^cite_note-60]

Winters are mild (an average of 11 °C (52 °F) in December and January) and summers are hot to very hot (where the daily maximum regularly exceeds 40 °C (104 °F)). The average annual temperature varies from 10 °C (50 °F) to more than 19 °C (66 °F).[^cite_note-61]

Precipitation varies from more than 600 millimetres (24 in) to less than 200 millimetres (7.9 in) per year, making this autonomous community the driest in [Spain](26667.md).[^cite_note-62] The region has between 120 and 150 days in the year where the sky is totally clear. April and October have the most precipitation, with frequent heavy downpours in a single day.

The distance to the sea and the relief causes a thermal difference between the coast and the interior, especially in winter, when the temperature rarely dips below 10 °C (50 °F) on the coast, while in the interior regions the minimum usually does not rise above 6 °C (43 °F) and the precipitation level is higher (up to 600 millimetres (24 in)).

The city of Murcia holds the Spanish record high temperature in the 20th century. It reached 46.1 °C (115.0 °F) on 4 July 1994. The winter of 2005 was the coldest in a long time, with snow falling even on the Murcian coast.[^cite_note-63]

#### Seas



Satellite view of the Mar Menor

The greatest natural lake of [Spain](26667.md) can be found in the region: the *[Mar Menor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar_Menor)* (Small Sea) lagoon. It is a [salt water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seawater) lagoon, adjacent to the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md). Its special ecological and natural characteristics make the Mar Menor a unique place and the largest saltwater lake in Europe. With a semicircular shape, it is separated from the Mediterranean Sea by a sand strip 22 kilometres (14 mi) in length and between 100 and 1,200 metres (330 and 3,940 ft) wide, which is known as *[La Manga del Mar Menor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Manga_del_Mar_Menor)* (the Minor Sea's Sandbar).[^cite_note-70] The lagoon has been designated by the United Nations as a Specially Protected Zone of Importance for the Mediterranean. Its coastal perimeter accounts for 73 kilometres (45 mi) of coast, along which beaches follow one another beside crystal clear shallow water (the maximum depth does not exceed 7 metres (23 ft)). The lake has an area of 170 square kilometres (66 sq mi).[^cite_note-71]

### Flora and fauna

#### Flora

There are more than 30 tree species, over 50 species of shrubs, and more than 130 herbaceous plant species in the region. Some species have been introduced but are now part of the landscape.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74]

Indigenous tree species in the region are [Aleppo pines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_halepensis), [Mediterranean buckthorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhamnus_alaternus), [tamarisk trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamarix), and [field elms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulmus_minor). There are also some species that have been introduced, such as the [Mediterranean cypress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupressus_sempervirens).

Native shrubs found in several parts of the region are [esparto grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stipa_tenacissima), a species of the genus [European fan palm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chamaerops), *Salsola genistoides* (close to the opposite-leaved saltworts), [rosemary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosemary), [lentisks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistacia_lentiscus), [black hawthorns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhamnus_lycioides), [Neptune grass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Posidonia_oceanica), [shaggy sparrow-wort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thymelaea_hirsuta), and *Retama sphareocarpa*. There are also species which have been introduced, such as the [tree tobacco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicotiana_glauca) and *Opuntia maxima*.

In regards to herbaceous plants, some native species are [slender sowthistles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonchus_tenerrimus), [false sowthistles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichardia_tingitana), [mallow bindweeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convolvulus_althaeoides), [wall barleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hordeum_murinum), [fennels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fennel), *Brachypodium retusum* (close to false-bromes), *Thymus hyemalis* (close to broad-leaved thymes), *Asphodelus ayardii* (of the same genus as onionweeds). Non-native species include the [African wood-sorrel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxalis_pes-caprae) and the [flax-leaf fleabane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erigeron_bonariensis).

#### Fauna

In the region, there are over 10 species of land mammals (not counting bats), 19 bat species, over 80 bird species, 11 species of amphibians, 21 reptile species, and 9 species of fish.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79]

Mammals inhabiting the area include [barbary sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_sheep), [European badgers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_badger), [beech martens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beech_marten), [Eurasian otters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_otter), [red foxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_fox), [wild boars](52255.md), [red squirrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_squirrel), [European wildcats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_wildcat), [garden dormice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden_dormouse), and [Cabreras vole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabrera's_vole) (of the same genus as field voles). In addition, some species of bats are the [common pipistrelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_pipistrelle), [Kukhl's pipistrelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuhl's_pipistrelle), the [common bent-wing bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_bent-wing_bat), the [soprano pipistrelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soprano_pipistrelle), the [greater horseshoe bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_horseshoe_bat), the [meridional serotine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meridional_serotine) (which only inhabits southern Spain, Morocco, Algeria and Tunisia), the [lesser horseshoe bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesser_horseshoe_bat), and the European [free-tailed bat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-tailed_bat).

In regard to birds, there are some raptor species, such as [Bonelli's eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonelli's_eagle), [golden eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_eagle), [peregrine falcons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_falcon), [little owls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_owl), and [Eurasian eagle-owls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasian_eagle-owl). There are also waterbirds, such as [yellow-legged gulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow-legged_gull), [mallards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallard), [black-winged stilts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Himantopus_himantopus), [little grebes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_grebe), and [garganeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garganey). Other bird species are the [house sparrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_sparrow), [European greenfinch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_greenfinch), [European robins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_robin), [common blackbirds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_blackbird), and [European turtle doves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_turtle_dove).

Some amphibians found in the Region of Murcia are [Perez's frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perez's_frog), [common parsley frog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_parsley_frog), [European toads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_toad), and [Natterjack toads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natterjack_toad).

Reptile species in the region are [Montpellier snakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malpolon_monspessulanus), [ladder snakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladder_snake), [horseshoe whip snakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe_whip_snake), [viperine water snakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natrix_maura), [Iberian worm lizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberian_worm_lizard), [Spanish pond turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_pond_turtle), [Iberian wall lizards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podarcis_hispanicus), [Spanish psammodromus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psammodromus_hispanicus), *[Tarentola mauritanica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarentola_mauritanica)*, [loggerhead sea turtles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loggerhead_sea_turtle), and [Greek tortoises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_tortoise).

Fish species in the region include the [Atlantic horse mackerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_horse_mackerel), [Spanish toothcarp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_toothcarp), [gilt-head bream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilt-head_bream), [greater amberjack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_amberjack), [sand steenbras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_steenbras), and [flathead grey mullet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flathead_grey_mullet).

## Symbols



Map of the Kingdom of Murcia in *La Geographia Blaviana* by [Joan Blaeu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Blaeu) (1659). In the upper left quadrant appears the [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) of the kingdom, which was included in the [flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_the_Region_of_Murcia) and [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_the_Region_of_Murcia) of the Region of Murcia.

The flag of the Region of Murcia is rectangular and contains four castle battlements in gold in the upper hoist [canton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canton_\(flag\)), distributed two over two (symbolizing the borders of the ancient Kingdom of Murcia and the four [borders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Border) that it had at some point in its history), and seven royal [crowns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_\(heraldry\)) in the lower fly canton (these being the [escutcheon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escutcheon_\(heraldry\)) of the historical coat of arms of the Kingdom of Murcia), arranged in four rows, with one, three, two and one elements, respectively; all on the [crimson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimson) background of Cartagena.[^cite_note-80]

The flag's origin dates back to the [Spanish transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_transition_to_democracy), when the president of the [Regional Council of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Council_of_Murcia), [Antonio Pérez Crespo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_P%C3%A9rez_Crespo), established a commission in 1978 to study the future flag of the Region of Murcia. The commission was formed by historians [Juan Torres Fontes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Torres_Fontes?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [José María Jover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Jover) and senators [Ricardo de la Cierva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_de_la_Cierva) and Antonio López Pina. The project was approved on 26 March 1979 and the flag was first hoisted on 5 May 1979 on a balcony of the Regional Council building, the former [Provincial Council of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincial_Council_of_Murcia?action=edit\&redlink=1) (now the Ministry of Finance).[^cite_note-81]

The same committee established that the [coat of arms of the Region of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms_of_the_Region_of_Murcia) had the same symbols and distribution as the flag, with the royal crown as a crest above. Flag and shield were specified by Article 4 of the [Statute of Autonomy of the Region of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statute_of_Autonomy_of_the_Region_of_Murcia?action=edit\&redlink=1), approved by organic law in 1982.

The [Day of the Region of Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_the_Region_of_Murcia?action=edit\&redlink=1) is celebrated on 9 June, commemorating the promulgation of the Statute of Autonomy.

### Religion

[Roman Catholicism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) is, by far, the largest religion in the Region of Murcia. In 2019, 80.1% of Murcians identified themselves as Roman Catholic.[^cite_note-CIS2019Murcia-83]

### Languages

The Spanish spoken in the region has its own accent and local vocabulary. The [Murcian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcian_Spanish) dialect is one of the southern dialects of Spanish and tends to eliminate many syllable-final consonants and emphasizes regional vocabulary, much of which is derived from [Aragonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aragonese_language), [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_language), and [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) words. The general intonation and some of the distinctive vocabulary of the Murcian dialect share several traits with the dialects spoken in the neighboring [province of Almería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Almer%C3%ADa), north of [Granada](143442.md), and the [Vega Baja del Segura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vega_Baja_del_Segura), in the [Alicante province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Alicante).[^cite_note-84]

The [Valencian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valencian_language) is spoken in a small area of the region known as El [Carche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carche).[^cite_note-85]

## Economy

The [Gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) of the autonomous region was 31.5 billion [euros](9472.md) in 2018, accounting for 2.6% of Spanish economic output. GDP per capita, adjusted for purchasing power, was 22,800 euros, or 76% of the [EU27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_enlargement_of_the_European_Union) average in the same year. The GDP per employee was 84% of the EU average.[^cite_note-86]

Agriculture, ranching, and fishing contributed 5.99% of Region of Murcia's [Gross Value Added](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_Value_Added) (GVA). Extraction industries, manufacturing industries, and several power supply activities constituted 18.32% of the GVA.[^cite_note-87] The tourism sector provided 11.4% of regional GDP in 2018.[^cite_note-88]

35.9% of the land in the region is given to arable farming. Major crops grown are oat, barley, lettuce, citrus fruits, peaches, almonds, apricots, olives, and grapes.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] It is common to find Murcia's tomatoes and lettuce, lemons, and oranges in European supermarkets.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] Murcia is a producer of wines, with about 29,000 hectares (72,000 acres) devoted to grape vineyards.[^cite_note-93] Most of the vineyards are located in [Jumilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumilla) and [Yecla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yecla).[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95] Jumilla is on a plateau where the vineyards are surrounded by mountains. [Migrant workers are used](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_Spain#Migrant_agricultural_workers_in_Southern_Spain) in the agriculture industry.[^cite_note-96] In regards to fishing sector, the most caught species are anchovies, round sardinellas, sardines, chub mackerels, gilt-head breams, and pompanoes. Aquaculture breeds Atlantic bluefin tuna, gilt-head breams, and sea bass.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-98]

Murcia has some industry, with foreign companies choosing it as a location for factories, such as [Henry Milward & Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Milward_&_Sons) (which manufactures surgical and knitting needles) and American firms such as General Electric and Paramount Park Studios.

During the 2000s, the economy of the region turned towards "residential tourism", in which people from northern European countries have a second home in the area.[^cite_note-99][^cite_note-100][^cite_note-101] Europeans and Americans are able to learn Spanish in the academies in the town center.

### Tourism

The Region of Murcia receives fewer tourists than the rest of Spanish Mediterranean coast. Nevertheless, its more than 300 sunny days a year with an average temperature of 21 °C (70 °F), and the 250 kilometres (160 mi) of beaches of the so-called [Costa Cálida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Costa_C%C3%A1lida) (Warm Coast) have attracted tourists for decades.

The region is also being promoted as a cultural destination with many highlights for visitors: monuments, gastronomy, cultural events, museums, historic remains, festivals, etc. The region is one of the Spanish autonomous communities that have grown the most in the last few years, and this has conferred on it the character of an ideal destination for services, shopping, cultural events, and conventions.

##### The castles itinerary

The interior of the Region of Murcia has plenty of castles and fortifications that show the importance of these frontier lands between the Christian [Castile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castile_\(historical_region\)) and the Muslim [Andalusia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus). They include:

- Castle of [Jumilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jumilla), a former Roman fortification turned by the Moors into an [Alcazaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcazaba). The Castilian kings and the [marquesses of Villena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquess_of_Villena) gave it its appearance of a Gothic royal residence.

- Castle of [Moratalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moratalla), one of the largest castles of the province, built to defend the town of Moratalla from invaders from the nearby Muslim [Kingdom of Granada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Granada).

- Castle of [Mula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mula,_Spain), of Muslim origin, but as with many castles, eventually restored and renovated.

- Royal Alcázar of [Caravaca de la Cruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravaca_de_la_Cruz), where the Holy sanctuary was built, also of Moorish origin, conquered by the Christians and finally home to several noble families.

- Concepción Castle, in [Cartagena](38101530.md), built on one of the five hills of the old Cartagena, following the Roman taste. Now it is home of the Centre for the Interpretation of the History of Cartagena.

- [Lorca Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorca_Castle), also known as the Fortress of the Sun.

##### Festivals

[Cartagena's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semana_Santa_in_Cartagena) and [Lorca's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_Lorca) [Holy Week processions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Week_in_Spain) have been declared of International Tourist Interest,[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] together with Murcia's *Bando de la Huerta* and "[The Burial of the Sardine in Murcia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Burial_of_the_Sardine_in_Murcia)", included in its spring festivities.[^cite_note-105] Murcia's Holy Week is also interesting since its processions include statues by Murcian sculptor [Francisco Salzillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Salzillo).

#### Beaches and golf



La Manga del Mar Menor

The *Costa Cálida* has 250 kilometres (160 mi) of beaches, from El Mojón, in the north near [Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Alicante), to [Águilas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81guilas), in southwest Murcia near [Almería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Almer%C3%ADa).

One of the major destinations of Murcia is the *[Mar Menor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar_Menor)* or Small Sea, located on the [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean). It is the largest natural lake in Spain and the largest salty lagoon in [Europe](9239.md).[^cite_note-108] It is separated from the Mediterranean by a 22-kilometre (14 mi) long narrow sandy strip known as *[La Manga del Mar Menor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Manga_del_Mar_Menor)* or simply *La Manga*.[^cite_note-109] La Manga del Mar Menor is a sandy barrier resting on a discontinuous lithological substrate shaped by the presence of tidal inlets (*golas*). The sandy substrate was deposited during the Quaternary, overlying Palaeozoic schists, volcanic rocks and Miocene sandstones.[^cite_note-110] It is probably the most developed and overcrowded holiday area of Murcia, despite being declared one of the [Specially Protected Areas of Mediterranean Importance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specially_Protected_Areas_of_Mediterranean_Importance) (SPAMI) by the [United Nations](31769.md).

Mar Menor's muds are famous for their therapeutic properties.[^cite_note-111] Apart from Mar Menor, the Murcian coast from Cartagena to the frontier with [Andalusia](2736.md) alternates between wild and unspoilt rocky areas, large sandy beaches, and the towns of [Mazarrón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazarr%C3%B3n) and [Águilas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81guilas).[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113]

The needs of tourism have forced the area to add all kinds of facilities and services. A construction boom resulted in a huge number of estates, including the controversial holiday resorts of [Polaris World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polaris_World), second residences, and numerous malls. Thanks to the [orography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orography) and climate of the region, these lands are suitable for golf courses, a fact that has been very controversial because of the need for water, which Murcia lacks, being a very dry region.

Other services include adventure tourism companies, tourist routes, guided visits, yacht facilities, nautical excursions, and sports federations.

Golf, and in particular golf tourism, has become important to the economy and draws visitors from around the world, particularly the United States, [United Kingdom](31717.md), [Scandinavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), and Germany.[^cite_note-114] Unlike other parts of Europe, especially northern Europe, the weather in high season can almost be guaranteed to be dry and sunny. This has led to the creation of specialist golf holidays[^cite_note-115] to bring in visitors from April to June and September to November, especially. Unlike in other parts of the country, [golf courses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golf_course) are quieter in July and August due to the extreme heat.

### Road

The region's highway network provides connectivity along the coast, with three highway links with Andalusia ([Autovía A-91](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_A-91), [Autovía A-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_A-7), and the tolled [Autopista AP-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopista_AP-7)) and another three with the Valencian Community (A-7 and the tolled AP-7 and Autopista AP-37), but only the [Autovía A-30](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autov%C3%ADa_A-30) connects Murcia with inland Spain. It is thus the goal of the regional government to provide alternative highway corridors that connect the interior to the coastal zones.

The autonomous government is investing heavily in its highway network, both for trips along the coast and inland–coast connectivity. Due to the expansion of the regional network that this effort is expected to produce, Murcia has recently implemented a new naming scheme for its regional highways, more in accordance with that of the national network. When the renaming is complete, all highways will be identified by white-on-blue names that start with RM (for *Región de Murcia*).

### Rail

The [Chinchilla–Cartagena railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinchilla%E2%80%93Cartagena_railway) provides the only rail route to Madrid from the region. The [Cercanías Murcia/Alicante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cercan%C3%ADas_Murcia/Alicante) [commuter rail](6286.md) network connects Murcia to Alicante, via [Orihuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orihuela) and [Elche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elche), along with a branch to [Águilas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81guilas).

The [Madrid–Levante high-speed rail network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrid%E2%80%93Levante_high-speed_rail_network) is due to reach Murcia in 2021, and the [Murcia–Almería high-speed rail line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia%E2%80%93Almer%C3%ADa_high-speed_rail_line) will connect the region to Almería by 2023.

### Air

The [Región de Murcia International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regi%C3%B3n_de_Murcia_International_Airport) opened in 2019, replacing the [Murcia San Javier Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murcia_San_Javier_Airport) for passenger flights. It was used by a million passengers in its first year of operation. [Alicante Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alicante_Airport), although outside Murcia, is also used by air travellers from the region.

### Sea

The [Port of Cartagena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Cartagena) is the region's only port. 60% of the region's exports and 80% of its imports go through the port.[^cite_note-123]
