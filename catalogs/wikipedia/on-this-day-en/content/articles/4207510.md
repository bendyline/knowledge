# Oil

This article is about the chemical substance. For crude oil, see [Petroleum](23195.md). For other uses, see [Oil (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_\(disambiguation\)).



A bottle of [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil) used in food

**Oil** is a [liquid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid) with varying degrees of [viscosity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viscosity) depending on temperature. Oil is any [nonpolar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_polarity) [chemical substance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_substance) that is composed primarily of [hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrocarbon) and is [hydrophobic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrophobe) and [lipophilic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipophilicity). Oils are usually [flammable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flammable) and [surface active](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surfactant). Most oils are [unsaturated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saturated_and_unsaturated_compounds) [lipids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipids) that are liquid at [room temperature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Room_temperature).

## Plant and animal oils

Diverse oils are produced by plants, animals, and other organisms.[^cite_note-alberts-1] *[Lipid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lipid)* is the scientific term for the [fats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fat), [steroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steroid), and [phospholipids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phospholipid) that are soluble in organic solvents.[^cite_note-2] Another large class of plant oils are [tall oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tall_oil), mixtures of fats and [rosins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosin) that are extracted from trees and used mainly as [solvent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvent) and fuel.[^cite_note-3] In addition to modern animal-derived oils (e.g. [tallow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tallow)), [whale oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale_oil) was once widely used.

From the perspective of commercial applications, fats are broadly significant because they are precursors to [soaps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soap) and are a component of many foods, e.g. [cooking oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooking_oil).[^cite_note-4] [Drying oils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drying_oil), consisting of highly unsaturated fats, are key components of some paints and lacquers.

## Petroleum-derived oils

Several sources of "petroleum-derived" oils exist including [distillates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distillation) from crude oil and [shale oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shale_oil).[^cite_note-5] Major uses of these oils include fuels (e.g. [heating oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heating_oil)) and [hydraulic fluids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydraulic_fluid).[^cite_note-6]

Additionally, synthetic oils, mainly used as [lubricants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubricant) are produced by chemical processing of [petrochemicals](67728.md).[^cite_note-7]

[Petroleum](23195.md)-based oils originate from ancient [fossilized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossilized) [organic materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organic_material), such as [zooplankton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zooplankton) and [algae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algae), which [geochemical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geochemical) processes convert into oil.[^cite_note-8]

## Etymology and history

[^cite_note-10] First usage in a form resembling the modern is [^cite_note-OED-11] in [Anglo-Norman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Norman_language)[^cite_note-OEDE-12] before (*[a](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/ante)*)1300 in [Land of Cokaygne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_of_Cockayne_\(poem\))[^cite_note-OED-11][^cite_note-16] in [Middle English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_English) from [Old French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_French) *oile* [^cite_note-OEDE-12] as a consequence of influence of [Edward the Confessor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_the_Confessor) (1042–1066)[^cite_note-CLA2006-17] and after the killing of [King Harold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Godwinson) on 14 October 1066 [^cite_note-18] after the [1066 invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Conquest) from [Normandy](21724.md),[^cite_note-CLA2006-17] the earliest extant source a translation from Latin during the 12th or 13th century ([Marbode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marbode) Lapidaire)[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] from [Classical Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Latin) *oleum*,[^cite_note-OEDE-12][^cite_note-OEtymD-21] (the earliest extant source being: [Plautus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plautus), [Poenulus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poenulus))[^cite_note-LS-22] [^cite_note-34] which in turn comes from the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) ἔλαιον (*elaion*), "olive oil, oil"[^cite_note-35] and that from ἐλαία (*elaia*), "[olive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive) tree", "olive fruit".[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-OEtymD-21] "Olive oil" in [Mycenaean Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mycenaean_Greek) ([transliteration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transliteration)) is *e-rai-wo*. [^cite_note-37]

Oil has been used throughout history as a religious medium. It is often considered a spiritually purifying agent and is used for [anointing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anointing) purposes. As a particular example, [holy anointing oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_anointing_oil) has been an important ritual liquid for [Judaism](15624.md)[^cite_note-38] and [Christianity](5211.md).[^cite_note-39]
