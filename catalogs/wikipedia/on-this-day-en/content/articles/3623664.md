The unicameral ***Assemblée nationale*** or **National Assembly** is [Guinea](12176.md)'s [legislative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative) body. Since the country's birth in 1958, it has experienced political turmoil, and elections have been called at irregular intervals, and only since 1995 have they been more than approval of a [one-party state](102459.md)'s slate of candidates. The number of seats has also fluctuated.

It is currently suspended, with the [National Council of the Transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_of_the_Transition) acting as Guinea's legislative body in the wake of the [2021 Guinean coup d'état](68651369.md).[^cite_note-transitional-1]

## Outline

The new constitution of 2025 outlines a bicameral legislature with a lower chamber, National Assembly, of 147 seats who are directly elected for five years.[^cite_note-guinee360-2] The upper chamber, [Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_of_Guinea), is indirectly elected for a term of six years.[^cite_note-const2025-3]

The National Assembly will have 147 seats, 49 of them are directly elected based on a national list on proportional representation. The remaining 98 seats are elected on constituency level.[^cite_note-guinee360-2]

## Legislative history

The first legislative arm was **Guinean Territorial Assembly** in [French Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Guinea) in 1952. After independence, a new unicameral **National Assembly** was established in 1958. [Saifoulaye Diallo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saifoulaye_Diallo) was the first [President of the National Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_National_Assembly_of_Guinea).[^cite_note-4] Single-party rule was quickly established by [Ahmed Sékou Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_S%C3%A9kou_Tour%C3%A9). The National Assembly was reformed as **Popular Revolutionary Assembly** in 1974. The legislature was dissolved in the [1984 coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Guinean_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) by colonel [Lansana Conté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansana_Cont%C3%A9), who ruled by decree.[^cite_note-1995elections-5]

A new unicameral **National Assembly** with 114 seats was established in 1995, along with first multiparty elections in Guinea. The National Assembly was disbanded after the [2008 coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Guinean_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). A temporary National Transitional Council worked from 2010 to 2014. A new unicameral National Assembly with 114 seats worked from 2014[^cite_note-6] until the [2021 coup](68651369.md). A temporary **[National Council of Transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Council_of_the_Transition)** was established in 2022 under the junta with 81 seats. A new constitution approved in the [2025 constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Guinean_constitutional_referendum) outlines a bicameral parliament with a popularly elected **National Assembly** and an indirectly elected less powerful **Senate**.[^cite_note-const2025-3]

## Organisation before 2021

See also: [List of presidents of the National Assembly of Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_presidents_of_the_National_Assembly_of_Guinea)

Two thirds of the members (76), called *députés*, are directly elected through a system of [proportional representation](23298.md), using national party-lists, while one third (38) are elected from single-member [constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituency), using the simple majority (or [first-past-the-post](261709.md)) system. Members must be over 25 years old and serve five-year terms.[^cite_note-GlobaLex-7]

The President of the National Assembly of Guinea is the presiding officer of the legislature. [Claude Kory Kondiano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Kory_Kondiano) has been President of the National Assembly since January 2014.[^cite_note-IPU-8][^cite_note-9]

The Assembly is made up of 12 commissions:[^cite_note-GlobaLex-7]

1. Commission of accountancy and control

2. Commission of delegations

3. Economic, financial and planning commission

4. Foreign Affairs Commission

5. Commission for legislation, internal rules of the Assembly, the general administration and justice

6. Commission of defense and security

7. Commission of natural resources and sustainable development

8. Commission of industries, mines, commerce and handcraft

9. Commission of territorial arrangement

10. Commission of civil service

11. Commission of youth, arts, tourism and culture

12. Commission of information and communication

On 5 February 2022, five months after a [military coup](68651369.md) which saw the National Assembly briefly dissolved, a transitional parliament chaired by former lawmaker Dansa Kurouma with 81 members was established.[^cite_note-transitional-1] The new parliament exists in the form of a National Transitional Council (CNT).[^cite_note-transitional-1]

## Duties and responsibilities

The Assembly is responsible for ordinary laws and the government's budget.[^cite_note-GlobaLex-7]

It ordinarily meets in two annual sessions, beginning 5 April and 5 October (or the next working day if a holiday) and lasting no more than 90 days. Special sessions can be called by either the President of Guinea or a majority of the Assembly members.[^cite_note-GlobaLex-7]

## Building

The National Assembly has its headquarters in the [Palais du Peuple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_du_Peuple_\(Guinea\)) (People's Palace),[^cite_note-10] which was built with Chinese assistance.[^cite_note-11]

## Elections

### 1963

Main article: [1963 Guinean legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Guinean_legislative_election)

Guinea was a [one-party state](102459.md), so the sole legal party, the [Democratic Party of Guinea – African Democratic Rally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Party_of_Guinea_%E2%80%93_African_Democratic_Rally), won all seats in the Assembly.[^cite_note-12]

### 1968

Main article: [1968 Guinean general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Guinean_general_election)

The Democratic Party of Guinea – African Democratic Rally once again secured all of the then-75 seats,[^cite_note-13] and [Ahmed Sékou Touré](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_S%C3%A9kou_Tour%C3%A9) retained the presidency.[^cite_note-14]

### 1974

Main article: [1974 Guinean general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Guinean_general_election)

With no other parties legally allowed, the Democratic Party of Guinea – African Democratic Rally took all now-150 seats, and Touré was reelected president unopposed.[^cite_note-IPU1974-15] Members were elected for seven-year terms.[^cite_note-IPU1974-15]

### 1980

Main article: [1980 Guinean legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Guinean_legislative_election)

The Democratic Party of Guinea – African Democratic Rally secured all now-210 seats as the only party, with Touré retaining the presidency.[^cite_note-16]

### 1995

Main article: [1995 Guinean legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_Guinean_legislative_election)

The first election in which multiple parties were permitted was boycotted by one of the main opposition parties, the Union of Democratic Forces, but 846 candidates from 21 parties contested the 114 seats.[^cite_note-IPU1995-17] The [Unity and Progress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_and_Progress_Party) led the way with 71 seats, 41 proportionally and 30 by constituency, and its leader, General [Lansana Conté](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lansana_Cont%C3%A9), head of the country since a 1984 military [coup d'état](25914154.md), became the second president.[^cite_note-IPU1995-17]

### 2002

Main article: [2002 Guinean legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Guinean_legislative_election)

The election was originally scheduled for April 2000, as the five-year terms of office expired, but was postponed four times for various reasons.[^cite_note-IPU2002-18] The 30 June 2002 election was won by President Conté's [Unity and Progress Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unity_and_Progress_Party), with 61.57% of the vote and 85 of the 114 seats.[^cite_note-IPU2002-18]

### 2013

Main article: [2013 Guinean legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Guinean_legislative_election)

Elections were held on 28 September 2013.[^cite_note-Reuters-19] [Alpha Condé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpha_Cond%C3%A9)'s party, the [Rally of the Guinean People](2726867.md), won the most seats, 53, but fell short of a majority.

### 2020

Main article: [2020 Guinean legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Guinean_legislative_election)

Elections were held on 22 March 2020. Alpha Condé's Rally of the Guinean People won 79 of the 114 seats, which is a [supermajority](638313.md).
