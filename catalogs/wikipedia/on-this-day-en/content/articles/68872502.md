**Deir ez-Zahrani** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): دير الزهراني, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Dayr az-Zahrānī*) is a municipality in southern Lebanon. It is located 75 kilometres (47 mi) from [Beirut](37428.md).[^cite_note-localiban-1]

## History

In 1875 [Victor Guérin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victor_Gu%C3%A9rin) noted: "\[Deir Zaharany] is located on a hill, has a population of 100 [Métualis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A9tualis), to which must be added about twenty Christians. It succeeded an ancient locality, as evidenced by several fragments of scattered [columns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Column) here and there and a number of [ashlars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashlar) embedded in a ruined mosque and in private houses. It was in one of these dwellings that was found in 1861, and brought back to the khan of Saida, an ancient funerary cippe, with palm, crown and lemnisque, and whose Greek inscription was reproduced by Mr. [Renan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_Renan)."[^cite_note-2]

On 15 August 2026, four people were killed in an [Israeli](9282173.md) airstrike in the village.[^cite_note-3]

## Demographics

In 2014 [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Lebanon) made up 97.67% of registered voters in Deir ez-Zahrani. 97.04% of the voters were [Shiite Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Shia_Muslims).[^cite_note-4]
