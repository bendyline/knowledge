**Pasadena** ([/ˌpæsəˈdiːnə/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/6a/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Flame%2C_not_lame-Pasadena%2C_California.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Flame%2C_not_lame-Pasadena%2C_California.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/6/6a/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Flame%2C_not_lame-Pasadena%2C_California.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Flame%2C_not_lame-Pasadena%2C_California.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*PASS-ə-DEE-nə*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a city in [Los Angeles County, California](79734.md), United States, 10 miles (16 km) northeast of [downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles). It is the primary cultural center of the [San Gabriel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gabriel_Valley).[^cite_note-soc-17] [Old Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Pasadena) is the city's original commercial district.

Its population was 138,699 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census),[^cite_note-quif-14] making it the [45th-largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_municipalities_in_California) in California[^cite_note-quif-14] and the ninth-largest in Los Angeles County. Pasadena was incorporated on June 19, 1886, 36 years after the city of Los Angeles but still one of the first in what is now Los Angeles County.[^cite_note-18]

Pasadena hosts the annual [Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_Game) [football](18951490.md) game and [Tournament of Roses Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Parade) each [New Year's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Year's_Day).

## History

Main articles: [History of Pasadena, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Pasadena,_California) and [Old Town Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Pasadena)

### Indigenous history

Native Americans lived in the [Los Angeles Basin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Basin) for thousands of years. The earliest known inhabitants of present-day Pasadena and its surroundings were members of the [Hahamog-na](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hahamog-na) tribe, a branch of the [Tongva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongva_people) Nation. They spoke the [Tongva language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongva_language), belonging to the [Northern subfamily of the Uto-Aztecan language group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uto-Aztecan_languages).[^cite_note-city-19]

Pasadena means "valley" in the [language of the](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe_language) [Ojibwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ojibwe), a Native American tribe not local to the region. The name was chosen by American settlers from Indiana who would later move to the area.[^cite_note-nameorigin-3]

### Spanish era

The Spanish first colonized the Los Angeles Basin in the 1770s as part of the [Viceroyalty of New Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viceroyalty_of_New_Spain), building the [San Gabriel Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gabriel_Mission) and renaming the local [Tongva people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tongva_people) "Gabrielinos" or "Gabrieleños", after the name of the mission. Today, several bands of Tongva people live in the Los Angeles area.[^cite_note-20]

### Mexican era

In 1821, [Mexico became independent of Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_\(Mexico\)), and California came under control of the Mexican government. In 1833, the mission lands were secularized and most of the lands in California were granted to private Mexican citizens in the form of ranchos. Present-day Pasadena was divided between [Rancho San Rafael](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_San_Rafael) (lands west of the Arroyo Seco extending to present-day [Burbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbank,_California) in the northwest to [Glassell Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glassell_Park) in the southwest), [Rancho del Rincon de San Pascual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_San_Pascual),[^cite_note-city-19] (present-day central Pasadena, [Altadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altadena), and [South Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pasadena,_California)), and [Rancho Santa Anita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Santa_Anita) (present-day east Pasadena, Arcadia, and Monrovia).[^cite_note-21] Rancho del Rincon de San Pascual was so named because it was deeded on Easter Sunday to [Eulalia Perez de Guillén Mariné](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eulalia_Perez_de_Guill%C3%A9n_Marin%C3%A9) of [Mission San Gabriel Arcángel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Gabriel_Arc%C3%A1ngel).

### American era

The last of the Mexican owners of Rancho del Rincon de San Pascual was [Manuel Garfias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Garfias),[^cite_note-city-19] who retained title to the property through the [American conquest of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_California) in 1848 and statehood in 1850. Garfias sold sections of the property to the first Anglo settlers to come into the area: [Dr. Benjamin Eaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_S._Eaton), the father of [Fred Eaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Eaton); and Dr. S. Griffin. Much of the property was purchased by [Benjamin Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Davis_Wilson), who established his [Lake Vineyard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Huerta_de_Cuati) property in the vicinity. Wilson, known as Don Benito to the local Indians,[^cite_note-city-19] also owned the [Rancho Jurupa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Jurupa) ([Riverside, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside,_California)) and was [mayor of Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Los_Angeles,_California). He was the grandfather of WWII General [George S. Patton Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_S._Patton_Jr.) and the namesake of [Mount Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Wilson_\(California\)).



View of Pasadena c. 1898

In 1873, Wilson was visited by Dr. Daniel M. Berry of Indiana, who was looking for a place in the country that could offer a mild climate for his patients, most of whom suffered from respiratory ailments. Berry, an asthmatic, concluded that he had his best three nights' sleep at Rancho San Pascual.[^cite_note-22] To keep the find a secret, Berry code-named the area "Muscat" after the grape that Wilson grew. To raise funds to bring the company of people to San Pascual, Berry formed the Southern California Orange and Citrus Growers Association and sold stock in it.[^cite_note-23] The newcomers were able to purchase a large portion of the property along the Arroyo Seco and on January 31, 1874, they incorporated the [Indiana Colony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indiana_Colony). As a gesture of good will, Wilson added 2,000 acres (8 km<sup>2</sup>) of then-useless highland property, part of which would become [Altadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altadena,_California). Colonel [Jabez Banbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jabez_Banbury) opened the first school on South Orange Grove Avenue. Banbury had twin daughters, named Jennie and Jessie. The two became the first students to attend Pasadena's first school on Orange Grove.[^cite_note-24]

At the time, the Indiana Colony was a narrow strip of land between the Arroyo Seco and [Fair Oaks Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Oaks_Avenue). On the other side of the street was Wilson's Lake Vineyard development.[^cite_note-National_Register-25] After more than a decade of parallel development on both sides, the two settlements merged into the City of Pasadena.[^cite_note-National_Register-25]

### Resort town



The former [Hotel Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Green) in 1900

The region drew people from across the country. In 1887, the [Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atchison,_Topeka_and_Santa_Fe_Railway) opened the *Second District of the Los Angeles Division*[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] and began making stops at the [Santa Fe Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Depot_\(Pasadena\)) in downtown Pasadena.[^cite_note-train-28] This triggered a real estate boom. Tourist hotels were developed in the city. Pasadena became a winter resort for wealthy Easterners, spurring the development of new neighborhoods and business districts, and increased road and transit connections with Los Angeles. In 1940, when the [Arroyo Seco Parkway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arroyo_Seco_Parkway), California's first freeway, connected Pasadena to Downtown Los Angeles. By that time, Pasadena had become the eighth-largest city in California and was widely considered a [twin city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twin_cities_\(geographical_proximity\)) to Los Angeles.[^cite_note-29]



Downtown Pasadena, c. 1910

The first of the hotels to be established in Pasadena was the Raymond (1886) atop Bacon Hill, renamed Raymond Hill after construction. The original Mansard Victorian 200-room facility burned down on Easter morning of 1895, was rebuilt in 1903, and razed during the Great Depression to make way for residential development. The Maryland Hotel existed from the early 1900s and was demolished in 1934. The world-famous [Mount Lowe Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lowe_Railway) and associated mountain hotels shut down four years later due to fire damage. Three hotel structures have survived, the Green Hotel (a co-op since 1926), the Vista Del Arroyo (now used as a Federal courthouse), and a residential tower of the Maryland at 80 North Euclid Avenue (a co-op since 1953).[^cite_note-30]

The [American Craftsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Craftsman) era in art and design is well represented in Pasadena.[^cite_note-31] The architectural firm [Greene and Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greene_and_Greene) developed the style; many of its residences still stand. Two examples of their [Ultimate bungalow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_bungalow) are the masterpiece [Gamble House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamble_House_\(Pasadena,_California\)) and the [Robert R. Blacker House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_R._Blacker_House), both designated [California Historical Landmarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Historical_Landmark) and enrolled on the U.S. [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places).

### Contemporary



The [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) in 1925

[World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War) was a boon to Pasadena. Southern California became a major [staging area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staging_area) for the [Pacific War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_War).[^cite_note-32] High-tech manufacturing and scientific companies made the city their home, a trend that continued in the decades following the war, notably with [NASA](18426568.md)'s [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory), [Tetra Tech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tetra_Tech), and Ameron International.

In the 1950s, Pasadena saw a steady influx of people from the [Southern United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_the_Southern_United_States), especially African-Americans from [Texas](29810.md) and [Louisiana](18130.md). Pasadena also began hosting a large immigrant community, particularly from China, Japan, Philippines, Mexico, Guatemala, El Salvador, Italy, Armenia, and India.

The [American Academy of Dramatic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Academy_of_Dramatic_Arts), founded in 1884 in New York, opened its Pasadena campus in 1974. However, in 2001 the conservatory moved from Pasadena to Hollywood. Training actors for the stage in a two year program, the conservatory was the first school in the United States to offer professional education in the field of acting. [Point Loma Nazarene University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Loma_Nazarene_University) was located in Pasadena for many years before relocating to [San Diego County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County,_California), and retained the names Pasadena University and Pasadena College.



Downtown Pasadena in 1945

In 1969, the [Pasadena Unified School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Unified_School_District) was desegregated, though the issue would continue to be fought in court for a decade. A year later, the [210 Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_210_and_State_Route_210_\(California\)) was built along a newly chosen route. The freeway's construction was controversial, as it caused the demolition of over a thousand homes, many historic, and many claimed that the route was designed to cut off the city's less wealthy neighborhoods.

[Downtown Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Pasadena,_California) became dangerous in some parts and deserted in others, and incidences of murder and arson skyrocketed. Old Pasadena faced destruction as plans for new high-rise developments were drawn up, though they were mostly stopped by increasingly active preservation advocates. Pasadena suffered demographically as many residents moved for the nearby suburbs or the [Inland Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Empire_\(California\)), causing an overall decrease in population. Despite these setbacks, many local artists and [hipsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hipster_\(1940s_subculture\)) moved in to take advantage of low property values. Their legacy can be seen today in the [Doo Dah Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doo_Dah_Parade) which began in 1976.

In 2014, several arrests were made involving an embezzlement scheme which stole an estimated \$6.4 million from the city's Underground Utility Program.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34]

In January 2025, portions of Pasadena were destroyed in the [Eaton Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eaton_Fire).[^cite_note-35]

## Geography



[Hahamongna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hahamongna,_California) Watershed Park

The greater Pasadena area is bounded by the [Raymond Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Fault) line, the [San Rafael Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Rafael_Hills), and the [San Gabriel Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gabriel_Mountains). The [Arroyo Seco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arroyo_Seco_\(Los_Angeles_County\)), a major geographic feature and home of the Rose Bowl, flows from headwaters in Pasadena's towering [Angeles National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angeles_National_Forest) greenbelt in the [San Gabriel Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gabriel_Mountains).[^cite_note-soc-17] According to the United States Census Bureau, the city has a total area of 23.1 square miles (60 km<sup>2</sup>), over 99% of it land; 0.68% is water.[^cite_note-U.S._Census-36]

Pasadena has a [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Csa*), with typically hotter summers and slightly cooler winters than nearby coastal areas. Its location relative to the San Gabriel mountains allows the orographic lift to add several more inches of rainfall per year than nearby areas. During the first few months of the year, Pasadena experiences cool to warm highs, typically in the upper 60s (16–18 °C) to lower 70s (21–24 °C). Colder days are usually accompanied by heavier rain. By April, temperatures warm further, and rain tapers off significantly.

By May and June, rain is typically sparse, but the [marine layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_layer) becomes more persistent. Locals have dubbed June "[June Gloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_Gloom)" as it is the cloudiest month despite being the 3rd driest month. By July, the marine layer subsides as inland areas cool due to an increased [monsoon](57630.md) flow. Heatwaves from July through October can be oppressive and lengthy. In addition, it rarely rains during the summer and fall months, and only does when the remnants of hurricanes and [tropical storms](8282374.md) pass by. In fact, some days in both July and August have never recorded rainfall.[^cite_note-37] It is not impossible to go 6 months without measurable precipitation.

The average highest temperature recorded each year is around 106 °F (41 °C). The hottest heatwaves of the year usually occur in mid to late September. By late October, temperatures drop off. By November, Pacific storms return to Pasadena, bringing increasingly heavy rain and cooler weather. Along with them, however, are the Santa Ana winds. The [Santa Ana winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Ana_wind) can produce heat, high winds, power outages, tree damage and an increased wildfire threat whenever they strike. By December, lows typically drop into the 40s (below 10 °C) with the occasional reading in the 30s (under 5 °C and down to freezing). Highs remain around 68 °F (20 °C) with heatwaves pushing temperatures into the mid-80s (around 30 °C). A high temperature of at least 85 °F (29 °C) has been recorded on all 365 days of the year, with temperatures over 100 °F (38 °C) possible April through early November.

Pasadena averages 20.08 in (510.0 mm) of rain a year, about 6 in (150 mm) more than nearby Los Angeles due to the [orographic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orographic_rainfall) effect created by the [San Gabriel Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gabriel_Mountains). The wettest "rain year" was from July 1940 to June 1941 with 46.32 in (1,176.5 mm) and the driest from July 1960 to June 1961 with 7.18 in (182.4 mm). Wet years are commonly associated with [El Niño](317311.md) warm surface water in the eastern Pacific and dry years with [La Niña](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ni%C3%B1a) cold water conditions. The most rainfall in one month was 19.70 in (500.4 mm) in February 1980. The most rainfall in 24 hours was 7.70 in (195.6 mm) on March 2, 1938.



[La Loma Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Loma_Bridge), built in 1914, spans the [Arroyo Seco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arroyo_Seco_\(Los_Angeles_County\)).

Situated at the base of the San Gabriel Mountains, snow is known to fall occasionally in Pasadena. The heaviest snowfall in Pasadena history occurred on January 11, 1949; 8 inches (20.3 cm) fell at Pasadena's city hall and more than 14 inches (35.6 cm) fell in the foothills above the city.[^cite_note-40] The most recent snowfall in Pasadena was 1 inch (2.5 cm) on February 21, 2019.

On November 30 and December 1, 2011, Pasadena, along with surrounding communities, was struck by a major windstorm caused by Santa Ana winds.[^cite_note-41] The city suffered heavy damage with trees toppled, buildings damaged and even the roof of a gas station torn off.

The official NOAA weather station for the city is located just north-west of the townhall on the other side of Garfield Avenue.

## Economy



Shops in [Old Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Pasadena)

[Old Town Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Pasadena) spans 21 blocks downtown. It boasts shops and a wide variety of restaurants, nightclubs, outdoor cafés, pubs, and comedy clubs. "One Colorado" features renovated historic architecture that attracted the new retail stores and restaurants. This development filled vacant buildings and was the impetus of the revitalization of Old Town on [Colorado Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Boulevard).[^cite_note-73]

[Paseo Colorado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paseo_Colorado) is an open-air mall that covers three city blocks, anchored on the west end by upscale grocery store Gelson's (recently closed), on the east end by Macy's (also closed) and Regal Cinemas centers the middle portion of the mall. Another shopping district is located in the [South Lake Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Lake,_Pasadena,_California) neighborhood. On Lake Avenue, a [Macy's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macy's) department store and furniture gallery is in a registered California historical landmark. The building was originally designed and built as the fourth [Bullock's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bullock's) department store in the mid-1950s (the last freestanding store they constructed).[^cite_note-74]

The Rose Bowl Flea Market is a large [swap meet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swap_meet) that involves thousands of dealers and tens of thousands of visitors in and around the grounds of the Rose Bowl. The merchandise on display ranges from old world antiques to [California pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_pottery) to vintage clothing. The flea market has been held every second Sunday of the month since 1967.[^cite_note-75]

## Arts and culture

### Tournament of Roses Parade



Theme float "2010: A Cut Above the Rest" rolling down Colorado Boulevard during the parade

Main article: [Tournament of Roses Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournament_of_Roses_Parade)

Pasadena is home to the Tournament of Roses Parade, held each year on January 1 (or on January 2, if the 1st falls on a Sunday). The first parade was held in 1890 and was originally sponsored by the [Valley Hunt Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valley_Hunt_Club), a Pasadena [social club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_club). The motivation for having the parade was, as member Professor Charles F. Holder said, "In New York, people are buried in snow. Here our flowers are blooming and our oranges are about to bear. Let's hold a festival to tell the world about our paradise."[^cite_note-Rose-79]

By 1895, the festivities had outgrown the Valley Hunt Club, and the [Tournament of Roses Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Tournament_of_Roses) was formed to take charge of the parade. The Rose Parade, as it is familiarly known, traditionally features elaborate floats, bands and equestrian units. According to the organizers, "Every inch of every float must be covered with flowers, or other natural materials, such as leaves, seeds, or bark. On average a float requires about 100,000 flowers and greenery. Volunteer workers swarm over the floats in the days after Christmas, their hands and clothes covered with glue and petals."[^cite_note-float-80] The most perishable flowers are placed in small vials of water, which are placed onto the float individually. Over the almost 3 hours of the parade, floats, and participants travel over five miles (8.0 km)[^cite_note-float-80] and pass by over one million viewers who traditionally camp out over New Year's Eve to have the best view along the parade route.[^cite_note-weather1-81]

The Rose Parade is satirized by the popular [Doo Dah Parade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doo_Dah_Parade), an annual event that originated in Old Pasadena in 1978, and soon gained national notoriety.[^cite_note-Pasdd-82] *[Reader's Digest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reader's_Digest)* named the Doo Dah Parade "America's Best Parade", and was a recent feature in *50 Places You Must Visit Before You Die!*.[^cite_note-Pasdd-82] It was formerly held around Thanksgiving, a month before the Rose Parade,[^cite_note-83] but the parade is now held in January. In 2011, after 33 years in Pasadena, the parade moved to East Pasadena for the first time.[^cite_note-weather1-81] It features unusual and absurd entrants such as the BBQ & Hibachi Marching Grill Team, the Men of Leisure, and the Bastard Sons of Lee Marvin.[^cite_note-Pasdd-82] Proceeds from the parade's pancake breakfast, T-shirts, and after-party are donated to charity.[^cite_note-Pasdd-82][^cite_note-84]

The Tournament of Roses also auditions local female high school students to be part of the Rose Court. There are in total 7 candidates that advance to the Rose Court, and one is chosen to be the Rose Queen; the others to be the Rose Princesses. The Rose Court's main goal is to support local communities and local stores. They visit small stores owned by local residents to boost the activity of the area and to keep them in the current flow of the economics.

During the Rose Parade, the Rose Court members are also on a float, going through the parade together with the line of other paraders. The Rose Court princesses also represent their own local communities and the high school that they attend.

### Rose Bowl Game

See also: [Rose Bowl Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_Game)

The [Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_\(stadium\)), a [National Historic Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Historic_Landmark), is host of the first and most famous [college football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_football) postseason [bowl game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowl_game), the [Tournament of Roses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Tournament_of_Roses) Rose Bowl Game, every New Year's Day. In 1895, the Tournament of Roses Association was formed to take charge of the parade. In 1902, the association declared that a football game would be added to the day's events. This was the first post-season college football game to be played on New Year's Day and is known as, "The Grandaddy of Them All"; many other football stadiums followed suit. After two decades, the game outgrew its original facility, and a new stadium was constructed in the Arroyo Seco area. The new stadium hosted its first New Year's Day football game in 1923. It was soon christened "The Rose Bowl", as was [the game itself](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_Game).[^cite_note-Rose-79]

### Performing arts



[Pasadena Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Playhouse)

The [Pasadena Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Playhouse), the State Theater of California, is a member supported theater company that celebrated their centennial season in 2018. The theater puts on five shows a year. In 1937, the Pasadena Playhouse established a record as the only [theatre in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theater_in_the_United_States) to have staged the entire [Shakespearean canon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare's_plays).[^cite_note-85] Today, the Playhouse is known for their innovative productions.

The [Pasadena Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Symphony_and_POPS), founded in 1928, offers several concerts a year at the [Ambassador Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_Auditorium) and the Pasadena Pops plays at the [Los Angeles County Arboretum and Botanic Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Arboretum_and_Botanic_Garden). The Civic Center also holds a few traveling [Broadway](725252.md) shows each year.

Boston Court Performing Arts Center, opened in 2003, is near Lake and Colorado. Its resident theatre company, the award-winning The Theatre @ Boston Court, presents four productions a year.[^cite_note-86] Music at the Court presents numerous music concerts each year, ranging from classical to [jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz). The Friends of the Levitt organization presents a free summer concert series in Memorial Park, with the 2008 summer season marking its sixth year.



Pasadena Conservatory of Music

Beckman Auditorium and other venues on the Caltech campus present a wide range of performing arts, lectures, films, classes and entertainment events, primarily during the academic year.[^cite_note-87]

Twice annually, Pasadena's cultural institutions open for free, during ArtNight Pasadena,[^cite_note-88] offering the public a sampling of art, artifacts and music within the city. This has evolved into the yearly PasadenART Weekend,[^cite_note-89] a three-day citywide event which, as of 2007, encompasses ArtNight, ArtWalk, ArtHeritage, ArtMarket, and ArtPerformance, an outdoor music event showcasing emerging and nationally recognized talent. Free concerts take place on multiple stages throughout Old Pasadena.[^cite_note-90]



[Ambassador Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_Auditorium)

[Ambassador Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_Auditorium) was built under the guidance of [Herbert W. Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herbert_W._Armstrong) as both a facility to be used by the [Worldwide Church of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Worldwide_Church_of_God) for religious services and as a concert hall for public performances celebrating the performing arts. In 2007, the native Pasadena band [Ozma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozma_\(band\)) reunited and produced the album *Pasadena* in tribute to the city. The album photos and artwork were shot at the [Colorado Street Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Street_Bridge_\(Pasadena,_California\)).[^cite_note-91]

The 1960s song "[The Little Old Lady from Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Little_Old_Lady_from_Pasadena)" parodies a popular Southern California image of Pasadena as home to a large population of aged eccentrics. In the song, [Jan and Dean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_and_Dean) sing of an elderly lady who drives a powerful "[Super Stock Dodge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dodge_330)" [muscle car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muscle_car) and is "the terror of Colorado Boulevard". The [Dead Kennedys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Kennedys) paid a tribute to this archetypal song in the track "Buzzbomb From Pasadena" in the album *[Give Me Convenience or Give Me Death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Give_Me_Convenience_or_Give_Me_Death)*. Pasadena was also the location of the 2012 film [Project X](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_X_\(2012_film\)).

### Visual arts

A number of artists of national repute, such as [Guy Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Rose), [Alson S. Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alson_S._Clark), [Marion Wachtel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marion_Wachtel) and [Ernest A. Batchelder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernest_A._Batchelder), of the [Arts and Crafts Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_and_Crafts_Movement), made Pasadena their home in the early twentieth century. The formation of the [California Art Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Art_Club), [Stickney Memorial Art School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stickney_Memorial_Art_School) (later known as Pasadena Arts Institute) and the [Pasadena Society of Artists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Society_of_Artists) heralded the city's emergence as a regional center for the visual arts.

### Museums and galleries



[Pasadena Museum of History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Museum_of_History)

Pasadena is home to a number of art museums and public galleries, including the [Norton Simon Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norton_Simon_Museum). The museum's collections include European paintings, sculpture, and tapestry; sculpture from [Southern Asia](21566765.md); and an extensive [sculpture garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sculpture_garden). The museum also has the [contemporary art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_art) collection of its predecessor, the Pasadena Museum of Art, which focused on [modern](163543.md) and contemporary art before being taken over by Simon in the early 1970s.[^cite_note-92]

Preserving and sharing the rich history and culture of Pasadena and its adjacent communities is the [Pasadena Museum of History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Museum_of_History). Located on a campus of 2 acres (8,100 m<sup>2</sup>), it has gardens, a history center, the Finnish Folk Art Museum, the Curtin House, and the Fenyes Mansion, a 1906 [Beaux Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaux-Arts_architecture)-style architectural residence and a Pasadena Cultural Heritage Landmark.[^cite_note-93]

The Pacific Asia Museum, with a garden courtyard in its center, features art from the many countries and cultures of Asia. The nearby [Pasadena Museum of California Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Museum_of_California_Art) (recently closed) hosts changing exhibitions of work by historical and contemporary California artists.[^cite_note-94] The [Armory Center for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armory_Center_for_the_Arts) has an extensive exhibition program as well as serving as a center for art education for all ages.[^cite_note-95] Art Center College of Design offers exhibitions at its Williamson Gallery, as well as frequent displays of student work.[^cite_note-96] Pasadena City College has an art gallery that shows work of professionals as part of their annual artist-in-residence program, as well as exhibiting work by students and faculty.[^cite_note-97]

The [Huntington Library and Botanical Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Huntington_Library), with painting and sculpture galleries, is adjacent to Pasadena in the city of [San Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Marino,_California).[^cite_note-98] The innovative [Kidspace Children's Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidspace_Children's_Museum) is located in [Brookside Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brookside_Park/Arroyo_Terrace,_Pasadena,_California).[^cite_note-99]

### Literature

[Red Hen Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Hen_Press), one of the largest independent literary publishers on the US west coast, is located in Pasadena. The press publishes over twenty titles of poetry, fiction, and nonfiction each year as well as a biannual literary magazine called The Los Angeles Review.

In 2002, [David Ebershoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ebershoff) published the novel *Pasadena*. The novel won praise for its accurate recreation of Pasadena before World War II.[^cite_note-100]

### Bungalow Heaven

Main article: [Bungalow Heaven](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bungalow_Heaven)

Bungalow Heaven is a neighborhood of 800 small Craftsman homes built from 1900 to 1930. Many of these homes are still occupied. Much of the area became a landmark district in 1989,[^cite_note-Yates-101] and annual historic home tours have been conducted since that designation.[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] Bungalow Heaven's borders are Washington Boulevard to the north, [Orange Grove Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Grove_Boulevard) to the south, Mentor Avenue to the west, and Chester Avenue to the east.[^cite_note-104] The neighborhood is usually extended to Lake Avenue to the west and Hill Avenue to the east.[^cite_note-Yates-101][^cite_note-105] Famed architects [Greene and Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greene_and_Greene) built several of their Japanese-inspired bungalows in Pasadena, including the [Gamble House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamble_House_\(Pasadena,_California\)); the style of the homes in Bungalow Heaven show the effects of their success.

### Orange Grove Boulevard

Main article: [Orange Grove Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Grove_Boulevard)



Tournament House

The [Norton Simon Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norton_Simon_Museum) is at the intersection of Orange Grove and Colorado Boulevards. This corner is the official start of the Rose Parade route and the museum can be quite clearly seen every year during the parade television broadcast.

Orange Grove Boulevard is one of several exclusive residential districts in Pasadena, and has been a home for the rich and famous since the early 20th century. Because of the number of landmark mansions, the street earned the name [Millionaire's Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millionaire's_Row), an appropriate sobriquet considering that the estates that once lined this spacious boulevard and the surrounding neighborhood read like a [Who's Who](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who's_Who) of American consumer products.

### Historical estates

The maker of [Wrigley's chewing gum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wm._Wrigley_Jr._Company), [William Wrigley Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Wrigley_Jr.)'s, substantial home was offered to the city of Pasadena after Mrs. Wrigley's death in 1958, under the condition that their home would be the Rose Parade's permanent headquarters.[^cite_note-106] The stately [Tournament House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournament_House) stands today, and serves as the headquarters for the Tournament of Roses Parade.[^cite_note-107] [Adolphus Busch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adolphus_Busch), co-founder of [Anheuser-Busch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anheuser-Busch), brewer of [Budweiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Budweiser_\(Anheuser-Busch\)) beer, established the first of a series of [Busch Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Busch_Gardens) in Pasadena. When Busch died at his Pasadena estate, his wife generously offered the property to the City of Pasadena, an offer the city inexplicably refused. [Henry Markham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Markham), who lived adjacent to Busch, was the 18th Governor of the state of California (1891–1895) and wrote *Pasadena: Its Early Years*.[^cite_note-108] The home of David Gamble, son of consumer product maker James Gamble of [Procter & Gamble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procter_&_Gamble), is located on the north end of Orange Grove Boulevard.

The [Gamble House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamble_House_\(Pasadena,_California\)), an [American Craftsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Craftsman) masterpiece, was built in 1908,[^cite_note-109] by architects [Charles and Henry Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greene_and_Greene), as an exemplification of their [ultimate bungalow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_bungalow). It is open to the public as both an architectural conservancy and museum.[^cite_note-110]

The [Gamble House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamble_House_\(Pasadena,_California\)) is a [California Historical Landmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Historical_Landmark) and a National Historic Landmark on the [National Register of Historic Places](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places). The home of Anna Bissell McCay, daughter of [carpet sweeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpet_sweeper) magnate Melville [Bissell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bissell), is a four-story [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_architecture) home, on the border of South Pasadena. Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thaddeus_S._C._Lowe)'s home of 24,000 square feet (2,200 m<sup>2</sup>) was on South Orange Grove. Ron Hubbard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._Ron_Hubbard), the founder of [Scientology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientology). Parsons died in an explosion while testing a new rocket fuel in his Pasadena home laboratory, in 1952.[^cite_note-117]

## Government



[Pasadena City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_City_Hall)

The city charter specifies a city council/manager form of government. In addition to city manager, the city council appoints the city attorney and prosecutor, and the city clerk. The city manager oversees 13 departments including Water and Power and Human Services. The city has municipal operating companies including the Rose Bowl Operating Company and the Pasadena Community Access Corporation.[^cite_note-pn-124] The city is one of three city members of the Burbank-Glendale-Pasadena Airport Authority, which is a joint powers agency that owns [Hollywood Burbank Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Burbank_Airport).[^cite_note-125]

According to the city's most recent Comprehensive Annual Financial Report of 2009, the city's various funds had \$583.0 million in revenues, \$518.1 million in expenditures, \$954,199,439 in net assets, \$732.3 million in total liabilities, and \$118,261,490 in cash and investments.[^cite_note-Finance-126][^cite_note-127]

The city operates its own public health department and alongside [Berkeley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkeley,_California), [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach,_California), and [Vernon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernon,_California), are the only cities in California doing so.[^cite_note-128] Dental and orthodontic services in Pasadena are provided through a network of private practices that complement public health infrastructure and support routine and specialized oral care needs.[^cite_note-129] In 2016, the Pasadena Public Health Department received accreditation by the national Public Health Accreditation Board (PHAB).[^cite_note-130] The city is primarily served by [Huntington Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_Hospital), located adjacent to the downtown area. The eastern half of the city was formerly served by [St. Luke Medical Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Luke_Medical_Center) until its closure in 2002.[^cite_note-131] The [Los Angeles County Department of Health Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Department_of_Health_Services) operates the Monrovia Health Center in [Monrovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monrovia,_California), serving portions of Pasadena.[^cite_note-132]



[Pasadena Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Police_Department_\(California\))

The [Pasadena Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Police_Department_\(California\)) serves most of the city of Pasadena. Unincorporated portions of the city are part of Los Angeles County and are served by the [Los Angeles County Sheriff's Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Sheriff's_Department) (LASD) and the Altadena Station in Altadena serves nearby portions of Pasadena.[^cite_note-133]

The Pasadena Fire Department moved into its first formal and permanent station in 1889. Before that they had been housed in a ramshackle structure and summoned by the church bell. There were 24 firemen for two shifts.[^cite_note-134] As of 2016, the Pasadena Fire Department is an [ISO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insurance_Services_Office) Class 1 department, consisting of 181 full-time employees (161 shift personnel, 20 administrative personnel) and eight modern fire stations that serve an area in a radius of 60 miles (97 km).[^cite_note-135][^cite_note-136]

The Department is dispatched by the Verdugo Fire Communications Center and is one of the three agencies that oversees its operations.[^cite_note-Verdugo_Fire_History-137]

## Education



[California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology), considered to be one of the world's most prestigious universities

The [California Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Institute_of_Technology) (Caltech) is in the southern-central area of Pasadena. Tyson School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Permanente_Bernard_J._Tyson_School_of_Medicine) matriculated its first class in 2020, and waived tuition and fees for its first 5 classes.[^cite_note-142] The school is highly competitive. ### Radio

Pasadena has been home to a number of notable radio stations. In 1967 radio iconoclasts Tom and Raechel Donahue took over an aging studio in the basement of the Pasadena Presbyterian Church and introduced Los Angeles to FM freeform radio. Broadcasting under the [KPPC-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPPC_\(defunct\)) call sign at 106.7 FM it quickly became the voice of the counterculture and provided the soundtrack to LA's hippie era.[^cite_note-155] Early on-air personalities included [Michael McKean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_McKean), [David Lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Lander), [Harry Shearer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harry_Shearer), and [Dr. Demento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr._Demento). The staff was fired en masse in 1971 and the station lost its distinctive personality.[^cite_note-156] KPPC later became [KROQ-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KROQ-FM), which is owned by [Audacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audacy).

Today the primary radio station in Pasadena goes by the call sign [KPCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KPCC_\(radio_station\)) located at 89.3 FM. Broadcasting from the Mohn Broadcast Center on South Raymond Avenue (and no longer on the Pasadena City College campus), this public radio station carries many shows from [National Public Radio](29697232.md) but maintains an independent streak, committing a large chunk of air time to presenting local and state news. Accordingly, the station has received numerous awards for journalistic excellence and continues to be an important part of the city's heritage.[^cite_note-157] [WilsonBlock1000 Radio](https://www.spreaker.com/user/wilsonblock1000-radio--6652226) conducts audio interviews with local artists and covers events related to the local music scene. Their name derives from Wilson Ave. in Pasadena's Bungalow Heaven neighborhood district.

### Newspapers and magazines

Pasadena's largest and oldest newspaper is the *[Pasadena Star-News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Star-News)*, first published in 1884. The daily newspaper covers local politics, sports, and other news, and also publishes the *Rose Magazine*.[^cite_note-158]

*[Pasadena Now](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Now)* is a community news website covering stories in the community since 2004.[^cite_note-159] *[Pasadena Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Weekly)*, an alternative weekly, has been published since 1984.[^cite_note-160] *Colorado Boulevard Newspaper* is a grassroots news outlet launched in 2013 and covers Pasadena, Altadena and other cities in the San Gabriel Valley, with stories written by residents of the communities. *[Pasadena Outlook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outlook_Newspapers_Group)* covers news from non-profit organizations, social events, private K-12 school and private high school sports coverage. It was launched in 2007.

Pasadena is served by various bus services. [Pasadena Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Transit) serves as the main public bus service for the city. ##### Rail



A Line Memorial Park Station

Pasadena is served by the [Los Angeles Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_County_Metropolitan_Transportation_Authority) [A Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)) [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_rail), which originates at the [Downtown Long Beach station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Long_Beach_station) in [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach,_California) and [Pomona-North station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomona-North_station) in [Pomona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomona,_California). Opening in 2003 as the [Gold Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L_Line_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)),[^cite_note-162] there are six A Line stations in Pasadena: [Fillmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fillmore_station), [Del Mar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Mar_station), and [Memorial Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memorial_Park_station) stations in [Old Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Pasadena), [Lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_station_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)) station in Downtown, [Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_station_\(Los_Angeles_Metro\)) and [Sierra Madre Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_Villa_station) stations. Construction began in June 2010 to extend the Gold Line east through several additional foothill communities of the San Gabriel Valley, including Arcadia, Monrovia, Duarte, Irwindale, Azusa, Glendora, San Dimas, La Verne, and Pomona. It began revenue service on March 5, 2016.[^cite_note-163]

Pasadena is also served by various bus services. [Pasadena Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Transit) exclusively serves the city, while bus services [Amtrak Thruway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Thruway) [19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Thruway#19), [Foothill Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foothill_Transit), [LADOT Commuter Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LADOT_Commuter_Express) [549](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Department_of_Transportation#549), [Metro Local](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metro_Bus), and [Metro Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Metro_Bus) also serve Pasadena.[^cite_note-164]

#### Trains



[Santa Fe Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Depot_\(Pasadena\)) c. 1900

Pasadena was served by the [Los Angeles and San Gabriel Valley Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_and_San_Gabriel_Valley_Railroad), which was acquired by the [Atchison, Topeka and Santa Fe Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atchison,_Topeka_and_Santa_Fe_Railway) in 1906, at a [Santa Fe Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Depot_\(Pasadena\)) in downtown when the Second District was opened in 1887.[^cite_note-train-28] In 1925, the historical and traditionally styled station in Pasadena was opened.[^cite_note-train-28] Originally, the Second District was an invaluable line; it served manufacturing and agricultural businesses throughout the entire San Gabriel Valley. But longer trains had great difficulty climbing the precipitous 2.2% grade at [Arroyo Seco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arroyo_Seco_\(Los_Angeles_County\)), between Pasadena and Los Angeles, requiring the costly addition of extra locomotives. The still-used Third District opened in 1888, just a year after the Second District, and rapidly took over most of the longer freight trains.[^cite_note-train-28]

The Second District and the Pasadena Depot became well known; up to 26 passenger trains went through Pasadena every day. To avoid the media in Los Angeles, many celebrities chose to use Pasadena as their main train station, bringing it an association with old Hollywood.[^cite_note-train-28][^cite_note-La_times-165]

[Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) took over passenger rail operations in 1971, serving Pasadena with trains such as the *[Southwest Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Chief)*, *[Las Vegas Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Vegas_Limited)*, and *[Desert Wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Wind)*. On January 15, 1994, the final *[Southwest Chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Chief)* train arrived in Pasadena.[^cite_note-La_times-165] ATSF sold the line between Los Angeles and San Bernardino via Pasadena (known as the "second division") following the [1994 Northridge earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Northridge_earthquake) which damaged a bridge in [Arcadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcadia,_California) used by the line.[^cite_note-166] (Now the *Southwest Chief* operates over the [transcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Transcon) via Fullerton.) The LACMTA A Line uses the [right-of-way](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right-of-way_\(transportation\)) after rebuilding the route to accommodate light rail in 2003. The old depot is still visible at the [Del Mar station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Mar_\(LACMTA_station\)), though it has since been converted into a restaurant. Electrified Light Rail was the preferred alternative to Metrolink or similar style rail service due to the city of Pasadena voting against any further diesel locomotives traversing through the city.[^cite_note-167] The construction of the Gold Line also allowed the closure of the former railroad crossing along Colorado Boulevard which meant that motorists and the Rose Parade would no longer be hindered by trains.

#### Airports

[Hollywood Burbank Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Burbank_Airport) in nearby [Burbank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burbank,_California) serves as the regional airport for Pasadena. The airport is owned and operated by the Burbank-Glendale-Pasadena Airport Authority. The airport is under the control of the governments of the three cities named. While most destinations from Hollywood Burbank Airport are within the United States, [Los Angeles International Airport](18131.md) and [Ontario International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ontario_International_Airport) are the major airports that provide domestic and international commercial service. Other nearby airports with commercial service include [Long Beach Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach_Airport) and [John Wayne Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Wayne_Airport).

#### Freeways and highways

Four freeways run through Pasadena, and Pasadena is a [control city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Control_city) for all of them. The most important is the [Foothill Freeway (I-210)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_210_and_State_Route_210_\(California\)) which enters the northwestern portion of the city from [La Cañada Flintridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ca%C3%B1ada_Flintridge,_California). The Foothill Freeway initially runs due south, passing the [Rose Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Bowl_Stadium) before its junction with the [Ventura Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_134). At this interchange, the Foothill Freeway shifts its alignment and direction, becoming an east-west freeway, exiting the city on its eastern boundary before entering [Arcadia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arcadia,_California). The Foothill Freeway connects Pasadena with [San Fernando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando,_California) (westbound) and [San Bernardino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Bernardino,_California) (eastbound).



[Foothill Freeway (I-210)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_210_and_State_Route_210_\(California\)) as seen from the Metro L Line [Sierra Madre Villa Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_Villa_\(LACMTA_station\))

The [Ventura Freeway (SR 134)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_134) starts at the junction of the [Foothill Freeway (I-210)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_210_and_State_Route_210_\(California\)) at the edge of downtown Pasadena and travels westward. This freeway is the main connector to the [Hollywood Burbank Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_Burbank_Airport) and the [San Fernando Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Fernando_Valley).

A spur of the [Long Beach Freeway (SR 710 in Pasadena)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_710) is also located in Pasadena. The Long Beach Freeway was intended to connect [Long Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Beach,_CA) to Pasadena but a gap, known as the [South Pasadena Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_710#The_South_Pasadena_Gap), between [Alhambra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alhambra,_California) and Pasadena has not been completed due to legal battles primarily involving the city of South Pasadena. The spur starts at the junction of the [Ventura Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_134) and [Foothill Freeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_210_and_State_Route_210_\(California\)) and travels south along the eastern edge of Old Pasadena with two exits for [Colorado Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_Boulevard) and Del Mar Boulevard before ending at an at-grade intersection with California Boulevard. Efforts to complete the Long Beach Freeway were met with strong opposition, including the possibility of using advanced tunneling technologies to overcome objections.[^cite_note-168] The gap will no longer be constructed, with the \$780 million earmarked for constructing the gap now allocated towards local infrastructure improvements.[^cite_note-169] Pasadena is exploring options on the future of the spur.[^cite_note-170]



Colorado Street Bridge seen from the Arroyo Seco below

The [Arroyo Seco Parkway (SR 110)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arroyo_Seco_Parkway), also known as the Pasadena Freeway, was the first freeway in California, connecting Los Angeles with Pasadena alongside the [Arroyo Seco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arroyo_Seco_\(Los_Angeles_County\)) and is the primary access to [Downtown Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Los_Angeles). The freeway enters the southern part of the city from South Pasadena. Only one exit is actually inside city limits, the southbound exit connecting to State Street with access to [Fair Oaks Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Oaks_Avenue). At Glenarm Street, the freeway ends and the four-lane Arroyo Parkway continues northward to Old Pasadena.

Three state highways enter the city of Pasadena. Arroyo Parkway (SR 110), maintained by the city of Pasadena, runs from the termination of the Pasadena Freeway at Glenarm Street to Colorado Boulevard in Old Town Pasadena. While Arroyo Parkway continues north two more blocks, SR 110 ends at Holly Street.

[Rosemead Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosemead_Boulevard) (formerly [SR 19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_19)) is a state highway in [unincorporated Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Pasadena,_California) from Huntington Drive to [Foothill Boulevard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foothill_Boulevard_\(Southern_California\)).

A portion of the [Angeles Crest Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angeles_Crest_Highway) ([SR 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_2)) in the [San Gabriel Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Gabriel_Mountains) cuts through Pasadena near the Angeles Crest Ranger Station. This 2-mile (3.2 km) stretch of highway in the Angeles National Forest is north of [La Cañada Flintridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ca%C3%B1ada_Flintridge,_California) and west of [Mount Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Wilson_\(California\)) and is approximately 3,000 feet (910 m) in elevation.

Historic [U.S. Route 66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_66_\(California\)) ran through Pasadena until it was decommissioned in 1964. The historic highway entered Pasadena from the east on Colorado Boulevard and then jogged south on Arroyo Parkway before becoming part of the [Pasadena Freeway (SR 110)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena_Freeway).

The intersection of [Fair Oaks Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fair_Oaks_Avenue_\(Pasadena,_California\)) and Colorado Boulevard in Old Pasadena is the zero-zero, east-west, north-south postal division of Pasadena.

### Water and power



Water and Light Fountain at the historic [Glenarm Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenarm_Power_Plant). Designed by Harold H. Lewis in 1938.

The city Water and Power Department (PWP) provides services to an area encompassing 23 sq mi (60 km<sup>2</sup>) that includes areas outside of the city proper, including unincorporated areas of southern Altadena, East Pasadena, Chapman Woods, and East San Gabriel. PWP has operated the [Glenarm Power Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenarm_Power_Plant) for over 110 years.

Pasadena created the Pasadena Municipal Light and Power Department in 1906. Expanding continued and more generating capacity was expanded and the city then offered power to commercial customers in 1908, and bought out [Southern California Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_California_Edison)'s Pasadena operations in 1920.[^cite_note-power-171] In 1911, the city began condemnation actions against a number of small, local water companies. In 1912, the Water Department was created; in 1913, it began actual operations. The city continued to acquire small, local water companies for several decades afterwards, usually *en toto*, such as the Pasadena Lake Vineyard and Land Company, and sometimes in part, such as Las Flores Water Company's southern portions and San Gabriel Valley Water Company's operations in the southern reaches of Pasadena. In 1967, the Water Department and the Light and Power department were consolidated into the "Pasadena Water and Power Department" (PWP).[^cite_note-power-171]

It operates a number of water wells, has a spreading ground for the capture of surface water from the Arroyo Seco, and purchases surface water from MWDSC. A number of wells on the west side of the service area had become contaminated with volatile organic chemicals and perchlorate and had to be shut down several years. A treatment plant was built to remove these chemicals which began operation in July 2011.

## Wildlife

Pasadena has a large, non-indigenous population of [naturalized parrots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feral_parrots). According to the "Parrot Project of Los Angeles",[^cite_note-172] the parrots are of at least six species.[^cite_note-CApp-173][^cite_note-par-174][^cite_note-175] Some residents have come to enjoy the birds as part of the city's unique culture,[^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] while others consider them to be loud pests. There are many theories explaining how the parrots came to inhabit Pasadena.[^cite_note-178] A widely accepted story is that they were part of the stock that were set free for their survival from the large pet emporium at Simpson's Garden Town on East Colorado Boulevard, which burned down in 1959.[^cite_note-par-174][^cite_note-ABC-179]
