**Sinaloa** (Spanish pronunciation: [\[sinaˈloa\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/58/Sinaloa.ogg/Sinaloa.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/5/58/Sinaloa.ogg/Sinaloa.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), officially the **_Estado Libre y Soberano de Sinaloa_** (English: Free and Sovereign State of Sinaloa), is one of the 31 states which, along with [Mexico City](18987.md), compose the [Federal Entities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Mexico) of [Mexico](3966054.md). It is divided into [20 municipalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Sinaloa), and its capital and largest city is [Culiacán Rosales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culiac%C3%A1n). Other large cities include [Mazatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazatl%C3%A1n), [Los Mochis](14675780.md), [Guamúchil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guam%C3%BAchil,_Sinaloa), and [Guasave](736658.md).

Sinaloa is located in northwest Mexico and is bordered by the states of [Sonora](83750.md) to the northwest, [Chihuahua](23962301.md) to the north, [Durango](222234.md) to the east, and [Nayarit](222245.md) to the southeast. To the west, Sinaloa faces [Baja California Sur](199801.md), across the [Gulf of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_California). Its natural geography is divided by the [Sierra Madre Occidental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_Occidental), fertile river valleys, and a broad coastal plain along the Gulf of California. The state covers an area of 58,328 square kilometers (22,521 sq mi) and includes the islands of Palmito Verde, Palmito de la Virgen, Altamura, Santa María, Saliaca, Macapule, and San Ignacio.&#x20;

Its economy is based on agriculture, fisheries, livestock breeding, tourism, mining, and food processing. Culturally, Sinaloa has a strong musical tradition, particularly in the [banda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_music) and [norteño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte%C3%B1o_\(music\)) genres, its cuisine, and the heritage of indigenous peoples such as the [Yaqui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaqui) and the [Yoreme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoreme).

## Etymology

*Sinaloa* combines two words from the [Cahita language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cahita_language): *sina* ('[pitahaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pitahaya) plant'), and *lobola* ('rounded'); *sinalobola* was shortened to *sinaloa*.[^cite_note-11] This most popular etymology is attributed to [Eustaquio Buelna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eustaquio_Buelna?action=edit\&redlink=1). Another etymology attributed to Pablo Lizárraga is Mexica *cintli* ('dry corn and cob') and *ololoa* ('to pile up'), and to locative, 'where they pile up or store corn on the cob'. Yet another etymology from Héctor R. Olea combines Cahia *sina* with the locative *ro* from the [Purépecha language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pur%C3%A9pecha_language) and *a* from Nahuatl *atl* ('water'), thus 'place of pithayas in the water'.[^cite_note-12]

## History

Inauguration of the [Huites Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huites_Dam).

Sinaloa belongs to the northern limit of [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica). To the north, the [Fuerte River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerte_River) marks the region known as [Aridoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aridoamerica), which includes the deserts and arid regions of [northern Mexico](48157875.md) and [southwestern United States](179571.md). Before European contact, the territory of Sinaloa was inhabited by groups such as the [Cahitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A1hita), the Tahues, the [Acaxees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acaxee), the [Xiximes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiximes), the [Totorames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Totorame_language), the [Achires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achires) and the [Guasaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guasave_language).[^cite_note-\:0-13]

In 1531, [Nuño Beltrán de Guzmán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nu%C3%B1o_de_Guzm%C3%A1n), with a force of over 10,000 men, established a Spanish and allied Indian outpost at San Miguel de Culiacán. Over the next decade, the Cahíta suffered severe depopulation from conquest, smallpox and other diseases brought by Europeans.[^cite_note-\:1-14] The northern region of the state was colonized by [Francisco de Ibarra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_Ibarra), who founded a settlement in 1563 that later became [El Fuerte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Fuerte,_Sinaloa), named after the fort built there in 1610 under the direction of [Diego Martínez de Hurdaide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diego_Mart%C3%ADnez_de_Hurdaide?action=edit\&redlink=1).

The Spanish organized Sinaloa as part of the *gobierno* of [Nueva Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Galicia). In 1564, the area was realigned: the area of Culiacán and [Cosalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosal%C3%A1) remained in control of Nueva Galicia, while the areas to the north, south, and west were made part of the newly formed [Nueva Vizcaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nueva_Vizcaya,_New_Spain) province, making the Culiacán area an exclave of Nueva Galicia. The first capital of Nueva Vizcaya was located in [San Sebastián](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Sebasti%C3%A1n,_Sinaloa?action=edit\&redlink=1), near [Copala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copala,_Sinaloa), but was moved to [Durango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durango,_Durango) in 1583.[^cite_note-15]

Starting in 1599, [Jesuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Society_of_Jesus) [missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_\(Christianity\)) spread out from a base at what is now [Sinaloa de Leyva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinaloa_de_Leyva) and by 1610, the Spanish influence had been extended to the northern edge of Sinaloa. In 1601, the Jesuits' movement into the eastern part of Sinaloa prompted the [Acaxee Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acaxee_Rebellion).[^cite_note-16] The Spanish eventually managed to subdue the indigenous peoples of the [Sierra Madre Occidental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_Occidental) region and executed 48 Acaxee leaders.[^cite_note-17] An account of the region's evangelization and colonial history was written by the Jesuit missionary [Andrés Pérez de Ribas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_P%C3%A9rez_de_Ribas) in *Historia de los Triunfos de Nuestra Santa Fe*, published in 1645.[^cite_note-18] During the late colonial period, Sinaloa formed part of the province of [New Navarre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Navarre) and later as part of the [intendancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intendant) of Arizpe within the [Provincias Internas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provincias_Internas), a frontier administrative region of New Spain established to consolidate control over the northwest as part of the [Bourbon Reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bourbon_Reforms).[^cite_note-19]

After the [Mexican War of Independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_War_of_Independence), Sinaloa was joined with Sonora as the [Estado de Occidente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estado_de_Occidente), but in 1830 it separated to become [the 20th state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Territorial_evolution_of_Mexico) of the [First Mexican Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mexican_Republic), with [Culiacán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culiac%C3%A1n) as its capital.[^cite_note-20] During the [Second French Intervention in Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_French_Intervention_in_Mexico) (1862–1867), several battles occurred in Sinaloa, including the [defense of Mazatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Mazatl%C3%A1n) and the [Battle of San Pedro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Pedro), where republican forces led by [Antonio Rosales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Rosales) resisted French and Mexican imperial troops to maintain control of the Pacific coast.[^cite_note-Nakayama-21][^cite_note-Niox-22]

The [Porfiriato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porfiriato) era was marked by the administration of Francisco Cañedo, who served multiple non-consecutive terms from 1877 to 1909. Cañedo oversaw modernization efforts in Sinaloa, including the expansion of railroads, the improvement of Mazatlán's port, and the promotion of agricultural exports. [Los Mochis](14675780.md) was founded in 1893 by American settlers led by Albert K. Owen as part of the [Topolobampo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topolobampo_\(colony\)) cooperative agricultural and industrial colony, envisioned around [utopian socialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utopian_socialism).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] During the [Mexican Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Revolution), Sinaloa saw early clashes between [Maderistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maderism) and Díaz's [Federal Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Army), and later naval engagements between [Huertistas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoriano_Huerta) and [Constitutionalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionalists_in_the_Mexican_Revolution) for control of the port of [Topolobampo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Topolobampo).[^cite_note-25]

In the postrevolutionary period, extensive irrigation projects such as the Sanalona, Miguel Hidalgo, and Humaya dams converted vast tracts of arid land into fertile farmland. State investment, credit, and [land reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_reform_in_Mexico) promoted large-scale production of wheat, rice, tomatoes, and other export-oriented crops, which led to the state being named "the granary of Mexico". Industrial and urban development accelerated in cities such as [Culiacán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culiac%C3%A1n), [Mazatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazatl%C3%A1n), and [Los Mochis](14675780.md), which became regional centers of agribusinesses, food processing, and tourism.[^cite_note-26]

Beginning in the mid-20th century, poppy and cannabis cultivation took root in the remote mountainous regions of the Sierra Madre Occidental, giving rise to a narcotics economy that would later shape the [Sinaloa Cartel](16881931.md), one of Mexico's largest criminal organizations.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] In 2008, the federal government launched [Operation Sinaloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Sinaloa), deploying military and police forces to curb cartel activity across the region during the [Mexican drug war](8442323.md).[^cite_note-31] Since then, the state has experienced recurring cycles of violence, most markedly the [Battle of Culiacán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Culiac%C3%A1n) in October 2019, and the [2023 Sinaloa unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Sinaloa_unrest), when clashes between security forces and cartel members brought the state capital to a standstill. Ongoing [infighting in the Sinaloa Cartel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infighting_in_the_Sinaloa_Cartel) has continued to drive instability in parts of the region.[^cite_note-32]

## Geography



Botanic garden "Benjamín F. Johnston" of Parque Sinaloa



[El Fuerte, Sinaloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Fuerte,_Sinaloa) seen from the [Fuerte River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerte_River)

The coastal plain is a narrow strip of land that stretches along the length of the state and lies between the [Gulf of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_California) and the foothills of the [Sierra Madre Occidental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_Occidental) mountain range, which dominates the eastern part of the state. Sinaloa is traversed by many rivers including the [Fuerte River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuerte_River), [Culiacán River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culiac%C3%A1n_River), [Sinaloa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinaloa_River), along with smaller rivers and tributaries such as the [Baluarte River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baluarte_River), [Tamazula River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamazula_River), and [Humaya River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humaya_River), which flow from the Sierra Madre toward the [Gulf of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_California). These fertile valleys and surrounding uplands form the [Sinaloan dry forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinaloan_dry_forests) and the [Sonoran–Sinaloan transition subtropical dry forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sonoran%E2%80%93Sinaloan_transition_subtropical_dry_forest) ecoregions.[^cite_note-33]

Sinaloa has a warm climate on the coast; a moderately warm climate in the valleys and foothills; a moderately cold climate in the lower mountains, and a cold climate in the higher elevations. Its weather characteristics range from subtropical and tropical on coastal plains to cold in the nearby mountains. Temperatures range from 22 °C (72 °F) to 43 °C (109 °F) with rain and thunderstorms during the rainy season (June to October) and dry conditions throughout most of the year. Its average annual [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation) is 790 millimetres.[^cite_note-34]

Numerous plant and animal species are found in Sinaloa. Notable among the tree species is the [elephant tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bursera_microphylla) (*Bursera microphylla*).[^cite_note-35] Notable fauna include the [black-throated magpie-jay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black-throated_magpie-jay) (*Calocitta colliei*), the [Mexican bobcat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_bobcat) (*Lynx rufus escuinapae*), and the [Mexican golden trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_golden_trout) (*Oncorhynchus chrysogaster*). Reptiles such as the [long-tailed rattlesnake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crotalus_stejnegeri) (*Crotalus stejnegeri*) and the [black-bellied slider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baja_California_slider) (*Trachemys nebulosa*) inhabit the drier lowlands, while mammals like the [mesquite mouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesquite_mouse) (*Peromyscus merriami*) are found across the state’s inlands. [Protected natural areas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protected_natural_areas_of_Mexico) include [Marismas Nacionales–San Blas mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marismas_Nacionales%E2%80%93San_Blas_mangroves) and [Meseta de Cacaxtla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meseta_de_Cacaxtla_Flora_and_Fauna_Protection_Area).[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

Sinaloa scores highly in socioeconomic development among Mexican states. In 2023, the state [ranked seventh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Mexican_states_by_Human_Development_Index) among Mexico’s 32 federal entities in [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) (HDI), with a score of 0.828.[^cite_note-38] According to the 2020 census, Sinaloa has a population of 3,026,943, 60% of whom reside in the capital city of [Culiacán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culiac%C3%A1n) and the municipalities of [Mazatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazatl%C3%A1n) and [Ahome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahome). It is a young state in terms of population, with 56% of its population younger than 30 years of age.[^cite_note-INEGI-39]

Other demographic particulars report 87% of the state practices the [Catholic faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church). 9.35% identified as indigenous,[^cite_note-40] with 1% of those over five years of age speak an indigenous language alongside Spanish.[^cite_note-41] Sinaloa’s [indigenous population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Mexico) includes mainly the [Yoreme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoreme) (Mayo) people in the northern portion of the state, and groups of [Rarámuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rar%C3%A1muri) (Tarahumara) and [Yoeme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoeme) (Yaqui) people. Life expectancy in the state follows the national trend of higher rates for women than for men, with a difference of almost 6 years in Sinaloa: 74.2 and 68.3 years, respectively.[^cite_note-42]

In terms of ethnic composition, Sinaloa has received large historic waves of immigration from Europe (mainly Spain, the United Kingdom, Ireland, France, Germany, Austria, Italy, and Russia) and Asia (namely China, Japan, South Korea, the Philippines, Armenia, Lebanon, and Syria). The last two countries also make up most of the [Arab Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Mexican) community in the state. In recent years, retirees from the U.S., Canada, Australia, and South America have arrived and made Sinaloa their home.[^cite_note-43]

There was also a sizable influx of [Ashkenazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashkenazi_Jews) and [Sephardi Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sephardi_Jews) in the first decades of the twentieth century. [Greeks](42056.md) form a notable presence in Sinaloa, where one can find local cuisine with kalamari and a few Greek Orthodox churches along the state's coast.[^cite_note-44] According to the 2020 Census, 1.39% of Sinaloa's population identified as Black, [Afro-Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afro-Mexican), or having African descent.[^cite_note-45]

Sinaloenses have moved to the United States in large numbers since 1970; a large community lives in the twin towns of [Indio, California](107924.md) and [Coachella, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coachella,_California) about 40 km (25 miles) east of the resort city of [Palm Springs, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palm_Springs,_California) in the Colorado Desert of [Southern California](62520.md).

## Government and politics



[Federal electoral districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_electoral_districts_of_Mexico) of Sinaloa.

The current [governor of Sinaloa](3001837.md) is [Graciela Domínguez Nava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graciela_Dom%C3%ADnguez_Nava), who is serving as interim.[^cite_note-GDN-2] [Rubén Rocha Moya](67890922.md), who has a [mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_\(politics\)) until 31 October 2026, left his office on 23 August 2026.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-RRM-3] The state is represented in the [Mexican Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Congress) by three [Senators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_\(Mexico\)) in the [upper house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_house) and fourteen [federal deputies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_\(legislator\)) in the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house). Deputies are elected to serve for a three-year term.[^cite_note-49]

### Municipalities

Main article: [Municipalities of Sinaloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipalities_of_Sinaloa)

Sinaloa is subdivided into 20 *[municipios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipio_\(Mexico\))*. Each municipality has a [city council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayuntamiento) (*ayuntamiento*), headed by the municipal president. Municipalities in Sinaloa have some administrative autonomy from the state according to the 115th article of the [1917 Constitution of Mexico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1917_Constitution_of_Mexico).[^cite_note-constitution-50] Every three years, citizens elect a [municipal president](2907414.md) ([Spanish](26825.md): *presidente municipal*) by a [plurality voting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plurality_voting) system who heads a concurrently elected municipal council (*ayuntamiento*) responsible for providing all the public services for their constituents. The municipal council consists of a variable number of trustees and councillors (*regidores y síndicos*).[^cite_note-51] Municipalities are responsible for public services (such as water and sewerage), street lighting, public safety, traffic, and the maintenance of public parks, gardens and cemeteries.[^cite_note-Mexicolaws-52] They may also assist the state and federal governments in education, emergency fire and medical services, environmental protection and maintenance of monuments and historical landmarks. Since 1984, they have had the power to collect [property taxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_tax) and [user fees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User_fee), although more funds are obtained from the state and federal governments than from their own income.[^cite_note-Mexicolaws-52][^cite_note-53]

## Economy

As of 2022, Sinaloa had a nominal GDP of approximately 668 billion pesos (US \$33 billion), representing about 2.35% of Mexico’s national total, with a GDP per capita of US \$10,840 and a PPP per capita of US \$ 22,630.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] The main economic activities of Sinaloa are agriculture, fishing, livestock breeding, tourism and [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing).[^cite_note-56] Sinaloa has on its license plates the image of a [tomato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato), as the state is widely recognized for harvesting this particular fruit in great abundance from Los Mochis in the North to Culiacán in the central region of the state. Agriculture produce aside from tomatoes include [cotton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cotton), [beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bean), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [sorghum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum), [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato), [soybeans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soybean), [mangos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut) and [squash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squash_\(fruit\)).[^cite_note-57] Sinaloa is the most prominent state in Mexico in terms of agriculture and is known as "Mexico's breadbasket". Livestock produce meat, sausages, cheese, milk, and sour cream. Additionally, Sinaloa has the second-largest fishing fleet in the country.[^cite_note-58] Tourism plays an important role in Sinaloa’s economy, driven by destinations such as Mazatlán, known for its beaches, historic center, and [ its carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnaval_de_Mazatl%C3%A1n), along with [ecotourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism). Mining has long been an important part of the economy, with [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), [gold](12240.md), [silver](27119.md), [lead](17747.md), [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese), and other mineral deposits extracted in the Sierra Madre Occidental with mining centers such as [Cosalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosal%C3%A1), [Concordia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concordia,_Sinaloa), and [El Rosario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Rosario,_Sinaloa) dating back to the colonial period. Sinaloa hosts major national companies including [Sukarne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukarne), [Coppel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coppel), and [Casa Ley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_Ley).

## Education



[Autonomous University of Sinaloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_University_of_Sinaloa)'s campus in Culiacán

In terms of education, average schooling is 8.27 years; 4.2% of those aged 15 or older are illiterate, and 3.18% of children under 14 do not attend school.[^cite_note-59] The state's literacy rate in 2020 was 96.3%.[^cite_note-60]

The [Autonomous University of Sinaloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_University_of_Sinaloa) (UAS), founded in 1873 as the Liceo Rosales, and attaining full autonomy in 1965, is the state's main public university and the largest, with multiple campuses across the state. Other institutions of higher education include [Universidad Autónoma de Sinaloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Aut%C3%B3noma_de_Sinaloa), [Mexico International University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_International_University), [TecMilenio University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TecMilenio_University), [Universidad Autónoma de Durango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Aut%C3%B3noma_de_Durango), [Universidad Autónoma de Occidente](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Aut%C3%B3noma_de_Occidente_\(Mexico\)), [Universidad Autónoma Indígena de México](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Aut%C3%B3noma_Ind%C3%ADgena_de_M%C3%A9xico), and [Universidad Casa Blanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universidad_Casa_Blanca).

### Arts

Sinaloa’s [colonial architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Colonial_architecture) is present in [Pueblos Mágicos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pueblos_M%C3%A1gicos) such as [El Fuerte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Fuerte,_Sinaloa), [Mocorito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mocorito), and [Cosalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosal%C3%A1).[^cite_note-62] [Neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) influences are visible in landmarks such as the [Ángela Peralta Theater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teatro_%C3%81ngela_Peralta,_Mazatl%C3%A1n) and [Baroque revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_Revival_architecture) in the [Basilica Cathedral of Mazatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_Cathedral_of_Mazatl%C3%A1n). The state government jointly awards the [José Limón National Contemporary Dance Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Lim%C3%B3n_National_Contemporary_Dance_Award), Mexico's most prestigious dance award.[^cite_note-63] Notable writers from Sinaloa include [Gilberto Owen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilberto_Owen), known for his modernist poetry, and [Inés Arredondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In%C3%A9s_Arredondo), acclaimed for her psychological and realist short stories.

### Music

The state is known for its popular styles of music [banda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_music) and [norteño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norte%C3%B1o_music).[^cite_note-69] Banda music, one of Mexico’s most distinctive [regional genres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_Mexican_music), was popularized in Sinaloa in the late 19th century through the blending of traditional Mexican and indigenous styles including [sones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Son_mexicano), [ranchera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchera), [corrido](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corrido),[^cite_note-\:2-70] with European [polka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polka), [waltz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waltz), [fanfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanfare), [mazurka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazurka), [schottische](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schottische), and [brass band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brass_band) ensembles.[^cite_note-71] Initially performed by local wind ensembles, it evolved into one of the country's most popular genres. It is typically played with [trumpets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpets), [clarinets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarinet), [trombones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trombone), [tubas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuba), and [tamboras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombo_criollo).[^cite_note-72]

Early bandas were formed by members of military and municipal bands who settled in the [Sierra Madre Occidental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Madre_Occidental) during the [Mexican Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Revolution), and were influenced by traditional [Yoreme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoreme) music.[^cite_note-\:2-70] Perhaps the most popular song of the genre is "[El Sinaloense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Sinaloense)", written by [Severiano Briseño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severiano_Brise%C3%B1o) in 1944 and recorded by hundreds of bandas, in both lyrical and instrumental versions. The song is considered the state's unofficial anthem.[^cite_note-73]

### Organized crime

The powerful [Sinaloa Cartel](16881931.md) (*Cártel de Sinaloa* or *CDS*) has significantly influenced the culture of Sinaloa.[^cite_note-74] The cartel is reportedly the largest [drug trafficking](197015.md), [money laundering](19390.md) and [organized crime](22625.md) syndicate in the Americas; it is based in the city of [Culiacán, Sinaloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culiac%C3%A1n,_Sinaloa).[^cite_note-75]

### Sports

Sinaloa is one of the few places where the ancient [Mesoamerican ballgame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_ballgame) is still played, in a handful of small, rural communities near [Mazatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazatl%C3%A1n). The ritual ballgame was central in the society, religion and [cosmology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cosmology) of all the great [Mesoamerican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica) cultures including the [Mixtecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixtec), [Aztecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztecs), and [Maya](18449273.md).[^cite_note-76] The Sinaloa version of the game is called *[ulama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulama_\(game\))* and is very similar to the original.[^cite_note-77] There are efforts to preserve this 3500-year-old unique tradition by supporting the communities and children who play it.[^cite_note-78]

The state is home to several [baseball](3850.md) teams such as [Tomateros de Culiacán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomateros_de_Culiac%C3%A1n), [Venados de Mazatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venados_de_Mazatl%C3%A1n), [Cañeros de Los Mochis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ca%C3%B1eros_de_Los_Mochis) and [Algodoneros de Guasave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algodoneros_de_Guasave) which take part in the [Mexican Pacific League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Pacific_League).[^cite_note-79] [Football](10568.md) is represented at the professional level by [Dorados de Sinaloa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorados_de_Sinaloa), based in [Culiacán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culiac%C3%A1n) and playing at [Estadio Dorados](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Dorados), and [Mazatlán F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazatl%C3%A1n_F.C.), based in [Mazatlán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazatl%C3%A1n) with home games at [Estadio El Encanto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_de_Mazatl%C3%A1n); several smaller clubs and amateur teams also compete throughout the state.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] Sinaloa hosts professional basketball teams, including [Caballeros de Culiacán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caballeros_de_Culiac%C3%A1n), [Frayles de Guasave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frayles_de_Guasave), and [Pioneros de Los Mochis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pioneros_de_Los_Mochis), which compete in the [Circuito de Baloncesto de la Costa del Pacífico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuito_de_Baloncesto_de_la_Costa_del_Pac%C3%ADfico).[^cite_note-82]
