**Cleveland** ([/ˈkliːvlənd/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [*KLEEV-lənd*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key)) is a city in the U.S. state of [Ohio](22199.md), located along the southern shore of [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie). It is across the lake from the Canadian province of [Ontario](22218.md), and is approximately 60 miles (97 kilometers) west of the Ohio–[Pennsylvania](23332.md) state line. Cleveland is the most populous city on Lake Erie, the [2nd-most populous city in Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_municipalities_in_Ohio) (after [Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbus,_Ohio)), and the [53rd-most populous city in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population), with a population of 372,624 at the [2020 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_census).[^cite_note-USCensusQuickFacts-6] It also serves as the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [Cuyahoga County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_County,_Ohio). The [Greater Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Cleveland) metropolitan area, with an estimated 2.17 million residents, is the [34th-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) in the U.S.[^cite_note-PopEstCBSA-10]

Cleveland was founded in 1796 near the mouth of the [Cuyahoga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_River) as part of the [Connecticut Western Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Western_Reserve) in modern-day [Northeast Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_Ohio) by General [Moses Cleaveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Cleaveland), after whom the city was named. The city's location on the river and the lake shore allowed it to grow into a major commercial and industrial [metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis) by the late 19th century, attracting large numbers of [immigrants](15051.md) and [migrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_\(African_American\)).[^cite_note-immigration-migration-11] It was among the [top 10 largest U.S. cities by population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Largest_cities_in_the_United_States_by_population_by_decade) for much of the 20th century, a period that saw the development of the city's cultural institutions.[^cite_note-largest-US-cities-12] By the 1960s, Cleveland's economy began to slow down as [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing_in_the_United_States) declined and [suburbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburb) occurred.

Cleveland is a [port city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Cleveland), connected to the Atlantic Ocean via the [St. Lawrence Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Lawrence_Seaway). [Its economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Greater_Cleveland) relies on diverse sectors that include higher education, manufacturing, financial services, healthcare, and biomedicals.[^cite_note-economy-13] The city serves as the headquarters of the [Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_Cleveland), as well as several major companies. The GDP for Greater Cleveland was US\$138.3 billion in 2022.[^cite_note-cleveland-MSA-GDP-8] Combined with the [Akron metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akron_metropolitan_area), the eight-county Cleveland–Akron metropolitan economy was \$176 billion in 2022, the largest in Ohio.[^cite_note-14]

Designated as a [global city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_city) by the [Globalization and World Cities Research Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Globalization_and_World_Cities_Research_Network),[^cite_note-15] Cleveland is home to several major cultural institutions, including the [Cleveland Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Museum_of_Art), the [Cleveland Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Museum_of_Natural_History), the [Cleveland Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Orchestra), the [Cleveland Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Public_Library), [Playhouse Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playhouse_Square), and the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](58902.md), as well as [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University). Known as "[The Forest City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forest_City)" among many other [nicknames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicknames_of_Cleveland), Cleveland serves as the center of the [Cleveland Metroparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Metroparks) nature reserve system.[^cite_note-forest-city-1] The city's major league [professional sports teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Cleveland) include the [Cleveland Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Browns) ([football](18951490.md); [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL)), the [Cleveland Cavaliers](72868.md) (basketball; [NBA](22093.md)), and the [Cleveland Guardians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Guardians) (baseball; [MLB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLB)).

## History

Main article: [History of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Cleveland)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Cleveland).

### Founding



1888 statue of city founder General [Moses Cleaveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Cleaveland) by [James G. C. Hamilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_G._C._Hamilton)

Cleveland was established on July 22, 1796, by surveyors of the [Connecticut Land Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Land_Company) when they laid out [Connecticut](6466.md)'s [Western Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_Western_Reserve) into townships and a capital city. They named the settlement "Cleaveland" after their leader, General [Moses Cleaveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses_Cleaveland), a veteran of the [American Revolutionary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Revolutionary_War). Cleaveland oversaw the [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England)–style design of the plan for what would become the modern downtown area, centered on [Public Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Square,_Cleveland).[^cite_note-moses-16] The town's name was often shortened to "Cleveland", including by Cleaveland's original surveyors. A myth emerged that the spelling was altered by *The Cleveland Advertiser* to fit the name on the newspaper's [masthead](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nameplate_\(publishing\)).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

The first permanent European settler in Cleveland was [Lorenzo Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Carter_\(settler\)), who arrived in May 1797 and built a cabin on the banks of the Cuyahoga River.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillerWheeler1997\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsclevelandconcise0000millpage14_14\]-19] The emerging community served as an important supply post for the U.S. during the [Battle of Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Lake_Erie) in the [War of 1812](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_of_1812).[^cite_note-20] Largely through the efforts of the settlement's first lawyer, [Alfred Kelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Kelley), the village of Cleveland was incorporated on December 23, 1814.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERose199074-21]

Despite the nearby swampy lowlands and harsh winters, the town's waterfront on [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie) was advantageous, giving it access to [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) trade. It grew rapidly after the 1832 completion of the [Ohio and Erie Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_and_Erie_Canal),[^cite_note-22] which linked the [Ohio River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_River) and the [Great Lakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes) to the Atlantic Ocean via the [Erie Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erie_Canal) and [Hudson River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_River), and later via the [Saint Lawrence Seaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Lawrence_Seaway).[^cite_note-economy-13] The town's growth continued with added [railroad links](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transportation_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillerWheeler1997\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsclevelandconcise0000millpage51_51\]-23] In 1836, Cleveland, then only on the eastern banks of the Cuyahoga, was incorporated as a city; [John W. Willey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_W._Willey) was elected its first mayor.[^cite_note-timeline-24] The neighboring municipality of [Ohio City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_City,_Cleveland) was a major rival to Cleveland until its [annexation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_annexation_in_the_United_States) by the city in 1854.[^cite_note-timeline-24]

### Mid to late 19th century



[Bird's-eye view](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird's-eye_view) of Cleveland in 1877

A center of [abolitionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abolitionism_in_the_United_States) activity,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeating202213–15-25][^cite_note-26] Cleveland (code-named "Station Hope") was a major stop on the [Underground Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underground_Railroad) for escaped African American [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_the_United_States) en route to Canada.[^cite_note-27] The city served as an [important center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_in_the_American_Civil_War) for the [Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_\(American_Civil_War\)) during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKeating202238-28][^cite_note-29] creating a large manufacturing industry within the city and unprecedented growth.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillerWheeler1997\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsclevelandconcise0000millpage69_69\]-30] Its geographic location as a transportation hub on the Great Lakes contributed to its development as an industrial and commercial center. In 1870, [John D. Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._Rockefeller) founded [Standard Oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Oil) in Cleveland;[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERose1990937-31] in 1885, he moved its headquarters to [New York City](645042.md).[^cite_note-32]

Cleveland's economic growth and industrial jobs attracted large waves of immigrants from [Southern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Europe), [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe), and [Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Ireland_\(1801%E2%80%931923\)).[^cite_note-immigration-migration-11] Urban growth was accompanied by labor unrest as workers demanded [better wages and working conditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_rights).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillerWheeler1997\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsclevelandconcise0000millpage87_87–89\]-33] The [Cleveland Streetcar Strike of 1899](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streetcar_strikes_in_the_United_States) was one of the more violent instances of labor strife in the city during this period.[^cite_note-34]

### Early to mid 20th century

See also: [ATCOR § Cleveland (1900-1920)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATCOR#Cleveland_\(1900-1920\))



1917 multilingual poster in English, Italian, [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language), [Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_language), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language), and [Yiddish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yiddish), advertising English classes for immigrants in Cleveland

By 1910, Cleveland had become known as the "Sixth City" due to its status at the time as the sixth-largest U.S. city.[^cite_note-nicknames-35] Its automotive companies included [Peerless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peerless_Motor_Company), [Chandler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandler_Motor_Car), and [Winton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winton_Motor_Carriage_Company), maker of the first car driven across the U.S. Other manufacturing industries in Cleveland included [steam cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steam_car) produced by [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Motor_Company) and [electric cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_car) produced by [Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baker_Motor_Vehicle).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillerWheeler1997\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsclevelandconcise0000millpage116_116\]-36] [Euclid Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid_Avenue_\(Cleveland\)) became known as "Millionaires' Row" for its prestige and elegance.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECigliano19911–2-37] Major [Progressive Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Era) politicians were leaders in the city, including [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism) Mayor [Tom L. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_L._Johnson), who was responsible for the development of the [Cleveland Mall Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Mall_\(Cleveland\)).[^cite_note-38] The era of the [City Beautiful movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Beautiful_movement) in Cleveland architecture saw wealthy patrons support the establishment of the city's major cultural institutions including the [Cleveland Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Museum_of_Art), which opened in 1916,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETurner1991\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsobject-lessonspage9_9\]-39] and the [Cleveland Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Orchestra), established in 1918.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERosenberg200043–44-40]

African American migrants from the rural [South](179553.md) arrived in Cleveland as part of the [Great Migration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Migration_\(African_American\)) for jobs, constitutional rights, and relief from [racial discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws).[^cite_note-african-americans-41] By 1920 Cleveland had grown into a densely populated metropolis of 796,841, making it the fifth-largest city in the nation,[^cite_note-largest-US-cities-12] with a foreign-born population of 30%.[^cite_note-42] Radical labor movements gained popularity in the city, such as the [Industrial Workers of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Workers_of_the_World) (IWW), in response to the conditions of the largely immigrant and migrant workers. In 1919, local [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialism) and IWW demonstrators clashed with anti-socialists in the [May Day Riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Day_riots_of_1919),[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] which occurred amid the [First Red Scare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Red_Scare) and the [strike wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_strike_wave_of_1919) that swept the U.S. that year.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBrecher2020\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsstrike0000brec_t8h1page110_110\]-45]

Cleveland's population continued to grow throughout the [Roaring Twenties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roaring_Twenties).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECondon1979\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsclevelandprodigy0000condpage99_99\]-46] The decade saw the establishment of the city's [Playhouse Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playhouse_Square),[^cite_note-playhouse-47] and the rise of the risqué [Short Vincent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Cleveland#Short_Vincent).[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49] The Bal-Masque balls of the avant-garde [Kokoon Arts Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kokoon_Arts_Club) scandalized the city.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAdamsWaldman201185–87-50][^cite_note-51] [Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz) came to prominence in Cleveland during this period.[^cite_note-52][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMosbrook201310-53] [Prohibition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_the_United_States) was established in Ohio in May 1919 (although it was not well-enforced in Cleveland), became law with the [Volstead Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volstead_Act) in 1920, and was [repealed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-first_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) nationally by [Congress](31756.md) in 1933.[^cite_note-prohibition-54] The ban on alcohol led to the rise of [speakeasies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speakeasy) throughout the city and organized crime gangs, such as the [Mayfield Road Mob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayfield_Road_Mob), who smuggled bootleg liquor across [Lake Erie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Erie) from Canada into Cleveland.[^cite_note-prohibition-54][^cite_note-55]



[Euclid Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid_Avenue_\(Cleveland\)) and East 9th Street with the Hickox Building in 1918

The era of the [flapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flapper) marked the beginning of the golden age in Downtown Cleveland retail, centered on major department stores [Higbee's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higbee's), Bailey's, the [May Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_Company_Ohio), [Taylor's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Taylor_&_Son), [Halle's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halle_Brothers_Co.), and [Sterling Lindner Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterling-Lindner_Co.), which collectively represented one of the largest and most fashionable shopping districts in the country.[^cite_note-56] In 1929, Cleveland hosted the first of many [National Air Races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Air_Races), and [Amelia Earhart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amelia_Earhart) flew to the city from [Santa Monica, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Monica,_California) in the [Women's Air Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Air_Derby).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] The [Van Sweringen brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Sweringen_brothers) commenced construction of the [Terminal Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terminal_Tower) skyscraper in 1926 and oversaw its completion in 1927.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHarwood2003134–135-59] When the building was dedicated as part of [Cleveland Union Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Union_Terminal) in 1930, the city had a population of over 900,000.[^cite_note-timeline-24]



Postcard of [Public Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Square_\(Cleveland\)) and the then-new [Cleveland Union Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Union_Terminal) in 1930

Cleveland's economy was greatly impacted by the [Wall Street Crash of 1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wall_Street_Crash_of_1929) and the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillerWheeler1997\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsclevelandconcise0000millpage146_146\]"Industrialist_\[\[Cyrus_S._Eaton|Cyrus_Eaton\]\]_would_later_say_that_Cleveland_was_hurt_more_by_the_Depression_than_any_other_city_in_the_United_States"-60] A center of [union activity](2474406.md), the city saw significant [labor struggles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labor_history_of_the_United_States) in this period, including strikes by workers against [Fisher Body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fisher_Body) in 1936 and against [Republic Steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_Steel) in 1937.[^cite_note-labor-61] The city was aided by major federal works projects sponsored by President [Franklin D. Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franklin_D._Roosevelt)'s [New Deal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Deal).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillerWheeler1997\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsclevelandconcise0000millpage136_136–139\]-62] In commemoration of the centennial of Cleveland's incorporation as a city, the [Great Lakes Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Exposition) debuted in June 1936 at the city's [North Coast Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Coast_Harbor), along the Lake Erie shore north of downtown.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPorter1976106–107-63] Conceived by Cleveland's business leaders as a way to revitalize the city during the Depression, it drew four million visitors in its first season, and seven million by the end of its second and final season in September 1937.[^cite_note-64] During [World War II](32927.md), Cleveland was a major hub of the [U.S. war effort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_home_front_during_World_War_II) as the fifth largest manufacturing center in the nation.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAlbrechtBanks201554-65] During the war, the city established the Cleveland Transit System, the predecessor to the [Greater Cleveland Regional Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Cleveland_Regional_Transit_Authority).[^cite_note-RTA-history-66]

### Late 20th and early 21st centuries

Cleveland experienced an [economic boom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_cycle) after the war and businesses declared the city to be the "best location in the nation".[^cite_note-nicknames-35][^cite_note-67] In 1950, its population reached 914,808.[^cite_note-1950-census-68] By the 1960s, however, Cleveland's economy slowed and residents began seeking new housing in the suburbs, reflecting national trends of suburbanization following federally subsidized highways.[^cite_note-suburbs-69] [Industrial restructuring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deindustrialization), particularly in the steel and automotive industries, resulted in the loss of numerous jobs in Cleveland and the city suffered economically.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillerWheeler1997\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsclevelandconcise0000millpage183_183\]-70] The [burning of the Cuyahoga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burning_of_the_Cuyahoga_River) in June 1969 brought national attention to the issue of [industrial pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pollution) in Cleveland and catalyzed the [American environmental movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environmental_movement_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-NYT-cuyahoga-71]



[Key Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_Tower) and the *[Fountain of Eternal Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fountain_of_Eternal_Life)* by [Marshall Fredericks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshall_Fredericks)

[Housing discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_discrimination_in_the_United_States) and [redlining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redlining) against African Americans led to racial unrest in Cleveland.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERothstein201714-72][^cite_note-73] The [Hough riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hough_riots) erupted from July 18 to 24, 1966,[^cite_note-74] and the [Glenville Shootout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenville_Shootout) took place on July 23, 1968.[^cite_note-75] In November 1967, Cleveland became the first major American city to elect an African American mayor, [Carl B. Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Stokes), who worked to restore the Cuyahoga River.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEStokes1973\[httpsarchiveorgdetailspromisesofpowerp00stokpage42_42\]-76][^cite_note-stokes-cuyahoga-77]

During the 1970s, Cleveland became known as "Bomb City U.S.A." due to several bombings that shook the city, mostly due to organized crime rivalries.[^cite_note-78] In December 1978, Cleveland became the first major American city since the Great Depression to enter a [financial default](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Default_\(finance\)) on federal loans.[^cite_note-79] The [national recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_1980s_recession_in_the_United_States) of the early 1980s saw the continued erosian of the city's traditional economic industries.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMillerWheeler1997\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsclevelandconcise0000millpage183_183\]-70] Cleveland's unemployment rate of 13.8% was higher than the national average due to the closure of several steel production centers.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81]

The city began an economic recovery in the 1980s. Downtown saw the construction of the [Key Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_Tower) and [200 Public Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/200_Public_Square) skyscrapers, as well as the development of the [Gateway Sports and Entertainment Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_Sports_and_Entertainment_Complex) – consisting of [Progressive Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Field) and [Rocket Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Arena) – and North Coast Harbor, including the [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](58902.md), [Cleveland Browns Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Browns_Stadium), and the [Great Lakes Science Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Science_Center).[^cite_note-82] Although the city emerged from default in 1987,[^cite_note-timeline-24] it suffered from the impact of the [subprime mortgage crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subprime_mortgage_crisis) and the [Great Recession](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Recession_in_the_United_States).[^cite_note-83] By the 21st century, Cleveland had developed a more diversified economy and gained a national reputation as a center for healthcare and the arts.[^cite_note-smithsonian-84] Downtown and several other neighborhoods experienced significant population growth since 2010, while overall population decline has slowed.[^cite_note-2020census-85]

## Geography



[NASA](18426568.md) satellite photograph of Cleveland at night

Cleveland has a total area of 82.47 square miles (213.6 km<sup>2</sup>), of which 77.70 square miles (201.2 km<sup>2</sup>) is land and 4.77 square miles (12.4 km<sup>2</sup>) is water.[^cite_note-Gazetteer_files-86] The shore of Lake Erie is 569 feet (173 m) above [sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_mean_sea_level); the city lies on a series of irregular bluffs lying roughly parallel to the lake. In Cleveland, these bluffs are cut principally by the [Cuyahoga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_River), Big Creek, and [Euclid Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid_Creek).[^cite_note-87]

The land rises quickly from the lake shore elevation of 569 feet. Public Square, less than one mile (1.6 km) inland, sits at an elevation of 650 feet (198 m), and Hopkins Airport, five miles (8.0 km) inland from the lake, is at an elevation of 791 feet (241 m).[^cite_note-88]

Cleveland borders several [inner-ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inner_suburb) and [streetcar suburbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streetcar_suburb).[^cite_note-suburbs-69] To the west, it borders [Lakewood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakewood,_Ohio), [Rocky River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_River,_Ohio), and [Fairview Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairview_Park,_Ohio), and to the east, it borders [Shaker Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaker_Heights,_Ohio), [Cleveland Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Heights,_Ohio), [South Euclid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Euclid,_Ohio), and [East Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Cleveland,_Ohio). To the southwest, it borders [Linndale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linndale,_Ohio), [Brooklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn,_Ohio), [Parma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parma,_Ohio), and [Brook Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brook_Park,_Ohio). To the south, the city borders [Newburgh Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newburgh_Heights,_Ohio), [Cuyahoga Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_Heights,_Ohio), and [Brooklyn Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brooklyn_Heights,_Ohio) and to the southeast, it borders [Warrensville Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warrensville_Heights,_Ohio), [Maple Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple_Heights,_Ohio), and [Garfield Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garfield_Heights,_Ohio). To the northeast, along the shore of Lake Erie, Cleveland borders [Bratenahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratenahl,_Ohio) and [Euclid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euclid,_Ohio).

### Architecture

See also: [List of tallest buildings in Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Cleveland) and [National Register of Historic Places listings in Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_Cleveland)



Skyline of Cleveland at night in January 2025

Many of Cleveland's government and civic buildings, including [City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_City_Hall), the [Cuyahoga County Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_County_Courthouse), the [Cleveland Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Public_Library), and [Public Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Auditorium), are clustered around the open Cleveland Mall and share a common [neoclassical architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture). They were built in the early 20th century as the result of the 1903 Group Plan. They constitute one of the most complete examples of [City Beautiful](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Beautiful_movement) design.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELawrence198020–25-89][^cite_note-90] Completed in 1927 and dedicated in 1930 as part of the [Cleveland Union Terminal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tower_City_Center) complex, the Terminal Tower is a prototypical [Beaux-Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaux-Arts_architecture) skyscraper. It was the tallest building in North America outside New York City until 1964 and the tallest in the city until 1991.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETomanCook200576-91] Key Tower (the tallest building in Ohio) and 200 Public Square combine elements of [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco) architecture with [postmodern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmodern_architecture) designs.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

Nicknamed Cleveland's "Crystal Palace", the five-story [Cleveland Arcade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Arcade) (also known as the Old Arcade) was built in 1890 and renovated in 2001 as a [Hyatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyatt) Regency Hotel.[^cite_note-94] Another major landmark, the [Cleveland Trust Company Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Trust_Company_Building), was completed in 1907 and renovated in 2015.[^cite_note-95] Cleveland's historic [ecclesiastical architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_architecture) includes the Presbyterian [Old Stone Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Stone_Church_\(Cleveland\)),[^cite_note-96] the [onion-domed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onion_dome) [St. Theodosius Russian Orthodox Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Theodosius_Russian_Orthodox_Cathedral),[^cite_note-97] and the Roman Catholic [Cathedral of St. John the Evangelist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral_of_St._John_the_Evangelist_\(Cleveland,_Ohio\)) along with several ethnically inspired Catholic churches.[^cite_note-98]

Cleveland exhibits a [humid continental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_continental) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Dfa*) or [humid subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfa*) climate, depending on the [isotherm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isotherm_\(contour_line\)) used.[^cite_note-108] Due to its proximity to Lake Erie, Cleveland experiences milder temperatures than much of Ohio, with relatively cooler summers and warmer winters.[^cite_note-109] East of the mouth of the Cuyahoga, the land elevation rises rapidly in the south. Together with the prevailing winds off Lake Erie, this feature is the principal contributor to the [lake-effect snow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake-effect_snow) that is typical in Cleveland (especially on the city's East Side) from mid-November until the surface of the lake freezes, usually in late January or early February. The lake effect causes a relative differential in geographical snowfall totals across the city. On the city's far West Side, the Hopkins neighborhood only reached 100 inches (254 cm) of snowfall in a season three times since record-keeping for snow began in 1893.[^cite_note-110] By contrast, seasonal totals approaching or exceeding 100 inches (254 cm) are not uncommon as the city ascends into the Heights on the east, where the region known as the "[Snow Belt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowbelt)" begins. Extending from the city's East Side, the Snow Belt reaches up the Lake Erie shore as far as [Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buffalo,_New_York).[^cite_note-111] The worst [winter storm](244878.md) in Cleveland's history was the [Great Blizzard of 1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Blizzard_of_1978).[^cite_note-112]

The all-time record high in Cleveland of 104 °F (40 °C) was established on June 25, 1988,[^cite_note-113] and the all-time record low of −20 °F (−29 °C) was set on January 19, 1994.[^cite_note-114] On average, July is the warmest month with a mean temperature of 74.5 °F (23.6 °C), and January, with a mean temperature of 29.1 °F (−1.6 °C), is the coldest. Normal yearly [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) based on the 30-year average from 1991 to 2020 is 41.03 inches (1,042 mm).[^cite_note-115] The least precipitation occurs on the western side and directly along the lake, and the most occurs in the eastern suburbs. Parts of [Geauga County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geauga_County,_Ohio) to the east receive over 44 inches (1,100 mm) of liquid precipitation annually.[^cite_note-116]

### Environment

See also: [Sustainable Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sustainable_Cleveland)



The west bank of [the Flats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Flats) and the [Cuyahoga River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_River) in Downtown Cleveland, with [Jacobs Pavilion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobs_Pavilion), Cleveland's [amphitheater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphitheater)

With its extensive cleanup of its Lake Erie shore and the Cuyahoga River, Cleveland has been recognized by national media as an environmental success story and a national leader in environmental protection.[^cite_note-NYT-cuyahoga-71] Since the city's industrialization, the Cuyahoga River had become so affected by industrial pollution that it "caught fire" a total of 13 times beginning in 1868.[^cite_note-burning-river-123] It was the river fire of June 1969 that spurred the city to action under Mayor Carl B. Stokes, and played a key role in the passage of the [Clean Water Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_Water_Act) in 1972 and the [National Environmental Policy Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Environmental_Policy_Act) later that year.[^cite_note-stokes-cuyahoga-77][^cite_note-burning-river-123] Since that time, the Cuyahoga has been extensively cleaned up through the efforts of the city and the [Ohio Environmental Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Environmental_Protection_Agency) (OEPA).[^cite_note-NYT-cuyahoga-71][^cite_note-124]

In addition to continued efforts to improve freshwater and air quality, Cleveland is now exploring renewable energy. The city's two main electrical utilities are [FirstEnergy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FirstEnergy) and [Cleveland Public Power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Public_Power). Its [climate action plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_climate_change_initiatives), updated in December 2018, has a 2050 target of 100% [renewable power](25784.md), along with reduction of [greenhouse gases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse_gas_emissions_by_the_United_States) to 80% below the 2010 level.[^cite_note-125] In recent decades, Cleveland has been working to address the issue of [harmful algal blooms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harmful_algal_bloom) on Lake Erie, fed primarily by agricultural runoff, which have presented new environmental challenges for the city and for northern Ohio.[^cite_note-126]

Cleveland has seen a population decline since the late 20th century. In 1950, the city's population was 914,808.[^cite_note-1950-census-68] By 1980, this number had fallen to 573,822.[^cite_note-131] According to the 2020 census, there were 372,624 people and 170,549 households in Cleveland. The population density was 4,901.51 inhabitants per square mile (1,892.5/km<sup>2</sup>). The median household income was \$30,907 and the per capita income was \$21,223. 32.7% of the population was living below the poverty line. Of the city's population over the age of 25, 17.5% held a bachelor's degree or higher, and 80.8% had a high school diploma or equivalent.[^cite_note-USCensusQuickFacts-6] The median age was 36.6 years.[^cite_note-132]

As of 2020, the racial and ethnic composition of the city was 47.5% African American, 32.1% [non-Hispanic white](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hispanic_whites), 13.1% [Hispanic or Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans), 2.8% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Americans), 0.2% Native American, and 3.8% from [two or more races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiracial_Americans).[^cite_note-2020census-85] 85.3% of Clevelanders age five and older spoke only English at home, while 14.7% spoke a language other than English, including [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_language_in_the_United_States), [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language_in_the_United_States), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language_in_the_United_States), [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_language), [Albanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_language), and various [Slavic languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavic_languages) ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language_in_the_United_States), [Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language), [Serbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_language), [Croatian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_language), and [Slovene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_language)).[^cite_note-USCensusQuickFacts-6] The city's [spoken accent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_English_regional_phonology) is an advanced form of [Inland Northern American English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Northern_American_English), similar to other Great Lakes cities, but distinctive from the rest of Ohio.[^cite_note-133][^cite_note-134]

### Ethnicity



Originally built in 1905 as the Jewish Temple B'nai Jeshurun, this building on Cleveland's East Side, today known as the [Shiloh Baptist Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiloh_Baptist_Church_\(Cleveland,_Ohio\)), now serves an African American congregation.

In the 19th and early 20th centuries, Cleveland saw a massive influx of immigrants from Ireland, [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy), and the [Austro-Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), and [Ottoman](22278.md) empires, most of whom were attracted by manufacturing jobs.[^cite_note-immigration-migration-11] As a result, Cleveland and Cuyahoga County today have substantial communities of [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Americans) (especially in West Park), [Italians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Americans) (especially in Little Italy), [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Americans), and several [Central](5188.md)-Eastern European ethnicities, including [Czechs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czech_American), [Hungarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Americans), [Lithuanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithuanian_American), [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Americans), [Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_American), [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Americans), [Rusyns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rusyn_Americans), [Slovaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovak_American), [Ukrainians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_American), and ex-[Yugoslav](34244.md) groups, such as [Slovenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovene_Americans), [Croats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatian_American) and [Serbs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbian_American).[^cite_note-immigration-migration-11] The presence of [Hungarians within Cleveland proper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Ohioans#Hungarians_in_Cleveland) was, at one time, so great that the city boasted the highest concentration of Hungarians in the world outside of [Budapest](36787.md).[^cite_note-135] Cleveland has a long-established [Jewish community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jews_and_Judaism_in_Greater_Cleveland), historically centered on the East Side neighborhoods of Glenville and Kinsman, but now mostly concentrated in East Side suburbs such as Cleveland Heights and [Beachwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beachwood,_Ohio), location of the [Maltz Museum of Jewish Heritage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltz_Museum_of_Jewish_Heritage).[^cite_note-136]

The availability of jobs attracted African Americans from the South. Between 1910 and 1970, the black population of Cleveland, largely concentrated on the city's East Side, increased significantly as a result of the First and [Second Great Migrations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Great_Migration_\(African_American\)).[^cite_note-african-americans-41] Cleveland's Latino community consists primarily of [Puerto Ricans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Ricans_in_the_United_States), as well as smaller numbers of immigrants from Mexico, [Cuba](5042481.md), the [Dominican Republic](8060.md), [South](26769.md) and Central America, and Spain.[^cite_note-137] The city's Asian community, centered on historical Asiatown, consists of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Americans), [Koreans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Americans), [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_Americans), and other groups.[^cite_note-138] Additionally, the city and the county have significant communities of [Albanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanian_Americans),[^cite_note-albanians-139] [Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_American) (especially [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_Americans), [Syrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Americans), and [Palestinians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Americans)),[^cite_note-140] [Armenians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Americans),[^cite_note-141] [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_American),[^cite_note-142] [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_American),[^cite_note-143] [Iranians](2234641.md),[^cite_note-144] [Scots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Americans),[^cite_note-immigration-migration-11] [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_Americans),[^cite_note-145] and [West Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Indian_American).[^cite_note-immigration-migration-11] A 2020 analysis found Cleveland to be the most ethnically and racially diverse major city in Ohio.[^cite_note-146]

### Religion

The influx of immigrants in the 19th and early 20th centuries drastically transformed Cleveland's religious landscape. From a homogeneous settlement of New England [Protestants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestants), it evolved into a city with a diverse religious composition. The predominant faith among Clevelanders today is Christianity, with [Jewish](15624.md), [Muslim](6037917.md), [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), and [Buddhist](3267529.md) minorities.[^cite_note-147]

### Immigration

Within Cleveland, the neighborhoods with the highest foreign-born populations are Asiatown/Goodrich–Kirtland Park (32.7%), Clark–Fulton (26.7%), West Boulevard (18.5%), Brooklyn Centre (17.3%), Downtown (17.2%), University Circle (15.9%, with 20% in Little Italy), and Jefferson (14.3%).[^cite_note-148] Recent waves of immigration have brought new groups to Cleveland, including [Ethiopians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Americans) and [South Asians](11353572.md),[^cite_note-149][^cite_note-150] as well as immigrants from Russia and the [former USSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-Soviet_states),[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] Southeast Europe (especially [Albania](738.md)),[^cite_note-albanians-139] the Middle East, East Asia, and Latin America.[^cite_note-immigration-migration-11] In the 2010s, the immigrant population of Cleveland and Cuyahoga County began to see significant growth.[^cite_note-153] A 2019 study found Cleveland to be the city with the shortest average processing time in the nation for immigrants to become [U.S. citizens](25151566.md).[^cite_note-154] The city's annual One World Day in Rockefeller Park includes a [naturalization ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naturalization_ceremony) of new immigrants.[^cite_note-culturalgardens-155]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Greater Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Greater_Cleveland)



Entrance of the [Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_Cleveland) on East 6th Street

Cleveland's location on the Cuyahoga River and Lake Erie has been key to its growth as a major commercial center.[^cite_note-economy-13] Steel and many other manufactured goods emerged as leading industries.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-labor-61] The city has since diversified its economy in addition to its manufacturing sector.[^cite_note-economy-13]

Established in 1914, the [Federal Reserve Bank of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank_of_Cleveland) is one of 12 U.S. [Federal Reserve Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Reserve_Bank).[^cite_note-fed-157] Its downtown building, located on East 6th Street and [Superior Avenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superior_Avenue), was completed in 1923 by the Cleveland architectural firm [Walker and Weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walker_and_Weeks).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohannesen199941-158][^cite_note-159] The headquarters of the Federal Reserve System's Fourth District, the bank employs 1,000 people and maintains branch offices in [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati) and [Pittsburgh](25101.md).[^cite_note-fed-157]

Cleveland and Cuyahoga County are home to *[Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500)* companies [Cleveland-Cliffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland-Cliffs), [Progressive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Corporation), [Sherwin-Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherwin-Williams_Company), [Parker-Hannifin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parker-Hannifin), [KeyCorp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Key_Bank), and [Travel Centers of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Travel_Centers_of_America). ### Healthcare

Healthcare plays a major role in Cleveland's economy. The city's "Big Three" hospital systems are the [Cleveland Clinic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Clinic), [University Hospitals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Hospitals_Cleveland_Medical_Center), and [MetroHealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MetroHealth).[^cite_note-161] The Cleveland Clinic is the largest private employer in the state of Ohio, with a workforce of over 60,000 as of 2025.[^cite_note-162] It carries the distinction of being one of the best hospital systems in the world.[^cite_note-163] The clinic is led by Croatian-born president and CEO Tomislav Mihaljevic and it is affiliated with [Case Western Reserve University School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University_School_of_Medicine).[^cite_note-164]

University Hospitals includes the University Hospitals Cleveland Medical Center and its [Rainbow Babies & Children's Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Babies_&_Children's_Hospital). Cliff Megerian serves as that system's CEO.[^cite_note-165] MetroHealth on the city's west side is led by president and CEO Christine Alexander-Rager.[^cite_note-166] Formerly known as City Hospital, it operates one of two Level I trauma centers in the city, and has various locations throughout Greater Cleveland.[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168]

In 2013, Cleveland's [Global Center for Health Innovation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Center_for_Health_Innovation) opened with 235,000 square feet (21,800 m<sup>2</sup>) of display space for healthcare companies across the world.[^cite_note-169] To take advantage of the proximity of universities and other medical centers in Cleveland, the [Veterans Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterans_Administration) moved the region's VA hospital from suburban [Brecksville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brecksville) to a new facility in University Circle.[^cite_note-170]

### Theater and performing arts



[Playhouse Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playhouse_Square)

Cleveland's [Playhouse Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playhouse_Square) is the second largest performing arts center in the U.S. behind New York City's [Lincoln Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln_Center).[^cite_note-171] It includes the [State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Theatre_\(Cleveland\)), [Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connor_Palace), [Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allen_Theatre), [Hanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanna_Theatre), and [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Theatre_\(Cleveland\)) theaters.[^cite_note-playhouse-47] The theaters host [Broadway musicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musical_theatre), special concerts, speaking engagements, and other events throughout the year. Playhouse Square's resident performing arts companies include [Cleveland Ballet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Ballet_\(founded_2014\)), the [Cleveland International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_International_Film_Festival), the [Cleveland Play House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Play_House), Cleveland State University Department of Theatre and Dance, DANCECleveland, the [Great Lakes Theater Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Theater_Festival), and the Tri-C Jazz Fest.[^cite_note-172] A city with strong traditions in theater and [vaudeville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaudeville), Cleveland has produced many renowned performers, most prominently comedian [Bob Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Hope).[^cite_note-173]

Outside Playhouse Square is [Karamu House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karamu_House), the oldest African American theater in the nation, established in 1915.[^cite_note-174] On the West Side, the Gordon Square Arts District in the Detroit–Shoreway neighborhood is the location of the Capitol Theatre, the [Near West Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_West_Theatre), and an [Off-Off-Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Off-Off-Broadway) playhouse, the [Cleveland Public Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Public_Theatre).[^cite_note-CLE-hustles-107] The [Dobama Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobama_Theatre) and the [Beck Center for the Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beck_Center_for_the_Arts) are based in Cleveland's streetcar suburbs of Cleveland Heights and Lakewood respectively.[^cite_note-175]

### Music



The [Cleveland Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Orchestra) performing at [Severance Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severance_Hall)

The Cleveland Orchestra is widely considered one of the world's finest orchestras, and often referred to as the finest in the nation.[^cite_note-176] It is one of the "[Big Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Five_\(orchestras\))" major orchestras in the U.S.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERosenberg2000522-177] The orchestra plays at [Severance Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severance_Hall) in University Circle during the winter and at [Blossom Music Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blossom_Music_Center) in [Cuyahoga Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_Falls,_Ohio) during the summer.[^cite_note-178] The city is also home to the [Cleveland Pops Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Pops_Orchestra), [Apollo's Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apollo's_Fire) Baroque Orchestra, the [Cleveland Youth Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Orchestra_Youth_Orchestra), the [Contemporary Youth Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_Youth_Orchestra), the [Cleveland Youth Wind Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Youth_Wind_Symphony), and the biennial [Cleveland International Piano Competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_International_Piano_Competition) which has, in the past, often featured the Cleveland Orchestra.[^cite_note-179]

One Playhouse Square, now the headquarters for Cleveland's [public broadcasters](178808.md), was initially used as the broadcast studios of WJW (AM), where disc jockey [Alan Freed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Freed) first popularized the term "rock and roll".[^cite_note-180] Beginning in the 1950s, Cleveland gained a strong reputation as a key breakout market for rock music.[^cite_note-rock-n-roll-181] Its popularity in the city was so great that Billy Bass, the program director at the [WMMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WMMS) radio station, referred to Cleveland as "The Rock and Roll Capital of the World".[^cite_note-rock-n-roll-181] The [Cleveland Agora Theatre and Ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agora_Theatre_and_Ballroom) has served as a major venue for rock concerts in the city since the 1960s.[^cite_note-Chogath_Kog'sante-182] From 1974 through 1980, the city hosted the [World Series of Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Series_of_Rock) at [Cleveland Municipal Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Stadium).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEToman199764–65-183]

Jazz and R\&B have a long history in Cleveland. Many major figures in jazz performed in the city, including [Louis Armstrong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Armstrong), [Cab Calloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cab_Calloway), [Duke Ellington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Ellington), [Ella Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ella_Fitzgerald), [Dizzy Gillespie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dizzy_Gillespie), and [Billie Holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billie_Holiday).[^cite_note-CLEjazz-184][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMosbrook201332–33,_59,_126-185] Legendary pianist [Art Tatum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Tatum) regularly played in Cleveland clubs in the 1930s,[^cite_note-CLEjazz-184] and [gypsy jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsy_jazz) guitarist [Django Reinhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Django_Reinhardt) gave his U.S. debut performance in Cleveland in 1946.[^cite_note-186] Prominent jazz artist [Noble Sissle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noble_Sissle) was a graduate of [Cleveland Central High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_High_School_\(Cleveland\)), and [Artie Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artie_Shaw) worked and performed in Cleveland early in his career.[^cite_note-CLEjazz-184] The Tri-C Jazz Fest has been held annually in Cleveland at Playhouse Square since 1980, and the Cleveland Jazz Orchestra was established in 1984.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMosbrook2013193-187]

The city has a history of [polka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polka) music being popular both past and present and is the location of the [Polka Hall of Fame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polka_Hall_of_Fame). A subgenre called [Cleveland-style polka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenian-style_polka), named after the city. The music's popularity is due in part to the success of [Frankie Yankovic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankie_Yankovic), a Cleveland native who was considered "America's Polka King".[^cite_note-188]

### Film and television

See also: [Category\:Films set in Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Films_set_in_Cleveland) and [Category\:Films shot in Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Films_shot_in_Cleveland)

*Cleveland Fire Department* (1900-03) by the [Edison Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edison_Manufacturing_Company), one of the first films made in Cleveland

The first film shot in Cleveland was in 1897 by the [company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edison_Manufacturing_Company) of Ohioan [Thomas Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Edison).[^cite_note-Cle-Enc-Film-189] Before [Hollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood,_Los_Angeles) became the center for [American cinema](418334.md), filmmaker Samuel Brodsky and playwright Robert McLaughlin operated a film studio at the [Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samuel_Andrews_\(chemist\)) mansion on Euclid Avenue (now the [WEWS-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WEWS-TV) studio).[^cite_note-190] There they produced major [silent-era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silent_film) features, such as *[Dangerous Toys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dangerous_Toys_\(film\))* (1921), which are now considered [lost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lost_film). Brodsky also directed the weekly *Plain Dealer Screen Magazine* that ran in theaters in Cleveland and Ohio from 1917 to 1924.[^cite_note-Cle-Enc-Film-189] In addition, Cleveland hosted over a dozen [sponsored film](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sponsored_film) studios, including [Cinécraft Productions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cin%C3%A9craft_Productions), which still operates in Ohio City.[^cite_note-Cle-Enc-Film-189][^cite_note-191]

In the "[talkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sound_film)" era, Cleveland featured in several [major studio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_film_studio) films, such as [Michael Curtiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Curtiz)'s [pre-Code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-Code_Hollywood) classic *[Goodbye Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goodbye_Again_\(1933_film\))* (1933) with [Warren William](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warren_William) and [Joan Blondell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Blondell). Players from the [1948 Cleveland Indians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Cleveland_Indians_season) appeared in *[The Kid from Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Kid_from_Cleveland)* (1949). [Billy Wilder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billy_Wilder)'s *[The Fortune Cookie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fortune_Cookie)* (1966) was set and filmed in the city and marked the first onscreen pairing of [Walter Matthau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Matthau) and [Jack Lemmon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Lemmon). Labor struggles in Cleveland were depicted in *[Native Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Land)* (1942), narrated by [Paul Robeson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Robeson), and in [Norman Jewison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norman_Jewison)'s *[F.I.S.T.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F.I.S.T._\(film\))* (1978) with [Sylvester Stallone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Stallone). Clevelander [Jim Jarmusch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Jarmusch)'s *[Stranger Than Paradise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stranger_Than_Paradise)* (1984) – a [deadpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadpan) comedy about two New Yorkers who travel to Florida by way of Cleveland – was a favorite of the [Cannes Film Festival](101460.md). *[Major League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_League_\(film\))* (1989) reflected the [perennial struggles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Guardians#1960%E2%80%931993:_The_33-year_slump) of the Cleveland Indians, while *[American Splendor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Splendor_\(film\))* (2003) reflected the life of Cleveland graphic novelist [Harvey Pekar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvey_Pekar). *[Kill the Irishman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kill_the_Irishman)* (2011) depicted the 1970s turf war between [Danny Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danny_Greene) and the Cleveland crime family.[^cite_note-Cle-Enc-Film-189]

Cleveland has doubled for other locations in films. The wedding and reception scenes in *[The Deer Hunter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Deer_Hunter)* (1978), while set in suburban Pittsburgh, were shot in Cleveland's Tremont neighborhood. *[A Christmas Story](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Christmas_Story)* (1983) was set in [Indiana](21883857.md), but drew many external shots from Cleveland. The opening shots of *[Air Force One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_One_\(film\))* (1997) were filmed in and above Severance Hall, and *[Judas and the Black Messiah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judas_and_the_Black_Messiah)* (2021) was filmed in Cleveland, although set in Chicago. Downtown Cleveland doubled for [Manhattan](45470.md) in *[Spider-Man 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spider-Man_3)* (2007), *[The Avengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Avengers_\(2012_film\))* (2012), and *[The Fate of the Furious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fate_of_the_Furious)* (2017), and for [Metropolis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolis_\(comics\)) in [James Gunn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Gunn)'s *[Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman_\(2025_film\))* (2025). Future productions are handled by the [Greater Cleveland Film Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Cleveland_Film_Commission) at the [Leader Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_Building) on Superior Avenue.[^cite_note-Cle-Enc-Film-189][^cite_note-192]

In television, the city is the setting for the popular network sitcom *[The Drew Carey Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Drew_Carey_Show)*, starring Cleveland native [Drew Carey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drew_Carey).[^cite_note-193] *[Hot in Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_in_Cleveland)*, a comedy that aired on [TV Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TV_Land), premiered on June 16, 2010, and ran for six seasons until its finale on June 3, 2015.[^cite_note-194][^cite_note-195] *[Cleveland Hustles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Hustles)*, the [CNBC](223441.md) reality show co-created by [LeBron James](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LeBron_James), was filmed in the city.[^cite_note-CLE-hustles-107]

### Literature



[Jazz poet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_poetry) and resident Clevelander [Langston Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langston_Hughes)

Cleveland has a thriving literary and poetry community, with regular poetry readings at bookstores, coffee shops, and various other venues.[^cite_note-196] In 1925, [Russian Futurist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Futurism) poet [Vladimir Mayakovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Mayakovsky) came to Cleveland and gave a poetry [recitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Recitation) to the city's ethnic working class, as part of his trip to America.[^cite_note-197][^cite_note-198] The [Cleveland State University Poetry Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_State_University_Poetry_Center) serves as an academic center for poetry in the city.[^cite_note-199]

[Langston Hughes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langston_Hughes), preeminent poet of the [Harlem Renaissance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlem_Renaissance) and child of an itinerant couple, lived in Cleveland as a teenager and attended Central High School in Cleveland in the 1910s.[^cite_note-200] At Central High, the young writer was taught by [Helen Maria Chesnutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Maria_Chesnutt), daughter of Cleveland-born African American novelist [Charles W. Chesnutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_W._Chesnutt).[^cite_note-201] Hughes authored some of his earliest poems, plays, and short stories in Cleveland and contributed to the school newspaper.[^cite_note-202] The African American avant-garde poet [Russell Atkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Atkins) lived in the city as well.[^cite_note-203]

The American modernist poet [Hart Crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hart_Crane) was born in nearby [Garrettsville, Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garrettsville,_Ohio) in 1899. His adolescence was divided between Cleveland and Akron before he moved to New York City in 1916.[^cite_note-204] Cleveland was the home of [Joe Shuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Shuster) and [Jerry Siegel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Siegel), who created the comic book character [Superman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superman) in 1933.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206] [Harlan Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harlan_Ellison), noted author of [speculative fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speculative_fiction), was born in Cleveland in 1934. As a young man, he published a series of short stories appearing in the *[Cleveland News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_News)*, and performed in a number of productions for the Cleveland Play House.[^cite_note-207]

Cleveland is the site of the [Anisfield-Wolf Book Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisfield-Wolf_Book_Award), which recognizes books that have contributed to the understanding of racism and human diversity.[^cite_note-208] Presented by the [Cleveland Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cleveland_Foundation), it remains the only American book prize focusing on works that address racism and diversity.[^cite_note-209]

### Museums and galleries

See also: [List of museums in Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_Cleveland)



The [Cleveland Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Museum_of_Art) lies at the edge of Wade Lagoon in [University Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Circle).



The [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](58902.md) on the shores of Lake Erie

Cleveland has two main [art museums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_museum). The [Cleveland Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Museum_of_Art) is a major American art museum, with a collection that includes more than 66,500 works of art.[^cite_note-210] The [Museum of Contemporary Art Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Contemporary_Art_Cleveland) showcases established and emerging artists, particularly from the Cleveland area, through hosting and producing temporary exhibitions.[^cite_note-MOCA-Cle-211] Both museums offer free admission to visitors.[^cite_note-212][^cite_note-MOCA-Cle-211]

The two museums are part of Cleveland's [University Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Circle), a 550-acre (2.2 km<sup>2</sup>) concentration of cultural, educational, and medical institutions located 5 miles (8.0 km) east of downtown. In addition to the art museums, the neighborhood includes the [Cleveland Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Botanical_Garden), Case Western Reserve University, University Hospitals, [Severance Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severance_Hall), the [Maltz Performing Arts Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltz_Performing_Arts_Center), the [Cleveland Museum of Natural History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Museum_of_Natural_History), and the [Western Reserve Historical Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Reserve_Historical_Society). Also located at University Circle is the [Cleveland Cinematheque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Cinematheque) at the Cleveland Institute of Art.[^cite_note-213]

The [I. M. Pei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._M._Pei)-designed [Rock and Roll Hall of Fame](58902.md) is located on Cleveland's Lake Erie waterfront at North Coast Harbor downtown. Neighboring attractions include [Cleveland Browns Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Browns_Stadium), the [Great Lakes Science Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Science_Center), the [Steamship Mather Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS_William_G._Mather_\(1925\)), the [International Women's Air & Space Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Women's_Air_&_Space_Museum), and the [USS *Cod*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Cod), a World War II [submarine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gato-class_submarine). Designed by architect [Levi T. Scofield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Scofield), the [Soldiers' and Sailors' Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldiers'_and_Sailors'_Monument_\(Cleveland\)) at Public Square is Cleveland's major Civil War memorial and a major attraction in the city.[^cite_note-soldiers-sailors-214] Other city attractions include [Grays Armory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grays_Armory),[^cite_note-215] the [Cleveland Masonic Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Masonic_Temple),[^cite_note-216] and the [Children's Museum of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Children's_Museum_of_Cleveland).[^cite_note-217] A Cleveland holiday attraction, especially for fans of [Jean Shepherd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Shepherd)'s *A Christmas Story*, is the [Christmas Story House and Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Christmas_Story_House) in Tremont.[^cite_note-218]

### Annual events



The [Feast of the Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Feast_of_the_Assumption_Festival) in Cleveland's [Little Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Circle#Little_Italy)

Cleveland hosts the WinterLand holiday display lighting festival annually at Public Square.[^cite_note-219] The [Cleveland International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_International_Film_Festival) has been held in the city since 1977,[^cite_note-220] and the Cleveland Silent Film Festival has been held since 2022.[^cite_note-221] The [Cleveland National Air Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_National_Air_Show), an indirect successor to the National Air Races, has been held at the city's [Burke Lakefront Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burke_Lakefront_Airport) since 1964.[^cite_note-222] The Great Lakes Burning River Fest, a two-night music and beer festival at Whiskey Island, has been sponsored by the Great Lakes Brewing Company since 2001.[^cite_note-223]

Many ethnic festivals are held in Cleveland throughout the year. These include the annual [Feast of the Assumption](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Feast_of_the_Assumption_Festival) in Little Italy,[^cite_note-224] Russian [Maslenitsa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maslenitsa) in Rockefeller Park,[^cite_note-225] the Puerto Rican Parade and Cultural Festival in Clark–Fulton,[^cite_note-226] the Cleveland Asian Festival in Asiatown,[^cite_note-227] the Tremont Greek Fest,[^cite_note-228] and the St. Mary Romanian Festival in West Park.[^cite_note-229] Cleveland also hosts annual Polish [Dyngus Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Amigus-dyngus) and Slovene [Kurentovanje](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurentovanje) celebrations.[^cite_note-230][^cite_note-231] The city's annual [Saint Patrick's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Patrick's_Day) parade brings hundreds of thousands to the streets of Downtown.[^cite_note-232] The [Cleveland Thyagaraja Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Thyagaraja_Festival) held each spring at Cleveland State University is the largest Indian classical music and dance festival in the world outside of India.[^cite_note-233] Since 1946, the city has annually marked One World Day in the [Cleveland Cultural Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Cultural_Gardens) in Rockefeller Park, celebrating all of its ethnic communities.[^cite_note-culturalgardens-155]

### Cuisine

See also: [Cuisine of Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Ohio)



The historic [West Side Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_Market) in Cleveland's [Ohio City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_City,_Cleveland) neighborhood

Cleveland's mosaic of ethnic communities and their various culinary traditions have long played an important role in defining the city's cuisine. Local mainstays include an abundance of Slavic, Hungarian, and Central-Eastern European contributions, such as [kielbasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kielbasa), [stuffed cabbage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stuffed_cabbage), [pierogies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierogi), [goulash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goulash), and [chicken paprikash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_paprikash).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTETaxel2009133–135-234] [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian-American_cuisine), [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_cuisine), [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_cuisine), and [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Jewish_cuisine) cuisines are also prominent in Cleveland, as are [Lebanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lebanese_cuisine), [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek-American_cuisine), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chinese_cuisine), [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_cuisine), [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_cuisine), and numerous other ethnic cuisines. Vendors at the [West Side Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Side_Market) in Ohio City offer many ethnic foods for sale.[^cite_note-235] In addition, the city boasts a vibrant [barbecue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbecue_in_the_United_States) and [soul food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soul_food) scene.[^cite_note-236]

Slyman's Deli on Cleveland's near East Side is notable for its [corned beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corned_beef) sandwich, with patrons including former U.S. Presidents [George W. Bush](3414021.md) and [Joe Biden](145422.md).[^cite_note-237] Another celebrated sandwich, the [Polish Boy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Boy), is a popular [street food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Street_food) and Cleveland original frequently sold at downtown [hot dog carts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_dog_cart) and stadium concession stands.[^cite_note-238] Brown [stadium mustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadium_Mustard) is synonymous with Cleveland, especially [Bertman Original Ballpark Mustard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bertman_Original_Ballpark_Mustard).[^cite_note-239] Another notable local food item with Depression-era roots is [city chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_chicken).[^cite_note-240]

With its [blue-collar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue-collar_worker) roots well intact, and plenty of Lake Erie [perch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yellow_perch) and [walleye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walleye) available, the tradition of Friday night [fish fries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish_fry) remains alive and thriving in Cleveland, particularly in ethnic parish-based settings, especially during the season of [Lent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lent).[^cite_note-241] [Clambakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clambake) are likewise embedded into the city's culinary culture.[^cite_note-242][^cite_note-243] For dessert, the Cleveland [Cassata Cake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassata#United_States) is a unique treat invented in the local Italian community and served in Italian establishments throughout the city.[^cite_note-244] Another popular dessert, the locally crafted Russian Tea Biscuit, is common in many Jewish bakeries in Cleveland.[^cite_note-245]

Cleveland is noted in the world of celebrity food culture. Famous local figures include chef [Michael Symon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Symon) and food writer [Michael Ruhlman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Ruhlman), both of whom achieved local and national attention for their contributions to the culinary world. In 2007, Symon helped gain the spotlight when he was named "[The Next Iron Chef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Next_Iron_Chef)" on the [Food Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_Network). That same year, Ruhlman collaborated with [Anthony Bourdain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Bourdain), to do an episode of his *[Anthony Bourdain: No Reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Bourdain:_No_Reservations)* focusing on Cleveland's restaurant scene.[^cite_note-246]

### Breweries

Ohio produces the fifth most beer in the U.S., with its largest brewery being Cleveland's [Great Lakes Brewing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_Brewing_Company).[^cite_note-247] Cleveland has had a long history of brewing, tied to many of its ethnic immigrants, and has reemerged as a regional leader in production.[^cite_note-248] Dozens of breweries exist within city limits, including large producers such as [Market Garden Brewery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_Garden_Brewery). Although breweries can be found throughout the city, the highest concentration is in the Ohio City neighborhood.[^cite_note-249] Cleveland hosts expansions from other countries as well, including the Scottish [BrewDog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BrewDog) and German [Hofbrauhaus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staatliches_Hofbr%C3%A4uhaus_in_M%C3%BCnchen#Franchises).[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-251]

Local sporting venues include [Progressive Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progressive_Field), [Huntington Bank Field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntington_Bank_Field), [Rocket Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_Arena), the [Wolstein Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolstein_Center), and [Public Auditorium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Auditorium).

The Cleveland Guardians – known as the Indians from 1915 to 2021 – won the [World Series](23489161.md) in [1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1920_World_Series) and [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_World_Series). They also won the [American League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League) pennant, making the World Series in the [1954](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_World_Series), [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_World_Series), [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_World_Series), and [2016 seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_World_Series). Between [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_MLB_season) and [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_MLB_season), the team sold out 455 consecutive games, a Major League Baseball record that stood until 2008.[^cite_note-253]

Historically, the Browns have been among the most successful franchises in [American football](18951490.md) history, winning eight titles during a short period of time – [1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1946_Cleveland_Browns_season), [1947](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Cleveland_Browns_season), [1948](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Cleveland_Browns_season), [1949](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_Cleveland_Browns_season), [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_NFL_Championship_Game), [1954](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_NFL_Championship_Game), [1955](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_NFL_Championship_Game), and [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1964_NFL_Championship_Game). The Browns have never played in a [Super Bowl](27718.md), getting close five times by making it to the [NFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_National_Football_League_championship#AFL_Championship_Game_and_NFL_Championship_Game_\(1966%E2%80%931969\))/[AFC Championship Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Championship_Game) in [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_NFL_Championship_Game), [1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_NFL_Championship_Game), [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_NFL_playoffs#Conference_championships), [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987%E2%80%9388_NFL_playoffs#AFC_Championship:_Denver_Broncos_38,_Cleveland_Browns_33), and [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989%E2%80%9390_NFL_playoffs#AFC_Championship:_Denver_Broncos_37,_Cleveland_Browns_21). Former owner [Art Modell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Modell)'s [relocation of the Browns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Browns_relocation_controversy) after the [1995 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_NFL_season) caused tremendous heartbreak and resentment among local fans.[^cite_note-254] After a series of lawsuits, a compromise was reached between Modell, Cleveland mayor [Michael R. White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_R._White_\(politician\)), and NFL and Commissioner [Paul Tagliabue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Tagliabue) to bring back the Browns beginning in the [1999 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NFL_season), retaining all team history.[^cite_note-255] In Cleveland's earlier football history, the [Cleveland Bulldogs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Bulldogs) won the [NFL Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NFL_Championship) in [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_NFL_season),[^cite_note-256] and the [Cleveland Rams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Rams) won the NFL Championship in [1945](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1945_NFL_season) before relocating to Los Angeles.[^cite_note-257]

The Cavaliers won the [Eastern Conference](1305847.md) in [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_NBA_season), [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_NBA_season), [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_NBA_season), [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_NBA_season) and [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_NBA_season) but were defeated in the [NBA Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_NBA_Finals) by the [San Antonio Spurs](72889.md) and [then by](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_NBA_Finals) the Golden State Warriors, respectively. The Cavs won the Conference again in 2016 and won their first NBA Championship coming back from a 3–1 deficit, finally defeating the [Golden State Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_State_Warriors). Afterwards, over 1.3 million people attended a parade held in the Cavs' honor on June 22, 2016, in downtown Cleveland.[^cite_note-258] Previously, the [Cleveland Rosenblums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Rosenblums) dominated the original [American Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Basketball_League_\(1925%E2%80%9355\)),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECondon1979\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsclevelandprodigy0000condpage145_145\]-259] and the [Cleveland Pipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Pipers), owned by [George Steinbrenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Steinbrenner), won the [American Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Basketball_League_\(1961%E2%80%9363\)) championship in 1962.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELivingston2015176–178-260]

The [Cleveland Monsters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Monsters) of the [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) won the [2016 Calder Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Calder_Cup_playoffs). They were the first Cleveland AHL team to do so since the [1964](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963%E2%80%9364_AHL_season) Barons.[^cite_note-261]

### Annual and special events

The [Cleveland Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Marathon) has been hosted annually since 1978,[^cite_note-265] and a monument commemorating one of Cleveland's most prominent track and field athletes, [Jesse Owens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jesse_Owens), stands at the city's Fort Huntington Park.[^cite_note-266] The second [American Chess Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Chess_Congress), a predecessor to the [U.S. Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Chess_Championship), was held in Cleveland in 1871, and won by [George Henry Mackenzie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Henry_Mackenzie).[^cite_note-267] The 1921 and 1957 [U.S. Open Chess Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Open_Chess_Championship) took place in the city, and were won by [Edward Lasker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Lasker) and [Bobby Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Fischer), respectively. The [Cleveland Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Open_\(chess\)) is held annually.[^cite_note-268] In 2014, Cleveland hosted the ninth official [Gay Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Gay_Games) ceremony.[^cite_note-269] In July 2024, the city hosted the [Pan American Masters Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_American_Masters_Games).[^cite_note-270]

## Parks and recreation

See also: [Cleveland Metroparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Metroparks) and [Cleveland Public Parks District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Public_Parks_District)



[Whiskey Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whiskey_Island_\(Cleveland\)) at sunset

Known locally as the "Emerald Necklace", the [Olmsted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Law_Olmsted)-inspired [Cleveland Metroparks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Metroparks) encircle Cleveland and Cuyahoga County. The city proper encompasses the Metroparks' Brookside and Lakefront Reservations, as well as significant parts of the Rocky River, Washington, and Euclid Creek Reservations. The Lakefront Reservation, which provides public access to Lake Erie, consists of four parks: Edgewater Park, Whiskey Island–Wendy Park, East 55th Street Marina, and [Gordon Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Park,_Cleveland).[^cite_note-271]

Three more parks fall under the jurisdiction of the Euclid Creek Reservation: Euclid Beach, Villa Angela, and Wildwood Marina.[^cite_note-272] Further south, bike and hiking trails in the [Brecksville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brecksville_Reservation) and Bedford Reservations, along with Garfield Park, provide access to trails in the [Cuyahoga Valley National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_Valley_National_Park).[^cite_note-273] Also included in the Metroparks system is the [Cleveland Metroparks Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Metroparks_Zoo), established in 1882. Located in Big Creek Valley, the zoo has one of the largest collections of [primates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primate) in North America.[^cite_note-274]

In addition to the Metroparks, the [Cleveland Public Parks District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Public_Parks_District) oversees the city's neighborhood parks, the largest of which is the historic [Rockefeller Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockefeller_Park). The latter is notable for its late 19th century landmark bridges, the Rockefeller Park Greenhouse, and the [Cleveland Cultural Gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Cultural_Gardens), which celebrate the city's ethnic diversity.[^cite_note-275][^cite_note-culturalgardens-155] Just outside of Rockefeller Park, the [Cleveland Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Botanical_Garden) in University Circle, established in 1930, is the oldest civic garden center in the nation.[^cite_note-276] In addition, the [Greater Cleveland Aquarium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Cleveland_Aquarium), located in the historic FirstEnergy Powerhouse in the Flats, is the only independent, free-standing aquarium in the state of Ohio.[^cite_note-277]

## Government and politics

See also: [Mayor of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Cleveland) and [Cleveland City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_City_Council)



[Cleveland City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_City_Hall)

### Government and courts

Cleveland operates on a [mayor–council (strong mayor)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strong-mayor) form of government, in which the mayor is the [chief executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_\(government\)) and the city council serves as the legislative branch. City council members are elected from 15 [wards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ward_\(politics\)) to four-year terms. From 1924 to 1931, the city briefly experimented with a [council–manager government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council%E2%80%93manager_government) under [William R. Hopkins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_R._Hopkins) and [Daniel E. Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_E._Morgan) before returning to the mayor–council system.[^cite_note-politics-278]

Cleveland is served by Cleveland Municipal Court, the first municipal court in the state.[^cite_note-279] The city also anchors the [U.S. District Court for the Northern District of Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Northern_District_of_Ohio), based at the [Carl B. Stokes U.S. Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_B._Stokes_United_States_Courthouse) and the historic [Howard M. Metzenbaum U.S. Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_M._Metzenbaum_United_States_Courthouse). The Chief Judge for the Northern District is [Sara Elizabeth Lioi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sara_Elizabeth_Lioi) and the Clerk of Court is Sandy Opacich.[^cite_note-280] The U.S. Attorney is David Toepfer and the U.S. Marshal is Peter Elliott.[^cite_note-281][^cite_note-282]

### Politics

The office of the mayor has been held by [Justin Bibb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Bibb) since 2022.[^cite_note-283] Previous mayors include progressive Democrat Tom L. Johnson, [World War I](4764461.md)-era [War Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Secretary_of_War) and [BakerHostetler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BakerHostetler) founder [Newton D. Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_D._Baker), [U.S. Supreme Court](31737.md) Justice [Harold Hitz Burton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harold_Hitz_Burton), two-term Ohio Governor and Senator [Frank J. Lausche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_J._Lausche), former U.S. Health, Education, and Welfare Secretary [Anthony J. Celebrezze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_J._Celebrezze), two-term Ohio Governor and Senator [George Voinovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Voinovich), former U.S. Congressman [Dennis Kucinich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dennis_Kucinich), and [Carl B. Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_B._Stokes), the first African American mayor of a major U.S. city. [Frank G. Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_G._Jackson) was the city's longest-serving mayor.[^cite_note-284]

The President of Cleveland City Council is Blaine Griffin, the council [Majority Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority_leader) is [Jasmin Santana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasmin_Santana), and the [Majority Whip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whip_\(politics\)) is Charles Slife.[^cite_note-285] Patricia Britt serves as the Clerk of Council.[^cite_note-286]

Cleveland is a major stronghold for the [Democratic Party](5043544.md) in Ohio. Although local elections are nonpartisan, Democrats still dominate every level of government.[^cite_note-politics-278] Politically, Cleveland and several of its neighboring suburbs comprise [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio's_congressional_districts)'s [11th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio's_11th_congressional_district). The district is represented by [Shontel Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shontel_Brown), one of five Democrats representing the state of Ohio in the [U.S. House of Representatives](19468510.md).[^cite_note-287]

Earlier, from the Civil War era to the 1940s, Cleveland had been dominated by the [Republican Party](32070.md), with the notable exceptions of the Johnson and Baker mayoral administrations.[^cite_note-politics-278] Businessman and Senator [Mark Hanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Hanna) was among Cleveland's most influential Republican figures, both locally and nationally.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHorner201033-288] Another nationally prominent Ohio Republican, former U.S. President [James A. Garfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Garfield), was born in Cuyahoga County's [Orange Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defunct_townships_of_Cuyahoga_County,_Ohio) (today the Cleveland suburb of [Moreland Hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moreland_Hills,_Ohio)). His resting place is the [James A. Garfield Memorial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_A._Garfield_Memorial) in Cleveland's [Lake View Cemetery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_View_Cemetery).[^cite_note-289]

Cleveland has hosted three Republican [national conventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_presidential_nominating_convention), in [1924](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1924_Republican_National_Convention), [1936](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_Republican_National_Convention), and [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Republican_National_Convention).[^cite_note-290] Although the city has not hosted a national convention for the Democrats, it has hosted the [1864 Radical Democratic National Convention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radical_Democratic_Party_\(United_States\)#Cleveland_convention).[^cite_note-291] Additionally, Cleveland has hosted several national election debates, including the [second 1980 U.S. presidential debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_United_States_presidential_debates#October_28:_Second_presidential_debate_\(Music_Hall\)), the [2004 U.S. vice presidential debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_United_States_presidential_debates#October_5:_Vice_presidential_debate_\(Case_Western_Reserve_University\)), one [2008 Democratic primary debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Democratic_Party_presidential_debates_and_forums#February_26,_2008_%E2%80%93_MSNBC_9_pm_EST_%E2%80%93_Cleveland,_Ohio,_Cleveland_State_University), and the [first 2020 U.S. presidential debate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_United_States_presidential_debates#September_29:_First_presidential_debate_\(Case_Western_Reserve_University\)).[^cite_note-debates-292] Founded in 1912, the [City Club of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Club_of_Cleveland) provides a platform for national and local debates and discussions. Known as Cleveland's "Citadel of Free Speech", it is one of the oldest continuous independent free speech and debate forums in the country.[^cite_note-293][^cite_note-294]

Cleveland's Department of Public Safety includes the Division of Police, the Division of Fire, the Division of Emergency Medical Service, and the Division of Animal Care and Control.[^cite_note-295] The director of Public Safety has been Wayne Drummond since 2024.[^cite_note-296]

### Police and law enforcement

Main article: [Cleveland Division of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Division_of_Police)

Like in other major American cities, crime in Cleveland is concentrated in areas with higher rates of poverty and lower access to jobs.[^cite_note-HUD-297][^cite_note-298] In recent decades, the rate of crime in the city, although higher than the national average, experienced a significant decline, following a nationwide trend in falling crime rates.[^cite_note-HUD-297][^cite_note-299] However, as in other major U.S. cities, crime in Cleveland saw an abrupt rise in 2020–21.[^cite_note-300]

Cleveland's law enforcement agency is the Cleveland Division of Police, established in 1866.[^cite_note-CLEpolice-301] The division had roughly 1,100 sworn officers as of 2024, covering five police districts.[^cite_note-302] The district system was introduced in the 1930s by Cleveland Public Safety Director [Eliot Ness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eliot_Ness) (of the [Untouchables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Untouchables_\(law_enforcement\))), who later [ran for mayor of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Cleveland_mayoral_election) in 1947.[^cite_note-CLEpolice-301][^cite_note-303] The [Chief of Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_police) is Dorothy A. Todd.[^cite_note-304] In addition, the [Cuyahoga County Sheriff's Office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_County_Sheriff's_Office) is based in Downtown Cleveland at the [Justice Center Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_Center_Complex).[^cite_note-305]

### Fire department

Main article: [Cleveland Division of Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Division_of_Fire)

Cleveland is served by the [firefighters](146303.md) of the Cleveland Division of Fire, established in 1863.[^cite_note-306] The fire department operates out of 22 active fire stations throughout the city in five battalions. Each Battalion is commanded by a Battalion Chief, who reports to an on-duty Assistant Chief.[^cite_note-fire-307][^cite_note-308]

The Division of Fire operates a [fire apparatus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_apparatus) fleet of twenty-two engine companies, eight ladder companies, three tower companies, two task force [rescue squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rescue_squad) companies, hazardous materials ("haz-mat") unit, and numerous other special, support, and reserve units. The interim [Chief of Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_chief) is Wayne Naida.[^cite_note-fire-307]

### Emergency medical service

Main article: [Cleveland EMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_EMS)

Cleveland EMS is operated by the city as its own municipal third-service EMS division. Cleveland EMS is the primary provider of [Advanced Life Support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advanced_Life_Support) and ambulance transport within the city of Cleveland, while Cleveland Fire assists by providing fire response medical care.[^cite_note-ems-309] Although a merger between the fire and EMS departments was proposed in the past, the idea was subsequently abandoned.[^cite_note-310] The EMS Commissioner is Orlando Wheeler.[^cite_note-ems-309]

### Military

Cleveland serves as headquarters to [Coast Guard District 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Guard_District_9) and is responsible for all [U.S. Coast Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Coast_Guard) operations on the five Great Lakes, the Saint Lawrence Seaway, and surrounding states accumulating 6,700 miles of shoreline and 1,500 miles of international shoreline with Canada. It reports up through the [U.S. Department of Homeland Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Homeland_Security). Station Cleveland Harbor, located in North Coast Harbor, has a responsibility covering about 550 square miles of the federally navigable waters of Lake Erie, including the Cuyahoga and [Rocky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_River_\(Ohio\)) rivers, as well as a number of their tributaries.[^cite_note-311]

### Primary and secondary

Cleveland is served by the [Cleveland Metropolitan School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Metropolitan_School_District). It is the only [K–12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%E2%80%9312_education) district in Ohio under the direct control of the mayor, who appoints a [school board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_education).[^cite_note-312] Approximately 1 square mile (2.6 km<sup>2</sup>) of Cleveland's Buckeye–Shaker neighborhood is part of the [Shaker Heights City School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaker_Heights_City_School_District). The area, which has been a part of the Shaker school district since the 1920s, permits these Cleveland residents to pay the same school taxes as the Shaker residents, as well as vote in the Shaker school board elections.[^cite_note-313]

There are several private and parochial schools in Cleveland.[^cite_note-314] These include [Benedictine High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benedictine_High_School_\(Cleveland,_Ohio\)), [Cleveland Central Catholic High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Central_Catholic_High_School), [Eleanor Gerson School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eleanor_Gerson_School), [St. Ignatius High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Ignatius_High_School_\(Cleveland\)), [St. Joseph Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Joseph_Academy_\(Cleveland,_Ohio\)), [Villa Angela-St. Joseph High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa_Angela-St._Joseph_High_School), and [St. Martin de Porres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Martin_de_Porres_High_School_\(Cleveland\)).

### Colleges and universities

Cleveland is home to a number of colleges and universities. Most prominent among them is [Case Western Reserve University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Case_Western_Reserve_University) (CWRU), a widely recognized research and teaching institution based in [University Circle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Circle) with several major graduate programs.[^cite_note-higher-ed-315]

University Circle also contains the [Cleveland Institute of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Institute_of_Art) and the [Cleveland Institute of Music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Institute_of_Music). Downtown Cleveland is home to [Cleveland State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_State_University), a public research university with eight constituent colleges, and the metropolitan campus of [Cuyahoga Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyahoga_Community_College).[^cite_note-higher-ed-315] [Ohio Technical College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_Technical_College) is also based in Cleveland.[^cite_note-316] Cleveland's suburban universities and colleges include [Baldwin Wallace University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baldwin_Wallace_University) in [Berea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berea,_Ohio), [John Carroll University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Carroll_University) in [University Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Heights,_Ohio), and [Ursuline College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursuline_College) in [Pepper Pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepper_Pike,_Ohio).[^cite_note-higher-ed-315]

### Public library system

Main article: [Cleveland Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Public_Library)

Established in 1869,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECramer1972\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsopenshelvesopenm0000crampage14_14–15\]-317] the Cleveland Public Library is one of the [largest public libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_libraries_in_the_United_States) in the nation with a collection of over 11 million physical materials in 2025.[^cite_note-318] It holds the Northeast Ohio Broadcast Archives,[^cite_note-319] and the [John G. White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Griswold_White) Special Collection, with the largest [chess library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chess_libraries) in the world and a rare collection of [folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore) and books on the Middle East and [Eurasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurasia).[^cite_note-320][^cite_note-321] The library's main building was designed by [Walker and Weeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walker_and_Weeks) and dedicated in 1925,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEJohannesen199968–69-322] under head librarian [Linda Eastman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linda_Eastman), the first woman to lead a major library system in the U.S.[^cite_note-323] Between 1904 and 1920, 15 [libraries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnegie_library) built with funds from [Andrew Carnegie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Carnegie) were opened in the city.[^cite_note-324] Known as the "People's University", the library presently maintains 27 branches.[^cite_note-325] It serves as the headquarters for the [CLEVNET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLEVNET) library consortium, which includes 47 public library systems in Northeast Ohio.[^cite_note-326]

## Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Cleveland)

### Transit

See also: [Greater Cleveland Regional Transit Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Cleveland_Regional_Transit_Authority) and [Streetcars in Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streetcars_in_Cleveland)



An [RTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTA_Rapid_Transit) train approaches [Settlers Landing station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Settlers_Landing_station) on the [Waterfront Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfront_Line)

Cleveland has a bus and rail [mass transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_transport) system operated by the Greater Cleveland Regional Transit Authority (RTA). The [rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_rail_transit) portion is officially called the [RTA Rapid Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RTA_Rapid_Transit), but local residents refer to it as *The Rapid*. It consists of three [light rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) lines, known as the [Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Line_\(Cleveland\)), [Green](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Line_\(Cleveland\)), and [Waterfront Lines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfront_Line), and a [heavy rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_rail) line, the [Red Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Line_\(Cleveland\)). In 2008, RTA completed the [HealthLine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HealthLine), a [bus rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_rapid_transit) line, for which naming rights were purchased by the Cleveland Clinic and University Hospitals. It runs along Euclid Avenue from downtown through University Circle, ending at the [Louis Stokes Station at Windermere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Stokes_Station_at_Windermere) in East Cleveland.[^cite_note-358] In 1968, Cleveland became the first city in the nation to have a direct rail transit connection linking the city's downtown to its major airport.[^cite_note-RTA-history-66]

### Walkability

In 2021, [Walk Score](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walk_Score) ranked Cleveland the 17th most walkable of the 50 largest cities in the U.S., with a Walk Score of 57, a Transit Score of 45, and a Bike Score of 55 (out of a maximum of 100). Cleveland's most walkable areas can be found in the Downtown, Ohio City, Detroit–Shoreway, University Circle, and Buckeye–Shaker neighborhoods.[^cite_note-359] Like other major cities, the urban density of Cleveland reduces the need for private vehicle ownership. In 2016, 23.7% of Cleveland households lacked a car, while the national average was 8.7%. Cleveland averaged 1.19 cars per household in 2016, compared to a national average of 1.8.[^cite_note-360]

### Roads



One of the "Guardians of Traffic" at the [Hope Memorial Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_Memorial_Bridge)

Cleveland's road system consists of numbered streets running roughly north–south, and named avenues, which run roughly east–west. The numbered streets are designated "east" or "west", depending on where they lie in relation to Ontario Street, which bisects Public Square.[^cite_note-361] The two downtown avenues which span the Cuyahoga change names on the west side of the river. Superior Avenue becomes Detroit Avenue on the West Side, and Carnegie Avenue becomes Lorain Avenue. The bridges that make these connections are the [Hope Memorial (Lorain–Carnegie) Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hope_Memorial_Bridge) and the [Veterans Memorial (Detroit–Superior) Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit%E2%80%93Superior_Bridge).[^cite_note-362][^cite_note-363]

### Freeways

Cleveland is served by three two-digit [interstate highways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_Highway_System) – [I-71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_71), [I-77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_77_in_Ohio), and [I-90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_90_in_Ohio) – and by two three-digit interstates – [I-480](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_480_\(Ohio\)) and [I-490](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_490_\(Ohio\)).  Running due east–west through the West Side suburbs, I-90 turns northeast at the junction with I-490, and is known as the Cleveland Inner Belt.[^cite_note-innerbelt-364] The [Cleveland Memorial Shoreway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Memorial_Shoreway) carries [SR 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_Route_2) along its length, and at varying points carries [US 6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_6_in_Ohio), [US 20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_20_in_Ohio) and I-90.[^cite_note-365] At the junction with the Shoreway, I-90 makes a 90-degree turn in the area known as [Dead Man's Curve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dead_Man's_Curve), then continues northeast.[^cite_note-366] The Jennings Freeway ([SR 176](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_Route_176)) connects I-71 just south of I-90 to I-480.[^cite_note-innerbelt-364] A third highway, the Berea Freeway ([SR 237](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_State_Route_237) in part), connects I-71 to the airport and forms part of the boundary between Brook Park and Cleveland's Hopkins neighborhood.[^cite_note-367]

### Airports

Cleveland is a major North American air market, serving 4.93 million people.[^cite_note-368] [Cleveland Hopkins International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Hopkins_International_Airport) is the city's primary major airport and an [international airport](1519705.md) that serves the broader region. Originally known as Cleveland Municipal Airport, it was the first municipally owned airport in the country.[^cite_note-369] Cleveland Hopkins is a significant regional air freight hub hosting [FedEx Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FedEx_Express), [UPS Airlines](9704675.md), [U.S. Postal Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Postal_Service), and major commercial freight carriers. In addition to Hopkins, Cleveland is served by [Burke Lakefront Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burke_Lakefront_Airport), on the north shore of downtown between Lake Erie and the Shoreway. Burke is primarily a commuter and business airport.[^cite_note-370]

### Seaport

Main article: [Port of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Cleveland)



Shipping containers at the [Port of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Cleveland) as seen from Lake Erie

The [Port of Cleveland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Cleveland), at the Cuyahoga River's mouth, is a major bulk freight and container terminal on Lake Erie, receiving much of the raw materials used by the region's manufacturing industries.[^cite_note-371] The Port of Cleveland is the only container port on the Great Lakes with bi-weekly container service between Cleveland and the [Port of Antwerp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Antwerp) in Belgium on a Dutch service called the Cleveland-Europe Express.[^cite_note-372] In addition to freight, the Port of Cleveland welcomes regional and international tourists who pass through the city on [Great Lakes cruises](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lakes_passenger_steamers).[^cite_note-373]

### Intercity rail and bus

Cleveland has a long [history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_railroad_history) as a major railroad hub in North America. Today, [Amtrak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak) provides service to Cleveland, via the *[Capitol Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitol_Limited_\(Amtrak\))* and *[Lake Shore Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Shore_Limited)* routes, which stop at [Cleveland Lakefront Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Lakefront_Station). Additionally, Cleveland hosts several inter-modal freight railroad terminals, for Norfolk Southern, CSX and several smaller companies.[^cite_note-374][^cite_note-375]

National [intercity bus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercity_bus) service is provided by [Greyhound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greyhound_Lines).[^cite_note-376] [Akron Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/METRO_RTA), [Brunswick Transit Alternative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brunswick_Transit_Alternative), [Laketran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laketran), [Lorain County Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorain_County_Transit), and [Medina County Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medina_County_Transit) provide connecting bus service to the Greater Cleveland Regional Transit Authority. [Geauga County Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geauga_County_Transit) and [Portage Area Regional Transportation Authority (PARTA)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portage_Area_Regional_Transportation_Authority) also offer connecting bus service in their neighboring areas.[^cite_note-377]

## International relations



[Cyrus S. Eaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_S._Eaton) and his wife Anne in [Leipzig](17955.md), [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) in 1960

Cleveland maintains cultural, economic, and educational ties with 28 [sister cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_city) around the world. It concluded its first sister city partnership with [Lima](85423.md), Peru, in 1964.[^cite_note-CLEsisters-378] In addition, Cleveland hosts the [Consulate General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_Slovenia) of the [Republic of Slovenia](27338.md), which, until Slovene independence in 1991, served as an official consulate for [Tito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Josip_Broz_Tito)'s [Yugoslavia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Federal_Republic_of_Yugoslavia).[^cite_note-379] The Cleveland Clinic operates the [Cleveland Clinic Abu Dhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Clinic_Abu_Dhabi) hospital, two outpatient clinics in [Toronto](64646.md), and a hospital campus in London.[^cite_note-380] The Cleveland Council on World Affairs was established in 1923.[^cite_note-381]

Historically, Cleveland industrialist [Cyrus S. Eaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrus_S._Eaton), an apprentice of [John D. Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_D._Rockefeller), played a significant role in promoting dialogue between the U.S. and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) during the [Cold War](325329.md).[^cite_note-382] In October 1915 at Cleveland's [Bohemian National Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohemian_National_Hall_\(Cleveland\)), Czech American and Slovak American representatives signed the [Cleveland Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_Agreement), calling for the formation of a [joint Czech and Slovak state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia).[^cite_note-383]
