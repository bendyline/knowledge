**Kharkiv Oblast** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Харківська область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Kharkivska oblast*, [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Харьковская область, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Russian): *Kharkovskaya oblast*), also referred to as **Kharkivshchyna** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Харківщина), is an [oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Ukraine) (province) in eastern [Ukraine](31750.md).

Kharkiv borders [Luhansk Oblast](494904.md) to the east, [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) to the southeast, [Dnipropetrovsk Oblast](494909.md) to the southwest, [Poltava Oblast](1567650.md) to the west, [Sumy Oblast](1567618.md) to the northwest and [Russia](25391.md)'s [Belgorod Oblast](478857.md) to the north. Its area is 31,400 square kilometres (12,100 sq mi), or 5.2% of the total territory of Ukraine.

The oblast is the third-most populous of Ukraine, with a population of 2,598,961 in 2021, more than half (1.42 million) of whom live in the city of [Kharkiv](2788558.md), the oblast's [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center).[^cite_note-ua2022estimate-3]

## Nomenclature

See also: [Romanization of Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian)

Most of Ukraine's oblasts are named after their [capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) cities, officially called "oblast centers" ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): обласний центр, [translit.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian) *oblasnyi tsentr*). The name of each oblast is a [relative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_clause) [adjective](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjective), formed by adding a feminine [suffix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffix) to the name of respective center city: *Kharkiv* is the center of the *Kharkivs’ka oblast’* (Kharkiv Oblast). Most oblasts are also sometimes referred to in a feminine noun form, following the convention of traditional regional place names, ending with the suffix "-shchyna", as is the case with the Kharkiv Oblast, *Kharkivshchyna*.

## Geography

The oblast borders [Russia](25391.md) ([Belgorod Oblast](478857.md)) to the north, [Luhansk Oblast](494904.md) to the east, [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) to the southeast, [Dnipropetrovsk Oblast](494909.md) to the southwest, [Poltava Oblast](1567650.md) to the west and [Sumy Oblast](1567618.md) to the northwest.

The northern and central parts are part of historic [Sloboda Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_Ukraine), and the southern part is part of historic [Zaporizhzhia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaporizhzhia_\(region\)).

## History

Further information: [Sloboda Ukraine § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sloboda_Ukraine#History)



Starved people on the street during the [Holodomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor) in Kharkiv, 1933

During the [Soviet administrative reform of 1923–1929](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okruhas_of_the_Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic#Reorganization_of_1923%E2%80%931926), the [Kharkov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkov_Governorate) was abolished in 1925 leaving its five [okruhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Okruhas_of_the_Ukrainian_SSR): Okhtyrka (originally Bohodukhiv), Izium, Kupiansk, Sumy, and Kharkiv. Introduced in the Soviet Union in 1923, a similar subdivisions existed in Ukraine back in 1918. In 1930 all okruhas were also abolished with [raions](6719452.md) becoming the first level of subdivision of Ukraine until 1932.

The modern Kharkiv Oblast was established on 27 February 1932. In the summer of 1932, some parts of the oblast were included in the newly created [Donetsk Oblast](494889.md) originally centered in [Artemivsk](3889507.md) (later in [Stalino](562951.md)). Then in the fall, some territories of the Kharkiv Oblast were used in the creation of [Chernihiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv_Oblast). More territories became part of [Poltava Oblast](1567650.md) in fall of 1937 and [Sumy Oblast](1567618.md) in winter of 1939.

During the [Holodomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holodomor) the population of the Kharkiv Oblast together with Kyiv Oblast suffered the most. During [World War II](32927.md), it was the site of large Soviet massacres of Poles ([Piatykhatky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piatykhatky,_Kharkiv_Oblast)) and German massacres of Jews ([Drobytsky Yar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drobytsky_Yar)). The region saw major fighting during World War II in several [Battles of Kharkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Kharkov_\(disambiguation\)) between 1941 and 1943.

During the [1991 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Ukrainian_independence_referendum), 86.33% of votes in Kharkiv Oblast were in favor of the [Declaration of Independence of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_of_Independence_of_Ukraine). A survey conducted in December 2014 by the Kyiv International Institute of Sociology found 4.2% of the oblast's population supported their region joining Russia, 71.5% did not support the idea, and the rest were undecided or did not respond.[^cite_note-DT150103-7]

Following the [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan), there was [pro-Russian unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_pro-Russian_unrest_in_Ukraine) in the region, and central government buildings were taken over by separatists in a failed attempt to create a separatist 'Kharkov's People's Republic'.[^cite_note-osw-8] The region also became a very popular destination for refugees from the [Russian-occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian-occupied_territories_of_Ukraine) [Donetsk](494889.md) and [Luhansk Oblast](494904.md) regions.[^cite_note-osw-8]

### Russian invasion of Ukraine

As part of the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), the Russian military launched a major [military offensive](15077993.md) in the region, which resulted in the [occupation of parts of the oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_occupation_of_Kharkiv_Oblast). By late August, about one third of the territory of Kharkiv Oblast was occupied including [Izium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izium) and [Kupiansk](5156384.md). In July 2022, the [Donetsk People's Republic](42425747.md) signed a memorandum to "liberate Kharkiv from Ukraine".[^cite_note-9]

In early September 2022, the Ukrainian military commenced a [counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Ukrainian_Kharkiv_counteroffensive) in the region. Several settlements in the region were recaptured from Russian control.[^cite_note-theguardian.com-10] By 10 September 2022, Ukraine had recaptured [Kupiansk](5156384.md) and [Izium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izium).[^cite_note-theguardian.com-10] On 11 September, Russia had retreated from many of the settlements it previously occupied in the oblast [^cite_note-11sept-11] and the Russian Ministry of Defense announced a formal withdrawal of Russian forces from most of Kharkiv Oblast stating that an "operation to curtail and transfer troops" was underway."[^cite_note-meduza20220911-12][^cite_note-13] By 12 September 2022, as the Russian front lines in Kharkiv Oblast continued to collapse, Ukrainian forces had managed to push back to the northeastern border with Russia in some areas of the region.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] By 3 October 2022, Russian forces had almost completely withdrawn from Kharkiv Oblast.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] As of 2023, fighting continues in the easternmost parts of the oblast in the [Battle of the Svatove–Kreminna line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Svatove%E2%80%93Kreminna_line).[^cite_note-18] In May 2024, Russia launched a [renewed offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Kharkiv_offensive) in northern Kharkiv Oblast, however the frontline stabilised shortly after and has remained at a halt since, with only marginal advances reported.[^cite_note-19] In late 2024, amidst a [Russian offensive in Donetsk Oblast](77627696.md) and a [Ukrainian offensive into Russia’s Kursk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_offensive_\(2024%E2%80%932025\)), Russian forces [started new offensive operations](79705727.md) in eastern Kharkiv Oblast, with the aim of recrossing the Oskil river and capturing [Kupiansk](5156384.md).[^cite_note-20]

## Economy



View of the [Turboatom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turboatom) plant.

The Kharkiv oblast has a primarily industrial economy, including [engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineering), [metallurgy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metallurgy), [manufacturing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manufacturing), production of chemicals and [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing). It also has an important [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) sector with 19,000 square kilometres of arable land (comprising 5.9% of the total arable lands of Ukraine). Agricultural production grew substantially in 2015.[^cite_note-24]

Also in Kharkiv is the airplane plant for space controlling systems. It is a major center for all branches of engineering, from large-scale manufacture to microelectronics. Also situated in Kharkiv Oblast is a gas field, which is one of the biggest in Ukraine.

## Transport



[Kharkiv Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_Metro)

The Kharkiv Oblast has an undeveloped transport network, 60% of the total transportation falls on the part of the [rail transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport). At the end of 2020, the operational life of the salivary lines of the gas station on the territory of the Kharkiv region was 1520 km.[^cite_note-25]

Kharkiv Salvage University serves 10 million passengers on the river, [road transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_transport), sedation, buses - about 12 million. The largest salvage stations are [Izium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Izium), [Kupiansk](5156384.md), [Liubotyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liubotyn), [Lozova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lozova).

In the Kharkiv Oblast, in 2019, 31.4878 million passengers traveled through travel transport services, or 96.5% of the total in 2018.[^cite_note-26]

The most important highways that pass through the region: [Kharkiv](2788558.md)-[Moscow](19004.md), Kharkiv-[Simferopol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simferopol), Kharkiv-[Rostov-on-Don](317562.md), Kharkiv-[Poltava](38581.md). The total length of highways in the past is more than 9.7 thousand km, of which 97.5% are on hard surfaces.[^cite_note-27] The length of the autoshlyakh of the sovereign venue in the Kharkiv Oblast is 2343.9 km, including 617.4 km - the MITERNINI, 108.2 km - National Monalni, 639.1 km - regions, 979.2 km - Teritorialne. On highways of national importance in the region there are 242 bridges with a total length of 1316.3 linear meters. m.[^cite_note-28]

[Kharkiv airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_International_Airport) serves domestic and international airlines, mainly engaged in passenger transportation.[^cite_note-29]

## Education



[Main building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Projects) of [V. N. Karazin Kharkiv National University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_University_of_Kharkiv)



[Kharkiv National University of Radioelectronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharkiv_National_University_of_Radioelectronics)

For a more comprehensive list, see [List of universities in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Ukraine).

In 2020, 104,900 people aged 15–70 worked in the education sector of the Kharkiv region, or 8.7% of the total number of people employed in the region's economy.[^cite_note-\:6-31]

At the end of 2020, there were 753 preschool education institutions in the region (11 more institutions compared to 2019), designed for 79.7 thousand places. The number of their pupils was 74,100 children (3,400 less than in 2019).[^cite_note-32]

At the beginning of the 2020/21 academic year, there were 734 institutions of general secondary education in Kharkiv region, 258,800 students studied in them and 22,700 teachers (including part-time teachers) worked in them. In 2020, 14,300 graduates received a certificate of complete general secondary education.[^cite_note-\:7-33]

At the end of 2020, there were 39 institutions of professional (vocational and technical) education in the region, in which 13,700 people studied.

At the beginning of the 2020/21 academic year, there were 55 institutions of higher education in the region, and 124,200 people studied in them. In 2020, higher education institutions of the region accepted 29,800 people to study, and graduated 44,000 specialists. Postgraduate students were trained by 55 institutions of higher education and scientific institutions, in which 3,400 postgraduate students received their education. 5.6 thousand people obtained professional preliminary education in 8 educational institutions. More than 19,000 scientific, scientific-pedagogical and pedagogical workers worked in institutions of higher and professional pre-higher education.[^cite_note-34]
