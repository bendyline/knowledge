Direct **presidential elections** are held in Brazil as part of the general elections every four years (which has been regular since 1994), typically in October. The current electoral law provides for a [two-round system](25932.md) in which a candidate must receive more than 50% of the vote to win in the first round; if no candidate passes the 50% threshold, a run-off is held between the top two candidates.[^cite_note-elect-1] Every candidate has a [running mate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_mate) who disputes the post of [vice-president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice-President_of_Brazil); prior to 1966, the vice-president was elected separately.

The country has held presidential elections since 1891, spanning over a period of several different republican governments and [national constitutions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Constitution_of_Brazil).

## Old Republic

See also: [First Brazilian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Brazilian_Republic)

[Presidentialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidentialism) was introduced in Brazil after the [Proclamation of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_Republic_\(Brazil\)) in 1889, and the first election was held in 1891. According to the [1891 Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Constitution_of_1891), the right to vote was restricted to men over 21 years old who were not illiterate, homeless or enlisted-rank soldiers.[^cite_note-2] The elections for president and vice-president were held separately and the same person could be a candidate for both.

Overall, only a small portion of the population voted.[^cite_note-CB\:lc-3] Since [coronelism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronelism) was common, the colonel elites often persuaded people to vote for certain candidates.

## [Vargas Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vargas_Era)

With the [Revolution of 1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_1930), the country was governed until 1930 by a [military triumvirate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilian_Military_Junta_of_1930), while [Getúlio Vargas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Get%C3%BAlio_Vargas) was the *de facto* president (officially President of the Provisional Government).[^cite_note-4] The [new Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Constitution_of_Brazil#Third_Constitution_\(1934\)) predicted that the first president would be chosen by the Congress in an indirect election.

## Fourth Republic

See also: [Fourth Brazilian Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Brazilian_Republic)

After Vargas was forced to resign in 1945, a new state was born under a [democratic constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Constitution_of_Brazil#Fifth_Constitution_\(1946\)) written in 1946. The new Constitution provided for direct elections every five years for both president and vice-president.

## New Republic

Main article: [History of Brazil (1985–present)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Brazil_\(1985%E2%80%93present\))

Under the [current Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Constitution_of_Brazil#Citizen_Constitution_\(1988_%E2%80%93_present\)) enacted in October 1988, elections are held every four years (except for the first one, which was held five years before the second to match the centenary of the [Proclamation of the Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_of_the_Republic_\(Brazil\))) and citizens elect a ticket for both president and vice-president. Voting is mandatory for men and women between 18 and 70 years old who are not illiterate, and optional for people aged 16–17, over 70, and illiterates.[^cite_note-elect-1]
