# Overtime (ice hockey)

**Overtime** is a method of determining a winner in an [ice hockey](14790.md) game when the score is tied after regulation. The main methods of determining a winner in a tied game are the overtime period (commonly referred to as **overtime**), the [shootout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shootout), or a combination of both. If league rules dictate a finite time in which overtime may be played, with no penalty shoot-out to follow, the game's winning team may or may not be necessarily determined.

## Overtime periods



A scoreboard showing an overtime period. Since there are typically three periods in hockey, the fourth period is the overtime period.

**Overtime periods** are extra [periods](14790.md#Periods_and_overtime) beyond the third regulation period during a game, where normal hockey rules apply. Although in the past, full-length overtime periods were played, overtimes today are *[golden goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_goal)* (a form of *[sudden death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudden_death_\(sport\))*), meaning that the game ends immediately when a player scores a [goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goal_\(ice_hockey\)).

### North American overtime

From November 21, 1942,[^cite_note-1] when overtime (a non-sudden death extra period of 10 minutes duration) was eliminated due to war time restrictions and continuing through the [1982–83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_NHL_season) season, all [NHL](21809.md) regular-season games tied after 60 minutes of play ended as ties. On June 23, 1983, the NHL introduced a regular-season sudden death overtime period of five minutes. If the five-minute overtime period ended with no scoring, the game ended as a tie. In contrast, the [World Hockey Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Hockey_Association) used a 10-minute, sudden death regular season overtime period for all seven years of its existence from 1972 to 1979 while awarding a point to both teams if the period resulted in no goals. In the first NHL games to go to overtime, on October 5, 1983, the Minnesota North Stars and Los Angeles Kings skated to a 3–3 tie, and the Detroit Red Wings and Winnipeg Jets tied 6–6. The first regular-season game decided by overtime was on October 8, 1983, as the New York Islanders beat the Washington Capitals 8–7.[^cite_note-2]



[Alexander Ovechkin](755749.md) has the most NHL overtime goals in history, with 27.

In [1987–88](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987%E2%80%9388_AHL_season) and since [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_AHL_season), the [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) has awarded teams one point in the standings for an overtime loss (OTL). In 1998, the AHL introduced a rule where teams will play the five-minute overtime period with four skaters and a goaltender, rather than at full strength (five skaters), except in two-man advantage situations. In a two-man advantage situation, the team with the advantage will play with five skaters against three skaters. The rule was popular and adopted by the NHL and [ECHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHL) the next season.

In the [Stanley Cup playoffs](10490910.md) and in all [tiebreaker games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One-game_playoff), overtime periods are played like regulation periods – teams are at full strength (five skaters, barring penalties), there is no shootout, and each overtime period is 20 minutes with full intermissions between overtime periods. The game ends when either team scores a goal.

In many leagues (including the NHL for regular-season games since the [2005–06 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_NHL_season)) and in international competitions, a failure to reach a decision in a single overtime may lead to a shootout. Some leagues may eschew overtime periods altogether and end games in shootout should teams be tied at the end of regulation. In the [ECHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHL), the [AHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League), and the [Southern Professional Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Professional_Hockey_League), regular season overtime periods are played three on three for one five-minute period, with penalties resulting in the opponents skating one additional player on ice (up to two additional players) for each penalty. If the penalised player returns to the ice, the game becomes 4-on-4 or 5-on-5 until the next stoppage of play, when it becomes 3-on-3. Prior to the [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_AHL_season), the [AHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) set the overtime period at seven minutes,[^cite_note-dandc71014-3] but reverted to the now-standard five-minute period the following year.[^cite_note-4] The idea of using 3-on-3 skaters for the entirety of a five-minute overtime period for a regular season game was adopted by the NHL on June 24, 2015, for use in the [2015–16 NHL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_NHL_season).[^cite_note-5] The [ECHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECHL) has changed the overtime to seven minutes for the 2019–20 season.

The [PWHL](74699992.md) follows the same overtime procedure as the NHL for both preseason and regular season (5 minutes before scoring, plus shootouts) and the playoffs (multiple 20-minute periods before scoring).

Since 1999–00, the team losing in overtime in the NHL (and in most other leagues) receives one point in the standings. If, however, a team pulls its goaltender in overtime other than for a delayed penalty, and the opposing team then scores into the empty net, the team that was scored on forfeits its standings point. As of November 2025, this rule has only come into play three times in the NHL.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

### International overtime

In [IIHF](16760449.md) play, rules for overtime depend on the stage of the competition.[^cite_note-\:0-9]

New overtime procedures debuted at the [2019 IIHF World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_IIHF_World_Championship) that will be in effect for all IIHF championships, including from the [2022 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Olympics) hereafter. All overtime periods will be 3-on-3 regardless of round robin or preliminary (five minutes with a three-round shootout), knockout rounds including third place games (ten minutes with a five-round shootout), or the championship (twenty minutes, no shootout). In the championship game only, if that overtime is scoreless, a full intermission will be conducted before the procedure repeats. The overtime ends on whoever scores next. In all cases, teams will change ends.[^cite_note-10]

### International shootouts

In international competition, **shootouts** (or more formally, **game-winning shots** (**GWS**), and in some European countries, **bullets**, or **bullits**[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]), are often used. Each coach selects three skaters from their team to take [penalty shots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shot_\(ice_hockey\)) one at a time against the opposing [goaltender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goaltender), with teams alternating shots. Each team gets one shot per round. The winner is the team with more goals after three rounds or the team that amasses an unreachable advantage before then (e.g., a team gains a two-goal lead with only one round left). If the shootout is tied after three rounds, tie-breaker rounds are played one at a time (with each team taking one additional shot) until there is a winner.

The IIHF first adopted the game-winning-shot procedure in 1992 when a new playoff procedure in the Winter Olympics and World Championships required a winner for each game. At that time, the shootout was five rounds and only used for knock-out games. In 2006, it was reduced to three rounds and used for all games, eliminating the possibility of tied games at IIHF events. Tie-breaker rounds are still used as needed, and the same or new players can take the tie-break shots, which is also done in reverse order.[^cite_note-13] As of May 2016, all IIHF preliminary round games that are not decided by overtime, are decided by a three-round shootout. However, all playoff & bronze medal games of IIHF top level championships (especially the Olympics) are decided by five round shootouts.[^cite_note-\:0-9] Since 2018 it's five rounds of shootout for all games including round robin games.

Since 2019, the gold-medal game uses multiple 20-minute overtime periods of 3-on-3, and applies to both genders.

### North American shootouts

Most lower minor leagues (ECHL, Central, [UHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Hockey_League)) have featured a shootout where, at the end of regulation, a shootout similar to the international tournament format is used.

However, in 2000, the ECHL adopted the AHL's four-on-four overtime before the shootout.

For the [2004–05 AHL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_AHL_season), the AHL adopted a five-man shootout, which was first used in that league in 1986–87. The standard five-man shootout is used after four-on-four overtime for all minor leagues in North America. The AHL switched to the NHL formatted three-man shootout for the 2014–15 season.[^cite_note-dandc71014-3]

The Central Collegiate Hockey Association added the shootout as of the 2008–09 season.

Following the lead of minor leagues, in the NHL's first post-lockout season of [2005–06](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_NHL_season), the league ends exhibition and regular season games still tied after a five-minute-length, three-skaters-per-side overtime period (as of the [2015–16 NHL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_NHL_season) onwards) with a shootout. The NHL format is a three-round shootout with tiebreaker rounds as needed. All skaters (except goalies) on a team's roster must shoot before any player can shoot a second time. On December 16, 2014, the longest shootout in NHL history went to 20 rounds before [Nick Bjugstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Bjugstad) of the [Florida Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Panthers) scored to defeat the [Washington Capitals](73125.md); the previous record was 15 rounds.[^cite_note-nhl-14]

The shootout is not used in the playoffs for any major North American league. Instead, full 20-minute overtime periods are played until [one team scores a goal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_goal).

In the [National Hockey League](21809.md) and [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) All-Star Skills Competitions, the competition ends in a penalty shootout known as the *Breakaway Relay*.

### Tactics



A shootout between the [Providence Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Providence_Bruins) ([Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins) [AHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) affiliate) and [Bridgeport Sound Tigers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgeport_Sound_Tigers) ([New York Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Islanders) [AHL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League) affiliate)

Tactics are very important during penalty shots and overtime shootouts for both the shooter and the goalie. Both shooters and goalies commonly consult their teammates and coaches for advice on the opposing player's style of play. Shooters often consider the goalie's strengths and weaknesses (such as a fast glove or stick [save](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Save_\(ice_hockey\))), preferred goaltending style (such as [butterfly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butterfly_style) or [stand-up](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stand-up_style)) and method of challenging the shooter. Goaltenders often consider the shooter's shot preference, expected angle of attack, a patented move a shooter commonly uses and even handedness of the shooter.

Most shooters attempt to [out-deke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deke_\(ice_hockey\)) the goalie in order to create a better scoring chance. Former [Detroit Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings) forward [Pavel Datsyuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Datsyuk) and [Tampa Bay Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning) forward [Martin St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_St._Louis) are examples of players who commonly use this tactic. However, it is not uncommon for a shooter to simply shoot for an opening without deking. This is commonly referred to as [sniping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ice_hockey_terminology). This is most commonly performed when a goalie challenges a shooter by giving them an open hole (by keeping a glove, pad or stick out of position or being out of sound goaltending position altogether to tempt the shooter to aim for the given opening). Former NHL forwards [Markus Näslund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markus_N%C3%A4slund) and [Brett Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Hull) are two players commonly referred to as [snipers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ice_hockey_terminology). Very rarely a shooter may take a [slapshot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slapshot) or [wrist shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wrist_shot) from the [point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_\(ice_hockey\)) or top of the [slot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slot_\(ice_hockey\)). This is almost exclusively performed when a shooter either has a high level of confidence in their shot or they attempt to catch the goalie by surprise. Retired players such as [Brian Rolston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Rolston), [Todd Bertuzzi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Bertuzzi), [Chris Pronger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Pronger), and [Daniel Sedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Sedin) have all used this tactic with success.

- March 24, 1936: The [Detroit Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings)' [Mud Bruneteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mud_Bruneteau) ends the longest Stanley Cup playoff game to date, scoring the game's only goal in a 1–0 victory over the [Montreal Maroons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Maroons). The goal came at 116\:30 of sextuple overtime, for a total of 176\:30 of game play. It is also the longest NHL game played, being a mere 3\:30 short of the equivalent of playing three games back-to-back-to-back. [Normie Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normie_Smith) made an unofficial record 92 saves in that game.

- February 4, 1939: The [Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins)' [Mel Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mel_Hill) scores his third overtime goal of the Bruins' Stanley Cup semi-final series against the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers), setting an unsurpassed (as of now) NHL record for most overtime goals in a single playoff series, earning him the nickname thereafter of "Sudden Death" Hill. The series itself involves four overtime games, two of which go to triple overtime.

- April 23, 1950: [Pete Babando](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Babando) scores at 8\:31 of double overtime to give the Detroit Red Wings a 4–3 win for the title in the seventh game of the [1950 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_Stanley_Cup_Final) over the New York Rangers. It is the first time that a seventh game of a Final series goes to overtime.

- April 21, 1951: [Bill Barilko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Barilko) scores at 2\:53 of overtime to give the [Toronto Maple Leafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs) a 3–2 win in the fifth game of the [1951 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_Stanley_Cup_Final) over the [Montreal Canadiens](42966.md) for a 4–1 title victory. All five games in the series need to have overtime to be decided. Barilko was killed in a private plane crash in a remote part of northern Ontario a few months later. His story later serves as inspiration for the song "[Fifty Mission Cap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifty_Mission_Cap)" by the Canadian band [The Tragically Hip](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tragically_Hip).

- April 16, 1954: [Tony Leswick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Leswick)'s shot hit Montreal Canadiens' defenceman [Doug Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Harvey_\(ice_hockey\))'s glove and went into the net at 4\:20 of overtime to give the Detroit Red Wings a 2–1 win and the title in the seventh game of the [1954 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_Stanley_Cup_Final) over the Canadiens. No seventh game of a Final series has gone to overtime since.

- April 23, 1964: [Bobby Baun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Baun) of the [Toronto Maple Leafs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Maple_Leafs) nets a game winner against Detroit 1\:43 into overtime in game six of the Finals to tie the series 3–all. The goal is notable because Baun broke his ankle earlier in the game. It was frozen and taped, and Baun returned to the ice to score the winning goal.

- May 10, 1970: One of the most indelible moments in sports history is the sight of [Bobby Orr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobby_Orr)'s "in flight" goal that gave the Boston Bruins a 4–3 win and a four-game sweep of the [St. Louis Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues) for the title.

- May 24, 1980: [Bob Nystrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Nystrom) of the [New York Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Islanders) scores the Stanley Cup clinching goal at 7\:11 of overtime, beating the Philadelphia Flyers in six games for the title.

- April 14, 1981: After sitting on the bench for the entire game, [Mike Crombeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Crombeen) scored the winning goal at 5\:16 of double overtime to give the [St. Louis Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues) a 4–3 win over the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins).

- April 10, 1982: "[Miracle on Manchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miracle_on_Manchester)" – Rookie [Daryl Evans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daryl_Evans) gives the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) a 6–5 win over the [Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton_Oilers) at 2\:35 of overtime. The Kings had trailed the Oilers 5–0 after the second period of game three of the Smythe Division semifinal. This still remains the largest single game playoff comeback in NHL history.

- May 12, 1986: [Doug Wickenheiser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Wickenheiser)'s overtime goal gives the St. Louis Blues a 6–5 win over the [Calgary Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Flames) in game six of the Campbell Conference Final. The goal, known as the "[Monday Night Miracle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Monday_Night_Miracle_\(ice_hockey\))", caps a 5–2 comeback and makes it more impressive that the three goals needed to tie the game were scored in the last ten minutes of the third period.

- May 18, 1986: A [Brian Skrudland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Skrudland) goal ends the shortest overtime in NHL history at just nine seconds. The winning goal gives the Montreal Canadiens a 3–2 victory over the Calgary Flames in game two of the [1986 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Stanley_Cup_Final).

- April 18, 1987: "[Easter Epic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Easter_Epic)" – [Pat LaFontaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_LaFontaine) of [New York Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Islanders) scores a goal against [Washington Capitals](73125.md) at 8\:47 of quadruple overtime which ends the longest game seven in NHL playoff history. Islanders goaltender [Kelly Hrudey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Hrudey) makes a record 73 saves.

- May 15, 1990: After hardly playing in overtime, [Petr Klima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Kl%C3%ADma) came off the bench late in triple overtime and scored almost immediately to end the longest overtime in NHL Finals history, with 4\:47 left. The goal gave the Edmonton Oilers a 3–2 victory over the Boston Bruins to open the [1990 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Stanley_Cup_Final), setting the stage for the Oilers' fifth cup in seven years.

- April 24, 1993: In game four of the Adams division semifinal between the [Buffalo Sabres](69918.md) and the Boston Bruins, Sabres forward [Brad May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brad_May) scores in overtime to give Buffalo a 6–5 win and a four-game sweep of the Bruins in the series. Due to Buffalo commentator [Rick Jeanneret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rick_Jeanneret)'s colorful play call when May scored, this game has been referred to in Buffalo as "May Day".

- April to June 1993: After losing in overtime of game one of the Adams division semifinal to the [Quebec Nordiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Nordiques), the Montreal Canadiens go on to win ten consecutive overtime games en route to winning the [Stanley Cup](66968.md). The Habs score another overtime winner the following year against the Boston Bruins, making it eleven consecutive playoff overtime wins.

- May 14, 1993: In game seven of the Patrick Division finals, [David Volek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Volek), who spent most of the season as a healthy scratch, scored the series winning goal to give the [New York Islanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Islanders) a 4–3 victory ending the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) chances at a three-peat.

- April 27, 1994: [Dominik Hašek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominik_Ha%C5%A1ek) of the Buffalo Sabres stops all 70 shots produced by the [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils) as [Dave Hannan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Hannan) scores the lone goal over a sprawling [Martin Brodeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Brodeur) at 5\:43 of quadruple overtime in game six of the Eastern Conference quarterfinal matchup.

- April 30, 1994: [Pavel Bure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavel_Bure) scores 2\:20 into double overtime of the seventh game of the opening round of Vancouver's playoff series with Calgary. The win gives the [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks) three consecutive overtime wins over the favored [Calgary Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Flames), who squander a 3–1 series lead.

- May 27, 1994: [Stéphane Matteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%A9phane_Matteau) scores the game-winning goal at 4\:24 of double overtime with a wrap-around, beating [Martin Brodeur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Brodeur) and the New Jersey Devils in game seven, advancing the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) to the Stanley Cup Final (where they would ultimately win it for the first time since 1940). It is Matteau's second goal in double overtime periods of the series. The game is best known for Rangers announcer [Howie Rose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howie_Rose)'s emphatic radio call of the winning goal.

- April 24, 1996: [Petr Nedvěd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Nedv%C4%9Bd) scores with 44.6 seconds remaining in quadruple overtime to give the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) a 3–2 win over the [Washington Capitals](73125.md) to tie their Eastern Conference quarterfinal series at two games apiece.

- May 16, 1996: [Detroit Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings) captain [Steve Yzerman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Yzerman) steals the puck from [Wayne Gretzky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_Gretzky) and scores on a [slap shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slap_shot) from the [blue line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_line_\(ice_hockey\)) with 18\:45 remaining in double overtime, eliminating the [St. Louis Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues) in seven games to cap off their matchup in the Western Conference semifinals.

- June 10, 1996: [Uwe Krupp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uwe_Krupp) became the 12th player in NHL history, and the first in 16 years, to end the Stanley Cup Final in overtime, scoring a goal at 4\:31 of triple overtime, giving the [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md) a 1–0 win and a sweep of the [Florida Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Panthers) for the title.

- June 19, 1999: [Brett Hull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Hull) scores with 5\:09 left in triple overtime of game six to win the title for the [Dallas Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Stars) over the [Buffalo Sabres](69918.md). The goal is considered controversial by some fans due to disagreement on if the goal by Hull was legal or not. (see [1999 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Stanley_Cup_Final)). Dallas won 2–1 for a 4–2 title victory.

- May 4, 2000: [Keith Primeau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Primeau) of the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) put a shot over the left shoulder of [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins) goaltender [Ron Tugnutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Tugnutt) with 7\:59 left in quintuple overtime, ending the longest game since the NHL expanded in 1967, and the game itself since 1936.

- June 8, 2000: [Jason Arnott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Arnott) scores on Dallas Stars' goalie [Ed Belfour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Belfour) in double overtime of game six to give the New Jersey Devils their second title.

- April 24, 2003: [Petr Sykora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_Sykora) of the Mighty Ducks of Anaheim scores on a centering feed from [Adam Oates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Oates) on [Marty Turco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Turco) of the Dallas Stars 48 seconds into quintuple overtime to win Game 1 of their Western Conference semifinal series. The Mighty Ducks appeared to win at 16\:41 of triple overtime with a goal by [Steve Rucchin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Rucchin), but it was waved off for the net coming off the pegs.

- April 11, 2007: [Roberto Luongo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Luongo), goaltender for the Vancouver Canucks, wins his first career playoff game while making 72 saves, one shy of Kelly Hrudey's record. [Henrik Sedin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Sedin) scores the winning goal at 18\:06 of quadruple overtime in Game 1 of the Western quarterfinals, beating the Dallas Stars 5–4. The game is Vancouver's longest in history, and the Stars' second longest, behind their five-overtime loss to Anaheim in 2003.

- May 4, 2008: [Brenden Morrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenden_Morrow) scores on [San Jose Sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Sharks) goalie [Evgeni Nabokov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeni_Nabokov) at 9\:03 of quadruple overtime in game six of the Western Conference Semi-final between the Sharks and Stars. The game sees an incredible goaltending duel as Nabokov makes 53 saves in the loss while [Marty Turco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Turco) of Dallas makes 61 saves for the win.

- June 2, 2008: In Game 5 of the [2008 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Stanley_Cup_Final), the [Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pittsburgh_Penguins)' [Petr Sýkora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petr_S%C3%BDkora) told [Pierre Maguire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre_Maguire) of [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC) that he would score the winning goal in overtime. At 9\:57 of triple overtime, Sýkora scored on Detroit Red Wings' goaltender [Chris Osgood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Osgood) and sent the series back to Pittsburgh for game six.

- June 9, 2010: [Patrick Kane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Kane) of the [Chicago Blackhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Blackhawks) shoots a goal past [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers) goalie [Michael Leighton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Leighton) 4\:10 into overtime of game six of the Stanley Cup Final to give the Blackhawks a 4–3 win over the Flyers for their first title since 1961.

- April 27, 2011: [Alexandre Burrows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandre_Burrows) of the [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks) scored at 5\:22 of overtime past [Corey Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Crawford) of the Chicago Blackhawks to win the series 4–3 and the game 2–1, sending the Canucks to the Western Conference Semifinals, avoiding squandering a 3–0 series lead and a 1–0 lead in game seven in the third period.

- June 4, 2011: Another Burrows goal ends the second-shortest overtime in NHL history at just eleven seconds. The winning goal gives the Vancouver Canucks a 3–2 victory over the Boston Bruins in game two of the Stanley Cup Final.

- May 25, 2012: [Adam Henrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Henrique) of the New Jersey Devils scored at 1\:03 into overtime past [Henrik Lundqvist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henrik_Lundqvist) of the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) in game six of the Eastern Conference Final to send the Devils to the Stanley Cup Final for the first time since 2003.

- May 13, 2013: [Patrice Bergeron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrice_Bergeron) of the Boston Bruins scored at 6\:05 of overtime past [James Reimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Reimer) of the Toronto Maple Leafs to win the series 4–3 and the game 5–4, to send the Bruins to the Conference Semifinals to face the Rangers. It also capped a historic comeback in a game seven, coming back from a three-goal deficit in the third period. With fifty–one seconds to go, they tied the game. The tying goal was also scored by Bergeron. The 2012–13 Boston Bruins became the first team in NHL history to do so in the [Stanley Cup playoffs](10490910.md).

- June 12, 2013: [Andrew Shaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Shaw_\(ice_hockey\)) scored with 7\:52 left in triple overtime as the Chicago Blackhawks beat the [Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins) 4–3 to open the Stanley Cup Final, ending the fifth-longest game in Stanley Cup Final history.

- June 13, 2014: [Alec Martinez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alec_Martinez) took a rebound off a Tyler Toffoli shot blocked by Henrik Lundqvist and scored with 5\:17 left in double overtime in game five of the [2014 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Stanley_Cup_Final), becoming the 16th player to win the Stanley Cup with an overtime goal as the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) beat the New York Rangers 3–2, clinching the title 4–1 in the longest game in Kings history to that point (total game time 94\:43). He became the first player to score two series-clinching playoff overtime goals in the same season since [Martin Gélinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_G%C3%A9linas) in 2004; on June 1, he scored 5\:47 into overtime to defeat the Chicago Blackhawks 5–4 in game seven of the Western Conference Finals.

- May 13, 2015: [Derek Stepan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Stepan) scored with 8\:36 left in the first overtime in game seven in the Eastern Conference second round against the [Washington Capitals](73125.md) to send the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) to the Eastern Conference Finals. The Capitals also had a 3–1 series lead before losing the final three games and ultimately losing the series.

- May 19, 2015: [Marcus Kruger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Kr%C3%BCger) scored with 3\:48 left in the third overtime in game two of the Western Conference Finals, as the Chicago Blackhawks beat the [Anaheim Ducks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anaheim_Ducks), 3–2, tying the series at 1–all. This was Anaheim's first playoff loss at home that season and Chicago's longest overtime game to date. Chicago goalie [Corey Crawford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corey_Crawford) made 60 saves while defenceman [Duncan Keith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan_Keith) played seconds shy of 50 minutes on ice. Earlier in the second overtime period, Andrew Shaw used his head to direct the puck into the net, but the goal did not count.

- April 24, 2016: [John Tavares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Tavares_\(ice_hockey\)) scored the series-winner with 9\:19 left in double overtime in game six of the Eastern Conference first round as the New York Islanders won a playoff series for the first time since 1993, beating the Florida Panthers 2–1, for a 4–2 series victory. He beat Roberto Luongo off a rebound of his own shot with a wraparound. It ended the longest home game in Islanders' history. Tavares also scored with 53.2 seconds left in regulation to force overtime. Three of those games in this series have gone to overtime, this and game five needed two.

- May 6, 2016: [Mike Fisher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Fisher_\(ice_hockey\)) scored with 8\:48 left in triple overtime in game four of the Western Conference second round as the [Nashville Predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Predators) beat the San Jose Sharks, 4–3, to tie the series at two games all. His goal ended the longest playoff game in Predators' history. [Joe Pavelski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joe_Pavelski) could have won it at 7\:34 of overtime, but officials waved it off as he crashed into [Pekka Rinne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pekka_Rinne), pinning him down before the puck crossed the goal line.

- May 25, 2017: [Chris Kunitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Kunitz) scored at 5\:09 of double overtime as the Pittsburgh Penguins beat the Ottawa Senators to advance to play the Nashville Predators in the Stanley Cup Final in winning game seven of the Eastern Conference Finals, 3–2. [Sidney Crosby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidney_Crosby) sent a soft backhand pass to Kunitz, whose knuckling shot beat [Craig Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Anderson_\(ice_hockey\)) from the right faceoff circle.

- May 7, 2018: [Evgeny Kuznetsov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evgeny_Kuznetsov) scored at 5\:27 of overtime as the Washington Capitals eliminated the two-time defending champion Pittsburgh Penguins in game six of the Eastern Conference second round to advance to the Conference Finals for the first time in 20 years. [Alexander Ovechkin](755749.md) passed the puck back to Kuznetsov, who tapped it away from Sidney Crosby on a turnover before beating [Matt Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matt_Murray_\(ice_hockey,_born_1994\)) between the pads.

- April 21, 2019: [Tomáš Hertl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Hertl) scored the first short-handed overtime goal in a multiple overtime game in Stanley Cup playoff history with his goal at 11\:17 in double overtime to beat the host [Vegas Golden Knights](47712000.md) 2–1 to force a seventh game in the Western Conference first round. Sharks goalie [Martin Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Jones_\(ice_hockey\)) saved 58 of the Knights' 59 shots, a Sharks playoff game record.

#### Canadian Interuniversity Sport – men

Nick MacNeil scored the game-winner at 11\:53 of the seventh period overall.

[York University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_University) [Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/York_Lions) and [Lakehead University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakehead_University) [Thunderwolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakehead_Thunderwolves) went to a fourth overtime period (50\:13 minutes of overtime) on February 14, 2007, in [Thunder Bay, Ontario](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thunder_Bay,_Ontario), to decide a winner in OUA men's playoff hockey action. Lakehead won the game at the 13-second mark of the fourth overtime period.

The [2025 University Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_U_Sports_University_Cup) quarterfinal between the [Toronto Metropolitan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toronto_Metropolitan_University) [Bold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TMU_Bold) and the [Mount Royal University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Royal_University) [Cougars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Royal_Cougars) ended in a 5–4 victory for the Bold after a goal from Spencer Shugrue at 3\:33 of quintuple overtime. The game set the record for the longest game in U Sports men's hockey history at 143\:33, of which 83\:33 were overtime.[^cite_note-40]

#### Canadian Interuniversity Sport – women

Morgan McHaffie scored at 7\:14 of sextuple overtime to lead the [Queen's Golden Gaels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Golden_Gaels) to a 2–1 win over the host [Guelph Gryphons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guelph_Gryphons) in the first game of the best-of-three OUA women's hockey final, March 2, 2011. The game, which lasted 167 minutes and 14 seconds, including 107\:14 of extra time, is the longest on record in CIS or NCAA hockey – women's or men's. Winning goaltender Mel Dodd-Moher made 66 saves, while Danielle Skoufranis made 44 saves in a losing cause. It is the longest game played sanctioned by [Hockey Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_Canada).

#### International Hockey League

On May 12, 2008, one of the longest games in IHL history, if not the longest, took place in [Fort Wayne, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne,_Indiana). It was the seventh game of the [Turner Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turner_Cup) Final between the hometown [Fort Wayne Komets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Wayne_Komets) and [Port Huron Icehawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Huron_Icehawks). The game was tied 2–2 through regulation. The first two extra periods solved nothing, but 23 seconds into the third overtime period, at some point after midnight ET, Justin Hodgman scored the winning goal to give the Komets their fifth Turner Cup title. It was the club's first since 1993, and their sixth overall, with their last championship being the [Colonial Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonial_Cup_\(hockey\)) in 2003. The Komets would win again the following year with an easy game five victory at home, which was the first time in franchise history they won back-to-back championships. They would follow up with a third consecutive Turner Cup in 2010, again clinching on home ice, securing a dynasty.

The longest game in NCAA hockey history was played at [Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame) in [Notre Dame, Indiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre_Dame,_Indiana), on March 6, 2015. [UMass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Massachusetts_Amherst) beat [Notre Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Notre_Dame), 4–3, in the [Hockey East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hockey_East) first round with 8\:18 left in quintuple overtime. Shane Walsh scored the winning goal just after 1\:00 am local time.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]

The previous longest was played on March 12, 2010. [Quinnipiac University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quinnipiac_University) beat [Union College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_College), 3–2, in the [ECAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_College_Athletic_Conference) Quarterfinals, as Greg Holt scored with 9\:38 left in quintuple overtime. The 3rd longest game in NCAA hockey history (the longest game in NCAA playoff history) was played on March 27–28, 2021. The [University of Minnesota Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_Duluth) beat the [University of North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_North_Dakota) 3–2. Luke Mylymok scored the game-winning goal with 17\:47 left in quintuple overtime.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] The 4th longest game in NCAA hockey history was played on March 5, 2006. [Yale University](34273.md) beat Union College, 3–2, in the [ECAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_College_Athletic_Conference) Hockey League first-round playoff game after 81\:35 of overtime. [David Meckler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Meckler) scored the winning goal with Yale shorthanded.[^cite_note-46]

The longest game in [NCAA Division III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Division_III) hockey history, and the fourth longest in NCAA history overall, began at 7\:05 pm on February 27, 2010, and ended at 12\:35 am of the following day. [Gustavus Adolphus College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavus_Adolphus_College) beat [Augsburg College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augsburg_College), 6–5, to advance to the [MIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_Intercollegiate_Athletic_Conference) championship game after 78\:38 of overtime. A 2000 NCAA regional final in men's ice hockey between St. Lawrence University and Boston University ended with 63\:53 of overtime. Manitoba native and minor hockey buddy of Craig McAulay, Robin Carruthers scored the game-winning goal after four periods of overtime play

A March 30, 1991, game between [Northern Michigan University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Michigan_University) and [Boston University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_University) ended with Northern Michigan earning an 8–7 victory over Boston University. Unlikely hero Darryl Plandowski scores in the third overtime period and fifth hour of play to give the Wildcats the title.

A March 8, 1997, game between [Colorado College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colorado_College) and the [University of Wisconsin–Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Wisconsin%E2%80%93Madison) ended with Colorado College winning, 1–0, after 69\:30 of overtime.

On March 26, 2006, the Wisconsin Badgers beat the [Cornell Big Red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornell_Big_Red) 1–0 at 11\:13 into the third overtime at the Midwest Regional Final in the [NCAA Tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCAA_Men's_Ice_Hockey_Championship) at the [Resch Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resch_Center) in [Green Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Bay,_Wisconsin). It was the second-longest NCAA Tournament game in its history and the longest 1–0 game in tournament history. It is currently the ninth-longest game all-time in NCAA Division I history.

A March 11, 2007, game between [St. Cloud State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Cloud_State_University) and [University of Minnesota Duluth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota_Duluth) during the first round of the [WCHA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Collegiate_Hockey_Association) playoffs ended with SCSU winning, 3–2, after 51\:33 of overtime. It is the eighth-longest NCAA Division I game in history.

In the first round of the 2008 [WCHA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Collegiate_Hockey_Association) hockey tournament featuring the fourth-seeded [Minnesota State University, Mankato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minnesota_State_University,_Mankato) Mavericks hosting the seventh-seeded [University of Minnesota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Minnesota) Golden Gophers, the Friday and Sunday games both went into double overtime, and the Saturday night game went into one overtime. The Gophers prevailed two games to one in the series, winning Saturday and Sunday.

On March 3, 2012, in the first round of the 2012 [ECAC Hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ECAC_Hockey) hockey tournament featuring the seventh-seeded [Clarkson Golden Knights men's ice hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clarkson_Golden_Knights_men's_ice_hockey) team hosting the tenth-seeded [RPI Engineers men's ice hockey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RPI_Engineers_men's_ice_hockey) team, Clarkson beat RPI 4–3 at 13\:48 in the third overtime period, after 113\:48 of play. It is currently the seventh-longest game all-time in NCAA Division I history.

#### High school

On February 20, 2020, in Black River Falls, Wisconsin, Black River Falls Co-op vs. Viroqua Co-op girls went a total of six overtimes - a combination of 8 and 12 minute overtimes - total elapsed playtime of 122 minutes, 51 seconds, a new US High School Record. Black River Falls won 2–1 in sextuple overtime. Viroqua goalie Abby Severson stopped 108 of 110 shots in the game.[^cite_note-51]

Marquette vs Orchard Lake St Marys went eight overtimes during the [Michigan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michigan_High_School_Athletic_Association) State Ice Hockey Division 1 Championship game before Tournament officials stopped the game in consideration of the health and welfare of the players on March 8, 2008. The 1–1 tie resulted in the two teams being declared co-champions. The game lasted 109 minutes.[^cite_note-52] Ryan Morley Stockton of St. Mary's had a MHSAA-record 58 saves.[^cite_note-53]

In a 1996 [FCIAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FCIAC) quarterfinal matchup in [Darien, Connecticut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darien,_Connecticut), between archrivals [Wilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilton_High_School) and [Ridgefield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridgefield_High_School_\(Connecticut\)), Wilton's Bill Lenich scored after 104 minutes of hockey, in the 8th eight-minute overtime period.[^cite_note-54]

The longest game in American high school history was an [Aurora High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurora_High_School_\(Aurora,_Ohio\))–[Solon High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solon_High_School) game in which Aurora won in the eighth overtime period of the [Ohio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ohio_High_School_Athletic_Association) state playoffs.[^cite_note-55] The winning goal was scored with 3\:52 left in the 8th overtime (105th minute), setting an American record.[^cite_note-56]

A 2026 12U hockey game in Minnesota between St. Paul and Cottage Grove went to twelve overtimes, for a total game time of 180 minutes. The game was played over the course of three days, and ended when organizers opted to hold a shootout instead of a 13th overtime. Cottage Grove won 2-1.[^cite_note-57]

Since 2015, most state tournaments allow up to 5 overtime periods (4-on-4 after first overtime), after which best-of-3-round shootouts and extra rounds if needed are conducted, to eliminate co-champions.
