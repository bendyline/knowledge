**Kabayan**, officially the **Municipality of Kabayan** ([Ilocano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilocano_language): *Ili ti Kabayan*; [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language): *Bayan ng Kabayan*), is a [municipality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipality_of_the_Philippines) in the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Province) of [Benguet](274018.md), [Philippines](23440.md). According to the 2024 census, it has a population of 15,841 people.[^cite_note-5]

The town is the site of centuries-old [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_people) [mummies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabayan_Mummies) buried inside caves scattered around its villages.[^cite_note-6] The third highest mountain in the Philippines, [Mount Pulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pulag), is located in the territorial boundary of the vegetable farming town.

## Etymology

The name *Kabayan* was derived from the term *Kaba-ayan*, from the [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_language) word *ba-ay*, a root crop vine thriving in the place. Most of the early [Ibaloi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibaloi_people) settlements, in the area, which include *Eddet* and *Duacan*, were named after grasses in the heavily forested area.[^cite_note-BenguetGov-7]

## History

### Pre-colonial period

The first Ibaloi settlers in Benguet arrived at *Imbose* (or *Embosi*), located in present-day Kabayan.[^cite_note-BenguetGov-7][^cite_note-DILG-CAR-8]

[Mummification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mummification) of the dead was practiced long before [Spanish colonizers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1521-1898\)) reached the place.[^cite_note-BenguetGov-7][^cite_note-MummyTombs-9]

### Spanish period

In the late 1800s, Spanish colonizers reached *Kabayan* via trails constructed throughout the mountain region. Organized into three [rancherias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancheria), namely *Adaoay*, *Kabayan*, and *Lutab* (or *Dutab*), Kabayan was registered under the *comandancia politico-militar* of Benguet in 1846.[^cite_note-BenguetGov-7][^cite_note-DILG-CAR-8] *Lutab* (currently [barangay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barangay) *[Poblacion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poblacion)* or *Kabayan Central*[^cite_note-BriefHistoryKabayan-10]) was later integrated into the *Kabayan* *rancheria*.[^cite_note-DILG-CAR-8]

The practice of mummification of the dead would be discouraged by the Spaniards, until it would die out.[^cite_note-MummyTombs-9][^cite_note-WMF-11]

### American period

During the [American rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Philippines_\(1898-1946\)), *Kabayan* and *Adaoay* were established as two of the 19 townships of the province of Benguet, upon the issuance of *Act No. 48* by the [Philippine Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Commission) on November 22, 1900.[^cite_note-DILG-CAR-8][^cite_note-NSCBGov-12][^cite_note-BenguetGov2-13]

On August 13, 1908, Benguet would be established with the enactment of *Act No. 1876* as a sub-province of the newly created [Mountain Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province). Six townships of Benguet were later abolished, including *Adaoay*, which was integrated into the township of Kabayan.[^cite_note-DILG-CAR-8][^cite_note-BenguetGov2-13]

### Post-war Era

On June 25, 1963, then-President [Diosdado Macapagal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diosdado_Macapagal) issued *[Executive Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_order_\(Philippines\)) No. 42* converting eight (8) of the thirteen (13) towns (designated as *municipal districts*) of Benguet sub-province into regular municipalities. Kabayan was among them.[^cite_note-GovPH-14]

On June 18, 1966, the sub-province of Benguet was separated from the old [Mountain Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Province) and would be converted into a [regular province](165100.md). Kabayan remained to be a component municipality of the newly established province.[^cite_note-BenguetGov2-13][^cite_note-RA4695-ChanRobles-15]

## Geography

The Municipality of Kabayan is located at [16°37′24″N 120°50′17″E  /  16.6233°N 120.8381°E  / 16.6233; 120.8381](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Kabayan,_Benguet\&params=16.6233_N_120.8381_E_), at the central-eastern section of Benguet. It is bounded by [Buguias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buguias,_Benguet) on the north, [Kibungan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kibungan,_Benguet) on the north-west, [Atok](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atok,_Benguet) on the south-west, [Bokod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bokod,_Benguet) on the south, [Kayapa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kayapa,_Nueva_Vizcaya) on the southeast, and [Tinoc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tinoc,_Ifugao) on the north-east.

According to the [Philippine Statistics Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippine_Statistics_Authority), the municipality has a land area of 242.69 square kilometres (93.70 sq mi) [^cite_note-nscb-area-16] constituting 8.76% of the 2,769.08-square-kilometre- (1,069.15 sq mi) total area of Benguet.

Kabayan is situated 63.50 kilometres (39.46 mi) from the provincial capital [La Trinidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Trinidad,_Benguet), and 316.43 kilometres (196.62 mi) from the country's capital city of [Manila](184334.md).

The economy of Kabayan is primarily driven by [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) and small-scale enterprises. Kabayan's farmers grow temperate [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetables), Arabica [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), and [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice).[^cite_note-35] [Mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining) and [natural resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_resources) contribute to Kabayan's economy through mineral extraction and forest products. Small-scale enterprises bolster the food processing and [handicraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handicraft) production of Kabayan.

## Government

Kabayan, belonging to the [lone congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguet's_at-large_congressional_district) of the province of [Benguet](274018.md), is governed by a mayor designated as its local chief executive and by a municipal council as its legislative body in accordance with the Local Government Code. The mayor, vice mayor, and the councilors are elected directly by the people through an election which is being held every three years.

## Tourism

Main article: [Kabayan Mummies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabayan_Mummies)

Kabayan is best known for the antiquated centuries-old [mummies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_Mummies) and [Mount Pulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pulag), the third highest mountain in the Philippines. The Kabayan mummy burial caves are officially proclaimed Philippine National Cultural Treasures pursuant to *Presidential Decree No. 374*, and is under consideration as a [World Heritage Site](44940.md).[^cite_note-37] The mummified body of Apo Annu, a tribal leader, was stolen but recovered by an antique collector and was returned to the town. Archaeologists from various countries have visited the town to promote preservation of the mummies due to deterioration of the cadavers. The caves containing the cadavers of these mummies have been declared by [Monument Watch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monument_Watch?action=edit\&redlink=1) as one of the "100 Most Endangered Sites" in the world.[^cite_note-38]

[Mount Pulag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Pulag) is a destination for mountaineers, hikers, including picnickers. At its summit, the climbers can see the surroundings of the whole north [Luzon](69375.md).

Kabayan also hosts a branch of the [National Museum of the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Museum_of_the_Philippines).[^cite_note-39]
