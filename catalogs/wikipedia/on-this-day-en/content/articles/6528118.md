**Canadian Americans** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Américains canadiens*) are [American citizens](25151566.md) (or in some uses residents) whose ancestry is wholly or partly [Canadian](19851291.md), American Canadian origin, or citizens of either country who hold [dual citizenship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_citizenship).[^cite_note-3] Today, many Canadian Americans and American Canadians hold both US and Canadian citizenship.

The term *Canadian* can mean a nationality or an [ethnicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnicity). Canadians are considered North Americans due their residing in the North American continent. [English-speaking Canadian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Canadian) immigrants easily integrate and assimilate into northern and western U.S. states as a result of many cultural similarities, and in the similar accent in spoken English.[^cite_note-4] [French Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French-Canadians), because of language and culture, tend to take longer to assimilate.[^cite_note-5] However, by the 3rd generation, they are often fully culturally assimilated, and the Canadian identity is more or less folklore.[^cite_note-HEAEG1980-6] This took place, even though half of the population of the province of [Quebec](7954867.md) emigrated to the US between 1840 and 1930.[^cite_note-7] Many [New England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England) cities formed '[Little Canadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Canada_\(term\))', but many of these have gradually disappeared.

This cultural "invisibility" within the larger US population is seen as creating stronger affinity among Canadians living in the US than might otherwise exist.[^cite_note-8] According to US Census estimates, the number of Canadian residents was around 640,000 in 2000.[^cite_note-9] Some sources have cited the number to possibly be over 1,000,000.[^cite_note-Stewart-10] This number, though, is far smaller than the number of Americans who can trace part or the whole of their ancestry to Canada. The percentage of these in the New England states is almost 25% of the total population.

In some regions of the United States, especially New England or the [Midwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midwestern_United_States), a Canadian American often means one whose ancestors came from Canada.[^cite_note-Richard_2002-11]

## Canadian American Day

The [Connecticut State Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connecticut_State_Senate) unanimously passed a bill in 2009, making June 24 Canadian American Day in the state of [Connecticut](6466.md). The bill allows state officials to hold ceremonies at the capitol and other places each year to honor Americans of Canadian ancestry.[^cite_note-12]

## Aboriginal Canadian Americans

As a consequence of Article 3 of the [Jay Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay_Treaty) of 1794, official [First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada) status, or in the United States, [Native American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Americans_in_the_United_States) status, also confers the right to live and work on either side of the border. Unlike the U.S., Canada has not codified the Jay Treaty. Canadian courts readily reject the Jay Treaty free passage of goods right.[^cite_note-13]

## Study

Some institutions in the United States focus on Canadian-American studies, including the Canadian-American Center at the [University of Maine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Maine),[^cite_note-14] the Center for Canadian American studies at [Western Washington University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Washington_University),[^cite_note-15] and the [University at Buffalo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_at_Buffalo) Canadian-American Studies Committee.[^cite_note-16]
