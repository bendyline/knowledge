The **demographic profile of Cameroon** is complex for a country of its population. [Cameroon](5447.md) comprises an estimated 250 distinct [ethnic groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups), which may be formed into five large regional-cultural divisions:

- **western highlanders** (**[Semi-Bantu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-Bantu)** or **grassfielders**), including the [Bamileke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamileke), [Bamum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamum_people) (or *Bamoun*), and many smaller [Tikar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tikar) groups in the [Northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Province,_Cameroon) (est. 38% of total population);

- **coastal tropical forest peoples**, including the [Bassa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bassa_people_\(Cameroon\)), [Duala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duala_people) (or *Douala*), and many smaller groups in the [Southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwest_Province,_Cameroon) (12%);

- **southern tropical forest peoples**, including the [Beti-Pahuin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beti-Pahuin), Bulu (a subgroup of Beti-Pahuin), [Fang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fang_people) (subgroup of Beti-Pahuin), [Maka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maka_people), [Njem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Njem), and [Baka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baka_\(nomadic_Central_African_people\)) [pygmies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pygmy) (18%);

- **predominantly [Islamic](6037917.md) peoples** of the northern semi-arid regions (the [Sahel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahel)) and central highlands, including the [Fulani](102383.md) ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Peul* or *Peuhl*; [Fula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_language): *Fulɓe*) (14%); *and*

- **the "[Kirdi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirdi)"**, non-Islamic or recently Islamic peoples of the northern desert and central highlands (18%).

The Cameroon government held two national censuses during the country's first 44 years as an independent country, in 1976 and again in 1987. Results from the second head count were never published. A third census, expected to take years to produce results, began on November 11, 2005, with a three-week interviewing phase. It is one of a series of projects and reforms required by the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) as prerequisites for foreign [debt relief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Debt_relief). The first results were published in 2010.[^cite_note-1]

## Population



Population, [fertility rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fertility_rate) and [net reproduction rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Net_reproduction_rate), United Nations estimates

According to the 2022 revision of the [World Population Prospects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Population_Prospects)[^cite_note-UN_WPP-2][^cite_note-UN_WPP_2022-3] the total population was 27,198,628 in 2021, compared to only 4 466 000 in 1950. The proportion of children below the age of 15 in 2010 was 40.6%, 55.9% was between 15 and 65 years of age, while 3.5% was 65 years or older.[^cite_note-WPP_2010-4]

## Languages



Linguistic survey of Nigeria, Cameroon, and Benin

Main article: [Languages of Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Cameroon)

There are 24 major African language groups in Cameroon; additionally, [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) are official languages. [Cameroonian Pidgin English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroonian_Pidgin_English) is also widely spoken.

Peoples concentrated in the Southwest and Northwest Provinces—around [Buea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buea) and [Bamenda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamenda)—use standard [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) and [Cameroonian Pidgin English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroonian_Pidgin_English), as well as their local languages. In the three northern provinces—[Adamawa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adamawa_Province), [North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Province,_Cameroon), and [Far North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_North_Province)—either [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language) or [Fulfulde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fula_language) (the language of the [Fulani](102383.md)) is widely spoken. Elsewhere, French is the principal second language, although pidgin and some local languages such as [Ewondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ewondo_language), the dialect of a Beti clan from the [Yaoundé](57671.md) area, have a wide currency. In [Far North Region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_North_Region,_Cameroon) the northernmost constituent province of Cameroon, [Mafa Language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mafa_people) [Arab Shuwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chadian_Arabic) (an [Arab dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varieties_of_Arabic)) and is spoken by the [Baggara Arabs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baggara) (also called Arab Shuwa).[^cite_note-13]

Indigenous languages of Cameroon include:

- [Kanuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanuri_language)

- [Lamnso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamnso_language)

## Religion

Main article: [Religion in Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Cameroon)

In the 2018 estimate, 38.3% of the population were Roman Catholic, 25.5% were Protestant, 6.9% were other Christians, 24.4% were Muslims, 2.2% were animists, 0.5% were other religions, and 2.2% were no religion.
