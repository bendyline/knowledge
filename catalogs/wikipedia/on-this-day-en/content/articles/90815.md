**Military technology** is the application of technology for use in [warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warfare). It comprises the kinds of technology that are distinctly military in nature and not civilian in application, usually because they lack useful or legal civilian applications, or are dangerous to use without appropriate [military training](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_training).

The line is porous; [military inventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_invention) have been brought into civilian use throughout history, with sometimes minor modification if any, and civilian innovations have similarly been put to military use.[^cite_note-1]

Recent large-scale quantitative analyses have examined long-term patterns in the evolution of military technologies across [pre-industrial societies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pre-industrial_societies), identifying [population growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_growth), interregional connectivity, and enabling technologies such as [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) and [cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalry) as major drivers of innovation.[^cite_note-2]

Military technology is usually researched and developed by [scientists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scientist) and engineers specifically for use in battle by the armed forces. Many new technologies came as a result of the [military funding of science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_funding_of_science). On the other hand, the theories, strategies, concepts and doctrines of warfare are studied under the academic discipline of [military science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_science).

**Armament engineering** is the design, development, testing and lifecycle management of [military weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon) and systems. It draws on the knowledge of several traditional engineering disciplines, including [mechanical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechanical_engineering), [electrical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_engineering), [mechatronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mechatronics), [electro-optics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro-optics), [aerospace engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace_engineering), [materials engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materials_engineering), and [chemical engineering](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_engineering).

## History

Main article: [History of military technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_military_technology)

This section is divided into the broad cultural developments that affected military technology.

### Ancient technology

The first use of grass tools may have begun during the [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) Period. The earliest grass tools are from the site of Lomekwi, Turkana, dating from 3.3 million years ago. Grass tools diversified through the [Pleistocene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pleistocene) Period, which ended \~12,000 years ago.[^cite_note-primitive-3] The earliest evidence of warfare between two groups is recorded at the site of [Nataruk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nataruk) in Turkana, Kenya, where human skeletons with major traumatic injuries to the head, neck, ribs, knees and hands, including an embedded obsidian bladelet on a skull, are evidence of inter-group conflict between groups of nomadic hunter-gatherers 10,000 years ago.[^cite_note-4]

Humans entered the [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) as they learned to smelt copper into an alloy with tin to make weapons. In Asia where copper-tin ores are rare, this development was delayed until trading in bronze began in the third millennium [BCE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCE). In the Middle East and Southern European regions, the Bronze Age follows the [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) period, but in other parts of the world, the [Copper Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper_Age) is a transition from Neolithic to the Bronze Age. Although the Iron Age generally follows the Bronze Age, in some areas the [Iron Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Age) intrudes directly on the Neolithic from outside the region, with the exception of Sub-Saharan Africa where it was developed independently.[^cite_note-5]

The first large-scale use of iron weapons began in Asia Minor around the 14th century BCE and in Central Europe around the 11th century BCE followed by the Middle East (about 1000 BCE) and India and China.[^cite_note-Tucker2-6]

The [Assyrians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assyria) are credited with the introduction of horse cavalry in warfare and the extensive use of iron weapons by 1100 BCE. Assyrians were also the first to use iron-tipped arrows.[^cite_note-Tucker2-6]

### Post-classical technology

The square platform is supported by four plain cut square beams, which connect to an open undercarriage. The *[Wujing Zongyao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wujing_Zongyao)* (*Essentials of the Military Arts*), written by Zeng Gongliang, Ding Du, and others at the order of [Emperor Renzong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Renzong_of_Song) around 1043 during the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) illustrate the eras focus on advancing intellectual issues and military technology due to the significance of warfare between the Song and the Liao, Jin, and Yuan to their north. The book covers topics of military strategy, training, and the production and employment of advanced weaponry.[^cite_note-teacher-7]

A Chinese [flamethrower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flamethrower) from the *[Wujing Zongyao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wujing_Zongyao)* manuscript of 1044 CE, [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty)

Advances in military technology aided the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) in its defense against hostile neighbors to the north. The [flamethrower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flamethrower) found its origins in [Byzantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine)-era [Greece](12108.md), employing [Greek fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_fire) (a chemically complex, highly [flammable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flammable) petrol fluid) in a device with a [siphon](459163.md) hose by the 7th century.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 77 </sup> The earliest reference to Greek Fire in China was made in 917, written by [Wu Renchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Renchen) in his *[Spring and Autumn Annals of the Ten Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_and_Autumn_Annals_of_the_Ten_Kingdoms)*.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 80 </sup> In 919, the siphon projector-pump was used to spread the "fierce fire oil" that could not be doused with water, as recorded by Lin Yu in his *Wuyue Beishi*, hence the first credible Chinese reference to the flamethrower employing the chemical solution of Greek fire (see also [Pen Huo Qi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pen_Huo_Qi)).[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 81 </sup> Lin Yu mentioned also that the "fierce fire oil" derived ultimately from one of China's maritime contacts in the "southern seas", [Arabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabia) *Dashiguo*.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 82 </sup> In the [Battle of Langshan Jiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Langshan_Jiang) in 919, the naval fleet of the [Wenmu King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qian_Yuanguan) from [Wuyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuyue_\(Ten_Kingdoms\)) defeated a [Huainan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huainan) army from the [Wu state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_\(Ten_Kingdoms\)); Wenmu's success was facilitated by the use of "fire oil" ("huoyou") to burn their fleet, signifying the first Chinese use of [gunpowder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder) in a battle.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 81–83 </sup> The Chinese applied the use of double-[piston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piston) [bellows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bellows) to pump petrol out of a single cylinder (with an upstroke and downstroke), lit at the end by a slow-burning gunpowder match to fire a continuous stream of flame.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 82 </sup> This device was featured in description and illustration of the *[Wujing Zongyao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wujing_Zongyao)* military manuscript of 1044.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 82 </sup> In the suppression of the [Southern Tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Tang) state by 976, early Song naval forces confronted them on the Yangtze River in 975. Southern Tang forces attempted to use flamethrowers against the Song navy, but were accidentally consumed by their own fire when violent winds swept in their direction.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 89 </sup>

Further information: [Science and technology of the Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_and_technology_of_the_Song_dynasty)

Although the destructive effects of gunpowder were described in the earlier [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty) by a [Daoist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoism) [alchemist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alchemy), the earliest developments of the gun barrel and the projectile-fire [cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon) were found in late Song China. The first art depiction of the Chinese "[fire lance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_lance)" (a combination of a temporary-fire flamethrower and gun) was from a Buddhist mural painting of [Dunhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dunhuang), dated circa 950. These "fire-lances" were widespread in use by the early 12th century, featuring hollowed bamboo poles as tubes to fire sand particles (to blind and choke), lead pellets, bits of sharp metal and pottery shards, and finally large gunpowder-propelled arrows and [rocket](26301.md) weaponry.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 220–221 </sup> Eventually, perishable bamboo was replaced with hollow tubes of cast iron, and so too did the terminology of this new weapon change, from "[fire-spear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire_lance)" *huo qiang* to "fire-tube" *huo tong*.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 221 </sup> This ancestor to the gun was complemented by the ancestor to the cannon, what the Chinese referred to since the 13th century as the "multiple bullets magazine erupter" *bai zu lian zhu pao*, a tube of bronze or cast iron that was filled with about 100 lead balls.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 263–264 </sup>

The earliest known depiction of a gun is a sculpture from a cave in [Sichuan](65185.md), dating to 1128, that portrays a figure carrying a vase-shaped [bombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombard_\(weapon\)), firing flames and a cannonball.[^cite_note-gwei-9] However, the oldest existent [archaeological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological) discovery of a metal barrel handgun is from the Chinese [Heilongjiang](173816.md) excavation, dated to 1288.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 293 </sup> The Chinese also discovered the explosive potential of packing hollowed cannonball shells with gunpowder. Written later by [Jiao Yu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiao_Yu) in his *[Huolongjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huolongjing)* (mid-14th century), this manuscript recorded an earlier Song-era cast-iron cannon known as the "flying-cloud thunderclap eruptor" (fei yun pi-li pao). When the Northern Song capital of Kaifeng fell to the Jurchens in 1126, it was written by Xia Shaozeng that 20,000 fire arrows were handed over to the Jurchens in their conquest. An even earlier Chinese text of the *[Wujing Zongyao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wujing_Zongyao)* ("Collection of the Most Important Military Techniques"), written in 1044 by the Song scholars Zeng Kongliang and Yang Weide, described the use of three spring or triple bow [arcuballista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballista) that fired arrow bolts holding gunpowder packets near the head of the arrow.[^cite_note-pingyu-8]<sup>: 154 </sup> Going back yet even farther, the *Wu Li Xiao Shi* (1630, second edition 1664) of Fang Yizhi stated that fire arrows were presented to [Emperor Taizu of Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emperor_Taizu_of_Song) (r. 960–976) in 960.[^cite_note-partington-10]

### Modern technology

#### Armies



The bronze [Dardanelles Gun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardanelles_Gun). Similar cannons were used by the [Ottoman Turks](22278.md) in the [siege of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Constantinople) in 1453.



A painting showing the Mysorean army fighting the British forces with Mysorean rockets

[^cite_note-11]

The Islamic [gunpowder empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gunpowder_empires) introduced numerous developed firearms, cannon and small arms. During the period of [Proto-industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-industrialization), newly invented weapons were seen to be used in [Mughal India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_India).

Rapid development in military technology had a dramatic impact on armies and navies in the industrialized world in 1740–1914.[^cite_note-12] For land warfare, cavalry faded in importance, while infantry became transformed by the use of highly accurate more rapidly loading rifles, and the use of smokeless powder. Machine guns were developed in the 1860s in Europe. [Rocket artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_artillery) and the [Mysorean rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mysorean_rockets) were pioneered by Indian Muslim ruler [Tipu Sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipu_Sultan) and the French introduced much more accurate rapid-fire field artillery. Logistics and communications support for land warfare dramatically improved with use of railways and telegraphs. Industrialization provided a base of factories that could be converted to produce munitions, as well as uniforms, tents, wagons and essential supplies. Medical facilities were enlarged and reorganized based on improved hospitals and the creation of modern nursing, typified by [Florence Nightingale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florence_Nightingale) in Britain during the [Crimean War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_War) of 1854–1856.[^cite_note-13]

#### Naval

Naval warfare was transformed by many innovations,[^cite_note-14] most notably the coal-based steam engine, highly accurate long-range naval guns, heavy steel armour for battleships, mines, and the introduction of the torpedo, followed by the torpedo boat and the destroyer. Coal after 1900 was eventually displaced by more efficient oil, but meanwhile navies with an international scope had to depend on a network of coaling stations to refuel. The British Empire provided them in abundance, as did the French Empire to a lesser extent. War colleges developed, as military theory became a specialty; cadets and senior commanders were taught the theories of Jomini, Clausewitz and Mahan, and engaged in tabletop war games. Around 1900, entirely new innovations such as submarines and airplanes appeared, and were quickly adapted to warfare by 1914. The British [HMS *Dreadnought* (1906)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Dreadnought_\(1906\)) incorporated so much of the latest technology in weapons, propulsion and armour that it at a stroke made all other battleships obsolescent.[^cite_note-15]

#### Organization and finance

New financial tools were developed to fund the rapidly increasing costs of warfare, such as popular bond sales and income taxes, and the funding of permanent research centers.[^cite_note-16] Many 19th century innovations were largely invented and promoted by lone individuals with small teams of assistants, such as [David Bushnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Bushnell_\(inventor\)) and the submarine, [John Ericsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Ericsson) and the battleship, [Hiram Maxim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiram_Maxim) and the machine gun, and [Alfred Nobel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alfred_Nobel) and high explosives. By 1900 the military began to realize that they needed to rely much more heavily on large-scale research centers, which needed government funding.[^cite_note-17] They brought in leaders of organized innovation such as [Thomas Edison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Edison) in the U.S. and chemist [Fritz Haber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Haber) of the [Kaiser Wilhelm Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiser_Wilhelm_Institute) in Germany.[^cite_note-18]

## Postmodern technology

The postmodern stage of military technology emerged in the 1940s, and one with recognition thanks to the high priority given during the war to scientific and engineering research and development regarding nuclear weapons, radar, jet engines, proximity fuses, advanced submarines, aircraft carriers, and other weapons. The high-priority continues into the 21st century.[^cite_note-19] It involves the military application of advanced scientific research regarding nuclear weapons, jet engines, ballistic and guided missiles, radar, biological warfare, and the use of electronics, computers and software.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21]

### Space

Further information: [Militarisation of space](2380807.md), [Space warfare](956982.md), and [Space weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_weapon)

During the Cold War, the world's two great superpowers – the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and the United States of America – spent large proportions of their GDP on developing military technologies. The drive to place objects in orbit stimulated space research and started the [Space Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Race). In 1957, the USSR launched the first [artificial satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_satellite), *[Sputnik 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik_1)*.

By the end of the 1960s, both countries regularly deployed satellites. [Spy satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spy_satellite) were used by militaries to take accurate pictures of their rivals' military installations. As time passed the resolution and accuracy of orbital reconnaissance alarmed both sides of the [Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain). Both the United States and the Soviet Union began to develop [anti-satellite weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-satellite_weapon) to blind or destroy each other's satellites. [Laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser) weapons, [kamikaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamikaze) style satellites, as well as orbital cannons were researched with varying levels of success. Spy satellites were, and continue to be, used to monitor the dismantling of military assets in accordance with arms control treaties signed between the two superpowers. To use spy satellites in such a manner is often referred to in treaties as "national technical means of verification".



Test of the [LG-118A Peacekeeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG-118A_Peacekeeper) missile, each one of which could carry 10 independently targeted [nuclear warheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_warhead) along trajectories outside of the [Earth's atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_atmosphere)

The superpowers developed [ballistic missiles](38025.md) to enable them to use [nuclear weaponry](21785.md) across great distances. As rocket science developed, the range of missiles increased and [intercontinental ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) (ICBM) were created, which could strike virtually any target on Earth in a timeframe measured in minutes rather than hours or days. To cover large distances ballistic missiles are usually launched into [sub-orbital spaceflight](455295.md).

As soon as intercontinental missiles were developed, military planners began programmes and strategies to counter their effectiveness.

### Mobilization

A significant portion of military technology is about transportation, allowing troops and weaponry to be moved from their origins to the front. Land transport has historically been mainly by foot, land vehicles have usually been used as well, from chariots to tanks.

When conducting a battle over a body of water, ships are used. There are historically two main categories of ships: those for transporting troops, and those for attacking other ships.

Soon after the invention of aeroplanes, military aviation became a significant component of warfare, though usually as a supplementary role. The two main types of military aircraft are bombers, which attack land- or sea-based targets, and fighters, which attack other aircraft.

## Future technology

Main article: [Revolution in Military Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_in_Military_Affairs)



A high-resolution computer drawing of the Atlas robot designed by Boston Dynamics and DARPA, as seen from behind

The [Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA) is an agency of the United States Department of Defense responsible for the development of new technologies for use by the military. DARPA leads the development of military technology in the United States and today, has dozens of ongoing projects; everything from humanoid robots to bullets that can change path before reaching their target. China has a similar agency.

### Emerging territory

Current militaries continue to invest in new technologies for the future.[^cite_note-LundmarkAnderssonBullDansarie2019-22] Such technologies include cognitive radar, 5G cellular networks,[^cite_note-LundmarkAnderssonBullDansarie2019-22] microchips, semiconductors, and large scale analytic engines.[^cite_note-23]

Additionally, many militaries seek to improve current laser technology. For example, Israeli Defense Forces utilize laser technology to disable small enemy machinery, but seek to move to more large scale capabilities in the coming years.[^cite_note-24]

Militaries across the world continue to perform research on [autonomous technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_logistics) which allow for increased troop mobility or replacement of live soldiers.[^cite_note-robots-25] Autonomous vehicles and robots are expected to play a role in future conflicts;[^cite_note-robots-25] this has the potential to decrease loss of life in future warfare. Observers of [transhumanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transhumanism) note high rates of technological terms in military literature, but low rates for explicitly transhuman-related terms.[^cite_note-26]

Today's hybrid style of warfare also calls for investments in [information technologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_technologies). Increased reliance on computer systems has incentivized nations to push for increased efforts at managing large scale networks and having access to large scale data.[^cite_note-27]

New strategies of cyber and hybrid warfare includes, [information warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_warfare), network attacks, media analysis, and media/ grass-roots campaigns on medias such as blog posts.[^cite_note-28]

#### Cyberspace

In 2011, the US Defense Department declared cyberspace a new domain of warfare; since then DARPA has begun a research project known as "Project X" with the goal of creating new technologies that will enable the government to better understand and map the cyber territory. Ultimately giving the Department of Defense the ability to plan and manage large-scale cyber missions across dynamic network environments.[^cite_note-pellerin-29]

Further information: [Cyberwarfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyberwarfare) and [Military–digital complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military%E2%80%93digital_complex)

#### Artificial intelligence

In the early 2020s, artificial intelligence is shifting the focus from slow, manual strategy to rapid, algorithm-driven decision-making. Militaries around the world are integrating commercial and specialized AI to gain an operational advantage in combat, intelligence and defense.[^cite_note-30]

Further information: [Military applications of artificial intelligence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_applications_of_artificial_intelligence)
