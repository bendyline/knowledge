**CBS News** is the news division of the American television broadcaster [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) headquartered in [New York City](645042.md). Along with [ABC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_News_\(United_States\)) and [NBC News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_News), it has long been among the big three [broadcast news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_broadcasting) networks in the United States.

CBS News emerged as a radio news broadcast service in 1929. In 1933, [Paul W. White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_White_\(journalist\)) was named head of the news division and saw it through [World War II](32927.md) where it expanded into Latin America, providing an alternative to [Nazi propaganda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi_propaganda) with assistance from the [Department of State](31975.md). Its first television broadcasts began in 1941 with [WCBW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WCBS-TV) in New York City. In 1962, [Walter Cronkite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Cronkite) became [anchor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_presenter) of its flagship television news program, the newly renamed to *[CBS Evening News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Evening_News)*.

Following parent company [Paramount's merger with Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_of_Skydance_Media_and_Paramount_Global), [Kenneth R. Weinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_R._Weinstein) (the former CEO of the [Hudson Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Institute), a conservative foreign policy think tank) was appointed as CBS News' [ombudsman](26289198.md), while *[The Free Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Free_Press_\(CBS_News\))* founder and owner [Bari Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari_Weiss) was appointed its editor-in-chief. These moves were interpreted by critics as a sign the organization was shifting politically [rightward](56522.md).

CBS News television programs include *[CBS Evening News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Evening_News)*, *[CBS Mornings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Mornings)*; [news magazine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_magazine) programs *[CBS News Sunday Morning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_Sunday_Morning)*, *[60 Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60_Minutes)*, and *[48 Hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/48_Hours_\(TV_program\))*; and [Sunday morning political affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_morning_talk_show) program *[Face the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_the_Nation)*. It also oversees CBS News [podcasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podcast) like *[The Takeout Podcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_Garrett)*. CBS News also operates [CBS News 24/7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_24/7), a 24-hour [digital streaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Digital_streaming) channel.

## History

In 1929, the [Columbia Broadcasting System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) began making regular radio news broadcasts, which were five-minute summaries taken from reports from [United Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Press_International), one of the three [wire services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wire_service) that supplied newspapers with national and international news. In December 1930, CBS chief [William S. Paley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Paley) hired journalist [Paul W. White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_White_\(journalist\)) away from United Press as CBS's news editor. Paley put the radio network's news operation at the same level as entertainment, and authorized White to interrupt programming if events warranted. Along with other networks, CBS chafed at the [breaking news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_news) embargo imposed upon radio by the wire services, which prevented them from using bulletins until they first appeared in print. CBS disregarded an embargo when it broke the story of the [Lindbergh kidnapping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lindbergh_kidnapping) in 1932, using live on-the-air reporting. Radio networks scooped print outlets with news of the [1932 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1932_United_States_presidential_election).[^cite_note-Dunning-1]<sup>: 485 </sup>

In March 1933, White was named vice president and general manager in charge of news at CBS.[^cite_note-News_on_the_Air_DJ-2] As the first head of CBS News, he began to build an organization that soon established a legendary reputation.[^cite_note-Dunning2-3]<sup>: 486 </sup>

In 1935, White hired [Edward R. Murrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_R._Murrow), and sent him to London in 1937 to run CBS Radio's European operation.[^cite_note-Dunning2-3]<sup>: 486 </sup> White led a staff that would come to include [Richard C. Hottelet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_C._Hottelet), [Charles Collingwood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Collingwood_\(journalist\)), [William L. Shirer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_L._Shirer), [Eric Sevareid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eric_Sevareid),[^cite_note-Rather_Keynote-4] [Bill Downs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Downs), [John Charles Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Charles_Daly), [Joseph C. Harsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_C._Harsch),[^cite_note-Dunning3-5]<sup>: 501 </sup> [Cecil Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Brown_\(journalist\)), [Elmer Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elmer_Davis), [Quincy Howe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quincy_Howe), [H. V. Kaltenborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._V._Kaltenborn), [Robert Trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Trout),[^cite_note-NYT_obit-6] and [Lewis Shollenberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_Shollenberger).[^cite_note-7]

"CBS was getting its ducks in a row for the biggest news story in history, [World War II](32927.md)", wrote radio historian John Dunning.[^cite_note-Dunning4-8]<sup>: 487 </sup>

### World War II

In 1940, [William S. Paley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_S._Paley) recruited [Edmund A. Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_A._Chester) from his position as Bureau Chief for [Latin America](18524.md) at the [Associated Press](18935732.md) to coordinate the development of the international shortwave radio Network of the Americas, called *[La Cadena de las Américas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Cadena_de_las_Am%C3%A9ricas)*, in 1942.[^cite_note-nyt1973-9][^cite_note-books.google.com-10][^cite_note-Han-11] Broadcasting in concert with the assistance of the [Department of State](31975.md), the [Office for Inter-American Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Coordinator_of_Inter-American_Affairs) chaired by [Nelson Rockefeller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nelson_Rockefeller) and [Voice of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voice_of_America) as part of President Roosevelt's support for [Pan-Americanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Americanism), this CBS radio network provided vital news and cultural programming throughout [South America](26769.md) and [Central America](6121.md) during the World War II era.[^cite_note-presidency.ucsb.edu-12][^cite_note-13]

&#x20;Through its operations in 20 nations, it fostered benevolent diplomatic relations between the United States and other nations in the region while providing an alternative to [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazism) propaganda.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-Han-11][^cite_note-presidency.ucsb.edu-12]



[Douglas Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Edwards), who worked as a CBS News television and radio correspondent for four decades, on the CBS News set in 1952



[Walter Cronkite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Cronkite), who was anchor of *[CBS Evening News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Evening_News)* for nearly two decades, from 1962 to 1981



[Katie Couric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Couric), the first solo female anchor of a major evening news program, served as anchor and managing editor of *[CBS Evening News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Evening_News)* from 2006 to 2011.

After becoming commercial station WCBW (channel 2, later [WCBS-TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WCBS-TV)) in 1941, the pioneer CBS television station in New York City broadcast two daily news programs, at 2\:30 p.m. and 7\:30 p.m. weekdays, anchored by [Richard Hubbell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Hubbell) (journalist). Most of the newscasts featured Hubbell reading a script with only occasional cutaways to a map or still photograph. When [Pearl Harbor was bombed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) on December 7, 1941, WCBW (which was usually off the air on Sunday to give the engineers a day off), took to the air at 8\:45 p.m. with an extensive special report. The national emergency even broke down the unspoken wall between CBS radio and television. WCBW executives convinced radio announcers and experts such as [George Fielding Elliot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Fielding_Elliot) and [Linton Wells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linton_Wells) to come down to the Grand Central studios during the evening and give information and commentary on the attack. The WCBW special report that night lasted less than 90 minutes. But that special broadcast pushed the limits of live television in 1941 and opened up new possibilities for future broadcasts. As CBS wrote in a special report to the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) (FCC), the unscheduled live news broadcast on December 7 "was unquestionably the most stimulating challenge and marked the greatest advance of any single problem faced up to that time."

Additional newscasts were scheduled in the early days of the war. In May 1942, WCBW, like most television stations, sharply cut back its live program schedule and the newscasts were canceled, since the station temporarily suspended studio operations, resorting exclusively to the occasional broadcast of films. This was primarily because much of the staff had either joined the service or were redeployed to war related technical research, and to prolong the life of the early, unstable cameras which were now impossible to repair due to the wartime lack of parts.

In May 1944, as [World War II](32927.md) began to turn in favor of the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_in_World_War_II), WCBW reopened the studios and the newscasts returned, briefly anchored by [Ned Calmer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ned_Calmer), and then by Everett Holles.[^cite_note-15] After the end of World War II, expanded news programs appeared on the WCBW schedule –(its call letters were changed to WCBS-TV in 1946) – first anchored by Milo Boulton, and later by [Douglas Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Edwards). On May 3, 1948, Edwards began anchoring *CBS Television News*, a regular 15-minute nightly newscast on the CBS television network, including WCBS-TV. It aired every weeknight at 7\:30 p.m., and was the first regularly scheduled, network television news program featuring an anchor (the nightly [Lowell Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowell_Thomas) NBC radio network newscast was simulcast on television locally on NBC's WNBT, which became [WNBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WNBC), for a time in the early 1940s, along with Richard Hubbell, Ned Calmer, Everett Holles, and Milo Boulton on WCBW in the early and mid-1940s, but these were local television broadcasts seen only in New York City). [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC)'s offering at the time, *NBC Television Newsreel* (which premiered in February 1948), was simply film footage with voice narration.

### Mid-late 20th century

In 1948, CBS Radio journalist [Edmund Chester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmund_A._Chester) emerged as the television network's new Director of News Special Events and Sports.[^cite_note-nyt1973-9][^cite_note-16]

In 1949, Chester collaborated with one of CBS' original [Murrow Boys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murrow_Boys), [Larry LeSueur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_LeSueur), to produce the innovative news series *United Nations In Action*. Underwritten by [Ford Motor Company](30433662.md) as a public service, the broadcasts endeavored to provide live coverage of the proceedings of the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) from its interim headquarters in [Lake Success, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Success,_New_York).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-gettyimages.com-18] They proved to be successful, and were honored with a [George Foster Peabody Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Foster_Peabody_Award) for Television News in 1949.[^cite_note-peabody-19]

In 1950, the name of the nightly newscast was changed to *Douglas Edwards with the News*, and the following year, it became the first news program to be broadcast on both coasts, thanks to a new coaxial cable connection, prompting Edwards to use the greeting "Good evening everyone, coast to coast." In 1962, the broadcast was renamed the *[CBS Evening News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Evening_News)* when [Walter Cronkite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Cronkite) replaced Edwards.[^cite_note-20] Edwards remained with CBS News, contributing to various daytime television newscasts and radio news broadcasts until his retirement on April 1, 1988.

From the 1990s until 2014, CBS News operated its own production unit CBS News Productions, to produce alternative programming for cable networks,[^cite_note-21] and CBS EyeToo Productions, later renamed CBS Eye Productions, a company that produced documentaries and nonfiction programs.[^cite_note-22]

CBS News ran a cable channel, [CBS Eye on People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Eye_on_People), from 1997 to 2000, and [Spanish language](26825.md) channel [CBS Telenoticias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_Telenoticias) from 1996 to 1998.

### 21st century

In 2021, CBS News had set up its own production unit. [See It Now Studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/See_It_Now_Studios), which was headed by [Susan Zirinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Zirinsky).[^cite_note-23]

Until April 2021,[^cite_note-24] the president and senior executive producer of CBS News was [Susan Zirinsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Zirinsky), who assumed the role on March 1, 2019.[^cite_note-25] Zirinsky, the first female president of the network's news division,[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] was announced as the choice to replace [David Rhodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Rhodes_\(CBS_News_President\)) on January 6, 2019.[^cite_note-WSJ2-28][^cite_note-29] The announcement came amid news that Rhodes would step down as president of CBS News "amid falling ratings and the fallout from revelations from an investigation into sexual misconduct allegations" against CBS News figures and Rhodes.[^cite_note-30]

In April 2021, CBS Television Stations and CBS News merged their two divisions into one entity named [CBS News and Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_and_Stations).

On April 15, 2021, CBS Television Stations and CBS News announced that their respective divisions would merge into one entity,[^cite_note-31] to be named [CBS News and Stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_and_Stations).[^cite_note-32] It was also announced that [Neeraj Khemlani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neeraj_Khemlani) (former executive vice president of [Hearst Newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearst_Communications)) and [Wendy McMahon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendy_McMahon_\(television_executive\)) (former president of the [ABC Owned Television Stations Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABC_Owned_Television_Stations)) were named presidents and co-heads. This transition was completed on May 3, 2021. On August 14, 2023, after Khemlani announced he was stepping down, CBS News named McMahon as its sole president and CEO.[^cite_note-33] The next day on August 15, CBS News appointed [Ingrid Ciprian-Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingrid_Ciprian-Matthews), who supervised the Washington, D.C. bureau as its president.[^cite_note-34] She stepped down in July 2024.[^cite_note-35]

In 2022, CBS News hired former [Trump administration](52231341.md) official [Mick Mulvaney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Mulvaney) as a paid on-air contributor.[^cite_note-Barr-2022-36] Mulvaney's hiring stirred controversy within the company due to his history of promoting [Donald Trump](4848272.md)'s false claims and attacking the press.[^cite_note-Barr-2022-36] CBS News co-president [Neeraj Khemlani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neeraj_Khemlani) told CBS morning show staff, "If you look at some of the people that we've been hiring on a contributor basis, being able to make sure that we are getting access to both sides of the aisle is a priority because we know the Republicans are going to take over, most likely, in the midterms".

In October 2024, President Donald Trump sued CBS News over a *60 Minutes* interview with former Vice President Kamala Harris, alleging that the network engaged in [election interference](65542411.md) through deceptive editing. The lawsuit, which sought \$10 billion in damages, claimed that CBS violated the Texas Deceptive Trade Practices Act by airing two different edits of Harris' response to a question about Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu. The lawsuit was widely described as frivolous by legal experts.[^cite_note-Reilly-2025-38][^cite_note-Mastrangelo-2025-39]

Initially, the network released a statement that it would "vigorously defend" against the suit.[^cite_note-40] In July 2025, CBS News' parent company, Paramount Global, settled the lawsuit for \$16 million, which would be given to [Trump's future presidential library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_J._Trump_Presidential_Library) and lawyer costs.[^cite_note-41] The settlement was described as a capitulation to Trump's executive power and blow to freedom of the press, with [Stephen Colbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Colbert) characterizing it as "a big fat bribe".[^cite_note-Reilly-2025-38][^cite_note-Mastrangelo-2025-39][^cite_note-Sullum-2025-42][^cite_note-43] Colbert's show was cancelled shortly thereafter.[^cite_note-Stelter-2025-44] [Paramount's merger with Skydance Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_of_Skydance_Media_and_Paramount_Global), which needed executive approval, was cited as a key motivating factor in the network's decision to settle.[^cite_note-Mastrangelo-2025-39][^cite_note-Sullum-2025-42][^cite_note-Reilly-2025-38]

In January 2025, [Norah O'Donnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_O'Donnell), who was based in the CBS News bureau in Washington, D.C., for over five years, departed, resulting in *CBS Evening News* to once again be broadcast from the CBS Broadcast Center's historic Studio 47 in New York City.[^cite_note-returntonyc-45] *[Face the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Face_the_Nation)* host and CBS News correspondent [Margaret Brennan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaret_Brennan), however, continue to be based in Washington, D.C.[^cite_note-returntonyc-45][^cite_note-Barr-2022-36]

Tom Cibrowski was hired as president in February 2025.[^cite_note-46]

In April 2025, *60 Minutes* executive producer Bill Owens left the network, citing deterioration of journalistic independence.[^cite_note-Owens-47][^cite_note-Mastrangelo_20250519-48] The following month, CBS News president Wendy McMahon also resigned due to disagreements with corporate leadership.[^cite_note-Mastrangelo_20250519-48] As the [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) required the appointment of an [ombudsman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organizational_ombudsman) to monitor "bias" at CBS News for agency approval of the Paramount–Skydance merger,[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] [Paramount Skydance](79917576.md) CEO [David Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ellison) appointed [Kenneth R. Weinstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_R._Weinstein) (the former CEO of the [Hudson Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson_Institute), a conservative foreign policy think tank) to the position in September 2025.[^cite_note-51] FCC chair [Brendan Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_Carr), speaking of approval of the merger, stated in an interview to *The Wall Street Journal* that "The new owners of CBS came in and said, 'It's time for a change'" and that they said "'We're going to reorient it towards getting rid of bias.'" Carr added: "At the end of the day, that's what made the difference for us."[^cite_note-Meek_07292025-52]

In October 2025, [Bari Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari_Weiss) was appointed editor-in-chief of CBS News. This announcement was interpreted by critics as a mark of the organization shifting rightward and more [pro-Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-Israel) in response to the Trump Era, and was likewise praised by Trump himself.[^cite_note-Baragona_20251003-53][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-Stelter-2025-44][^cite_note-55] In October, around 100 predominantly non-white employees were laid off, including eight on-air hosts, all of whom were women.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-\:0-57] This came amidst a broader removal in the United States of non-white employees from political and journalistic positions.[^cite_note-\:0-57] Also after the appointments of Weiss and Weinstein as editor-in-chief and ombudsman respectively, the head of the [Standards and Practices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadcast_Standards_and_Practices) department for CBS News resigned in October 2025.[^cite_note-58] Also in October, the conservative, anti-woke digital website *[The Free Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Free_Press_\(CBS_News\))* was bought by Paramount and incorporated as an independent entity within Paramount. Weiss stated that the merger "gives The Free Press a chance to help reshape a storied media organization — to help guide CBS News into a future that honors those great values that underpin The Free Press and the best of American journalism".[^cite_note-Mullin-2025-59]

In November 2025, in "redrawing the lines of what \[fell] in the 40-yard lines of acceptable debate and acceptable American politics and culture" to create a new form of "centrist" news, Weiss was criticized for citing the example of a "charismatic" conversation between American lawyer [Alan Dershowitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Dershowitz) and former spokeswoman for the [National Rifle Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rifle_Association_of_America), [Dana Loesch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Loesch) as "an opportunity to speak for the 75%, for the people that are on the center-left and the center-right" by social media users, journalists, as well as staffers at CBS, due to the figures representing fringe political points of view, with journalist [Glenn Greenwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Greenwald) connecting this with its low viewcount reception on YouTube. Dershowitz was criticized as out-of-step with the "clear majority" of Democratic voters on Israel, specifically over his denial of the concurrent commission of the [Gaza Genocide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_Genocide), while Loesch was viewed as politicizing [gun rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_keep_and_bear_arms_in_the_United_States) advocacy.[^cite_note-60] By April 2026, though still identifying as a "lifelong Democrat", Dershowitz registered as a Republican because the Democratic Party had "become the most anti-Israel party in U.S. history."[^cite_note-61]

Amid the editorial changes being undertaken by Bari Weiss, new *CBS Evening News* anchor [Tony Dokoupil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Dokoupil) released a promo promising that the newscast planned to remain objective and editorially independent from politicians, advertisers, and corporate interests (including those of CBS itself). He argued that news media coverage at CBS on topics such as the Iraq war, COVID lockdowns, Hillary Clinton's emails, and Hunter Biden's laptop was "skewed" in favor of "political and academic elites and away from the concerns of normal people".[^cite_note-62] *CBS Evening News* reported its 38-page editorial standards handbook was reduced to "5 simple values" of which one was "we love America", establishing an explicit pro-United States editorial stance.[^cite_note-Spangler-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] *[Variety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Variety_\(magazine\))* reported the pro-America pledge "reinforce\[d] speculation that Ellison put Weiss in charge of CBS News in an effort to boost its appeal among MAGA supporters generally — and with President Trump specifically".[^cite_note-Spangler-63]

In January 2026, new editor-in-chief Bari Weiss held an all-staff meeting outlining her strategic vision for the troubled news division. She said she planned to hire about 18 paid commentators to broaden the range of voices and content at the network and indicated that she expects to make significant newsroom cuts as part of reshaping the organization.[^cite_note-66]

In February 2026, it was revealed that CBS News was considering another round of layoffs which could see cuts totalling at least 15% of the current staff.[^cite_note-67]

In March 2026, CBS News announced it would shutter its radio news service by May 22, 2026, after nearly 100 years and eliminate all positions tied to it as part of a larger restructuring by Weiss. CBS cited changes in the media landscape and financial pressures as driving the move.[^cite_note-Swai_03302026-68]

CBS News Bulletin's coverage of the [assassination of John F. Kennedy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_John_F._Kennedy) in 1963

According to the CBS News Library and source Sandy Genelius (Vice President, CBS News Communications), the "CBS Evening News" was the program title for both Saturday and Sunday evening broadcasts. ## CBS News Radio

Main article: [CBS News Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_Radio)

CBS News produced newscasts and features for radio stations through [CBS News Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_Radio), which was the oldest unit of CBS and traced its roots to the company's founding in 1927, and the news division took shape over the decade that followed. The list of CBS News correspondents (below) includes those reporting on CBS News Radio.

CBS News Radio produced the oldest daily news show on radio or television, the *[CBS World News Roundup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_World_News_Roundup)*, which first aired in 1938; in 2018, it celebrated its 80th anniversary. The *World News Roundup* aired twice every weekday, broadcasting a morning edition anchored by Steve Kathan and produced by Paul Farry, and a late edition anchored by Dave Barrett and produced by James Hutton. The evening *Roundup*, previously known as *The World Tonight*, had aired since 1956, and had been anchored by Blair Clark, [Douglas Edwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_Edwards), [Dallas Townsend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dallas_Townsend), and [Christopher Glenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Glenn), Glenn also anchored the morning *Roundup* prior to his death in 2006.

CBS Radio Network provided newscasts at the top of the hour, regular updates at\:31 minutes past the hour, the popular Newsfeeds for affiliates, including [WCBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WCBS_\(AM\)) in New York City and [KYW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KYW_\(AM\)) in [Philadelphia](50585.md), at\:35 minutes past the hour, and breaking news updates when developments warrant, often at\:20 and\:50 minutes past the hour. [Audacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audacy,_Inc.)'s Infinity Networks handled the distribution.

In 2026, CBS announced its radio news service would be shut down on May 22, 2026, as part of a larger restructuring due in part to changes in the media landscape and financial pressures.[^cite_note-Swai_03302026-68]

## CBS Newspath

CBS Newspath is CBS News' satellite news-gathering service, similar in format to [CNN Newsource](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CNN_Newsource). Newspath provides national hard news, sports highlights, regional spot news, features and live coverage of major [breaking news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breaking_news) events for affiliate stations to use in their local news broadcasts. The service has a team of domestic and global correspondents and freelance reporters dedicated to reporting for affiliates, and offers several different national or international stories fronted by reporters on a daily basis. CBS Newspath also relies heavily on local affiliates sharing content. Stations will often contribute locally obtained footage that may be of national interest. It replaced a similar service, CBS News NewsNet.

In late 1999, the news-gathering arms of CBS (Newspath), ABC (NewsOne) and Fox (NewsEdge) agreed to form a joint-venture [footage sharing pool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Press_pool), known as *Network News Service*.[^cite_note-92]

## CBS News 24/7

Main article: [CBS News 24/7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_24/7)

CBS News 24/7 is a 24-hour streaming news channel which launched on November 4, 2014, as CBSN.[^cite_note-93] At the time as CBSN, the channel features live news from 9 a.m. to midnight on weekdays. The channel makes all of the resources of CBS News available directly on digital platforms with live, anchored coverage 15 hours each week. It is a first for a U.S. 24-hour news channel to forgo cable and be available exclusively only online and on smart devices such as smart TV's [Apple TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apple_TV_\(device\)), [Roku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roku), [Amazon Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_Fire) and others.[^cite_note-94] The channel is based at CBS's New York City headquarters.[^cite_note-95]

The morning hours are typically anchored by [Errol Barnett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Errol_Barnett) and [Vladimir Duthiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Duthiers), with afternoons anchored by a rotating team. Various correspondents in Washington D.C. anchor a late-afternoon political program titled, 'America Decides' and [John Dickerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Dickerson_\(journalist\)) anchors "The Daily Report", which airs Mondays through Thursdays.

Unionized writers of CBS News 24/7 held a 24-hour walkout on March 17, 2026, after their contract expired on March 9, and negotiations failed.[^cite_note-96] [CBS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS) and [Paramount](79917576.md) signed a three-year agreement with the [Writers Guild of America East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Writers_Guild_of_America_East) on April 2, pending a ratification vote by union members.[^cite_note-97]

## Reporting partnerships

In 2017, CBS News entered into a content-sharing agreement with [BBC News](1139893.md), respectively replacing previous arrangements between the BBC and ABC News, and CBS and [Sky News](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_News) (which was partially controlled by [21st Century Fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/21st_Century_Fox) until 2018 when ownership was then transferred to [Comcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comcast)). The partnership includes the ability to share resources, footage, and reports, and conduct "efficient planning of news gathering resources to increase the content of each broadcaster's coverage of world events".[^cite_note-104]

Although they do not have an official partnership, CNN and CBS News share correspondents and contributors, including [Anderson Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anderson_Cooper) and [Sanjay Gupta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjay_Gupta).[^cite_note-thr-guptacoopercbs-105]

In 2022, CBS News entered into a content-sharing partnership with [The Weather Channel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Weather_Channel), where The Weather Channel meteorologists will appear on CBS News programs.[^cite_note-106]

Beginning in 2025, *[The Free Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Free_Press_\(CBS_News\))* operates as an independent entity within Paramount and has its stories featured online at CBS News.[^cite_note-Mullin-2025-59]

## Controversies

Main article: [CBS News controversies and criticism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBS_News_controversies_and_criticism)

Notable controversies include the resignation of CBS News president [Fred Friendly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_W._Friendly) in 1966 to protest against [Vietnam War](32611.md) coverage,[^cite_note-107] the 2004 [Killian documents controversy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killian_documents_controversy) involving [Dan Rather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Rather) presenting improperly verified documents,[^cite_note-108] accusations of liberal bias[^cite_note-109] and plagiarism,[^cite_note-110] and several instances of misrepresented or erroneously attributed footage.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

### Paramount Skydance ownership controversies

In 2025, CBS News President [Wendy McMahon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendy_McMahon_\(television_executive\)) and [60 Minutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/60_Minutes) executive producer Bill Owens resigned; Owens stated his departure was due to a loss of journalistic independence.[^cite_note-Owens-47][^cite_note-Mastrangelo_20250519-48] Following editorial changes by owner [Larry Ellison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larry_Ellison) and editor-in-chief [Bari Weiss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari_Weiss) that same year, CBS News faced allegations of a conservative, pro-Trump, pro-[MAGA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trumpism) bias.[^cite_note-Baragona_20251003-53][^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] Pro-Trump comments by [Federal Communications Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Communications_Commission) chair [Brendan Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_Carr) regarding CBS News were criticized for highlighting the extent politics played a part in approval of the Paramount-Skydance merger, such as his statement that "President Trump is fundamentally reshaping the media landscape" and that Paramount's promise to remove "bias" at CBS News "made the difference to us".[^cite_note-Meek_07292025-52] In early 2026, under newly appointed editor-in-chief Bari Weiss, CBS Evening News' [Tony Dokoupil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Dokoupil) introduced "5 simple values", a set of principles that inform how the show presents news. The principles, which espoused American-centric values, led to increased speculation that Weiss's appointment was aimed at gaining approval from MAGA supporters, including Donald Trump.[^cite_note-Spangler-63]

*[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* wrote that among the new contributors Weiss had hired was [Mark Hyman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Hyman_\(doctor\)), who had "been called a '[germ theory denialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_theory_denialist)' by the medical author [Harriet Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harriet_Hall)".[^cite_note-\:1-115] *The Guardian* also wrote he is associated with [Robert F. Kennedy Jr.](522298.md) and that he is "a doctor who has claimed that he has reduced his [biological age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_age) by 20 years with therapies including cold plunges; that [cod liver oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cod_liver_oil) can [treat autism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Management_of_autism) and that conditions like Alzheimer's and dementia can be reversed with the kind of [nutritional supplements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nutritional_supplements) he also sells on his online store."[^cite_note-\:1-115] [Jonathan Jarry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jonathan_Jarry), a [science communicator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_communicator) for [McGill University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/McGill_University)'s [Office for Science and Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_for_Science_and_Society) said, "By hiring Mark Hyman, CBS News will be misinforming its large audience on the subject of health. Hyman is the face of [functional medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_medicine) … CBS viewers will be told that true health is about testing for everything – at a cost – and gorging yourself on an array of unproven and unnecessary [dietary supplements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietary_supplement). This is in keeping with what is happening under Trump: the institutionalization of pseudoscience."[^cite_note-\:1-115]

In April 2026, David Ellison and CBS News' Bari Weiss, Norah O'Donnell, Tom Cibrowski, CBS News president, Makan Delrahim, Paramount's chief legal officer, Jan Crawford, the network's chief legal correspondent, chief White House correspondent Nancy Cordes, and Weijia Jiang, White House correspondent and president of the White House Correspondents' Association; all attended a party "honoring" Donald Trump and the First Amendment with Trump, acting attorney general [Todd Blanche](74006584.md), Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](5502549.md), and [Stephen Miller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Miller), deputy White House chief of staff. The DOJ's antitrust division was in the process of [reviewing a merger](81768101.md) by Ellison to acquire Warner Bro. Discovery which would give him ownership of [CNN](62028.md).[^cite_note-116] *The New York Times* reported that party invitations sent to guests said the event was for "honoring the Trump White House", and several CBS News journalists expressed surprise and consternation over the dinner, worried that the event had the "potential to create a perception of coziness between the news division and the Trump administration".[^cite_note-117]

Former *CBS Evening News* anchor [Katie Couric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katie_Couric) expressed concerns over the direction of the network, saying that the focus should be on the truth and not appealing to a certain audience. She called out CBS News' 2026 highlight of the [January 6 United States Capitol attack](66307490.md) as potentially engaging in a [false balance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_balance) by presenting the Trump and Democratic view of the event and questioned whether news broadcasts were supposed to repeat false claims uncritically to appear unbiased.[^cite_note-118]

In June 2026, veteran *60 Minutes* correspondent [Scott Pelley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scott_Pelley) was fired after a series of contentious meetings with its newly installed executive producer, [Nick Bilton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Bilton). After his departure, Pelley accused Weiss and her management team of instructing him to inject "falsehoods and bias" into politicall*y* sensitive *60 Minutes* coverage, pressuring him to make assertions that could not be verified, and allowing politicians to influence which correspondents covered them.[^cite_note-119]
