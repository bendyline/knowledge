**Jammu** ([/ˈdʒʌmuː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)) is a city in Indian-administered [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) in the [Jammu division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_division). It serves as the [winter capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_capital) of the [union territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_territory) of Jammu and Kashmir. Lying on the banks of the river [Tawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tawi_River), the city of Jammu, with an area of 240 km<sup>2</sup> (93 sq mi),[^cite_note-JMC-19] is surrounded by the [Himalayas](37966.md) in the north and the [northern plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain) in the south.

Jammu is the second-most populous city of the union territory. Jammu is known as "City of Temples" for its ancient temples and Hindu shrines.

## Etymology

According to local tradition, Jammu is named after its founder, Raja Jambulochan, who is believed to have ruled the area in the 9th century.[^cite_note-foundation-27] Local tradition holds the city to be 3,000 years old but this is not supported by historians.[^cite_note-Kapur80-28]<sup>: 9 </sup>

## History

According to *[Tarikh-i-Azmi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tarikh-i-Azmi)*, Jammu came into existence around 900 CE. The state of *Durgara* (modern forms "Duggar" and "[Dogra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogras))") is also attested from around this time.[^cite_note-Kapur80-28]<sup>: 9–10 </sup> [^cite_note-Charak_&_Billwaria-29]<sup>: 6 </sup> The capital of the Durgara state at that time is believed to have been Vallapura (identified with modern [Billawar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Billawar)). Its rulers are repeatedly mentioned in Kalhana's *[Rajatarangini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajatarangini)*.[Bamzai, P. N. K.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._N._K._Bamzai) (1994). [*Culture and Political History of Kashmir: Ancient Kashmir*](https://books.google.com/books?id=1eMfzTBcXcYC). M.D. Publications Pvt. Ltd. p. 184. [ISBN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISBN_\(identifier\)) [978-81-85880-31-0](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special\:BookSources/978-81-85880-31-0). Babbapura (modern Babor) is another state mentioned in *Rajatarangini*, some of whose rulers also appear by in the *Vamshavali* (family chronicles) of later Jammu rulers. These rulers are believed to have enjoyed almost independent status and allied themselves with the [Sultans of Delhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate).

Jammu is mentioned by name in the chronicles of [Timur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timur) (r. 1370–1406), who invaded Delhi in 1398 and returned to [Samarkand](51491.md) via Jammu. Raja Bhim Dev is prominently mentioned in the Delhi chronicles as a supporter of [Mubarah Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubarak_Shah_\(Sayyid_dynasty\)) (r. 1421–1434) against [Jasrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasrat).[^cite_note-Charak_&_Billwaria-29]<sup>: 6–7 </sup> Between 1423 and 1442, Jammu came under control of Jasrat (r. 1405–1442) who [conquered it](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jasrat#Invasion_of_Jammu_\(1423\)) after killing his arch-enemy Bhim Dev in 1423. Later, Jasrat appointed Manik Dev (also known as Ajeo Dev) as vassal, and married his daughter.[^cite_note-30] In the Mughal chronicles of [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur) in the early 16th century, Jammu is mentioned as a powerful state in the Punjab hills. It is said to have been ruled by [Manhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manhas) Rajputs. Emperor [Akbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Akbar) brought the hill kingdoms of the region under Mughal suzerainty, but the kings enjoyed considerable political autonomy. In addition to Jammu, other kingdoms of the region such as [Kishtwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kishtwar) and [Rajauri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajauri) were also prominently mentioned. It is evident that the Mughal empire treated these hill chiefs as allies and partners in the empire.[^cite_note-31]

### Modern history



Maharaja [Gulab Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulab_Singh) of Jammu and Kashmir



Maharaja of Kashmir, [Hari Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hari_Singh) (1895–1961)

After the decline of the Mughal power in the 18th century, the Jammu state under Raja Dhruv Dev of the *Jamuwal* (*[Jamwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamwal)*) family asserted its supremacy among the Dugar states. Its ascent reached its peak under his successor, Raja Ranjit Dev (r. 1728–1780), who was widely respected among the hill states.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-Panikker30-33]<sup>: 10 </sup> Ranjit Dev promoted religious freedom and security, which attracted many craftsmen and traders to settle in Jammu, contributing to its economic prosperity.[^cite_note-34]

Towards the end of Ranjit Dev's rule, the Sikh clans of Punjab (*[misls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misls)*) gained ascendency, and Jammu began to be contested by the [Bhangi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhangi_Misl), [Kanhaiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanhaiya_Misl) and [Sukerchakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukerchakia_Misl) *misls*. Around 1770, the Bhangi *misl* attacked Jammu and forced Ranjit Dev to become a tributary. Brij Lal Dev, Ranjit Dev's successor, was defeated by the Sukerchakia chief Mahan Singh, who sacked Jammu and plundered it. Thus Jammu lost its supremacy over the surrounding country. In the Battle of Rumal, the Jammu ruler was killed by Sikhs.[^cite_note-\:0-35][^cite_note-Panikker30-33]<sup>: 10–12 </sup>

In 1808, Jammu itself was annexed to the [Sikh Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire) by [Ranjit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjit_Singh), the son of Mahan Singh.[^cite_note-Panikker30-33]<sup>: 15–16 </sup> In 1818 Raja Kishore Singh, father of Raja [Gulab Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulab_Singh), was appointed and anointed the ruler of Jammu principality, and hence started the Jamwal dynasty, aka [Dogra dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dogra_dynasty), which came to rule the princely state of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(princely_state\)) under British suzerainty. The rulers built large temples, renovated old shrines, built educational institutes and many more. A 43 km (27 mi) long [railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu%E2%80%93Sialkot_line) connecting Jammu with [Sialkot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot) was laid in 1897[^cite_note-36]

Jammu has historically been the capital of [Jammu Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_Division) and the winter capital of the princely state of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_and_Jammu_\(princely_state\)) (1846–1952).

After the partition of India, Jammu continues as the winter capital of the Indian state of [Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_and_Kashmir_\(state\)).

## Geography



The Valley of Jammu

Jammu is located at [32°44′N 74°52′E  /  32.73°N 74.87°E  / 32.73; 74.87](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Jammu\&params=32.73_N_74.87_E_).[^cite_note-39] It has an average elevation of 300 m (980 ft). Jammu city is situated on a series of uneven ridges of low heights in the [Shivalik hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivalik_hills). It is surrounded by the Shivalik range to the north, east, and southeast while the Trikuta Range borders it in the northwest. It is approximately 600 km (370 mi) from the national capital, [New Delhi](51585.md).

The city straddles the Tawi river. The old city overlooks the river from the north (right bank) while the new neighbourhoods are spread around the southern side (left bank) of the river. There are five bridges over the river.

## Climate

Jammu, like the rest of north-western India, features a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) *Cwa*), with extreme summer highs reaching 46 °C (115 °F), and temperatures in the winter months occasionally falling below 4 °C (39 °F). June is the hottest month with average highs of 40.6 °C (105.1 °F), while January is the coldest month with average lows reaching 7 °C (45 °F). Average yearly precipitation is about 1,400 mm (55 in) with the bulk of the rainfall in the months from June to September, although the winters can also be rather wet. In winter dense fog causes much inconvenience and temperature even drops to 2 °C (36 °F). In summer, particularly in May and June, extremely intense sunlight or hot winds can raise the temperature to 46 °C (115 °F). Following the hot season, the [monsoon](57630.md) lashes the city with heavy downpours along with thunderstorms; rainfall may total up to 669 mm (26.3 in) in the wettest months. The city is exposed to heatwaves.

Highest recorded temperature: 47.4 °C (117.3 °F) on 31 May 1988.[^cite_note-NCEI-40]

Lowest recorded temperature: 0.5 °C (32.9 °F) on 24 January 2016.[^cite_note-NCEI-40]

## Transport



Aerial view of [Jammu Tawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_Tawi) station

Jammu city has a railway station called [Jammu Tawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_Tawi) (station code JAT) that is connected with major cities of India. The old [railway link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu%E2%80%93Sialkot_line) to Sialkot was suspended by Pakistan in September 1947, and Jammu had no rail services until 1971, when the [Indian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Railways) laid the [Pathankot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathankot)-Jammu Tawi [Broad Gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad_Gauge) line. The new Jammu Tawi station opened in October 1972 and is an origination point for express trains. With the commencement of the [Jammu–Baramulla line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu%E2%80%93Baramulla_line), all trains to the [Kashmir Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Valley) will pass through Jammu Tawi. A part of the Jammu–Baramulla project has been executed and the track has been extended to [Katra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katra,_Jammu_and_Kashmir). [Jalandhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jalandhar) - [Pathankot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pathankot)-Jammu Tawi section has been doubled and electrified.

In January 2025, the Jammu Division of [Indian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Railways) was inaugurated. The new division will create employment opportunities, infrastructure development, promote tourism and lead to overall socio-economic development of the region.[^cite_note-42]

[National Highway 44](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NH_44) which passes through Jammu connects it to the [Kashmir Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir_Valley). National Highway 1B connects Jammu with Poonch town. Jammu is 80 km (50 mi) from Kathua town, while it is 68 km (42 mi) from Udhampur city. The famous pilgrimage town of [Katra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katra,_Jammu_and_Kashmir) is 49 km (30 mi) from Jammu.

[Jammu Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jammu_Airport) is in the middle of Jammu. It has direct flights to [Srinagar](294148.md), [Delhi](37756.md), [Amritsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amritsar), [Chandigarh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandigarh), [Leh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leh), [Mumbai](19189.md) and [Bengaluru](44275267.md). Jammu Airport operates daily 30 arrival and departure of flights which are served by [Air India](26457880.md), [SpiceJet](1701189.md), and [IndiGo](2085799.md).

The city has [JKSRTC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JKSRTC) city buses and minibusses for local transport which run on some defined routes. These minibusses are called "Matadors". Besides this auto-rickshaw and cycle-rickshaw service is also available. Local taxis are also available.

Jammu city serves as the winter capital of [Jammu and Kashmir](61449812.md) state from November to April when all the offices move from [Srinagar](294148.md) to Jammu. ## Economy

Jammu city is the main cultural and economic centre of the administrative division of Jammu. A famous local [basmati rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basmati_rice) is produced in the RS Pura area near Jammu, and processed in rice mills in Jammu. The industrial estate at [Bari Brahamna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bari_Brahmana) has a large presence of industrial units manufacturing a variety of products including carpets and electronic goods.

## Tourism

Main article: [Tourism in Jammu and Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Jammu_and_Kashmir)

Tourism is the largest industry in Jammu city. It is also a focal point for the pilgrims going to [Vaishno Devi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vaishno_Devi) and Kashmir valley as it is second last railway terminal in North India. All the routes leading to Kashmir, Poonch, Doda and Laddakh start from Jammu city. So throughout the year, the city remains full of people from all the parts of India. Places of interest include old historic palaces like [Mubarak Mandi Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mubarak_Mandi_Palace), Purani Mandi, Rani Park, [Amar Mahal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amar_Mahal), [Bahu Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahu_Fort), [Raghunath Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raghunath_Temple), Ranbireshwar Temple, Karbala, Peer Meetha, Old city.

### Muslim communities

Main article: [1947 Jammu massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Jammu_massacres)

The city of Jammu had a significant Muslim population prior to the [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India), 30 per cent by the 1941 census.[^cite_note-50] During the [1947 Jammu massacres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Jammu_massacres), which preceded and continued during the [Pakistan tribal invasion of Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1947_Indo-Pakistani_War), many Muslims were killed and many driven away to Pakistan.[^cite_note-Bhasin-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

After the conflict ended, many of the displaced Muslims returned and resettled back in the state.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Others settled in [Sialkot District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sialkot_District) and other parts of Pakistani Punjab. Many prominent Punjabi residents in Pakistan, including politician [Chaudhry Amir Hussain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaudhry_Amir_Hussain), economist [Mahbub ul Haq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahbub_ul_Haq), Air Marshal [Asghar Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asghar_Khan), journalist [Khalid Hasan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_Hasan) and singer [Malika Pukhraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malika_Pukhraj) were from Jammu.[^cite_note-Across-56]<sup>: 3, 31 </sup> In 1961, about 17 per cent of the population in the city was Muslim.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-Across-56]<sup>: 30 </sup>

## Cuisine



[Kalari cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalari_cheese)

Jammu is known for its [sund panjeeri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panjiri), [patisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patisa), [rajma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajma) with rice and [Kalari cheese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalari_cheese). Dogri food specialties include ambal, khatta meat, [kulthein di dal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macrotyloma_uniflorum), dal patt, maa da madra, [rajma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajma), and auriya. Pickles typical of Jammu are made of [kasrod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiddlehead_fern), girgle, mango with [saunf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saunf), [jimikand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yam_\(vegetable\)), tyaoo, seyoo, and potatoes. Auriya is a dish made with potatoes. Jammu cuisine features various [chaats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaat), especially [gol gappas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panipuri), kachalu, [Chole bhature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chole_bhature), gulgule, [rajma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajma) [kulche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulcha) and [dahi palla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahi_vada), among various others.[^cite_note-58]

## Refugees

### Kashmiri Pandit refugees

Main article: [Exodus of Kashmiri Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exodus_of_Kashmiri_Hindus)

Being comparatively safe from terrorism, Jammu city has become a hub of refugees. These primarily include [Kashmiri Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmiri_Hindus) who migrated from Kashmir Valley in 1989. Hindus from [Pakistan-administered Jammu and Kashmir](2745.md) who migrated to India have also settled in Jammu city. According to records, approximately 31,619 Hindu families had migrated from Pakistan administered Jammu and Kashmir to India. Of these 26,319 families are settled in Jammu.[^cite_note-59]

### Rohingya refugees

Main article: [2015 Rohingya refugee crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Rohingya_refugee_crisis)

[Rohingyas](1918632.md) who fled [Myanmar during 2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohingya_conflict) have also currently settled in Jammu.[^cite_note-Firstpost-60][^cite_note-61] Some believes the settlements of Rohingya Muslims have also raised security threats in Jammu.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] During the [2018 Sunjuwan attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Sunjuwan_attack), intelligence agencies suspected but did not prove involvement of Rohingya Muslims in the attack.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]
