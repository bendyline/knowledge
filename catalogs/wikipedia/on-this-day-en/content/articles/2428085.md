**Vodafone Ukraine** (originally **UMC Ukraine**, and later **MTS Ukraine**) is the second-largest [mobile operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_network) in [Ukraine](31750.md) with 23.1 million users and thus a 38 percent market share (in September 2014).[^cite_note-1] In November 2009 it had 17.74 million [GSM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSM) subscribers.[^cite_note-2] The company is fully owned by [NEQSOL Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEQSOL_Holding).[^cite_note-654450NEQSOL-3][^cite_note-4] In October 2015 [Mobile TeleSystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_TeleSystems) (MTS) and [Vodafone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone) expanded their 2008 strategic partnership; this resulted in the [rebranding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebranding) of MTS Ukraine to Vodafone Ukraine.[^cite_note-vdfuro-5]

The company's main competitors are [Kyivstar](2254340.md) and [Lifecell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Life:\)_Ukraine).

Vodafone's network codes in Ukraine are +380 50, +380 66, +380 95, and +380 99 (postpay and prepaid users mixed within the codes). The new +380 75 code was allocated in 2024.

## History

The company Ukrainian Mobile Communications (UMC) introduced [mobile services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_telephony) in Ukraine, [Sotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sotel_\(Ukraine\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) initially using the [analog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Analog_signal) [NMT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordic_Mobile_Telephone) standard, then using [GSM-900](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSM-900) and [1800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GSM-1800). In 1993 UMC Ukraine was Ukraine's SIM SIM major [SIM SIM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azercell) [cellular operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Network_Operator). On July 1, 1993 the first call in the company's network was made, by the first [president of Ukraine](20688479.md) [Leonid Kravchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Kravchuk), who called the ambassador of Ukraine to Germany Ivan Piskov.[^cite_note-6]

In 2003 [Mobile TeleSystems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_TeleSystems) (MTS) bought the company. In October 2006, MTS Ukraine terminated its NMT service with plans to use the spectrum for the [CDMA-450](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CDMA) rollout. From late 2006, MTS Ukraine has developed its [3G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3G) [mobile network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_network) with the CDMA-450 [1xEV-DO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1xEV-DO) standard. As of 2016 the Ukrainian MTS network supports various value-added services based on [GPRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GPRS) / [EGPRS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EGPRS) / [EDGE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EDGE_\(telecommunication\)) and [SMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Short_Message_Service) / [WAP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_Application_Protocol) / [MMS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multimedia_Messaging_Service) / [USSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSD). MTS is the only [BlackBerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BlackBerry) [service provider](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Service_provider) in Ukraine; it also provides [Wi-Fi hotspots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi_hotspot), mainly in large cities and at airports.

Corporate social responsibility is one of the major strategic priorities of MTS Ukraine activity.

On March 15, 2007, MTS Ukraine became the first telecommunication company in Ukraine to receive the Certificate of [European Foundation for Quality Management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Foundation_for_Quality_Management) (EFQM) "Perfection Recognition", which confirms the high standards of communication-operator work.



MTS Ukraine logo (2007—2015)

In 2008, and MTS Ukraine's owner MTS and [Vodafone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vodafone) signed a strategic partnership.[^cite_note-vdfuro-5]

In 2011 MTS Ukraine openly declared its interest in acquiring [Utel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utel_\(telecommunications\)), a [subsidiary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsidiary) of [Ukrtelecom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrtelecom), Ukraine's monopolist telephone company, after Ukrtelecom announced plans to divest its mobile business.[^cite_note-7] [Vasyl Latsanych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasyl_Latsanych?action=edit\&redlink=1) was appointed as company general director this year.[^cite_note-8]

In October 2015 MTS and Vodafone expanded their strategic partnership; this resulted in the [rebranding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rebranding) of MTS Ukraine as Vodafone Ukraine.[^cite_note-vdfuro-5]

On 11 and 12 January 2018, the company operations in Ukraine's separatist entities on the occupied territories of Luhansk Oblast (on 11 January) and Donetsk Oblast (on 12 January) were discontinued after a [fibre-optic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiber-optic_communication) line cut.[^cite_note-afp.com-9][^cite_note-10] This resulted in two million people losing mobile phone access.[^cite_note-afp.com-9] Until the Vodafone Ukraine line was repaired the remaining mobile phone carriers available in the region were the two operated by the separatist authorities.[^cite_note-afp.com-9] On 19 January the repair of the fibre optic line was completed and Vodafone Ukraine's services in territory controlled by the Luhansk People's Republic completely resumed.[^cite_note-sg.news.yahoo.com-11] However, the territory controlled by the Donetsk People's Republic remained without a mobile connection; "the network does not work for reasons unknown to us" according to Vodafone Ukraine.[^cite_note-sg.news.yahoo.com-11] On 23 March 2018, "Donetsk People's Republic" stated that Vodafone Ukraine could only resume operating in the territory controlled by it if it would start to pay taxes to the unrecognised state.[^cite_note-korrespondent.net-12] Vodafone Ukraine refused.[^cite_note-korrespondent.net-12]

In 2019 MTS sold its Ukraine operations to [NEQSOL Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NEQSOL_Holding),[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-654450NEQSOL-3] an [Azerbaijani](746.md) holding company founded in the 1990s and led by Chairman Yusif Jabbarov, who also serves as Deputy Chairman of Vodafone Ukraine.[^cite_note-14]

## War in Ukraine

Since the beginning of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) on February 24, 2022, Vodafone Ukraine has been committed to helping civilians victims of the security situation on the ground. The company has been providing free national roaming in order to allow soldiers as well as civilians to remain connected, even in damaged areas.[^cite_note-15]
