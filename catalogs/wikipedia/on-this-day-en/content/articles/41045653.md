The **2026 Winter Paralympics** ([Italian](14708.md): *Giochi paralimpici invernali di Milano Cortina 2026*), also known as the **Milano Cortina 2026 Winter Paralympic Games**, were the 14th edition of the [Winter Paralympic Games](18670284.md), an international [winter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_sports) [multi-sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-sport_event) [parasports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasports) event governed by the [International Paralympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Paralympic_Committee). It took place from 6 to 15 March at sites across [Lombardy](43807.md), [Veneto](43780.md) and [Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol](246766.md), with competition in [wheelchair curling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelchair_curling) beginning on 4 March.

Marking the 50th anniversary of the [inaugural Winter Paralympics in 1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Winter_Paralympics), this was the [third Paralympic Games hosted in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_at_the_Paralympics), following the [1960 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1960_Summer_Paralympics) in [Rome](25458.md) (the inaugural Paralympic Games) and the [2006 Winter Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Winter_Paralympics) in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin). These were the final winter games to take place under the IPC presidency of [Andrew Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Parsons_\(sports_administrator\)).

[China](82570638.md) finished at the top of the [medal table](82544813.md) for the second successive Winter Paralympics with 15 gold and 44 medals overall, and the [United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_at_the_2026_Winter_Paralympics) finished second with 13 gold and 24 medals. [Russia](82446138.md) finished in third with 8 gold and 12 medals. The host nation [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) finished in fourth with 7 gold and 16 medals, setting a new record for number of medals won. [Brazil](82569844.md) won its first medal in their Winter Paralympic history, becoming the first [tropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_nations_at_the_Winter_Olympics), [Latin American](18524.md) and [South American](26769.md) National Paralympic Committee to win a medal at the Winter Paralympics.[^cite_note-7] [Latvia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latvia_at_the_2026_Winter_Paralympics) also won its first medal in its Winter Paralympic history.[^cite_note-8]

The edition drew controversy surrounding its inclusion of Russian and Belarusian athletes for the first time since the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).

## Host selection

Main article: [Bids for the 2026 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bids_for_the_2026_Winter_Olympics)

In October 2018, the [Italian National Olympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_National_Olympic_Committee) officially confirmed plans for a [bid by Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milan%E2%80%93Cortina_d'Ampezzo_bid_for_the_2026_Winter_Olympics) to host the Games.[^cite_note-9] The bid was presented at the general assembly of the [Association of National Olympic Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_National_Olympic_Committees) on 28 November 2018 in [Tokyo](30057.md).[^cite_note-10] The two cities were elected as hosts of both the 2026 Paralympics and [Olympics](38416091.md) on 24 June 2019 at the [134th IOC Session](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/134th_IOC_Session) in [Lausanne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lausanne), Switzerland, defeating Sweden's joint bid of [Stockholm](26741.md) and [Åre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%85re).[^cite_note-11]

## Development and preparations

### Handover ceremony

The handover occurred during the [closing ceremony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Paralympics_closing_ceremony) of the [2022 Winter Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Winter_Paralympics) in [Beijing](18603746.md), China, on 13 March. The [Paralympic flag](7895540.md) was passed from [Beijing mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Beijing) [Chen Jining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Jining) to IPC president [Andrew Parsons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Parsons_\(sports_administrator\)), and then to Anna Scavuzzo, vice-mayor of Milan, and Gianpietro Ghedina, mayor of Cortina d'Ampezzo.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The flag was then flown back to Italy, where it arrived the following day.[^cite_note-16] Both the Olympic flag and the Paralympic flag are displayed at [Palazzo Marino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palazzo_Marino) and will remain there until the end of their respective events.[^cite_note-17]

### Torch relay



The Paralympic flame arrives in [Mestre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestre), [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) (4 March).

Main article: [2026 Winter Paralympics torch relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Winter_Paralympics_torch_relay)

The [torch relay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_torch_relay) took place from 24 February to 6 March 2026, with 501 torchbearers covering 2,000 kilometres (1,200 mi). The flame was lit at the [Stoke Mandeville Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoke_Mandeville_Hospital) in the United Kingdom and was sent to five Italian cities; the relay visited [Cortina d'Ampezzo](568829.md), [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice), and [Padua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padua), ending at the [Verona Arena](703367.md) for the [opening ceremony](77701912.md).[^cite_note-18]

The Paralympic torch was presented in a simultaneous event held in Milan in Italy and [Osaka](52230.md) in Japan. It was revealed at the [Triennale di Milano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triennale_di_Milano) and at the Italian pavilion at [Expo 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2025) as a way of connecting the two [twin cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_city), as Milan previously hosted [Expo 2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_2015).[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] The torch is bronze whereas the corresponding Olympic torch is light blue.[^cite_note-22]

Named "Essential"; developed by [Eni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eni) and its subsidiary [Versalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Versalis), designed by Studio [Carlo Ratti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlo_Ratti) Associati and produced in Italy by Cavagna Group, the torches are made primarily of an alloy of recycled aluminium and bronze. They run on bio-LPG, a fuel made from renewable materials, produced at the [Enilive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enilive?action=edit\&redlink=1) biorefinery in [Gela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gela), and have been designed to be refilled up to ten times in order to cut down on the number of torches produced.[^cite_note-23]

### Volunteers

A total of 18,000 volunteers were involved with both the 2026 Paralympic and Olympic Games. Applications for volunteering opened on 19 September 2024.[^cite_note-24] As of December 2024, almost 70,000 applications have been received.[^cite_note-25]

### Ticketing

Tickets for the 2026 Paralympic Games started at €10 for children under 14. About 89% of all available tickets – more than 200,000 – were priced at under €35. Registration for the ticketing platform opened on 4 October 2024 and ticket sales started in March 2025.[^cite_note-26]

## The Games

### Opening ceremony

Main article: [2026 Winter Paralympics opening ceremony](77701912.md)

The opening ceremony took place on 6 March 2026 at the [Verona Arena](703367.md) in [Verona](263991.md), entitled *Life in Motion*, with production by [Filmmaster Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filmmaster_Group?action=edit\&redlink=1). The Paralympic cauldrons were lit by Italian Paralympians [Gianmaria Dal Maistro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gianmaria_Dal_Maistro) in Milan and [Francesca Porcellato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesca_Porcellato) in Cortina d'Ampezzo.[^cite_note-28]

A number of delegations skipped the opening ceremony entirely; some of these absences were for logistical reasons, as Verona is 250 kilometres (160 mi) from Cortina d'Ampezzo. A number of teams additionally boycotted the opening ceremony in protest of the IPC allowing Russian and Belarusian athletes to compete under their national flags.[^cite_note-\:3-29][^cite_note-\:4-30][^cite_note-\:5-31] Organisers stated that all participating [National Paralympic Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Paralympic_Committee) ([NPCs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Paralympic_Committee)) would still be featured in the parade of nations regardless of athlete presence, with volunteer flag bearers and video packages being used to represent NPCs which could not attend.[^cite_note-\:5-31]

The Games are expected to feature 79 events in six [Winter Paralympic sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralympic_sports#Winter_Paralympics). A [mixed doubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mixed_doubles) event made its debut in [wheelchair curling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheelchair_curling).[^cite_note-32] A total of 665 athlete quota places are available: 323 for men, 176 for women, and 166 gender-free.[^cite_note-33]

### Closing ceremony

Main article: [2026 Winter Paralympics closing ceremony](77701915.md)

The closing ceremony took place on 15 March 2026 at the [Cortina Olympic Ice Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stadio_olimpico_del_ghiaccio) in [Cortina d'Ampezzo](568829.md), entitled *Italian Souvenir*, with production by G2 Eventi – Casta Diva Group.[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] Several nations, led by Ukraine, boycotted the ceremony due to the participation of Russian and Belarusian athletes at the Games.[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

## Participating National Paralympic Committees

A total of 55 [National Paralympic Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Paralympic_Committee) have qualified athletes for the Games.[^cite_note-39] For the first time since 2014 (due to the [Russian doping scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_doping_scandal) and [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md)), Russian and Belarusian athletes were allowed to compete under their flags, rather than neutrally (as they were [at the Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_Neutral_Athletes_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics)).[^cite_note-\:0-40][^cite_note-\:1-41]

El Salvador, Haiti, Montenegro, North Macedonia and Portugal made their Winter Paralympic debuts. El Salvador was also the only country not participating in the 2026 Winter Olympics that was participating in the 2026 Winter Paralympics. Iran was scheduled to participate, but its only athlete withdrew a few hours before the opening ceremony due to being unable to travel to Italy as a result of the [United States and Israel attack on Iran](82533582.md).[^cite_note-\:8-42]

## Marketing

### Emblem

On 30 March 2021, following a public vote between two candidates designed by [Landor Associates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landor_Associates), a design named "Futura" was announced as the [emblem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_emblem) of both the 2026 Winter Olympics and Paralympics. The Paralympic version is coloured with a red, blue, and green [gradient](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gradient) to symbolise an [aurora](49658.md) and the colours of the [Paralympic agitos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralympic_agitos).[^cite_note-emblem-51][^cite_note-52][^cite_note-53]

### Mascot

Main article: [Tina and Milo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tina_and_Milo)



&#x20;Tina and Milo

An online vote closing on 28 February 2023 was held among a list of candidates to select the mascots of the Olympics and Paralympics, with the winning candidates being inspired by [stoats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoat).[^cite_note-54] Their names were revealed to be Tina and Milo (derived from the names of the host cities),[^cite_note-55] with Milo, the brown stoat, being the [Paralympic mascot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paralympic_mascot). The character is portrayed as having been born without a leg and having learned to use his tail as a leg. Milo represents ingenuity, willpower and creativity.[^cite_note-56]

### Song

In March 2022, "Fino all'alba" (transl. Until dawn) by [Arisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arisa) was unveiled as the official song for both the 2026 Winter Olympics and Paralympics.[^cite_note-57] The song was composed by Francesco Marrone and Giulio Gianni.[^cite_note-58]

### Visual identity

The [brand elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brand_elements) for both the 2026 Paralympic and Olympic Games were unveiled on 7 November 2024, drawing inspiration from [gestures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gestures), which are commonly used [in Italian communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesticulation_in_Italian). These include [pictograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_pictograms) for the six Paralympic sports inspired by the movements of athletes.[^cite_note-look-59][^cite_note-60] Five graphic elements named "vibes" were designed, with contemporary Italian figures selected to represent them on social media: musician [Dardust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dardust) (creativity), athlete [Bebe Vio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bebe_Vio) (energy), comedian [Federico Basso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federico_Basso?action=edit\&redlink=1) (imagination), chef [Davide Oldani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davide_Oldani?action=edit\&redlink=1) (passion), and dancer [Nicoletta Manni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicoletta_Manni) (style).[^cite_note-look-59]

## Broadcasting

At least 300 hours of television coverage was provided.[^cite_note-61] In October 2025, the [European Broadcasting Union](10380.md) acquired rights to the Games in Europe, with rights to be sub-licensed to its members; the EBU re-gained the pan-European rights for the first time since 2018.[^cite_note-62] Rights agreements were also renewed with [CBC Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBC_Sports) (Canada, in partnership with the [Canadian Paralympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Paralympic_Committee)), and [Grupo Globo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grupo_Globo) (Brazil).[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

## Concerns and controversies

### Participation of Russian and Belarusian athletes

Further information: [International sporting sanctions during the Russian invasion of Ukraine](82507892.md)

Russian athletes had been prohibited from competing in the Paralympic Games under their name, flag, and [national anthem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_anthem_of_Russia) since 2016. This sanction originally stemmed from the discovery of a [state-sponsored doping program](55980489.md) that had been active during the [2014 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Winter_Olympics) and [Paralympics](2311504.md) in Sochi.[^cite_note-\:2-65][^cite_note-66] Russia was banned entirely from the [2016 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Paralympics),[^cite_note-\:2-65] and its athletes were allowed to compete [neutrally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Paralympic_Athletes_at_the_2018_Winter_Paralympics) under the Paralympic flag in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Winter_Paralympics).[^cite_note-67]

Following a ruling by the [Court of Arbitration for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport) that challenged an outright ban from international sport by the [World Anti-Doping Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Anti-Doping_Agency), Russia was allowed to send a [semi-neutral team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Paralympic_Committee_athletes_at_the_2020_Summer_Paralympics) under the emblem of the [Russian Paralympic Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Paralympic_Committee) (RPC) at the [2020 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Summer_Paralympics). The team was, with exceptions, still prohibited from using the name, flag, or national anthem of Russia.[^cite_note-68] RPC was also expected to compete in the 2022 Winter Paralympics. Due to the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) that began shortly before the Games, the IPC initially announced that RPC and Belarusian athletes would compete neutrally under the Paralympic flag. However, following the threat of boycotts by multiple NPCs, the IPC walked back the decision and banned Russian and Belarusian athletes from the Games entirely.[^cite_note-athletes_from_russia_belarus_banned_from_competing_beijing_2022-69][^cite_note-russian_belarusian_athletes_to_still_receive_medals_beijing_2022-70] Both nations' athletes competed under the banner of "[Neutral Paralympic Athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neutral_Paralympic_Athletes_at_the_2024_Summer_Paralympics)" at the [2024 Summer Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Summer_Paralympics).[^cite_note-71]

In September 2025, the IPC reinstated the Russian Paralympic Committee, allowing Russian athletes to participate in IPC-sanctioned competitions with their flag and anthem.[^cite_note-72] The IPC cited a desire to separate politics from sport, and believed that continuing to ban Russia from competition while allowing Israel to participate despite the [Gaza war](74998519.md) was inconsistent.[^cite_note-\:4-30] The IPC noted in October 2025 that it was unlikely that Russia would be able to qualify any athletes to the Games: the [FIS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Ski_and_Snowboard_Federation), [IBU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Biathlon_Union), and [World Curling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Curling) continued to prohibit Russian and [Belarusian](3457.md) athletes from participating in Olympic and Paralympic qualifying competitions, even under a neutral status,[^cite_note-73] and it was "not possible in practice" for Russia to qualify for Para ice hockey.[^cite_note-74] In December 2025, the [Court of Arbitration for Sport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_Arbitration_for_Sport) ruled in an appeal that Russian athletes who meet the IOC's criteria for [individual neutral athletes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individual_Neutral_Athletes_at_the_2026_Winter_Olympics), must be able to compete neutrally in FIS qualifying events for the 2026 Winter Olympics and Paralympics.[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-\:1-41]

On 17 February 2026, it was reported that six Russian athletes and four Belarusian athletes across alpine skiing, cross-country skiing, and snowboarding had been awarded invitations by the Bipartite Commission, allowing them to compete in the Games. The decision was met with condemnation from Ukraine and its European allies, who considered it inappropriate for Russia and Belarus to return to international sport amid the still-ongoing Russian invasion of Ukraine.[^cite_note-\:0-40][^cite_note-\:1-41] Ukrainian sports minister [Matvii Bidnyi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matvii_Bidnyi) announced that Ukrainian officials would boycott the opening ceremony and some other events in protest against the participation of Russian athletes. Bidnyi argued that Russia uses Paralympic sport for propaganda purposes and criticized the International Paralympic Committee for allowing Russian representation at the Games.[^cite_note-76] IPC president Andrew Parsons also stated that Russian soldiers injured in the war could be eligible to compete in future Paralympic Games.[^cite_note-77]

Following the decision, Ukraine confirmed that its delegation would not attend the opening ceremony, and several other countries expressed solidarity with the boycott. These boycotts did not impact athlete participation, while some of these countries, among others, had already planned to not send their athletes to the opening ceremony for logistical reasons.[^cite_note-\:5-31][^cite_note-\:3-29][^cite_note-\:4-30][^cite_note-\:6-78][^cite_note-79] EU commissioner for sport [Glenn Micallef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Micallef) announced that he would not attend the opening ceremony, in solidarity with Ukraine.[^cite_note-a694-80] Ukraine's public broadcaster [Suspilne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suspilne) announced that it would not broadcast the opening ceremony,[^cite_note-81] while Estonian broadcaster [Eesti Rahvusringhääling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eesti_Rahvusringh%C3%A4%C3%A4ling) (ERR) announced that it would not broadcast any event featuring Russian or Belarusian athletes.[^cite_note-82]

The [Ukrainian delegation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_at_the_2026_Winter_Paralympics) provided its athletes with a ceremonial uniform decorated with a map of the country, including Crimea and Donbas. However, the IPC prohibited its use, claiming that the uniform had political content, which was not appropriate for the context.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

On 10 March, after Russia won a gold medal with [Anastasiia Bagiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasiia_Bagiian) and her guide Sergei Siniakin in the women's sprint classic vision impaired event, the German silver medalists [Linn Kazmaier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Linn_Kazmaier) and Florian Baumann turned their backs towards the Russian athletes on the podium, citing not supporting the "system" in Russia. They were also turned their backs by the Czech silver medalists [Simona Bubeníčková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simona_Buben%C3%AD%C4%8Dkov%C3%A1) and David Srutek and German bronze medalists [Leonie Walter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonie_Walter) and Christian Krasman on 11 March, and a third time by the same Czech silver medalists [Simona Bubeníčková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simona_Buben%C3%AD%C4%8Dkov%C3%A1) and David Srutek on 15 March.[^cite_note-c936-85]

On 15 March, IPC president Andrew Parsons said he was ″disappointed″ with the complaints from the Ukrainian National Paralympic Committee, saying that ″I do hope this is a lesson learned also for those NPCs that have chosen to focus more on the political side than on the sport side, because sport has prevailed″. In turn, [Valerii Sushkevych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valeriy_Sushkevych), president of Ukraine's National Paralympic Committee, said that calling for peace and stopping the war was a human-rights issue, not a political statement. Sushkevych also said that the ceremonies are not sporting competitions, but are used as a political event by the IPC.[^cite_note-86]

### Israel and United States attack on Iran

Main article: [2026 Iran war](82533582.md)

On 28 February, during the period of the [Olympic Truce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Truce)[^cite_note-\:12-87][^cite_note-\:22-88] and six days before the opening ceremony, a coordinated joint attack was launched in Iran by Israel and the United States in an attempt to force [regime change](154074.md), including the [killing](82537558.md) of Supreme Leader [Ali Khamenei](385653.md) and [several other Iranian officials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Iranian_officials_killed_during_the_2026_Iran_conflict).[^cite_note-\:7-89][^cite_note-90] Iran was scheduled to send one athlete—cross-country skier Aboulfazl Khatibi Mianaei—to the Games.[^cite_note-\:8-42]

The IPC stated that it was monitoring the impact of the situation and Middle Eastern airspace closures on travel logistics, especially for athletes coming from the [East Asia](19605700.md) and [Oceania](22621.md) regions.[^cite_note-91] On 6 March, the IPC confirmed that Khatibi Mianaei had withdrawn from the Games; president Andrew Parsons stated, "\[W]ith the conflict ongoing across the Middle East, the risk to human life is too high", and said that damage to infrastructure in the country made it difficult to communicate with the Iranian delegation.[^cite_note-\:8-42]

### Insufficient snowfall

The unusually mild conditions of the host venues were a cause of concern amongst athletes, with Canadian athlete [Kurt Oatway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Oatway) stating: "These games need to be held a month earlier." The [para-alpine skiing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para-alpine_skiing) events at [Olimpia delle Tofane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olimpia_delle_Tofane) saw multiple crashes, with some athletes blaming the [slush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slush) conditions at the venue. Overall, unlike the Winter Olympics held a month before, [snowmaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmaking) machines were used more often due to a lack of snowfall. In response, the [International Olympic Committee](15147.md) stated that it was considering moving up both the Olympic and Paralympic Games due to [climate change](5042951.md).[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]
