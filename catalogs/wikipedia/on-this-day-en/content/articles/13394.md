**Honduras**,[^cite_note-10] officially the **Republic of Honduras**,[^cite_note-11] is a country in [Central America](6121.md). It is bordered to the west by [Guatemala](17238567.md), to the southwest by [El Salvador](9356.md), to the southeast by [Nicaragua](21362.md), to the south by the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) at the [Gulf of Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Fonseca), and to the north by the [Gulf of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Honduras), a large inlet of the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md). Its [capital](181337.md) and largest city is [Tegucigalpa](25994100.md).

Honduras was home to several important [Mesoamerican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica) cultures, most notably the [Maya](18449273.md), before [Spanish colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_colonization_of_the_Americas) in the 16th century. The Spanish introduced [Catholicism](606848.md) and the [Spanish language](26825.md), along with numerous customs that have blended with the indigenous culture. Honduras became independent in 1821 and has since been a republic, although it has consistently endured much social strife and political instability, and remains one of the poorest countries in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md). In 1960, the northern part of what was the [Mosquito Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast) was transferred from Nicaragua to Honduras by the [International Court of Justice](14918.md).[^cite_note-auto-12]

The economy is primarily [agricultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), making it vulnerable to [natural disasters](58921.md) such as [Hurricane Mitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Mitch) in 1998.[^cite_note-\:9-13] Honduras has a [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) of 0.624, ranking 138th in the world.[^cite_note-\:1-14] In 2022, according to the National Institute of Statistics of Honduras, 73% of the country's population lived in poverty, and 53% lived in [extreme poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_poverty).[^cite_note-15] The lower class is primarily agriculturally based while wealth is concentrated in the urban centers.[^cite_note-LOC-16] The country is one of the most [economically unequal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_inequality) in [Latin America](18524.md).[^cite_note-17] Honduran society is predominantly [Mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo); however, there are also significant Indigenous, Black, and White communities.[^cite_note-cia.gov-18]

In the early 20th century, Honduras was dominated by transnational agricultural companies, whose interests were protected militarily by the United States. The term "[banana republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_republic)" was originally coined to describe Honduras's politically unstable, corporate-dominated democracy. The civilian government was overthrown in a 1963 military coup, and the nation returned to civilian rule in 1979. The United States re-established its military presence in Honduras in the 1980s, using Honduras as a base of operations to oppose the Nicaraguan government in the [Contra war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicaraguan_Revolution).

A [2009 military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Honduran_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) toppled the government of [Manuel Zelaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Zelaya), and controversy arose from claims of electoral fraud in the subsequent [2013](36492620.md) and [2017 presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Honduran_general_election).[^cite_note-\:7-19][^cite_note-\:4-20] In [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Honduran_general_election) [Xiomara Castro](6016595.md) was elected to the presidency, returning the left to power for the first time since the 2009 coup. [Nasry Asfura](39721295.md) won the [2025 election](77491476.md) amid claims of fraud and electoral interference by the United States.[^cite_note-\:19-21]

Honduras spans about 112,492 km<sup>2</sup> (43,433 sq mi) and has a population exceeding 10 million.[^cite_note-UN_WPP-22][^cite_note-UN_WPP_2022-23] Its northern portions are part of the [western Caribbean zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Caribbean_zone), as reflected in the area's demographics and culture. Honduras is known for its rich natural resources, including minerals, coffee, tropical fruit, and sugar cane, as well as for its growing textiles industry, which serves the international market.

## Etymology

The literal meaning of the Spanish term *honduras* is 'depths'. The name could refer either to the bay of [Trujillo](2342345.md) as an anchorage, *fondura* in the [Leonese dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonese_dialect) of Spain, or to [Christopher Columbus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Columbus)'s alleged quote that "*Gracias a Dios que hemos salido de esas honduras*" ("Thank God we have departed from those depths").[^cite_note-timeline-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-Davidson2006-26]

It was not until the end of the 16th century that *Honduras* was used for the whole province. Prior to 1580, *Honduras* referred to only the eastern part of the province, and *Higueras* referred to the western part.[^cite_note-Davidson2006-26] Another early name is [Guaymuras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaimura), revived as the name for the [political dialogue in 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guaymuras_dialogue) that took place in Honduras as opposed to Costa Rica.[^cite_note-hoduras_claire-27]

[Hondurans](14387143.md) are often referred to as either the masculine *catracho* or the feminine *catracha* in Spanish.

## History

Main article: [History of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Honduras)



A [Maya stela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maya_stelae), an emblematic symbol of the Honduran Mayan civilization at Copan

### Pre-colonial period

Further information: [Mesoamerican chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_chronology)

See also: [Bajo Aguán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajo_Agu%C3%A1n)

In the pre-Columbian era, the territory was split between two pan-cultural regions: [Mesoamerica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerica) in the west and the [Isthmo-Colombian area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isthmo-Colombian_area) in the east. Each complex had a "core area" within Honduras (the [Sula Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sula_Valley) for Mesoamerica, and [La Mosquitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Mosquitia_\(Honduras\)) for the Isthmo-Colombian area), and the intervening area was one of gradual transition. However, these concepts had no meaning in the Pre-Columbian era and represent diverse areas. The [Lenca people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenca) of the interior highlands are also generally considered to be culturally Mesoamerican, though the extent of linkage with other areas varied over time (for example, expanding during the zenith of the [Toltec Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toltec_Empire)).

In the extreme west, [Maya civilization](18449273.md) flourished for hundreds of years. The dominant, best known, and best studied state was in [Copán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cop%C3%A1n), which was located in a mainly non-Maya area, or on the frontier between Maya and non-Maya areas. Copán declined with other Lowland centres during the conflagrations of the [Terminal Classic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_Maya_collapse) in the 9th century. The Maya of this civilization survive in western Honduras as the [Ch'orti'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch'orti'_people), isolated from their Choltian linguistic peers to the west.[^cite_note-28]

However, Copán represents only a fraction of the pre-Columbian history, with remnants of other civilizations throughout the country. Archaeologists have studied sites such as [Naco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naco_\(Honduras\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and La Sierra in the Naco Valley, [Los Naranjos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Naranjos,_Honduras) on [Lake Yojoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Yojoa), [Yarumela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yarumela) in the Comayagua Valley,[^cite_note-29] [La Ceiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ceiba) and Salitron Viejo[^cite_note-30] (both now under the [Cajón Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Caj%C3%B3n_Dam_\(Honduras\)) [reservoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reservoir)), Selin Farm and Cuyamel in the Aguan valley, [Cerro Palenque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cerro_Palenque), Travesia, Curruste, Ticamaya, Despoloncal, and [Playa de los Muertos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Playa_de_los_Muertos) in the lower [Ulúa River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ul%C3%BAa_River) valley, and many others.

In 2012, LiDAR scanning revealed that several previously unknown high density settlements existed in La Mosquitia, corresponding to the legend of "[La Ciudad Blanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ciudad_Blanca)". Excavation and study has since improved knowledge of the region's history. It is estimated that these settlements reached their zenith from 500 to 1000 AD.

### Spanish conquest (1524–1539)

Main article: [Spanish conquest of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_conquest_of_Honduras)



[Hernán Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s), one of the conquerors of Honduras

On his fourth and the final voyage to the [New World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_World) in 1502, Columbus landed near the modern town of [Trujillo](2342345.md), near Guaimoreto Lagoon, becoming the first European to visit the [Bay Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Islands_Department).[^cite_note-31] On 30 July 1502, Columbus sent his brother [Bartholomew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomew_Columbus) to explore the islands, and Bartholomew encountered a Mayan trading vessel from [Yucatán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yucat%C3%A1n_Peninsula), carrying well-dressed Maya and a rich cargo.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-clendinnen2003-33] Bartholomew's men stole the cargo and kidnapped the ship's elderly captain to serve as an interpreter[^cite_note-clendinnen2003-33] in the first recorded encounter between the Spanish and the Maya.[^cite_note-34]

In March 1524, [Gil González Dávila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gil_Gonz%C3%A1lez_D%C3%A1vila) became the first Spaniard to enter Honduras as a [conquistador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquistador).[^cite_note-Apuntes-35][^cite_note-Guide-36] followed by [Hernán Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hern%C3%A1n_Cort%C3%A9s), who had brought forces down from Mexico. Much of the conquest took place in the following two decades, first by groups loyal to [Cristóbal de Olid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crist%C3%B3bal_de_Olid), and then by those loyal to [Francisco de Montejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_de_Montejo) but most particularly by those following Alvarado. In addition to Spanish resources, the conquerors relied heavily on armed forces from Mexico – [Tlaxcalans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tlaxcalans) and [Mexica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aztec) armies of thousands who remained garrisoned in the region.

Resistance to conquest was led in particular by [Lempira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lempira_\(Lenca_ruler\)). Many regions in the north of Honduras never fell to the Spanish, notably the [Miskito Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito_Coast). After the Spanish conquest, Honduras became part of Spain's vast empire in the New World within the [Kingdom of Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_Guatemala). Trujillo and [Gracias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracias_a_Dios_Department) were the first city-capitals. The Spanish ruled the region for approximately three centuries.

### Spanish Honduras (1524–1821)



[Church of San Manuel de Colohete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_San_Manuel_de_Colohete?action=edit\&redlink=1)

Honduras was organized as a province of the [Kingdom of Guatemala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captaincy_General_of_Guatemala), and the capital was first at Trujillo on the Atlantic coast and later at [Comayagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comayagua), and finally at [Tegucigalpa](25994100.md) in the central part of the country.

[Silver mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_mining) was a key factor in the Spanish conquest and settlement of Honduras.[^cite_note-Newson1-37] Initially the mines were worked by local people through the [encomienda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Encomienda) system, but as disease and resistance made this option less available, slaves from other parts of Central America were brought in. When local slave trading stopped at the end of the 16th century, African slaves, mostly from [Angola](701.md), were imported.[^cite_note-Cost-38] After about 1650, very few slaves or other outside workers arrived in Honduras.

Although the Spanish conquered the southern or Pacific portion of Honduras fairly quickly, they were less successful on the northern (Atlantic) side. They managed to found a few towns along the coast—at [Puerto Caballos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Cort%C3%A9s) and Trujillo in particular—but failed to conquer the eastern portion of the region and many pockets of independent indigenous people as well. The Miskito Kingdom in the northeast was particularly effective at resisting conquest, which found support from northern European privateers, pirates and especially the British formerly English colony of [Jamaica](15660.md), which placed much of the area under its protection after 1740.



The [Fortaleza de San Fernando de Omoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Fernando_de_Omoa) was built by the Spanish to protect the coast of Honduras from English pirates.

### Independence (1821)

Honduras gained independence from Spain in 1821 and [was a part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_America_under_Mexican_rule) of the [First Mexican Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mexican_Empire) until 1823, when it became part of the [United Provinces of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Provinces_of_Central_America). It has been an independent [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic) and has held regular elections since 1838. In the 1840s and 1850s Honduras participated in several failed attempts at Central American unity, such as the Confederation of Central America (1842–1845), the covenant of Guatemala (1842), the Diet of Sonsonate (1846), the Diet of Nacaome (1847) and National Representation in Central America (1849–1852). Although Honduras eventually adopted the name Republic of Honduras, the unionist ideal never waned, and Honduras was one of the Central American countries that pushed hardest for a policy of regional unity.

Policies favoring [international trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_trade) and investment began in the 1870s. Foreign interests became involved, first in shipping from the north coast, especially tropical fruit and most notably bananas, and then in building railroads. [Comayagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comayagua) was the capital until 1880, when the capital moved to [Tegucigalpa](25994100.md). In 1888, a projected railroad line from the Caribbean coast to Tegucigalpa ran out of money when it reached [San Pedro Sula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_Sula). As a result, San Pedro grew into the nation's primary industrial center and second-largest city. Since independence, nearly 300 small internal rebellions and civil wars have occurred, including some changes of régime.[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

### 20th century and the role of American companies

See also: [Banana Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_Wars)

In the late 19th century, Honduras granted land and substantial exemptions to several US-based fruit and infrastructure companies in return for developing the country's northern regions. Thousands of workers came to the north coast as a result to work in [banana plantations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_production_in_Honduras) and other businesses that grew up around the export industry. Banana-exporting companies, dominated until 1930 by the [Cuyamel Fruit Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyamel_Fruit_Company), as well as the [United Fruit Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Fruit_Company), and [Standard Fruit Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Fruit_Company), built an [enclave economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enclave_economy) in northern Honduras, controlling infrastructure and creating self-sufficient, tax-exempt sectors that contributed relatively little to economic growth. American troops landed in Honduras in 1903, 1907, 1911, 1912, 1919, 1924 and 1925.[^cite_note-Becker-41]

American writer [O. Henry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/O._Henry) coined the term "[banana republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banana_republic)"[^cite_note-BananaRep-42] in his 1904 novel *[Cabbages and Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabbages_and_Kings_\(novel\))* to describe a fictional country, Anchuria, that was inspired by his experiences in Honduras, where he had lived for six months.[^cite_note-43] In *The Admiral*, one of the interlinking stories from which he composed the novel, O. Henry refers to Anchuria as a "small maritime banana republic"; naturally, the fruit was the entire basis of its economy.[^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45] According to a literary analyst writing for *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)*, "his phrase neatly conjures up the image of a tropical, agrarian country. But its real meaning is sharper: it refers to the fruit companies from the United States that came to exert extraordinary influence over the politics of Honduras and its neighbors."[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-BananaRep-42] In addition to drawing Central American workers north, the fruit companies encouraged immigration of workers from the [English-speaking Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English-speaking_Caribbean), notably Jamaica and [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize), which introduced an African-descended, English-speaking and largely Protestant population into the country, although many of these workers left following changes to immigration law in 1939.[^cite_note-Chambers-47]

Constitutional crises in the 1940s led to reforms in the 1950s. One reform gave workers permission to organize, and a [1954 general strike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_strike_of_1954) paralyzed the northern part of the country for more than two months, but led to reforms. A [1963 military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Honduran_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) unseated democratically elected President [Ramón Villeda Morales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Villeda_Morales). In 1960, the northern part of what was the Mosquito Coast was transferred from Nicaragua to Honduras by the [International Court of Justice](14918.md).[^cite_note-auto-12]

### War and upheaval (1969–1999)

In 1969, Honduras and El Salvador fought what became known as the [Football War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_War).[^cite_note-football_war-48] Border tensions led to acrimony between the two countries after Honduran President [Oswaldo López Arellano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswaldo_L%C3%B3pez_Arellano) blamed the deteriorating economy on immigrants from El Salvador. The relationship reached a low when El Salvador met Honduras for a three-round football elimination match preliminary to the [World Cup](11370.md).[^cite_note-FW-49] On 14 July the Salvadoran army invaded Honduras.[^cite_note-football_war-48] The [Organization of American States](22788.md) negotiated a cease-fire which took effect on 20 July and brought about a withdrawal of Salvadoran troops in early August.[^cite_note-FW-49] Contributing factors to the conflict were a boundary dispute and the presence of thousands of Salvadorans living in Honduras illegally. After the week-long war, as many as 130,000 Salvadoran immigrants were expelled.[^cite_note-LOC-16]



Part of the massive damage caused by [Hurricane Mitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Mitch) in Tegucigalpa, 1998

[Hurricane Fifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Fifi%E2%80%93Orlene) caused severe damage when it skimmed the northern coast on 18 and 19 September 1974. Following Fifi, Presidents [Melgar Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Alberto_Melgar_Castro) and [Policarpo Paz García](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Policarpo_Paz_Garc%C3%ADa) largely built the current physical infrastructure and telecommunications system of Honduras.[^cite_note-DOS-Honduras-50] In 1979, the country returned to civilian rule. A [constituent assembly](266437.md) was popularly elected in April 1980 to write a new constitution, and general elections were held in November 1981. The constitution was approved in 1982 and the [PLH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Honduras) government of [Roberto Suazo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Suazo_C%C3%B3rdova) won the election with a promise to carry out an ambitious program of economic and social development to tackle an economic recession. He launched ambitious social and economic development projects sponsored by American development aid. Honduras became host to the largest [Peace Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_Corps) mission in the world, and nongovernmental and international voluntary agencies proliferated. The Peace Corps withdrew its volunteers in 2012, citing safety concerns.[^cite_note-CSM-51]

During the early 1980s, the United States established a continuing military presence in Honduras to support El Salvador, the [Contra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contras) guerrillas fighting the Nicaraguan government, and also develop an airstrip and modern port in Honduras. Though spared the bloody civil wars wracking its neighbors, the [Honduran Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_Army) quietly waged campaigns against [Marxist–Leninist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marxist%E2%80%93Leninist) militias such as the [Cinchoneros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Movement-Cinchoneros) Popular Liberation Movement, notorious for kidnappings and bombings,[^cite_note-52] and against many non-militants. The operation included a campaign of [extrajudicial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extrajudicial_punishment) killings by government units, most notably the CIA-trained [Battalion 316](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion_3-16_\(Honduras\)).[^cite_note-53] Honduras was found internationally liable for a series of [enforced disappearances](686148.md) during this time period, culminating in *[Velásquez Rodríguez v. Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vel%C3%A1squez_Rodr%C3%ADguez_v._Honduras)*.[^cite_note-54]

In 1998, [Hurricane Mitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Mitch) caused massive and widespread destruction. President [Carlos Roberto Flores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Roberto_Flores) said that 50 years of progress in the country had been reversed. Mitch destroyed about 70% of the crops and an estimated 70–80% of the transportation infrastructure, including nearly all bridges and secondary roads. Across Honduras 33,000 houses were destroyed, and an additional 50,000 damaged. Some 5,000 people killed, and 12,000 more injured. Total losses were estimated at US\$3 billion.[^cite_note-55]

### 21st century



President [Ricardo Maduro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Maduro) with U.S. Secretary of Defense [Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld) in August 2003

In 2007, President [Manuel Zelaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Zelaya) and President of the United States [George W. Bush](3414021.md) began talks on US assistance to Honduras to tackle the growing drug cartels in the Mosquito Coast region using US special forces. This provided a new foothold for the US military's continued presence in Central America.[^cite_note-56]



[2009 Honduran coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Honduran_coup_d'%C3%A9tat)

Under Zelaya, Honduras joined [ALBA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALBA) in 2008 but withdrew in 2010 after the [2009 Honduran coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Honduran_coup_d'%C3%A9tat).[^cite_note-americasquarterly.org-57] In 2009 a [constitutional crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Honduran_political_crisis) resulted when power was transferred in a coup from the president to the head of Congress. The [OAS](22788.md) suspended Honduras because it did not regard its government as legitimate.[^cite_note-OAS-58][^cite_note-New_Honduran_leader_sworn_in-59] Countries around the world, the OAS, and the [United Nations](31769.md)[^cite_note-UN-60] formally and unanimously condemned the action as a [coup d'état](25914154.md), refusing to recognize the *de facto* government, even though the lawyers consulted by the [Library of Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Library_of_Congress) submitted to the [United States Congress](31756.md) an opinion that declared the coup legal.[^cite_note-UN-60][^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] The [Honduran Supreme Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_Supreme_Court) also ruled that the proceedings had been legal. The government that followed the *de facto government* established a truth and reconciliation commission, *Comisión de la Verdad y Reconciliación*, which after more than a year of research and debate concluded that the ousting had been a coup d'état and therefore illegal in the commission's opinion.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65]

President [Juan Orlando Hernández](25895048.md) took office in 2014. After managing to stand for a second term,[^cite_note-66] a very close [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Honduran_general_election) in 2017 left uncertainty as to whether Hernandez or his main challenger, television personality [Salvador Nasralla](41103646.md), had prevailed.[^cite_note-AP-67] The disputed election caused protests and violence. Hernández was declared the winner of the election after a partial recount.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

The former first lady [Xiomara Castro](6016595.md), leftist presidential candidate of opposition [Liberty and Refoundation](36492435.md) Party, won 53% of the votes in the [2021 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Honduran_general_election) to become the first female president of Honduras, bringing an end to the 12-year reign of the right-wing National Party.[^cite_note-70] Her husband Manuel Zelaya held the same office from 2006 until 2009.[^cite_note-71] Castro's government re-nationalized the nation's telecommunications and energy industries, subsidized electricity, education and school meals for more than a million impoverished families, and extended financial aid to students and the elderly. The government made major investments in the construction of eight new hospitals and in the electricity grid. The government also focused on shutting down [special economic zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_economic_zone). The Castro government sought to diversify Honduras's agricultural production by incentivizing farmers to grow food in addition to [cash crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_crop) through low-interest loans, domestic production quotas, and investments in the agricultural sector. Castro's [Libre](36492435.md) party was not able to gain control of congress and was consequently unable to nationalize private land, leading to protests and dissatisfaction among the rural poor.[^cite_note-\:4-20]

In April 2022, former President [Juan Orlando Hernández](25895048.md) was extradited to the United States to face charges of drug trafficking and money laundering.[^cite_note-72] In 2025 Hernández was pardoned by the Trump administration. [Nasry Asfura](39721295.md) was declared the winner of the [2025 Honduran election](77491476.md), with Castro and both of Asfura's opponents, [Salvador Nasralla](41103646.md) and [Rixi Moncada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rixi_Moncada), alleging fraud. Roughly 14.5% of the electoral tally sheets contained inconsistencies and required a review.[^cite_note-\:19-21] On 27 January 2026, Nasry Asfura was sworn in as president.[^cite_note-73]

## Geography

Main article: [Geography of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Honduras)



Honduras's topography

The north coast of Honduras borders the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md), and the Pacific Ocean lies south through the [Gulf of Fonseca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Fonseca). Honduras consists mainly of mountains, with narrow plains along the coasts. A large undeveloped lowland jungle, *[La Mosquitia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Mosquitia_\(Honduras\)),* lies in the northeast and the heavily populated lowland Sula valley in the northwest.&#x20;

The [Islas de la Bahía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Islands_Department) and the [Swan Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swan_Islands,_Honduras) are off the north coast. [Misteriosa Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misteriosa_Bank) and [Rosario Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rosario_Bank), 130 to 150 kilometres (81 to 93 miles) north of the Swan Islands, fall within the [exclusive economic zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_Economic_Zone) of Honduras. Natural resources include timber, gold, silver, copper, lead, [zinc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zinc), iron ore, [antimony](898.md), coal, fish, shrimp, and [hydropower](14073.md).

### Climate

Main article: [Geography of Honduras § Climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Honduras#Climate)



[Köppen climate types](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification_system) of Honduras

The climate varies from tropical in the [lowlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lowland) to [temperate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate) in the mountains. The Pacific coast is generally drier than the Caribbean.

### Biodiversity

Main article: [Flora and Fauna of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora_and_Fauna_of_Honduras)

See also: [List of birds of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_birds_of_Honduras) and [List of national parks of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_parks_of_Honduras)

The region is considered a [biodiversity hotspot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biodiversity_hotspot) because of the many plant and animal species found there. Like other countries in the region, it contains vast biological resources, with more than 6,000 species of [vascular plants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vascular_plant), of which 630 (described so far) are [orchids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchid); around 250 [reptiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reptile) and [amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian); more than 700 bird species; and 110 [mammalian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammal) species, of which half are [bats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat).[^cite_note-74] In the northeastern region of La Mosquitia lies the [Río Plátano Biosphere Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%ADo_Pl%C3%A1tano_Biosphere_Reserve), a lowland rainforest which is home to a great diversity of life. The reserve became a UNESCO [World Heritage Site](44940.md) in 1982.

Honduras has rain forests, [cloud forests](58903.md) (which can rise up to nearly 3,000 metres or 9,800 feet [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level)), [mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove), [savanna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Savanna)s and mountain ranges with pine and oak trees, and the [Mesoamerican Barrier Reef System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoamerican_Barrier_Reef_System). In the [Bay Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Islands_\(department\)) there are [bottlenose dolphins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bottlenose_dolphin), [manta rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manta_ray), [parrot fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parrot_fish), schools of [blue tang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acanthurus_coeruleus) and [whale shark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whale_shark).

[Deforestation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deforestation) resulting from [logging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logging) is rampant in [Olancho Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olancho_Department). The clearing of land for agriculture is prevalent in the largely undeveloped La Mosquitia region, causing [land degradation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_degradation) and [soil erosion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soil_erosion). Honduras had a 2019 [Forest Landscape Integrity Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Landscape_Integrity_Index) mean score of 4.48/10, ranking it 126th globally out of 172 countries.[^cite_note-FLII-Supplementary-75] [Lake Yojoa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Yojoa), which is Honduras's largest source of fresh water, is polluted by [heavy metals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_metals) produced from mining activities.[^cite_note-76] Some rivers and streams are also polluted by mining.[^cite_note-77]

Honduras is governed within a framework of a [presidential](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system) [representative democratic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_democracy) [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic). The [President of Honduras](23420892.md) is both [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state) and [head of government](37570.md). [Executive power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_power) is exercised by the [Honduran government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Honduras). [Legislative power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_power) is vested in the [National Congress of Honduras](1604127.md). The [judiciary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judiciary) is independent of both the executive branch and the legislature.

The [National Congress of Honduras](1604127.md) (*Congreso Nacional*) has 128 members (*diputados*), elected for a four-year term by [proportional representation](23298.md). Congressional seats are assigned the parties' candidates on a [departmental basis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Departments_of_Honduras) in proportion to the number of votes each party receives.[^cite_note-cia-78]

### Political culture

Following the 1963 military coup, a string of authoritarian [military governments](216815.md) held power uninterrupted until 1981, when [Roberto Suazo Córdova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Suazo_C%C3%B3rdova) was elected president. The party system was dominated by the conservative [National Party of Honduras](1641714.md) (Partido Nacional de Honduras: PNH) and the liberal [Liberal Party of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Party_of_Honduras) (Partido Liberal de Honduras: PLH) until the 2009 Honduran coup d'état removed Zelaya from office and put [Roberto Micheletti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Micheletti) in his place.

In late 2012, 1,540 persons were interviewed by [ERIC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Progreso_&_ERIC-SJ) in collaboration with the Jesuit [university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_American_University_\(San_Salvador\)), as reported by [Associated Press](18935732.md). The survey found that 60% believed the police were involved in crime, 45% had "no confidence" in the Supreme Court, and 72% thought there was electoral fraud in the primary elections of November 2012. Also, 56% expected the presidential, legislative and municipal elections of 2013 to be fraudulent.[^cite_note-79]

### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Honduras)



Map of [Honduran diplomatic missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_of_Honduras)



Map of [diplomatic missions in Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_diplomatic_missions_in_Honduras)

Honduras and Nicaragua had tense relations throughout 2000 and early 2001 due to a boundary dispute off the Atlantic coast. Nicaragua imposed a 35% [tariff](55551.md) against Honduran goods due to the dispute.[^cite_note-80]

In June 2009 a coup d'état ousted President Zelaya; he was taken in a military aircraft to Costa Rica. The General Assembly of the United Nations voted to denounce the coup and called for the restoration of Zelaya. Several [Latin American](18524.md) nations, including Mexico, temporarily severed diplomatic relations with Honduras. In July 2010, full diplomatic relations were once again re-established with Mexico.[^cite_note-81] The United States sent out mixed messages after the coup; U.S. President Obama called the ouster a coup and expressed support for Zelaya's return to power. US Secretary of State [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton)—advised by [John Negroponte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Negroponte), the former Reagan-era ambassador to Honduras implicated in the [Iran–Contra affair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Contra_affair)—refrained from expressing support.[^cite_note-82] She later explained that the US would have had to cut aid if it called Zelaya's ouster a military coup, although the US has a record of ignoring these events when it chooses.[^cite_note-83] Zelaya had expressed an interest in Venezuelan president [Hugo Chávez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Ch%C3%A1vez)' [Bolivarian Alliance for Peoples of our America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ALBA) (ALBA) and had actually joined in 2008. After the 2009 coup, Honduras withdrew its membership.[^cite_note-americasquarterly.org-57]

This interest in regional agreements may have increased the alarm of establishment politicians. When Zelaya began calling for a "fourth ballot box" to determine whether Hondurans wished to convoke a special constitutional congress, this sounded a lot to some like the constitutional amendments that had extended the terms of both Chávez and Bolivian president [Evo Morales](284163.md). "Chávez has served as a role model for like-minded leaders intent on cementing their power. These presidents are barely in office when they typically convene a constitutional convention to guarantee their reelection," said a 2009 Spiegel International analysis,[^cite_note-spiegel-84] which noted that one reason to join ALBA was discounted Venezuelan oil. In addition to Chávez and Morales, Argentinian presiden [Carlos Menem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Menem), Brazilian president [Fernando Henrique Cardoso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Henrique_Cardoso), and Colombian President [Álvaro Uribe](243680.md) had all taken this step, and Washington and the EU were both accusing the [Sandinista National Liberation Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandinista_National_Liberation_Front) government in Nicaragua of tampering with election results.[^cite_note-spiegel-84] Politicians of all stripes expressed opposition to Zelaya's referendum proposal, and the attorney-general accused him of violating the constitution. The Honduran Supreme Court agreed, saying that the constitution had put the [Supreme Electoral Tribunal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Electoral_Tribunal_\(Honduras\)) in charge of elections and referendums, not the National Statistics Institute, which Zelaya had proposed to have run the count.[^cite_note-crs-85] Whether or not Zelaya's removal from power had constitutional elements, the Honduran constitution explicitly protects all Hondurans from forced expulsion from Honduras.

The United States maintains a small military presence at one Honduran base. The two countries conduct joint military exercises in peacekeeping, counter-narcotics, humanitarian, disaster relief, as well as humanitarian, medical and civic action. U.S. troops conduct and provide logistics support for a variety of bilateral and multilateral exercises. The United States is Honduras's chief trading partner.[^cite_note-DOS-Honduras-50]

Honduras has been a member of [The Forum of Small States (FOSS)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Forum_of_Small_States_\(FOSS\)) since the group's founding in 1992.[^cite_note-singaporebook-86]

## Economy

This section is an excerpt from [Economy of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Honduras).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Economy_of_Honduras\&action=edit)]

The [economy of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Honduras) is based mostly on [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), which accounts for 14% of its [gross domestic product](12594.md) (GDP) in 2013. The country's leading export is [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) (US\$340 million), which accounted for 22% of the total Honduran export revenues. Bananas, formerly the country's second-largest export until being virtually wiped out by 1998's [Hurricane Mitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Mitch), recovered in 2000 to 57% of pre-Mitch levels. Cultivated [shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrimp) is another important export sector. Since the late 1970s, towns in the north began industrial production through [maquiladoras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquiladora), especially in [San Pedro Sula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_Sula) and [Puerto Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Cort%C3%A9s).[^cite_note-88]

Honduras has extensive forests, marine, and mineral [resources](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resource_\(economics\)), although widespread [slash and burn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slash_and_burn) agricultural methods continue to destroy Honduran forests. The Honduran economy grew 4.8% in 2000, recovering from the Mitch-induced recession (−1.9%) of 1999. The Honduran [maquiladora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maquiladora) sector, the third-largest in the world, continued its strong performance in 2000, providing employment to over 120,000 and generating more than \$528  million in foreign exchange for the country. [Remittances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remittance) from Hondurans living abroad (mostly in the United States) rose 28% to \$410 million in 2000. As of 2026, remittances from Hondurans living in the United States accounted for more than a quarter of the country's GDP.[^cite_note-89] [Inflation](38286.md), as measured by the [consumer price index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_price_index), was 10.1% in 2000, down slightly from the 10.9% recorded in 1999. The country's international reserve position continued to be strong in 2000, at slightly over US\$1 billion. The Lempira (currency) was [devaluing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devaluation) for many years, but stabilized at L19 to the [United States dollar](18717338.md) in 2005. The Honduran people are among the poorest in [Latin America](18524.md); [gross national income](1242956.md) per capita (2007) is US\$1,649; the average for Central America is \$6,736.[^cite_note-90] Honduras is the fourth poorest country in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md); only [Haiti](13373.md), [Nicaragua](21362.md), and [Guyana](17238691.md) are poorer. Using [alternative](12594.md#Limitations_of_GDP_to_judge_the_health_of_an_economy) statistical measurements in addition to the gross domestic product can provide greater context for the nation's poverty.

The country signed an [Enhanced Structural Adjustment Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enhanced_Structural_Adjustment_Facility) (ESAF) – later converted to a [Poverty Reduction and Growth Facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_Reduction_and_Growth_Facility) (PRGF) with the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) in March 1999. Honduras (as of the about year 2000) continues to maintain stable [macroeconomic policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macroeconomic_policies). It has not been swift in implementing structural changes, such as privatization of the publicly owned telephone and energy distribution companies—changes which are desired by the IMF and other international lenders. Honduras received significant debt relief in the aftermath of [Hurricane Mitch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Mitch), including the suspension of bilateral debt service payments and bilateral debt reduction by the [Paris Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Club)—including the United States – worth over \$400  million. In July 2000, Honduras reached its decision point under the [Heavily Indebted Poor Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavily_Indebted_Poor_Countries) Initiative (HIPC), qualifying the country for interim multilateral debt relief.

Land appears to be plentiful and readily exploitable, but the presence of apparently extensive land is misleading because the nation's rugged, mountainous terrain restricts large-scale agricultural production to narrow strips on the coasts and to a few fertile valleys. Honduras's manufacturing sector has not yet developed beyond simple textile and agricultural processing industries and assembly operations. The small domestic market and competition from more industrially advanced countries in the region have inhibited more complex [industrialization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrialization).

In 2022, according to the National Institute of Statistics of Honduras (INE), 73% of the country's population is poor and 53% lives in extreme poverty.[^cite_note-91] The country is among the most unequal in the world.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93]

### Poverty

The [World Bank](36752.md) categorizes Honduras as a low middle-income nation.[^cite_note-\:0-94] The per capita income is around US\$4,000, making it one of the lowest in North America and Central America.[^cite_note-IMFWEO.HN-5][^cite_note-\:2-95] In 2016, more than 66% of the population was living below the [poverty line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_line).[^cite_note-\:0-94]

Economic growth has averaged 7% per year, one of the highest rates in Latin America as of 2010.[^cite_note-\:0-94] Despite this, Honduras has seen the least development amongst all Central American countries.[^cite_note-96] Honduras is ranked 130 of 188 countries with a [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) of .625 that classifies the nation as having medium development (2015).[^cite_note-\:1-14] The three factors that go into HDI (an extended and healthy life, accessibility of knowledge and standard of living) have all improved since 1990 but still remain relatively low with life expectancy at birth being 73.3, expected years of schooling being 11.2 (mean of 6.2 years) and [GNI per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GNP_per_capita) being \$4,466 (2015).[^cite_note-\:1-14] The HDI for Latin America and the Caribbean overall is 0.751 with life expectancy at birth being 68.6, expected years of schooling being 11.5 (mean of 6.6) and GNI per capita being \$6,281 (2015).[^cite_note-\:1-14]

The 2009 Honduran coup d'état led to a variety of economic trends in the nation.[^cite_note-elcorreo.eu.org-97] Overall growth averaged 5.7 percent from 2006 to 2008 but slowed to 3.5 percent annually between 2010 and 2013.[^cite_note-elcorreo.eu.org-97] The nation saw a poverty increase of 13.2 percent and in extreme poverty of 26.3 percent in just 3 years.[^cite_note-elcorreo.eu.org-97] Furthermore, unemployment grew between 2008 and 2012 from 6.8 percent to 14.1 percent.[^cite_note-elcorreo.eu.org-97]

Because much of the economy is based on small scale agriculture of only a few exports, natural disasters have a particularly devastating impact. Natural disasters, such as 1998 Hurricane Mitch, have contributed to this inequality as they particularly affect poor rural areas.[^cite_note-worldbank.org-98] Additionally, they are a large contributor to food insecurity in the country as farmers are left unable to provide for their families.[^cite_note-worldbank.org-98] A study done by Honduras NGO, World Neighbors, determined the terms "increased workload, decreased basic grains, expensive food, and fear" were most associated with Hurricane Mitch.[^cite_note-99]

The rural and urban poor were hit hardest by Hurricane Mitch.[^cite_note-worldbank.org-98] Those in southern and western regions specifically were considered most vulnerable as they both were subject to environmental destruction and home to many subsistence farmers.[^cite_note-worldbank.org-98] The agricultural economic sector has declined a third in the past 20 years,[^cite_note-worldbank.org-98] mostly representing a decline in exports, such as bananas and coffee, that were affected by natural disasters.[^cite_note-worldbank.org-98] Indigenous communities along the [Patuca River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patuca_River) were hit hard as well.[^cite_note-\:9-13] The mid-Pataca region was almost completely destroyed.[^cite_note-\:9-13] Over 80% of rice harvest and all of banana, plantain, and manioc harvests were lost.[^cite_note-\:9-13] Relief and reconstruction efforts following the storm were partial and incomplete, reinforcing existing levels of poverty rather than reversing those levels, especially for indigenous communities.[^cite_note-\:9-13] The period between the end of food donations and the following harvest led to extreme hunger, causing deaths amongst the Tawahka population.[^cite_note-\:9-13] Those that were considered the most "land-rich" lost 36% of their total land on average.[^cite_note-\:9-13] Those that were the most "land-poor", lost less total land but a greater share of their overall total.[^cite_note-\:9-13] This meant that those hit hardest were single women as they constitute the majority of this population.[^cite_note-\:9-13]

### Poverty reduction strategies

Since the 1970s when Honduras was designated a "food priority country" by the UN, organizations such as [World Food Programme](33582.md) (WFP) have worked to decrease malnutrition and food insecurity.[^cite_note-www1.wfp.org-100] Currently one fourth of children are affected by chronic malnutrition.[^cite_note-www1.wfp.org-100] WFP is currently working with the Honduran government on a school feeding program which provides meals for 21,000 schools, reaching 1.4 million school children.[^cite_note-www1.wfp.org-100] WFP also participates in disaster relief through reparations and emergency response in order to aid in quick recovery that tackles the effects of natural disasters on agricultural production.[^cite_note-www1.wfp.org-100]

The [Poverty Reduction Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_Reduction_Strategy_in_Honduras) was implemented in 1999 and aimed to cut extreme poverty in half by 2015.[^cite_note-econstor.eu-101] While spending on poverty-reduction aid increased, there was only a 2.5% increase in GDP between 1999 and 2002.[^cite_note-\:10-102] This improvement left Honduras still below that of countries that lacked aid through Poverty Reduction Strategy behind those without it.[^cite_note-\:10-102] The World Bank believes that this inefficiency stems from a lack of focus on infrastructure and rural development.[^cite_note-\:10-102] Extreme poverty saw a low of 36.2 percent only two years after the implementation of the strategy but then increased to 66.5 percent by 2012.[^cite_note-elcorreo.eu.org-97]

Poverty Reduction Strategies were intended to affect social policy through increased investment in education and health sectors.[^cite_note-\:11-103] This was expected to lift poor communities out of poverty while also increasing the workforce as a means of stimulating the Honduran economy.[^cite_note-\:11-103] Conditional cash transfers were used to do this by the Family Assistance Program.[^cite_note-\:11-103] This program was restructured in 1998 in an attempt to increase effectiveness of cash transfers for health and education specifically for those in extreme poverty.[^cite_note-\:11-103] Overall spending within Poverty Reduction Strategies have been focused on education and health sectors increasing social spending from 44% of Honduras's GDP in 2000 to 51% in 2004.[^cite_note-\:11-103]

Critics of aid from International Finance Institutions believe that the World Bank's Poverty Reduction Strategy result in little substantive change to Honduran policy.[^cite_note-\:11-103] Poverty Reduction Strategies also excluded clear priorities, specific intervention strategy, strong commitment to the strategy and more effective macro-level economic reforms according to Jose Cuesta of Cambridge University.[^cite_note-\:10-102] Due to this he believes that the strategy did not provide a pathway for economic development that could lift Honduras out of poverty resulting in neither lasting economic growth of poverty reduction.[^cite_note-\:10-102]

Prior to its 2009 coup Honduras widely expanded social spending and an extreme increase in minimum wage.[^cite_note-elcorreo.eu.org-97] Efforts to decrease inequality were swiftly reversed following the coup.[^cite_note-elcorreo.eu.org-97] When Zelaya was removed from office social spending as a percent of GDP decreased from 13.3 percent in 2009 to 10.9 recent in 2012.[^cite_note-elcorreo.eu.org-97] This decrease in social spending exacerbated the effects of the recession, which the nation was previously relatively well equipped to deal with.[^cite_note-elcorreo.eu.org-97]

### Economic inequality



[Slum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slum) in Tegucigalpa

Levels of income inequality are higher than in any other Latin American country.[^cite_note-elcorreo.eu.org-97] Unlike other Latin American countries, inequality steadily increased between 1991 and 2005.[^cite_note-econstor.eu-101] Between 2006 and 2010 inequality saw a decrease but increased again in 2010.[^cite_note-elcorreo.eu.org-97]

When the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) is adjusted for inequality (known as the IHDI) Honduras's development index is reduced to .443.[^cite_note-\:1-14] The levels of inequality in each aspect of development can also be assessed.[^cite_note-\:1-14] In 2015 inequality of life expectancy at birth was 19.6%, inequality in education was 24.4% and inequality in income was 41.5% [^cite_note-\:1-14] The overall loss in human development due to inequality was 29.2.[^cite_note-\:1-14]

The IHDI for Latin America and the [Caribbean](18956035.md) overall is 0.575 with an overall loss of 23.4%.[^cite_note-\:1-14] In 2015 inequality of life expectancy at birth was 22.9%, inequality in education was 14.0%, and inequality in income was 34.9%.[^cite_note-\:1-14] While Honduras has a higher life expectancy (before and after inequality adjustments), its quality of education and economic standard of living are lower.[^cite_note-\:1-14] Income inequality and education inequality have a large impact on the overall development of the nation.[^cite_note-\:1-14]

Inequality also exists between rural and urban areas as it relates to the distribution of resources.[^cite_note-104] Poverty is concentrated in southern, eastern, and western regions where rural and indigenous peoples live. North and central Honduras are home to the country's industries and infrastructure, resulting in lower levels of poverty.[^cite_note-\:2-95] Poverty is concentrated in rural Honduras, a pattern that is reflected throughout Latin America.[^cite_note-cia.gov-18] The effects of poverty on rural communities are vast. Poor communities typically live in [adobe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adobe) homes, lack material resources, have limited access to medical resources, and live off of basics such as rice, maize and beans.[^cite_note-105]

The lower class predominantly consists of rural subsistence farmers and landless peasants.[^cite_note-\:3-106] Since 1965 there has been an increase in the number of landless peasants in Honduras which has led to a growing class of urban poor individuals.[^cite_note-\:3-106] These individuals often migrate to urban centers in search of work in the service sector, manufacturing, or construction.[^cite_note-\:3-106] Demographers believe that without social and economic reform, rural to urban migration will increase, resulting in the expansion of urban centers.[^cite_note-\:3-106] Within the lower class, underemployment is a major issue.[^cite_note-\:3-106] Individuals that are underemployed often only work as part-time laborers on seasonal farms meaning their annual income remains low.[^cite_note-\:3-106] In the 1980s peasant organizations and labor unions such as the National Federation of Honduran Peasants, The National Association of Honduran Peasants and the National Union of Peasants formed.[^cite_note-\:3-106]



An indigenous family in a small mountain village in Honduras

It is not uncommon for rural individuals to voluntarily enlist in the military, however this often does not offer stable or promising career opportunities.[^cite_note-\:8-107] The majority of high-ranking officials in the army are recruited from military academies.[^cite_note-\:8-107] Additionally, the majority of enlistment in the military is forced,[^cite_note-\:8-107] which largely relies on an alliance between the government, military and the upper class.[^cite_note-\:8-107] In urban areas males are often sought out from secondary schools while in rural areas roadblocks aided the military in handpicking recruits.[^cite_note-\:8-107] Higher socio-economic status enables individuals to more easily evade the draft.[^cite_note-\:8-107]

The middle class is a small group defined by relatively low income levels.[^cite_note-\:3-106] Movement from lower to middle class is typically facilitated by higher education.[^cite_note-\:3-106] Professionals, students, farmers, merchants, business employees, and civil servants are all considered a part of the middle class.[^cite_note-\:3-106] Opportunities for employment and the industrial and commercial sectors are slow-growing, limiting the middle class.[^cite_note-\:3-106] The upper class has much higher income levels, reflecting large amounts of income inequality.[^cite_note-\:3-106] Much of the upper class affords their success to the growth of cotton and livestock exports post-[World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-\:3-106] The wealthy are not politically unified and differ in political and economic views.[^cite_note-\:3-106]

### Trade



[San Pedro Sula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_Sula) is a major center of business and commerce in Honduras, and is home to many large manufacturers and companies. It is often referred to as "La Capital Industrial".

[^cite_note-108]



CAFTA countries

The currency is the [Honduran lempira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_lempira). In 2005 Honduras signed [CAFTA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_Republic%E2%80%93Central_America_Free_Trade_Agreement), a free trade agreement with the United States. In December 2005, [Puerto Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Cort%C3%A9s), the primary seaport of Honduras, was included in the U.S. [Container Security Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Container_Security_Initiative).[^cite_note-109] Gold, silver, lead and zinc are mined.[^cite_note-110]

The government operates both the electrical grid, [Empresa Nacional de Energía Eléctrica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empresa_Nacional_de_Energ%C3%ADa_El%C3%A9ctrica) (ENEE) and the land-line telephone service, [Hondutel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hondutel). ENEE receives heavy subsidies to counter its chronic financial problems, but Hondutel is no longer a monopoly. The telecommunication sector was opened to private investment in 2005, as required under CAFTA. The price of petroleum is regulated, and the [Congress](1604127.md) often ratifies temporary price regulation for basic commodities.

In 2006 the [U.S. Department of Homeland Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Homeland_Security) and the [Department of Energy](62866.md) announced the first phase of the [Secure Freight Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Freight_Initiative)), which built upon existing port security measures, gives the U.S. government enhanced authority, allowing it to scan containers from overseas for nuclear and radiological materials in order to improve the risk assessment of individual US-bound containers. The Secure Freight Initiative is in place in Puerto Cortés.[^cite_note-111]

For economic development a 2012 memorandum of understanding with a group of international investors obtained Honduran government approval to build a zone (city) with its own laws, tax system, judiciary and police, but opponents brought a suit against it in the Supreme Court, calling it a "state within a state".[^cite_note-112] In 2013, Congress ratified Decree 120, which led to the establishment of [Zone for Employment and Economic Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zone_for_Employment_and_Economic_Development). The government began construction of the first zones in June 2015.[^cite_note-113]

### Energy

Further information: [Electricity sector in Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electricity_sector_in_Honduras)



Electricity generation in Honduras in terawatt-hours

About half of the electricity sector in Honduras is privately owned. The remaining generation capacity is run by ENEE (*Empresa Nacional de Energía Eléctrica*). Key challenges in the sector are:

- Financing investments in generation and transmission without either a financially healthy utility or concessionary funds from external donors

- Re-balancing [tariffs](55551.md), cutting [arrears](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrears) and reducing losses, including electricity theft, without social unrest

- Reconciling environmental concerns with government objectives – two large new dams and associated hydropower plants.

- Improving access to electricity in rural areas.

A highway in Honduras

Infrastructure for [transport in Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Honduras) consists of: 699 kilometres (434 miles) [of railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Honduras); 13,603 kilometres (8,453 miles) of roadways;[^cite_note-cia-78] six ports;[^cite_note-114] and 112 airports altogether (12 Paved, 100 unpaved).[^cite_note-cia-78] The Ministry of Public Works, Transport and Housing is responsible for transport sector policy.

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Honduras)

Honduras had a population of 10,278,345 in 2021.[^cite_note-UN_WPP-22][^cite_note-UN_WPP_2022-23] The proportion of the population below age 15 in 2010 was 36.8%; 58.9% were between 15 and 65 years old, and 4.3% were 65 years old or older.[^cite_note-WPP_2012-115]

Since 1975, emigration from Honduras has accelerated as economic migrants and political refugees sought a better life elsewhere. A majority of [expatriate Hondurans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_diaspora) live in the United States. A 2012 US State Department estimate suggested that between 800,000 and one million Hondurans lived in the United States at that time, nearly 15% of the Honduran population.[^cite_note-DOS-Honduras-50] The large uncertainty about numbers is because numerous Hondurans live in the United States without a visa. In the 2010 census in the United States, 617,392 residents identified as Hondurans, up from 217,569 in 2000.[^cite_note-116]

### Race and ethnicity

Racial groups in Honduras (2013 census)

[^cite_note-human_rights-1]

1. [Mestizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizos) (82.9%)

2. [Whites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Latin_Americans) (7.87%)

3. [Indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_peoples_of_Honduras) (7.25%)

4. [Blacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Hondurans) (1.39%)

5. Others (0.24%)

According to the 2013 census, the ethnic breakdown was 83% [mestizo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestizo), 8% white, 7% indigenous (primarily [Lenca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenca)), and 2% black.[^cite_note-human_rights-1] The 1927 Honduran census provides no racial data, but in 1930 five classifications were created: white, Indian, Negro, yellow, and mestizo.[^cite_note-Classifications-118] This system was used in the 1935 and 1940 census.[^cite_note-Classifications-118] Mestizo was used to describe individuals that did not fit neatly into the categories of white, American Indian, negro or yellow or who are of mixed white-American Indian descent.[^cite_note-Classifications-118]

John Gillin considers Honduras to be one of 13 "Mestizo countries" (Mexico, Guatemala, El Salvador, Nicaragua, Panama, Colombia, Venezuela, Cuba, Ecuador, Peru, Bolivia, Paraguay).[^cite_note-\:5-119] He claims that in much of Spanish America little attention is paid to race and race mixture resulting in social status having little reliance on one's physical features.[^cite_note-\:5-119] However, in "Mestizo countries," this is not the case.[^cite_note-\:5-119] Social stratification from Spain was able to develop in these countries through colonization.[^cite_note-\:5-119]

During colonization the majority of the indigenous population died of diseases like [smallpox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smallpox) and [measles](58911.md) resulting in a more homogenous indigenous population compared to other colonies.[^cite_note-\:3-106] Nine indigenous and African groups are recognized by the government.[^cite_note-\:6-120] The majority of Amerindians are [Lenca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenca_people), followed by the Miskito, Cho'rti', Tolupan, Pech and Sumo.[^cite_note-\:6-120] Around 50,000 Lenca individuals live in the west and western interior, while the other small native groups are located throughout the country.[^cite_note-\:3-106]

The majority of blacks are [ladino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladino_people), meaning they are culturally Latino.[^cite_note-\:3-106] Non-ladino groups include the [Garifuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garifuna), [Miskito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miskito_people), Bay Island Creoles, and Arab immigrants.[^cite_note-\:3-106] The Garifunas descended from freed slaves on [Saint Vincent and the Grenadines](27228.md).[^cite_note-\:3-106] The Bay Island Creoles are the descendants of freed African slaves from the British Empire, which administered the Bay Islands from early 17th century to 1850. The Creoles, the Garinagu, and the Miskitos are racially diverse.[^cite_note-\:3-106] While the Garinagu and Miskitos have similar origins, Garifunas are considered black while Miskitos are considered indigenous.[^cite_note-\:3-106] This is largely a reflection of cultural differences, as Garinagu have retained much of their original African culture.[^cite_note-\:3-106] The majority of Arab Hondurans are of Palestinian and Lebanese descent.[^cite_note-\:3-106] They are known as "turcos" because of migration during the rule of the Ottoman Empire.[^cite_note-\:3-106] They have maintained cultural distinctiveness and prospered economically.[^cite_note-\:3-106]

### Gender

See also: [Gender inequality in Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_inequality_in_Honduras) and [Health in Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Honduras)

The male to female ratio is 1.01. This ratio stands at 1.05 at birth, 1.04 from 15 to 24 years old, 1.02 from 25 to 54 years old, .88 from 55 to 64 years old, and .77 for those 65 years or older.[^cite_note-cia.gov-18] [The Gender Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_Development_Index) (GDI) was .942 in 2015 with an HDI of .600 for females and .637 for males.[^cite_note-\:1-14] Life expectancy at birth for males is 70.9 and 75.9 for females.[^cite_note-\:1-14] Expected years of schooling is 10.9 years for males (mean of 6.1) and 11.6 for females (mean of 6.2).[^cite_note-\:1-14] These measures do not reveal a large disparity between male and female development levels, however, GNI per capita is vastly different by gender.[^cite_note-\:1-14] Males have a GNI per capita of \$6,254 while that of females is only \$2,680.[^cite_note-\:1-14] Honduras's overall GDI is higher than that of other medium HDI nations (.871) but lower than the overall HDI for Latin America and the Caribbean (.981).[^cite_note-\:1-14]

The [United Nations Development Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme) ranks Honduras 116th for measures including women's political power, and female access to resources.[^cite_note-\:15-121] The [Gender Inequality Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_Inequality_Index) (GII)depicts gender-based inequalities according to reproductive health, empowerment, and economic activity.[^cite_note-\:1-14] Honduras has a GII of .461 and ranked 101 of 159 countries in 2015.[^cite_note-\:1-14] 25.8% of parliament is female, and 33.4% of adult females have a secondary education or higher while 31.1% of adult males do.[^cite_note-\:1-14] Despite this, while male participation in the labor market is 84.4, female participation is 47.2%.[^cite_note-\:1-14] The [maternal mortality ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_mortality_ratio) is 129, and the adolescent birth rate is 65.0 for women ages 15–19.[^cite_note-\:1-14]

[Familialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Familialism) and [machismo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machismo) carry a lot of weight within society.[^cite_note-\:14-122] Familialism refers to the idea of individual interests being second to that of the family, most often in relation to dating and marriage, abstinence, and parental approval and supervision of dating.[^cite_note-\:14-122] Aggression and proof of masculinity through physical dominance are characteristic of machismo.[^cite_note-\:14-122] Honduras has historically functioned with a patriarchal system like many other Latin American countries.[^cite_note-\:12-123] Men claim responsibility for family decisions including reproductive health decisions.[^cite_note-\:12-123] Recently Honduras has seen an increase in challenges to this notion as feminist movements and access to global media increases.[^cite_note-\:12-123] There has been an increase in educational attainment, labor force participating, urban migration, late-age marriage, and contraceptive use amongst women.[^cite_note-\:12-123]

Between 1971 and 2001 total fertility rate decreased from 7.4 births to 4.4 births.[^cite_note-\:12-123] This is largely attributable to an increase in educational attainment and workforce participation by women, as well as more widespread use of [contraceptives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contraceptives).[^cite_note-\:12-123] In 1996 50% of women were using at least one type of contraceptive.[^cite_note-\:12-123] By 2001 62% were largely due to [female sterilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Female_sterilization), birth control in the form of a pill, injectable birth control, and IUDs.[^cite_note-\:12-123] A study done in 2001 reflects conceptualization of reproductive health and decision making in Honduras;[^cite_note-\:12-123] 28% of men and 25% of women surveyed believed men were responsible for decisions regarding family size and family planning uses;[^cite_note-\:12-123] 21% of men believed men were responsible for both.[^cite_note-\:12-123]

Sexual violence against women has proven to be a large issue that has caused many to migrate to the U.S.[^cite_note-\:16-124] The prevalence of [child sexual abuse](18721790.md) was 7.8% with the majority of reports being from children under age 11.[^cite_note-\:18-125] Women that experienced sexual abuse as children were found to be twice as likely to be in violent relationships.[^cite_note-\:18-125] [Femicide](1686164.md) is widespread.[^cite_note-\:16-124] In 2014, 40% of unaccompanied refugee minors were female.[^cite_note-\:16-124] Gangs are largely responsible for sexual violence against women as they often use sexual violence.[^cite_note-\:16-124] Between 2005 and 2013 according to the UN Special Repporteur on Violence Against Women, violent deaths increased 263.4 percent.[^cite_note-\:16-124] Impunity for sexual violence and femicide crimes was 95 percent in 2014.[^cite_note-\:16-124] Additionally, many girls are forced into human trafficking and prostitution.[^cite_note-\:16-124]

Between 1995 and 1997 Honduras recognized domestic violence as both a public health issue and a punishable offense due to efforts by the [Pan American Health Organization](1246706.md) (PAHO).[^cite_note-\:15-121] PAHO's subcommittee on Women, Health and Development was used as a guide to develop programs that aid in domestic violence prevention and victim assistance programs.[^cite_note-\:15-121] However, a study done in 2009 showed that while the policy requires health care providers to report cases of sexual violence, emergency contraception, and victim referral to legal institutions and support groups, very few other regulations exist within the realm of registry, examination and follow-up.[^cite_note-\:17-126] Unlike other Central American countries, Honduras does not have detailed guidelines requiring service providers to be extensively trained and respect the rights of sexual violence victims.[^cite_note-\:17-126] Since the study was done the UNFPA and the Health Secretariat of Honduras have worked to develop and implement improved guidelines for handling cases of sexual violence.[^cite_note-\:17-126]

An educational program known as *Sistema de Aprendizaje Tutorial* (SAT) has attempted to "undo gender" through focusing on gender equality in everyday interactions.[^cite_note-\:13-127] The SAT program is one of the largest in the world, second only to Colombia's with 6,000 students.[^cite_note-\:13-127] It is currently sponsored by *Asociacion Bayan*, a Honduran NGO, and the Ministry of Education.[^cite_note-\:13-127] It functions by integrating gender into curriculum topics, linking gender to the ideas of justice and equality, encouraging reflection, dialogue and debate and emphasizing the need for individual and social change.[^cite_note-\:13-127] This program was found to increase gender consciousness and a desire for gender equality amongst Honduran women through encouraging discourse surrounding existing gender inequality in the communities.[^cite_note-\:13-127]

### Languages

[Spanish](26825.md) is the official, national language, spoken by virtually all [Hondurans](14387143.md). In addition to Spanish, numerous indigenous languages are spoken in some small communities. Other languages spoken by some include Honduran sign language and [Bay Islands Creole English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bay_Islands_Creole_English).[^cite_note-Ethnologue-128]

The main indigenous languages are:

- [Garifuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garifuna_language) ([Arawakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arawakan_languages)) (almost 100,000 speakers in Honduras including monolinguals)

- [Mískito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%ADskito_language) ([Misumalpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misumalpan_languages)) (29,000 speakers in Honduras)

- [Mayangna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sumu_language) ([Misumalpan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misumalpan_languages)) (less than 1000 speakers in Honduras, more in [Nicaragua](21362.md))

- [Pech/Paya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pech_language), ([Chibchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibchan_languages)) (less than 1000 speakers)

- [Tol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jicaque_language) ([Jicaquean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jicaquean_languages)) (less than 500 speakers)

- [Ch'orti'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch'orti'_language) ([Mayan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayan_languages)) (less than 50 speakers)

The [Lenca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lenca_language) isolate lost all its fluent native speakers in the 20th century but is currently undergoing revival efforts among the members of the ethnic population of about 100,000. The largest immigrant languages are Arabic (42,000), Armenian (1,300), Turkish (900), Yue Chinese (1,000).[^cite_note-Ethnologue-128]

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Honduras)

Religions in Honduras:

[^cite_note-CIA_Factbook-129]

1. [Protestantism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) (55.0%)

2. [Catholicism](606848.md) (34.0%)

Historically, Honduras had been predominantly [Catholic](606848.md). Protestant Christianity is now the largest religion in Honduras. The 2025 [CIA Factbook](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA_Factbook) lists Honduras as 55% Protestant and 34% Catholic.[^cite_note-cia-78] The 2008 International Religious Freedom Report notes that 51.4% of the population identified themselves as Catholic, 36.2% as [evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical) [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant), 1.3% claiming to be from other religions, including [Muslims](19541.md), [Buddhists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhists), [Jews](25955086.md), [Rastafarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastafari), etc. and 11.1% do not belong to any religion or unresponsive. 8% reported as being either atheistic or agnostic. Previous customary Catholic church tallies of membership, where priests (in more than 185 parishes) filled out a pastoral account of their parishes each year, estimated 81% Catholic.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131]

Commenting on statistical variations everywhere, John Green of Pew Forum on Religion and Public Life notes "It isn't that ... numbers are more right than \[someone else's] numbers ... but how one conceptualizes the group."[^cite_note-Dart-132] Often people attend one church without giving up their "home" church. Many who attend evangelical megachurches in the US, for example, attend more than one church.[^cite_note-133] This shifting and fluidity is common in Brazil where two-fifths of those who were raised evangelical are no longer evangelical and Catholics seem to shift in and out of various churches, often while still remaining Catholic.[^cite_note-134]

Most pollsters suggest an annual poll taken over several years would provide the best method of knowing religious demographics and variations in any single country. Still, in Honduras are thriving [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican), [Presbyterian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presbyterian), [Methodist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodist), [Seventh-day Adventist](28632.md), [Lutheran](23371382.md), [Latter-day Saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints) ([Mormon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mormonism)) and [Pentecostal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pentecostal) churches. There are Protestant seminaries. The Catholic Church maintains a presence through schools, hospitals, and pastoral institutions (including its own medical school) that it operates. Its [archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop), Cardinal [Óscar Andrés Rodriguez Maradiaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%93scar_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Maradiaga), is also very popular with the government, other churches, and in his own church. Practitioners of the [Buddhist](3267529.md), Jewish, Islamic, [Baháʼí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bah%C3%A1%CA%BC%C3%AD_Faith), [Rastafari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastafari) and indigenous denominations and religions exist.[^cite_note-135]

### Education

Main article: [Education in Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Honduras)

About 83.6% of the population are literate, and the net primary enrollment rate was 94% in 2004.[^cite_note-hdrstats.undp.org-136] In 2014, the primary school *completion* rate was 90.7%.[^cite_note-137] Honduras has bilingual (Spanish and English) and even trilingual (Spanish with English, Arabic, or German) [schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Honduras) and [numerous universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Honduras).[^cite_note-La_Prensa-138]

The higher education is governed by the [National Autonomous University of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Autonomous_University_of_Honduras) which has centers in the large cities. Honduras was ranked 119th in the [Global Innovation Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Innovation_Index) in 2025.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-140]

### Crime

[Crime in Honduras](24904228.md) is rampant, and criminals operate with a high degree of impunity. Honduras has one of the highest national murder rates in the world; cities such as San Pedro Sula and the Tegucigalpa have registered homicide rates among the highest in the world. The violence is associated with [drug trafficking](218449.md) as Honduras is often a transit point, and with several urban gangs, mainly the [MS-13](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS-13) and the [18th Street gang](293711.md). Homicide violence reached a peak in 2012 with an average of 20 homicides per day.[^cite_note-141] Official statistics from the Honduran Observatory on National Violence show the homicide rate was 60 per 100,000 in 2015 with the majority of homicide cases unprosecuted.[^cite_note-Travel_Warnings-142] But as recently as 2017, organizations such as InSight Crime's show figures of 42 per 100,000 inhabitants,[^cite_note-143] a 26% drop from 2016 figures.

Highway assaults and carjackings at roadblocks or checkpoints set up by criminals with police uniforms and equipment occur frequently. Although reports of kidnappings of foreigners are not common, families of kidnapping victims often pay ransoms without reporting the crime to police out of fear of retribution, so kidnapping figures may be underreported.[^cite_note-Travel_Warnings-142]

Violence increased after [Plan Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plan_Colombia) was implemented and after Mexican President [Felipe Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felipe_Calder%C3%B3n) declared the war against drug trafficking in Mexico.[^cite_note-144] Along with El Salvador and Guatemala, Honduras forms part of the [Northern Triangle of Central America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Triangle_of_Central_America), which has been characterized as one of the most violent regions in the world.[^cite_note-145] As a result of crime and increasing murder rates, the flow of migrants from Honduras to the U.S. also went up. The rise in violence in the region has received international attention.

[Roatán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roat%C3%A1n) and the other Bay Islands have lower crime rates than the mainland. This has been attributed to measures taken by government and business in 2014 to improve tourist safety.[^cite_note-Travel_Warnings-142] In the less populated region of [Gracias a Dios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gracias_a_Dios_Department), narcotics-trafficking is rampant and police presence is scarce. Threats against U.S. citizens by drug traffickers and other criminal organizations have resulted in the U.S. Embassy placing restrictions on the travel of U.S. officials through the region.[^cite_note-Travel_Warnings-142]

### National symbols



The national bird, *[Ara macao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ara_macao)*

The [flag of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Honduras) is composed of three equal horizontal stripes. The blue upper and lower stripes represent the Pacific Ocean and the Caribbean Sea. The central stripe is white. It contains five blue stars representing the five states of the [Central American Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_Republic_of_Central_America). The middle star represents Honduras, located in the center of the Central American Union.

The coat of arms was established in 1945. It is an equilateral triangle, at the base is a volcano between three castles, over which is a rainbow and the sun shining. The triangle is placed on an area that symbolizes being bathed by both seas. Around all of this an oval containing in golden lettering: "Republic of Honduras, Free, Sovereign and Independent".

The "[National Anthem of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Anthem_of_Honduras)" is a result of a contest carried out in 1914 during the presidency of [Manuel Bonilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manuel_Bonilla). Poet [Augusto Coello](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Augusto_Coello) wrote the anthem, with German-born Honduran composer [Carlos Hartling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlos_Hartling) writing the music. The anthem was officially adopted on 15 November 1915, during the presidency of [Alberto de Jesús Membreño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberto_de_Jes%C3%BAs_Membre%C3%B1o?action=edit\&redlink=1).

The national flower is the orchid *[Rhyncholaelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyncholaelia) digbyana*, which replaced the rose in 1969. The change of the national flower was carried out during the administration of general [Oswaldo López Arellano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswaldo_L%C3%B3pez_Arellano), thinking that *Brassavola digbyana* "is an indigenous plant of Honduras; having this flower exceptional characteristics of beauty, vigor and distinction", as the decree dictates it.

The national tree was declared in 1928 to be simply "the Pine that appears symbolically in our Coat of Arms" (*el Pino que figura simbólicamente en nuestro Escudo*),[^cite_note-146] even though pines comprise a genus and not a species, and even though legally there's no specification as for what kind of pine should appear in the coat of arms *either*. Because of its commonality in the country, the *[Pinus oocarpa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_oocarpa)* species has become since then the species most strongly associated as the national tree, but legally it is not so. Another species associated as the national tree is the *[Pinus caribaea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinus_caribaea)*.

The national mammal is the [white-tailed deer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White-tailed_deer) (*Odocoileus virginianus*), which was adopted as a measure to avoid excessive depredation. It is one of two endemic species of deer. The national bird is the [scarlet macaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarlet_macaw) (*Ara macao*). This bird was much valued by the pre-Columbian civilizations of Honduras.

### Celebrations

Further information: [Public holidays in Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holidays_in_Honduras)



[Sawdust carpets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sawdust_carpet) of [Comayagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comayagua) during [Easter](9325.md) celebrations

National holidays include Honduras Independence Day on 15 September and Children's Day or Día del Niño, which is celebrated in homes, schools and churches on 10 September; on this day, children receive presents and have parties similar to Christmas or birthday celebrations. Some neighborhoods have piñatas on the street. Other holidays are Easter, [Maundy Thursday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maundy_Thursday), [Good Friday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Good_Friday), Day of the Soldier (3 October to celebrate the birth of [Francisco Morazán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francisco_Moraz%C3%A1n)), Christmas, El Dia de [Lempira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lempira_\(Lenca_ruler\)) on 20 July,[^cite_note-147] and New Year's Eve.

Independence Day festivities start early in the morning with marching bands. Each band wears different colors and features cheerleaders. Fiesta Catracha takes place this same day: typical Honduran foods such as [beans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bean), [tamales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamale), baleadas, [cassava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava) with [chicharrón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicharr%C3%B3n), and [tortillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortilla) are offered.

On Christmas Eve people reunite with their families and close friends to have dinner, then give out presents at midnight. In some cities fireworks are seen and heard at midnight. On New Year's Eve there is food and "cohetes", fireworks and festivities. Birthdays are also great events, and include piñatas filled with candies and surprises for the children.

[La Ceiba Carnival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ceiba_Carnival) is celebrated in [La Ceiba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Ceiba) in the north coast in the second half of May to celebrate the day of the city's patron saint [Isidore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isidore_the_Laborer). People from all over the world come for one week of festivities. Every night there is a little carnaval (carnavalito) in a neighborhood. On Saturday there is a big parade with floats and displays with people from many countries. This celebration is also accompanied by the Milk Fair, where many Hondurans come to show off their farm products and animals.

### Art

Main article: [Art of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_of_Honduras)



The Cathedral of [Comayagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comayagua)

The most renowned painter is [José Antonio Velásquez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Antonio_Vel%C3%A1squez). Other important painters include Carlos Garay, and Roque Zelaya. Notable writers include [Lucila Gamero de Medina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucila_Gamero_de_Medina), [Froylán Turcios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Froyl%C3%A1n_Turcios), [Ramón Amaya Amador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ram%C3%B3n_Amaya_Amador) and [Juan Pablo Suazo Euceda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Pablo_Suazo_Euceda), Marco Antonio Rosa,[^cite_note-148] [Roberto Sosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Sosa_\(poet\)), [Eduardo Bähr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_B%C3%A4hr), [Amanda Castro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Castro), [Javier Abril Espinoza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Abril_Espinoza), [Teófilo Trejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Te%C3%B3filo_Trejo?action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Roberto Quesada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roberto_Quesada).

The José Francisco Saybe theater in [San Pedro Sula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_Sula) is home to the Círculo Teatral Sampedrano (Theatrical Circle of San Pedro Sula) Honduras has experienced a boom from its film industry for the past two decades. Since the premiere of the movie "Anita la cazadora de insectos" in 2001, the level of Honduran productions has increased, many collaborating with countries such as Mexico, Colombia, and the United States. The most well known Honduran films are *El Xendra*, *Amor y Frijoles*, and *Cafe con aroma a mi tierra*.

### Folklore

Main article: [Honduran folklore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_folklore)

Legends and fairy tales are paramount, such as [Lluvia de Peces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lluvia_de_Peces) (Rain of Fish). The legends of El [Cadejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadejo) and [La Llorona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Llorona) are also popular.

### Music

Main article: [Music of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Honduras)

[Punta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punta) is the main music of Honduras, with other sounds such as Caribbean salsa, [merengue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merengue_music), reggae, and [reggaeton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reggaeton) all widely heard, especially in the north, and Mexican [rancheras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchera) heard in the rural interior of the country. The most well known musicians are [Guillermo Anderson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guillermo_Anderson) and [Polache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polache). [Banda Blanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banda_Blanca) is a widely known music group in both Honduras and internationally.

### Media

Main article: [Media of Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_of_Honduras)

At least half of households have at least one television. Public television has a far smaller role than in most other countries. The main newspapers are [La Prensa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Prensa_\(Honduras\)), [El Heraldo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Heraldo_\(Tegucigalpa\)), [La Tribuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Tribuna_\(Honduras\)) and Diario Tiempo. The official newspaper is *[La Gaceta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Gaceta_\(Honduras\))*.

### Cuisine

[Honduran cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduran_cuisine) is a fusion of indigenous Lenca cuisine, [Spanish cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuisine_of_Spain), [Caribbean cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_cuisine) and [African cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/African_cuisine). There are also dishes from the Garifuna people. Coconut and coconut milk are featured in both sweet and savory dishes. Regional specialties include fried fish, [tamales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamale), [carne asada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carne_asada) and [baleadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baleada). Other popular dishes include meat roasted with [chismol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chismol) and [carne asada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carne_asada), chicken with rice and corn, and fried fish with pickled onions and jalapeños. Some of the ways seafood and meats are prepared in coastal areas and in the Bay Islands involve coconut milk.

The soups Hondurans enjoy include bean soup, mondongo soup ([tripe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripe) soup), [seafood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seafood) soups and [beef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beef) soups. Generally these soups are served mixed with [plantains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantain_\(cooking\)), [yuca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cassava), and cabbage, and served with [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) [tortillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortilla). Other typical dishes are the montucas or corn [tamales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamale), stuffed tortillas, and tamales wrapped in [plantain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plantain_\(cooking\)) leaves. Honduran typical dishes also include an abundant selection of tropical fruits such as [papaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papaya), [pineapple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pineapple), [plum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plum), [sapote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sapote), [passion fruit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passion_fruit) and bananas which are prepared in many ways while they are still green.

### Sports



[Estadio Olímpico Metropolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estadio_Ol%C3%ADmpico_Metropolitano) in [San Pedro Sula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pedro_Sula) is the official ground of [football](10568.md) in the [FIFA World Cup Qualifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup_qualification_%E2%80%93_CONCACAF_third_round).



[Mauricio Dubón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Dub%C3%B3n)–the first born and raised Honduran in the [MLB](38776.md)–preparing his [shortstop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortstop) play

Football is the most popular [sport in Honduras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_in_Honduras).[^cite_note-149] Honduras's first international competition began in 1921 at the [Independence Centenary Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independence_Centenary_Games) featuring neighboring countries in [Central America](6121.md).[^cite_note-150] The [highest division](648049.md) of football is [The Honduran National Professional Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_Nacional_de_F%C3%BAtbol_Profesional_de_Honduras) ([Spanish](26825.md): *La Liga Nacional de Fútbol Profesional de Honduras*), which was established in 1964.[^cite_note-151] The league is recognized on a [continental level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF), as [C.D. Olimpia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C.D._Olimpia)–the only Honduran club to win the competition–won the [CONCACAF Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Champions_League) in [1972](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_CONCACAF_Champions'_Cup) and [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_CONCACAF_Champions'_Cup).[^cite_note-152][^cite_note-153] The [Honduras national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_football_team) ([Spanish](26825.md): *Selección de fútbol de Honduras*) is considered one of the best nations in [North America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF), as the country last won the [CONCACAF Gold Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CONCACAF_Gold_Cup) in [1981](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_CONCACAF_Championship) and placed third in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_CONCACAF_Gold_Cup).[^cite_note-154][^cite_note-155] On a [global scale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_at_the_FIFA_World_Cup), Honduras has competed in the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) three times in [1982](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_FIFA_World_Cup), [2010](168079.md), and [2014](656933.md), although *Los Catrachos* have yet to win a game.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157][^cite_note-158]

[Baseball](3850.md) is the second most popular sport.[^cite_note-159] Honduras's first international competition began in [1950](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_Amateur_World_Series) in the [Baseball World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baseball_World_Cup), which was the most prestigious global competition at the time.[^cite_note-160] The country lacks a division in baseball, likely due to the absence of competition in [international baseball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Baseball_Softball_Confederation) since [1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_Amateur_World_Series_\(in_Nicaragua\)). The [Honduras national baseball team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_national_baseball_team) ([Spanish](26825.md): *Selección de béisbol de Honduras*) is shy of being a top ten nation in [North and South America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WBSC_Americas) due to infrequent scheduling, although competition is consistent and growing at the [youth level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_sports).[^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162] Inspiration at the youth level came from [Mauricio Dubón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mauricio_Dub%C3%B3n) being the first born and raised Honduran to start in [Major League Baseball](38776.md), who is currently competing today.[^cite_note-163]

All other sports tend to be minor at best, as Honduras has not won a medal in the [Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_at_the_Olympics) and has not made notable results in other [world championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_championship).[^cite_note-164] However, Hondurans have consistently entered [track & field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletics_at_the_Summer_Olympics) and [swimming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swimming_at_the_Summer_Olympics) games at the [Summer Olympics](27566.md) since [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_at_the_1968_Summer_Olympics) and [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_at_the_1984_Summer_Olympics), respectively.[^cite_note-165] Occasionally, Honduras has competed in [combat sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_sport) ranging from [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) to [boxing](4243.md) at the Summer Olympics as well.[^cite_note-166][^cite_note-167] Gender inequality is present in the sports industry, as teams like the [Honduras women's national football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honduras_women's_national_football_team) ([Spanish](26825.md): *Selección de fútbol de Honduras Femenina*) has yet to qualify in global and continental tournaments and [softball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Softball) being nearly nonexistent in the country.[^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169]
