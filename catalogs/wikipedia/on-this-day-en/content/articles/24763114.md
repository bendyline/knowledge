**Futbol Club Barcelona Femení**, commonly referred to as **Barça Femení** (Catalan pronunciation: [\[ˈbaɾsə fəməˈni\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Catalan))[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-13] or simply **Barça**, is a Spanish professional [women's football](191466.md) team based in [Barcelona](4443.md), Catalonia. It is the women's football section of [FC Barcelona](68187.md) and competes in the [Liga F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liga_F), the top tier of [Spanish women's football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_football_in_Spain), playing home games at the [Johan Cruyff Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Cruyff_Stadium) in [Sant Joan Despí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Joan_Desp%C3%AD), and occasionally at the [Camp Nou](222488.md).

The club was formed in 1970 with some financial and logistical support from FC Barcelona, but without being an official section. In its inaugural season it competed in matches against other Catalan teams; it was originally known as **Selecció Ciutat de Barcelona** before taking the name **Peña Femenina Barcelonista** in 1971. Shortly after women's football was officially recognised in Spain, the team became known as **Club Femení Barcelona** in 1983 until 2002, when it officially became a section of FC Barcelona and took the official name Futbol Club Barcelona.

Domestically, Barcelona Femení is the most successful Spanish women's team, with eleven league titles, twelve [Copas de la Reina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_de_la_Reina_de_F%C3%BAtbol), and six [Supercopas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a_Femenina), as well as eleven [Copas Catalunyas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Catalunya_\(women's_football\)). It also won the previous second tier of Spanish women's football a record four times after battling relegation and fighting for promotion throughout the 2000s.

Since the club's professionalisation in 2015, Barcelona has cemented itself as Spain's dominant women's football team, becoming the league's most successful team in the [UEFA Women's Champions League](1985338.md), and [one of the most successful women's football teams in Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_clubs_by_competitive_honours_won). It won the Champions League in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final), [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final) and [2026](76987356.md); with its first Champions League title, Barcelona became the first (and only) Spanish women's team to complete a [continental treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_\(association_football\)), and with its third Champions League win, they became the first (and only) Spanish women's team to complete a [continental quadruple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_association_football_teams_to_have_won_four_or_more_trophies_in_one_season#Four_titles_in_one_season).[^cite_note-\:19-14]

## History

### 1970s: Beginnings

See also: [Women's football in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_football_in_Spain) and [1971–72 P.F. Barcelona season § Founding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_P.F._Barcelona_season#Founding)

[Women's football in Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_football_in_Spain), particularly [Barcelona](4443.md), had a certain level of popularity in the 1960s, but was amateur, unofficial and technically banned.[^cite_note-Alcalde_20202-15] The "embryo" of Barcelona Femení was the Selecció Ciutat de Barcelona, formed in November 1970.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-\:02-17] In at least as early as the 1960s, [FC Barcelona](68187.md) had women's teams play in exhibition games at the [Camp Nou](222488.md); the club does not consider these as antecedents to its current women's team as they "\[were] teams that had been created only for that occasion, unlike Selecció Ciutat \[de Barcelona], which was created with the idea of continuity and laying the groundwork for a future project."[^cite_note-18] Upon Barcelona announcing Selecció Barcelona as its new women's team, *[El Noticiero Universal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Noticiero_Universal?action=edit\&redlink=1)* reported that the club had almost pioneered women's football in Spain "many years earlier", having gone so far as to organise a game that could not be played for reasons beyond their control.[^cite_note-19]

The team was created after footballer [Immaculada Cabecerán](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immaculada_Cabecer%C3%A1n) approached the club with the proposition in November 1970; the resulting team has continuity to the present. The Selecció Barcelona played [their first match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selecci%C3%B3_Barcelona_0%E2%80%930_UE_Centelles) on Christmas Day 1970, winning on [penalties](511302.md) against Unió Esportiva Centelles in front of a crowd of around 60,000 people.[^cite_note-\:02-17] Although the team was not officially part of the club structure, it was the first match played by a women's team formally associated with FC Barcelona (then known as CF Barcelona). The women's team then changed its name to Penya Femenina Barça (Peña Femenina Barcelonista) in early 1971,[^cite_note-\:02-17] quickly becoming very popular,[^cite_note-\:23-20] and played more matches at the Camp Nou with large crowds.[^cite_note-\:02-17] PF Barcelona wore the club's *blaugrana* strip from 1971, without the crest.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] They were runner-up in the first cup competition in Catalonia, [in 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_P.F._Barcelona_season#Pernod_Cup_Trophy),[^cite_note-23] before winning the Catalonia-based league [in 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_P.F._Barcelona_season).[^cite_note-24]

The initial strength of women's football in Spain was flagging by 1975,[^cite_note-25] and by September 1977 there was no longer a women's league in Catalonia, with the idea of one also considered somewhat unrealistic.[^cite_note-FutbolFemeninoYa-26] In February 1977, P.F. Barcelona had to publish a call for players in the *Revista Barcelonista*, with the magazine suggesting there was a crisis in lack of players. It promoted the team as a way to "keep in shape" and look good, rather than emphasising sport.[^cite_note-27] During the down period of the late 1970s, Barcelona was one of the large teams that continued playing, including against women's teams from leagues in other countries. [Maria Teresa Andreu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Teresa_Andreu), president of P.F. Barcelona at the time, felt that FC Barcelona respected the team, with the club supporting and involving them.[^cite_note-FutbolFemeninoYa-26] A Catalan women's league was established again by the 1978–79 season, with a concurrent cup competition in which [P.F. Barcelona Atlético](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Barcelona_B_\(women\)) finished as runners-up after losing the final on penalties,[^cite_note-28] before P.F. Barcelona was second in the league in [1979–80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979%E2%80%9380_P.F._Barcelona_season).[^cite_note-29]

### 1981–1988: Lliga catalana and rivalry with Barcilona

See also: [Club Femení Barcelona in the Lliga catalana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Femen%C3%AD_Barcelona_in_the_Lliga_catalana)

The [Spanish Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Spanish_Football_Federation) (then FEF, now RFEF) formally recognised women's football in 1980[^cite_note-30] and in 1981 approved for a women's football department of the [Catalan Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalan_Football_Federation) (FCF) to create the [Women's Catalan Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Catalan_Football_League?action=edit\&redlink=1),[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] of which Barcelona was a founding member.[^cite_note-TomasHistoria-33] In the 1982–83 season, the team took former player and advocate [Núria Llansà](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N%C3%BAria_Llans%C3%A0) as coach,[^cite_note-Llansa-34] and changed its name. Andreu suggested the team be called Club Femenino/Femení (CF) Barcelona, to be closer to the name of the men's team, with this change made official before the 1983 [Copa de la Reina de Fútbol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_de_la_Reina_de_F%C3%BAtbol);[^cite_note-35] they were still sometimes known as Penya Femenina Barcelona later in the decade,[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] and were not an official section of FC Barcelona.[^cite_note-Lucha-38] How the women's team was run had caused a splinter group to leave and form the independent team [Penya Barcelonista Barcilona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penya_Barcelonista_Barcilona?action=edit\&redlink=1) in 1981, led by former Barcelona players [Francina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francina_Pubill) and [Vicenta Pubill i Font](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicenta_Pubill_i_Font?action=edit\&redlink=1). In terms of titles, Barcilona was much more successful than Barcelona in the 1980s,[^cite_note-39] though the two were considered the teams to beat within Catalan women's football and "fought to the death" in the league.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-BCampeon-41][^cite_note-Empieza-42] Other top Catalan teams at the time were [*derbi femení*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbi_Femen%C3%AD) rivals [Espanyol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCD_Espanyol_\(women\)) and another independent team, Vallès Occidental.[^cite_note-43] Barcelona won its first official competition – the Copa Generalitat/Copa Catalana, a predecessor to the [Copa Catalunya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Catalunya_\(women's_football\)) — by defeating Barcilona and Vallès Occidental [in 1985](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Femen%C3%AD_Barcelona_in_the_Lliga_catalana#1985_Copa_Generalitat).[^cite_note-Title2020-44]

Support from FC Barcelona varied in the era: in January 1983, ahead of the Copa de la Reina, Barcelona did not have fixed training facilities or times (unlike the majority of teams in the league, though Barcilona also did not) and the relationship with FC Barcelona was considered among the worst of women's teams with parent clubs. Reportedly, FC Barcelona "didn't want to know" when it came to them.[^cite_note-Lucha-38] In 1984, the year Llansà was promoted to director of the women's football team,[^cite_note-Llansa-34] FC Barcelona was thanked for taking a much more sustained interest in women's football, having offered its stadiums (including the Camp Nou) and facilities.[^cite_note-45] Since at least 1985, the team played most home games at the [Mini Estadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Estadi).[^cite_note-46][^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48] Llansà would remain director until the team was incorporated as an official section of FC Barcelona.[^cite_note-49]

### 1988–2001: Liga Nacional and inconsistency

In 1988, Club Femení Barcelona, which was sponsored by FC Barcelona, was a founding member of the Liga Nacional, the first top-flight national women's league in Spain.[^cite_note-\:26-50] Their performances in this iteration of the league were inconsistent from the start, losing to weak teams and beating otherwise unbeatable ones, cementing themselves as a mid-table team. They had a successful three-year run in the early 1990s, placing in the league top three between 1992 and 1994, and winning the 1994 Copa de la Reina.[^cite_note-\:26-50] They subsequently declined to bottom table positions but, despite their weaker performances in the later years of the Liga Nacional, always managed to stay in the top division.[^cite_note-\:26-50]

Andreu joined the board of FC Barcelona in 2000,[^cite_note-51] and within the year had brought Club Femení Barcelona into the Barcelona Foundation's organisational structure, making the women's team part of the club in administration.[^cite_note-\:0-52]

### 2002–2007: Becoming an official section and Segunda División

In 2001, the league was rebranded into the [Superliga Femenina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superliga_Femenina) due to natural growth; Barcelona was not accepted into the top division, as they could not pay the inscription fee of [₧](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_peseta)3 million, and were installed in the new Superliga second division, the lowest tier of national football at the time (though sub-divided by regional group).[^cite_note-TomasBelenguer-53] Llansà pushed for the team to be recognised and supported as a section of the club;[^cite_note-54] in the summer of 2002, the FC Barcelona board unanimously approved ratifying the decision to incorporate CF Barcelona as an official section, and the club rebranded the women's section to Futbol Club Barcelona Femení.[^cite_note-\:0-52][^cite_note-\:1-55][^cite_note-Parking-56] The next year, the team nominally became professional, but did not receive livable salaries and still had to maintain other jobs.[^cite_note-Parking-56]

Barcelona won its group in the [Segunda División](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segunda_Divisi%C3%B3n_\(women\)) in 2002 and 2003, but remained in the second division after unsuccessful appearances in the promotion playoffs. It was eventually promoted to the Superliga Femenina in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004-05_Superliga_Femenina). Once promoted, the team enjoyed some popularity in the [2004–05 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_FC_Barcelona_Femen%C3%AD_season) due to the signing of the [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico_women's_national_football_team) international [Maribel "Marigol" Domínguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maribel_Dom%C3%ADnguez) and the Spanish María Luisa Coimbra, though this did not translate into quality results for the team.[^cite_note-20minutos-2005-57] [Xavi Llorens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xavi_Llorens) was hired as manager in 2006 to replace [Natalia Astrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Astrain),[^cite_note-58] who was among the figures to leave when the club restructured and significantly reduced the women's section in the summer of 2006.[^cite_note-59] Llorens was unable to keep the team in the Superliga, and at the end of the [2006–07 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_FC_Barcelona_Femen%C3%AD_season), the team was relegated and the club considered dissolving the section.[^cite_note-60]

### 2008–2015: Return to the top flight and first Golden Era



Barcelona celebrating their 2011 [Copa Catalunya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copa_Catalunya_\(women's_football\)) win

Barcelona returned to the Superliga in 2008, and between 2009 and 2011, they consolidated themselves in top positions in the league table.[^cite_note-\:8-61] In [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Copa_de_la_Reina_de_F%C3%BAtbol), they won their second Copa de la Reina, beating local rivals [Espanyol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCD_Espanyol_Femenino) 1–0 in the final.[^cite_note-\:8-61] In [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011-12_Superliga_Femenina), they won their first league title with a then-record 94 points,[^cite_note-62] qualifying for the [UEFA Women's Champions League](1985338.md) for the first time before exiting in the first round.[^cite_note-\:9-63] They successfully defended the league in [2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012-13_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_\(women\)) with a last matchday away win over leading team [Athletic Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Bilbao_\(women\)), before winning the [2013 Copa de la Reina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Copa_de_la_Reina_de_F%C3%BAtbol) to become the fifth Spanish women's team to achieve the [domestic double](88184.md).[^cite_note-\:9-63]

Barcelona qualified for the quarterfinals of the Women's Champions League for the first time in the [2013–14 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League),[^cite_note-\:4-64] a season in which they won their third straight league title. Between 17 April 2011 and 11 December 2011, Barcelona maintained a 15-match winning streak, the longest consecutive run in Spanish women's football history by 2015; and between 23 September 2012 and 20 April 2014, they had a 54-game unbeaten run, another record at the time.[^cite_note-65] By the end of the 2013–14 season, the club referred to the previous four years of Barcelona Femení as a Golden Era in which they "absolutely dominated the Spanish football scene", and the team had received their own section in the [Barça Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bar%C3%A7a_Museum).[^cite_note-\:4-64] In the following [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Primera_Divisi%C3%B3n_\(women\)), they became the first team in the Spanish women's league to win four straight league titles.[^cite_note-66]

### 2015–2018: Professionalism and rivalry with Atlético Madrid

In the summer of 2015, the club made the decision to [professionalise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professionalization) the women's section.[^cite_note-\:1-55] Despite their domestic success, players were unable to make a living playing for Barcelona Femení, and the club knew it could not compete in the Champions League against professional teams from other countries.[^cite_note-\:5-67] The team had made a sponsorship deal with [Stanley Tools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Hand_Tools) in 2014, which allowed them to become professional,[^cite_note-\:5-67] and added a shirt deal in 2018 that made Stanley the first shirt sponsor specific to the women's team.[^cite_note-68] Having not been included in the deal when [Rakuten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rakuten) sponsored the men's team's shirts in 2016 allowed Barcelona Femení to sign an exclusivity deal with Stanley for around €3.5 million per year, separating the team's finances and making them individually profitable.[^cite_note-69] With the professionalisation, the club began changes, including bringing in Markel Zubizarreta first as women's team manager in 2015.[^cite_note-\:14-70]

Barcelona's domestic dominance ended as they went through the process of becoming professional, with then-captain [Vicky Losada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vicky_Losada) later saying that the jump "was a big change" initially, disrupting the team.[^cite_note-\:5-67] As part of their new professionalism, Barcelona placed a greater priority on competing in the Champions League,[^cite_note-\:6-71] though focused on improving conditions rather than immediately trying to match the level of foreign teams.[^cite_note-\:7-72] They reached their first Champions League semi-final in the [2016–17 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League), which was compared to the rapid rise of [Manchester City W.F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_City_W.F.C.), who had become professional and then achieved this milestone at the same times as Barcelona.[^cite_note-\:6-71]

This period also coincided with [Atlético Madrid Femenino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atl%C3%A9tico_Madrid_Femenino) "emerg\[ing] as a really strong side". Atlético became a challenger to Barcelona's success, and did so by playing with a style that counteracted Barcelona's: a rivalry developed between the teams that Losada described as the women's *Clásico*.[^cite_note-\:5-67][^cite_note-73] Atlético won the three league titles between the 2016–17 and 2018–19 seasons, and Barcelona were runners-up in each of those seasons.[^cite_note-\:3-74][^cite_note-\:5-67]

After struggling against Atlético for a few years,[^cite_note-\:5-67] Barcelona beat them in the final of the [2017 Copa de la Reina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Copa_de_la_Reina_de_F%C3%BAtbol), in Llorens' final match as coach.[^cite_note-75] Llorens had come in to restructure the women's section in 2006 when it was weak, and his departure also brought about change. Under his replacement, [Fran Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fran_S%C3%A1nchez),[^cite_note-\:5-67][^cite_note-\:6-71] and with Zubizarreta becoming sporting director in summer 2017,[^cite_note-\:14-70] Barcelona Femení moved away from its focus on development and integration of youth players.[^cite_note-\:5-67][^cite_note-\:6-71] The process briefly changed to signing multiple big names in international football, including [England](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/England_women's_national_football_team) star [Toni Duggan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toni_Duggan)[^cite_note-\:6-71] and [Lieke Martens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieke_Martens), who they made the highest-paid female footballer. In the summer of 2018, Barcelona Femení joined the men's team on a pre-season tour for the first time, one of the measures that made *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* consider them a groundbreaker in "growing commercial opportunities" and promoting equality for women's football.[^cite_note-\:12-76]

### 2019–2024: The new dynasty&#x20;

Barcelona defeated Atlético at the [Wanda Metropolitano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitano_Stadium) on 17 March 2019, in a match that broke the [world record for attendance at a women's club football match](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_women's_association_football_attendance_records) with 60,739 attendees.[^cite_note-\:3-74] Later that season, Barcelona progressed to their first ever Champions League final, meeting and losing 1–4 to five-time Champions League winners [Olympique Lyonnais Féminin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais_F%C3%A9minin) (Lyon).[^cite_note-77] Sánchez had been replaced by [Lluís Cortés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llu%C3%ADs_Cort%C3%A9s) in January 2019 and,[^cite_note-78] starting in summer 2019, Barcelona aimed to reduce the number of signings each year, returning to the long-term project of homegrown talent as the basis of the team;[^cite_note-\:5-67] Zubizarreta would gain a reputation as the "architect", building the team from youth acquisitions and convincing higher profile players, a "low-cost" method of creating a strong, cohesive team.[^cite_note-\:15-79] Barcelona Femení began only incorporating external players who connected to the team's style, with Losada insisting that "the style of the club is sacred."[^cite_note-\:5-67] After losing the 2019 Champions League final, the team directed investment to other areas, including improvement in conditions and an expanded support staff.[^cite_note-\:15-79] The period from Barcelona's surge in success and cementing of identity in 2019, to the present as of 2024, is described as a dynastic era for the team and as the "new dynasty" of Europe.[^cite_note-\:11-80][^cite_note-81][^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-ElPokerNuncaVisto-84] In 2024, the success of Barcelona Femení was considered by local sportswriters and the FC Barcelona club historian to be the prevailing sense of identity and club narrative for FC Barcelona.[^cite_note-\:25-85]

After having been runners-up in all competitions for two years, Barcelona won four titles for the 2019–20 season,[^cite_note-86] but did not reach the final of the [2019–20 Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League), which the club considered disappointing.[^cite_note-87] Since 2020, Barcelona has won the league and reached the Champions League final every season, while also winning at least one of the Copa de la Reina and [Supercopa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supercopa_de_Espa%C3%B1a_Femenina) each year.[^cite_note-88]

&#x20;They won their first Champions League title by defeating [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_LFC) 4–0 in [the 2021 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final).[^cite_note-89] A month later, Cortés left, as the club continued to strive to build on their success and standards of professionalism.[^cite_note-\:10-90][^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92] Losing their manager did not harm the team: [their 2021–22 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_FC_Barcelona_Femen%C3%AD_season) is considered one of the most dominant in football.[^cite_note-\:11-80][^cite_note-WahlSub-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-Trophies2022-96] [Grant Wahl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Wahl) said the team was one of the best women's sides in history, and attributed their resilience and increasing success to having established a new culture within the team itself.[^cite_note-WahlSub-93] They failed to retain the Champions League this season,[^cite_note-Trophies2022-96] but would win it again in the following two.[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-\:20-98] [Their 2023–24 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_FC_Barcelona_Femen%C3%AD_season) exceeded their previous successes,[^cite_note-ElPokerNuncaVisto-84] with the team completing their [first continental quadruple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_association_football_teams_to_have_won_four_or_more_trophies_in_one_season#Four_titles_in_one_season)[^cite_note-99] by defeating Lyon 2–0 in the [2024 Champions League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_UEFA_Women's_Champions_League_final);[^cite_note-\:20-98] it was their third Champions League title in four years, and their first defeat of European rivals Lyon. [Maria Tikas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maria_Tikas) opined that the match confirmed Barcelona as the new holders of the European dynasty.[^cite_note-\:16-100]



The official record attendance for a women's football match was set at Camp Nou on 22 April 2022, with 91,648 people watching Barcelona defeat [Wolfsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfL_Wolfsburg_\(women\)) 5–1 (pictured).

[^cite_note-101]

During this period, the team also dominated individual awards: captain [Alexia Putellas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexia_Putellas) swept all individual awards for two seasons (corresponding to [2020–21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020%E2%80%9321_FC_Barcelona_Femen%C3%AD_season) and 2021–22),[^cite_note-102][^cite_note-103] with [Aitana Bonmatí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aitana_Bonmat%C3%AD) then winning all individual titles for [the 2022–23 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_FC_Barcelona_Femen%C3%AD_season). Including her achievements with the [Spain national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spain_women's_national_football_team), Bonmatí had the most decorated season of any footballer, male or female.[^cite_note-104] Other records set by the team include twice setting the highest-ever attendance for a women's football match – Barcelona beat [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid_Femenino) and then [Wolfsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfL_Wolfsburg_\(women\)) at the Camp Nou, with respective attendances of 91,553 and 91,648[^cite_note-WahlSub-93][^cite_note-Gulino-105][^cite_note-ESPN-UWCLSF-106] – and breaking the [world record for a transfer fee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_association_football_transfers#Women) in women's football to bring [Keira Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keira_Walsh) from [Manchester City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_City_W.F.C.) in the summer of 2022.[^cite_note-\:13-107]

The team's dominance in Spain also reached new heights after they summarily defeated main rivals Atlético 7–0 in the 2021–22 Supercopa final.[^cite_note-108] That season, they had mathematically won the league by March.[^cite_note-109] Real Madrid incorporated its women's team in 2020, aiming to challenge Barcelona's monopoly within Spain; Barcelona has won every [women's Clásico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Cl%C3%A1sico) as of 2024.[^cite_note-\:5-67][^cite_note-\:21-110]

## Supporters and popularity



Barcelona Femení supporters at [Plaça Sant Jaume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pla%C3%A7a_Sant_Jaume) in 2023

Barcelona Femení has a fan base distinct from that of the men's team, even when the team has sold out the main stadiums. The popularity of the team grew vastly in the years after it became professional.[^cite_note-\:2-111] Part of the reason for the growth in popularity is connected to the growth of [feminism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feminism) in the 2010s. The audience for Barcelona Femení includes both women and men of all ages – reportedly mostly men who do not watch the Barcelona men's team,[^cite_note-\:2-111] though, in the 2020s, Barcelona Femení's fan base has grown to include long-time Barcelona fans who have more recently been exposed to and enjoy the women's team's style of play.[^cite_note-\:22-112] [*El Periódico*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Peri%C3%B3dico_de_Catalunya) wrote in 2024 that the team plays for people "who have become hooked on \[their] way of playing and understanding football".[^cite_note-\:22-112] The same year, authors Jim O'Brien and Xavier Ginesta questioned whether the club itself promoted the women's team to be "in the vanguard of genuine change in gender politics" or as a form of brand marketing due to their increased profile and wanting "to breath\[e] fresh life into the fading traditions of the club."[^cite_note-\:25-85] Supporters of the team typically support feminist values in society and sport, as well as the team itself.[^cite_note-\:2-111]

Around the time of their professionalisation, the team made an effort to gain more regular supporters attending matches. Having been dominant within Spain, the staff knew that winning was not enough and worked on ways to engage potential fans.[^cite_note-\:7-72] Research conducted around this time suggested that the lack of widespread support for Barcelona Femení was primarily because of the power hierarchy within global and [glocalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glocalization) football that under-appreciated women.[^cite_note-113]<sup>: 60 </sup> By the time Barcelona Femení successively broke the official world record attendance twice in 2022, the increasing support tracked with global trends but also showed the dedication of the domestic support the team had fostered, in part by having many [Catalan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catalans) players promoted as local stars.[^cite_note-\:25-85]

The team moved to the [Johan Cruyff Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johan_Cruyff_Stadium) in 2019, located in [Sant Joan Despí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sant_Joan_Desp%C3%AD), a town west of the city of Barcelona. They previously played at the [Mini Estadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mini_Estadi), next to the Camp Nou. Despite playing outside of the city, by 2022 the team had averaged crowds four times larger than when they played at the Mini Estadi. This was attributed by Zubizarreta not to the team becoming more successful, but to the club treating the women's players equal to the men's in advertising and merchandising.[^cite_note-114] The [travelling support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_\(sports\)) for the team also grew in this period, attributed by [ESPN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESPN) as a post-[COVID-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_sports) development of fans wanting to follow the team in major away games.[^cite_note-115]

In 2022, the audience for Barcelona Femení games was also more varied than that of the men's team, which has been suggested to be due to the fact the women's team did not offer [season tickets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Season_ticket_\(sports\)). The crowd for men's games were mostly season ticket holders and tourists who bought last-minute remaining tickets, while most tickets to women's games were bought as part of four-ticket packs.[^cite_note-\:2-111]

## Rivalries

Main articles: [Women's Athletic–Barcelona clásico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic%E2%80%93Barcelona_cl%C3%A1sico#Women's_rivalry), [Derbi Femení](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbi_Femen%C3%AD), and [Women's Clásico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_Cl%C3%A1sico)

Barcelona Femení's first rivalry was against local rivals [Espanyol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCD_Espanyol_\(women\)), against whom they played their first matches in 1971.[^cite_note-\:02-17] The first league match between them, in 1988, resulted in a 2–2 draw; though most teams in the league at the time were from the Barcelona area, the *[Derbi Femení](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derbi_Femen%C3%AD)* was still a highlighted fixture.[^cite_note-116]

The main rivalry for Barcelona Femení was Atlético Madrid, with the two teams having similar prominence and success within Spain and Europe in the 2010s, as well as having playing styles that contrasted each other.[^cite_note-\:5-67] Barcelona and [Athletic Club Bilbao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Athletic_Club_\(women\)) also shared a rivalry in Spain in the 2000s and early 2010s, seeing large attendances at the [San Mamés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Mam%C3%A9s_Stadium_\(1913\)) for their matches. In the 2012–13 season, Barcelona were at one stage 8 points behind Athletic before winning the league on the last day at San Mamés.[^cite_note-117] Barcelona, Atlético and Athletic were the first major professional women's teams in Spain, sustaining league rivalries because they were the three largest teams for quite some time. Most other large clubs (i.e. those with historic men's teams) did not incorporate women's sections until 2018 or later, so the other women's teams prior to this were smaller or independent.[^cite_note-118]

Real Madrid incorporated a women's section in 2020. After their matches that season, Losada dismissed the idea that the teams had a rivalry: "For me, it's not a clásico but on a media level it helps that at last they're in our league. Those who don't follow women's football might think it's a clásico; those that do, know it's not."[^cite_note-\:5-67] Real Madrid improved in the following seasons, and though Barcelona did, too – having always beaten Real Madrid by 2024 – staff and players at Barcelona said that a stronger Real Madrid was important to keep up the competitive status of the league and for the global image of the game, with [Caroline Graham Hansen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caroline_Graham_Hansen) saying "\[*El Clásico*] is a big game, a game the whole world knows and it's important that it reflects the level in the women's game."[^cite_note-\:21-110]

Barcelona also has rivalries in the Champions League, including against Wolfsburg and Lyon, the latter of which were champions in the first two finals Barcelona lost. After the first lost final, reaching Lyon's level became an aspiration or "obsession" for Barcelona;[^cite_note-\:5-67] Lyon were called Barcelona's [*bestia negra*](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/bestia%20negra) ahead of Barcelona defeating them for the first time in 2024.[^cite_note-\:16-100][^cite_note-119]
