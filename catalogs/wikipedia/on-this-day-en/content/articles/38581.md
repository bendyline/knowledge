**Poltava**[^cite_note-6] is a city on the [Vorskla River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorskla) in [Central](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Ukraine) [Ukraine](31750.md). It serves as the administrative center of [Poltava Oblast](1567650.md) as well as [Poltava Raion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Raion) within the oblast. It also hosts the administration of [Poltava urban hromada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_urban_hromada), one of the [hromadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hromada) of Ukraine.[^cite_note-admreform_2020_poltava_city-7] It had an estimated population of 279,593 in 2022.

Potava was first mentioned as Ltava in [Rus' chronicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rus'_chronicles) of the 12th century. Known as Poltava since 1430, in the 17th century the city became part of the [Hetmanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate), serving as a centre of an eponymous [Cossack regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Regiment) in [Left-bank Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left-bank_Ukraine). In 1709 it was the site of a [decisive battle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poltava) of the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War). Following the dissolution of Cossack Hetmanate, in 1802 Poltava became the centre of an [eponymous governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Governorate) of the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire), and housed the residence of the [governor general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_general) of [Little Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Russia).&#x20;

Starting from the late 18th century, Poltava became a major cultural centre, with many activists of the [Ukrainian national revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_national_revival) living and working in the city. The city was severely damaged during [World War II](32927.md), but was restored and expanded in the following decades.[^cite_note-enc1-8][^cite_note-9] In modern times Poltava remains an important cultural and educational centre, as well as a location of several notable industrial enterprises. Known for its parks, it is reputed to be one of the greenest cities in Ukraine.[^cite_note-10]

## History

See also: [Timeline of Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Poltava)

It is still unknown when Poltava was founded, although the town was not attested before 1174. However, municipal authorities chose to celebrate the city's 1100th anniversary in 1999. As part of the 800th anniversary of Poltava celebrations, in 1974, the Urozhai Stadium was reopened after a six year of renovations.[^cite_note-11] The settlement is indeed an old one, as archeologists unearthed an ancient [Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleolithic) dwelling, as well as [Scythian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scythian) remains, within the city limits.

### Middle Ages

The present name of the city is traditionally connected to the settlement Ltava, which is mentioned in the *[Hypatian Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypatian_Chronicle)* in 1174.[^cite_note-hop-12][^cite_note-13] According to the chronicle, on Saint Peter's Day (12 July) of 1182, [Igor Sviatoslavich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Sviatoslavich), chasing hordes of the Cuman khans Konchak and Kobiak, crossed the [Vorskla River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorskla_River) near *Ltava* and moved towards [Pereiaslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pereiaslav)), where Igor's army was victorious over the Cumans.[^cite_note-hop-12] During the [Mongol invasion of Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_Rus') in 1238–39, many cities of the middle Dnipro region were destroyed, possibly including Ltava.[^cite_note-hop-12]

In the mid-14th century the region was part of the Duchy of Kyiv, which was a vassal of the [Algirdas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algirdas)' [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania).[^cite_note-hop-12] According to the Russian historian Aleksandr Shennikov, the region around modern Poltava was a Cuman Duchy belonging to Mansur, who was a son of [Mamai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamai).[^cite_note-shennikov-14] Shennikov also claims that the Mansur Duchy joined the Grand Duchy of Lithuania as an [associated state](346414.md) rather than a [vassal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vassal_state), and that the city of Poltava already existed at that time.[^cite_note-shennikov-14] In 1399, Mansur's army assisted the [Grand Ducal Lithuanian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ducal_Lithuanian_Army) in the [battle of the Vorskla River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Vorskla_River). According to legend, after the battle, the [Cossack Mamay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Mamay) helped [Vytautas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vytautas) to escape death.[^cite_note-shennikov-14]

The city is mentioned for the first time under the name of Poltava no later than 1430.[^cite_note-hop-12] Supposedly, in 1430 the Lithuanian duke [Vytautas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vytautas) gave the city, along with Glinsk (today a village near the city of [Romny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romny)) and Glinitsa, to [Murza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murza) Olexa (Loxada Mansurxanovich), who moved to the Grand Duchy of Lithuania from the [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde).[^cite_note-hop-12] In 1430 Murza Olexa was baptized as Alexander Glinsky, who was a progenitor of the [Glinsky family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glinski).[^cite_note-hop-12] According to Shenninkov, Alexander Glinsky must have been baptized in 1390 by [Cyprian, Metropolitan of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyprian,_Metropolitan_of_Kiev), who had just regained his title of Metropolitan of Kyiv and all Russia (rather than the Metropolitan of [Russia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Russia) and Lithuania). On 6 March 1390 Cyprian permanently moved to [Muscovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow).[^cite_note-shennikov-14]

In 1482, Poltava was razed by the [Crimean Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate) [Mengli I Giray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Me%C3%B1li_I_Giray).[^cite_note-hop-12]

### Early modern period

In 1537 Ografena Vasylivna Glinska (Baibuza) passed Poltava to her son-in-law Mykhailo Ivanovych Hrybunov-Baibuza.[^cite_note-hop-12]

After the [Union of Lublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Lublin) in 1569, Poltava became part of the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) within the [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth). In 1630 Poltava was passed to a Polish magnate, Bartholomew Obalkowski.[^cite_note-hop-12] In 1641 it changed ownership again, to Alexander Koniecpolski.[^cite_note-hop-12] In 1646 Poltava became part of Wiśniowiecki Ordynatsia (a large Wiśniowiecki estate in Left-bank Ukraine centered in [Lubny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lubny)), governed by the Ruthenian-Polish [magnate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnate) [Jeremi Wiśniowiecki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeremi_Wi%C5%9Bniowiecki) (1612–1651).[^cite_note-hop-12]

In 1648, the city became the base of a distinguished regiment of [Ukrainian](145144.md) [Cossacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossacks), and served as a Cossack stronghold during the [Khmelnytsky Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khmelnytsky_Uprising).[^cite_note-hop-12] In 1650, to commemorate a victory of the Cossack Host over the Polish army at the [Poltavka River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltavka_River?action=edit\&redlink=1), the Metropolitan of Kyiv, [Sylvester Kossov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvester_Kossov), ordered the establishment of the [Holy Cross Exaltation Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Cross_Exaltation_Monastery,_Poltava) in Poltava. The project was financed by a number of prominent local residents, including [Martyn Pushkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyn_Pushkar), [Ivan Iskra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Iskra), [Ivan Kramar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Kramar?action=edit\&redlink=1) and many others.[^cite_note-hop-12]





Shields of the [Poltava Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Regiment) and Poltava Regiment headquarters

During the 1654 [Pereyaslav Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pereyaslav_Council), the Poltava city delegates pledged their allegiance to the Czar of Muscovy, after which [stolnik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stolnik) Andrei Spasitelev arrived in Poltava and recorded 1,335 residents who had pledged their allegiance.[^cite_note-hop-12] In 1658 Poltava became a center of anti-government revolt led by [Martyn Pushkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyn_Pushkar), who contested the legitimacy of [Ivan Vyhovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Vyhovsky)'s election to the post of [Hetman of Zaporizhian Host](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetman_of_Zaporizhian_Host).[^cite_note-hop-12] The uprising was extinguished with the help of [ Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Khanate).[^cite_note-hop-12]

On the issue [boyar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyar) [Vasily Borisovich Sheremetev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Borisovich_Sheremetev) wrote to [Alexei Mikhailovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Mikhailovich) on 8 June 1658: "... the *Cherkas* \[Cossack] city of Poltava is ravaged and burned to the ground and only if the Great Sovereign orders to rebuild on the Tatar Sokma (pathway) of [Bakeyev Route](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%91%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%B0%D0%B5%D0%B2%20%D1%88%D0%BB%D1%8F%D1%85) and protect many his sovereign cities from Tatar visits. And if the Great Sovereign allows to place a [voivode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivode) in the city and rebuilt the city until the fall that in Poltava *Cherkasy* \[Cossacks] and residents built their houses and stock-piled their food".[^cite_note-hop-12] With the signing of the 1667 [truce of Andrusovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Truce_of_Andrusovo), the city was finally subjected to the [Tsardom of Muscovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsardom_of_Muscovy), while remaining part of the [Cossack Hetmanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate).

The city suffered from the [Great Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Turkish_War) when in 1695 Petro Ivanenko led an anti-Muscovite uprising with the help of Crimean Tatars, who ravaged the local monastery.[^cite_note-hop-12] The same year the [Poltava Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Regiment) actively participated in the [Azov campaigns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_campaigns_\(1695%E2%80%9396\)) which resulted in the taking of the Turkish fortress of Kyzy-Kermen (today the city of [Beryslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beryslav), [Kherson Oblast](1273370.md)).[^cite_note-hop-12]



The Column of Glory commemorates the centenary of the [Battle of Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poltava) (1709)

On 8 July (New Style) or 27 June (Old Style) 1709 the [Battle of Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poltava) took place near the city during the [Great Northern War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War). The battle ended in a decisive victory of [Peter I of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_I_of_Russia) over the [Swedish](5058739.md) forces.[^cite_note-hop-12] As a result, the [Swedish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swedish_Empire) lost its status as a European great power and the Russian Empire began an era of supremacy in [eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe).[^cite_note-15] In 1710 there was a plague in the city and its surrounding area.[^cite_note-hop-12] In the mid-18th century the Kolomak Woods near Poltava became a base of [haidamaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haidamak) (Cossack paramilitary bands).[^cite_note-hop-12]

By 1770, Poltava had several brick factories, a regimental doctor, and a pharmacy; that same year the city conducted four fairs.[^cite_note-hop-12] In 1775 it became a city of [Novorossiysk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiysk_Governorate), guarded by the 8th Company of the Dnieper Pike Regiment headquartered in [Kobeliaky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kobeliaky).[^cite_note-hop-12] In 1775 Poltava's Holy Cross Exaltation Monastery became the seat of bishops of the newly created [Eparchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eparchy) ([Diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese)) of Slaviansk and Kherson. This large new diocese included the lands of the [Novorossiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novorossiya) Governorate and the [Azov Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azov_Governorate) north of the [Black Sea](3386.md).[^cite_note-bio-16][^cite_note-theo-17]

Since much of that area had only recently been seized from the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) by Russia, and a large number of Orthodox Greek settlers had been invited to settle in the region, the [imperial government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) selected a renowned Greek scholar, [Eugenios Voulgaris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eugenios_Voulgaris), to preside over the new diocese. After his retirement in 1779, he was replaced by another Greek theologian, [Nikephoros Theotokis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikephoros_Theotokis).[^cite_note-bio-16][^cite_note-theo-17]

In 1779 the city established the Poltava county school, which became its first secular educational institution.[^cite_note-hop-12] In 1787 [Catherine the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_the_Great) stopped in Poltava on the way from [Crimea](163045.md), escorted by [Grigori Potemkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigori_Potemkin), [Alexander Suvorov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Suvorov) and [Mikhail Kutuzov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhail_Kutuzov).[^cite_note-hop-12] In Poltava, on 7 June 1787, before another [Russo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Turkish_War_\(1787%E2%80%9392\)), Potemkin received his title "Prince of Taurida", while Suvorov received a [snuffbox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snuffbox) with monogram.[^cite_note-hop-12] In 1802 the city became the seat of the newly established [Poltava Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Governorate).[^cite_note-hop-12] The city's population in 1802 consisted of some 8,000 residents.[^cite_note-hop-12] That same year Poltava opened a government-funded hospital of 20 beds.[^cite_note-hop-12]

### 19th century



Alexander Square in 1850

On 2 February 1808, the Poltava Male Gymnasium was established.[^cite_note-hop-12] On 20 June 1808 some 54 families of craftsmen were invited to the city from German principalities and settled in the newly established German Sloboda neighborhood with about 50 clay-made houses.[^cite_note-hop-12] In 1810 there were 8,328 people living in Poltava;[^cite_note-hop-12] that same year, the city's first theater was built.[^cite_note-hop-12] In August 1812, on orders of Little Russia Governor General [Lobanov-Rostovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakov_Lobanov-Rostovsky_\(1760%E2%80%931831\)), the famed Ukrainian writer and statesman [Ivan Kotlyarevsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Kotlyarevsky) formed the 5th Poltava Cavalry Cossack Regiment.[^cite_note-hop-12]

By 1860, Poltava had around 30,000 inhabitants, a district school, a gymnasium, an [Institute for Noble Maidens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_Noble_Maidens), a spiritual academy, a [cadet corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadet_corps), a library and a number of schools. In 1870, [Poltava railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava-Pivdenna_railway_station) was opened, leading to rapid economic growth in the region. However, by 1914 the Population of Poltava (around 60,000) was mostly working in small enterprises. In the late 19th and early 20th centuries Poltava became an important cultural centre, where many representatives of [Ukrainian national revival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_National_Revival) were active.



The 200th Anniversary celebrations of the Battle of Poltava in June 1909

### 20th century

#### Ukrainian War of Independence

During the [events of 1917–1920](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence), Poltava was under the rule of a number of governments, including the [Central Rada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Rada), [Hetmanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_State), [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic), [White Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Movement) and [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik). On 6 March 1918, Poltava became the seat of Poltavshchyna, a newly established [zemlia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_division_of_Ukraine_\(1918\)) of the Ukrainian People's Republic, which was disbanded on 29 April 1918 by [Hetman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetman_of_all_Ukraine) of Ukraine [Pavlo Skoropadsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Skoropadsky), who brought back old [governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governorate_\(Russia\)) divisions of the Russian Empire.

#### Soviet rule

From 1918 to 1919 Poltava [was occupied by the Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupation_of_Poltava_by_the_Bolsheviks_\(1918%E2%80%941919\)). After becoming a part of the [Ukrainian Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), it was the capital of the [Poltava Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Governorate).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] The city experienced accelerated industrial growth, and its population increased to 130,000 by 1939.

In [World War II](32927.md), the [German](21212.md) *[Wehrmacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht)* [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ukraine) Poltava from 18 September 1941 until 23 September 1943, when it was retaken during the Chernigov-Poltava Strategic Offensive of the [Battle of the Dnieper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Dnieper). During the German occupation the Jewish population (9.9% of the total population in 1939) was imprisoned in a [ghetto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_ghettos_established_by_Nazi_Germany) before being murdered during mass executions perpetrated by an [Einsatzgruppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Einsatzgruppen) and buried in mass graves in the area.[^cite_note-yadvashem-21] The Germans also operated a Nazi prison in the city.[^cite_note-22]



The [Poltava Museum of Long-Range and Strategic Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Museum_of_Long-Range_and_Strategic_Aviation)

By the summer of 1944, the [United States Army Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Army_Air_Forces) conducted a number of [shuttle bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuttle_bombing) raids against Nazi Germany under the name of [Operation Frantic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Frantic). [Poltava Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Air_Base), as well as [Myrhorod Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrhorod_Air_Base), were used as eastern locations for landing [B-17 Flying Fortress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_B-17_Flying_Fortress) heavy bombers involved in those operations.[^cite_note-23]

The post-war restoration of Poltava continued in the 1950s and 1960s. The city became an important centre of military education in the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), where missile and communications officers were prepared, and was also home to a [Soviet Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Force) division of heavy bombers.[^cite_note-24]

### Independent Ukraine

Until 18 July 2020, Poltava was designated as a [city of oblast significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_regional_significance_\(Ukraine\)) and did not belong to Poltava Raion even though it was the center of the raion. As part of the administrative reform of Ukraine, which reduced the number of raions of Poltava Oblast to four, the city was merged into Poltava Raion.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Government and subdivisions

See also: [List of mayors of Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Poltava)



City Hall

Poltava is the [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_\(political\)) of the [Poltava Oblast](1567650.md) ([province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oblast)) as well as of the Poltava Raion housed within the city. However, Poltava is a [city of oblast subordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Ukraine), thus being subject directly to the oblast authorities rather to the raion administration housed in the city itself.

Poltava's government consists of the 50-member Poltava City Council ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Полтавська Міська рада) which is headed by the Secretary (currently Oleksandr Kozub). The city's current mayor is Oleksandr Mamay, who was sworn in on 4 November 2010 after being elected with more than 61 percent of the vote.[^cite_note-37] In 2015 he was re-elected as a candidate of [Conscience of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscience_of_Ukraine) with 62.9% in a second round of Mayoral election.[^cite_note-38]

The territory of Poltava is divided into 3 [urban districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_districts_of_Ukraine):[^cite_note-39]

1. Shevchenkivskyi District,

   [^cite_note-40]

   [^cite_note-41]

   &#x20;to the south-west with an area of 2077 hectares and a population of 147,600 in 2005. It is a largely residential area and includes the city centre.

2. Kyivskyi District,

   [^cite_note-42]

   &#x20;is the largest by area, comprising 5437 hectares, or 52.8% of the city total, situated in the north and north-west. Its census in 2005 was 111,900. This district has a large industrial zone.

3. Podilskyi District,

   [^cite_note-43]

   &#x20;to the east and south-east, in the valley of the Vorskla River, with an area of 2988 hectares and a population of 53,700 in 2005.

The village of [Rozsoshentsi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rozsoshentsi), [Shcherbani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shcherbani?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Tereshky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tereshky,_Poltava_Oblast), [Kopyly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kopyly) and [Suprunivka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suprunivka?action=edit\&redlink=1) are officially considered to be outside the city, but constitute part of the Poltava agglomeration.

## Culture



Assumption Cathedral

The centre of the old city is a semicircular Neoclassical square with the [Tuscan column](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscan_column) of cast iron (1805–11), commemorating the centenary of the [Battle of Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poltava) and featuring 18 Swedish cannons captured in that battle. As [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_I_of_Russia) celebrated his victory in the Saviour church, this 17th-century wooden shrine was carefully preserved to this day. The five-domed city cathedral, dedicated to the [Exaltation of the Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exaltation_of_the_Cross), is a superb monument of [Cossack Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Baroque), built between 1699 and 1709. As a whole, the cathedral presents a unity which even the Neoclassical belltower has failed to mar. Another frothy Baroque church, dedicated to the Dormition of the Theotokos, was destroyed in 1934 and rebuilt in the 1990s.

A [minor planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_planet) [2983 Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2983_Poltava) discovered in 1981 by [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) astronomer [Nikolai Stepanovich Chernykh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nikolai_Stepanovich_Chernykh) is named after the city.[^cite_note-44]

## Economy and infrastructure

### Transportation



Poltava-Kyivska railway station

Poltava's transportation infrastructure consists of two major train stations: [Poltava-Pivdenna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava-Pivdenna_railway_station) and [Poltava-Kyivska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poltava_Kyivska_railway_station), with railway links to [Kyiv](585629.md), [Kharkiv](2788558.md), and [Kremenchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremenchuk). Poltava-Kyiv line is electrified and is used by the Poltava Express. The electrification of the Poltava-Kharkiv line was completed in August 2008.[^cite_note-45]

The Avtovokzal serves as the city's intercity bus station. Buses for local municipal routes depart from "AC-2" (autostation No. 2 – along [Shevchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko) Street) and "AC-3" (Zinkivska Street). Local municipal routes are parked along the [Taras Shevchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taras_Shevchenko) Street. [Marshrutka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marshrutka) minibuses serve areas where regular bus access is unavailable; however, they are privately owned and cost more per ride. In addition, a 10-route [trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) network of 72.6 kilometres (45.1 mi) runs throughout the city. On the routes of the city go more than 50 units of trolleybuses.

Poltava is also served by an International Airport, situated outside the city limits near the village of Ivashky. The international highway [M03](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Highways_\(Ukraine\)), linking Poltava with Kyiv and Kharkiv, passes through the southern outskirts of the city. There is also a regional highway P-17 crossing Poltava and linking it with Kremenchuk and [Sumy](561837.md).[^cite_note-46]

## Sports

The most popular sport is [football (soccer)](10568.md). Two professional football teams are based in the city: [Vorskla Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vorskla_Poltava) and [SC Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Poltava), there was also [FC Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Poltava) dissolved in 2018.
