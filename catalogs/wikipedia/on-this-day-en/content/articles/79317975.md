The **2026 United States House of Representatives elections in Missouri** will be held on November 3, 2026, to elect the eight [U.S. representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._representative) from the [state](18618239.md) of [Missouri](19571.md), one from each of the state's eight [congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri's_congressional_districts). The elections will coincide with [other elections](78311563.md) to the House of Representatives, [elections](65785708.md) to the [United States Senate](24909346.md), and various [state and local elections](70357296.md). Primary elections took place on August 4, 2026.

## Background

Main article: [2026 Missouri Proposition A](80917394.md)

This section is an excerpt from [2026 Missouri Proposition A](80917394.md).\[[edit](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=2026_Missouri_Proposition_A\&action=edit)]



Map of Missouri's congressional districts used in the [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Missouri) and [2024 congressional elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_United_States_House_of_Representatives_elections_in_Missouri)\
[Interactive map version](79317975.md)



Map of Missouri's congressional districts subject to the veto referendum\
[Interactive map version](79317975.md)

[Proposition A](80917394.md) will appear on the November 3, 2026, ballot in the state of Missouri as a [veto referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_referendum). Voters will decide whether [congressional districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri's_congressional_districts) that the [Missouri General Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri_General_Assembly) adopted in 2025 will or will not take effect.

In September 2025, Missouri lawmakers passed—and Missouri governor [Mike Kehoe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Kehoe) signed into law—an act changing the state's congressional districts. The new districts dismantled the strongly democratic leaning [5th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missouri's_5th_congressional_district).&#x20;

However, before the map took effect, it faced a lawsuit and a veto referendum. The lawsuit argued the new map was unconstitutional, and violated the part of Missouri's Constitution stating that redistricting had to occur after a [United States census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_census). But the Missouri Supreme Court disagreed, ruling 4–3 that the state constitution requires redistricting to happen after a census, but does not otherwise explicitly prohibit it.

The veto referendum, in which voters decide whether to pass or reject a law, was initiated by a group named People Not Politicians Missouri after they gathered over 300,000 signatures. Although the number of signatures submitted was sufficient, Missouri Secretary of State [Denny Hoskins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denny_Hoskins) refused to place the measure on the ballot, stating that it was an [unconstitutional](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutionality) measure.

On September 3, 2026, the [Supreme Court of Missouri](1771393.md) ruled that the veto referendum was constitutional, and that it could be held on redistricting, ordering the Secretary of State to use the enacted map from 2022 in the November 2026 elections.[^cite_note-2026_Missouri_Proposition_A_\:6-1] Following the ruling, Missouri Attorney General [Catherine Hanaway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catherine_Hanaway) filed an emergency appeal to the US Supreme Court and filed a motion requesting the state Supreme Court to pause its ruling from taking effect while the appeal plays out.[^cite_note-2] However, U.S. Supreme Court Justice [Brett Kavanaugh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brett_Kavanaugh) denied this request on September 8, keeping the 2022 map in effect for the 2026 election.[^cite_note-3] However, the same day, Chief U.S. District Judge [Stephen Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_R._Clark) of the [Eastern District of Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Eastern_District_of_Missouri) issued a [temporary restraining order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temporary_restraining_order) in a separate lawsuit prohibiting the state from using anything but the new 2025 map.[^cite_note-4] On September 10, the U.S. Supreme Court granted an emergency stay of Judge Clark's order, putting the 2022 map back into place for the 2026 elections.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] On September 21, the [United States Court of Appeals for the Eighth Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Court_of_Appeals_for_the_Eighth_Circuit), ruling on the merits, affirmed the temporary restraining order and remanded the case to the lower court for entry of a [permanent injunction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_injunction) enjoining Missouri to use the 2025 map, but stayed their ruling for a week pending appeal to the U.S. Supreme Court.[^cite_note-7] On September 25, the U.S. Supreme Court granted an emergency stay of the Eighth Circuit's order and Judge Clark's injunction, barring either court from issuing any new orders requiring the use of the 2025 map.[^cite_note-8]
