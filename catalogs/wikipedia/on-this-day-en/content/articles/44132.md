'God's Height'[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]) is a city in the central [West Bank](33209.md), [Palestine](241405.md). It serves as the administrative capital of Palestine, as well as capital of the [Ramallah and al-Bireh Governorate](12007076.md). The city is situated on the [Judaean Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judaean_Mountains), 10 km (6 miles) north of [Jerusalem](16043.md), at an average elevation of 872 meters (2,861 ft) above sea level, adjacent to [al-Bireh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Bireh).[^cite_note-\:0-8]

Ramallah has buildings containing masonry from the period of [Herod the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_the_Great), but no complete building predates the [Crusades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusades) of the 11th century.[^cite_note-\:0-8] In the 14th century, during [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate) rule (1260–1516), Ramallah was a mixed Christian and Muslim village whose residents engaged in orchard farming and whose revenues belonged to an [endowment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqf) for the [Cave of the Patriarchs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_of_the_Patriarchs) in [Hebron](38577.md). It declined toward the end of Mamluk rule, but was resettled in the early 16th century, at first by small numbers of local Muslims then in significant numbers by Christians from Palestine and Transjordan. The local tradition ascribes modern Ramallah's founding at this time by the Hadaddin, Arab Christians who claimed descent from the [Ghassanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghassanids), which partially correlates with official Ottoman records. Its lands remained endowed through the late 19th century, though by then for the [Dome of the Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock).&#x20;

Palestine came under British rule in 1920 and Ramallah's economy and status improved during this period. The [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War) saw the entire West Bank, including Ramallah, [occupied and annexed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) by [Transjordan](7515964.md). Ramallah was later captured by [Israel](9282173.md) in the 1967 [Six-Day War](29328.md). Since the 1995 [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords), Ramallah has been governed by the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md), as part of [Area A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Area_A) in the West Bank. Ramallah has emerged as a key political, cultural, and economic center since then.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-Loungo-10] It houses various Palestinian governmental bodies, including the [Mukataa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukataa), the official residence of the [president of the Palestinian National Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Palestinian_National_Authority), the [Palestinian Legislative Council](40067464.md), and the headquarters of the [Palestinian Security Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Security_Services). It is also home to several museums and cultural centers, and has a notable nightlife scene. Historically, the city was a predominantly Christian town; however, the population of [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) has increased to constitute a majority of Ramallah's 38,998 residents by 2017,[^cite_note-pcbs2017-11] while Christians make up a significant minority.

## History

### Early history

[Ancient rock-cut tombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock-cut_tombs_in_ancient_Israel) have been found near Ramallah.[^cite_note-12] Located just south of the built-up area is [Tell en-Nasbeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tell_en-Nasbeh), an [archeological site](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site) where biblical [Mizpah in Benjamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizpah_in_Benjamin) is likely to have been located.[^cite_note-\:0-8]

Several Ramallah buildings [incorporate masonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spolia) dating back to the reign of [Herod the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herod_the_Great) (37–4 BCE).[^cite_note-\:0-8] [Potsherds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsherd) from the [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states)/[Ayyubid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty) and early Ottoman period have also been found there.[^cite_note-Finkelstein-13] Ramallah was mentioned as collateral of an 1186 agreement between [Guy of Lusignan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_of_Lusignan) and the [hospital in Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muristan).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201439-14] In a [Crusader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusader_states)-period manuscript from 1198,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201439-14] Ramallah was identified as *Ramalie*,[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] a farming settlement in the environs of [Jerusalem](16043.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201439-14] Remains of a building with an [arched](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arch) doorway from the Crusader era, called *al-Burj*, have been identified,[^cite_note-17] but the original use of the building is undetermined.[^cite_note-18]

### Mamluk period

Ramallah, along with many other villages in its vicinity, is mentioned in a corpus of [waqf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waqf) (endowment) documents stored in Jerusalem dating to the first decade of the 14th century, during [Mamluk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate) rule in Palestine (1260–1516).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERapoport202593-19] The waqf was most likely established by Sultan [Qalawun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qalawun) in c. 1280.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201440-20] While some of the villages' agricultural output was earmarked for the [Haram al-Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haram_al-Sharif) (Temple Mount) of Jerusalem, Ramallah's revenues were for the benefit of the [Ibrahimi Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahimi_Mosque) (Cave of the Patriarchs) of [Hebron](38577.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERapoport202593-19] The document concerning Ramallah was an acknowledgement by twelve of the village's *[fellahin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fellahin)* (peasants) of their mutually guaranteed and equally shared obligation to pay 950 [dirhams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirham) for the Hebron waqf from the sale revenues of their apples, olives and cotton; the villagers also cultivated grain and [vetch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vetch). Ramallah's reliance on orchard farming distinguished it from many of the other villages named in the waqf corpus (all located in its environs or in the Hebron area) and implies there was a greater degree of investment and private ownership by Ramallah's residents.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERapoport2025105-21] The names of the villagers shows a mixed population of Muslims and Christians, with historian Yossef Rapoport speculating Ramallah had a Christian-majority population at the time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTERapoport2025106-22][^cite_note-24] Ramallah was referenced as the eastern boundary of [Ein Qiniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ein_Qiniya) in a 1330 waqf designated by the Mamluk viceroy of Syria, [Tankiz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tankiz), for the [Tankiziyya Madrasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tankiziyya_Madrasa) of Jerusalem. The waning years of Mamluk rule were characterized economic and political upheaval and Ramallah may have been deserted during this period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201440-20]

### Ottoman period

The area of Ramallah was incorporated into the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) in 1516 with all of [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(region\)). A 1525 Ottoman tax register confirms Ramallah's revenues as earmarked for the Ibrahimi Mosque waqf but lists it as an uninhabited farm.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201441-25] Ottoman tax registers from 1531 to 1532 and 1538 and Jerusalem court records from 1541 and 1548 all indicate Ramallah was resettled by this period, its revenues divided between waqfs for the [Dome of the Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome_of_the_Rock), the Ibrahimi Mosque and the Tankiziyya Madrasa. Its population consisted of a few Muslim families, including the Labud from Ein Qiniya and Abu al-Thanaya.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201441–42-26][^cite_note-27]

Christian families began settling Ramallah in significant numbers by the mid-16th century, as evidenced by Ottoman tax registers and oral traditions among Ramallah's Christians. The tax register of 1553–1554 showed the Christian village of [Beit Jala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Jala) swelled with new residents referred to as the 'Christian Kasabra group', while the 1562 register shows that many from the Kasabra and 27 other households and eight Christian bachelors relocated from Beit Jala to Ramallah by that time. There, they joined the ten Muslim families still living in the village, including the Labud.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201443–47-28] The 1562 record also shows an increase in tax revenue, all of which emanated from wheat and barley and one quarter of which was designated for the waqf.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201447-29] The 'Kasabra group' referred to Christians from the region of [Karak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Karak) in modern southern Jordan who migrated to the area of al-Kusbar in the Hebron hill country near the village of [Nuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuba,_Hebron) sometime after 1539 before moving to localities across Palestine.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201444–45-30][^cite_note-31] A 1561 Jerusalem court document shows responsibility for cultivating Ramallah's waqf lands was split into three tracts, one to the Labud and Abu al-Thanaya families, one to the Kasabra Christians led by Ghunaym ibn Salim and one to other Christians, presumably not belonging to the Kasabra.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201448-32]

The Christian migration from Karak to Ramallah, as traced in the 16th-century tax records, largely correlate with the local traditions of Ramallah's history.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201438,_43-33][^cite_note-35] This tradition attributes Ramallah's founding by five sons of Rashid ibn Saqr al-Haddadin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201438,_43-33][^cite_note-37] According to local historian Khalil Abu Rayya, the Haddadin (Arabic for 'blacksmiths') originated in the village of [Ma'in](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'in,_Jordan?action=edit\&redlink=1) near [Madaba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madaba) in the general vicinity of Karak, and the British orientalist [Frederick Peake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Peake) noted the Haddadin were one of the oldest Christian clans of Karak, claiming descent from the [Ghassanids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghassanids), an ancient/medieval Arab Christian tribal confederation. The local tradition of Ramallah holds the Haddadin first migrated to [Halhul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halhul) for several months then to Beit Jala and ultimately to Ramallah, with Halhul likely corresponding to al-Kusbar (which was located in Halhul's environs).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201444-38] Likewise, names of the Kasabra Christians recorded in the tax registers correspond to the local tradition.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHammoudeh201448-32][^cite_note-40] In 1596, the last known Ottoman tax register of Palestinian villages until the 19th century, Ramallah was listed as a village in the *[nahiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahiye_\(Ottoman\))* of Quds (Jerusalem), part of the *[Liwa of Quds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Sanjak)* with a population of 71 Christian households and 9 Muslim households. It paid a fixed tax rate of 25% on wheat, barley, olives, vines or fruit trees, and goats or beehives; a total of 9,400 [akçe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ak%C3%A7e). All of the revenue went to the waqf.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEHütterothAbdulfattah1977121-41]

In 1838, American biblical scholar [Edward Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Robinson_\(scholar\)) visited the area, noting that the inhabitants were Christian "of the Greek rite". There were 200 taxable men, which gives an estimated total population of 800–900 people. The village "belonged" to the [Haram al-Sharif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haram_al-Sharif), Jerusalem, to which it paid an annual tax of 350 Mids of grain.[^cite_note-42] In 1883, the [PEF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Exploration_Fund)'s *[Survey of Western Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PEF_Survey_of_Palestine)* described Ramallah as

> A large Christian village, of well-built stone houses, standing on a high ridge, with a view on the west extending to the sea. It stands amongst gardens and olive-yards, and has three springs to the south and one on the west; on the north there are three more, within a mile from the village. On the east there is a well. There are rock-cut tombs to the north-east with well-cut entrances, but completely blocked with rubbish. In the village is a Greek church, and on the east a Latin convent and a Protestant schoolhouse, all modern buildings. The village lands are [Wakuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wakuf), or ecclesiastical property, belonging to the Haram of Jerusalem. About a quarter of the inhabitants are Roman Catholics, the rest Orthodox Greeks.[^cite_note-43]

### British Mandate



[Ramallah Friends Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramallah_Friends_Schools), 1937

During [World War I](4764461.md), the [British Army](4887.md) captured and [occupied](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied_Enemy_Territory_Administration) Ramallah in December 1917. The city remained occupied until the designation of the [Palestine Mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine) in 1920, resulting in Ramallah falling under [British Mandatory control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Palestine) until 1948. In the 1920s, the economy of Ramallah started to improve, resulting in the local Arab [upper class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_class) (consisting primarily of landowners and merchants) ordering the construction of several multi-storied villas, many of which still stand today.[^cite_note-44] In 1939, the Jerusalem Electric Company introduced electricity to Ramallah, and a majority of the city's homes became wired shortly thereafter. On the same year, the British Mandatory authorities inaugurated the state-owned [Palestine Broadcasting Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Broadcasting_Service) in Ramallah, with [BBC](19344654.md) members training local radio staff to deliver daily broadcasts in Arabic, [Hebrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebrew_language), and English. The station was later renamed [Jerusalem Calling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_Calling).

The city became a center of insurgent activity when the [1936–39 Arab revolt in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936%E2%80%9339_Arab_revolt_in_Palestine) broke out. The rebels subsequently established a court near Ramallah, in order to provide legal alternatives to the courts of the British Mandate. One British schoolteacher noted that the Ramallah court judge began to produce "news sheets on typewriters and duplicators, aimed at publicizing the alternative rebel regime."[^cite_note-45]

### Jordanian and Israeli occupation

Following the [creation of the State of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence) and the [ensuing conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War), Jordan seized part of the territory, which they named the West Bank, which included Ramallah. Jordan annexed the West Bank, applying its national law to the conquered territory. The West Bank was relatively peaceful during the years of [Jordanian occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_annexation_of_the_West_Bank) between 1948 and 1967, with its residents enjoying freedom of movement between the [West Bank](33209.md), Jordan, [Lebanon](17771.md), and Syria. However, many Palestinians were arrested and jailed for being members of "illegal political parties", which included the [Palestine Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Communist_Party), and other socialist and pro-independence groups. The city's population had doubled by 1953, but the economy and infrastructure could not accommodate the influx of poor villagers. Natives of Ramallah began to emigrate, primarily to the United States. About one fourth of Ramallah's 6,000 natives had left by 1956, with Arabs from the surrounding towns and villages (particularly [Hebron](38577.md)) buying the homes and land the émigrés left behind.



2018 [United Nations](1602284.md) map of the area, showing the [Israeli occupation](59076253.md) arrangements

During the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967, [Israel](9282173.md) captured Ramallah from Jordan, imposing a military closure and conducting a census a few weeks later. Every person registered in the census was given an Israeli identity card which allowed the bearer to continue to reside there. Those who were abroad during the census lost their residency rights.[^cite_note-46] Because of Israeli control of the West Bank and Gaza Strip, for the first time in 19 years, residents of Ramallah could freely visit the [Gaza Strip](12047.md), as well as Israel, and engage in commerce there.

Unlike the Jordanians, Israel did not offer citizenship to the residents, who remained Jordanian citizens until 1988. Ramallah residents were issued permits to work in Israel, but did not gain the rights associated with Israeli citizenship. The city remained under Israeli military rule for more than four decades. The [Israeli Civil Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Civil_Administration) (CA), established in 1981, was in charge of civilian and day-to-day services such as issuing permission to travel, build, export or import, and host relatives from abroad.[^cite_note-47] The CA reprinted Jordanian textbooks for distribution in schools but did not update them. The CA was in charge of tax collection and land expropriation, which sometimes included Israeli seizure of olive groves that Arab villagers had tended for generations.[^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

According to the Israeli Human Rights activists, the development of Jewish settlements in the Ramallah area, such as [Beit El](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_El) and [Psagot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psagot), prevented the expansion of the city and cut it off from the surrounding Arab villages.[^cite_note-50] As resistance increased, Ramallah residents who were members of the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization) were jailed or deported to neighboring countries.[^cite_note-51] The popular uprising known as the [First Intifada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada) erupted in December 1987, protesting against the continued [Israeli occupation](59076253.md).

Ramallah residents were among the early joiners of the First Intifada. The Intifada Unified Leadership, an umbrella organization of various Palestinian factions, distributed weekly bulletins on the streets of Ramallah with a schedule of the daily protests, strikes and action against Israeli patrols in the city. At the demonstrations, tires were burned in the street, and the crowds threw stones and [Molotov cocktails](20922.md). The [IDF](39237.md) responded with tear gas and rubber bullets. Schools in Ramallah were forcibly shut down, and opened gradually for a few hours a day. The Israelis conducted house arrests, imposing curfews that restricted travel and exports in what Palestinians regarded as collective punishment. In response to the closure of schools, residents organized home schooling sessions to help students make up missed material; this became one of the few symbols of civil disobedience.[^cite_note-52] Following the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_Accords), the Israeli army abandoned the [Mukataa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukataa) in December 1995 and withdrew to the city outskirts. The newly-established [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) assumed civilian and security responsibility for the city, which was designated "Area A" under the accords.

### Palestinian Authority control

&#x20;The years between 1993 and 2000 (known locally as the "Oslo Years") brought relative prosperity to Ramallah. Ramallah and its immediate environs were classified as [Area A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_enclaves) in the [Oslo Accords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oslo_II_Accord), under full civil and security control of the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) (PA) administration in September 1995.[^cite_note-53] Many expatriates returned to establish businesses there, and the atmosphere was one of optimism. In 2000, unemployment began to rise and the economy of Ramallah declined.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] The [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) remained in control of the territories and its government did not restore the freedom of movement enjoyed by Ramallah residents prior to the first Intifada. Travel to [Jerusalem](16043.md) required special permits. The number and size of [Israeli settlements](15123.md) around Ramallah increased dramatically. A network of bypass roads for use of Israeli citizens only was built around Ramallah, and Israel expropriated land for settlements.[^cite_note-56][^cite_note-57] Many official documents previously handled by the Israeli Civil Administration were now handled by the Palestinian Authority but still required Israeli approval. A Palestinian passport issued to Ramallah residents was not valid unless the serial number was registered with the Israeli authorities, who controlled border crossings.[^cite_note-58]



[Israeli settlement](15123.md) of [Psagot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psagot) on *Jabal al-Tawil* (Tawil Hill), east of Ramallah and Al-Bireh (2004)

The failure of the [Camp David summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_David_2000_Summit) in July 2000 led to the outbreak of the [Second Intifada](71717.md) (al-Aqsa Intifada) in September 2000. Young Ramallah residents demonstrated daily against the Israeli army, with marches to the Israeli checkpoints at the outskirts of the city. Over time, the marches were replaced by sporadic use of live ammunition against Israeli soldiers; and various attacks targeting Jewish settlers, particularly on the Israeli-only bypass roads. Army checkpoints were established to restrict movement in and out of Ramallah.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60][^cite_note-61] On October 12, 2000, two Israeli army reservists, Vadim Norzhich and Yosef Avrahami were [lynched in Ramallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_Ramallah_lynching). They had taken a wrong turn, and were set upon by a mob, enraged in particular by the [Muhammad al-Durrah incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muhammad_al-Durrah_incident) in Gaza. A frenzied crowd killed the two IDF reservists, mutilated their bodies, and dragged them through the streets.[^cite_note-62] Later that afternoon, the Israeli army carried out an air strike on Ramallah, demolishing the police station. Israel later succeeded in capturing and prosecuting some of those involved in the deaths of the reservists.



[Mukataa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukataa) in 2013

The IDF has occasionally operated inside Ramallah, in breach of the 1995 Oslo Accords. The first and largest incursion was the 2002 [Operation Defensive Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Defensive_Shield), with a more recent intervention coming in March 2017 while attempting to arrest a suspected terrorist.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-International_Business_Times1-64] In 2002, the army imposed curfews, electricity cuts, school closures and disruptions of commercial life.[^cite_note-65] Many Ramallah institutions, including government ministries, were vandalized, and equipment was destroyed or stolen.[^cite_note-66] The IDF took over local Ramallah television stations, and social and economic conditions deteriorated.[^cite_note-67] Many expatriates left, as did many other Palestinians who complained that the living conditions had become intolerable.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] Construction of the [Israeli West Bank barrier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_West_Bank_barrier) has added to Ramallah's isolation. [Yasser Arafat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yasser_Arafat) established his West Bank headquarters, the [Mukataa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mukataa), in Ramallah. Although considered an interim solution, Ramallah became the *de facto* capital of the Palestinian Authority, now officially known as the State of Palestine. It hosts almost all governmental headquarters. In December 2001, Arafat held meetings at the Mukataa, but lived with his wife and daughter in [Gaza City](55387.md). After suicide bombings in [Haifa](55606.md), Arafat was confined to the Ramallah compound. In 2002, the compound was partly demolished by the Israeli Defense Forces and Arafat's building was cut off from the rest of the compound. On November 11, 2004, Arafat died at the [Percy training hospital of the Armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percy_training_hospital_of_the_Armies) near Paris. He was buried in the courtyard of the Mukataa on November 12, 2004. The site still serves as the Ramallah headquarters of the Palestinian Authority, as well the official West Bank office of [Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md). Throughout 2005, while the [Disengagement Plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_disengagement_from_Gaza) was underway, some US government officials suggested to the Palestinian leadership to move the provisional capital back to Gaza, where it had been when the Palestinian Authority was first established in 1994. President Abbas, however, refrained from doing so, arguing that at this point, it was important to keep the administrative center in the West Bank in order to remind the international community that the West Bank was still awaiting a territorial solution.[^cite_note-71]



Residential neighborhood in Ramallah, 2005

In December 2005, local elections were held in Ramallah in which candidates from three different factions competed for a four-year term on the fifteen-seat municipal council. The council elected [Janet Mikhail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janet_Mikhail) as [mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Ramallah), the first woman to hold the post.[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

Munir Hamdan, a member of [Fatah](11104.md) and a Ramallah businessman, discussed the concentration of government offices with a journalist. He said, "The president and prime minister have their offices here.[^cite_note-Toameh-74] So do the parliament and all the government ministries", representing a "collusion" between the Palestinian Authority and Israel to turn Ramallah into the political as well as the financial capital of the Palestinians. He is particularly worried by the construction of a large new governmental complex by the PA.[^cite_note-Toameh-74] Hatem Abdel Kader, a Jerusalem resident, Fatah legislator and former Minister for Jerusalem Affairs, complained that "If they are building a new government compound here, that means they have no plans to be based in Jerusalem... Unfortunately, the Palestinian government of [Salam Fayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salam_Fayyad) has abandoned Jerusalem in favor of Ramallah."[^cite_note-Toameh-74] In November 2011, King [Abdullah II of Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_II_of_Jordan) visited Ramallah for the first time since 2000.[^cite_note-75]

## Geography and climate



Map of Mediterranean with the Köppen Climate Classifications: Csa and Csb are noted in yellow.



[Deir Ghassana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deir_Ghassana) near Ramallah

This area enjoys a [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) climate of a dry summer and mild, rainy winter with occasional snowfall. The recorded average of Ramallah's rainfall is about 615 mm (24 in)[^cite_note-Climate-76] and minimum rainfall is 307 mm (12 in) and maximum rainfall is 1,591 mm (63 in).

The [Köppen climate classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification) places Ramallah in the [Csa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate) category. Climates of this class generally occur on the western sides of continents between the latitudes of 30° and 45°. These climates are in the polar front region in winter, and thus have moderate temperatures and changeable, rainy weather. Summers are hot and dry, due to the domination of the subtropical high pressure systems, except in the immediate coastal areas, where summers are milder due to the nearby presence of cold ocean currents that may bring fog but prevent rain.

## Economy



Construction of Rawabi and industrial area



[Ammar Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ersal_Commercial_Center) in Ramallah

Ramallah is the financial and commercial center of the Palestinian Authority, and is home to the country's numerous financial institutions.[^cite_note-\:1-79] Currently, Ramallah is the seat of power of the Palestinian Authority, with most of its offices located within the city.[^cite_note-\:1-79] The city also serves as the headquarters for most international NGOs and embassies.[^cite_note-\:1-79] Hundreds of millions of dollars in aid flowing into the city have greatly boosted Ramallah's economy since the end of the [Second Intifada](71717.md).[^cite_note-\:1-79] Ramallah's buoyant economy continues to draw Palestinians from other West Bank towns where jobs are fewer.[^cite_note-\:1-79] The built-up area has grown fivefold since 2002.[^cite_note-\:1-79]

Construction boom is one of the most obvious signs of [West Bank](33209.md) economic growth, estimated at an annual rate of 8 percent.[^cite_note-\:2-80] This has been attributed to relative stability and Western donor support to the Palestinian Authority.[^cite_note-\:2-80] The [PIF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Investment_Fund) have begun work on a \$400 million commercial center comprising 13 towers which will be some of the tallest in Ramallah.[^cite_note-\:2-80] The [Ersal Commercial Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ersal_Commercial_Center) has drawn investment from a Saudi Arabian firm, The Land Holding, which has a 10% stake.[^cite_note-\:2-80] It is not the only Gulf Arab firm investing in Ramallah and its outskirts.[^cite_note-\:2-80] The Qatari Diar Real Estate Investment Company has a stake in [Rawabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawabi), a completely new town being constructed in the hills outside Ramallah at a cost of \$1 billion.[^cite_note-\:2-80]

Ramallah has highest concentration of high-tech companies.[^cite_note-\:3-81] ASAL technologies, an information technology company in Ramallah, has 120 employees and is looking forward to "exponential growth".[^cite_note-82] In collaboration with the [Republic of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_India), a new tech park named, the India Palestine Techno Park is located in [Birzeit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birzeit).[^cite_note-\:3-81] [Apple Inc](856.md) operates a research & development center in Rawabi with ASAL Technologies.[^cite_note-83] A large number multinational companies operates facilities in Ramallah, which outsource Palestinians.[^cite_note-\:3-81]

By 2010, Ramallah had become the leading center of economic and political activity in the territories under the control of the Palestinian Authority.[^cite_note-Toameh-74] During a building boom in the early years of the 21st century, apartment buildings and "five-star" hotels were erected, particularly in the [Al-Masyoun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Masyoun) neighborhood.[^cite_note-Toameh-74] In 2010, "more than one hundred" Palestinian businesses were reported to have moved to Ramallah from [East Jerusalem](343508.md), because "Here they pay less taxes and have more customers."[^cite_note-Toameh-74] One local boasted to a journalist that "Ramallah is becoming the de facto capital of Palestine."[^cite_note-Toameh-74] This boast was seconded by *[The New York Times](30680.md)* which, in 2010, called Ramallah the "de facto capital of the West Bank".[^cite_note-Loungo-10] According to [Sani Meo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sani_Meo), the publisher of *[This Week in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/This_Week_in_Palestine)*, "Capital or no capital, Ramallah has done well and Palestine is proud of its achievements."[^cite_note-Toameh-74] Some Palestinians allege that Ramallah's prosperity is part of an Israeli "conspiracy" to make the city the capital of a Palestinian state instead of Jerusalem.[^cite_note-Toameh-74]

An [Ottoman](22278.md) village list of about 1870 showed that Ramallah had 249 houses and a population of 635, though the population count included men only. The village was described as being in the *Bire* area, "north of [Mikhmas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikhmas), on a rocky hill."[^cite_note-Socin-93][^cite_note-94][^cite_note-Arieh90-84] In 1896, the population of Ramallah was estimated to be about 2,061 persons.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-Arieh90-84]

In the [1922 census of Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1922_census_of_Palestine) conducted by the [British Mandate authorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Palestine), Ramallah had a population of 3,104 (2,972 [Christians](18337522.md), 125 [Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim), and seven Jews[^cite_note-Census1922-85][^cite_note-96]), the Christians consisting of 2,162 Orthodox, 1 [Syriac Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syriac_Orthodox_Church) (Jacobite), 332 [Roman Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholics), 144 Greek Catholic ([Melkite Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Catholic)), 211 [Church of England](5955.md), and 122 "other".[^cite_note-Census1922b-97] The population increased at the time of the [1931 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1931_census_of_Palestine) to 4,286 (3,766 Christians, 519 Muslims and one Jew) in a total of 1,014 houses.[^cite_note-Census1931-86] In the 1938 village statistics, the population is listed as 4,900.[^cite_note-vs1938-87] In the [1945 statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_Statistics,_1945), the population stood at 5,080,[^cite_note-vs1945-88] with Christians forming the majority of the population (4,440 Christians and 640 Muslims).

However, the demographic makeup of the town changed drastically under Jordanian occupation, when considerable emigration of Christians took place. This left slightly more than half of the city's 12,134 inhabitants Christian by 1967, and the remainder Muslim.[^cite_note-britannica-90]

Ramallah expatriates created one of the largest Arab communities in the United States, settling mainly in [Washington](13015878.md), New York, Florida, California, Texas, and especially in [Michigan](18859.md). Many worked in the auto industry. In 1959, the American Federation of Ramallah, Palestine (AFRP) was established in [Detroit](8687.md). The AFRP has several branches in the United States, and holds an annual convention every summer attended by a sizable number of former Ramallah residents and their offspring.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

Ramallah's population drastically decreased in the late 20th century from 24,722 inhabitants in 1987 to 17,851 in 1997. In the [Palestinian Central Bureau of Statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_Central_Bureau_of_Statistics) (PCBS) census in 1997, [Palestinian refugees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_refugee) accounted for 60.3% of the population, which was 17,851.[^cite_note-pcbs1997-91] There were 8,622 males and 9,229 females. People younger than 20 years of age made up 45.9% of the population, while those aged between 20 and 64 were 45.4%, and residents aged over 64 constituted 4.7%.[^cite_note-100]

Only in 2005 did the population reach more than 24,000. In a PCBS projection in 2006, Ramallah had a population of 25,467 inhabitants.[^cite_note-101] In the 2007 PCBS census, there were 27,460 people living in the city.[^cite_note-PCBS07-92] Sources vary about the current Christian population in the city, ranging around 25%.[^cite_note-hall-102][^cite_note-keulemans-103]

## Religion

The [Jamal Abdel Nasser Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamal_Abdel_Nasser_Mosque) is one of the city's largest [mosques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosques). The [Orthodox Church of Ramallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Church_of_Ramallah), an [Orthodox Christian](10186.md) [convent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convent), [Melkite Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Catholic_Church), [Evangelical Lutheran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical_Lutheran) Church, Arab [Episcopal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Episcopal_Church_in_Jerusalem_and_the_Middle_East) ([Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican)) Church, Ramallah Local Church ([Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelical)/[Born Again](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Born_again_\(Christianity\))) and Ramallah [Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptist) Church all operate schools in the city.[^cite_note-RamMuniRel-105]

### Christian presence



An old Christian cemetery in Ramallah

Ramallah grew dramatically throughout the 17th and 18th centuries as an agricultural village, attracting more (predominantly Christian) inhabitants from all around the region.[^cite_note-visit-106] In 1700, Yacoub Elias was the first Ramallah native to be ordained by the [Greek Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Church) of Jerusalem, the Christian denomination that prevailed in the Holy Land at the time. In the early 19th century, the first Jerusalemite Greek Orthodox Christian church was built. Later, in 1852, the [Greek Orthodox Church of the Transfiguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Transfiguration,_Ramallah), was built to replace it; it is the sole [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md) in Ramallah today.[^cite_note-visit-106]

There is also a [Melkite Greek Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Church) in Ramallah, built in 1895.[^cite_note-visit-106] The [Roman Catholic Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Church) also established its presence in Ramallah the 19th century and constitutes today the second-largest Christian denomination in the city. The Roman Catholic Church established the St. Joseph's Girls' School run by St. Joseph sisters, as well as the co-educational Al-Ahliyyah College high school run by Rosary sisters. In 1913, construction of the Catholic [Holy Family Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Family_Church,_Ramallah) was started.

As of 2022, Ramallah also has a [Coptic Orthodox Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church),[^cite_note-107] an [Evangelical Lutheran Church](23371382.md)[^cite_note-108] and an [Episcopalian (Anglican) Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism).[^cite_note-109] In the 19th century, the [Religious Society of Friends](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers) ([Quakers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quakers)) established a presence in Ramallah and built the [Ramallah Friends Schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramallah_Friends_Schools), one for girls and later a boys' school, to alleviate the dearth of education for women and girls. Eli and Sybil Jones opened "The Girls Training Home of Ramallah" in 1869. A medical clinic was established in 1883, with Dr. George Hassenauer serving as the first doctor in Ramallah. In 1889, the girls academy became the Friends Girls School (FGS). As the FGS was also a boarding school, it attracted a number of girls from surrounding communities, including Jerusalem, [Lydda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydda), [Jaffa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaffa), and [Beirut](37428.md). The Friends Boys School (FBS) was founded in 1901 and opened in 1918. The Quakers opened a Friends Meeting House for worship in the city center in 1910.[^cite_note-110] According to the school's official website, most high school students choose to take the [International Baccalaureate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Baccalaureate) exams (IBE) instead of the traditional "Tawjihi" university exams.[^cite_note-RamMuniRel-105][^cite_note-111]

The activity of foreign churches in Palestine in the late 19th century increased awareness of prosperity in the West. In Ramallah and [Bethlehem](4312.md), a few miles south, local residents began to seek economic opportunity overseas. In 1901, merchants from Ramallah emigrated to the United States and established import-export businesses, selling handmade rugs and other exotic wares across the Atlantic. Increased trade dramatically improved living standards for Ramallah's inhabitants. American cars, mechanized farming equipment, radios, and later televisions became attainable luxuries for upper-class families. As residents of Jaffa and Lydda moved to Ramallah, the balance of Muslims and Christians began to change.

In the 21st century, a large community of people with direct descent from the Haddadins who founded Ramallah live in the United States. The town is now predominately Muslim, but still contains a Christian minority. The change in demographics is due mostly to new migration of Muslims to the area, and emigration of Christians from the area.[^cite_note-laoc-36]

## Culture

Ramallah is generally considered the most affluent and cultural, as well as the most liberal, of all Palestinian cities,[^cite_note-112][^cite_note-113] and is home to a number of popular Palestinian activists, poets, artists, and [musicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Palestine).

In 2004, the Ramallah Cultural Palace opened in the city. The only cultural center of its kind in the Palestinian-governed areas, it houses a 736-seat auditorium, as well as conference rooms, exhibit halls, and movie-screening rooms. It was a joint venture of the Palestinian Authority, the [United Nations Development Programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Development_Programme) (UNDP), and the Japanese government.[^cite_note-114] Ramallah hosted its first annual international film festival in 2004.

### Palestinian costume

Main article: [Palestinian traditional costumes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_traditional_costumes)

Until the 1940s, traditional Palestinian costumes reflected a woman's economic and marital status and her town or district of origin, with knowledgeable observers discerning this information from the [fabric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabric), colours, [cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cut_\(clothing\)), and [embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery) motifs (or lack thereof) used in the apparel.[^cite_note-Grutz-115]

Villages in the Levant under Ottoman rule remained isolated due to the difficulty of travel in the 19th century. As a result, clothing and accessories became a statement of region. In Ramallah, the back panels of dresses often incorporated a palm tree motif embroidered in cross-stitch.[^cite_note-116]
