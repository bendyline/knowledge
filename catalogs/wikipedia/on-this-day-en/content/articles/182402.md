**Warmian–Masurian Voivodeship** ([Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_language): *Województwo warmińsko-mazurskie*, pronounced [\[vɔjɛˈvut͡stfɔ varˈmij̃skɔ maˈzurskʲɛ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Polish) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/45/LL-Q809\_%28pol%29-Poemat-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_warmi%C5%84sko-mazurskie.wav/LL-Q809\_%28pol%29-Poemat-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_warmi%C5%84sko-mazurskie.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/4/45/LL-Q809_%28pol%29-Poemat-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_warmi%C5%84sko-mazurskie.wav/LL-Q809_%28pol%29-Poemat-wojew%C3%B3dztwo_warmi%C5%84sko-mazurskie.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) is a [voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voivodeships_of_Poland) ([province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Poland)) in northeastern [Poland](22936.md). Its capital and largest city is [Olsztyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsztyn). The voivodeship has an area of 24,192 km<sup>2</sup> (9,341 sq mi) and in 2019 had a population of 1,425,967.

Warmian–Masurian Voivodeship was created on 1 January 1999 from the entire [Olsztyn Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsztyn_Voivodeship), the western half of [Suwałki Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suwa%C5%82ki_Voivodeship), and part of [Elbląg Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbl%C4%85g_Voivodeship), pursuant to the [Polish local government reforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_local_government_reforms) adopted in 1998. The province's name derives from two historic regions, [Warmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warmia) and [Masuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masuria), although also parts of other regions are located within the province, i.e. of [Chełmno Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Che%C5%82mno_Land), [Powiśle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Powi%C5%9Ble_\(region\)), [Mazovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazovia), [Bartia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartia) and [Natangia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natangia).

The province borders [Podlaskie Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podlaskie_Voivodeship) to the east, [Masovian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masovian_Voivodeship) to the south, [Kuyavian-Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuyavian-Pomeranian_Voivodeship) to the southwest, [Pomeranian Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomeranian_Voivodeship) to the west, the [Vistula Lagoon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vistula_Lagoon) to the northwest, and the [Kaliningrad Oblast](17311.md) (an [exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave) of Russia) to the north.

## History

The region was originally inhabited by several pagan [Old Prussian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Prussians) clans, including the [Bartians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartians), [Pogesanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogesanians) and [Warmians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warmians), from whom the name *Warmia* originated. During the [Northern Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Crusades), the Old Prussians were conquered by the Catholic [Teutonic Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teutonic_Order); their land was granted to the order by the pope, and the region became part of the theocratic [State of the Teutonic Order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_of_the_Teutonic_Order). The Order encouraged colonization by German settlers in Warmia (*[Ostsiedlung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostsiedlung)*) and Polish colonists from the region of [Masovia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masovia), called [Masurians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masurians) (*Mazurzy*), hence the name [Masuria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masuria). The Old Prussians were heavily decimated during the crusade and the following revolt. What remained of them became Christianized and assimilated into the newcomers and thus became extinct.



[Battle of Grunwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Grunwald), painting by [Jan Matejko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jan_Matejko) (1878)

During the Teutonic rule, the region experienced a process of urbanization and economic boost due to the expansion of the [Hanseatic League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanseatic_League) into the region. The Order later attacked their former ally [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Poland_\(1025-1385\)) and conquered the region of [Pomerelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomerelia), beginning a long-lasting conflict with Poland, which subsequently entered into an alliance with [Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania). In Masuria, the Poles and Lithuanians defeated the Order at the [Battle of Grunwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Grunwald). The wars eventuated in a rebellion by the urban populations of Pomerelia and Warmia, who were affected by the Teutons' numerous wars; upon the urban populations' request, the region was incorporated into Poland by King [Casimir IV Jagiellon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casimir_IV_Jagiellon), and after the [Thirteen Years' War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteen_Years'_War_\(1454%E2%80%931466\)) it remained under Polish suzerainty, but was divided into two parts: [Elbląg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbl%C4%85g) and [Warmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Warmia) were incorporated directly into the Kingdom of Poland, while Masuria became a Polish [fief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief) under the control of the Teutonic Order, also considered an integral part of "one and indivisible" Kingdom of Poland.[^cite_note-3]



[Elbląg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbl%C4%85g) was one of the main port cities of the [Kingdom of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_of_the_Kingdom_of_Poland) and one of the largest and most influential cities in the entire [Polish–Lithuanian Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish%E2%80%93Lithuanian_Commonwealth).

The state of the Teutonic Order ceased to exist in 1525 when Grandmaster [Albert Hohenzollern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albert,_Duke_of_Prussia) introduced secularisation, proclaimed the [Duchy of Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Prussia), and became a vassal of [Sigismund I of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sigismund_I_of_Poland). The Prussian Hohenzollern line became extinct in 1618 with the death of Albert Frederick, and the Duchy was inherited by the Brandenburgian line; Prussia simultaneously entered into a [personal union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_union) with the electorate of Brandenburg known as [Brandenburg-Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brandenburg-Prussia), remaining under Polish suzerainty until the [Treaty of Oliva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Oliva) in 1660. The throne was inherited by Frederick I of Prussia who wanted to unite the Duchy with Brandenburg and also wanted to proclaim himself king of Prussia and therefore participated in the Russian-initiated [Partitions of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partitions_of_Poland) in which Warmia was annexed by the Kingdom of Prussia, and henceforth became part of the newly established province of [East Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Prussia). Together with the rest of the Kingdom, the region became part of the North German Confederation and the [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire).



Monument to victims of the German Nazi [Soldau concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldau_concentration_camp) in [Działdowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzia%C5%82dowo)

In 1914, the province turned into a battlefield, seeing notable battles such as the [Battle of Tannenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Tannenberg) as part of the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_I\)) of [World War I](4764461.md). Later, the region became part of the [Weimar Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weimar_Republic), and [Nazi Germany](21212.md), whereas some areas in the south became again part of Poland, following the restoration of its independence in 1918. In the interbellum, the Polish population was subjected to persecution from both the pre-Nazi and Nazi authorities of Germany. With the German [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) at the start of [World War II](32927.md) in 1939, the Germans eventually carried out mass arrests of local Poles, shut down or seized Polish newspapers and libraries,[^cite_note-4] and occupied the pre-war Polish areas of the present-day province, in which they established the [Soldau concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldau_concentration_camp), and carried out massacres of Poles, including at [Bratian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bratian), [Nawra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nawra,_Warmian-Masurian_Voivodeship), [Malinowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malinowo,_Dzia%C5%82dowo_County) and [Komorniki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Komorniki,_Warmian-Masurian_Voivodeship).[^cite_note-5] Several subcamps of the [Stutthof concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stutthof_concentration_camp) were located in the region. [Stalag I-B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stalag_I-B), a major [German prisoner-of-war camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) for Polish, Belgian, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_prisoners_of_war_in_World_War_II), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Military_Internees), Serbian and Soviet POWs,[^cite_note-6] and [Wolf's Lair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf's_Lair), [Adolf Hitler](2731583.md)'s first [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_II\)) military headquarters were located in Masuria. After the end of World War II, the German population was expelled in accordance with the [Potsdam Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potsdam_Agreement), whereas many Masurians emigrated in the following decades.

### Ethnic and religious structure

In year 1824, shortly before its [merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Prussia) with [West Prussia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Prussia), the population of East Prussia was 1,080,000 people.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlater182517-7] Of that number, according to [Karl Andree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_Andree), Germans were slightly more than half, while 280,000 (\~26%) were [ethnically Polish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people) and 200,000 (\~19%) were [ethnically Lithuanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prussian_Lithuanians),[^cite_note-Karl_Andree-8] however large portions of its German and Lithuanian populations lived in the northern half of the region, outside of the present Warmian–Masurian Voivodeship. As of year 1819 there were also some 2,400 Jews, according to Georg Hassel.[^cite_note-9] Similar numbers are given by [August von Haxthausen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/August_von_Haxthausen) in his 1839 book, with a breakdown by county.[^cite_note-Haxthausen-10] But the majority of East Prussian Polish and Lithuanian inhabitants were [Lutherans](23371382.md), not [Roman Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholicism) like their ethnic kinsmen across the border in the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire). Only in Southern [Warmia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warmia) [Catholic Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Poland) – so called [Warmiaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warmiak) (not to be confused with predominantly [Protestant Masurians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masurians)) – comprised the majority of population, numbering 26,067 people (\~81%) in [county Allenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landkreis_Allenstein) (Polish: [Olsztyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsztyn)) in 1837.[^cite_note-Haxthausen-10] Another minority in 19th-century East Prussia, were ethnically Russian Old Believers, also known as [Philipponnen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philipons) – their main town was Eckersdorf ([Wojnowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wojnowo,_Warmian-Masurian_Voivodeship)).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] The Polish population was subjected to [Germanisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanisation_of_Poles_during_the_Partitions) and *[Kulturkampf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kulturkampf)* policies.

In year 1817, East Prussia had 796,204 [Evangelical Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism), 120,123 [Roman Catholics](606848.md), 864 [Mennonites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mennonites) and 2,389 Jews.[^cite_note-14]

The Warmian–Masurian Voivodeship has the largest number of ethnic [Ukrainians living in Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_minority_in_Poland)[^cite_note-15] due to forced relocations (such as [Operation Vistula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Vistula)) carried out by the Soviet and [Polish Communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_Communist) authorities.

[Pasłęk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pas%C5%82%C4%99k) in the western part of the voivodeship is considered the first place in present-day Poland where [Dutch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dutch_people) immigrants settled (in 1297).[^cite_note-16] There is a claim that they were participants in the killing of [Floris V, Count of Holland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floris_V,_Count_of_Holland) in 1296, who then fled east, which is alluded to by Dutch poet [Joost van den Vondel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joost_van_den_Vondel) in his work *Gijsbrechcie van Aemstel* (1637).[^cite_note-17]

## Sights and tourism



Bust of [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus) with the [Olsztyn Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsztyn_Castle) in the background

Amongst the most visited sights is the [Masurian Lake District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masurian_Lake_District), which contains more than 2,000 lakes, including the largest lakes of Poland, [Śniardwy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Aniardwy) and [Mamry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Mamry). [Giżycko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gi%C5%BCycko), [Iława](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I%C5%82awa), [Mrągowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mr%C4%85gowo), [Mikołajki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miko%C5%82ajki) and [Ruciane-Nida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruciane-Nida) are thus popular summer destinations.

Other recognizable landmarks are the Warmian castles ([Lidzbark Warmiński Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidzbark_Warmi%C5%84ski_Castle), [Olsztyn Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsztyn_Castle), [Reszel Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reszel_Castle), Pieniężno Castle) and the [Cathedral Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frombork_Cathedral) in [Frombork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frombork), where Polish astronomer [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus) lived and worked, and which contains his tomb. In collections of the Warmia and Mazury Museum in [Olsztyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olsztyn), visitors can find numerous tokens from the time when [Nicolaus Copernicus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaus_Copernicus) lived in Warmia.[^cite_note-20] The Lidzbark Warmiński Castle was later the residence of [Ignacy Krasicki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignacy_Krasicki), nicknamed *the Prince of Polish Poets*, whereas [Smolajny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smolajny) hosts a [Rococo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rococo) palace, his former summer residence. There are also multiple other [Gothic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gothic_architecture) castles and palaces in various styles in the voivodeship. [Święta Lipka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%9Awi%C4%99ta_Lipka) in Masuria and [Gietrzwałd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gietrzwa%C5%82d) in Warmia are popular pilgrimage sites, and other notable historic churches include the Gothic collegiate church in [Dobre Miasto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobre_Miasto) and churches in [Orneta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orneta) and [Kętrzyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C4%99trzyn), which delight visitors with the uniqueness of their architectonic details, and the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque_in_Poland) Sanctuary of Saint Mary in [Stoczek Klasztorny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stoczek_Klasztorny).[^cite_note-21] Several towns contain entirely or partly preserved medieval town walls with towers and gates, i.e. [Lidzbark Warmiński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidzbark_Warmi%C5%84ski), [Nowe Miasto Lubawskie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowe_Miasto_Lubawskie), [Pasłęk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pas%C5%82%C4%99k).



The [Lidzbark Warmiński Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lidzbark_Warmi%C5%84ski_Castle) is considered to be a great artistic and historical value

The [Elbląg Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbl%C4%85g_Canal) is considered one of the greatest landmarks of the western part of the province. The [Grunwald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Grunwald) battlefield in Masuria is site of the annual reenactment of one of the largest battles of Medieval Europe. [Gołdap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go%C5%82dap) is a [spa town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spa_town) in the north-eastern part of the province. There is a museum dedicated to composer [Feliks Nowowiejski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feliks_Nowowiejski) at his birthplace in [Barczewo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barczewo).

There are multiple World War II memorials in the voivodeship, including at the site of the [Soldau concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldau_concentration_camp) in [Działdowo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dzia%C5%82dowo), at the locations of Nazi massacres of Poles, and the war cemeteries in [Sudwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sudwa) and [Bogusze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogusze,_Warmian-Masurian_Voivodeship), where victims of [German prisoner-of-war camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_prisoner-of-war_camps_in_World_War_II) were buried. The [Wolf's Lair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wolf's_Lair), Adolf Hitler's first Eastern Front military headquarters in World War II is located in [Gierłoż](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gier%C5%82o%C5%BC,_K%C4%99trzyn_County).

There is a small cemetery of the [Commonwealth War Graves Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_War_Graves_Commission) in [Markajmy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markajmy) at which British and Commonwealth [prisoners-of-war of Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_War_I_prisoners_of_war_in_Germany) from [World War I](4764461.md) are buried.[^cite_note-22]

## Cuisine

In addition to traditional nationwide [Polish cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_cuisine), Warmian–Masurian Voivodeship has its variety of [traditional foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_food) and drinks, specific to its Warmia and Masuria regions, officially protected by the [Ministry of Agriculture and Rural Development of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Agriculture_and_Rural_Development_\(Poland\)). Masuria produces several types of various meat products, especially [kiełbasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kie%C5%82basa), and a plethora of traditional Polish honey. Masuria also shares with neighboring [Podlaskie Voivodeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podlaskie_Voivodeship) the tradition of producing [sękacz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C4%99kacz), a traditional spit cake of northeastern Poland.

The protected traditional alcoholic beverages of the province are *Okowita miodowa warmińska*, a beverage of 42% [alcohol by volume](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_by_volume) made from Warmian [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey), the Warmian [porter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porter_\(beer\)), a local type of [Polish beer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_beer), and Masurian [nalewka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nalewka) *niedźwiedziówka*.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

## Education



[University of Warmia and Mazury in Olsztyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warmia_and_Mazury_in_Olsztyn)

The chief institutions of higher learning in the voivodeship are the [University of Warmia and Mazury in Olsztyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Warmia_and_Mazury_in_Olsztyn) and Higher State College of Vocational Education in Elbląg. [Kortowiada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kortowiada), one of the largest annual university students' holidays in Poland, is held in Olsztyn.

The Police Academy in [Szczytno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szczytno), the main police academy in Poland, is located in the voivodeship.
