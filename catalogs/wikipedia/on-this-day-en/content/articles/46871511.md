**Sami Mahmud Mohammed al-Oraydi** or **Abu Mahmoud al-Shami** (also: *al-Uraydi*; [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): سامي محمود محمد العُرَيدي, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Sāmī Maḩmūd Muḥammad al-ʿUraydī*; 1973 – 20 June 2026) is a Jordanian militant who served as a senior [sharia](28840.md) official for the [al-Qaeda](1921.md)-affiliated [Hurras al-Din](56718004.md).[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] He was previously the chief religious authority for [al-Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front) and the group's former second-in-command.[^cite_note-cep-4][^cite_note-formation-5]

## Background

Sami al-Oraydi was born in [Amman](45007.md), Jordan, in 1973, and received his [bachelor's degree](188874.md) in religious studies from the [University of Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Jordan). In 1997 he received a [master's degree](170346.md) from the same university in [Hadith studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadith_studies) and, in 2001, completed his PhD in the same subject. He wrote a number of books about the 14th century scholar [Ibn Taymiyyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibn_Taymiyyah).[^cite_note-6]

Al-Oraydi was influenced by the teachings of Syrian jihadist [Abu Musab al-Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Musab_al-Suri), who fought against the Syrian government in the 1970s and '80s.[^cite_note-cep-4]

## Syrian Civil War

Main article: [Syrian Civil War](30741795.md)

Al-Oraydi was a member of the [al-Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front). He acted as the group's chief [sharia](28840.md) authority and reportedly served as the group's second-in-command. He was previously al-Nusra's second highest sharia authority under former religious leader and military commander [Abu Maria Al-Qahtani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Maria_Al-Qahtani). Al-Oraydi was promoted over al-Qahtani after the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) (ISIL) overran Nusra Front positions in eastern Syria in 2014.[^cite_note-cep-4]

He used social media to release sermons and declarations on behalf of the [Al-Nusra Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Nusra_Front). He maintained a Twitter account under the handle @sami_oride and reportedly tweeted that the "ideal" jihadist is a moral individual watched by God and the public. Al-Oraidi also used social media to attack [ISIL](9087364.md), particularly after the group declared its caliphate in June 2014. Al-Oraydi said ISIL spokesman [Abu Mohammad al-Adnani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Mohammad_al-Adnani) "does not know what comes out of his head." He also referred to ISIL's leaders as "Muslim killers." He declared war on ISIL in a December 2014 sermon.[^cite_note-cep-4]

Al-Oraydi accused ISIL of being *[Kharijites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kharijites)* and denounced the group for being too extreme, saying in an audio released in 2014 that "Jihad has taught me that leniency in dealing with extremists and Kharijites is disregard for the blood of the Sunnis and the Mujaheddin. Every Jihad arena they enter, they shed the blood of the mujahideen."[^cite_note-7]

After the formation of [Hay'at Tahrir al-Sham](53015755.md), al-Oraydi left the group, along with another senior al-Nusra leader, and was later arrested by HTS with several other pro-al-Qaeda elements from the group, including [Abu Humam al-Shami](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Humam_al-Shami), Abu al-Qassem al-Urduni and Abu Julaybib al-Urduni; the arrests were condemned by ISIL.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] His resignation was confirmed by [Abu Muhammad al-Maqdisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Muhammad_al-Maqdisi).[^cite_note-formation-5] Al-Oraydi joined the [shura council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majlis-ash-Shura) of [Hurras al-Din](56718004.md) in February 2018.[^cite_note-swd-1]

The U.S. Department of State's Rewards for Justice Program is offering up to \$5 million reward for information on his whereabouts.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]

## Reported death

Al-Oraydi reportedly died in a U.S.-led coalition airstrike in northern Syria on 20 June 2026, although the specific strike in Northwest Syria killed a senior ISIS leader named Ali Husayn al-'Ulaywi, claimed by CENTCOM, with no mention of al-Oraydi.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] In August 2026, an article penned by him was released by a pro-al-Qaeda media group.[^cite_note-15]
