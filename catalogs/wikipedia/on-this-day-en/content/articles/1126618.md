**Benguela** (Portuguese pronunciation: [\[bẽˈɡɛlɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese); [Umbundu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umbundu): Luombaka) is a city in western [Angola](701.md), capital of [Benguela Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguela_Province).[^cite_note-b-lobito-3] Benguela is one of Angola's most populous cities with a population of 555,124 in the city and 561,775 in the municipality, at the 2014 census.[^cite_note-Censo2014-1]

## History

Main article: [Timeline of Benguela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Benguela)

### Portuguese rule

Main article: [Portuguese Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Angola)



17th century map of Benguela.



[Portuguese colonial architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_colonial_architecture) in the historic center of Benguela.

Benguela was founded in 1617 as *São Felipe de Benguela* by the [Portuguese](23033.md) under Manuel Cerveira Pereira, 8th [Governor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor) of [Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_West_Africa) (1604–1607).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-b-lobito-3] It was long the centre of an important trade, especially in [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavery_in_Angola) to [Brazil](3383.md) and [Cuba](5042481.md). Ships anchored about 1.6 kilometres (1 mi) off the shore, in depths of 7 to 11 metres (23 to 36 ft) and [transferred loads to smaller boats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightering) which used five or six [jetties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jetty) in the town. However, the nearby deep-water sheltered harbour of [Lobito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobito) was a much larger port.&#x20;

Besides the churches of S. Felipe and S. António, the hospital, and the fortress, as of 1911 there were only a few stone-built houses.[^cite_note-eb1911-5] A short way beyond Benguela is [Baía Farta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba%C3%ADa_Farta), where salt was manufactured and [sulphur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulphur) was extracted. Close to [Baia Farta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baia_Farta) was the beach of [Baia Azul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baia_Azul). The city prospered and grew in the following decades.&#x20;

The [Benguela Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguela_Railway) was built in the early 20th century by Portugal to connect the city and [Lobito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobito) to the interior, and it achieved great success when linked to the [Copperbelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copperbelt) of [Katanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katanga_Province), [DR Congo](76762.md) and [Zambia](34415.md).[^cite_note-6]

Starting in the early 20th century, Benguela attracted, developed, and retained quality businesses and professionals into its growing economy. [Sisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisal) and [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing_in_Angola) industries expanded and the financial, construction and services market boomed until 1974.[^cite_note-7]

### Post-independence

In 1975, after the April 1974 [Carnation Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnation_Revolution) in [Lisbon](18091.md), Portugal, the Portuguese [Overseas Province of Angola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Province_of_Angola) became independent.&#x20;



Benguela City Hall.

Due to the [Angolan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angolan_Civil_War) (1975–2002), which lasted more than 20 years after independence from Portugal, the important [Benguela railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguela_railway) line closed, with only the short distance of 30 kilometres (19 mi) between Benguela and [Lobito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobito) remaining operational. In 1983 Benguela had a population of 155,000. During the civil war the city of Benguela increased its population due to refugees from the countryside.

### 21st century

In the mid-2000s, with a more peaceful environment, restoration of the railway between Benguela and [Huambo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huambo) commenced.

While the colonial part of the city consists of relatively good-quality houses, as of 2011 most of the refugees live in slum areas.

## Transportation

Benguela possesses an [airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguela_Airport) and a [railway connection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benguela_Railway) to the rest of Angola and the border with the [Democratic Republic of the Congo (DRC)](76762.md), from which it continues on to the city of [Tenke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenke,_Democratic_Republic_of_the_Congo) and connecting with the south-central section of the [Cape-Cairo railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_to_Cairo_Railway) that provides further connections to [Zambia](34415.md), [Zimbabwe](34399.md), [Botswana](3464.md) and [South Africa](17416221.md).

## Economy



Estádio Nacional de Ombaka.

Benguela was one of the centres of Portuguese trade to the African interior. The city remains an important commercial link between western and eastern Angola. [Coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee), [corn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize), [sisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sisal), [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane), and [tobacco](30942.md) are grown in the interior regions of Angola and widely traded in Benguela.&#x20;

[Manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese) from the interior is also traded in Benguela. Industries local to the city include fish processing and the milling of sugarcane. The city also produces pottery, soap, and tools.[^cite_note-b-lobito-3]

Foreign trade is handled through the deep-water port of [Lobito](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lobito), which is located 29 kilometres (18 mi) north of Benguela. Lobito, once the busiest port in Angola, was severely disrupted during the Angolan Civil War. The port has since revived and supports trade in the Benguela region.[^cite_note-b-lobito-3]
