**Chega** (Portuguese: [\[ˈʃeɣɐ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Portuguese); officially stylised as **CHEGA!**, lit. 'Enough!'; **CH**) is a [national conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_conservatism) and [right-wing populist](8354382.md)[^cite_note-7] [political party in Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_political_parties_in_Portugal), formed in 2019 by [André Ventura](60708639.md).[^cite_note-\:3-8] It is on the [far-right](221220.md)[^cite_note-9] of the political spectrum.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-Almeida-11][^cite_note-expresso.pt-12]

The party started as a movement within the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)) (PSD) led by [André Ventura](60708639.md). After failing to unseat the PSD's leader, [Rui Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rui_Rio), Ventura left and registered Chega as its own party.[^cite_note-\:3-8]

The party's ideology focuses heavily on issues of criminality and the misuse of tax payer money. They have pledged to support the police services of the country and control the misuse of taxpayer funds by reducing overstaffing in the civil service and reducing welfare.[^cite_note-\:2-13]

Chega won one seat in the [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md) in the [2019 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Portuguese_legislative_election). Since this election, the party has rapidly grown in popularity, gaining significant support for its anti-establishment positions.[^cite_note-14] It was the third most voted party in the elections of [2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Portuguese_legislative_election) taking 12 seats. It saw a surge in support in the [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Portuguese_legislative_election) winning 50 seats, more than quadrupling its previous seat count.[^cite_note-15] It improved its position further in the [2025 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Portuguese_legislative_election), winning 60 seats and overtaking the [Socialist Party](403237.md)'s tally to achieve second place.

[André Ventura](60708639.md) was the [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)) (PSD) candidate for mayor of [Loures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loures) in the [Lisbon District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_District) in the [2017 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Portuguese_local_elections). During his campaign, he made comments about [Romani people in Portugal](28870464.md) that led to a criminal complaint by [Left Bloc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Bloc_\(Portugal\)) candidate [Fabian Figueiredo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fabian_Figueiredo), and the withdrawal of the endorsement from the [CDS – People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CDS_%E2%80%93_People's_Party).[^cite_note-16]

After [Rui Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rui_Rio)'s election as [leader of the PSD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)), André Ventura founded a movement within the party called "Chega", with the objective of gathering enough signatures to unseat Rio.[^cite_note-17] After not being able to do so, in October 2018, he left the PSD, turned his movement into a new party with the same name, and resigned his seat on Loures city council.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

Chega had been initially prevented from registering as a political party as some of the 8,000 signatures presented to the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_\(Portugal\)) included minors and police officers.[^cite_note-20] The court accepted the party's registration on 9 April 2019.[^cite_note-21] Ventura made a coalition with the [People's Monarchist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Monarchist_Party_\(Portugal\)), [Citizenship and Christian Democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Citizenship_and_Christian_Democracy) and Democracy 21, for which he would be the lead candidate in the [2019 European Parliament election in Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_European_Parliament_election_in_Portugal). The coalition was approved by the Constitutional Court at the third request as it was rejected the first two times for having a name that included "Chega"; it was finally named [Basta!](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basta!_\(coalition\)), a synonym of Chega in Portuguese.[^cite_note-22] Ventura garnered controversy for not attending an electoral debate and instead appearing on [CMTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CMTV) in his role as a sports pundit; coalition representative Sofia Afonso Ferreira said that this was due to a late change of the debate's timing by broadcaster [Rádio e Televisão de Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R%C3%A1dio_e_Televis%C3%A3o_de_Portugal).[^cite_note-23] The coalition targeted one or two seats in the [European Parliament](9581.md) but won none, taking 1.49% of the vote.[^cite_note-24]

### 2019 and 2022 elections



[André Ventura](60708639.md) on the night of the [2022 legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Portuguese_legislative_election), after Chega became the third largest party

In the [2019 Portuguese legislative election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Portuguese_legislative_election), Chega won one seat in the [Assembly of the Republic](1123482.md), taken by Ventura for the [Lisbon constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lisbon_\(Assembly_of_the_Republic_constituency\)).[^cite_note-25] Throughout the 2010s, Portugal was widely seen internationally as an exception to the advance of right-wing populism in Europe.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

Chega entered the [Legislative Assembly of the Azores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_the_Azores) with two seats in the [2020 regional election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Azorean_regional_election). While the [Socialist Party](403237.md) (PS) won the most seats, Chega gave support to a right-leaning government led by [José Manuel Bolieiro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Manuel_Bolieiro) of the PSD, in exchange for a review of the region's constitutional status.[^cite_note-28] Ricardo Cabral Fernandes of *[Jacobin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobin_\(magazine\))* reflected that "The Azores are a small region — but this was a big step in the normalization of Chega, and a trial run for a similar solution at a national scale".[^cite_note-29]

Ventura ran for the mainly ceremonial role of [President of Portugal](516093.md) in the [2021 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Portuguese_presidential_election), coming third with 12% of the vote, marginally behind runner-up [Ana Gomes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ana_Gomes) of the PS. Ventura celebrated his result as the "first time an openly anti-system party has disrupted the traditional right".[^cite_note-30]

In the [2022 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Portuguese_legislative_election), receiving 7.2% of the vote, it increased its seat count to 12, coming third behind the PS and PSD.[^cite_note-31]

### 2024 election

In the [2024 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Portuguese_legislative_election) it received 18.07% of the vote, quadrupling its seat count to a final total of 50.[^cite_note-32]

In the 2024 election, the party was the most voted in the [Faro constituency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faro_\(Assembly_of_the_Republic_constituency\)), which corresponds to the [Algarve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algarve). This was the first time that a third party was the most voted in a district since the [Unitary Democratic Coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unitary_Democratic_Coalition) (CDU) won the [Beja District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beja_District) in [1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Portuguese_legislative_election).[^cite_note-33] The party was the most voted in ten municipalities, five of which were in the Algarve, though its highest percentage was 36.53% in [Elvas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elvas) in the [Portalegre District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portalegre_District).[^cite_note-34] Portuguese political scientists credited Chega's advances to a [protest vote](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protest_vote) against the two largest parties, and as the result of the difficulty locals face when finding housing in the tourism-heavy region.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37]

[Luís Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_Montenegro), leader of the PSD and the [Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Democratic_Alliance_\(Portugal,_2024\)) (AD) coalition that took the plurality of votes in the election, refused the prospect of forming a coalition with Chega. When invited to form a government by President [Marcelo Rebelo de Sousa](1652059.md), he continued with this position and chose to form a [minority government](271434.md).[^cite_note-38] Ventura said that Chega would vote against the state budget proposed by AD if his party were not included in the government.[^cite_note-39]

During the 2024 election, Chega nominated two [Evangelical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evangelicalism) pastors, both of whom were elected as MPs for the party.[^cite_note-Batista-40]

### 2025 election

Chega improved its position again in the [2025 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Portuguese_legislative_election), winning 60 seats, beating the [Socialist Party](403237.md), and gathering 22.8% of the votes. The party strongly outperformed polls, which had put their vote share under 20%.[^cite_note-41] Retaining its position as the most voted party in the Faro constituency, Chega added three more southern districts from the Socialists: Beja, Portalegre and Setúbal.[^cite_note-42] Alison Roberts of [BBC News](1139893.md) put forward that Chega had been helped by recent scandals involving prime ministers from the Socialist and Social Democratic parties, respectively; the election had been triggered by a motion of no confidence in Luís Montenegro for alleged [conflict of interest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conflict_of_interest).[^cite_note-43]

In the [2025 local elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Portuguese_local_elections), Chega gained its first three mayors – [Albufeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albufeira), [Entroncamento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Entroncamento) and [São Vicente, Madeira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%A3o_Vicente,_Madeira) – though it had set a target of 30. It came third for votes with 11.86%, a halving since the general election, but fifth for mayors[^cite_note-44] and more than doubling the [2021 local election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Portuguese_local_elections) results (4.16%).

### 2026 election

In the [2026 presidential election](68734153.md), party leader [André Ventura](60708639.md) repeated his presidential run from five years ago, polling this time at second place with 23.5% of the votes and faced the PS supported candidate, [António José Seguro](35591042.md), in a runoff.[^cite_note-45] Ventura was defeated, gathering 33% of the votes against the 67% of Seguro.[^cite_note-46]

## Ideology

Chega considers itself a party with [nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalism),[^cite_note-47] [conservative](6675.md) and [personalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personalism) roots.[^cite_note-CHEGA-48] Third-party sources have defined the party as [ultranationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultranationalism).[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50][^cite_note-51] It defends the promotion of an effective judicial system and the decrease of the state's intervention in the economy. The party also presents itself as [national conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_conservatism)[^cite_note-52] and [social conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_conservatism).[^cite_note-JSOL1-53]

The agenda of Chega is heavily focused on criminality issues, support for [the police forces of the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement_in_Portugal), and to control the misuse of taxpayers' money in terms of [corruption](3608404.md) at the top, [overstaffing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overstaffing) in the civil service at the middle and [underserving welfare recipients](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welfare_fraud) at the bottom.[^cite_note-\:2-13][^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] Chega's president, André Ventura, has protested with a group of police officers called Movement Zero, who have suspected extremist ties.[^cite_note-57]

The party's slogan, "God, country, family and work" is an appropriation and elaboration of the slogan "God, country, family" used by the Portuguese dictator [António de Oliveira Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_de_Oliveira_Salazar).[^cite_note-58]

### Constitutional revision

Chega supports a rewriting of the [Constitution of the Portuguese Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_the_Portuguese_Republic) to be "smaller and less ideological".[^cite_note-Constitution_CMJ-59] The current Portuguese constitution was drafted during the [Ongoing Revolutionary Process](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ongoing_Revolutionary_Process) (PREC), a tumultuous period of Portuguese history where a transition to some form of socialist society seemed imminent. Two revisions in 1982 and 1989 removed some of the overtly leftist ideological bent (including allowing for [privatizations](24661.md)) as well as more charged language, but crucially, the phrase that the constitution itself "opens the way for a socialist society" remains. This is taken by many as an official declaration that [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Portuguese_Republic) is, or at the very least, ought to be an ideologically socialist state. Despite being a relatively small section of the constitution, Chega takes this to be a form of ideological favoritism that ignores and alienates large sections of the Portuguese population, resulting in an overall unrepresentative constitution.

Proposed constitutional changes involve getting rid of several government positions and jobs (including but not limited to, the Ministry of Education), reducing the number of members of the [Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assembly_of_the_Portuguese_Republic) to 100 down from 230, reviewing [parliamentary immunity](320344.md), and requiring that the [prime-minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Portugal) be a Portuguese citizen from birth. More drastically, Ventura has argued for the abolishment of the position of [prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_Minister_of_Portugal),[^cite_note-60] thus ushering in a "Fourth Portuguese Republic" under a [presidential system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_system), as well as getting rid of the constitution's "material guardrails", which would facilitate further amendments to any and all of its parts.[^cite_note-61]

### Society

Chega supports [life imprisonment](148681.md) sentences, the reintroduction of the [death penalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capital_punishment_in_Portugal) and [chemical castration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_castration) for reoffending rapists.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

Chega opposes abortion, same-sex marriage, and the rights of [transgender people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transgender_people).[^cite_note-Batista-40]

Chega has ties to conservative Christian movements and prominent members such as the party's leader, André Ventura and vice president Pedro Frazão are well-known Catholics.[^cite_note-Batista-40] In 2024, 67.9% of Chega supporters identified as having a religious identity.[^cite_note-64] Chega also has ties with and enjoys support from many Christian movements, including several [Evangelical Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism_in_Portugal) where the majority of congregants are of [Brazilian origin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brazilians_in_Portugal). While this fact may be used to dissuade charges of xenophobia, critics of the party have highlighted the hypocrisy of touting anti-immigrant rhetoric while enjoying broad support from certain immigrant demographics, with some immigration restrictionists both within and outside the party even alleging a conflict of interest.[^cite_note-65]

#### Immigration and minorities

Describing itself a "strong proponent of [Western civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Civilization)", the party positions itself against "[Islamist extremism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamist_extremism)" and proposes stronger border controls and a decrease of "mass and illegal immigration".[^cite_note-CHEGA-48] It has been also described as [antiziganist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiziganism).[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68] The party supports integration measures for immigrants and states that all immigrants and foreign residents should be "obliged to respect our rules, rites, customs and traditions." It also supports bilateral agreements and priority for skills-based immigration from the [Community of Portuguese Language Countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Community_of_Portuguese_Language_Countries) and former Portuguese colonies such as [Brazil](3383.md), [Portuguese-speaking African countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese-speaking_African_countries), [Macau](19068.md) and [East Timor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Timor) while taking a more critical stance on non-Western immigration and arguing that all work visa applicants must undergo a selection test and demonstrate adaptability to the Portuguese language. It also calls for a [zero tolerance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_tolerance) policy on illegal immigration and for the deportation of immigrants with criminal records or those who are economically inactive.[^cite_note-auto-69] It is also opposed to [multiculturalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multiculturalism). The party claims that it rejects xenophobia on its platform.[^cite_note-70] The party opposes [illegal immigration](7124412.md) and "open door immigration" policies for both Portugal[^cite_note-71] and the [external border of the European Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/External_border_of_the_European_Union).[^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73]

### Economy

The party advocates for a decrease of the [tax incidence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tax_incidence), considering the current system to be "brutal and aggressive to the ones who work and build wealth, taking away half of their incomes". It additionally defends a reduction of both [bureaucracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureaucracy) and the number of [bureaucrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bureaucrat), asserting that it is one of the main reasons for the "Portuguese competitive economic backwardness".[^cite_note-CHEGA-48] Some of this reduction includes allowing more private options education and healthcare.[^cite_note-74]

### Foreign policy

#### Europe

Chega's stance on the [European Union](9317.md) has been described as [Eurosceptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosceptic).[^cite_note-75] The party states that it supports the original "[four freedoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_freedoms_\(European_Union\))" principle of free movement of goods, capital, services and people among member states, but argues for a "Europe of sovereign nations united by shared [Greco-Roman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greco-Roman_world) and [Judeo-Christian principles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judeo-Christian_ethics)" and opposes interference into national political decision-making within member states by the EU. It also calls on Portugal to pursue more independent foreign and economic policies from Brussels and rejects compulsory EU migrant and refugee quotas. Furthermore, the party also argues that Portugal should work to drastically reform the EU and that the country must exit the block if the EU tries to become a [federal state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federalisation_of_the_European_Union).[^cite_note-auto-69]

On the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), [António Tânger Corrêa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_T%C3%A2nger_Corr%C3%AAa), the party's vice president and leading candidate for the [2024 European Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_Portugal), has been critical of Ukraine,[^cite_note-76] advocating a peace deal that would involve "reinforced cooperation" between Ukraine and Russia in a way that the "Russians feel comfortable".[^cite_note-77] On the other hand, Ventura has said that the defeat of Ukraine will be the defeat of the entire [West](21208200.md), and Portugal, as a member of [NATO](21133.md) and the [European Union](9317.md), should send troops "in case of extreme need".[^cite_note-78] However, Ventura later stated that he was opposed to sending Portuguese troops to Ukraine and advocated greater scrutiny of Ukraine aid, suggesting that "hundreds of millions" of taxpayer funds were being used to "support a political clientele around [Zelensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Zelensky)".[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

#### Brazil

Chega supported the [presidency of Jair Bolsonaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Jair_Bolsonaro) in Brazil, but party leader Ventura said that the former [President of Brazil](273638.md) deserves condemnation if proven to have instigated the [2023 Brazilian Congress attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Brazilian_Congress_attack); Ventura also said that sections of the Brazilian population were right to be frustrated by [Luiz Inácio Lula da Silva](99328.md)'s [victory over Bolsonaro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Brazilian_general_election) before the attack.[^cite_note-\:1-81]

#### Middle East

Chega has taken a strongly [pro-Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pro-Israel) stance on the [Gaza war](74998519.md), criticising other Portuguese political parties for their stances on the conflict. The party has advocated for the recognition of [Jerusalem](16043.md) as the capital of Israel and the eventual transfer of the Portuguese embassy to that city. Representatives of Israel's [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud) party have already participated in Chega conventions, thanking the Portuguese party for its support for this position.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84]

## Organization

### Leadership elections

The 2020 Chega leadership election was held on 6 September 2020. [André Ventura](60708639.md) was re-elected with more than 99% of the vote, facing no opposition.[^cite_note-85][^cite_note-86] The party's statutes have been rejected by the [Constitutional Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Court_of_Portugal) several times for concentrating excessive power in the hands of Ventura.[^cite_note-\:0-87]

### Factions

In 2020, it was reported that there was a "guerrilla atmosphere" within the party, the result of tensions between the different factions that make up the party.[^cite_note-88]

## Public profile

### Critical response

Due to its [anti-immigration](6491626.md), [anti-Islam](2394308.md) and [populist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Populism) stances,[^cite_note-94] Chega has been the target of its critics who underline the party's extreme views on various subjects, some of which include the negative comments regarding immigration and minorities, namely the [Romani](28870464.md),[^cite_note-95] its opposition to certain aspects of the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Portugal),[^cite_note-96][^cite_note-97] its criticism of the judicial leniency regarding serious crimes,[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99] and governmental over-expenditure with public services.[^cite_note-100]

The party has also been targeted by critics for reusing a slightly modified version of the motto of the Portuguese dictator [António de Oliveira Salazar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ant%C3%B3nio_de_Oliveira_Salazar) "Deus, Pátria, Família" (God, Fatherland, Family).[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] The party has been criticized for having supporters of Salazar within their ranks.[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104]

The Global Project against Hate and Extremism (GPAHE), an American NGO specialising in the study of extremist movements, warned in a 2023 report that Chega is an "anti-immigrant, anti-women, anti-LGBT, [anti-Roma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Romani_sentiment), anti-Muslim and conspiratorial party". The organisation also highlights the presence of numerous [white supremacists](33178.md), [identitarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Identitarians) and [neo-Nazis](54361.md) in the party's ranks.[^cite_note-\:0-87][^cite_note-105] A subsequent investigatory article by GPAHE found evidence of "more extreme" members in the Chega Youth group, including "white supremacists, fans of former dictator Antonio Salazar, and [fascist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fascism) sympathizers" including the President of the Coimbra branch João Antunes, the leader of the Porto branch Francisco Araujo, and Vila Nova de Famalicão leader Joana Pinto Azevedo.[^cite_note-106]

In a TV debate in 2024, [Social Democratic Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)) (PSD) [President](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Social_Democratic_Party_\(Portugal\)) [Luís Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu%C3%ADs_Montenegro) called Chega leader André Ventura "xenophobic, racist and demagogic".[^cite_note-107]

### Accusations of racism

In response to criticism of the [mainstream media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mainstream_media), party president André Ventura denounced the accusations of [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism) against his party, claiming that Chega defends equal rights and duties, and that it "doesn't desire a country on which minorities can believe they have more rights than others simply for being minorities".[^cite_note-108] On 27 June 2020, the party organized a protest entitled "Portugal is not racist", where Ventura further mentioned that there is no [structural racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_racism) in Portugal, and that the political left uses racism as a pretext to foment political agendas.[^cite_note-109]
