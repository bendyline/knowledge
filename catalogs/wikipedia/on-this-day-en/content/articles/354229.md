The **Aruwimi River** ([Swahili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swahili_language): *Mto Aruwimi*, [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Rivière Aruwimi*) is a [tributary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tributary) of the [Congo River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_River), located to the north and east of the Congo.[^cite_note-Stanley-2]<sup>: Vol.Two, 214 </sup>

The Aruwimi begins as the [Ituri River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ituri_River), which rises near [Lake Albert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Albert_\(Africa\)),[^cite_note-bossche-3] in the savannas north of the Kibale River watershed. It then runs generally south southwest until it is joined by the Shari River which flows by Bunia. The Ituri then turns west, through the [Ituri Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ituri_Forest), becoming the Aruwimi where the [Nepoko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nepoko_River) (or Nepoki) River joins it, at the town of [Bomili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bomili?action=edit\&redlink=1). The river continues westward, joining the Congo at [Basoko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basoko). The length of the Aruwimi is 1,245.0 km (773.6 mi), with the Upper Ituri (above the confluence with the Shari) being 249.1 kilometres (154.8 mi), the Lower Ituri being 505.2 kilometres (313.9 mi) and the main trunk of the Aruwimi being 490.7 kilometres (304.9 mi). The Aruwimi is about 1.5 kilometres (0.93 mi) wide where it joins the Congo.

The watershed of the Ituri/Aruwimi is almost entirely dense forest, with just a handful of villages along its course, and crossed by roads in about four places. The [Kango language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kango_language) ([SIL code](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIL_code) KZY) is spoken by several thousand villagers just south of Avakubi, and upper reaches of the Ituri are inhabited by the [Mbuti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mbuti_people) ([Pygmies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pygmy)).

The Aruwimi was explored by [Henry Morton Stanley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Morton_Stanley) during his [1887 expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emin_Pasha_Relief_Expedition) to "rescue" [Emin Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emin_Pasha). The [cataracts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterfall) above [Yambuya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yambuya) made it impossible to use the river for navigation, and the expedition had to go by land, with tremendous difficulty.
