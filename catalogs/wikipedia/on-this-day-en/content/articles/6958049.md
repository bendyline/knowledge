# Editorial independence

**Editorial independence** refers to the freedom of [journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist) and [media organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mass_media) to make content decisions—such as what to report, how to report it, and when to report it—without external influence from owners, [governments](12229.md), advertisers, or other outside forces.[^cite_note-1] It is a sacrosanct principle for [independent media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_media) organizations.[^cite_note-2]

Countries that want to foster an independent media ecosystem and protect editorial independence often pursue laws against [concentration of media ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_of_media_ownership) and provide [subsidies](146719.md) for independent media.[^cite_note-3]

## Discussion

Editorial independence has been at the center of debates when labeling outlets as [state media](20803507.md).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] Journalists and [public broadcasters](178808.md) with editorial independence object to labeling that focuses on whether or not they get funding from a government as misleading audiences to believe that it impacts their coverage. Before Elon Musk, Twitter did not label media like [The BBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_BBC) as 'state-affiliated' because of that independence explaining that the independence is what readers really wanted to know.[^cite_note-6] [Elon Musk's move to change that in 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twitter_under_Elon_Musk#State-affiliated_media_labeling) drew backlash from critics arguing it represented a false equivalence between outlets tightly controlled by the state and those free to criticize their own government.[^cite_note-7] YouTube also faced similar criticisms in 2018 and 2019 for its label attempts that focused on funding instead of editorial independence.[^cite_note-8] The transition away from editorial independence also led to protests in Hungary in 2024.[^cite_note-9]

[Student publications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Student_publication) also have a range of editorial independence from their schools, some of which try to prevent any negative publicity that the student journalists might uncover.[^cite_note-10]

*[The Washington Post](102226.md)* and its owner [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos) received criticism from some staff and journalism pundits for spiking a 2024 endorsement of Kamala Harris as an example of a lack of editorial independence.[^cite_note-11]
