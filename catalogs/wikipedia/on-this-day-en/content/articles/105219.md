**Cancer** is a group of [diseases](8072.md) involving [uncontrolled cell growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_growth#Disorders) typically resulting in [tumors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplasm) with the potential to [invade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_\(cancer\)) or [spread](152509.md) to other parts of the body.[^cite_note-WHO2018-2][^cite_note-WhatIsCancerNCI-8] These [malignant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malignant) tumors contrast with [benign tumors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benign_tumor), which do not spread.[^cite_note-WhatIsCancerNCI-8] Over 100 [types of cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_cancer) affect humans.[^cite_note-WhatIsCancerNCI-8][^cite_note-9]

About 33% of deaths from cancer are caused by [tobacco](30942.md) and [alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_\(drug\)), [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity), lack of fruit and vegetables in the diet and lack of exercise.[^cite_note-WHO2018-2][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] Other factors include certain infections, exposure to [ionizing radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionizing_radiation), and environmental pollutants.[^cite_note-Enviro2008-3] [Infection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_causes_of_cancer) with specific viruses, bacteria, and parasites causes approximately 16–18% of cancers worldwide.[^cite_note-de_Martel_2020-12] These infectious agents include *[Helicobacter pylori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicobacter_pylori)*, [hepatitis B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_B_virus), [hepatitis C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_C_virus), and [HPV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_papillomavirus_infection). [Human immunodeficiency virus](14170.md) (HIV) does not directly cause cancer, but it causes immune deficiency that can increase the risk of cancer from other infections, sometimes up to several thousandfold. Vaccination against the hepatitis B virus and the [human papillomavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_papillomavirus) have been shown to nearly eliminate the risk of cancers caused by these viruses.

These environmental factors act, at least partly, by changing the [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene) of a cell.[^cite_note-WCR2014Scr-13] Typically, many genetic changes are required before cancer develops.[^cite_note-WCR2014Scr-13] Approximately 5–10% of cancers are due to inherited genetic defects.[^cite_note-ACS-heredity-14] Cancer can be detected by certain signs and symptoms or screening tests.[^cite_note-WHO2018-2] It is then typically further investigated by [medical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging) and confirmed by [biopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biopsy).[^cite_note-15]

The risk of developing cancer can be reduced by not smoking, maintaining a healthy weight, limiting alcohol intake, eating plenty of vegetables, fruits, and [whole grains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whole_grain), [vaccination](32473.md) against certain infectious diseases, limiting consumption of [processed meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processed_meat) and [red meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_meat), and limiting exposure to direct sunlight.[^cite_note-Kushi2012-16][^cite_note-17] Early detection through screening is useful for [cervical](53338.md) and [colorectal cancer](206979.md).[^cite_note-WCR2014Scr-13] The benefits of [screening for breast cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_cancer_screening) are controversial.[^cite_note-WCR2014Scr-13][^cite_note-Got2013-18] Cancer is often treated with some combination of [radiation therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_therapy), surgery, [chemotherapy](7172.md) and [targeted therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeted_therapy).[^cite_note-WHO2018-2][^cite_note-TCT2018-5] More [personalized therapies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personalized_medicine) that harness a patient's immune system are emerging in the field of [cancer immunotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_immunotherapy).[^cite_note-19] [Palliative care](275509.md) is a medical specialty that delivers advanced pain and symptom management, which may be particularly important in those with advanced disease.[^cite_note-WHO2018-2] The chance of survival depends on the type of cancer and [extent of disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_staging) at the start of treatment.[^cite_note-WCR2014Scr-13] In children under 15 at diagnosis, the [five-year survival rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Five-year_survival_rate) in the [developed world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_world) is on average 80%.[^cite_note-WCR2014Peads-20] For cancer in the United States, the average five-year survival rate is 66% for all ages.[^cite_note-Seer2014-6]

In 2015, about 90.5 million people worldwide had cancer.[^cite_note-GBD2015Pre-21] In 2019, annual cancer cases grew by 23.6 million people, and there were 10 million deaths worldwide, representing over the previous decade increases of 26% and 21%, respectively.[^cite_note-kocarnik-7][^cite_note-22]

The most common types of cancer in males are [lung cancer](18450.md), [prostate cancer](88078.md), [colorectal cancer](206979.md), and [stomach cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach_cancer).[^cite_note-WCR2014-23][^cite_note-auto-24] In females, the most common types are [breast cancer](70547.md), colorectal cancer, lung cancer, and [cervical cancer](53338.md).[^cite_note-WCR2014Scr-13][^cite_note-auto-24] If [skin cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_cancer) other than [melanoma](716631.md) were included in total new cancer cases each year, it would account for around 40% of cases.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-Cak2012-26] In children, [acute lymphoblastic leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acute_lymphoblastic_leukemia) and [brain tumors](37284.md) are most common, except in Africa, where [non-Hodgkin lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Hodgkin_lymphoma) occurs more often.[^cite_note-WCR2014Peads-20] In 2012, about 165,000 children under 15 years of age were diagnosed with cancer.[^cite_note-WCR2014-23] The risk of cancer increases significantly with age, and many cancers occur more commonly in developed countries.[^cite_note-WCR2014Scr-13] Rates are increasing as [more people live to an old age](720684.md) and as lifestyle changes occur in the developing world.[^cite_note-Epi11-27] The global total economic costs of cancer were estimated at [US\$](18717338.md)1.16 trillion (equivalent to \$1.71 trillion in 2025) per year as of 2010.[^cite_note-WCR2014Eco-28]

## Etymology and definitions

The word comes from the ancient Greek καρκίνος, meaning 'crab' and 'tumor'. Greek physicians [Hippocrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocrates) and [Galen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galen), among others, noted the similarity of crabs to some tumors with swollen veins. The word was introduced in English in the modern medical sense around 1600.[^cite_note-29]

Cancers comprise a large family of diseases that involve abnormal [cell growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_growth) with the potential to invade or spread to other parts of the body.[^cite_note-WHO2018-2][^cite_note-WhatIsCancerNCI-8] They form a subset of [neoplasms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplasm). A neoplasm or tumor is a group of cells that have undergone unregulated growth and will often form a mass or lump, but may be distributed diffusely.[^cite_note-ACSglossary-30][^cite_note-WhatIsCancerNCI-8]

All tumor cells show the [six hallmarks of cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hallmarks_of_Cancer). These characteristics are required to produce a malignant tumor. They include:[^cite_note-Han2000-31]

- [Cell growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_growth) and division absent the proper signals

- Continuous growth and division even given contrary signals

- Avoidance of [programmed cell death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoptosis)

- [Limitless number of cell divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_immortality)

- Promoting [blood vessel construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiogenesis)

- [Invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_\(cancer\)) of tissue and formation of [metastases](152509.md)

  [^cite_note-Han2000-31]

The progression from normal cells to cells that can form a detectable mass to cancer involves multiple steps known as malignant progression.[^cite_note-Han2000-31][^cite_note-Han2011-32]

## Signs and symptoms

Main article: [Signs and symptoms of cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Signs_and_symptoms_of_cancer)



Symptoms of cancer [metastasis](152509.md) depend on the location of the tumor.

When cancer begins, it produces no symptoms. Signs and symptoms appear as the mass grows or [ulcerates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulcer_\(dermatology\)). The findings that result depend on cancer's type and location. Few symptoms are [specific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-specific_symptom). Many frequently occur in individuals who have other conditions. Cancer can be difficult to diagnose and can be considered a "[great imitator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_great_imitator)".[^cite_note-Holland_2000-33]

People may become anxious or depressed post-diagnosis. The risk of [suicide](16831059.md) in people with cancer is approximately double.[^cite_note-34]

### Local symptoms

Local symptoms may occur due to the mass of the tumor or its ulceration. For example, mass effects from [lung cancer](18450.md) can block the [bronchus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronchus) resulting in cough or [pneumonia](52135.md); [esophageal cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esophageal_cancer) can cause narrowing of the [esophagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esophagus), making it difficult or painful to swallow; and [colorectal cancer](206979.md) may lead to narrowing or blockages in the [bowel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowel), affecting bowel habits. Masses in breasts or testicles may produce observable lumps. [Ulceration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulcer_\(dermatology\)) can cause bleeding that can lead to symptoms such as [coughing up blood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hemoptysis) (lung cancer), [anemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anemia) or [rectal bleeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_gastrointestinal_bleeding) (colon cancer), [blood in the urine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hematuria) (bladder cancer), or [abnormal vaginal bleeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abnormal_vaginal_bleeding) (endometrial or cervical cancer). Although localized pain may occur in advanced cancer, the initial tumor is usually painless. Some cancers can cause a buildup of fluid within the chest or [abdomen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ascites).[^cite_note-Holland_2000-33]

### Systemic symptoms

Systemic symptoms may occur due to the body's response to the cancer. This may include fatigue, unintentional weight loss, or skin changes.[^cite_note-35] Some cancers can cause a systemic inflammatory state that leads to ongoing muscle loss and weakness, known as [cachexia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cachexia).[^cite_note-36]

Some cancers, such as [Hodgkin's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hodgkin's_disease), [leukemias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia), and [liver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_cancer) or [kidney cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_cancer), can cause a persistent [fever](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fever_of_unknown_origin).[^cite_note-Holland_2000-33]

Shortness of breath, called [dyspnea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shortness_of_breath), is a common symptom of cancer and its treatment. The causes of cancer-related dyspnea can include tumors in or around the lung, blocked airways, fluid in the lungs, pneumonia, or treatment reactions including an [allergic response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allergic_response).[^cite_note-37] Treatment for dyspnea in patients with advanced cancer can include [fans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fan_\(machine\)), bilevel ventilation, [acupressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acupressure)/[reflexology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reflexology) and multicomponent [nonpharmacological interventions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-pharmaceutical_intervention).[^cite_note-38]

Some systemic symptoms of cancer are caused by hormones or other molecules produced by the tumor, known as [paraneoplastic syndromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraneoplastic_syndromes). Common paraneoplastic syndromes include [hypercalcemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypercalcemia), which can cause [altered mental state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Altered_mental_state), constipation and dehydration, or [hyponatremia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyponatremia), which can also cause altered mental status, vomiting, headaches, or seizures.[^cite_note-39]

### Metastasis

Main article: [Metastasis](152509.md)

[Metastasis](152509.md) is the spread of cancer to other locations in the body. The dispersed tumors are called metastatic tumors, while the original is called the primary tumor. Almost all cancers can metastasize.[^cite_note-metastasis-40] Most cancer deaths are due to cancer that has metastasized.[^cite_note-What_is_Metastasized_Cancer-41]

Metastasis is common in the late stages of cancer and it can occur via the blood or the [lymphatic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphatic_system) or both. The typical steps in metastasis are:

1. Local [invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_\(cancer\))

2. [Intravasation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravasation) into the blood or lymph

3. Circulation through the body.

4. [Extravasation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extravasation) into the new tissue

5. Proliferation

6. [Angiogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiogenesis)

Different types of cancers tend to metastasize to particular organs. Overall, the most common places for metastases to occur are the [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung), [liver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver), brain, and the [bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone).[^cite_note-metastasis-40]

While some cancers can be cured if detected early, metastatic cancer is more difficult to treat and control. Nevertheless, some recent treatments are demonstrating encouraging results.[^cite_note-42]

## Causes

Main article: [Causes of cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Causes_of_cancer)



The GHS [Hazard pictogram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazard_symbol) for carcinogenic substances



Share of cancer deaths attributed to tobacco in 2016

[^cite_note-43]

The majority of cancers, some 90–95% of cases, are due to environmental and lifestyle factors.[^cite_note-Enviro2008-3] The remaining 5–10% are due to [inherited genetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heredity).[^cite_note-Enviro2008-3] *[Environmental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_\(biophysical\))* refers to any cause that is not inherited, such as lifestyle, economic, and behavioral factors and not merely pollution.[^cite_note-isbn0-387-78192-7-44] Common environmental factors that contribute to cancer death include tobacco use (25–30%), diet and [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity) (30–35%), infections (15–20%), [radiation](25856.md) (both ionizing and non-ionizing, up to 10%), lack of [physical activity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_exercise), and pollution.[^cite_note-Enviro2008-3][^cite_note-45] Psychological stress does not appear to be a risk factor for the onset of cancer,[^cite_note-Cohen2019-46][^cite_note-pmid23393080-47] though it may worsen outcomes in those who already have cancer.[^cite_note-Cohen2019-46]

Environmental or lifestyle factors that caused cancer to develop in an individual can be identified by analyzing mutational signatures from genomic sequencing of tumor DNA. For example, this can reveal if lung cancer was caused by tobacco smoke, if skin cancer was caused by UV radiation, or if [secondary cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_malignant_neoplasm) were caused by previous chemotherapy treatment.[^cite_note-cds-48]

Cancer is generally not a [transmissible disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmission_\(medicine\)).[^cite_note-Tolar-49] Exceptions include rare transmissions that occur with pregnancies and occasional [organ donors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_donation). However, transmissible infectious diseases such as [hepatitis B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_B), [Epstein–Barr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein%E2%80%93Barr_virus), [HPV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_papillomavirus_infection) and [HIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AIDS/HIV) infections, can contribute to the development of cancer.[^cite_note-50]

### Chemicals

Further information: [Alcohol and cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_and_cancer) and [Smoking and cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smoking_and_cancer)



The incidence of lung cancer is highly correlated with smoking.

Exposure to particular substances has been linked to specific types of cancer. These substances are called *[carcinogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogen)*.

[Tobacco smoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_smoking), for example, causes 90% of lung cancer.[^cite_note-pmid9594919-51] Tobacco use can cause cancer throughout the body including in the mouth and throat, [larynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larynx), [esophagus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Esophagus), stomach, bladder, kidney, cervix, colon/rectum, liver and [pancreas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancreas).[^cite_note-pmid12270001-52][^cite_note-53] Tobacco smoke contains over fifty known carcinogens, including [nitrosamines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nitrosamine) and [polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycyclic_aromatic_hydrocarbon).[^cite_note-Kuper-54]

Tobacco is responsible for about one in five cancer deaths worldwide[^cite_note-Kuper-54] and about one in three in the developed world.[^cite_note-Sasco-55] Lung cancer death rates in the [United States](3434750.md) have mirrored [smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_smoking) patterns, with increases in smoking followed by dramatic increases in lung cancer death rates and, more recently, decreases in smoking rates since the 1950s followed by decreases in lung cancer death rates in men since 1990.[^cite_note-pmid16998161-56][^cite_note-pmid18434333-57]

Alcohol increases the risk of cancer of the breast (in women), throat, liver, osophagus, mouth, larynx, and colon.[^cite_note-58] In Western Europe, 10% of cancers in males and 3% of cancers in females are attributed to alcohol, especially liver and digestive tract cancers.[^cite_note-pmid21474525-59] Cancer from work-related substance exposures may cause between 2 and 20% of cases,[^cite_note-pmid18055160-60] causing at least 200,000 deaths.[^cite_note-WHO_occup-61] Cancers such as lung cancer and [mesothelioma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesothelioma) can come from inhaling tobacco smoke or [asbestos](21492663.md) fibers, or [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia) from exposure to [benzene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benzene).[^cite_note-WHO_occup-61]

Exposure to [perfluorooctanoic acid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfluorooctanoic_acid) (PFOA), which is predominantly used in the production of [Teflon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polytetrafluoroethylene), has been associated with an increased risk of kidney cancer.[^cite_note-62]

Chemotherapy drugs such as [platinum-based](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum-based_antineoplastic) compounds are [carcinogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogens) that increase the risk of [secondary cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_malignant_neoplasm).[^cite_note-cds-48]

[Azathioprine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azathioprine), an [immunosuppressive medication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunosuppressive_medication), is a [carcinogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogen) that can cause [primary tumors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_tumors) to develop.[^cite_note-cds-48]

### Diet and exercise

Main article: [Diet and cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_and_cancer)

&#x20;Diet, [physical inactivity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sedentary_lifestyle), and [obesity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity) are related to up to 30–35% of cancer deaths.[^cite_note-Enviro2008-3][^cite_note-Nutri06-63] In the United States, excess body weight is associated with the development of many types of cancer and is a factor in 14–20% of cancer deaths.[^cite_note-Nutri06-63] A UK study including data on over 5 million people showed higher [body mass index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Body_mass_index) to be related to at least 10 types of cancer and responsible for around 12,000 cases each year in that country.[^cite_note-BhaskaranLancet-64] Physical inactivity is believed to contribute to cancer risk, not only through its effect on body weight but also through negative effects on the [immune system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system) and [endocrine system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine_system).[^cite_note-Nutri06-63] More than half of the effect from the diet is due to [overnutrition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overnutrition) (eating too much), rather than from eating too few vegetables or other healthful foods.[^cite_note-65]

Some specific foods are linked to specific cancers. A high-salt diet is linked to [gastric cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastric_cancer).[^cite_note-pmid18990005-66] [Aflatoxin B1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aflatoxin_B1), a frequent food contaminant, causes liver cancer.[^cite_note-pmid18990005-66] [Betel nut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Betel_nut) chewing can cause oral cancer.[^cite_note-pmid18990005-66] National differences in dietary practices may partly explain differences in cancer incidence. For example, gastric cancer is more common in Japan due to its high-salt diet[^cite_note-pmid19107449-67] while [colon cancer](206979.md) is more common in the United States. Immigrant cancer profiles mirror those of their new country, often within one generation.[^cite_note-pmid14278899-68]

### Infection

Main article: [Infectious causes of cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_causes_of_cancer)

Worldwide, approximately 18% of cancer deaths are related to [infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease).[^cite_note-Enviro2008-3] This proportion ranges from a high of 25% in Africa to less than 10% in the developed world.[^cite_note-Enviro2008-3] Viruses[^cite_note-69] are the usual infectious agents that cause cancer but [bacteria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bacteria) and [parasites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parasites) may also play a role. [Oncoviruses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncovirus) (viruses that can cause cancer) include:

- [Human papillomavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_papillomavirus) ([cervical cancer](53338.md)),

- [Epstein–Barr virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epstein%E2%80%93Barr_virus) ([B-cell lymphoproliferative disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B-cell_lymphoproliferative_disease) and [nasopharyngeal carcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasopharyngeal_carcinoma)),

- [Kaposi's sarcoma herpesvirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaposi's_sarcoma_herpesvirus) ([Kaposi's sarcoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaposi's_sarcoma) and primary effusion lymphomas),

- [Hepatitis B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_B) and [hepatitis C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_C) viruses ([hepatocellular carcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatocellular_carcinoma))

- [Human T-cell leukemia virus-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_T-cell_leukemia_virus-1) (T-cell leukemias).

- [Merkel cell polyomavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkel_cell_polyomavirus) ([Merkel cell carcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merkel_cell_carcinoma))

Bacterial infection may also increase the risk of cancer, as seen in

- *[Helicobacter pylori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicobacter_pylori)*-induced [gastric carcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastric_carcinoma).

- [Colibactin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colibactin), a [genotoxin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genotoxin) associated with *[Escherichia coli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Escherichia_coli)* infection ([colorectal cancer](206979.md))

Parasitic infections associated with cancer include:

- The [liver flukes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_fluke), *[Opisthorchis viverrini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opisthorchis_viverrini)* and *[Clonorchis sinensis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clonorchis_sinensis)* ([cholangiocarcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cholangiocarcinoma)).

### Radiation

Main article: [Radiation-induced cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation-induced_cancer)

Radiation exposure such as [ultraviolet radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet_radiation) and radioactive material is a risk factor for cancer.[^cite_note-NCI2019Rad-73][^cite_note-WHO2019-74][^cite_note-75] Many [non-melanoma skin cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-melanoma_skin_cancer) are due to ultraviolet radiation, mostly from sunlight.[^cite_note-WHO2019-74] Sources of ionizing radiation include [medical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging) and [radon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon) gas.[^cite_note-WHO2019-74]

Residential exposure to [radon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon) gas has similar cancer risks as [passive smoking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passive_smoking). Radiation is a more potent source of cancer when combined with other cancer-causing agents, such as radon plus tobacco smoke. Radiation can cause cancer in most parts of the body, and at any age. Children are twice as likely to develop radiation-induced leukemia as adults; radiation exposure before birth has ten times the effect.[^cite_note-Little-76]

Medical use of ionizing radiation is a small but growing source of radiation-induced cancers. Ionizing radiation may be used to treat other cancers, but this may, in some cases, induce a second form of cancer.[^cite_note-Little-76] It is also used in some kinds of [medical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging).[^cite_note-pmid18046031-77]

Prolonged exposure to [ultraviolet radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet_radiation) from the [sun](26751.md) can lead to [melanoma](716631.md) and other skin malignancies.[^cite_note-Cleaver_2000-78] Ultraviolet radiation, especially the non-ionizing medium wave [UVB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UVB), is the cause of most non-melanoma [skin cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_cancer), which are the most common forms of cancer in the world.[^cite_note-Cleaver_2000-78][^cite_note-wang-79]

Non-ionizing [radio frequency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_frequency) radiation from mobile phones, [electric power transmission](38824.md) and other similar sources has been described as a [possible carcinogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Possible_carcinogen) by the [World Health Organization](33583.md)'s [International Agency for Research on Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Agency_for_Research_on_Cancer).[^cite_note-80] Evidence, however, has not supported a concern.[^cite_note-NCI2019EF-81][^cite_note-NCI2019Rad-73] There is no demonstrable link between mobile phone radiation and cancer risk.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-WCR_ion_2020-83]

### Heredity

Main article: [Cancer syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_syndrome)

Most cancers are non-hereditary (sporadic). [Hereditary cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditary_cancer) are caused by inherited genetics. Less than 0.3% of the population are carriers of a genetic mutation that has a large effect on cancer risk and these cause less than 3–10% of cancer.[^cite_note-Expert09-84] Some of these [syndromes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syndrome) include: certain inherited mutations in the genes *[BRCA1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRCA1)* and *[BRCA2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRCA2)* with a more than 75% risk of breast cancer and [ovarian cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovarian_cancer),[^cite_note-Expert09-84] and [hereditary nonpolyposis colorectal cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditary_nonpolyposis_colorectal_cancer) (HNPCC or Lynch syndrome), which is present in about 3% of people with [colorectal cancer](206979.md),[^cite_note-Lancet10-85] among others.

The [relative risk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_risk) of developing [colorectal cancer](206979.md) (the cancer with the highest mortality) when a [first-degree relative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-degree_relative) (parent, sibling or child) has been diagnosed with it is about 2.[^cite_note-Kampman2007-86] The corresponding relative risk is 1.5 for lung cancer,[^cite_note-CotéLiu2012-87] and 1.9 for [prostate cancer](88078.md).[^cite_note-Watkins_BrunerMoore2003-88] For breast cancer, the relative risk is 1.8 with a first-degree relative having developed it at 50 years of age or older, and 3.3 when the relative developed it when being younger than 50 years of age.[^cite_note-Singletary2003-89]

Taller people have an increased risk of cancer because they have more cells than shorter people. Since height is genetically determined to a large extent, taller people have a heritable increase in cancer risk.[^cite_note-90]

### Physical agents

Some substances cause cancer primarily through their physical, rather than chemical, effects.[^cite_note-Maltoni_2000-91] A prominent example of this is prolonged exposure to [asbestos](21492663.md), naturally occurring mineral fibers that are a major cause of [mesothelioma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesothelioma) (cancer of the [serous membrane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serous_membrane)) usually the serous membrane surrounding the lungs.[^cite_note-Maltoni_2000-91] Other substances in this category, including both naturally occurring and synthetic asbestos-like fibers, such as [wollastonite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wollastonite), [attapulgite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attapulgite), [glass wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glass_wool) and [rock wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rock_wool), are believed to have similar effects.[^cite_note-Maltoni_2000-91] Non-fibrous particulate materials that cause cancer include powdered metallic [cobalt](24580536.md) and [nickel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nickel) and [crystalline silica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crystalline_silica) ([quartz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartz), [cristobalite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cristobalite) and [tridymite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tridymite)).[^cite_note-Maltoni_2000-91] Usually, physical carcinogens must get inside the body (such as through inhalation) and require years of exposure to produce cancer.[^cite_note-Maltoni_2000-91]

Physical trauma resulting in cancer is relatively rare.[^cite_note-Gaeta_2000-92] Claims that breaking bones resulted in bone cancer, for example, have not been proven.[^cite_note-Gaeta_2000-92] Similarly, physical trauma is not accepted as a cause for cervical cancer, breast cancer or brain cancer.[^cite_note-Gaeta_2000-92] One accepted source is frequent, long-term application of hot objects to the body. It is possible that repeated burns on the same part of the body, such as those produced by [kanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanger) and kairo heaters (charcoal [hand warmers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hand_warmer)), may produce skin cancer, especially if carcinogenic chemicals are also present.[^cite_note-Gaeta_2000-92] Frequent consumption of scalding hot tea may produce esophageal cancer.[^cite_note-Gaeta_2000-92] Generally, it is believed that cancer arises, or a pre-existing cancer is encouraged, during the process of healing, rather than directly by the trauma.[^cite_note-Gaeta_2000-92] However, repeated injuries to the same tissues might promote excessive cell proliferation, which could then increase the odds of a cancerous mutation.[^cite_note-93]

Chronic [inflammation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflammation) has been hypothesized to directly cause mutation.[^cite_note-Gaeta_2000-92][^cite_note-ColottaAllavena2009-94] Inflammation can contribute to proliferation, survival, angiogenesis and migration of cancer cells by influencing the [tumor microenvironment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumor_microenvironment).[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96] [Oncogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncogene) build up an inflammatory pro-tumorigenic microenvironment.[^cite_note-97]

### Hormones

[Hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone) also play a role in the development of cancer by promoting [cell proliferation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_growth).[^cite_note-Henderson_2000-98] [Insulin-like growth factors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insulin-like_growth_factor) and their binding proteins play a key role in cancer cell proliferation, differentiation and [apoptosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoptosis), suggesting possible involvement in carcinogenesis.[^cite_note-99]

Hormones are important agents in sex-related cancers, such as cancer of the breast, [endometrium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endometrium), prostate, ovary and [testis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testicle) and also of [thyroid cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid_cancer) and [bone cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_cancer).[^cite_note-Henderson_2000-98] For example, the daughters of women who have breast cancer have significantly higher levels of [estrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrogen) and [progesterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Progesterone) than the daughters of women without breast cancer. These higher hormone levels may explain their higher risk of breast cancer, even in the absence of a breast-cancer gene.[^cite_note-Henderson_2000-98] Similarly, men of African ancestry have significantly higher levels of [testosterone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testosterone) than men of European ancestry and have a correspondingly higher level of prostate cancer.[^cite_note-Henderson_2000-98] Men of Asian ancestry, with the lowest levels of testosterone-activating [androstanediol glucuronide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Androstanediol_glucuronide), have the lowest levels of prostate cancer.[^cite_note-Henderson_2000-98]

Other factors are relevant: obese people have higher levels of some hormones associated with cancer and a higher rate of those cancers.[^cite_note-Henderson_2000-98] Women who take [hormone replacement therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone_replacement_therapy_\(menopause\)) have a higher risk of developing cancers associated with those hormones.[^cite_note-Henderson_2000-98] On the other hand, people who exercise far more than average have lower levels of these hormones and lower risk of cancer.[^cite_note-Henderson_2000-98] [Osteosarcoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteosarcoma) may be promoted by [growth hormones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growth_hormone).[^cite_note-Henderson_2000-98] Some treatments and prevention approaches leverage this cause by artificially reducing hormone levels and thus discouraging hormone-sensitive cancers.[^cite_note-Henderson_2000-98]

### Autoimmune diseases

There is an association between [celiac disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celiac_disease) and an increased risk of all cancers. People with untreated celiac disease have a higher risk, but this risk decreases with time after diagnosis and strict treatment. This may be due to the adoption of a [gluten-free diet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gluten-free_diet), which seems to have a protective role against the development of malignancy in people with celiac disease. However, the delay in diagnosis and initiation of a gluten-free diet seems to increase the risk of malignancies.[^cite_note-pmid26402826-100] Rates of gastrointestinal cancers are increased in people with [Crohn's disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crohn's_disease) and [ulcerative colitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulcerative_colitis), due to chronic inflammation. [Immunomodulators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunotherapy) and [biologic agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_therapy_for_inflammatory_bowel_disease) used to treat these diseases may promote the development of extra-intestinal malignancies.[^cite_note-101]

## Mechanism

Main article: [Carcinogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogenesis)

### Genetics

Main article: [Oncogenomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncogenomics)



Cancers are caused by a series of mutations. Each mutation alters the behavior of the cell somewhat.

Cancer is fundamentally a disease of tissue growth regulation. For a normal cell to [transform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malignant_transformation) into a cancer cell, the [genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene) that regulate cell growth and differentiation must be altered.[^cite_note-pmid18234754-102]

The affected genes are divided into two broad categories. [Oncogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncogene) are genes that promote cell growth and reproduction. [Tumor suppressor genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumor_suppressor_gene) are genes that inhibit cell division and survival. Malignant transformation can occur through the formation of novel oncogenes, the inappropriate over-expression of normal oncogenes, or by the under-expression or disabling of tumor suppressor genes. Typically, changes in multiple genes are required to transform a normal cell into a cancer cell.[^cite_note-pmid11905807-103]

Genetic changes can occur at different levels and by different mechanisms. The gain or loss of an entire [chromosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome) can occur through errors in [mitosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitosis). More common are [mutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation), which are changes in the [nucleotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleotide) sequence of genomic DNA.

Large-scale mutations involve the deletion or gain of a portion of a chromosome. [Genomic amplification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_duplication) occurs when a cell gains copies (often 20 or more) of a small chromosomal locus, usually containing one or more oncogenes and adjacent genetic material. [Translocation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosomal_translocation) occurs when two separate chromosomal regions become abnormally fused, often at a characteristic location. A well-known example of this is the [Philadelphia chromosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_chromosome), or translocation of chromosomes 9 and 22, which occurs in [chronic myelogenous leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_myelogenous_leukemia) and results in production of the [BCR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BCR_\(gene\))-[Abl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABL1) [fusion protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_protein), an oncogenic [tyrosine kinase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrosine_kinase).

Small-scale mutations include point mutations, deletions, and insertions, which may occur in the [promoter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Promoter_\(genetics\)) region of a gene and affect its [expression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_expression), or may occur in the gene's [coding sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coding_sequence) and alter the function or stability of its [protein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protein) product. Disruption of a single gene may also result from [integration of genomic material](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provirus) from a [DNA virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_virus) or [retrovirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retrovirus), leading to the expression of *viral* oncogenes in the affected cell and its descendants.

Replication of the data contained within the DNA of living cells will [probabilistically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Probability) result in some errors (mutations). Complex error correction and prevention are built into the process and safeguard the cell against cancer. If a significant error occurs, the damaged cell can self-destruct through programmed cell death, termed [apoptosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apoptosis). If the error control processes fail, then the mutations will survive and be passed along to [daughter cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_division).

Some environments make errors more likely to arise and propagate. Such environments can include the presence of disruptive substances called [carcinogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogen), repeated physical injury, heat, ionising radiation, or [hypoxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypoxia_\(medical\)).[^cite_note-104]

The errors that cause cancer are self-amplifying and compounding, for example:

- A mutation in the error-correcting machinery of a cell might cause that cell and its children to accumulate errors more rapidly.

- A further mutation in an oncogene might cause the cell to reproduce more rapidly and more frequently than its normal counterparts.

- A further mutation may cause the loss of a tumor suppressor gene, disrupting the apoptosis signaling pathway and immortalizing the cell.

- A further mutation in the signaling machinery of the cell might send error-causing signals to nearby cells.

The transformation of a normal cell into cancer is akin to a [chain reaction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_reaction) caused by initial errors, which compound into more severe errors, each progressively allowing the cell to escape more controls that limit normal tissue growth. This rebellion-like scenario is an undesirable [survival of the fittest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Survival_of_the_fittest), where the driving forces of [evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evolution) work against the body's design and enforcement of order. Once cancer has begun to develop, this ongoing process, termed *[clonal evolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somatic_evolution_in_cancer)*, drives progression towards more invasive [stages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_staging).[^cite_note-pmid17109012-105] Clonal evolution leads to intra-[tumour heterogeneity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumour_heterogeneity) (cancer cells with heterogeneous mutations) that complicates the design of effective treatments, as therapies must account for the selective pressures they exert on the tumour cell population.

Characteristic abilities developed by cancers are divided into categories, specifically evasion of apoptosis, replicating independently of external growth signals, insensitivity to anti-growth signals, sustained blood vessel formation, limitless replicative potential, spreading to other parts of the body ([metastasis](152509.md)), reprogramming of energy metabolism and evasion of immune destruction.[^cite_note-Han2000-31][^cite_note-Han2011-32]

### Epigenetics

Main article: [Cancer epigenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_epigenetics)



The central role of DNA damage and epigenetic defects in DNA repair genes in carcinogenesis

The classical view of cancer is a set of diseases driven by progressive [genetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetics) [abnormalities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abnormalities) that include mutations in tumor-suppressor genes and [oncogenes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncogene), and in [chromosomal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome) abnormalities. A role for [epigenetic alterations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics) was identified in the early 21st century.[^cite_note-106]

[Epigenetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epigenetics) alterations are functionally relevant modifications to the genome that do not change the nucleotide sequence. Examples of such modifications are changes in [DNA methylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_methylation) (hypermethylation and hypomethylation), [histone modification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histone_modification)[^cite_note-107] and changes in chromosomal architecture (caused by inappropriate expression of proteins such as [HMGA2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMGA2) or [HMGA1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMGA1)).[^cite_note-108] Each of these alterations regulates gene expression without altering the underlying [DNA sequence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_sequence). These changes may remain through [cell divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cell_division), endure for multiple generations, and can be considered as equivalent to mutations.

Epigenetic alterations occur frequently in cancers. As an example, one study listed protein coding genes that were frequently altered in their [methylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methylation) in association with colon cancer. These included 147 hypermethylated and 27 hypomethylated genes. Of the hypermethylated genes, 10 were hypermethylated in 100% of colon cancers and many others were hypermethylated in more than 50% of colon cancers.[^cite_note-Sch-109]

While epigenetic alterations are found in cancers, the epigenetic alterations in DNA repair genes, causing reduced expression of DNA repair proteins, may be of particular importance. Such alterations may occur early in the progression to cancer and are a possible cause of the [genetic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genome_instability) instability characteristic of cancers.[^cite_note-110][^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112]

Reduced expression of DNA repair genes disrupts DNA repair. This is shown in the figure at the 4th level from the top. (In the figure, red wording indicates the central role of DNA damage and defects in DNA repair in the progression to cancer.) When DNA repair is deficient DNA damage remains in cells at a higher than usual level (5th level) and causes increased frequencies of mutation and/or epimutation (6th level). Mutation rates increase substantially in cells defective in [DNA mismatch repair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_mismatch_repair)[^cite_note-113][^cite_note-114] or in [homologous recombinational](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homologous_recombination) repair (HRR).[^cite_note-115] Chromosomal rearrangements and [aneuploidy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aneuploidy) (an abnormal number of chromosomes in a cell) also increase in HRR defective cells.[^cite_note-116]

Higher levels of DNA damage cause increased mutation (right side of figure) and increased epimutation. During repair of DNA double strand breaks, or repair of other DNA damage, incompletely cleared repair sites can cause epigenetic gene silencing.[^cite_note-117]

Deficient expression of DNA repair proteins due to an inherited mutation can increase cancer risks. Individuals with an inherited impairment in any of 34 DNA repair genes (see article [DNA repair-deficiency disorder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA_repair-deficiency_disorder)) have increased cancer risk, with some defects ensuring a 100% lifetime chance of cancer (e.g. p53 mutations).[^cite_note-118] Germline DNA repair mutations are noted on the figure's left side. However, such [germline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germline) mutations (which cause highly penetrant cancer syndromes) are the cause of only about 1 percent of cancers.[^cite_note-119]

In sporadic cancers, deficiencies in DNA repair are occasionally caused by a mutation in a DNA repair gene but are much more frequently caused by epigenetic alterations that reduce or silence expression of DNA repair genes. This is indicated in the figure at the 3rd level. Many studies of heavy metal-induced carcinogenesis show that such heavy metals cause a reduction in expression of DNA repair enzymes, some through epigenetic mechanisms. DNA repair inhibition is proposed to be a predominant mechanism in heavy metal-induced carcinogenicity. In addition, frequent epigenetic alterations of the DNA sequences code for small RNAs called [microRNAs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MicroRNA) (or miRNAs). miRNAs do not code for proteins, but can "target" protein-coding genes and reduce their expression.

Cancers usually arise from an assemblage of mutations and epimutations that confer a selective advantage leading to clonal expansion (see [Field defects in progression to cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplasm#Field_defects_in_progression_to_cancer)). Mutations, however, may not be as frequent in cancers as epigenetic alterations. An average cancer of the breast or colon can have about 60 to 70 protein-altering mutations, of which about three or four may be "driver" mutations and the remaining ones may be "passenger" mutations.[^cite_note-120]



Metastasis

### Metastasis

Main article: [Metastasis](152509.md)

[Metastasis](152509.md) is the spread of cancer to other locations in the body. The dispersed tumors are called metastatic tumors, while the original is called the primary tumor. Almost all cancers can metastasize.[^cite_note-metastasis-40] Most cancer deaths are due to cancer that has metastasized.[^cite_note-What_is_Metastasized_Cancer-41]

Metastasis is common in the late stages of cancer and it can occur via the blood or the [lymphatic system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphatic_system) or both. The typical steps in metastasis are local [invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_\(cancer\)), [intravasation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intravasation) (entry of cancer cells into a blood or lymph vessel), circulation through the body, [extravasation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extravasation) (exit from the vessel into surrounding tissue), proliferation and [angiogenesis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiogenesis) (formation of new blood vessels to supply the tumour). Different types of cancers tend to metastasize to particular organs, but overall the most common places for metastases to occur are the [lungs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lung), [liver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver), brain and the [bones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone).[^cite_note-metastasis-40]

### Metabolism

Main article: [Tumor metabolome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tumor_metabolome)

Normal cells typically generate about 30% of energy from [glycolysis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glycolysis),[^cite_note-pmid23226794-121] whereas most cancers rely on glycolysis for energy production ([Warburg effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warburg_effect_\(oncology\))).[^cite_note-pmid20181022-122][^cite_note-pmid31781842-123] But a minority of cancer types rely on [oxidative phosphorylation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxidative_phosphorylation) as the primary energy source, including [lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoma), [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia), and [endometrial cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endometrial_cancer).[^cite_note-pmid33028168-124] However, even in cancers which derive most of their energy from glycolysis, the use of glycolysis as an energy source rarely exceeds 60%.[^cite_note-pmid23226794-121] A few cancers use [glutamine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glutamine) as the major energy source, partly because it provides nitrogen required for [nucleotide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nucleotide) (DNA, RNA) synthesis.[^cite_note-pmid26771115-125] [Cancer stem cells](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_stem_cell) often use oxidative phosphorylation or glutamine as a primary energy source.[^cite_note-pmid32670883-126]

Most cancers are initially recognized either because of the appearance of signs or symptoms or through [screening](22848246.md).[^cite_note-127] Neither of these leads to a definitive diagnosis, which requires the examination of a tissue sample by a [pathologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anatomical_pathology).[^cite_note-HollandFrei6-128] People with suspected cancer are investigated with [medical tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_test). These commonly include [blood tests](336557.md), [X-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Projectional_radiography), ([contrast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contrast_CT)) [CT scans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan) and [endoscopy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endoscopy).[^cite_note-129]

The tissue [diagnosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_diagnosis) from the biopsy indicates the type of cell that is proliferating, its [histological grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histological_grade), genetic abnormalities and other features. Together, this information is useful to evaluate the [prognosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prognosis) and to choose the best treatment.

[Cytogenetics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytogenetics) and [immunohistochemistry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunohistochemistry) are other types of tissue tests. These tests provide information about molecular changes (such as [mutations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutation), [fusion genes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fusion_gene) and numerical [chromosome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chromosome) changes) and may thus also indicate the prognosis and best treatment.

Cancer diagnosis can cause psychological distress and psychosocial interventions, such as talking therapy, may help people with this.[^cite_note-130] Some people choose to disclose the diagnosis widely; others prefer to keep the information private, especially shortly after the diagnosis, or to disclose it only partially or to selected people.[^cite_note-131]

A large proportion of cancers are [adenocarcinomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenocarcinoma), with typical [histopathology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Histopathology) features shown, although they vary substantially from case to case.

Cancers are classified by the [type of cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_distinct_cell_types_in_the_adult_human_body) that the tumor cells resemble and is therefore presumed to be the origin of the tumor. These types include:

- [Carcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinoma): Cancers derived from [epithelial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epithelium) cells. This group includes many of the most common cancers and include nearly all those in the [breast](70547.md), [prostate](88078.md), [lung](18450.md), [pancreas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pancreas) and [colon](206979.md). Most of these are of the [adenocarcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adenocarcinoma) type, which means that the cancer has [gland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gland)-like differentiation.

- [Sarcoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarcoma): Cancers arising from [connective tissue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connective_tissue) (i.e. [bone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone), [cartilage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cartilage), fat, [nerve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nerve)), each of which develops from cells originating in [mesenchymal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesenchyme) cells outside the [bone marrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_marrow).

- [Lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoma) and [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia): These two classes arise from hematopoietic (blood-forming) cells that leave the marrow and tend to mature in the [lymph nodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymph_node) and blood, respectively.

  [^cite_note-132]

- [Germ cell tumor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germ_cell_tumor): Cancers derived from [pluripotent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluripotent) cells, most often presenting in the [testicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testicular_cancer) or the [ovary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovarian_cancer) ([seminoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminoma) and [dysgerminoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dysgerminoma), respectively).

- [Blastoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blastoma): Cancers derived from immature "precursor" cells or embryonic tissue.

Cancers are usually named using *-carcinoma*, *-sarcoma* or *-blastoma* as a suffix, with the Latin or Greek word for the [organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Organ_\(anatomy\)) or tissue of origin as the root. For example, cancers of the liver [parenchyma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parenchyma) arising from malignant epithelial cells is called *[hepatocarcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatocellular_carcinoma)*, while a malignancy arising from primitive liver precursor cells is called a [hepatoblastoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatoblastoma) and a cancer arising from fat cells is called a *[liposarcoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liposarcoma)*. For some common cancers, the English organ name is used. For example, the most common type of breast cancer is called *[ductal carcinoma of the breast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammary_ductal_carcinoma)*. Here, the adjective *ductal* refers to the appearance of cancer under the microscope, which suggests that it has originated in the milk ducts.

[Benign tumors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benign_tumor) (which are not cancers) are named using *-oma* as a suffix with the organ name as the root. For example, a benign tumor of smooth muscle cells is called a *[leiomyoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leiomyoma)* (the common name of this frequently occurring benign tumor in the uterus is *[fibroid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uterine_fibroid)*). Confusingly, some types of cancer use the *-noma* suffix, examples including [melanoma](716631.md) and [seminoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminoma).

Some types of cancer are named for the size and shape of the cells under a microscope, such as giant cell carcinoma, [spindle cell carcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spindle_cell_carcinoma) and [small-cell carcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Small-cell_carcinoma).

## Prevention

Cancer prevention involves changes on the individual level, as well as the public policy level. Around 40% of cancer can be prevented by avoiding or eliminating risk factors.[^cite_note-WHOcancer-135][^cite_note-GBD_2023-136][^cite_note-Lancet_2022-134][^cite_note-Soerjomataram_2026-137]

Cancer prevention is more cost-effective than treatment. On an individual level, cancer can be prevented by avoiding [carcinogens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogens) such as with smoking, limiting sun exposure, maintaining a healthy weight and active lifestyle, and following medical guidance designed to decrease risk for cancer. Public policy changes are required to expand individual access to cancer prevention interventions, as well as to address collective issues that cause cancer such as [air pollution](10934212.md). Due to having shared causes in common, efforts to prevent cancer often help prevent other diseases as well as improve the environment.[^cite_note-WCRF/AICR_Third_Expert_Report-138]

### Diet and lifestyle

Main article: [Diet and cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diet_and_cancer)

Cancer prevention through diet generally emphasizes broad, healthy eating habits over focusing on specific foods. The risk for cancer is decreased with a diet high in [fiber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dietary_fiber), fruits and vegetables, and low in [red](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_meat) and [processed meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Processed_meat).[^cite_note-WCRFI_2025-139][^cite_note-WCRF/AICR_Third_Expert_Report-138][^cite_note-WCR_diet_2020-140] Replacing refined sugars with whole grains, and consuming plant-based proteins and fats also helps reduce the risk of cancer.[^cite_note-WCR_diet_2020-140]

Maintaining a healthy body weight and being physically active helps prevent cancer.[^cite_note-WCRFI_2025-139][^cite_note-WCRF/AICR_Third_Expert_Report-138] [Obesity increases the risk for cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity_and_cancer), partly through causing [inflammation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inflammation) and metabolic and hormonal abnormalities.[^cite_note-WCRF/AICR_Third_Expert_Report-138] Exercise promotes healthy immune and hormonal systems that reduce cancer.[^cite_note-WCRF/AICR_Third_Expert_Report-138]

Diets high in calcium (such as dairy products) and coffee, and low in processed meat, can help prevent [colorectal cancer](206979.md).[^cite_note-WCRFI_2025-139]

#### Alcohol

Main article: [Alcohol and cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcohol_and_cancer)

Alcohol is a [group 1 carcinogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IARC_group_1), and is responsible for around 4% of all cancer deaths.[^cite_note-WCR_Alcohol_2020-4] Any amount of alcohol increases the risk for cancer.[^cite_note-WCR_Alcohol_2020-4] Alcohol causes several types of cancer, through different mechanisms including the breakdown of ethanol into [acetaldehyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acetaldehyde), which damages DNA.[^cite_note-WCR_Alcohol_2020-4]

Alcohol increases [estrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrogen), which contributes to the development of [breast cancer](70547.md).[^cite_note-WCRFI_2025-139][^cite_note-WCRF/AICR_Third_Expert_Report-138] Alcohol acts as a solvent for some carcinogens, allowing them to penetrate cells more easily.[^cite_note-WCR_Alcohol_2020-4][^cite_note-WCRF/AICR_Third_Expert_Report-138] For instance, consuming alcohol along with tobacco helps carcinogens in tobacco penetrate the cells of the mouth and cause [mouth cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mouth_cancer).[^cite_note-NCI_Alcohol_2025-141]

#### Tobacco

See also: [Health effects of smoking tobacco § Cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_effects_of_smoking_tobacco#Cancer)

Smoking is the number one preventable cause of cancer.[^cite_note-Soerjomataram_2026-137] Smoking causes at least 20 different types of cancer.[^cite_note-WCR_tobacco_2020-142] Cigars, [pipes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pipe_smoking) (including water pipes), and smokeless tobacco cause cancer.[^cite_note-WCR_tobacco_2020-142]

More than 1.2 million people die every year from exposure to [second-hand smoke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second-hand_smoke), around 100,000 of these deaths from cancer.[^cite_note-WCR_tobacco_2020-142]

The health impact of [electronic cigarettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronic_cigarettes) (vaping) and similar products is unknown and subject to debate.[^cite_note-WCR_tobacco_2020-142]

#### Breastfeeding

Breastfeeding reduces the mother's risk for developing breast cancer.[^cite_note-WCRF/AICR_Third_Expert_Report-138] Infants who breastfed are less likely to gain excess weight later in life.[^cite_note-WCRF/AICR_Third_Expert_Report-138] A healthy weight helps to reduce the risk for several types of cancer.[^cite_note-WCRF/AICR_Third_Expert_Report-138]

### Environment

#### UV radiation



A man wearing sunscreen only on the right side of his face. The left image is a regular photo of his face. The right image is a photo of reflected UV light; the right side of the man's face is darker because the sunscreen absorbs the UV light.

[Ultraviolet radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet_radiation) is the main cause of [skin cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_cancer).[^cite_note-ECAC_2015-143] The incidence of skin cancer is increasing, and it is the most common type of cancer in lighter-skinned populations.[^cite_note-ECAC_2015-143][^cite_note-WCR_UV_2020-144] UV radiation, whether from sunlight or [tanning beds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanning_bed), directly and indirectly damages DNA.[^cite_note-WCR_UV_2020-144]

The most effective way to prevent skin cancer is to limit sun exposure and not use tanning beds, wear protective clothing (such as a hat), wear sunscreen, and make use of the shade.[^cite_note-WCR_UV_2020-144][^cite_note-ECAC_2015-143] The best time to avoid sunlight is between 9\:00 a.m. and 3\:00 p.m. when UV radiation is strongest.[^cite_note-WCR_UV_2020-144] Even on cloudy days about 80% of UV radiation is still present.[^cite_note-WCR_UV_2020-144] UV radiation does not correlate with temperature—there is just as much UV radiation from the sun on a cold day as on a hot day.[^cite_note-WCR_UV_2020-144] About 10% of [UVB radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultraviolet#UVB) passes through glass windows.[^cite_note-WCR_UV_2020-144]

UV exposure during childhood is particularly harmful.[^cite_note-ECAC_2015-143] However, the [beneficial effects of sunlight exposure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_effects_of_sunlight_exposure) (such as the production of [vitamin D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_D)) can be achieved while avoiding harmful amounts of sun exposure.[^cite_note-ECAC_2015-143]

The [ozone layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ozone_layer) absorbs UV radiation from the sun, reducing rates of skin cancer.[^cite_note-EEAP_2023-145] However, atmospheric pollution has decreased the ozone layer, and increased rates of skin cancer globally. In 1987, an international treaty named the [Montreal Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montreal_Protocol) was signed to limit the release of ozone-damaging chemicals. Since then the ozone layer has been recovering, and it is expected that by 2100 the Montreal Protocol will have prevented 11 million cases of [melanoma](716631.md) in the United States.[^cite_note-EEAP_2023-145]

#### Air quality

Air pollution, both outdoor and indoor, is the largest environmental contributor to cancer.[^cite_note-WCR_Air_2020-146] Air pollution caused an estimated 350,167 deaths from lung cancer in 2017.[^cite_note-WCR_Air_2020-146]

Airborne [particulate matter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate_matter) smaller than 2.5 [µm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Micrometre) (PM<sub>2.5</sub>) is a [group 1 carcinogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IARC_group_1).[^cite_note-IASLC_air_2023-147] The concentration of PM<sub>2.5</sub> in the air is the most reliable predictor of how carcinogenic the air is.[^cite_note-WCR_Air_2020-146] PM<sub>2.5</sub> primarily comes from the burning of fuel for transportation, power generation, and industry, the burning of [biomass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biomass) (e.g. [forest fires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_fires), [stubble burning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stubble_burning)), and heating and cooking in the home.[^cite_note-WCR_Air_2020-146] In 2017, an estimated 14.1% of all lung cancer deaths were caused by PM<sub>2.5</sub> pollution.[^cite_note-IASLC_air_2023-147]

Local PM<sub>2.5</sub> and PM<sub>10</sub> levels can be monitored online,[^cite_note-aqicn-148] and wearing a close-fitting [particulate respirator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Particulate_respirator) reduces personal exposure when levels are high.[^cite_note-IASLC_air_2023-147] Exposure to cancer-causing air pollution can also be reduced by avoiding motorized transportation, selecting routes away from pollution sources such as coal plants, and keeping vehicle windows closed in heavy traffic.[^cite_note-IASLC_air_2023-147]

Decreasing outdoor air pollution requires action on the public policy level, and involves international collaborative efforts.[^cite_note-IASLC_air_2023-147]

Indoor air pollution includes PM<sub>2.5</sub> from the burning of fuels, as well as other pollutants such as [radon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon) and hazardous construction materials (e.g. [asbestos](21492663.md), glues, formaldehyde, lead).[^cite_note-WCR_Air_2020-146] In 2019, the percent of preventable cases of lung cancer by cause was estimated as 62% for smoking, 15% for PM<sub>2.5</sub> in outdoor air, 5.8% for secondhand smoke, 4% from using [solid fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid_fuel) for cooking, and 4% for radon.[^cite_note-IASLC_air_2023-147] Individuals can decrease air pollution in the home by using [clean fuels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_fuel), maintaining adequate ventilation, and using [air purifiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_purifier).[^cite_note-IASLC_air_2023-147]

#### Ionizing radiation

Around 80% of [ionizing radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionizing_radiation) that people are exposed to comes from natural sources, around half of which comes from breathing in radioactive [radon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon) gas.[^cite_note-WCR_ion_2020-83] Radon is produced by the decay of [uranium](31743.md) in the ground, becoming a radioactive gas that can accumulate in the buildings above it.[^cite_note-WCR_ion_2020-83] Exposure to radon can be reduced by testing homes and, when found to be high, taking steps to [reduce indoor radon levels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radon_mitigation).[^cite_note-A_Citizen's_Guide_to_Radon-149] The primary method to reduce indoor radon levels is a vent pipe system and fan, which pulls radon from beneath the building and vents it to the outside.[^cite_note-A_Citizen's_Guide_to_Radon-149]

Around 20% of ionizing radiation that people are exposed to comes from artificial sources, primarily from [x-rays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/X-rays) used for medical imaging.[^cite_note-WCR_ion_2020-83] [CT scans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scans), which take x-rays at many angles to produce a 3D image, expose patients to a higher level of radiation than standard 2D x-rays.[^cite_note-WCR_ion_2020-83] Cancer prevention focuses on avoiding unnecessary diagnostic procedures, and minimizing the dose of ionizing radiation that patients are exposed to during these procedures.[^cite_note-WCR_ion_2020-83]

#### Occupational exposure

Main article: [Occupational cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupational_cancer)



A worker grinds concrete flooring to prepare it for an [epoxy coating](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epoxy_coating). He wears a [respirator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Respirator) to avoid breathing in silica dust, which causes [silicosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silicosis) and [lung cancer](18450.md).

There have been 38 workplace chemicals and 12 workplace circumstances classified as [carcinogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IARC_group_1), and 41 chemicals and 6 circumstances classified as [probably carcinogenic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IARC_group_2A).[^cite_note-WCR_occupation_2020-150] Despite being known to cause cancer, workers are still commonly exposed to [silica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silica), diesel engine exhaust, heavy metals, asbestos, and [polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polycyclic_aromatic_hydrocarbons).[^cite_note-WCR_occupation_2020-150]

Occupational cancer can be reduced, and in recent decades successful regulations and programs have reduced or eliminated exposure to certain carcinogens in the workplace.[^cite_note-WCR_occupation_2020-150] Prevention involves decreasing the use of certain chemicals and if this isn't possible limiting exposure to these substances, a process known as [hierarchy of controls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hierarchy_of_hazard_controls).[^cite_note-HS2025-151][^cite_note-152] Occupational cancer can also be prevented by improving workplace procedures and training, and by using [personal protective equipment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_protective_equipment).[^cite_note-WCR_occupation_2020-150]

### Medical

#### Infection

Main article: [Infectious causes of cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_causes_of_cancer)

In 2018, 2.2 million cases of cancer were caused by infection, primarily by *[Helicobacter pylori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helicobacter_pylori)* (810,000), [human papillomavirus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_papillomavirus) (690,000), [hepatitis B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_B) (360,000), and [hepatitis C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatitis_C) (160,000).[^cite_note-de_Martel_2020-12] All four of these infections can either be vaccinated against or treated, and collectively they cause more than 90% of infection-related cancers worldwide.[^cite_note-de_Martel_2020-12]

*Helicobacter pylori*, the bacteria responsible for most cases of [stomach ulcers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peptic_ulcer_disease), can also [cause stomach cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinogenesis#Bacterial).[^cite_note-WCR_Infectious_2020-153] Treatment of *Helicobacter pylori* with [antibiotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antibiotic) may help prevent [stomach cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach_cancer).[^cite_note-WCR_Infectious_2020-153] Almost all cases of [cervical cancer](53338.md) are caused by the human papillomavirus, which can be vaccinated against ([Gardasil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gardasil) and [Cervarix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cervarix)).[^cite_note-WCR_Infectious_2020-153][^cite_note-Bergman_2025-154] Hepatitis B and hepatitis C are responsible for 3/4 of the [most common type of liver cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepatocellular_carcinoma).[^cite_note-WCR_Infectious_2020-153] A vaccine for the hepatitis B virus has been available since 1982, and more than 95% of people with hepatitis C can be cured with [antiviral drugs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antiviral_drug#Direct-acting_antivirals).[^cite_note-WCR_Infectious_2020-153]

#### Medication

Medications can be used to prevent cancer in a few circumstances.[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156] In the general population, [NSAIDs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-steroidal_anti-inflammatory_drug) reduce the risk of [colorectal cancer](206979.md); however, due to cardiovascular and gastrointestinal side effects, they cause more harm than benefit when used for prevention.[^cite_note-pmid17339623-157] Aspirin is not recommended for reducing cancer risk.[^cite_note-USPSTF_Aspirin_2020-158] [COX-2 inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COX-2_inhibitor) may decrease the rate of [polyp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyp_\(medicine\)) formation in people with [familial adenomatous polyposis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Familial_adenomatous_polyposis); however, it is associated with the same adverse effects as NSAIDs.[^cite_note-pmid20594533-159]

Women who have an increased risk for breast cancer can lower their chances of getting breast cancer by taking [tamoxifen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamoxifen), [raloxifene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raloxifene), or [aromatase inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aromatase_inhibitor).[^cite_note-USPSTF_breast_cancer_2019-160]

No [vitamin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin), multivitamin, or nutritional supplement is recommended for the prevention of cancer.[^cite_note-USPSTF_supplements_2022-161] [Vitamin E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitamin_E) supplements do not prevent cancer, and [beta-carotene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beta-carotene) supplements increase the odds of lung cancer in those already at a high risk for it.[^cite_note-USPSTF_supplements_2022-161]

#### Hormones

See also: [Hormone-sensitive cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone-sensitive_cancer)

[Hormone-sensitive cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormone-sensitive_cancer) are dependent on hormones for growth and survival. Medications that effect hormone levels may therefore have a harmful or beneficial effect on cancer rates. Women who are on, or recently took, [combined oral contraceptives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_oral_contraceptives) (COCs) have an increased risk for breast cancer and cervical cancer.[^cite_note-WCR_pharm_2020-162] However, CBCs also decrease the risk for [ovarian cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ovarian_cancer), and this protective effect continues years after stopping their use.[^cite_note-WCR_pharm_2020-162]

Estrogen-only [menopausal hormone therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menopausal_hormone_therapy) increases the risk of breast, [endometrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endometrial_cancer), and ovarian cancer.[^cite_note-WCR_pharm_2020-162] Combined estrogen–progestogen hormone therapy increases the risk of breast and endometrial cancer.[^cite_note-WCR_pharm_2020-162]

## Screening

Main article: [Cancer screening](22848246.md)

Unlike diagnostic efforts prompted by [symptoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symptom) and [medical signs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_sign), cancer screening involves efforts to detect cancer after it has formed, but before any noticeable symptoms appear.[^cite_note-NIH-163] This may involve [physical examination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Physical_examination), [blood](336557.md) or [urine tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urinalysis) or [medical imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_imaging).[^cite_note-NIH-163]

[Cancer screening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_biomarker) is not available for many types of cancers. Even when tests are available, they may not be recommended for everyone. *[Universal screening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_screening)* or *mass screening* involves screening everyone.[^cite_note-Wilson-164] *Selective screening* identifies people who are at higher risk, such as people with a family history.[^cite_note-Wilson-164] Several factors are considered to determine whether the benefits of screening outweigh the risks and the costs of screening.[^cite_note-NIH-163] These factors include:

- Possible harms from the screening test: for example, X-ray images involve exposure to potentially harmful [ionizing radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionizing_radiation)

- The likelihood of the test correctly identifying cancer

- The likelihood that cancer is present: Screening is not normally useful for rare cancers.

- Possible harms from follow-up procedures

- Whether a suitable treatment is available

- Whether early detection improves treatment outcomes

- Whether cancer will ever need treatment

- Whether the test is acceptable to the people: If a screening test is too burdensome (for example, extremely painful), then people will refuse to participate.

### Predisposition and genetic testing

See also: [Cancer syndrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_syndrome)

For many cancers there is a genetic predisposition that increases the chance of getting cancer. In a study of 1948 children that developed cancer before the age of 8, 132 (6.8%) had germline variants in known cancer genes (e.g. [RB1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retinoblastoma_protein), n=69), or [TP53](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P53) (n=24). In some cancers, there is a very high chance of getting cancer with one of these variants, e.g. certain germline variants are found in 100% in [medullary thyroid carcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medullary_thyroid_cancer) and in 40% of [retinoblastoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Retinoblastoma)s.[^cite_note-177]

| Gene | Cancer types |
| --- | --- |
| [BRCA1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRCA1), [BRCA2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BRCA2) | Breast, ovarian, pancreatic |
| [HNPCC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hereditary_nonpolyposis_colorectal_cancer), [MLH1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MLH1), [MSH2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSH2), [MSH6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSH6), [PMS1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PMS1), [PMS2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PMS2) | Colon, uterine, small bowel, stomach, urinary tract |

[Genetic testing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genetic_testing) for individuals at high risk of certain cancers is recommended by some groups.[^cite_note-pmid24176569-178][^cite_note-BRCA08-179] Carriers of these mutations may then undergo enhanced surveillance, chemoprevention, or preventative surgery to reduce their subsequent risk.[^cite_note-BRCA08-179]

## Management

Main articles: [Treatment of cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treatment_of_cancer) and [Oncology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oncology)

Many treatment options for cancer exist. The primary ones include surgery, [chemotherapy](7172.md), [radiation therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_therapy), [hormonal therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hormonal_therapy_\(oncology\)), [targeted therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeted_therapy) and [palliative care](275509.md). Which treatments are used depends on the type, location and grade of the cancer as well as the patient's health and preferences. The [treatment intent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Therapy#By_treatment_intent) may or may not be curative.[^cite_note-WHO2018-2]

### Chemotherapy

[Chemotherapy](7172.md) is the treatment of cancer with one or more [cytotoxic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cytotoxicity) anti-[neoplastic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoplastic) drugs ([chemotherapeutic agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_chemotherapeutic_agents)) as part of a [standardized regimen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemotherapy_regimen). The term encompasses a variety of drugs, which are divided into broad categories such as [alkylating agents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alkylating_antineoplastic_agent) and [antimetabolites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antimetabolite).[^cite_note-Lind2008-180] Traditional chemotherapeutic agents act by killing cells that divide rapidly, a critical property of most cancer cells.

It was found that providing combined cytotoxic drugs is better than a single drug, a process called the [combination therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combination_therapy), which has an advantage in the statistics of survival and response to the tumor and in the progress of the disease.[^cite_note-181] A Cochrane review concluded that combined therapy was more effective in treating metastasized breast cancer. However, generally it is not certain whether combination chemotherapy leads to better health outcomes, when both survival and toxicity are considered.[^cite_note-182]

[Targeted therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targeted_therapy) is a form of chemotherapy that targets specific molecular differences between cancer and normal cells. The first targeted therapies blocked the [estrogen receptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estrogen_receptor) molecule, inhibiting the growth of breast cancer. Another common example is the class of [Bcr-Abl inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bcr-Abl_tyrosine-kinase_inhibitor), which are used to treat [chronic myelogenous leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronic_myelogenous_leukemia) (CML).[^cite_note-TCT2018-5] Currently, targeted therapies exist for many of the most common cancer types, including [bladder cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bladder_cancer), breast cancer, [colorectal cancer](206979.md), [kidney cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kidney_cancer), [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia), [liver cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liver_cancer), lung cancer, [lymphoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoma), [pancreatic cancer](363559.md), [prostate cancer](88078.md), [skin cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_cancer), and [thyroid cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid_cancer) as well as other cancer types.[^cite_note-TCT2018-5]

The efficacy of chemotherapy depends on the type of cancer and the stage. In combination with surgery, chemotherapy has proven useful in cancer types including breast cancer, colorectal cancer, [pancreatic cancer](363559.md), [osteogenic sarcoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osteosarcoma), [testicular cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Testicular_cancer), ovarian cancer and certain lung cancers.[^cite_note-HollandTx40-183] Chemotherapy is curative for some cancers, such as some [leukemias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemias),[^cite_note-184][^cite_note-185] ineffective in some [brain tumors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brain_tumors),[^cite_note-186] and needless in others, such as most [non-melanoma skin cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-melanoma_skin_cancer).[^cite_note-187] The effectiveness of chemotherapy is often limited by its toxicity to other tissues in the body. Even when chemotherapy does not provide a permanent cure, it may be useful to reduce symptoms such as pain or to reduce the size of an inoperable tumor in the hope that surgery will become possible in the future.Wang M, Zhang C, Yang G, Shi Y (March 2017). ["Neoadjuvant Chemotherapy Creates Surgery Opportunities For Inoperable Locally Advanced Breast Cancer"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5361098). *Scientific Reports*. **7** (3) 44673. [Bibcode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bibcode_\(identifier\)):[2017NatSR...744673W](https://ui.adsabs.harvard.edu/abs/2017NatSR...744673W). [doi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doi_\(identifier\)):[10.1038/srep44673](https://doi.org/10.1038%2Fsrep44673). [PMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PMC_\(identifier\)) [5361098](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5361098). [PMID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PMID_\(identifier\)) [28322234](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28322234).

### Radiation

[Radiation therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation_therapy) involves the use of [ionizing radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionizing_radiation) in an attempt to either cure or improve symptoms. It works by damaging the DNA of cancerous tissue, causing [mitotic catastrophe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mitotic_catastrophe) resulting in the death of the cancer cells.[^cite_note-188] To spare normal tissues (such as skin or organs, which radiation must pass through to treat the tumor), shaped radiation beams are aimed from multiple exposure angles to intersect at the tumor, providing a much larger dose there than in the surrounding, healthy tissue. As with chemotherapy, cancers vary in their response to radiation therapy.[^cite_note-189][^cite_note-190][^cite_note-191]

Radiation therapy is used in about half of cases. The radiation can be either from internal sources ([brachytherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brachytherapy)) or external sources. The radiation is most commonly low energy X-rays for treating skin cancers, while higher energy X-rays are used for cancers within the body.[^cite_note-192] Radiation is typically used in addition to surgery and/or chemotherapy. For certain types of cancer, such as early [head and neck cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_and_neck_cancer), it may be used alone.[^cite_note-De_Felice_2023-193] Radiation therapy after surgery for brain metastases has been shown to not improve overall survival in patients compared to surgery alone.[^cite_note-194] For painful [bone metastasis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bone_metastasis), radiation therapy has been found to be effective in about 70% of patients.[^cite_note-195]

### Surgery

Surgery is the primary method of treatment for most isolated, solid cancers and may play a role in palliation and prolongation of survival. It is typically an important part of the definitive diagnosis and staging of tumors, as biopsies are usually required. In localized cancer, surgery typically attempts to remove the entire mass along with, in certain cases, the [lymph nodes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymph_node) in the area. For some types of cancer this is sufficient to eliminate the cancer.[^cite_note-Wang_2019-196]

### Palliative care

Some [medical specialty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Specialty_\(medicine\)) organisations recommend limiting treatment to palliative care for patients with poor [performance status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Performance_status), those who have not benefited from prior [evidence-based treatments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evidence-based_medicine), those ineligible for appropriate [clinical trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_trial), and those for whom no strong evidence supports the effectiveness of further treatment.[^cite_note-ASCOfive-197]

Palliative care may be confused with [hospice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hospice) and therefore only indicated when people approach [end of life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/End-of-life_care). Like hospice care, palliative care attempts to help the patient cope with their immediate needs and to increase comfort. Unlike hospice care, palliative care does not require people to stop treatment aimed at the cancer. ["Palliative Care in Cancer"](https://www.cancer.gov/about-cancer/advanced-cancer/care-choices/palliative-care-fact-sheet). National Cancer Institute. 12 January 2018. Retrieved 2 July 2026.

Multiple national [medical guidelines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medical_guideline) recommend early palliative care for patients whose cancer has produced distressing symptoms or who need help coping with their illness. In patients first diagnosed with metastatic disease, palliative care may be immediately indicated. Palliative care is indicated for patients with a prognosis of less than 12 months of life even given aggressive treatment.[^cite_note-198][^cite_note-199][^cite_note-200]

### Immunotherapy

Main article: [Cancer immunotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_immunotherapy)

A variety of therapies using [immunotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_immunotherapy), stimulating or helping the [immune system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system) to fight cancer, have come into use since 1997. Approaches include:[^cite_note-201]

- [Monoclonal antibody therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monoclonal_antibody_therapy)

- [Checkpoint therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Checkpoint_therapy) (therapy that targets the [immune checkpoints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_checkpoint) or regulators of the [immune system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immune_system))

- [Adoptive cell transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adoptive_cell_transfer)

- [CAR T cell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAR_T_cell)

### Laser therapy

Main article: [Lasers in cancer treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lasers_in_cancer_treatment)

[Laser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser) therapy uses high-intensity light to treat cancer by shrinking or destroying tumors or precancerous growths. Lasers are most commonly used to treat superficial cancers that are on the surface of the body or the lining of internal organs. It is used to treat basal cell skin cancer and the very early stages of others like cervical, penile, vaginal, vulvar, and non-small cell lung cancer. It is often combined with other treatments, such as surgery, chemotherapy, or radiation therapy. [Laser-induced interstitial thermotherapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_ablation) (LITT), or interstitial laser [photocoagulation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_coagulation), uses lasers to treat some cancers using hyperthermia, which uses heat to shrink tumors by damaging or killing cancer cells. Lasers are more precise than surgery and cause less damage, pain, bleeding, swelling, and scarring. A disadvantage is that surgeons must have specialized training. It may be more expensive than other treatments.[^cite_note-NIHlaser-202]

### Alternative medicine

Main article: [Alternative cancer treatments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_cancer_treatments)

Complementary and alternative cancer treatments are a diverse group of therapies, practices and products that are not part of conventional medicine.[^cite_note-mnalt-203] "Complementary medicine" refers to methods and substances used along with conventional medicine, while "alternative medicine" refers to compounds used instead of conventional medicine.[^cite_note-204] Most complementary and alternative medicines for cancer have not been studied or tested using conventional techniques such as clinical trials. Some alternative treatments have been investigated and shown to be ineffective but still continue to be marketed and promoted. Cancer researcher Andrew J. Vickers stated, "The label 'unproven' is inappropriate for such therapies; it is time to assert that many alternative cancer therapies have been 'disproven'."[^cite_note-pmid15061600-205]

## Prognosis

See also: [Cancer survival rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_survival_rates), [List of cancer mortality rates in the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cancer_mortality_rates_in_the_United_States), and [Cancer survivor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cancer_survivor)



Cancer death rates per 100,000



Three measures of global cancer mortality from 1990 to 2017

[^cite_note-206]

Survival rates vary by cancer type and by the stage at which it is diagnosed, ranging from majority survival to complete mortality five years after diagnosis. Once a cancer has metastasized, prognosis normally becomes much worse. About half of patients receiving treatment for invasive cancer (excluding [carcinoma *in situ*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carcinoma_in_situ) and non-melanoma skin cancers) die from that cancer or its treatment.[^cite_note-Epi11-27] A majority of cancer deaths are due to metastases of the primary tumor.[^cite_note-207]

Survival is worse in the [developing world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developing_world),[^cite_note-Epi11-27] partly because the types of cancer that are most common there are harder to treat than those associated with [developed countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_countries).[^cite_note-208]

Those who survive cancer develop a second primary cancer at about twice the rate of those never diagnosed.[^cite_note-isbn1-55009-213-8-209] The increased risk is believed to be due to the random chance of developing any cancer, the likelihood of surviving the first cancer, the same risk factors that produced the first cancer, unwanted side effects of treating the first cancer (particularly radiation therapy), and better compliance with screening.[^cite_note-isbn1-55009-213-8-209]

Predicting short- or long-term survival depends on many factors. The most important are the cancer type and the patient's age and overall health. Those who are [frail](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/frailty) with other health problems have lower survival rates than otherwise healthy people. [Centenarians](195007.md) are unlikely to survive for five years even if treatment is successful. People who report a higher quality of life tend to survive longer.[^cite_note-210] People with lower quality of life may be affected by [depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_depressive_disorder) and other complications and/or disease progression that both impairs quality and quantity of life. Additionally, patients with worse prognoses may be depressed or report poorer quality of life because they perceive that their condition is likely to be fatal.Satin JR, Linden W, Phillips MJ (November 2009). "Depression as a predictor of disease progression and mortality in cancer patients: a meta-analysis". *Cancer*. **115** (22): 5349–61. [doi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doi_\(identifier\)):[10.1002/cncr.24561](https://doi.org/10.1002%2Fcncr.24561). [PMID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PMID_\(identifier\)) [19753617](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19753617).

People with cancer have an increased risk of [blood clots in their veins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venous_thromboembolism) which can be life-threatening.[^cite_note-\:1-211] The use of [blood thinners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anticoagulant) such as [heparin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heparin) decrease the risk of blood clots but have not been shown to increase survival in people with cancer.[^cite_note-\:1-211] People who take blood thinners also have an increased risk of bleeding.[^cite_note-\:1-211]

Although extremely rare, some forms of cancer, even from an advanced stage, can heal spontaneously. This phenomenon is known as [spontaneous remission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spontaneous_remission).[^cite_note-212]

## Epidemiology

Main article: [Epidemiology of cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epidemiology_of_cancer)

See also: [List of countries by cancer rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_cancer_rate)

Estimates are that in 2018, 18.1 million new cases of cancer and 9.6 million deaths occur globally.[^cite_note-IARC2018-213] About 20% of males and 17% of females will get cancer at some point in time while 13% of males and 9% of females will die from it.[^cite_note-IARC2018-213]

In 2008, approximately 12.7 million cancers were [diagnosed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diagnosis) (excluding [non-melanoma skin cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-melanoma_skin_cancer) and other non-invasive cancers)[^cite_note-Epi11-27] and in 2010 nearly 7.98 million people died.[^cite_note-Loz2012-214] Cancers account for almost one in six deaths. The cancers causing the most deaths, as of 2020 are lung cancer (1.8 million), [colorectal cancer](206979.md) (916,000), liver cancer (830,000), [stomach cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stomach_cancer) (769,000) and breast cancer (685,000).[^cite_note-WHO2018-2] This makes invasive cancer the leading cause of death in the [developed world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Developed_country) and the second leading in the [developing world](78449.md).[^cite_note-Epi11-27] Over half of these cases occur in the developing world.[^cite_note-Epi11-27]

Deaths from cancer were 5.8 million in 1990.[^cite_note-Loz2012-214] Deaths have been increasing primarily due to longer lifespans and lifestyle changes in the developing world.[^cite_note-Epi11-27] The most significant [risk factor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risk_factor_\(epidemiology\)) for developing cancer is age.[^cite_note-Coleman-215] Although it is possible for cancer to strike at any age, most patients with invasive cancer are over 65.[^cite_note-Coleman-215] According to cancer researcher [Robert A. Weinberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_Weinberg_\(biologist\)), "If we lived long enough, sooner or later we all would get cancer."[^cite_note-Weinberg-216] Some of the association between aging and cancer is attributed to [immunosenescence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immunosenescence),[^cite_note-217] errors accumulated in [DNA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DNA) over a lifetime[^cite_note-218] and age-related changes in the [endocrine system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Endocrine_system).[^cite_note-219] Aging's effect on cancer is complicated by factors such as DNA damage and inflammation promoting it and factors such as vascular aging and endocrine changes inhibiting it.[^cite_note-220]

Some slow-growing cancers are particularly common, but often are not fatal. [Autopsy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autopsy) studies in Europe and Asia showed that up to 36% of people have undiagnosed and apparently harmless [thyroid cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thyroid_cancer) at the time of their deaths and that 80% of men develop [prostate cancer](88078.md) by age 80.[^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222] As these cancers do not cause the patient's death, identifying them would have represented [overdiagnosis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overdiagnosis) rather than useful medical care.Lowenstein LM, Basourakos SP, Williams MD, Troncoso P, Gregg JR, Thompson TC, et al. (March 2019). ["Active surveillance for prostate and thyroid cancers: evolution in clinical paradigms and lessons learned"](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6501050). *Nature Reviews. Clinical Oncology*. **16** (3): 168–184. [doi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doi_\(identifier\)):[10.1038/s41571-018-0116-x](https://doi.org/10.1038%2Fs41571-018-0116-x). [PMC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PMC_\(identifier\)) [6501050](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6501050). [PMID](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PMID_\(identifier\)) [30413793](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/30413793).

The three most common [childhood cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Childhood_cancer) are [leukemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leukemia) (34%), [brain tumors](37284.md) (23%) and [lymphomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymphoma) (12%).[^cite_note-Euro10-223] In the United States, cancer affects about 1 in 285 children.[^cite_note-Eli2014-224] Rates of childhood cancer increased by 0.6% per year between 1975 and 2002 in the United States[^cite_note-225] and by 1.1% per year between 1978 and 1997 in Europe.[^cite_note-Euro10-223] Death from childhood cancer decreased by half between 1975 and 2010 in the United States.[^cite_note-Eli2014-224]

## History

Main article: [History of cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_cancer)



[Engraving](60125.md) with two views of a Dutch woman who had a tumor removed from her neck in 1689

Cancer has existed for all of human history.[^cite_note-Hist1-226] The earliest written record regarding cancer is from c. 1600 BC in the Egyptian [Edwin Smith Papyrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwin_Smith_Papyrus) and describes breast cancer.[^cite_note-Hist1-226] [Hippocrates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippocrates) (c. 460 BC – c. 370 BC) described several kinds of cancer, referring to them with the [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_language) word [καρκίνος](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%CE%BA%CE%B1%CF%81%CE%BA%CE%AF%CE%BD%CE%BF%CF%82#Ancient_Greek) *karkinos* (crab or [crayfish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crayfish)).[^cite_note-Hist1-226] This name comes from the appearance of the cut surface of a solid malignant tumor, with "the veins stretched on all sides as the animal the crab has its feet, whence it derives its name".[^cite_note-227] [Galen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galen) stated that "cancer of the breast is so called because of the fancied resemblance to a crab given by the lateral prolongations of the tumor and the adjacent distended veins".[^cite_note-Majno2004-228]<sup>: 738 </sup> [Celsus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aulus_Cornelius_Celsus) (c. 25 BC – 50 AD) translated *karkinos* into [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin) as *cancer*, also meaning crab and recommended surgery as treatment.[^cite_note-Hist1-226] Galen (2nd century AD) disagreed with the use of surgery and recommended [purgatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purgatives) instead.[^cite_note-Hist1-226] These recommendations largely stood for 1000 years.[^cite_note-Hist1-226]

In the 15th, 16th and 17th centuries, it became acceptable for doctors to [dissect bodies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissection) to discover the cause of death.[^cite_note-Hist2-229] The German professor [Wilhelm Fabry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wilhelm_Fabry) believed that breast cancer was caused by a milk clot in a mammary duct. The Dutch professor [Francois de la Boe Sylvius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francois_de_la_Boe_Sylvius), a follower of [Descartes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ren%C3%A9_Descartes), believed that all disease was the outcome of chemical processes and that acidic [lymph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lymph) fluid was the cause of cancer. His contemporary [Nicolaes Tulp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolaes_Tulp) believed that cancer was a poison that slowly spreads and concluded that it was [contagious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease).[^cite_note-230]

The physician John Hill described [tobacco sniffing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobacco_sniffing) as the cause of nose cancer in 1761.[^cite_note-Hist2-229] This was followed by the report in 1775 by British surgeon [Percivall Pott](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percivall_Pott) that [chimney sweeps' carcinoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimney_sweeps'_carcinoma), a cancer of the [scrotum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrotum), was a common disease among [chimney sweeps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chimney_sweep).[^cite_note-231] With the widespread use of the microscope in the 18th century, it was discovered that the 'cancer poison' spread from the primary tumor through the lymph nodes to other sites ("[metastasis](152509.md)"). This view of the disease was first formulated by the English surgeon [Campbell De Morgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campbell_De_Morgan) between 1871 and 1874.[^cite_note-232]

## Society and culture

Although many diseases (such as heart failure) may have a worse prognosis than most cases of cancer, cancer is the subject of widespread fear and taboos. The [euphemism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euphemism) of "a long illness" to describe cancers leading to death is still commonly used in obituaries, rather than naming the disease explicitly, reflecting an apparent [stigma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_stigma).[^cite_note-233] Cancer is also euphemised as "the C-word";[^cite_note-234][^cite_note-235][^cite_note-236] [Macmillan Cancer Support](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macmillan_Cancer_Support) uses the term to try to lessen the fear around the disease.[^cite_note-237] In Nigeria, one local name for cancer translates into English as "the disease that cannot be cured".[^cite_note-\:0-238] This deep belief that cancer is necessarily a difficult and usually deadly disease is reflected in the systems chosen by society to compile cancer statistics: the most common form of cancer—non-melanoma [skin cancers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skin_cancer), accounting for about one-third of cancer cases worldwide, but very few deaths[^cite_note-Bolognia-239][^cite_note-240]—are excluded from cancer statistics specifically because they are easily treated and almost always cured, often in a single, short, outpatient procedure.[^cite_note-241]

Western conceptions of [patients' rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patients'_rights) for people with cancer include a duty to fully disclose the medical situation to the person, and the right to engage in [shared decision-making](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shared_decision-making_in_medicine) in a way that respects the person's own values. In other cultures, other rights and values are preferred. For example, most African cultures value whole families rather than [individualism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Individualism). In parts of Africa, a diagnosis is commonly made so late that cure is not possible, and treatment, if available at all, would quickly bankrupt the family. As a result of these factors, African healthcare providers tend to let family members decide whether, when and how to disclose the diagnosis, and they tend to do so slowly and circuitously, as the person shows interest and an ability to cope with the grim news.[^cite_note-\:0-238] People from Asian and South American countries also tend to prefer a slower, less candid approach to disclosure than is idealized in the United States and Western Europe, and they believe that sometimes it would be preferable not to be told about a cancer diagnosis.[^cite_note-\:0-238] In general, disclosure of the diagnosis is more common than it was in the 20th century, but full disclosure of the prognosis is not offered to many patients around the world.[^cite_note-\:0-238]

In the United States and some other [cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_differences_in_breast_cancer_diagnosis_and_treatment), cancer is regarded as a disease that must be "fought" to end the "civil insurrection"; a [war on cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_on_cancer) was declared in the US. Military metaphors are particularly common in descriptions of cancer's human effects, and they emphasize both the state of the patient's health and the need to take immediate, decisive actions himself rather than to delay, to ignore or to rely entirely on others. The military metaphors also help rationalize radical, destructive treatments.[^cite_note-Gwyn-242][^cite_note-243] In the 1970s, a relatively popular [alternative cancer treatment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_cancer_treatment) in the US was a specialized form of [talk therapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talk_therapy), based on the idea that cancer was caused by a bad attitude.[^cite_note-Olson-244] People with a "cancer personality"—depressed, repressed, self-loathing and afraid to express their emotions—were believed to have manifested cancer through subconscious desire. Some psychotherapists claimed that treatment to change the patient's outlook on life would cure the cancer.[^cite_note-Olson-244] Among other effects, this belief allowed society to [blame the victim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victim_blaming) for having caused the cancer (by "wanting" it) or having prevented its cure (by not becoming a sufficiently happy, fearless and loving person).[^cite_note-Ehrenreich-245] It also increased patients' anxiety, as they incorrectly believed that natural emotions of sadness, anger or fear shorten their lives.[^cite_note-Ehrenreich-245] The idea was ridiculed by [Susan Sontag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Sontag), who published *[Illness as Metaphor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illness_as_Metaphor)* while recovering from treatment for breast cancer in 1978.[^cite_note-Olson-244] Although the original idea is now generally regarded as nonsense, the idea partly persists in a reduced form with a widespread, but incorrect, belief that deliberately cultivating a habit of [positive thinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Optimism) will increase survival.[^cite_note-Ehrenreich-245] This notion is particularly strong in [breast cancer culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breast_cancer_culture).[^cite_note-Ehrenreich-245]

One idea about why people with cancer are blamed or stigmatized, called the [just-world fallacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Just-world_fallacy), is that blaming cancer on the patient's actions or attitudes allows the blamers to regain a sense of control. This is based upon the blamers' belief that the world is fundamentally just and so any dangerous illness, like cancer, must be a type of punishment for bad choices, because in a just world, bad things would not happen to good people.[^cite_note-246]

### Economic effect

The total health care expenditure on cancer in the US was estimated to be \$80.2 billion in 2015.[^cite_note-247] Even though cancer-related health care expenditure have increased in absolute terms during recent decades, the share of health expenditure devoted to cancer treatment has remained close to 5% between the 1960s and 2004.[^cite_note-248][^cite_note-249] A similar pattern has been observed in Europe where about 6% of all health care expenditure are spent on cancer treatment.[^cite_note-250][^cite_note-EJC2018-251] In addition to health care expenditure and [financial toxicity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Financial_toxicity), cancer causes indirect costs in the form of productivity losses due to sick days, permanent incapacity and disability as well as premature death during working age. Cancer causes also costs for informal care. Indirect costs and informal care costs are typically estimated to exceed or equal the health care costs of cancer.[^cite_note-252][^cite_note-EJC2018-251]

### Effect on divorce

A study found women were around six times more likely to be [divorced](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Divorced) soon after a diagnosis of cancer compared to men.[^cite_note-253] Rate of separation for cancer-survivors showed correlations with race, age, income, and [comorbidities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comorbidity) in a study.[^cite_note-254] A review found a somewhat decreased divorce rate for most cancer types, and noted [study heterogeneity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Study_heterogeneity) and [methodological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methodology) weaknesses for many studies on the effects of cancer on divorce.[^cite_note-255]

Because cancer is a class of diseases,[^cite_note-WhatIsCancerNCI-8][^cite_note-256] it is unlikely that there will ever be a single "[cure for cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cure_for_cancer)" any more than there will be a single treatment for all [infectious diseases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infectious_disease).[^cite_note-257] [Angiogenesis inhibitors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angiogenesis_inhibitor) were once incorrectly thought to have potential as a "[silver bullet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silver_bullet)" treatment applicable to many types of cancer.[^cite_note-258] Angiogenesis inhibitors and other cancer therapeutics are used in combination to reduce cancer morbidity and mortality.[^cite_note-259]

[Experimental cancer treatments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Experimental_cancer_treatment) are studied in [clinical trials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_trial) to compare the proposed treatment to the best existing treatment. Treatments that succeeded in one cancer type can be tested against other types.[^cite_note-260] Diagnostic tests are under development to better target the right therapies to the right patients, based on their individual biology.

Cancer research focuses on the following issues:

- Agents (e.g. viruses) and events (e.g. mutations) that cause or facilitate genetic changes in cells destined to become cancer.

- The precise nature of the genetic damage and the genes that are affected by it.

- The consequences of those genetic changes on the biology of the cell, both in generating the defining properties of a cancer cell and in facilitating additional genetic events that lead to further progression of the cancer.

Competition for financial resources appears to have suppressed the creativity, cooperation, risk-taking and original thinking required to make fundamental discoveries, unduly favoring low-risk research into small incremental advancements over riskier, more innovative research. Other consequences of competition appear to be many studies with dramatic claims whose results cannot be replicated and perverse incentives that encourage grantee institutions to grow without making sufficient investments in their own faculty and facilities.[^cite_note-261]

## Pregnancy

Cancer affects approximately 1 in 1,000 pregnant women. The most common cancers found during pregnancy are the same as the most common cancers found in non-pregnant women during childbearing ages: breast cancer, cervical cancer, leukemia, lymphoma, melanoma, ovarian cancer and colorectal cancer.[^cite_note-yarbro-262]

Diagnosing a new cancer in a pregnant woman is difficult, in part because any symptoms are commonly assumed to be a normal discomfort associated with pregnancy. As a result, cancer is typically discovered at a somewhat later stage than average. Some imaging procedures, such as [MRIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnetic_resonance_imaging) (magnetic resonance imaging), [CT scans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CT_scan), ultrasounds and [mammograms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mammography) with fetal shielding are considered safe during pregnancy; some others, such as [PET scans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Positron_emission_tomography), are not.[^cite_note-yarbro-262]

Treatment is generally the same as for non-pregnant women. However, radiation and radioactive drugs are normally avoided during pregnancy, especially if the fetal dose might exceed 100 cGy. In some cases, some or all treatments are postponed until after birth if the cancer is diagnosed late in the pregnancy. Early deliveries are often used to advance the start of treatment. Surgery is generally safe, but pelvic surgeries during the first trimester may cause miscarriage. Some treatments, especially certain chemotherapy drugs given during the [first trimester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_trimester), increase the risk of [birth defects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birth_defect) and pregnancy loss (spontaneous abortions and stillbirths).[^cite_note-yarbro-262]

Elective abortions are not required and, for the most common forms and stages of cancer, do not improve the mother's survival. In a few instances, such as advanced uterine cancer, the pregnancy cannot be continued and in others, the patient may end the pregnancy so that she can begin aggressive chemotherapy.[^cite_note-yarbro-262]

Some treatments can interfere with the mother's ability to give birth vaginally or to breastfeed.[^cite_note-yarbro-262] Cervical cancer may require birth by [Caesarean section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caesarean_section). Radiation to the breast reduces the ability of that breast to produce milk and increases the risk of [mastitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mastitis). Also, when chemotherapy is given after birth, many of the drugs appear in breast milk, which could harm the baby.[^cite_note-yarbro-262]

## Other animals

[Veterinary oncology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veterinary_oncology), concentrating mainly on cats and dogs, is a growing specialty in wealthy countries and the major forms of human treatment such as surgery and radiotherapy may be offered. The most common types of cancer differ, but the cancer burden seems at least as high in pets as in humans. Animals, typically rodents, are often used in cancer research and studies of natural cancers in larger animals may benefit research into human cancer.[^cite_note-263]

Across wild animals, there is still limited data on cancer. Nonetheless, a study published in 2022, explored cancer risk in (non-domesticated) zoo mammals, belonging to 191 species for a total of 110,148 individuals, demonstrated that cancer is a ubiquitous disease of mammals and can emerge anywhere along the mammalian phylogeny.[^cite_note-vincze-264] This research also highlighted that cancer risk is not uniformly distributed along mammals. For instance, species in the order [Carnivora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnivora) are particularly prone to be affected by cancer (e.g. over 25% of [clouded leopards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clouded_leopard), [bat-eared foxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bat-eared_fox) and [red wolves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_wolves) die of cancer), while [ungulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ungulate) (especially [even-toed ungulates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Even-toed_ungulate)) appear to face consistently low cancer risks.[^cite_note-vincze-264]

In non-humans, a few types of [transmissible cancer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transmissible_cancer) have also been described, wherein the cancer spreads between animals by transmission of the tumor cells themselves. This phenomenon is seen in dogs with [Sticker's sarcoma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sticker's_sarcoma) (also known as canine transmissible venereal tumor), and in [Tasmanian devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tasmanian_devils) with [devil facial tumour disease](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devil_facial_tumour_disease) (DFTD).[^cite_note-pmid16901782-265]
