**Paris Saint-Germain Football Club**, commonly referred to as **Paris Saint-Germain** (French: [\[paʁi sɛ̃ʒɛʁmɛ̃\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French), ult. ref. "Saint [Germanus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germain_of_Paris)"), **PSG**, **Paris**, or **Paris SG**, is a French professional [football](10568.md) club based in [Paris](22989.md). Founded in 1970 through the merger of [Paris FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_FC) and [Stade Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Saint-Germain), the club competes in [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1), the [highest division](3480022.md) of [French football](1963109.md), and plays its home matches at the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes). PSG is France's most widely supported football club and one of the most followed clubs worldwide. The club has rivalries with [Olympique de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille), against whom it contests [Le Classique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Classique), and Paris FC in the [Paris derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_derby).

The Parisians are the [most successful club in French football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_records_and_statistics_in_France) and rank among the leading clubs worldwide by [number of competitive trophies won](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_clubs_by_competitive_honours_won). PSG have won a record 14 Ligue 1 titles, one [Ligue 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2) title, 16 Coupes de France, 9 [Coupes de la Ligue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_la_Ligue), and 14 [Trophée des Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troph%C3%A9e_des_Champions). PSG is the most decorated French club internationally, holding French records with two [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) titles and two [UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Super_Cup), as well as having won the [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup), the [UEFA Intertoto Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Intertoto_Cup), and the [FIFA Intercontinental Cup](73290908.md).

Before the [Qatar Sports Investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Sports_Investments) (QSI) takeover of PSG in 2011, the club had won 18 trophies, including two European titles, two Ligue 1 championships and 13 [national cups](1963109.md#Cup_competitions), placing among the top five in French football at the time. Following the acquisition, PSG entered a period of sustained domestic dominance, attracting numerous high-profile players and becoming the country's most successful club. Under manager [Luis Enrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Enrique), PSG also established itself as a dominant force in international football, winning consecutive UEFA Champions League and UEFA Super Cup titles, the FIFA Intercontinental Cup, as well as the [continental treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_\(association_football\)) and [sextuple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextuple_\(association_football\)) in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season).

Since 2011, PSG have been majority-owned by QSI, with [Arctos Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctos_Partners) acquiring a minority stake in 2023 and [Boardroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boardroom_\(company\)) in 2025. As of May 2026, the club is the wealthiest in France and ranks among the [richest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte_Football_Money_League) and [most valuable football clubs in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes'_list_of_the_most_valuable_football_clubs). In addition to its men's football team, PSG operates a [youth academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_Youth_Academy), a [women's football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_\(women\)), and sections in [handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Handball), [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Judo), and [esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSG_Esports). The club has also previously fielded teams in [basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Basketball), [volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Volleyball), [boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Boxing), and [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Rugby_League).

## History

Main article: [History of Paris Saint-Germain FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC)

### Formation, split and early successes (1970–1991)



[Dominique Rocheteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Rocheteau) helped PSG win their first major title in 1982.

By the late 1960s, Paris lacked a club in the top division of French football. With support from the [French Football Federation](682002.md) (FFF), Parisian businessmen [Guy Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Crescent) and [Pierre-Étienne Guyot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-%C3%89tienne_Guyot) founded [Paris FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_FC) in 1969 and later brought in [Henri Patrelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Patrelle), president of [Stade Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Saint-Germain). The two clubs merged on 12 August 1970 to form Paris Saint-Germain. The club began in [Ligue 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2) and won the title in its first season, securing promotion to [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) for 1971–72. The club's early years were affected by financial difficulties and disagreements over its future structure. In 1972, Paris FC and PSG split. Paris FC retained its place in Ligue 1, while PSG was administratively relegated to [Division 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Division_3_\(1971%E2%80%931993\)) and continued under its existing name. PSG won back-to-back promotions and returned to Ligue 1 in 1974, moving into the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes).[^cite_note-History1978-1][^cite_note-PSG50Years-2]

During the second half of the 1970s, [Daniel Hechter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Hechter) became a driving force behind PSG's development and its establishment in the top flight. He became club president in 1974 and remained in the position until January 1978. Hechter was then replaced by [Francis Borelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Borelli) following the double-ticketing scandal at the Parc des Princes. Under Borelli, PSG won its first major trophies. The club reached its first [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France) final in 1982, where it faced [Saint-Étienne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Saint-%C3%89tienne) at the Parc des Princes. After a 2–2 draw following extra time, PSG won the final on penalties to claim its first major trophy. The club retained the Coupe de France the following year by defeating [Nantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Nantes) 3–2 in the final.[^cite_note-UEFA-3][^cite_note-psg70-4][^cite_note-psgfc-5]

PSG continued to develop as a leading French club during the 1980s and featured several notable players, including [Jean-Pierre Dogliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Pierre_Dogliani), [Mustapha Dahleb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustapha_Dahleb), [Safet Sušić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safet_Su%C5%A1i%C4%87), [Luis Fernandez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Fernandez) and [Dominique Rocheteau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Rocheteau). In 1986, under manager [Gérard Houllier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%A9rard_Houllier), the club won its first French championship. PSG finished three points ahead of Nantes and lost only three matches during the season. The championship was followed by a sporting decline and increasing financial difficulties. PSG struggled to maintain its position among the leading French clubs, with declining league performances and growing financial problems affecting the club during the latter part of Borelli's presidency.[^cite_note-PSG50Years-2][^cite_note-UEFA-3][^cite_note-psg70-4][^cite_note-psgfc-5][^cite_note-History1990-6]

### First golden era and decline (1991–2011)



PSG team against [AC Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Milan) in 1995.

[Canal+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_S.A.) took control of PSG in 1991, beginning a period of increased investment in the club. [Michel Denisot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Denisot) became president, while the new ownership strengthened the squad and appointed prominent coaches. PSG recruited several high-profile players during the 1990s, including [Raí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C3%AD), [George Weah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Weah), [David Ginola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Ginola), [Bernard Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lama) and [Valdo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valdo_\(footballer,_born_1964\)). The club won the Coupe de France in 1993, defeating Nantes 3–0 in the final, and won its second French championship in 1994.[^cite_note-PSG50Years-2][^cite_note-UEFA-3][^cite_note-psg70-4][^cite_note-psgfc-5][^cite_note-History2000-7]

PSG also became a regular participant in European competition. In the [1994–95 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_UEFA_Champions_League), the club won its group and advanced to the semi-finals, where it was eliminated by [AC Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AC_Milan). In 1996, PSG won its first European trophy by defeating [Rapid Wien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Rapid_Wien) 1–0 in the [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) final in Brussels, with [Bruno Ngotty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruno_Ngotty) scoring the only goal. PSG reached the same competition's final again in 1997 but lost 1–0 to [Barcelona](68187.md). The club continued to win domestic trophies during the late 1990s. PSG won the [Coupe de la Ligue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_la_Ligue) in 1998 and defeated [Lens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RC_Lens) in the Coupe de France final later that year to complete a domestic cup double.[^cite_note-PSG50Years-2][^cite_note-UEFA-3][^cite_note-psg70-4][^cite_note-psgfc-5][^cite_note-History2000-7]

Despite having notable players such as [Jay-Jay Okocha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jay-Jay_Okocha), [Ronaldinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldinho) and [Pauleta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauleta), PSG's results became less consistent after the end of the 1990s. Changes in management, sporting directors and ownership were accompanied by a decline in league performances. PSG remained capable of winning domestic trophies, taking the Coupe de France in 2004, 2006 and 2010 and the Coupe de la Ligue in 2008, but spent several seasons outside the upper positions of Ligue 1. The club finished 15th in 2006–07 and 16th in 2007–08, its lowest league finishes during this period. In 2008, [Colony Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DigitalBridge) became PSG's majority shareholder. The club subsequently improved its league position, finishing sixth in 2008–09, 13th in 2009–10 and fourth in 2010–11. PSG also won the Coupe de France in 2010, defeating [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AS_Monaco_FC) 1–0 after extra time.[^cite_note-PSG50Years-2][^cite_note-UEFA-3][^cite_note-psg70-4][^cite_note-psgfc-5][^cite_note-History2000-7]

### Rise to European dominance (2011–present)



PSG during their [UEFA Champions League title-winning campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Champions_League) in 2025.

In 2011, [Qatar Sports Investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Sports_Investments) (QSI) acquired a majority stake in PSG and subsequently became the club's sole owner. [Nasser Al-Khelaifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasser_Al-Khelaifi) became president, and the new ownership significantly increased investment in the playing squad. Early major signings included [Javier Pastore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javier_Pastore), [Zlatan Ibrahimović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zlatan_Ibrahimovi%C4%87), [Thiago Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiago_Silva), [Edinson Cavani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edinson_Cavani) and [Marco Verratti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marco_Verratti). After finishing as runners-up in 2012, PSG won Ligue 1 in 2013, their first league championship since 1994, and successfully defended it in 2014. The club subsequently established sustained domestic dominance, winning multiple Ligue 1 championships and cup titles. In 2014–15, PSG became the first French club to win all four major domestic trophies in a single season: Ligue 1, the Coupe de France, the Coupe de la Ligue and the [Trophée des Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troph%C3%A9e_des_Champions). The club repeated the feat in 2015–16, when it also set a Ligue 1 record with 96 points.[^cite_note-PSG50Years-2][^cite_note-psgfc-5][^cite_note-Journey-8][^cite_note-Heartbreak-9]

PSG then built a squad around superstars, signing [Neymar](15326144.md) from Barcelona and [Kylian Mbappé](48711857.md) from Monaco in 2017, before [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) joined the club in 2021. The trio helped PSG win further domestic trophies but did not deliver a Champions League title. During this period, PSG reached the Champions League final in 2020, losing 1–0 to [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich), and the semi-finals in 2021 and 2024. Neymar left the club in 2023, followed by Messi later that year, while Mbappé departed in 2024.[^cite_note-PSG50Years-2][^cite_note-psgfc-5][^cite_note-Journey-8][^cite_note-Heartbreak-9]

Following the departures of Neymar, Messi and Mbappé, PSG moved away from a squad centered on high-profile individual forwards. Under [Luis Enrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Enrique), who became manager in 2023, the club placed greater emphasis on collective play, positional movement and contributions across the squad. PSG won its first Champions League title in 2025, defeating [Inter Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan) 5–0 in the final in Munich. [Achraf Hakimi](52004386.md), [Désiré Doué](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A9sir%C3%A9_Dou%C3%A9), [Khvicha Kvaratskhelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khvicha_Kvaratskhelia) and [Senny Mayulu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senny_Mayulu) scored in the final. The victory made PSG the second French club to win the Champions League, after [Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille) in 1993.[^cite_note-Journey-8][^cite_note-Heartbreak-9][^cite_note-Treble-10] PSG also claimed the [2025 UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Super_Cup) and the [2025 FIFA Intercontinental Cup](76156932.md), achieving an unprecedented [sextuple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextuple_\(association_football\)).[^cite_note-Spurs-11][^cite_note-Flamengo-12][^cite_note-Sextuple-13]

PSG retained the Champions League in 2026. After finishing 11th in the league phase, the club defeated Monaco in the knockout play-offs, [Chelsea](7473.md) in the round of 16, [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) in the quarter-finals, and Bayern Munich in the semi-finals. In the final at the [Puskás Aréna](27530864.md) in Budapest, PSG drew 1–1 with [Arsenal](2174.md) before winning 4–3 on penalties. [Ousmane Dembélé](48621084.md) scored PSG's goal in normal time, while goalkeeper [Matvey Safonov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matvey_Safonov) made the decisive save in the shootout. PSG thus became only the second club in the Champions League era to win consecutive titles.[^cite_note-UCL2026-14]

## Identity

### Colours and mascot



Germain the Lynx, the club's mascot.

Since their foundation, Paris Saint-Germain have represented both the city of [Paris](22989.md) and the nearby royal town of [Saint-Germain-en-Laye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Germain-en-Laye).[^cite_note-psgfc-5] As a result, red, blue and white are the club's traditional colours.[^cite_note-lepsg-15] The red and blue are Parisian colours, a nod to revolutionary figures [Lafayette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquis_de_Lafayette) and [Jean Sylvain Bailly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Sylvain_Bailly), and the white is a symbol of [French royalty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_French_monarchs) and Saint-Germain-en-Laye.[^cite_note-lepsg-15][^cite_note-know-16]

On the club's crest, the [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower) in red and the blue background represent Paris, while the [fleur de lys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleur-de-lis) in white is a hint to the [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) of Saint-Germain-en-Laye.[^cite_note-lepsg-15][^cite_note-know-16] The fleur de lys is a royal symbol as well and recalls that French King [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV) was born in the town.[^cite_note-lepsg-15] Throughout its history, PSG have brandished several crests, but all of them have featured the club's three historical colours.[^cite_note-psghe-17]

Likewise, PSG's most distinctive shirts have been predominantly red, blue or white, with the remaining two colours included as well.[^cite_note-sofoot-18] The club's official mascot, Germain the [Lynx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lynx), also sports PSG's traditional colours.[^cite_note-know-16] It was unveiled during the 2010 [Tournoi de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournoi_de_Paris) in commemoration of the club's 40th anniversary, and can be seen entertaining kids in the stands of the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes) or near the pitch with the players during the warm-up.[^cite_note-west-19]

### Anthems and mottos

"Allez Paris!", recorded by Belgian actress and singer [Annie Cordy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annie_Cordy) in 1971, was the club's first official anthem. A dedicated PSG supporter from the very beginning, Cordy was part of an association of hundreds of celebrities who contributed to the club's founding in 1970. At that time, an appeal was made to the public to purchase season tickets at newsstands. A year later, Cordy was named PSG's official godmother and recorded the aforementioned anthem.[^cite_note-allezparis-20][^cite_note-Cordy-21]

The club's second anthem, "Allez Paris‑Saint‑Germain!" by Les Parisiens, was recorded in 1977, replacing Annie Cordy's original version. It was produced and released by long-time PSG leader and music producer Charles Talar.[^cite_note-allezpsg-22][^cite_note-listen-23][^cite_note-24] The chorus became a popular chant among PSG fans during matches.[^cite_note-Supporters-25] A new version of the anthem, retaining the same title, was recorded in 2010 as part of the club's 40th anniversary celebrations. Performed to the tune of "[Go West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Go_West_\(song\))" by [Village People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village_People), the lyrics were rewritten with input from the fans. This version serves as the club's current official anthem.[^cite_note-know-16][^cite_note-west-19][^cite_note-listen-23]

"Ô Ville Lumière", set to the tune of "[Flower of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flower_of_Scotland)", is another official club anthem for PSG fans.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-scot-27] This chant was created by the former KoB ultra group Boulogne Boys.[^cite_note-28] Other notable chants include "Le Parc est à nous" ("The Parc is ours"), "Ici, c'est Paris" ("This is Paris"), and "Paris est magique" ("Paris is magical").[^cite_note-Supporters-25][^cite_note-trust-29][^cite_note-blizzard-30] "[Who Said I Would](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Who_Said_I_Would)" by English musician [Phill Collins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phill_Collins) is also a traditional fan anthem. The song has accompanied the players' entrance onto the field since 1992. Collins was preceded by French singer [Michel Fugain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Fugain) and his 1972 song "Attention, Ladies and Gentlemen."[^cite_note-31]

"Ici, c'est Paris" and "Paris est magique" are also the club's most iconic mottos.[^cite_note-know-16][^cite_note-earning-32][^cite_note-richest-33] The "Ici, c'est Paris" slogan was created by the former VA ultra group Supras Auteuil, which registered it as a trademark in 2008.[^cite_note-blizzard-30][^cite_note-brand-34] PSG began incorporating the slogan into their marketing, resulting in a legal dispute with the Supras. In February 2016, the group rejected the club's offer of €2,000 for the rights to the slogan.[^cite_note-blizzard-30] An agreement was reached in August 2019, allowing the club to continue using the slogan in advertising while fans retained the right to use it freely.[^cite_note-35]

### Iconic shirts

Wikimedia Commons logo

Wikimedia Commons has media related to [Paris Saint-Germain FC kits](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Paris%20Saint-Germain%20FC%20kits).

For its first three seasons, Paris Saint-Germain's home shirt was red with blue and white detailing on the sleeves and collar to unify the club's three colours: the red and blue of [Paris](22989.md) and the white of [Saint-Germain-en-Laye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Germain-en-Laye).[^cite_note-sofoot-18][^cite_note-shirts1970-36] During the 2010–11 season, PSG wore a red shirt for home matches to mark its 40th anniversary.[^cite_note-shirts2010-37] French fashion designer [Daniel Hechter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Hechter) took over as president of PSG in 1973, and designed the club's traditional home kit that same year: a blue shirt with a red vertical stripe flanked by two thinner white stripes (blue–white–red–white–blue).[^cite_note-shirts1970-36][^cite_note-style-38]

The three most iconic shirts of Paris Saint-Germain Football Club.

PSG's three most iconic shirts.

First worn in the [1973–74 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), the so-called "Hechter shirt" has remained PSG's classic identity ever since.[^cite_note-sofoot-18][^cite_note-shirts1980-39][^cite_note-shirts1990-40][^cite_note-shirts2020-41] The famous shirt was debuted against [Red Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Star_F.C.) in November 1973.[^cite_note-42] This was also the club's first match at the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes). PSG won 3–1 with [Othniel Dossevi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Othniel_Dossevi) scoring the club's first goal in a Hechter shirt.[^cite_note-dossevi-43] PSG stars from the 1990s and 2000s such as [Raí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ra%C3%AD), [Ronaldinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronaldinho) and [Pauleta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pauleta) are associated with this kit. In it, the club reached five consecutive European semi-finals between 1993 and 1997, lifted the [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) in 1996 and achieved eight consecutive victories against arch-rivals [Olympique de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille) between 2002 and 2004.[^cite_note-sofoot-18][^cite_note-shirts2000-44]

It is commonly believed that Hechter based his design on the red-and-white jersey worn by [Ajax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFC_Ajax), the dominant European club at the time.[^cite_note-shirts1970-36][^cite_note-style-38] Hechter himself denied this, stating that he was inspired by the [Ford Mustang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ford_Mustang), transposing the car's hood stripes onto the jersey and using the club's three colors.[^cite_note-shirts1970-36] The design has two alternate versions: the "Reverse Hechter" (red–white–blue–white–red), introduced in the [1974–75 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), and the "White Hechter" (white–blue–red–blue–white), which debuted in the [1994–95 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season).[^cite_note-shirts1970-36][^cite_note-shirts2020-41][^cite_note-45]

However, it was in the club's most distinctive away kit that fans saw PSG's first great team, who won their first [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France) titles in 1982 and 1983, enjoyed their first European campaign in 1983, and clinched their first [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) crown in 1986. The shirt was white with blue and red vertical stripes down the left side.[^cite_note-sofoot-18][^cite_note-shirts1980-39] Like Hechter's shirt, it debuted in the 1973–74 season as the away kit.[^cite_note-shirts1970-36] Promoted by PSG president [Francis Borelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Borelli), the white shirt was the club's home identity from 1981 to 1990.[^cite_note-shirts1980-39] Now known as the "Borelli shirt", it is synonymous with 1980s PSG legends such as [Safet Sušić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safet_Su%C5%A1i%C4%87), [Luis Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Fern%C3%A1ndez) and [Dominique Bathenay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominique_Bathenay).[^cite_note-sofoot-18][^cite_note-shirts1990-40][^cite_note-46]

### Crest evolution

Following the merger of [Paris FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_FC) (PFC) and [Stade Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Saint-Germain) to create Paris Saint-Germain, the club's first crest was the same as the original PFC logo. It featured a blue football and a red sailing ship with its sails billowing in the wind. This ship is a historical symbol of Paris and is present on the city's coat of arms with its motto "Fluctuat nec mergitur" ("\[She] is tossed \[by the waves], but does not sink").[^cite_note-crests-47] After separating from PFC in 1972, PSG needed a new crest. Representing both Paris and [Saint-Germain-en-Laye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Germain-en-Laye), the club's second crest became the basis of the one fans know today, featuring the [Eiffel Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiffel_Tower) in red against a blue background and, below, two symbols of Saint-Germain in white: a [fleur-de-lis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fleur-de-lis) and the cradle of [Louis XIV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_XIV).[^cite_note-crests-47] This crest was first used in 1972 and was created by Christian Lentretien, a former PSG director and advertising professional.[^cite_note-crests-47][^cite_note-48]

In 1982, an image of the club's stadium, the Parc des Princes, was added to the bottom of the crest, remaining there until 1990, when it was removed and the crest reverted to its original form.[^cite_note-crests-47] Two years later, in 1992, the crest was radically modified by the club's owners, [Canal+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_S.A.). The new design featured the initials "PSG" in white on a blue, white, red, white, and blue background, mimicking the design of the Hechter shirt, with "Paris Saint-Germain" below, also in white on a black background. Following pressure from the fans, the traditional crest returned in 1995 with "Paris Saint-Germain" above the tower and "1970" below the cradle. In 2002, it underwent a minor modification, primarily the addition of a darker shade of blue.[^cite_note-crests-47]

At the request of the club's Qatari owners, the traditional crest was significantly redesigned in 2013.[^cite_note-crests-47] Now, the word "Paris" is written in large, bold white letters above the Eiffel Tower, clearly emphasizing the "Paris" brand rather than "Paris Saint-Germain". Below, "Saint-Germain" is written in smaller letters beneath the fleur-de-lis.[^cite_note-richest-33][^cite_note-dragon-49] The cradle of Louis XIV and the club's founding year, "1970", have been omitted.[^cite_note-dragon-49] PSG deputy general manager [Jean-Claude Blanc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Blanc) stated: "We are called Paris Saint-Germain but, above all, we are called Paris".[^cite_note-richest-33]

### Friendly tournaments

Main articles: [Tournoi de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournoi_de_Paris) and [Tournoi Indoor de Paris-Bercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournoi_Indoor_de_Paris-Bercy)

Paris Saint-Germain used to host two famous invitational competitions: the [Tournoi de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournoi_de_Paris) and the [Tournoi Indoor de Paris-Bercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournoi_Indoor_de_Paris-Bercy).[^cite_note-tdp-50][^cite_note-Bercy-51] Considered the most prestigious friendly tournament in French football, the Tournoi de Paris is regarded as the precursor to both the [Intercontinental Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_Cup_\(1960%E2%80%932004\)) and the [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup).[^cite_note-tdp-50][^cite_note-52] PSG began organizing it in 1975 and have been crowned champions a record seven times.[^cite_note-tdp-50] Held at the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes), the Tournoi de Paris was last organized in 2012.[^cite_note-tdp-50][^cite_note-53] The Tournoi Indoor de Paris-Bercy was an [indoor football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indoor_soccer) tournament founded by PSG in 1984 and held annually until 1991 at the [Accor Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accor_Arena). The Parisians have lifted the trophy twice, more than any other club.[^cite_note-Bercy-51]

## Grounds

### Stadiums

Main articles: [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes) and [Stade Georges Lefèvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Georges_Lef%C3%A8vre)



The [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes) in October 2022.

Since its inception, Paris Saint-Germain have played in five main stadiums: the [Stade Jean-Bouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Jean-Bouin_\(Paris\)), the [Stade Georges Lefèvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Georges_Lef%C3%A8vre), the [Stade Bauer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Bauer), the [Stade Yves-du-Manoir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Yves-du-Manoir), and the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes), their current home ground.[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54][^cite_note-55] PSG took on [Ligue 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2) promotion rivals [Red Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Star_F.C.) on 10 November 1973, for the club's first match at the Parc des Princes.[^cite_note-dossevi-43][^cite_note-StadesPSG-54] PSG moved into the ground upon its return to Ligue 1 in July 1974, the same year that rivals [Paris FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_FC) were relegated.[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54][^cite_note-pfc-56] Up until that point it had hosted Paris FC.[^cite_note-pfc-56]

From that moment on, the Parc des Princes has been the home stadium of PSG.[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54] Its most prolific season in terms of average attendance was [2024–25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), during which an average of 47,639 spectators went to the stadium for each match.[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54][^cite_note-Attendance-57] The attendance record for a PSG match dates back to 13 March 1983, with 49,575 spectators present in the stands of the Parc des Princes. This match pitted PSG against Belgian side [Waterschei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Waterschei_S.V._Thor_Genk) for the [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) quarterfinals. The final score was a 2–0 victory for the Parisians.[^cite_note-Waterschei-58]

The club played at the Stade Jean-Bouin, concurrently with the Stade Georges Lefèvre, in [1970–71](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970%E2%80%9371_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), as it attracted significantly higher levels of fan support.[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54][^cite_note-Saisons-59] The Georges Lefèvre regularly hosted PSG matches from 1972 to 1974 as well.[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54] Between 1904 and 1970, it was the home of [Stade Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Saint-Germain), club which merged with Paris FC to form PSG in 1970.[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54][^cite_note-AllPSG-60] PSG played again at the Jean-Bouin in [1973–74](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973%E2%80%9374_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), alternating their home games with the Georges Lefèvre and the Parc des Princes.[^cite_note-Saisons-59] PSG played at the Stade Bauer for the first time in [1971–72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season).[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54] It was used again by the club once in [1976–77](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976%E2%80%9377_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season) and [1977–78](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977%E2%80%9378_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), and twice in [1978–79](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978%E2%80%9379_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season) due to construction work on the lawn of the Parc des Princes.[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54][^cite_note-Saisons-59] Similarly, the Stade Yves-du-Manoir welcomed PSG for three matches in 1971–72, once more in [1974–75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), and one last time in [1975–76](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975%E2%80%9376_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season) because the Parc des Princes was under renovation.[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54][^cite_note-Saisons-59]

### Training facilities

Main articles: [Campus PSG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_PSG) and [Camp des Loges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_des_Loges)

[Campus PSG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campus_PSG), located in [Poissy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poissy), has been the club's [training ground](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Training_ground_\(association_football\)) since 2023.[^cite_note-Reprise-61] Owned and funded by the club, it houses the men's football team, the [women's football team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_\(women\)), and the [football academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_Youth_Academy), as well as the [handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Handball) and [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Judo) teams and their academies.[^cite_note-what-62][^cite_note-psg2022-63][^cite_note-64] Campus PSG will feature a stadium that will complement the club's stadium, the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes), which will be built during the second phase of the project, after 2024.[^cite_note-what-62][^cite_note-arena-65] PSG's male team moved into Campus PSG on 10 July 2023.[^cite_note-Reprise-61] They completed their first group training session there on 18 July 2023, and played their first game on 21 July 2023.[^cite_note-Session-66][^cite_note-Victory-67] It was a friendly match against [Le Havre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Havre_AC) which ended in a 2–0 victory for PSG.[^cite_note-Victory-67]

[Camp des Loges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_des_Loges), located in [Saint-Germain-en-Laye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint-Germain-en-Laye), was previously the club's training facility from July 1970 until July 2023.[^cite_note-TheEnd-68][^cite_note-camp-69] Its main stadium, the [Stade Georges Lefèvre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Georges_Lef%C3%A8vre), served as the home ground for the men's team between 1972 and 1974.[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54] Camp des Loges also hosted the training sessions of the club's academy and women's team until 2024.[^cite_note-camp-69][^cite_note-TheEnd-68][^cite_note-FirstDay-70] The men's team moved to Campus PSG in July 2023.[^cite_note-Reprise-61] The academy and women's team followed in January 2024.[^cite_note-FirstDay-70][^cite_note-71] However, the Camp des Loges remains the headquarters of the [Association Paris Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_Youth_Academy#Association_Paris_Saint-Germain), as has been the case since 1970.[^cite_note-Association-72][^cite_note-APSG-73]

## Statistics

### Records

Main article: [List of Paris Saint-Germain FC records and statistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_records_and_statistics)

Coupes de la Ligue remportées par le PSG.

Trophy room in 2008.

Paris Saint-Germain hold numerous records, most notably as the [most successful French club in history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_records_and_statistics_in_France) in terms of major official titles, with 60.[^cite_note-statistiques-74][^cite_note-Palmares-75] They hold the record in all domestic competitions, having won 14 [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) titles, 16 [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France), nine [Coupe de la Ligue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_la_Ligue), and 14 [Trophée des Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troph%C3%A9e_des_Champions). Internationally, PSG have won two [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) titles, two [UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Super_Cup), one [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup), one [UEFA Intertoto Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Intertoto_Cup), and one [FIFA Intercontinental Cup](73290908.md). Their honours also include one [Ligue 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2) title, bringing their total number of official titles to 61.[^cite_note-Palmares-75] They have also secured several unofficial titles, including a record seven [Tournoi de Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournoi_de_Paris), a record two [Tournoi Indoor de Paris-Bercy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournoi_Indoor_de_Paris-Bercy), and two [International Champions Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Champions_Cup) trophies.[^cite_note-Saisons-59][^cite_note-unofficial-76]

PSG's victory in the [1995–96 UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) made them the only French club to win that competition and one of only two French teams to have claimed a [major European trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_UEFA_club_competition_winners).[^cite_note-UCWC-77][^cite_note-Acte3-78] By winning the [2024–25 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Champions_League), together with the [2024–25 Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Ligue_1) and the [2024–25 Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Coupe_de_France), the club became the first French side to achieve a [continental treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_\(association_football\)).[^cite_note-Treble-10] Subsequent victories in the [2025 UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Super_Cup) and the [2025 FIFA Intercontinental Cup](76156932.md) made PSG the first French club to win both trophies and delivered the first [club world title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_world_champion_football_clubs) in the history of French football.[^cite_note-Spurs-11][^cite_note-Flamengo-12][^cite_note-Intercontinental-79] Having also won the [2024 Trophée des Champions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Troph%C3%A9e_des_Champions), PSG completed an unprecedented [sextuple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sextuple_\(association_football\)), a feat previously achieved only by [Barcelona](68187.md) in 2009 and [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) in 2020.[^cite_note-Sextuple-13][^cite_note-Unprecedented-80]

The Parisians hold the record for the most consecutive seasons in the French top flight, with 52 seasons in [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) since [1974–75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season).[^cite_note-50th-81][^cite_note-Consecutive-82] They are also the only club to have won the Ligue 1 title while leading the table from the first to the final matchday ([2022–23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Ligue_1)),[^cite_note-83] the Coupe de France without conceding a goal ([1992–93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992%E2%80%9393_Coupe_de_France) and [2016–17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016%E2%80%9317_Coupe_de_France)),[^cite_note-84] five consecutive Coupe de la Ligue titles (2014–2018),[^cite_note-85] four successive Coupe de France trophies (2015–2018),[^cite_note-86] and eight consecutive Trophée des Champions titles (2013–2020).[^cite_note-87]

Influential figures in the history of PSG include the club's most decorated president [Nasser Al-Khelaifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasser_Al-Khelaifi),[^cite_note-PalmaresPresidents-88] the most decorated manager [Luis Enrique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luis_Enrique),[^cite_note-Managers-89] record appearance holder [Marquinhos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquinhos),[^cite_note-apps-90] all-time top scorer [Kylian Mbappé](48711857.md),[^cite_note-TopScorer-91] assist leader [Ángel Di María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngel_Di_Mar%C3%ADa),[^cite_note-assist-92] clean-sheet record holder [Bernard Lama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Lama),[^cite_note-sheets-93] longest-serving captain [Thiago Silva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thiago_Silva).[^cite_note-TS-94] Other notable players include [Ballon d'Or](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballon_d'Or) winners [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) and [Ousmane Dembélé](48621084.md),[^cite_note-BallonPSG-95] as well as [world-record transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_expensive_association_football_transfers) signing [Neymar](15326144.md).[^cite_note-Neymar-96]

### Seasons

Main article: [List of Paris Saint-Germain FC seasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_seasons)



Starting eleven during the [2012–13 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season).

Paris Saint-Germain have played 56 seasons, of which 53 have been played in the [top division](3480022.md) of [French football](1963109.md), the [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1). In the remaining three seasons, the club have played twice in the second division, the [Ligue 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_2), and once in the third division, the [Division 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Division_3_\(1971%E2%80%931993\)).[^cite_note-Saisons-59][^cite_note-50th-81][^cite_note-History-97][^cite_note-Chiffres-98] In the [2025–26 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025%E2%80%9326_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), PSG celebrated their 52nd consecutive Ligue 1 campaign, making them the longest-serving club in the competition.[^cite_note-50th-81][^cite_note-Consecutive-82] The club played its first competitive match on 23 August 1970, drawing 1–1 away to [Poitiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Poitevin_FC) in the opening matchday of Ligue 2.[^cite_note-History-97][^cite_note-Poitiers-99] PSG won the Ligue 2 title at the end of the campaign, earning promotion to the top flight.[^cite_note-UEFA-3][^cite_note-psgfc-5] The Parisians made their Ligue 1 debut on 11 August 1971, in a 2–0 away defeat to [Angers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angers_SCO).[^cite_note-50th-81] Their momentum soon came to a halt and the club split in June 1972, with Paris FC remaining in Ligue 1 and PSG being administratively relegated to Division 3.[^cite_note-UEFA-3][^cite_note-divorce-100]

After two consecutive promotions, PSG returned to Ligue 1 in the [1974–75 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974%E2%80%9375_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season) and never looked back.[^cite_note-50th-81][^cite_note-Consecutive-82] Since then, the club have won a record thirteen league titles, finishing first more often than in any other position. Having finished second in nine league campaigns, PSG have regained the top two places on 22 occasions. They have also reached the top five 30 times, representing more than half of the club's seasons in Ligue 1. PSG's lowest ever finish is 16th, achieved in both the [1971–72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1971%E2%80%9372_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season) and the [2007–08](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007%E2%80%9308_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season) seasons, when they escaped relegation on the final day with a 2–1 win at [Sochaux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Sochaux_Montb%C3%A9liard).[^cite_note-50th-81]

PSG enjoyed their best season to date in the [2024–25 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season), winning the [continental treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_\(association_football\)) by lifting the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) title, the league championship and the Coupe de France. They played a record 65 matches, winning 48 of them, and averaged a record attendance of 47,639 per home league game.[^cite_note-Treble-10][^cite_note-Attendance-57][^cite_note-collectifs-101] The club also set several records during the [2015–16 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season): PSG collected 96 points, their highest total in Ligue 1, while conceding just 19 goals, and [Zlatan Ibrahimović](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zlatan_Ibrahimovi%C4%87) became the player with the most goals scored in a single season, scoring 50. Other notable records across all competitions include the 28 goals conceded in the [1993–94 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season) and the 171 goals scored in the [2017–18 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017%E2%80%9318_Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_season).[^cite_note-collectifs-101][^cite_note-individuels-102]

## International football

Main article: [Paris Saint-Germain FC in international football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_in_international_football)



PSG legend [Safet Sušić](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safet_Su%C5%A1i%C4%87) in 1983.

Paris Saint-Germain are the most successful French club in international competitions. They have won [six European titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_UEFA_club_competition_winners) (the [UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup) in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup), the [UEFA Intertoto Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Intertoto_Cup) in [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_UEFA_Intertoto_Cup), the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_UEFA_Champions_League) and [2026](74127997.md), and the [UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UEFA_Super_Cup) in [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Super_Cup) and [2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_UEFA_Super_Cup)) as well as the [2025 FIFA Intercontinental Cup](76156932.md). PSG are also the only French side to have won the UEFA Cup Winners' Cup and the first to win the [continental treble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treble_\(association_football\)) and the Super Cup.[^cite_note-Treble-10][^cite_note-Spurs-11][^cite_note-UCWC-77][^cite_note-Acte3-78] They were runners-up in the [1996 UEFA Super Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_UEFA_Super_Cup), the [1996–97 UEFA Cup Winners' Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996%E2%80%9397_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup), the [2019–20 UEFA Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019%E2%80%9320_UEFA_Champions_League), and the [2025 FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup).[^cite_note-Dazzling-103][^cite_note-Ronaldo-104][^cite_note-105][^cite_note-Palmer-106]

The Parisians made their international debut in the [1982–83](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982%E2%80%9383_European_Cup_Winners'_Cup) season, qualifying for the now-defunct Cup Winners' Cup as [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France) winners.[^cite_note-psgfc-5] Their first match was against [Lokomotiv Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PFC_Lokomotiv_Sofia) and reached the quarter-finals, where they were eliminated by [Waterschei Thor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K._Waterschei_S.V._Thor_Genk).[^cite_note-Bulgarie-107][^cite_note-Valence-108] PSG subsequently competed in the [UEFA Europa League](232175.md) in the [1984–85](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984%E2%80%9385_UEFA_Cup) campaign, before taking their first steps in Europe's premier club competition, the Champions League, in the [1986–87](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986%E2%80%9387_European_Cup) season.[^cite_note-109][^cite_note-110][^cite_note-Exploits-111]

Between 1992 and 1997, the club reached five consecutive semi-finals: three in the Cup Winners' Cup, one in the Champions League, and one in the Europa League.[^cite_note-Top15-112][^cite_note-semis-113] They won their first European trophy by defeating [Rapid Wien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SK_Rapid_Wien) in the [1996 UEFA Cup Winners' Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup_final).[^cite_note-UCWC-77][^cite_note-First-114] This victory allowed PSG to compete in the UEFA Super Cup, where they lost to [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC).[^cite_note-Dazzling-103] They reached a second consecutive Cup Winners' Cup final in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_UEFA_Cup_Winners'_Cup_final), this time losing to [Barcelona](68187.md).[^cite_note-Ronaldo-104] PSG played in the now-defunct Intertoto Cup once. They did so in 2001, winning their second continental trophy against [Brescia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brescia_Calcio) on away goals in the [two-legged final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_UEFA_Intertoto_Cup#Finals).[^cite_note-France-115]

PSG reached their first UEFA Champions League final in [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_UEFA_Champions_League_final), where they were defeated by [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich), marking their first European final appearance since 2001.[^cite_note-Heartbreak-9][^cite_note-116] The club went on to win their first Champions League title in 2025, defeating [Inter Milan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inter_Milan) in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_UEFA_Champions_League_final).[^cite_note-Inter-117] As reigning European champions, Paris competed in the [FIFA Club World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Club_World_Cup), the UEFA Super Cup and the [FIFA Intercontinental Cup](73290908.md).[^cite_note-Inter-117][^cite_note-Glory-118] They finished runners-up in the Club World Cup, losing the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_FIFA_Club_World_Cup_final) to [Chelsea](7473.md), but secured victories in both the Super Cup, defeating [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) on penalties, and the Intercontinental Cup, also winning on penalties against [Flamengo](187239.md).[^cite_note-Spurs-11][^cite_note-Flamengo-12][^cite_note-Palmer-106] PSG won a second consecutive Champions League title in 2026, defeating [Arsenal](2174.md) in the [final](76987191.md) on penalties.[^cite_note-UCL2026-14]

## Supporters

Main article: [Paris Saint-Germain FC supporters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_supporters)

OM-PSG en finale de la coupe de la France en 2006.

PSG fans at the [2006 French Cup final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Coupe_de_France_final).

Paris Saint-Germain are the most popular football club in France and one of the most widely followed teams in the world.[^cite_note-Popular-119][^cite_note-Support-120][^cite_note-RRSS-121] Its home ground, the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes), has hosted the team since July 1974.[^cite_note-StadesPSG-54] The stadium is divided into four main stands: Tribune Auteuil, Tribune Paris, Tribune Borelli, and Tribune Boulogne.[^cite_note-prince-122] Historically, the Auteuil and Boulogne stands—better known as the Virage Auteuil (VA) and the Kop of Boulogne (KoB)—have been the strongholds of PSG's [ultras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultras), making the Parc one of the most intimidating venues in Europe and renowned for its electric atmosphere.[^cite_note-BestTifos-123][^cite_note-UltrasPSG-124] Meanwhile, more family-friendly and welcoming fan groups typically gather in the Paris and Borelli stands, including the club's first [supporters' group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supporters'_group), Les Amis du PSG, which was founded in 1975.[^cite_note-HAL-125][^cite_note-kop-126]

Lacking a large and passionate following in its early years, the club began offering cheaper season tickets to young fans in 1976. These supporters were placed in Kop K, located in the Blue K Section of the Paris stand at the Parc des Princes.[^cite_note-kop-126][^cite_note-trust-29] After ticket prices increased, the Kop K fans moved to the Boulogne stand in 1978, giving rise to the Kop of Boulogne.[^cite_note-trust-29][^cite_note-earning-32][^cite_note-boys-127] The club's first Italian-style ultra group, the Boulogne Boys, was founded there in 1985, followed by the English-inspired kopistes Gavroches and Rangers.[^cite_note-boys-127][^cite_note-soul-128][^cite_note-90min-129] However, some KoB groups, including Commando Pirate and Casual Firm, took English hooligans as questionable role models, and violence escalated rapidly.[^cite_note-HAL-125][^cite_note-new-130]

In response, the club's owners, [Canal+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_S.A.), encouraged non-violent KoB fans to gather at the opposite end of the stadium, leading to the creation of the Virage Auteuil in 1991. This section was spearheaded by the ultra groups Supras Auteuil, Lutece Falco, and Tigris Mystic.[^cite_note-earning-32][^cite_note-HAL-125][^cite_note-supras-131] The initiative initially succeeded, but a violent, racially charged rivalry gradually developed between the two stands.[^cite_note-earning-32][^cite_note-supras-131][^cite_note-opposition-132] Tensions peaked in 2010, before a match against [Olympique de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille), when Boulogne fan Yann Lorence was killed following a brawl outside the Parc between supporters from both stands.[^cite_note-earning-32][^cite_note-ultras-133] This tragedy prompted PSG president [Robin Leproux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Leproux) to implement the Plan Leproux, which exiled all supporter groups from the Parc and banned them from away matches.[^cite_note-UltrasPSG-124] After former VA supporters formed the Collectif Ultras Paris (CUP) in May 2016, the club allowed their return in October of the same year.[^cite_note-UltrasPSG-124][^cite_note-ultras-133][^cite_note-CUP-134]

Based in Auteuil, the CUP are currently the only ultra group officially recognized by the club.[^cite_note-divergences-135] PSG allowed the CUP to take over the Boulogne stand starting in the 2025–26 season.[^cite_note-BoulogneCUP-136][^cite_note-KoBCUP-137] They share the stadium with other licensed groups: Hoolicool, Vikings 27, and Handicap PSG in the Paris stand; and Les Amis du PSG, Titi Fosi, and PSG Grand Sud in the Borelli stand.[^cite_note-earning-32][^cite_note-AS06-138][^cite_note-AS08-139] In the past, PSG also recognized Supras Auteuil, Lutece Falco, and Tigris Mystic from Auteuil; Boulogne Boys, Gavroches, and Rangers from Boulogne; and Authentiks, Puissance Paris, and Brigade Paris from Paris.[^cite_note-HAL-125][^cite_note-AS06-138][^cite_note-AS08-139] The [hooligan firm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hooligan_firms) Karsud remains active but has been banned from all PSG matches since 2017.[^cite_note-HAL-125][^cite_note-lose-140][^cite_note-threats-141]

## Rivalries

### Le Classique

Main articles: [Le Classique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Classique) and [Le Classique (women)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Classique_\(women\))

[Le Classique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Classique), also referred to as Le Classico, Le Clasico, or Le Derby de France, is a [French football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_rivalries_in_France) contested between PSG and [Olympique de Marseille](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_de_Marseille) (OM). It is widely regarded as the biggest rivalry in [French football](1963109.md) and one of the most notable fixtures in world football, involving the [two most successful clubs in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_records_and_statistics_in_France) and the only French teams to have won [major European trophies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_UEFA_club_competition_winners). The match is often compared to Spain's [El Clásico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cl%C3%A1sico) and attracts significant attention in domestic and international football circles.[^cite_note-Bleacher-142][^cite_note-term-143][^cite_note-144][^cite_note-145]



PSG/OM at the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes) in 2007.

PSG and OM dominated French football before [Olympique Lyonnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympique_Lyonnais) disrupted their domestic supremacy in the early 2000s and remain the most followed French clubs internationally. Both clubs consistently rank among the highest in French attendances.[^cite_note-Popular-119][^cite_note-Bleacher-142][^cite_note-rivalry-146] Early clashes in the 1970s gave little indication of a major rivalry: PSG, a newly formed club, was still building competitiveness, while OM were established [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) contenders.[^cite_note-ompsg-147] The rivalry began in earnest in the 1980s, particularly after PSG won their first league title in 1986 and Marseille was acquired by businessman [Bernard Tapie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernard_Tapie). By the end of the decade, the two clubs were competing closely for the Ligue 1 title, with tensions heightened by accusations of [match-fixing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_football_bribery_scandal) and other controversies.[^cite_note-rivalry-146][^cite_note-ompsg-147][^cite_note-archrivals-148]

In the 1990s, the rivalry intensified. French media company [Canal+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_S.A.) purchased PSG in 1991, partly to challenge Marseille's dominance, while media coverage helped promote the animosity between the clubs. With financial backing and growing media attention, PSG and OM became the main contenders for national honours. Although both teams were less successful in the 2000s, the rivalry remained strong. In the 2010s, PSG's significant investment from [Qatar Sports Investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Sports_Investments) (QSI) allowed the club to dominate domestically, further intensifying the rivalry. Matches regularly draw large crowds, high television audiences, and require heightened security due to passionate fan support.[^cite_note-rivalry-146][^cite_note-ompsg-147][^cite_note-archrivals-148][^cite_note-fallen-149]

The rivalry has a female equivalent, featuring [Paris Saint-Germain FC (women)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_\(women\)) and [OL Lyonnes](8587183.md) (Lyon). Matches between the two sides are referred to as the [women's Le Classique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Classique_\(women\)). By the end of the 2000s, [French women's football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_football_in_France) was dominated by Lyon, which won a record number of [Première Ligue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premi%C3%A8re_Ligue) and [UEFA Women's Champions League](1985338.md) titles.[^cite_note-ClassiqueW-150][^cite_note-unbeaten-151][^cite_note-invincibility-152] PSG first faced Lyon in 1994, but it was not until the club's acquisition by QSI in 2012 that the two sides emerged as genuine rivals, consistently competing for league honours.[^cite_note-ClassiqueW-150][^cite_note-LyonPSG-153][^cite_note-Dates-154][^cite_note-WomenPSG-155]

### Paris derby

Main articles: [Paris derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_derby), [Paris derby (women)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_derby_\(women\)), and [Football in Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Paris)

The [Paris derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_derby), also referred to as Derby de Paris, Derby de la Capitale, or Le Derby Parisien, is a [French football rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_rivalries_in_France) contested between PSG and [Paris FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_FC) (PFC), the two largest professional clubs based in Paris, France. PSG, founded in 1970 through the merger of Paris FC and [Stade Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Saint-Germain), has grown into the [dominant club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_records_and_statistics_in_France) in both the capital and [French football](1963109.md), regularly competing in [Ligue 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ligue_1) and European competitions. Paris FC, which split from PSG in 1972, spent decades in the lower divisions before returning to the top flight in 2025.[^cite_note-Derby1-156][^cite_note-Special-157][^cite_note-Forgotten-158]



PSG, the dominant club in Paris, in 2001.

The rivalry traces its roots to the early history of football in the French capital, where historic clubs like [Racing Club de France Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Club_de_France_Football) and [Red Star FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Star_FC) once competed. PSG emerged from efforts by the [French Football Federation](682002.md) (FFF) to create a major inner‑city club in the late 1960s, while Paris FC originally remained in Ligue 1 following the 1972 split. PSG quickly established itself as the capital's leading club, taking over the Parc des Princes and embarking on a period of sustained success that left Paris FC absent from the top tier for decades. Consequently, there were no first‑team meetings between the two between 1978 and 2025.[^cite_note-Derby1-156][^cite_note-Special-157][^cite_note-Forgotten-158]

Following Paris FC's promotion to Ligue 1 in 2025, the Paris derby returned. The two clubs' home stadiums—PSG's [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes) and PFC's [Stade Jean-Bouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stade_Jean-Bouin_\(Paris\))—are separated by only a short distance, creating one of the closest geographic derbies in world football. The first top‑flight meeting since 1990 took place on 4 January 2026 at the Parc des Princes, with PSG winning 2–1 in a closely contested match. A subsequent [Coupe de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coupe_de_France) encounter saw Paris FC secure a 1–0 victory, marking PSG's first home defeat in the competition in several years.[^cite_note-Forgotten-158][^cite_note-Different-159][^cite_note-Shine-160][^cite_note-FrenchCup2026-161]

Beyond the men's game, the Paris derby extends to [women's football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_football_in_France), where [Paris Saint-Germain FC (women)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_\(women\)) and [Paris FC (women)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_FC_\(women\)) also compete at the highest level, adding further depth to the rivalry. Paris FC, formerly known as Juvisy, was the capital's dominant women's club in the 2000s, while PSG largely dominated the [Paris derby (women)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_derby_\(women\)) during the 2010s. In the early 2020s, Paris FC established itself as the third force in the league, highlighted by their victory over PSG in the [2025 Coupe de France Féminine final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Coupe_de_France_F%C3%A9minine_final). Together with PSG, they the only clubs capable of contesting the dominance of [OL Lyonnes](8587183.md).[^cite_note-Derby1-156][^cite_note-Juvisy-162][^cite_note-WomenPFC-163][^cite_note-Derby2-164]

## Ownership and finances

Main article: [Paris Saint-Germain FC ownership and finances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_ownership_and_finances)



PSG's [Kylian Mbappé](48711857.md), [Lionel Messi](2150841.md) and [Neymar](15326144.md) in 2021.

Paris Saint-Germain were initially [fan-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_fan-owned_sports_teams), with around 20,000 members under a non-profit association governed by French law (Association PSG). The club was led by board members [Guy Crescent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guy_Crescent), [Pierre-Étienne Guyot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pierre-%C3%89tienne_Guyot) and [Henri Patrelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henri_Patrelle).[^cite_note-History-97][^cite_note-santiago-165] In 1973, a group of French businessmen led by [Daniel Hechter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Hechter) and [Francis Borelli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Borelli) took control of the club.[^cite_note-hechter-166] PSG changed ownership in 1991 when [Canal+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_S.A.) created a professional sports company (SASP) structure, followed by another takeover in 2006 by [Colony Capital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_Capital).[^cite_note-asso-167][^cite_note-finances-168] Since 2011, [Qatar Sports Investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Sports_Investments) (QSI) have been the majority owners, currently holding 85.9% of shares, with [Arctos Partners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arctos_Partners) (12.5%) and [Boardroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boardroom_\(company\)) (1.6%) holding the remainder.[^cite_note-qatar-169][^cite_note-Arctos-170][^cite_note-Boardroom_(2)-171]

Backed by the Qatari state, QSI acquired a controlling stake in 2011 and became sole owner in 2012.[^cite_note-finances-168][^cite_note-qatar-169][^cite_note-subsidiary-172] Since then, PSG have been widely described as a state-backed club and among the wealthiest in world football.[^cite_note-money-173][^cite_note-rule-174][^cite_note-gulf-175] QSI chairman [Nasser Al-Khelaifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasser_Al-Khelaifi) has served as PSG president since the takeover.[^cite_note-fortunes-176] However, the [Emir of Qatar](399230.md), [Tamim bin Hamad Al Thani](6146151.md), as head of the [Qatar Investment Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Investment_Authority) (QIA) and founder of QSI, is widely regarded as the ultimate decision-maker on major strategic matters.[^cite_note-thani-177][^cite_note-boss-178] Upon arrival, QSI pledged to build a squad capable of winning the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md).[^cite_note-psgfc-5] Since 2011, PSG have invested heavily in transfer fees and wages.[^cite_note-179] This spending has led to domestic dominance and two consecutive Champions League titles, but also scrutiny under [UEFA Financial Fair Play Regulations](34255206.md).[^cite_note-spent-180][^cite_note-fortunes-176][^cite_note-leaks-181]

As of May 2026, PSG ranked [fourth globally in football revenue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte_Football_Money_League) with €837 million according to [Deloitte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deloitte), and were valued at around €5 billion by [Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes), making them the [fifth-most valuable club in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes'_list_of_the_most_valuable_football_clubs).[^cite_note-Deloitte-182][^cite_note-Forbes-183] This growth has been driven by sustained investment, sporting success, global branding, and commercial partnerships.[^cite_note-spent-180][^cite_note-postqta-184][^cite_note-Glitter-185][^cite_note-Jordan-186][^cite_note-QatarAirways-187] Deloitte also ranked PSG's [women's team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_\(women\)) among the top revenue-generating sides in world women's football, while the club's [handball section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Handball) operates with one of the largest budgets in European handball.[^cite_note-188][^cite_note-Season2018-189][^cite_note-Season2019-190][^cite_note-Figaro-191][^cite_note-Monde-192]

## Other teams

### Women's football

Main article: [Paris Saint-Germain FC (women)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_\(women\))

[Paris Saint-Germain FC (women)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_\(women\)) are a [French](1963109.md) professional [women's football](191466.md) [club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_football_clubs_in_France) based in Paris, France. It operates as the women's football department of Paris Saint-Germain FC. Founded in 1971, the club competes in the [Première Ligue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premi%C3%A8re_Ligue), the [top tier](3480022.md) of [women's football in France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_football_in_France). PSG spent its early decades moving between divisions before establishing itself in the top flight in 2001. Following the takeover by [Qatar Sports Investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Sports_Investments) (QSI) in 2012, the women's team received increased investment, leading to the professionalization of the squad and its emergence as one of the leading clubs in French women's football. The club subsequently established itself as a regular contender for domestic honours and in European competition.[^cite_note-Dates-154][^cite_note-WomenPSG-155]

The [Paris Saint-Germain FC Youth Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_Youth_Academy) is the [youth system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youth_system) for the men's and [women's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_FC_\(women\)) football teams of Paris Saint-Germain. ## Other sports

### Handball

Main article: [Paris Saint-Germain Handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Handball)

[Paris Saint-Germain Handball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Handball) is a French professional [handball](13730.md) club based in Paris, France. It operates as the handball department of Paris Saint-Germain FC. Founded in 1941, the club currently competes in the top tier of French handball, the [LNH Division 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LNH_Division_1). Originally established as Patriotes d'Asnières, the club underwent several name changes and relocations before becoming part of Paris Saint-Germain ahead of the 1992–93 season. Following a period of decline and separation from PSG in 2002, the club was rebranded as Paris Handball and later entered a new era after being acquired by [Qatar Sports Investments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Sports_Investments) (QSI) in 2012, adopting its current name.[^cite_note-PSGH-216]

The club's history has been marked by steady development and several pivotal turning points. After experiencing both successes and setbacks in the mid-20th century, PSG Handball rose to prominence during the 1990s under the PSG banner. However, the early 2000s saw the club suffer relegation and financial difficulties. The post-2002 period involved a gradual rebuilding process, with Paris Handball stabilising independently and achieving consistent domestic performances, laying the foundations for its resurgence prior to the QSI takeover. Since 2012, PSG Handball have established themselves as the dominant force in French handball.[^cite_note-PSGH-216]

### Esports

Main article: [PSG Esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSG_Esports)

[Paris Saint-Germain Esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PSG_Esports) is a French professional [esports](564204.md) club based in Paris, France. It operates as the esports department of Paris Saint-Germain FC. The organisation currently fields teams in *[EA Sports FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EA_Sports_FC)*.[^cite_note-Effectifs-217] It has previously competed in *[League of Legends](21838352.md)*, *[Rocket League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_League)*, *[Dota 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dota_2)*, *[FIFA Online](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIFA_Online)*, *[Brawl Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brawl_Stars)*, *[Mobile Legends: Bang Bang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Legends:_Bang_Bang)*, *[Arena of Valor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arena_of_Valor)*, *[Fortnite Battle Royale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortnite_Battle_Royale)*, and *[Tom Clancy's Rainbow Six Siege](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Clancy's_Rainbow_Six_Siege)*.[^cite_note-PalmaresEsports-218]

Founded in 2016, PSG Esports entered competitive esports through *League of Legends* and *EA Sports FC* before expanding into multiple titles. The organisation has competed across a variety of esports scenes, establishing partnerships with teams including [LGD Gaming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGD_Gaming), [Talon Esports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talon_Esports), [Team Vitality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Vitality), Team New Age, and Tundra Esports. Across all divisions, PSG Esports has won several titles, including championships in *Dota 2*, *League of Legends*, *EA Sports FC*, *Brawl Stars*, *Rocket League*, and *Rainbow Six Siege*.[^cite_note-PalmaresEsports-218]

### Judo

Main article: [Paris Saint-Germain Judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Judo)

[Paris Saint-Germain Judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Judo) is a French professional [judo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Judo) club based in Paris, France. It operates as the judo department of Paris Saint-Germain FC. Founded in September 1992 at the initiative of former judoka [Thierry Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thierry_Rey) and with financial support from [Canal+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_S.A.), owner of PSG, the judo section was the club's first to win a continental trophy.[^cite_note-Bilan-219][^cite_note-PSGJudo93-220][^cite_note-PSGJudo95-221] Led by star judokas [David Douillet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Douillet), [Cécile Nowak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C%C3%A9cile_Nowak), [Djamel Bouras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djamel_Bouras) and [Frédéric Demontfaucon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fr%C3%A9d%C3%A9ric_Demontfaucon), the Parisians clinched the [Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Champions_League_\(judo\)) in 1995 and the French 1st Division Championship in 1997.[^cite_note-PSGJudo95-221][^cite_note-PSGJudo97-222] However, PSG judo experienced a sharp decline thereafter, leading Canal+ to withdraw in 2002, before PSG closed the section in June 2003.[^cite_note-PSGJudo02-223][^cite_note-PSGJudo03-224]

Conceived by Bouras, who was part of the original iteration of the section, PSG Judo was officially reestablished on 1 September 2017. Paris Saint-Germain president [Nasser Al-Khelaifi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasser_Al-Khelaifi) announced the project in a press release. He also confirmed the signing of French judoka [Teddy Riner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teddy_Riner) as the team's leading figure, while Bouras was appointed president. Initially an all-male project, PSG Judo eventually evolved into a mixed-gender team.[^cite_note-psgjudo-225][^cite_note-bouras-226] Since their return, the PSG men's and women's teams have enjoyed domestic and European success.[^cite_note-Merci-227]

### Basketball

Main article: [Paris Saint-Germain Basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Basketball)

[Paris Saint-Germain Basketball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Basketball) was a French professional [basketball](3921.md) club based in Paris, France. It operated as the basketball department of Paris Saint-Germain FC. Founded in 1992, following the takeover of [Racing Paris Basket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Basket_Racing) by PSG and its owners [Canal+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_S.A.), the club competed in the [LNB Élite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LNB_%C3%89lite), the top tier of French basketball. Despite an ambitious recruitment drive, PSG-Racing experienced early difficulties before establishing itself as a competitive force in French basketball during the mid-1990s.[^cite_note-Season1993-228][^cite_note-Season1996-229]

The club reached its peak during the 1996–97 season, when it won the French championship, securing the only LNB Élite title in its history.[^cite_note-Season1997-230] By the late 1990s, financial uncertainty, particularly following Canal+'s decision to reduce its involvement, combined with injuries and squad instability, led to the dissolution of PSG-Racing in May 2000. Its place in French basketball was subsequently taken by [Paris Basket Racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Basket_Racing), bringing an end to an eight-year chapter of PSG's involvement in professional basketball.[^cite_note-Season2000-231]

### Volleyball

Main article: [Paris Saint-Germain Volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Volleyball)

[Paris Saint-Germain Volleyball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Volleyball) was a French professional [volleyball](32558.md) club based in Paris, France. It operated as the volleyball department of Paris Saint-Germain FC. It was founded as PSG-Asnières Volley in 1992, following the takeover of [Asnières Sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asni%C3%A8res_Volley_92), based in the Paris suburb of [Asnières-sur-Seine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asni%C3%A8res-sur-Seine), by PSG and its owners [Canal+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_S.A.). The club competed in the top tier of French volleyball, the [LNV Ligue A Masculine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LNV_Ligue_A_Masculine).[^cite_note-Volley1993-232]

In its inaugural 1992–93 season, PSG-Asnières won the French championship.[^cite_note-Volley1993-232] The team remained competitive in subsequent seasons and came close to a domestic double in 1993–94, winning the Coupe de France and finishing runners-up in the league.[^cite_note-Season1994-233] Ahead of the 1995–96 season, PSG withdrew from its partnership with Asnières and merged its top-flight licence with [Racing Club de France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_Club_de_France), creating PSG-Racing Volley. Despite occasional strong performances and playoff appearances, PSG-Racing failed to replicate its early success.[^cite_note-Season1995-234]

The club's future was ultimately decided off the court. In 1998, discussions between officials from PSG and [Paris Université Club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Universit%C3%A9_Club) (PUC) intensified, with the aim of consolidating elite volleyball in the capital by creating a single, stable, and internationally competitive Parisian club. Despite initial administrative and financial obstacles, a protocol agreement was signed in July 1998, leading to the formation of [Paris Volley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Volley).[^cite_note-Season1998-235]

### Boxing

Main article: [Paris Saint-Germain Boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Boxing)

[Paris Saint-Germain Boxing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Boxing) was a French [professional boxing](4283645.md) club based in Paris, France. It operated as the boxing department of Paris Saint-Germain FC. The section was established in 1992 on the initiative of former French professional boxer [Jean-Claude Bouttier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Claude_Bouttier) and trained at a dedicated boxing gym located within the [Parc des Princes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parc_des_Princes). Throughout its existence, the club was led by president Bouttier, along with managers René Acquaviva and [Gilbert Delé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilbert_Del%C3%A9), and featured a roster of prominent French boxers.[^cite_note-BoxingPSG-236]

During its five years of competition, PSG Boxing achieved notable success, winning four French championships, three European titles, and one world championship. Among its principal achievements were Patrice Aouissi's French and European cruiserweight titles, [Julien Lorcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julien_Lorcy) and [Djamel Lifa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Djamel_Lifa)'s European championships, and [Khalid Rahilou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalid_Rahilou)'s WBA light welterweight world title. The section entered its final phase following the departure of head coach René Acquaviva in mid-1997. With limited support from the [French Boxing Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Boxing_Federation), PSG Boxing was disbanded later that year, and the boxing gym at the Parc des Princes was closed.[^cite_note-BoxingPSG-236]

### Rugby League

Main article: [Paris Saint-Germain Rugby League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Rugby_League)

[Paris Saint-Germain Rugby League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paris_Saint-Germain_Rugby_League) was a French professional [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league) club based in Paris, France. It operated as the rugby league department of Paris Saint-Germain FC. Founded in 1995, the club was established as part of an expansion project for the [Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_League), the top division of the [British rugby league system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rugby_league_system). Created under president [Jacques Fouroux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Fouroux) with the support of French media giants [Canal+](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canal+_S.A.) and Australian media conglomerate [News Corp Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_Corp_Australia), PSG RL became the first French team to participate in the competition.[^cite_note-psgrl-237][^cite_note-hist-96-238]

In its [inaugural 1996 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Paris_Saint-Germain_season), PSG RL contested [the first match in Super League history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_Super_League_season), defeating the [Sheffield Eagles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheffield_Eagles) 30–24 in front of nearly 18,000 spectators, the largest rugby league crowd in France in several decades.[^cite_note-Kirwan-239] Despite a promising start, the club's form declined, and it finished 11th of 12 teams with three wins and one draw.[^cite_note-hist-96-238] In the [1997 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Paris_Saint-Germain_season), PSG RL recorded several notable results, including a 30–28 home victory over [Wigan Warriors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigan_Warriors), and competed in the [1997 Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Challenge_Cup), the [1997 World Club Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_World_Club_Championship), and the [1997 Rugby League Premiership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Rugby_League_Premiership) before again finishing 11th in the league standings. The club ceased operations later in 1997 following financial difficulties, declining attendances, and administrative issues.[^cite_note-hist-97-240]
