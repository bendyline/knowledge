**Pune district** (Marathi pronunciation: [\[puɳeː\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Marathi)) is a district in Western Maharashtra with Administrative Headquarters in [Pune](164634.md) city. Pune district is the most populous district in the Indian state of [Maharashtra](20629.md). It is one of the most industrialized districts in India.

## History

### Ancient and medieval history

According to archaeological discoveries of the [Jorwe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jorwe) culture in [Chandoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandoli_Budruk) and [Inamgaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inamgaon), portions of the district have been occupied by humans since the [Chalcolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalcolithic) (the Copper Age, 5th–4th millennium BCE).[^cite_note-5] Many ancient trade routes linking ports in western India (particularly those of coastal [Konkan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konkan)) with the [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau) pass through the district. The town of [Junnar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junnar) has been an important trading and political center for the last two thousand years, and it was first mentioned by Greco-Roman travellers in the early first millennium CE.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The [Karla Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karla_Caves) in [Karli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karli,_India), near [Lonavala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonavala), are near the [Western Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ghats) and a major ancient [trade route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_route) running eastward from the [Arabian Sea](2747.md) to the Deccan Plateau. The caves are a complex of ancient Indian [Buddhist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buddhist) [rock-cut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_rock-cut_architecture) shrines which were developed from the second century BCE to the fifth century CE; the oldest of the shrines is believed to date to 160 BCE. Traders and [Satavahana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satavahana_dynasty) rulers financed construction of the caves.[^cite_note-public-9] Buddhists, identified with commerce and manufacturing through their early association with traders, tended to locate their monasteries in natural formations near major trade routes to provide lodging for travelling traders.[^cite_note-keay-10] Inscriptions at Karla and Junnar suggest that in the early part of the [Common Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_Era), the area was controlled by the [Shaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saka) ruler [Nahapana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nahapana).[^cite_note-11] Coins found further east in the district, near [Indapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indapur), suggest that the region may have been controlled by the [Traikutaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traikutaka_dynasty) king Dahragana in 465 CE; silver coins found at [Junnar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Junnar) suggest that the region may also have been ruled by [Satavahana kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satavahana_dynasty).[^cite_note-12]

The first reference to the Pune region is found on two copper plates, dated to 758 and 768 CE and issued by the [Rashtrakuta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashtrakuta) ruler Krishna I. The plates call the region "Puny [Vishaya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishaya)" and "Punaka Vishaya", respectively. The [Pataleshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pataleshwar) rock-cut temple complex was built during this time, and the area included [Theur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theur), [Uruli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruli_Kanchan), Chorachi Alandi, and [Bhosari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhosari).[^cite_note-Kantak489-13] The region became part of the [Yadava Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yadava_Dynasty) of [Deogiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daulatabad,_Maharashtra) from the ninth to the 13th centuries.

The Muslim [Khalji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalji_dynasty) rulers of the [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate) overthrew the Yadavas in 1317, beginning three hundred years of Islamic control. The Khalji were followed by another sultanate dynasty, the [Tughlaqs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tughlaq_dynasty). A Tughlaq governor on the Deccan Plateau rebelled and created the [Bahamani Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamani_Sultanate), which later dissolved into the [Deccan sultanates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_sultanates). During the 1400s, Russian traveler [Afanasy Nikitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afanasy_Nikitin) spent many months in Junnar during the monsoon season and vividly describes life in the region under [Bahamani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahamani) rule.[^cite_note-14] The fort at Chakan played an important role in the history of the Deccan sultanates.[^cite_note-15] The Bahamani Sultanate broke up in the early 16th century; the Nizamshahi kingdom controlled the region for most of the century, with Junnar its first capital.[^cite_note-gazett1490-16] During the early 1600s, the [Nizam Shahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizam_Shahi) general [Malik Ambar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Ambar) moved his capital there.[^cite_note-17]

### Deccan sultanates and the Bhosale jagir

The district became politically important when the Nizamshahi capital was moved to Junnar at the beginning of the 16th century. The [Bhosale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhonsle) family received a [jagir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagir) (land grant), and control of the region shifted among the Bhosale rulers, the sultanates and the Mughals during the century. The district was central to the founding of the [Maratha Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) by [Shivaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivaji).

#### Nizamshahi

With the establishment of [Nizamshahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadnagar_Sultanate) rule, with [Ahmednagar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmednagar) its headquarters, nearly all of the region was controlled by the Nizamshahi. It was formed into a district (or *sarkar*), with sub-divisions (*paragana*) and smaller ranges (*prant* or *desh*). Revenue collection was delegated to important chieftains of the Nizamshahi.

At Ahmednagar, the Sultan bore the brunt of a heavy attack from [Mughal armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) who converged on the capital in 1595. To rally the strongest possible local support against the Mughal invaders, and stabilise the territories ruled by Ahmednagar, local Maratha chieftains were given increased power. Amongst the chieftains was [Maloji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maloji_Bhosale), who was made a [raja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raja) in 1595; the districts of Pune and [Supa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supa,_Parner) were given to him as a [jagir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagir) ([fief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fief)). Maloji was also given charge of the forts at [Shivneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivneri) and [Chakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakan,_Maharashtra), which played an important role in the district's early political history.[^cite_note-Joseph1900-18]

In 1600, Ahmednagar was captured by the Mughals. Nizamshahi minister [Malik Ambar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malik_Ambar) raised [Murtaza Nizam Shah II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murtaza_Nizam_Shah_II) to the throne, with its temporary headquarters at Junnar.[^cite_note-gazett1490-16] For nearly a generation, Ambar guided the Nizamshahi kingdom and the Pune region benefited from his leadership. By his death in 1626, the region's revenue system was sound and fair.

#### Bhosale jagir under the Adilshahi

The Pune region was administered as a jagir during much of the 17th century by Maloji Bhosale, his son [Shahaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahaji) and his grandson [Shivaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivaji). Its nominal sovereignty changed with shifting allegiances of the Bhosale family. In 1632, Shahaji forsook the Mughals and accepted the friendship of the [Adilshahi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adil_Shahi_dynasty) rulers of [Bijapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bijapur) (the traditional rivals of [Ahmadnagar Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmadnagar_Sultanate)).

After the fall of the Ahmadnagar (Nizamshahi) Sultanate, its territory was divided between the Adilshahi and the Mughals with Pune region going to the former. Shahaji refused to surrender Junnar (the seat of the Nizamshahi dynasty) before he finally capitulated. However, Shahaji was apparently considered important enough by the Adilshah to play a key role in the new regime's administration. His jagir was confirmed, continuing the region's connection with the Bhosale family.[^cite_note-Eaton2005-19]

#### Pune district under Shivaji and the Mughals

Shahaji second son, Shivaji (founder of the Maratha Empire), was born on the hill fort of [Shivneri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shivneri) near Junnar on 19 February 1630.[^cite_note-SibPada-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-sen2-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESarkar,_Shivaji_and_His_Times192019-24] His mother was [Jijabai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jijabai), the daughter of [Lakhuji Jadhavrao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakhuji_Jadhavrao) of [Sindhkhed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sindhkhed) (a Mughal-allied *sardar* claiming descent from the [Yadavas of Devagiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seuna_\(Yadava\)_dynasty).[^cite_note-Metha2004-25][^cite_note-Menon2011-26]

The Pune Jagir which was restored to Shahaji after he joined the Adilshahi service in 1637.Since he was required to be based in [Bengaluru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore) as the [Adilshah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adilshah) commander, Shahaji selected Pune as the residence for \[Jijabai and son Shivaji, and appointed [Dadoji Konddeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dadoji_Konddeo) as administrator of the Pune jagir. Konddeo oversaw the construction of their Lal Mahal palace. Konddeo established complete control over the [Maval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maval) region, winning over (or subduing) most of the local Maval leaders.[^cite_note-Sarkar1919-27] He rebuilt the settlement of Pune, and prominent families who had left the town during its 1631 destruction by the Adilshahi general Murar Jaggdeo returned.[^cite_note-A-28] Among Kondadeo's reported reforms was a tax of one-fourth the cash equivalent of a land's yield, and the [Fasli calendar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fasli_calendar) was introduced at this time. He is said to have focused on the western Pune region, and has been credited with overseeing Shivaji's education and training.[^cite_note-Duff-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESarkar,_Shivaji_and_His_Times192020–25-31] Kondadeo died in 1647, and Shivaji became his father's deputy.

Many of Shivaji's comrades (and, later, a number of his soldiers) came from the [Maval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maval) region in the district's western mountains,  including [Yesaji Kank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yesaji_Kank?action=edit\&redlink=1), Suryaji Kakade, Baji Pasalkar, [Baji Prabhu Deshpande](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baji_Prabhu_Deshpande) and [Tanaji Malusare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanaji_Malusare).[^cite_note-32] Shivaji traveled the hills and forests of the [Sahyadri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahyadri) range with his Maval friends, acquiring skills and familiarity with the land which would be useful in his military career.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEdwardes_&_Garrett,_Mughal_Rule_in_India1995128-33][^cite_note-FOOTNOTESarkar,_History_of_Aurangzib192022–24-34] Around 1645, the teenaged Shivaji first expressed his concept of [Hindavi Swarajya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindavi_Swarajya) (Indian self-rule) in a letter.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-40] According to legend, he took an oath to that effect at the temple of [Raireshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raireshwar) near Bhor in the district.[^cite_note-Kapadia2004-41]

Shivaji began his rule in 1648 of the Pune region, taking possession of the key [Torna Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torna_Fort) and controlling the [Chakan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakan,_Maharashtra) and [Purandar forts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purandar_fort) and raiding Junnar. He moved his administration to the renovated [Rajgad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajgad) fort in 1648, and kept it there until his coronation in 1674.[^cite_note-Gordon59-42]

During the 1660s, the Mughals under [Aurangzeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurangzeb) began paying attention to Shivaji. Pune and the region's forts frequently changed hands between the Mughals and Shivaji.[^cite_note-43] In the [Treaty of Purandar (1665)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Purandar_\(1665\)), signed by the Mughal general Mirza Jaisingh and Shivaji, Shivaji ceded control of a number of forts in the district to the Mughals.[^cite_note-Gordon73-44] Shivaji recaptured many of these forts when the truce ended.

He was succeeded on the Marathi throne by his eldest son, [Sambhaji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sambhaji), in 1680. Shortly afterwards, the Mughal army under Aurangzeb moved into the Deccan Plateau and remained there for nearly three decades. Sambhaji was captured and executed, at Aurangzeb's order, in the village of Tulapur at the confluence of the [Bhima river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhima_river) and the [Indrayani Rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indrayani_River).[^cite_note-Gokhale1978-45][^cite_note-Organiser-46][^cite_note-47]

The period following his 1689 death was one of political ferment in the Deccan Plateau, and the Pune region experienced major fluctuations in administrative authority. Shivaji's younger son, [Rajaram I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajaram_I), ruled after his brother's death. He spent most of his time in [Gingee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gingee), fighting the Mughal siege. Before the Mughals captured Gingee, Rajaram returned to Maharashtra and died in [Sinhagad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinhagad) in 1700. Ambikabai,[^cite_note-48] one of his widows, committed [sati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sati_\(practice\)) at Rajaram's death.[^cite_note-49] The [Bhimthadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimthadi_horse) (or Deccani) horse was developed in the region under Maratha rule by crossing Arabian and Turkic breeds with local ponies.[^cite_note-CABI-50][^cite_note-51]

### Peshwa rule (1714–1818)

Shivaji's grandson, [Shahu I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahu_I), appointed the [Chitpavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitpavan) Brahmin [Balaji Vishwanath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaji_Vishwanath) as his [Peshwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwa) in 1714. Vishwanath received the area around Pune from the grateful mother of one of Shahu's ministers for saving her son's life.[^cite_note-52] In 1718, Shahu sent him to Delhi to assist the Sayyads; in return for this help, Muhammad Shah (the Mughal emperor in Delhi) granted Shahu [sardeshmukhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chauth) rights for Pune, Supa, Baramati, Indapur and Junnar. Shahu appointed [Baji Rao I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baji_Rao_I) Peshwa in 1720, succeeding his father.[^cite_note-53] Baji Rao moved his administration from [Saswad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saswad) to nearby Pune in 1728, laying the foundation for turning a [kasbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasbah) into a large city.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] Pune grew in size and influence as Maratha rule extended through the subcontinent in subsequent decades. A well-known saying in the era before the third battle of Panipat was that the "ponies of [Bhimthadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhimthadi)[^cite_note-CABI-50] drank the water of the [Indus river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_River)".

#### Pune under the Peshwas

Pune gained more influence under the rule of Baji Rao I's son, [Balaji Baji Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balaji_Baji_Rao) (Nanasaheb). Maratha influence waned after the disastrous 1761 [Battle of Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Panipat_\(1761\)), and the [Nizam of Hyderabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizam_of_Hyderabad) looted the city. It (and the empire) recovered during the brief reign of Peshwa Madhavrao. The rest of the Peshwa era was rife with family intrigue and political machinations. The leading role was played by the ambitious [Raghunathrao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raghunathrao), the younger brother of Nanasaheb, who wanted power at the expense of his nephews [Madhavrao I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhavrao_I) and [Narayanrao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayan_Rao). After Narayanrao's 1775 murder by order of Raghunathrao's wife, power was exercised in the name of his son [Madhavrao II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhavrao_II) by a [regency council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent) led by [Nana Fadnavis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nana_Fadnavis) for most of the century.[^cite_note-56] Under Peshwa rule, the urban elite came from the Chitpavan Brahmin community; they were the military commanders, the bureaucrats and the bankers, and had ties to each other by marriage.[^cite_note-57]

Nanasaheb built a lake in [Katraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katraj), on the city's outskirts, and a still-operational underground aqueduct to bring water from the lake to Shaniwar Wada.[^cite_note-58] The city received an underground sewage system in 1782 which discharged into the river.[^cite_note-A-28][^cite_note-59] Pune prospered during Nanasaheb's reign. On the southern fringe of the city, he built a palace on the [Parvati Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parvati_Hill), developed a garden known as Heera Baug, and dug [a lake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saras_Baug) near the hill with a [Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha) temple on an island in its centre which is called [Sarasbaug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saras_Baug). Nanasaheb also developed new commercial, trading, and residential localities: [Sadashiv Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadashiv_Peth,_Pune), [Narayan Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayan_Peth,_Pune), [Rasta Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasta_Peth,_Pune) and [Nana Peth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nana_Peth,_Pune). During the 1790s, the city had a population of 600,000. In 1781, after a city census, a household tax (*gharpatti*) was levied on the more affluent: one-fifth to one-sixth of the property value.[^cite_note-60]

Order in Peshwa Pune was maintained by the [kotwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kotwal), who was a police chief, magistrate and [municipal commissioner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_commissioner#India) and whose duties included investigating, levying and collecting fines for offences. The kotwal was assisted by police officers from the *chavdi* (police station), and clerks collected fines and paid informants who provided intelligence. Crimes included illicit affairs, violence and murder; in the case of murder, sometimes only a fine was imposed. Inter-caste or inter-religious affairs were also resolved with fines.[^cite_note-61] Although the kotwal's salary was as high as 9,000 rupees per month, it included officer salaries (mainly from the [Ramoshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramoshi) caste).[^cite_note-62] The best-known kotwal in Pune during Peshwa rule was [Ghashiram Kotwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghashiram_Kotwal), and the city's police force was admired by European visitors.[^cite_note-63]

The patronage of the Brahmin Peshwas resulted in Pune's expansion, with the construction of about 250 temples and bridges (including the Lakdi Pul and the temples on [Parvati Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parvati_Hill)).[^cite_note-64] Many temples like [Maruti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanuman), [Vithoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vithoba), [Vishnu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vishnu), [Mahadeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shiva), [Rama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rama), Krishna and [Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha) temples were built during this era. Their patronage extended to 164 schools (*pathshalas*) in the city which taught Hindu holy texts ([shastras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shastra)) to [Brahmin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahmin) men.[^cite_note-65]

Pune also had many public festivals. Major festivals were [Ganeshotsav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesh_Chaturthi), the Deccan New Year ([Gudi Padwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gudi_Padwa)), [Holi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holi), and [Dasara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navratri#Maharashtra). Holi at the Peshwa court was celebrated over a five-day period. The Dakshina festival, celebrated in the Hindu month of [Shravan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shraavana) (when millions of rupees were distributed), attracted Brahmins from throughout India to Pune.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67] The festivals, the building of temples and temple rituals led to religion being responsible for about 15 percent of the city's economy during this period.[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70]

Peshwas and knights residing in the city had individual hobbies and interests; [Madhavrao II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madhavrao_II) had a private collection of exotic animals, such as lions and rhinoceros, near the [Peshwe Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peshwe_Park) zoo.[^cite_note-71] The last Peshwa, [Baji Rao II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baji_Rao_II), was a strength and wrestling enthusiast. The sport of pole gymnastics ([mallakhamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mallakhamba)) was developed in Pune under his patronage by Balambhat Deodhar.[^cite_note-72] Many Peshwas and their courtiers were patrons of [lavani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavani) and Maharashtrian dance, and a number of composers (such as Ram Joshi, Anant Phandi, Prabhakar and [Honaji Bala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honaji_Bala)) flourished during this period. The dancers primarily came from the [Mang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mang_\(caste\)) and [Mahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahar) castes.[^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74] Lavani used to be essential part of Holi celebrations in the region's Peshwa courts.[^cite_note-75]

Peshwa influence in India declined after the defeat of Maratha forces in the 1761 [Battle of Panipat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Panipat_\(1761\)), but Pune remained the seat of power. However, the city's fortunes declined rapidly after the 1795 accession of [Baji Rao II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baji_Rao_II). Pune was captured by [Yashwantrao Holkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yashwantrao_Holkar) in the 1802 [Battle of Pune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poona), precipitating the [Second Anglo-Maratha War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Maratha_War) of 1803–1805. Peshwa rule ended with the defeat of Baji Rao II by the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company), under the leadership of [Mountstuart Elphinstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountstuart_Elphinstone), in 1818.

### British rule and independence

1896 map of the district

Poona (Pune) district in 1896

Long dam

When the [Khadakwasla Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadakwasla_Dam) was built in 1878, it was considered one of the world's largest dams.



Photograph of the railway reversing station at Bhor Ghat from 1880s

In 1818, the Pune region and the rest of the Peshwa territories came under the control of the British [East India Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_India_Company).One of the first tasks that the company did was to destroy Hill forts previously under Maratha control. This was to stop Maratha forces regrouping in the hills. The forts destroyed in Pune district included those in the Junnar region such Shivaji's birthplace of Shivneri, [Hadsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hadsar), [Narayangad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narayangad), [Chavand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chavand,_Maharashtra), and the important for of Sinhagad overlooking the city of Pune.[^cite_note-76] The governor of the new territories, [Mountstuart Elphinstone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountstuart_Elphinstone), appointed a commissioner and left the district's boundaries almost intact. Elphinstone and other British officers enjoyed [Saswad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saswad) and the fertile valley around it.[^cite_note-77]

The annual [Pandharpur Wari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandharpur_Wari) starts in two places in the district, namely Alandi and Dehu. In its present form the wari dates back to 1820s. At that time, Sant Tukaram's descendants, and a devotee of Sant [Dnyaneshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnyaneshwar) named Haibatravbaba Arphalkar, who was a courtier of [Scindias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scindia), the Maratha rulers of [Gwalior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gwalior) made changes to wari.[^cite_note-78][^cite_note-79] Haibatravbaba's changes involved carrying the paduka in a palkhi, having horses involved in the procession, and organizing the devotees or varkaris in Dindis (Dindi stands for a specific group of varkaris.).[^cite_note-80]

Company rule came to an end when, under the terms of [a proclamation issued by Queen Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_India_Act_1858), the [Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency), Pune and the rest of British India came under the British crown in 1858.[^cite_note-victoriap221-81]

Villages in the district saw rioting in 1875 by peasants protesting Marwari and Gujarati moneylenders. The disturbances involved peasants getting the moneylenders to burn their documents and, in some cases, torching their houses. The riots were responsible for the Bombay presidency government enacting the Deccan Agriculturists Relief Act to protect peasants from [land grabbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_grabbing) by money lenders.[^cite_note-82][^cite_note-Bandyo2004-83]

During the first and second Anglo-Maratha wars, it took four or five weeks to move materials from Mumbai to Pune. An 1804 military road constructed by the British East India Company reduced the journey to four or five days. The company built a [macadam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macadam) road between the two cities in 1830 which permitted mail-cart service.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

A rail line from Bombay, operated by the [Great Indian Peninsula Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Indian_Peninsula_Railway) (GIPR), reached Pune in 1858.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] In the following decades, the line was extended east and south of the city. The GIPR extended its line east to [Raichur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raichur) in 1871, where it met the [Madras Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madras_Railway) and connected the city to [Madras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chennai).[^cite_note-EarlyII-88] The [metre-gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metre_gauge_railway) Pune-[Miraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraj) line was completed in 1886, making the city a [rail junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Junction_railway_station). The Bombay-Poona line was electrified in the 1920s; this cut travel time between the cities to three hours, enabling day trips for events such as the [Poona races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Race_Course).[^cite_note-89] Many villages in the west, east and south of the district, such as [Lonavla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonavla), [Uruli Kanchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruli_Kanchan) and [Daund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daund), were connected by rail.

Pune's first bus service began in 1941 with the Silver Bus Company, and [Tanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanga_\(carriage\)) (horse-drawn carriage) drivers went on strike in protest.[^cite_note-90] Tangas were a common mode of public transport well into the 1950s, and bicycles were a private vehicle choice in the 1930s.[^cite_note-91]

The British installed a [telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_telegraph) system in Pune in 1858.[^cite_note-92] According to the 1885 *Gazetteer of the Bombay Presidency: Poona*, the city and the GIPR had telegraph offices. In 1928, a relay station was built in [Khadki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadki) to relay telegraph signals for the [Imperial Wireless Chain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Wireless_Chain). In 1885, Pune was a post-distribution hub for the district. There were two post offices in the city, which also offered money-order and savings-bank services.[^cite_note-93]

Areas east of Pune receive less rainfall than areas west of the city adjacent to the [Western Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ghats). To minimize drought risk, a masonry dam was built on the [Mutha River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutha_River) at [Khadakwasla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadakwasla) in 1878. At the time, the dam was considered one of the world's largest. Two canals were dug on each riverbank to irrigate land east of the city and supply drinking water to the city and its [cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Camp).[^cite_note-94] In 1890 Poona Municipality spent Rs. 200,000 to install water filtration works.[^cite_note-95]

In the early part 20th century, hydroelectric plants were installed in the Western Ghats between Pune and Mumbai. The Poona electric-supply company, a [Tata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tata_Group) company, received power from the [Khopoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khopoli) (on the Mumbai side of the Ghats) and [Bhivpuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhivpuri) plants near the [Mulshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulshi) dam.[^cite_note-96] Power was used to electrify trains running between Mumbai and Pune and for industrial and residential use, and a dam was built on the Velvandi River in [Bhor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhor).[^cite_note-97][^cite_note-mywebPune-98]

## Geography and climate

Rock formations with holes

[Potholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothole_\(landform\)) in the [Kukdi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kukadi_River) near [Nighoj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nighoj)

See caption

Confluence of the Indrayani and Bhima Rivers at [Tulapur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulapur)

The district is surrounded by [Thane district](638817.md) on the northwest, [Raigad district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raigad_district) on the west, [Satara district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satara_district) on the south, [Solapur district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solapur_district) on the southeast, and [Ahmednagar district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmednagar_district) on the north and northeast. On the leeward side of the [Western Ghats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Ghats), it extends to the [Deccan Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deccan_Plateau) on the east. Pune is at an altitude of 559 metres (1,863 feet). The district is located between 17.5° and 19.2° north latitude and 73.2° and 75.1° east longitude.

The [Bhima River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhima_River), the [Krishna River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Krishna_River)'s main tributary, rises in the Western Ghats and flows east. All the district's rivers (the Pushpavati, Krushnavati, [Kukadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kukadi_River), Meena, [Ghod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghod_River), Bhama, Andhra, [Indrayani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indrayani_River), [Pavna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavana_River), [Mula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mula_River_\(India\)), [Mutha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutha_River), Ambi, Mose, Shivganga, Kanandi, Gunjavni, Velvandi, [Nira](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nira_River), [Karha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karha_River) and Velu) flow into the Bhima or its tributaries. Major dams are on the [Kukadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yedgaon_Dam), Pushpavati, [Ghod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manikdoh_Dam), [Bhima](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjani_Dam), Pavna, [Bhama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhama_Asakhed_Dam), [Mula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulshi_Dam), Mutha (the [Temghar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temghar_Dam) and [Khadakwasla Dams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadakwasla_Dam)) and [Mose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varasgaon_Dam).[^cite_note-99]

Nine of the district's fifteen [talukas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talukas_in_Pune_district) are identified as drought-prone, covering a total area of 1,562,000 hectares (6,030 sq mi) and a cropped area of 1,095,000 hectares (4,230 sq mi). Of the cropped area, only 116,000 hectares (450 sq mi) are irrigated—nearly half by wells and tanks, and 40 percent by government canals. The district had a population of 4.2 million in 1991, of which 52 percent was rural. There were 1,530 villages in the district.[^cite_note-100] Its average rainfall is 600 to 700 millimetres (24 to 28 in), most of which falls during the monsoon months (July to October). The area adjacent to the Western Ghats gets more rain than areas further east. The [Daund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daund_taluka) and [Indapur talukas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indapur_taluka) experience more-frequent droughts than Maval, on the district's western edge. Temperatures are moderate and rainfall is unpredictable, in tune with the Indian monsoon. Summers, from early March to July, are dry and hot. Temperatures range from 20 to 38 °C (68 to 100 °F), and may reach 40 °C (104 °F). Winter runs from November to February. Temperatures usually hover around 7 to 12 °C (45 to 54 °F), sometimes dipping to 3 °C (37 °F). June is the driest month, and the agricultural sector is considered vulnerable until 20 September.

Pune district had a [population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_India) of 9,429,408 in the [2011 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Census_of_India),[^cite_note-census-102] roughly equal to the nation of [Benin](3459.md).[^cite_note-cia-103] The fourth most populous of India's 640 districts,[^cite_note-census-102] it has a population density of 603 inhabitants per square kilometre (1,560/sq mi).[^cite_note-census-102] The district's [population-growth rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_planning_in_India) between 2001 and 2011 was 30.34 percent.[^cite_note-census-102] Pune has a [sex ratio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sex_ratio) of 910 [females](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women_in_India) to every 1,000 males,[^cite_note-census-102] and a [literacy rate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Literacy_in_India) of 87.19 percent.[^cite_note-census-102] By age, 685,022 were age four or younger; 1,491,352 were between ages five and 15; 4,466,901 were 15 to 59, and 589,280 were 60 years of age or older. The district has two [municipal corporations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Municipal_corporations_in_India) in the city of [Pune](164634.md) namely [Pune Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Municipal_Corporation) (PMC) and [Pimpri Chinchwad Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pimpri-Chinchwad_Municipal_Corporation) (PCMC). There are thirteen [panchayat samitis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panchayat_samiti_\(block\)). The district has 1,866 villages and 21 [Vidhan Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vidhan_Sabha) constituencies: Junnar, Ambegaon, Khed-Alandi, Maval, Mulshi, Haveli, Bopodi, Shivajinagar, Parvati (SC), Kasba Peth, Bhvani Peth, [Camp Cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Cantonment), Shirur, Daund, Indapur, Baramati, Purandhar and Bhor. Its four [Lok Sabha](275456.md) constituencies are Pune, Baramati, Shirur and Maval (shared with Raigad district).

## District court

Pune District Court administers justice at the district level, and is the principal court of original jurisdiction in civil matters. The district court is also a [Sessions Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sessions_Court) for criminal matters. It is presided over by a Principal District and Sessions Judge appointed by the [state government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Maharashtra).

Court decisions are subject to the appellate jurisdiction of [Bombay High Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_High_Court). Pune District Court is under the High Court's administrative control.

### Primary and secondary education

State primary schools in the cities and district are run by the city corporation and [Zilla Parishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/District_Councils_of_India), respectively; private schools are operated by charitable trusts. ## Economy

Although the district is an industrial center, its economy also has a significant agricultural component. Pune is also considered an educational hub of the state of Maharashtra with students coming from all over India to attend the numerous colleges and institutes.

### Agriculture

Although the region around Pune is industrialized, agriculture continues to be important elsewhere in the district. Since most arable land is still rain-fed, the [southwest monsoon](57630.md#Southwest_monsoon) season (between June and September) is crucial to the district's food sufficiency and quality of life. Fluctuations in time, distribution or quantity of monsoon rains may lead to floods or droughts. The eastern part of the district has been historically drought-prone, but [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) provided by dams, canals and wells have made agriculture less dependent on rainfall.[^cite_note-119] The over-pumping of aquifers has led to increased water salinity in the talukas of Purandhar, Baramati, Daund, Indapur and Shirur (in the eastern part of the district), threatening agriculture and the drinking-water supply.[^cite_note-120]

Monsoon crops include [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [jwari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorghum_bicolor) and [bajri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_millet). Other crops include [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [pulses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulse), vegetables and [onions](51258.md). [Ambemohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambemohar), a mango-scented rice grown in Bhor taluka and areas near the Western Ghats, is popular throughout Maharashtra. Since it has a low yield, many farmers in the region grow the crossbred Indrayani rice instead.[^cite_note-121]

Six outdoor barrels against a background of vineyards and a hill

Barrels at the Narayangaon Winery

Major cash crops include [sugarcane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugarcane) and [oil seeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_seed), including [groundnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut) and [sunflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunflower). The district has significant fruit orchards, particularly [mango](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mango), [grape](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape) and [orange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_\(fruit\)). A winery in Narayangaon produces [sparkling wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sparkling_wine) from locally-grown [Thompson seedless grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultana_\(grape\)).[^cite_note-122] Most growers of cash crops (including cotton) in the district belong to [agricultural cooperatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agricultural_cooperative) and [sugar](27712.md) is produced in mills owned by local cooperative societies whose members of supply sugarcane to the mills.[^cite_note-123] During the last fifty years, the local sugar mills have played an important role in encouraging political participation and have been a stepping stone for aspiring politicians.[^cite_note-124]

### Public transport

Bus service by private companies was introduced in Pune shortly before the independence. The city took over the service after the independence in 1947 as Poona Municipal Transport (PMT) which later became Pune Municipal Transport. During the 1990s, PMT and Pimpri-Chinchwad Municipal Transport (PCMT, the bus-service provider in Pimpri-Chinchwad) had a combined fleet of over 1,000 buses. Because municipal transport coverage was patchy, a number of employers in the industrial belt near Pimpri-Chinchwad and Hadapsar offered bus service to their employees.[^cite_note-silly-125] The companies used many more private buses than the municipal providers used.[^cite_note-silly-125] The [Pune Municipal Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Municipal_Corporation) began a [bus rapid transit system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainbow_Bus_Rapid_Transit_System#History) (India's first) in 2006, but it encountered a number of difficulties. The two municipal bus companies merged in 2007 to form [Pune Mahanagar Parivahan Mahamandal Limited](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Mahanagar_Parivahan_Mahamandal_Limited) (PMPML). The [Commonwealth Youth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Youth_Games) were held the following year, which encouraged additional development in north-western Pune and added a fleet of buses running on [compressed natural gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compressed_natural_gas) (CNG) to the city's streets. [Maharashtra State Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_State_Transport) buses began operating in 1951 throughout the state.

During the 1960s, motorized three-wheeled [auto rickshaws](593765.md) began replacing horse-drawn [tangas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanga_\(carriage\)) in the district's urban areas; their number grew from 200 in 1960 to over 20,000 in 1996. Although Pune was known as the bicycle city of India in the 1930s, motorcycles began replacing bicycles in the 1970s; the number of motorcycles increased from five per 1,000 people in 1965 to 118 per 1,000 in 1995.[^cite_note-silly-125]

### Air

[Pune Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Airport) ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA): PNQ) is a [civil enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civil_enclave) at Lohegaon Air Base, northeast of the city, with service to a number of domestic and international destinations. Since Pune's air traffic is controlled by the [Indian Air Force](403355.md) (IAF),[^cite_note-126] there is occasional conflict between the [Airports Authority of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airports_Authority_of_India) and the IAF over flight schedules or night landings. The [airport apron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airport_apron) is becoming inadequate to handle the growing number of flights into Pune since the airport's upgrade to international status with flights to Dubai, Singapore and Frankfurt.[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] Pune Airport handled about 165 passengers a day in 2004–05, increasing to 250 passengers a day in 2005–06. There was a sharp rise in 2006–2007, when the number of daily passengers reached 4,309. In 2010–2011, the number of passengers was about 8,000 a day.[^cite_note-IE-129]

The [government of Maharashtra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Maharashtra) has entrusted responsibility to [Maharashtra Airport Development Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Airport_Development_Company) (MADC) for the [greenfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenfield_project) [Chhatrapati Sambhaji Raje International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chhatrapati_Sambhaji_Raje_International_Airport) project,[^cite_note-130] in the Purandar area. [Baramati Airport](30640828.md), 12 kilometres (7.5 mi) from [Baramati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baramati) and 100 kilometres (62 mi) from Pune and used for pilot training and charter flights, was being planned in 2011 as a private-jet hub by [Reliance Infrastructure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reliance_Infrastructure).[^cite_note-131]

### Rail

White-and-yellow electric train at a station

Lonavla [EMU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_multiple_unit) at Pune's platform 6



Bhima river and bridge near Daund

The district's two major rail junctions are Pune Junction and Daund Junction. All rail lines through Pune are [broad gauge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broad-gauge_railway) double track, and are part of [Indian Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Railways)' [Central Railway zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Railway_zone). The Pune–Mumbai line, the district's most important rail route, was built during the [British Raj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Raj). Khandala and Lonavala are on this route, which has a number of daily high-speed trains. The Mumbai–Kolhapur line also passes through the district, and other major Indian cities are connected to Pune by rail.

The district's rail lines are:

- Pune–[Kalyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalyan) (towards Mumbai)

- Pune–Daund

- Daund–[Kurduvadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurduvadi)

- Daund–[Manmad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmad)

- Daund–Baramati branch line (single-track)

- Pune–[Miraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miraj) (single-track from Pune to Miraj, towards [Bangalore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangalore))

Although express trains on these routes skip many smaller stations, local "passenger trains" stop at each station.

#### Suburban Rail

A suburban rail system, operated by Central Railway, connects Pune to its suburbs and neighboring villages west of the city. The system has two routes: from [Pune Junction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pune_Junction_railway_station) to [Lonavala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonavala_railway_station) and to [Talegaon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talegaon_railway_station). Five trains operate on the Pune Junction-Talegaon route, and eighteen trains operate on the Pune Junction-Lonavla route.[^cite_note-132] Eight passenger trains run between Pune Junction and [Daund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daund) as suburban trains, making the Pune-Daund section a third suburban route. Major stations on this route are at [Loni Kalbhor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loni_Kalbhor) and [Urali Kanchan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urali_Kanchan).

## Healthcare



A Primary Healthcare center in the village of Amboli in Pune district

Healthcare in the district is provided by private and public facilities. Primary care is provided by practitioners of [conventional medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allopathic_medicine) as well as traditional and alternative medicine (i.e. *[Ayurved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayurved)*, [Homeopathy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeopathy) and [Unani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unani)). For minor and chronic ailments, people in the district often rely on practitioners of traditional medicine.[^cite_note-133] Thedistrict is served by three government hospitals: [Sassoon Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassoon_Hospital), Budhrani and Dr Ambedkar Hospital. There are also a number of private hospitals in the city of Pune such as Ranka Hospital, [Sahyadri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahyadri_Hospital), Jahangir Nursing Home, Sancheti Hospital, [Aditya Birla Memorial Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aditya_Birla_Memorial_Hospital), [KEM Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somwar_Peth#KEM_Hospital), [Ruby Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruby_Hall_Clinic), Naidu Hospital[^cite_note-134] and Smile Inn Dental Clinic Pune.[^cite_note-135]

## Tourism

Khandoba temple at Jejuri, a kuldaiwat for a large percentage of Marathi Hindu families

Large building, with steps down to a river

Alandi, on the Indrayani River



Bhimashankar temple is considered one of the twelve [Jyotirlinga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyotirlinga)

Pune district has been at the center of Maharashtraian and Marathi history for more than four hundred years, beginning with the Deccan sultanates and followed by the Maratha Empire. The district has a number of mountain forts and buildings from these eras, in addition to shrines revered by Marathi Hindus (including five of the eight [Ashtavinayaka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashtavinayaka) [Ganesha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ganesha) temples). [Samadhis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samadhi_\(shrine\)) of the two most revered Marathi Bhakti saints ([Dnyaneshwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnyaneshwar) and [Tukaram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tukaram)) are in [Alandi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alandi) and Dehu, respectively. The main temple of [Khandoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khandoba), the family deity for most Marathi Hindus, is in [Jejuri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jejuri).[^cite_note-136]

The British designated Pune as the monsoon capital of the [Bombay Presidency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombay_Presidency), and many buildings and parks from the era remain. [Hill stations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hill_stations_in_India) such as [Lonavla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lonavla) and [Khandala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khandala) also date back to the Raj, and remain popular with residents of Pune and Mumbai for holidays.[^cite_note-137]

The district's mountains, forests and reservoirs are popular for hiking and birdwatching. [Bhigwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhigwan), a catchment area of the [Ujjani Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ujjani_Dam), is about  from Pune on [NH-65](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highway_65_\(India\)), the Pune–Solapur highway. An area of about 18,000 hectares (69 sq mi) has been proposed as a sanctuary for [migratory birds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bird_migration).

## Sports

The [Maharashtra cricket team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_cricket_team) has its home ground in Pune, playing at the new [Maharashtra Cricket Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maharashtra_Cricket_Association) MCA Cricket Stadium in Gahunje. The [I-League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I-League) Pune Football Club plays in the league's First Division, and finished third in the 2009–10 season. [FC Pune City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Pune_City) played in the [Indian Super League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Super_League) since the league's inception in 2014 until they folded in 2019, as did their [women's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Pune_City_\(women\)) and [reserve and academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Pune_City_Reserves_and_Academy) teams.

The 1993 [National Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Games_of_India) were held in Pune, and the new Sports City hosted the [Commonwealth Youth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Youth_Games) in 2008. [Puneri Paltan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puneri_Paltan), one of ten teams in the professional [kabaddi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kabaddi) league, has its home ground in [Balewadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balewadi).
