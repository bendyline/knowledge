The **2026 EHF European Men's Handball Championship**, commonly referred to as the **EHF Euro 2026**, was the 17th edition of the [EHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Handball_Federation) [European Men's Handball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Men's_Handball_Championship), the biennial international men's handball championship of Europe organized by [EHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Handball_Federation). It was co-hosted by Denmark, Norway and Sweden from 15 January to 1 February 2026, marking the second time the event was held in three countries, after [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_European_Men's_Handball_Championship). The final was held in [Herning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herning), Denmark.

24 teams participated for the fourth time. Qualification took place in January 2023 to May 2025. The three co-hosts qualified automatically, alongside the defending champions, [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_men's_national_handball_team). [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_men's_national_handball_team) returns after a 28-year absence.

This tournament acts as a qualifier for the [2027 World Men's Handball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2027_World_Men's_Handball_Championship) in [Germany](11867.md) and [2028](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2028_European_Men's_Handball_Championship) edition in [Portugal](23033.md), [Spain](26667.md) and [Switzerland](26748.md).

[France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_men's_national_handball_team) were the defending champions, having beaten [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_men's_national_handball_team) in the [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Men's_Handball_Championship) final in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne), but they couldn't defend their title after being eliminated in the main round and placed 7th. Co-host [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_men's_national_handball_team) won their third title and first in 14 years after defeating [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_men's_national_handball_team) in the final. [Croatia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Croatia_men's_national_handball_team) captured the bronze medal after defeating [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_men's_national_handball_team) who appeared in the medal race after 16 years. Co-hosts [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_men's_national_handball_team) and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_handball_team) finished 9th and 6th. [Portugal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portugal_men's_national_handball_team) and [Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroe_Islands_men's_national_handball_team) achieved their best results, placing 5th and 13th respectively.

Denmark's [Mathias Gidsel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mathias_Gidsel) won the MVP award, after scoring 68 goals, which is a tournament record at any European Championship.[^cite_note-2]

### Host selection

As only the Scandinavian bid remained it was unanimously selected at the 14th EHF Extraordinary Congress on 20 November 2021.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] This marks the second time the event is held in three countries, after [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_European_Men's_Handball_Championship). This is Denmark's second (after [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_European_Men's_Handball_Championship)), Norway's third (after [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_European_Men's_Handball_Championship) and 2020) and Sweden's third (after [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_European_Men's_Handball_Championship) and 2020) time hosting.

36 teams registered for participation and competed for 20 places at the final tournament. After the qualification round 1, the remaining 32 teams take part in qualification round 2, where each team is drawn into eights groups of four. The top-two placed teams in each group qualified for the final tournament, alongside the four best-ranked third-placed teams, not counting the matches against fourth-placed teams. The draw took place on 21 March 2024 in [Copenhagen](5166.md), Denmark.[^cite_note-Exec-18][^cite_note-19] Round 2 started in November 2025 and ended in May 2026.

Of the 24 qualifiers, 22 return from the previous edition. [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_men's_national_handball_team) qualified for only their second appearance, with their first being back in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_European_Men's_Handball_Championship). [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_men's_national_handball_team) come back having missed out in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Men's_Handball_Championship).

Of the non-qualifiers, [Bosnia and Herzegovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bosnia_and_Herzegovina_men's_national_handball_team) miss out for the first time since [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_European_Men's_Handball_Championship), while 2024 debutants, [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_men's_national_handball_team) also failed to qualify.

The lowest ranked team from the EHF rankings to qualify was [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_men's_national_handball_team), ranked 30th. The highest ranked team to not qualify was [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_men's_national_handball_team), ranked 22nd.

## Venues

A first draft of the venues was proposed in August 2023.[^cite_note-22] The tournament will be played across four venues in four cities: one in Denmark ([Herning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herning), Groups A and B), one in Norway ([Bærum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A6rum), Groups C and D), and two in Sweden ([Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6) and [Kristianstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristianstad), Groups E and F).[^cite_note-23] The main round will take place in [Herning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herning) and [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6), with the former organising the final weekend.[^cite_note-24] The following proposed cities in the bid didn't make the final cut: [Aarhus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aarhus) and [Copenhagen](5166.md) in Denmark, and [Bergen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bergen), [Stavanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stavanger) and [Trondheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trondheim) in Norway. In regards to Norway, [Oslo](22309.md) was chosen over [Trondheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trondheim), after negotiations with [Trondheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trondheim) fell through due to financial reasons.[^cite_note-25]

In September 2024, one team per venue was selected. [Denmark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denmark_men's_national_handball_team) will play in [Herning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herning) (Group B), [Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norway_men's_national_handball_team) in [Bærum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/B%C3%A6rum) (Group C) and [Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweden_men's_national_handball_team) in [Malmö](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malm%C3%B6) (Group E). If they qualify, [Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_men's_national_handball_team) will play in Herning (Group A), the [Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroe_Islands_men's_national_handball_team) in Bærum (Group D) and [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_men's_national_handball_team) in Kristianstad (Group F).[^cite_note-Venues-26] The arena in [Kristianstad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristianstad) is renovated with new seats being put in by June 2025.[^cite_note-27]

### Overview of venues

- The [Jyske Bank Boxen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jyske_Bank_Boxen) in [Herning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herning) is the biggest arena in Denmark, seating 15,000. The venue has hosted several handball championships, including: the [European Men's Handball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Men's_Handball_Championship) in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_European_Men's_Handball_Championship), [European Women's Handball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Women's_Handball_Championship) in [2010](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_European_Women's_Handball_Championship) and [2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_European_Women's_Handball_Championship), [World Men's Handball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHF_World_Men's_Handball_Championship) in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_World_Men's_Handball_Championship) and [2025](62967643.md) and [World Women's Handball Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHF_World_Women's_Handball_Championship) in [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Women's_Handball_Championship) and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Women's_Handball_Championship). It has also held the [IIHF World Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_Hockey_World_Championships) in [2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_IIHF_World_Championship) and [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_IIHF_World_Championship).

The venue has experience in hosting handball tournaments, previously hosting two [World Men's Handball Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IHF_World_Men's_Handball_Championship), in [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_World_Men's_Handball_Championship) and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Men's_Handball_Championship). ## Draw



[Herning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herning) hosted the draw.

Danish international, [Simon Pytlick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Pytlick), French national team player, [Hugo Descat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugo_Descat), Swedish player [Andreas Nilsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Nilsson_\(handballer\)) and former Norwegian left back [Kristian Kjelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kristian_Kjelling) were the guests and assisted with the draw. The draw started with, in order, pots 1, 2, 3 and 4 being drawn, with each team selected then allocated into the first available group alphabetically.

### Tiebreakers

In the group stages (preliminary and main rounds), teams are ranked according to points (2 points for a win, 1 point for a draw, 0 points for a loss). If two or more teams have the same number of points, the ranking will be determined as follows:

During the round matches

1. Superior goal difference from all group matches;

2. Higher number of goals scored in all group matches;

3. Alphabetical order.

After completion of the round matches

1. Highest number of points in matches between the teams directly involved;

2. Superior goal difference in matches between the teams directly involved;

3. Highest number of goals scored in matches between the teams directly involved;

4. Superior goal difference in all matches of the group;

5. Highest number of plus goals in all matches of the group;

If the ranking of one of these teams is determined, the above criteria are consecutively followed until the ranking of all teams is determined. If no ranking can be determined, a decision shall be obtained by EHF through drawing of lots.

The teams ranked fourth in each group after the completion of the preliminary round matches will be ranked 19 to 24, while teams ranked third in each group after the completion of the preliminary round matches will be ranked 13 to 18 according to the number of points won in the preliminary round. Places seven and eight will be attributed to the two teams ranked fourth in the main round groups, places nine and ten to the two teams ranked fifth in the main round groups, places eleven and twelve to the two teams ranked sixth in the main round groups according to the number of points won by the respective teams after completion of the main round matches. Places one to six will be decided by play–off or knock–out.

#### Notable occurrences

- On 16 January, [Slovenia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slovenia_men's_national_handball_team) and [Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montenegro_men's_national_handball_team) broke the record for most goals (combined) in a Euro game. Slovenia won 41–40, bringing the total to 81 goals.

  [^cite_note-auto8-79]

  &#x20;Consequently, the highest number of goals ever scored by the losing team in a match is 40.

- On 17 January, [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/France_men's_national_handball_team)'s 46–26 win over [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukraine_men's_national_handball_team) broke the record for the biggest win at the tournament's history and most goals ever scored by a team in one game.

  [^cite_note-auto1-75]

- On 17 January, the 9,130 spectators for Serbia vs Germany broke the record for most fans at a match with Denmark not involved on the day.

  [^cite_note-auto2-70]

  &#x20;The record was later broken again with 9,526 spectators for Germany vs Spain.

  [^cite_note-auto4-66]

- On 18 January, [Faroe Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faroe_Islands_men's_national_handball_team) secured their first ever win at Euro tournaments by beating Montenegro 37–24.

  [^cite_note-auto6-81]

- On 18 January, [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_men's_national_handball_team) won their fourth consecutive game at Euro tournaments for the first time.

  [^cite_note-127]

- On 20 January, [Switzerland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzerland_men's_national_handball_team) reached the main round for the first time since [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_European_Men's_Handball_Championship).

  [^cite_note-auto7-83]

- On 20 January, [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy_men's_national_handball_team) won their first game in 28 years after beating [Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poland_men's_national_handball_team) 29–28.

  [^cite_note-auto5-95]

- On 24 January, France and Portugal broke the record of most goals (combined) in a game at a Euro, with a 46–38 win for the French equating to 84 goals. This result beats the record that Slovenia's 41–40 win over Montenegro held for eight days.

  [^cite_note-auto9-100]

  &#x20;The French also broke the record of most goals at half time with 28.

## Controversies

### Schedule criticism

Before their semifinal against Germany, Croatian coach, [Dagur Sigurðsson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dagur_Sigur%C3%B0sson) criticised the schedule as his team had to play two games in less than 24 hours and then go on a bus the [Herning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herning), meaning they had no training day before their semifinal, as well as the fact that the Croatian team as the only semifinalists had to stay in [Silkeborg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silkeborg), 40 km away, rather than in Herning.[^cite_note-130] As a response, the [EHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Handball_Federation) acknowledged the criticism and stated that changes will be made in the future.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-132]
