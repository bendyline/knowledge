# Patron saint

For the racehorse, see [Patron Saint (horse)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patron_Saint_\(horse\)).



[Saint Matthew the Apostle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_the_Apostle), depicted by [Nicolas Régnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolas_R%C3%A9gnier), is the patron saint of [Salerno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salerno), Italy, bankers, and tax collectors.

A **patron saint**, **patroness saint**, **patron hallow** or **heavenly protector** is a [saint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint) who in [Catholicism](606848.md), [Anglicanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism), [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy) or [Oriental Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodoxy) is regarded as the heavenly [advocate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advocacy) of a nation, place, craft, activity, class, clan, family, or person.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

The term may be applied to individuals to whom similar roles are ascribed in other [religions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion).

## In Christianity

Saints often become the patrons of places where they were born or had been active. However, there were cases in [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) Europe where a city which grew to prominence obtained for its cathedral the remains or some [relics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relic) of a famous saint who had lived and was buried elsewhere, thus making them the city's patron saint – such a practice conferred considerable prestige on the city concerned. In [Latin America](18524.md) and the [Philippines](23440.md), Spanish and Portuguese explorers often named a location for the saint on whose [feast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_day) or commemoration day they first visited the place, with that saint naturally becoming the area's patron.



A branch of [Saint Honore Cake Shop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Honore_Cake_Shop), a [Hong Kong](13404.md) chain bakery ([Honoratus of Amiens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honoratus_of_Amiens) is the patron saint of bakers and confectioners)

Some occupations have a patron saint associated with them whose acts or miracles in some way recall the profession. For example, when [photography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photography) emerged in the 19th century, [Saint Veronica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Veronica) became its patron saint, owing to how her [veil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veil_of_Veronica) miraculously received the imprint of [Christ's face](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Face_of_Jesus) after she wiped off the blood and sweat.[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5].

The [veneration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veneration) or commemoration and recognition of patron saints or saints in general is found in [Catholicism](606848.md) (including [Eastern Catholicism](214903.md)), [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy), [Oriental Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oriental_Orthodox_Churches), and among some [Lutherans](23371382.md) and [Anglicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglicanism).[^cite_note-Brandsrud2022-6] According to the [Catholic catechism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catechism_of_the_Catholic_Church) a [person's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faithful_\(Catholicism\)) patron saint, having already attained the [beatific vision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatific_vision), is able to intercede with God for their needs.[^cite_note-7]

Apart from Lutheranism and Anglicanism, it is, however, generally discouraged in other [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestantism) branches, such as [Reformed Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calvinism), where the practice is considered a form of [idolatry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idolatry).[^cite_note-8]

### Catholicism

A saint can be assigned as a patron by a venerable tradition, or chosen by election. The saint is considered a special intercessor with God and the proper advocate of a particular locality, occupation, etc., and merits a special form of religious observance. A term in some ways comparable is "titular", which is applicable only to a church or institution.[^cite_note-9]

## In Islam

Although Islam has no codified doctrine of patronage on the part of saints, it has nevertheless been an important part of both [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) and [Shia](26961.md) Islamic traditions that particularly important classical saints have served as the heavenly advocates for specific Muslim [empires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empire), [nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation), [cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City), [towns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Town), and [villages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Village).[^cite_note-Lings-10] [Martin Lings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Lings) wrote: "There is scarcely a region in the empire of Islam which has not a Sufi for its Patron Saint."[^cite_note-Lings-10]<sup>: 119 </sup> As the veneration accorded saints often develops purely organically in Islamic climates, in a manner different from Catholic and Eastern Orthodox [Christianity](5211.md), "patron saints" are often recognized through popular acclaim rather than through official declaration.[^cite_note-Lings-10] Traditionally, it has been understood that the patron saint of a particular place prays for that place's wellbeing and for the health and happiness of all who live therein.[^cite_note-Lings-10]

However, the [Wahhabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wahhabism) and [Salafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salafi_movement) movements have latterly attacked the veneration of saints (as patron or otherwise), which they claim are a form of idolatry or *[shirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shirk_\(Islam\))*.[^cite_note-Lings-10] More mainstream Sunni clerics have critiqued this argument since Wahhabism first emerged in the 18th century.[^cite_note-11]

## In Druze faith





Patron saints of the [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze):

[^cite_note-Fukasawa_2017_310-12]

&#x20;[Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George) (left) and Saint [Elijah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elijah) (right)

[Elijah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elijah) and [Jethro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jethro_\(biblical_figure\)) ([Shuaib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shuaib)) are considered patron saints of the [Druze people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze_people).[^cite_note-Fukasawa_2017_310-12][^cite_note-13] In the [Old Testament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Testament), Jethro was [Moses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moses)' father-in-law, a [Kenite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenite) shepherd and priest of [Midian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Midian).[^cite_note-Harris-14] [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) scholars and the [Druze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Druze) identify Jethro with the prophet Shuaib, also said to come from Midian.[^cite_note-15] Shuaib or Jethro of Midian is considered an ancestor of the Druze who revere him as their spiritual founder and chief prophet.[^cite_note-16]

Druze identify Elijah as "al-[Khidr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khidr)".[^cite_note-Swayd_2015_77-17] Druze believe that the Prophet Elijah came back as [Saint John the Baptist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_the_Baptist),[^cite_note-Swayd_2015_77-17][^cite_note-Bennett_2010_77-18] since they believe in [reincarnation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reincarnation) and the transmigration of the soul, Druze believe that El Khidr and Saint John the Baptist are one and the same; along with [Saint George](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George).[^cite_note-Bennett_2010_77-18]

Due to the [Christian influence on the Druze faith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianity_and_Druze), two [Christian saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_saints) become the Druze's favorite venerated figures: Saint George and Saint Elijah.[^cite_note-Beaurepaire-19] Thus, in all the villages inhabited by Druzes and Christians in central [Mount Lebanon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Lebanon) a Christian church or Druze maqam is dedicated to either one of them.[^cite_note-Beaurepaire-19] According to scholar Ray Jabre Mouawad the Druzes appreciated the two saints for their bravery: [Saint George because he confronted the dragon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_George_and_the_Dragon) and the Prophet Elijah because he competed with and defeated the pagan priests of [Baal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baal).[^cite_note-Beaurepaire-19] In both cases the explanations provided by Christians is that Druzes were attracted to [warrior saints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_saint) that resemble their own militarized society.[^cite_note-Beaurepaire-19]

## In Eastern religions

In [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), certain sects may devote themselves to the veneration of a saint, such as the [Balmiki sect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balmikism) that reveres [Valmiki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valmiki).[^cite_note-Kananaikil1983-20]

[Buddhism](3267529.md) also includes the idea of protector deities, which are called "Dharma protectors" ([Dharmapala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dharmapala)).
