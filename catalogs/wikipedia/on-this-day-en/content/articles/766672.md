**Jhelum District**[^cite_note-3] is a [district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_districts_in_Punjab,_Pakistan) within the [Rawalpindi Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_Division) of [Punjab, Pakistan](24751.md), located on the border of the [Pothohar Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothohar_Plateau) and the [Indus Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab_Plain) of [northern Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_Division). It is one of the oldest districts of Punjab, being established on 23 March 1849.[^cite_note-4] It borders the districts of [Chakwal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakwal_District), and [Rawalpindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_District) to the west; [Khushab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khushab_District) to the south; [Gujrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujrat_District), [Mandi Bahauddin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandi_Bahauddin_District), and [Sargodha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sargodha_District) to the east, along the [Jhelum River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum_River); and [Mirpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirpur_District) to the north.

[Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum) is known for providing many soldiers to the [British](31717.md) and later to the [Pakistan armed forces](23369.md) due to which it is also known as the 'city of soldiers' or 'land of martyrs and warriors'.[^cite_note-5] Salt is quarried at the [Khewra Salt Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khewra_Salt_Mine) in the [Salt Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Range). There are two coal mines in the district from which the North-Western railway used to obtain part of its supply. These are the only coal mines in Punjab province which are in working condition. The chief center of the salt trade is [Pind Dadan Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pind_Dadan_Khan). The district is crossed by the main line of the North-Western railway and also traversed along the south by a branch line.[^cite_note-EB1911-6]

## History

Main article: [History of Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Jhelum)



[Tilla Jogian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilla_Jogian), the highest peak in Jhelum District

### Early history

The history of the district dates back to the [Hindu mythological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_mythology) period of the [Mahabharata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahabharata). The epic represents the Salt Range as the refuge of the five [Pandava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pandava) brethren during the period of their exile, and every salient point in its scenery is connected with some legend of the national heroes. Modern research has fixed the site of the conflict between [Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) and [Porus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porus_the_Elder) as within Jhelum district, though the exact spot at which the Macedonian king affected the passage of the Jhelum (or Hydaspes) has been hotly disputed. The Panhwars, [Janjuas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janjua) and [Jats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jat_people), who now hold the Salt Range and its northern plateau respectively, appear to have been the earliest inhabitants.[^cite_note-EB1911-6][^cite_note-IGI-12]

The [Janjuas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janjua), who appear to represent the oldest breed of Punjab and who still inhabit a large tract in the east of the District; while the [Awans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awan_\(tribe\)) and [Ghakars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gakhars) who cluster in the western plain, are apparently later invaders, the Janjuas were the dominant race during the before and early Muslim era and they long continued to retain their independence[^cite_note-EB1911-6] until the time of Sikh invaders, both in Jhelum itself and in the neighboring District of Rawalpindi.[^cite_note-IGI-12]

### Sultanate era



A masjid on Jhelum-Pind Dadan Khan Road

In 997 CE, [Sultan Mahmud Ghaznavi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni), took over the [Ghaznavid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids) empire established by his father, [Sultan Sebuktegin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabuktigin), he conquered the [Shahis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Shahi) in [Kabul](16826.md) in 1005, and followed it by the conquests of northern [Punjab region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab). The [Delhi Sultanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delhi_Sultanate) and later [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) ruled the region. The Punjab region became predominantly [Muslim](19541.md) due to [missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) saints whose [dargahs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dargah) dot the landscape of [Punjab region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab), which is also reflected in Jhelum.

### British era

Jhelum district was annexed by the British from its former [Sikh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs) rulers after the [Second Anglo-Sikh War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Sikh_War) of 1848–1849.[^cite_note-13] During British rule, Jhelum was a district of [Rawalpindi Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_Division), and was larger than the current district of Jhelum. On 1 April 1904, the tehsil of [Talagang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talagang_Tehsil) was detached from the District and incorporated with the new District of Attock. According to the Gazetteer of the Jhelum District of 1904, 88.7% of the population were Muslim.[^cite_note-14]

The old Jhelum district (minus Talagang) covered an area of 2,813 square miles (7285 km<sup>2</sup>) and included [Chakwal Tehsil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chakwal_Tehsil) – it was bordered by [Shahpur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shahpur_District) and [Attock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attock_District) to the west, and by [Rawalpindi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawalpindi_District) to the north – the [Jhelum River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum_River) separated it from [Kashmir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kashmir) to the north-east and from [Gujrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gujrat_District) and Shahpur to the south-east and south.[^cite_note-IGI-12]

## Independence

The predominantly Muslim population supported under the leadership of Raja Ghazanfar Ali khan of PD Khan [Muslim League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-India_Muslim_League) and [Partition of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India). After the [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India) of [Pakistan](23235.md) in 1947, the minority [Hindus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindus) and [Sikhs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhs) migrated to [India](14533.md) while the [Muslim](19541.md) refugees from [India](14533.md) settled in the Jhelum District.

Since independence the agriculture and industry of Jhelum developed and forms part of the [economy of Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Pakistan).

## Topography

### Jhelum City

Main article: [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum)

The district capital, [Jhelum City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum), is situated on the right and left bank of the [Jhelum River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum_River), the left side of Jhelum is known as [Sarai Alamgir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarai_Alamgir) and it also contains the [Military College Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_College_Jhelum) (MCJ). The 16th-century [Grand Trunk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Road) passes through the city. [Jhelum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jhelum) city is near the site of the [Battle of the Hydaspes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Hydaspes) between the armies of [Alexander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) and [Porus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porus_the_Elder) This battle took place a few miles downstream from the city center, along the river banks. Population of the Jhelum city (proper) is about 172,073[^cite_note-15] (2009) and it is the 35th largest city of [Pakistan](23235.md) by population. A [cantonment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonment) was built during the British rule, which has grown up into a strong Garrison, with an Infantry Division commanded by a Major General.

The River Jhelum below the bridge beside Jhelum City

The river Jhelum is navigable throughout the district, which forms the south-eastern portion of a rugged Himalayan spur, extending between the Indus and Jhelum to the borders of the Sind Sagar Doab. Its scenery is very picturesque, although not of so wild a character as the mountain region of Rawalpindi to the north, and is lighted up in places by smiling patches of the cultivated valley. The backbone of the district is formed by the Salt Range, a treble line of parallel hills running in three long forks from east to west throughout its whole breadth.[^cite_note-EB1911-6]

The range rises in precipices, broken by gorges, clothed with brushwood, and traversed by streams which are at first clear but become impregnated with the saline matter over which they pass. Between the line of hills lies a table-land, in which the small lake of [Kallar Kahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kallar_Kahar) nestles amongst the minor ridges. North of the Salt Range, the country extends upwards in an elevated plateau, diversified by a number of ravines and fissures, until it loses itself in tangled masses of Rawalpindi mountains. In this rugged tract, cultivation is rare and difficult, the soil being choked with saline matter. At the foot of the Salt Range, however, a small strip of level soil lies along the banks of the Jhelum and is dotted with prosperous villages.[^cite_note-EB1911-6]

The drainage of the district is determined by a low central watershed running north and south at right angles to the Salt Range. The waters of the western portion find their way into the [Sohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soan_River), and finally into the Indus; those of the opposite slope collect themselves into small torrents and empty themselves into the Jhelum River.[^cite_note-EB1911-6]

### Khewra Salt Mine

Main article: [Khewra Salt Mine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khewra_Salt_Mine)



A small [masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masjid) made of salt bricks inside the Khewra salt mine complex

The Khewra Salt Mine (or Mayo Salt Mine) is located in [Khewra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khewra,_Jhelum), north of [Pind Dadan Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pind_Dadan_Khan),[^cite_note-16] an administrative subdivision of Jhelum District, which rises from the [Indo-Gangetic Plain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Gangetic_Plain).[^cite_note-17] It is Pakistan's largest and oldest salt mine[^cite_note-18] and the world's second largest.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] It is a major tourist attraction, drawing up to 40,000 visitors a year.[^cite_note-21] Its history dates back to its discovery by Alexander's troops in 320 BC, but it started trading in the Mughal era.[^cite_note-22] The main tunnel at ground level was developed by Dr. H. Warth, a mining engineer, in 1872 during British rule. After [independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_India), the Pakistan Mineral Development Corporation took over the mine, which still remains the largest source of salt in the country, producing more than 350,000 tons per annum[^cite_note-23] of about 99% pure [halite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halite).

Estimates of the reserves of salt in the mine vary from 82 million tons to 600 million tons.

### Tilla Jogian

Main article: [Tilla Jogian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilla_Jogian)



Tilla Jogian

Tilla Jogian is the highest peak in the Eastern Salt Range. At 975 meters (3200 ft) above sea level, it is about 25 km to the west of Jhelum City and 10 km west of the model village of [Khukha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khukha). The view from the top of [Tilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tilla_Satellite_Launch_Centre) is highly rewarding. [Rohtas Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohtas_Fort) is located to the east of Tilla Jogian at a distance of about 7 km from [Dina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dina_Tehsil), a rapidly expanding town on the [Grand Trunk Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Trunk_Road).

### Rohtas Fort

Main article: [Rohtas Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rohtas_Fort)

Rohtas Fort (Qila Rohtas) is a historical garrison fort located near the city of Jhelum. It was built by [Raja Todar Mal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todar_Mal), under the orders of the [Afghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Afghanistan) king [Sher Shah Suri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sher_Shah_Suri), to subdue the rebellious tribes of the northern Punjab region, in the 16th century. This fort is about 4 km in circumference. The Rohtas fort was built to crush the local Gakhar tribes of Potohar, who rebelled against the [Sur dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sur_Empire) after the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_emperors) emperor [Humayun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humayun) was ousted by the former.

It took eight years to build the fort, it was captured by [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_emperors) emperor [Humayun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humayun) in 1555.[^cite_note-24] [Nader Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah), the [Turkic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) ruler of [Persia](14653.md), Afghan ruler [Ahmed Shah Abdali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani) and the [Maratha army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maratha_Empire) also camped here during their respective campaigns in the [Punjab region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjab). Rohtas was also occasionally used for administrative purposes by [Maharaja Ranjit Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranjit_Singh) of the [Sikh Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikh_Empire), after he captured it in 1825.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

Jhelum District has a total area of 858,767 acres (3,475.31 km<sup>2</sup>), out of which 316,815 acres (1,282.10 km<sup>2</sup>) are cultivated. The area is located on the eastern part of [Potohar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pothohar_Plateau) upland along with River Jhelum.

[Agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture) in the District Jhelum depends mainly on rainfall. The average rainfall of the area varies from 20 to 40 inches (1,000 mm). About three-fourths of this precipitation is received in [monsoon](57630.md) season and the remaining one-fourth is received during the rest of the year. The irrigated area at present is limited but the emphasis on the construction of small dams is gradually increasing. Wheat remains the main crop.

In Tehsil P.D. Khan, salt is the predominant feature that is spoiling the rich agricultural land day by day. There is a long strip of very rich and virgin soil along the river which could be made a paradise of citrus plantation by drip irrigation if the local people are motivated and the [Government of Punjab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Punjab,_Pakistan) expressed some interest in it.

Vegetation of the forests of Jhelum Forest Division is dry, deciduous shrub type, phulai, Kahu (wild olive), and sanatha are the main species. The stocking, on the whole, is poor and the forests are open. Vegetation is poor on sandstone and red marl. The southern slopes are often devoid of vegetation while northwestern slopes carry good forests. The forests of Jhelum Forests Division are burdened with the right of grazing, browsing, and firewood. Under settlement out of total area 93,566 acres (378.65 km<sup>2</sup>) only 5,468 acres (22.13 km<sup>2</sup>) about (45%) are right free. The remaining 55% are open to grazing.

The fauna of the district is mostly indigenous restricted, like the vegetation, but similarly varied and interesting. The rugged and rough terrain, low rainfall, the scantly cover of vegetation, and the burning passions of the increasing number of hunters, all have their share in limiting the animal kingdom in the district. The river offers a better environment than elsewhere though the hills support more interesting wildlife. Urial (an animal from a deer family) and chinckara are spot aids while wild boar are found in the Salt Range. Wolves, foxes, and wild cats are also found. Hare is fairly common. Chikor grey and black partridge are also found in the parts of the district. Migratory ducks like teal pintail and mallard and some geese visit during winter.

## Climate

The climate of the tract is extreme. In winter it is very cold and summer is very hot. The average rainfall varies from 48 to 69 m.m per annum which is much below the required quantity but in the rainy season, the water torrents flow from north to the river Jhelum at a very fast speed and cause damages to the crops, bridges, roads, and are responsible for the soil erosion in the District.

### Population

As of the [2023 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Pakistani_census), Jhelum district has 229,064 households and a population of 1,382,308. The district has a sex ratio of 103.70 males to 100 females and a literacy rate of 80.65%: 86.55% for males and 74.53% for females.[^cite_note-2023table1-29][^cite_note-2023table12-30] 284,927 (20.85% of the surveyed population) are under 10 years of age.[^cite_note-2023table5-31] 541,318 (39.16%) live in urban areas.[^cite_note-2023table1-29]

## Development organizations

Rehmat Welfare Foundation Jhelum is a charity project working for poor and needy people of fifty-mile area including Jhelum which cannot afford the expenses of their daily life and very expensive general medical and kidney dialysis treatment.[^cite_note-50]

Civil Society Human and Institutional Development Program[^cite_note-51] (CHIP) is working in [Sohawa Tehsil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sohawa_Tehsil) since 2004, with its field office in the area CHIP has successfully mobilized community people to get organize into Community Based Organizations, Community Citizen Boards and Women Organizations. Further, CHIP has duly built the capacity of these local entities to take new initiatives. These community-level organizations, in collaborative partnerships with CHIP, are working on several development projects. The main focus of these projects is to eliminate illiteracy from villages of Sohawa especially those where government education structure does not exist, make clean drinking water available, provide technical support to the local farmers, raise skill development opportunities for women, and include them in the decision-making process, aiding social inclusion, so as to better represent the communities they live in. The local community-level organizations developed by CHIP are making successful efforts in implementing development projects by deriving funds from local government bodies.
