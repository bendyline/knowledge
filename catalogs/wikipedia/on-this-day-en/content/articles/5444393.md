The **2006 Stanley Cup Final** was the [championship series](3314237.md) of the [National Hockey League](21809.md)'s (NHL) [2005–06 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_NHL_season), and the culmination of the [2006 Stanley Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Stanley_Cup_playoffs). The first Stanley Cup Final since [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Stanley_Cup_Final) after a [lockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NHL_lockout) canceled the entirety of the [2004–05 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NHL_season), it was contested between the [Eastern Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Conference_\(NHL\)) champion [Carolina Hurricanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_Carolina_Hurricanes_season) and the [Western Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Conference_\(NHL\)) champion [Edmonton Oilers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_Edmonton_Oilers_season). Carolina defeated Edmonton in seven games to win the franchise's first [Stanley Cup](66968.md) and become the tenth post-1967 expansion team and third former WHA team to win the Cup. Carolina's 2006 win was also the team's second league championship (the club, then known as the [New England Whalers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_England_Whalers) won the [WHA Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avco_World_Trophy) in 1973). This was the first [professional sports championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) won by a team from [North Carolina](21650.md).

It was Carolina's second appearance in the Finals, the other being in [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Stanley_Cup_Final), a loss to the [Detroit Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings). It was Edmonton's seventh appearance in the Finals and their first since winning their fifth Stanley Cup in [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Stanley_Cup_Final). It was also the first (and to date only) Finals matchup between [teams that entered the league in 1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1979_NHL_expansion).[^cite_note-2]

This was Carolina’s last Stanley Cup championship until [2026](80157147.md).

## Paths to the Finals

See also: [2006 Stanley Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Stanley_Cup_playoffs), [2005–06 Carolina Hurricanes season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_Carolina_Hurricanes_season), and [2005–06 Edmonton Oilers season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_Edmonton_Oilers_season)

### Carolina Hurricanes

The Hurricanes won the Southeast Division and clinched the second seed in the Eastern Conference. In the first round, they defeated the seventh-seeded [Montreal Canadiens](42966.md) in six games. In the second round, they defeated the third-seeded [New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Jersey_Devils) in five games. In the Eastern Conference Final, the Hurricanes defeated the fourth-seeded [Buffalo Sabres](69918.md) in seven games to make their second Stanley Cup Final in five years.

### Edmonton Oilers

The Oilers entered the playoffs as the 8th seed in the Western Conference. In the first round, they shockingly upset the [Presidents' Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidents'_Trophy) winner [Detroit Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings) in six games. They then beat the fifth-seeded [San Jose Sharks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Jose_Sharks) in six games in the second round after trailing two games to none. In the Western Conference Final, the Oilers beat the sixth-seeded [Mighty Ducks of Anaheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mighty_Ducks_of_Anaheim) in five games to return to the Stanley Cup Final for the first time since [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Stanley_Cup_Final).

The Oilers became the first team since the NBA's [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_NBA_Finals) [New York Knicks](72855.md) and the first team in the history of the NHL to make it to the Stanley Cup Final as a #8 seed. This feat would also be accomplished by the [Los Angeles Kings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Kings) in [2012](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Stanley_Cup_Final), the [Nashville Predators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nashville_Predators) in [2017](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Stanley_Cup_Final), and the [Florida Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Panthers) in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Stanley_Cup_Final).

## Game summaries

This series marked the first time that two former [World Hockey Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Hockey_Association) teams played against each other for the Stanley Cup since they merged with the NHL in 1979. As a result of [the new scheduling formula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Season_structure_of_the_NHL) that was implemented before the [2005–06 NHL season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005%E2%80%9306_NHL_season), the Hurricanes and the Oilers did not meet during the regular season.

These were also the first ever finals contested by two teams that had both missed the playoffs the [previous season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) (skipping 2005 due to the [lockout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004%E2%80%9305_NHL_lockout)). It was also to be the first finals contested by teams that would both go on to miss [the following year's playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Stanley_Cup_playoffs). Prior to these Finals, only the 1938–39 [Chicago Blackhawks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Blackhawks) had ever missed the playoffs one year, then played in the Stanley Cup Final (win or lose) the following season, and then missed the playoffs again the season after that. Both the Hurricanes and Oilers have now accomplished this dubious feat.

Hurricanes head coach [Peter Laviolette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Laviolette) joined [Ron Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Wilson_\(ice_hockey_b._1955\)) as the only people to have head coached in an Olympics and Stanley Cup finals in the same year, having coached the American ice hockey team during the [Torino Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Winter_Olympics). Both would coach in either one in 2010; Wilson coached the American ice hockey team to silver medal at the [Vancouver Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics), while Laviolette would coach in the [Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Stanley_Cup_Final) with the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers).

### Game one

| June 5 | Edmonton Oilers |  | **4–5** |  | **Carolina Hurricanes** | [RBC Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PNC_Arena) | [Recap](https://www.nhl.com/gamecenter/edm-vs-car/2006/06/05/2005030411) |  |

In Game 1, Carolina tied the biggest comeback in Stanley Cup finals history, overcoming a three-goal deficit to win 5–4. Edmonton scored first, 8\:18 into the first period, with a goal from [Fernando Pisani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Pisani). In the second period, [Chris Pronger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Pronger) scored the first [penalty shot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penalty_shot_\(ice_hockey\)) goal in finals history after defenceman [Niclas Wallin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Niclas_Wallin) illegally covered the puck inside his own goal crease, and [Ethan Moreau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethan_Moreau)'s goal at 16\:23 gave the Oilers a 3–0 lead. But at the 17\:17 mark, [Rod Brind'Amour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rod_Brind'Amour) scored the Hurricanes' first goal of the game. Carolina then tied the game in the third period with two scores by [Ray Whitney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Whitney_\(ice_hockey\)). The Hurricanes jumped ahead 4–3 on a shorthanded breakaway goal by [Justin Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Williams), but Edmonton's [Ales Hemsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ale%C5%A1_Hemsk%C3%BD) scored on a power play to tie the game with 6\:29 remaining. Late in the final period, Oilers goaltender [Dwayne Roloson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dwayne_Roloson) suffered a series-ending [knee injury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knee) in a collision and was replaced with [Ty Conklin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ty_Conklin). With 32 seconds remaining in regulation, Conklin misplayed the puck behind his own net, where it deflected off [Jason Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jason_Smith_\(hockey\))'s stick to the front of the empty net, allowing Brind'Amour to score the winning goal. Hurricanes goaltender [Cam Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cam_Ward_\(ice_hockey\)) had to make the last of his 34 saves with 3.8 seconds remaining, robbing [Shawn Horcoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shawn_Horcoff) for the second time in the third period with a glove save to preserve the victory.

### Game two

| June 7 | Edmonton Oilers |  | **0–5** |  | **Carolina Hurricanes** | RBC Center | [Recap](https://www.nhl.com/gamecenter/edm-vs-car/2006/06/07/2005030412) |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

With Roloson's injury, [Jussi Markkanen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jussi_Markkanen) started for the Oilers in game two. Although Markkanen had played 37 games in the regular season, sharing the job with Ty Conklin and [Mike Morrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Morrison_\(ice_hockey\)), he and Conklin switched back and forth during the playoff as the backup (with the other sitting in the press box as a healthy scratch); he also had not played in a game since March 1, 2006. Late in the third period, Edmonton forward [Georges Laraque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georges_Laraque) checked Carolina forward [Andrew Ladd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Ladd) into the boards, resulting in a major penalty and Laraque being ejected from the game. The Hurricanes went on to shut out the Oilers, 5–0, with five different Carolina players scoring goals, and [Cam Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cam_Ward_\(ice_hockey\)) making 25 saves to record his second shutout of the postseason. It was the first time three goaltenders had been used in the Stanley Cup Final since [1970](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_Stanley_Cup_Final), when the [St. Louis Blues](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Louis_Blues) employed [Jacques Plante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Plante), [Glenn Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Hall) and [Ernie Wakely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernie_Wakely) on their way to being swept by the [Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boston_Bruins).

### Game three

| June 10 | Carolina Hurricanes |  | **1–2** |  | **Edmonton Oilers** | [Rexall Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northlands_Coliseum) | [Recap](https://www.nhl.com/gamecenter/car-vs-edm/2006/06/10/2005030413) |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Markkanen once again started in net with Roloson still out. Georges Laraque returned to the Edmonton lineup after being ejected the previous game. Shawn Horcoff scored just over two minutes into the first period. During the second period, a short-handed goal was waved off by the referee, because he had lost sight of the puck and had blown the whistle, despite the fact that the puck had not yet been covered. The Hurricanes responded midway through the third period with their captain, Rod Brind'Amour, taking a rebound off a blocked shot past Markkanen. However, with 2\:15 left in the game, Edmonton's [Ryan Smyth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Smyth) scored the winning goal after crashing into Ward inside the crease as they both tried to get control of a rebound off of a shot by Ales Hemsky.

### Game five

| June 14 | **Edmonton Oilers** |  | **4–3** | **OT** | Carolina Hurricanes | RBC Center | [Recap](https://www.nhl.com/gamecenter/edm-vs-car/2006/06/14/2005030415) |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

Carolina had a 3–1 lead in the series and a chance to win the Stanley Cup on their home ice. However, Edmonton scored first on Fernando Pisani's redirect of a Pronger slapshot 16 seconds into the game. The Hurricanes then went ahead 2–1 on two power play goals by Eric Staal and Ray Whitney before the Oilers scored on the power play with a one-timer by Ales Hemsky to tie the game. [Michael Peca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Peca) then gave Edmonton a 3–2 lead with 17.4 seconds left in the first period. In the second period, Staal poked a goal between Jussi Markkanen and the post to tie the game. Early in the third period, Hurricanes centre [Doug Weight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doug_Weight) got sandwiched by Chris Pronger and [Raffi Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raffi_Torres), separating his shoulder and ending his night early. Weight would not return to play for the rest of the series, but would eventually raise the Cup. Carolina defenceman [Aaron Ward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aaron_Ward_\(ice_hockey\)) also was injured in the third period, and with Carolina running out of healthy skaters, the Hurricanes were desperate to close out the game. With 7\:47 remaining in the third period, Whitney missed what might have been the Hurricanes' best chance to win the series with a shot that just hit the post. The game went to overtime, where [Steve Staios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steve_Staios) drew a penalty early in the period to put the Hurricanes on the power play. Fernando Pisani picked off a cross-ice pass from Cory Stillman and streaked in short-handed to beat Cam Ward top right corner (glove side) with a left-handed shot to score the first short-handed overtime goal in finals history, giving the Oilers the upset win.

Despite the emotional boost of Carolina winger [Erik Cole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Erik_Cole) returning to the ice for the first time since breaking his neck in March, Edmonton dominated for the entire 60 minutes. They shut out Carolina 4–0, tying the series after down 1–3 in front of a raucous crowd at Rexall Place. They scored three power-play goals and limited the Hurricanes to only 16 shots on goal. ### Game seven

| June 19 | Edmonton Oilers |  | **1–3** |  | **Carolina Hurricanes** | RBC Center | [Recap](https://www.nhl.com/gamecenter/edm-vs-car/2006/06/19/2005030417) |  |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |

The Hurricanes returned to the RBC Center defeating the Oilers in Game 7, 3–1, to win the Stanley Cup. Aaron Ward and [Frantisek Kaberle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franti%C5%A1ek_Kaberle) gave Carolina a 2–0 lead before Fernando Pisani scored for Edmonton 1\:03 into the third period to cut the lead in half.

With just over a minute to go in regulation, the Oilers [pulled the goalie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extra_attacker) in hopes of forcing overtime. A loose puck wound up on the stick of [Bret Hedican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bret_Hedican), who passed to Eric Staal, who himself passed it down-ice to Justin Williams. Williams skated down the length of the ice and tapped the puck into the empty net, sealing the Stanley Cup for the Hurricanes. Cam Ward became the first NHL rookie goaltender to win the Stanley Cup Final series since [Patrick Roy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Roy) led the [Montreal Canadiens](42966.md) in [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Stanley_Cup_Final), and he was also the first rookie since the [Philadelphia Flyers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philadelphia_Flyers)' [Ron Hextall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Hextall) in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Stanley_Cup_Final) to be awarded the [Conn Smythe Trophy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conn_Smythe_Trophy) as most valuable player in the playoffs. Staal (21 years and eight months) became the youngest player to lead the playoffs in scoring since [Gordie Howe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordie_Howe) in 1949.[^cite_note-Observer-3] The Hurricanes were the last team to win Game 7 of the Stanley Cup Final at home until the [Florida Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Panthers) did so in [2024](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Stanley_Cup_Final), coincidentally against the Oilers as well.

[Cory Stillman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cory_Stillman) earned a Stanley Cup title for the second-straight season, having won in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_NHL_season) with the [Tampa Bay Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tampa_Bay_Lightning), becoming the first player to win back-to-back titles with different teams since [Claude Lemieux](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claude_Lemieux) ([1995 New Jersey Devils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_New_Jersey_Devils_season), [1996 Colorado Avalanche](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_Colorado_Avalanche_season)). [Peter Laviolette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Laviolette) became the second American-born coach to win the Stanley Cup.[^cite_note-Observer-3]

The Hurricanes' victory ended [Glen Wesley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glen_Wesley)'s 18-year drought without winning the Cup. He had played close to 1,500 regular season and playoff games before winning the Cup, the longest such drought in the NHL. Wesley was the last player remaining from the franchise's days as the [Hartford Whalers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hartford_Whalers). Other notable veterans to win their first Cup were Rod Brind'Amour, Doug Weight, Ray Whitney and Bret Hedican. Hedican was on the losing end of another final decided in a game seven, in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Stanley_Cup_Final), while with the [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks). Mark Recchi won the second Cup of his career, having won it 15 years prior as a member of the [1991 Pittsburgh Penguins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990%E2%80%9391_Pittsburgh_Penguins_season). He would later retire a Stanley Cup winner with the [2011 Boston Bruins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010%E2%80%9311_Boston_Bruins_season), where he teamed up with Frantisek Kaberle's brother [Tomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom%C3%A1%C5%A1_Kaberle).

The Hurricanes became the third former World Hockey Association franchise to win the Stanley Cup, following the Oilers and [Quebec Nordiques](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quebec_Nordiques), who won as the [Colorado Avalanche](70948.md) in [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_NHL_season) and [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000%E2%80%9301_NHL_season).

The [2006 Stanley Cup playoffs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Stanley_Cup_playoffs) marked the second time in a row that an [Alberta](717.md)-based team had made it to the finals only to lose in seven games to the [Southeast Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_Division_\(NHL\)) champions, as the [Calgary Flames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary_Flames) were defeated by the [Tampa Bay Lightning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003%E2%80%9304_Tampa_Bay_Lightning_season) in the [2004 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Stanley_Cup_Final). The Oilers were not able to complete their [Cinderella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinderella_\(sports\)) run, having entered the playoffs seeded eighth, denying General Manager [Kevin Lowe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Lowe) and Head Coach [Craig MacTavish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_MacTavish) from joining [Lester Patrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lester_Patrick) and [Frank Boucher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frank_Boucher) as the only duos to win the Stanley Cup together as players and then as a coach-managerial team, as the two were with the Oilers as players in [1987](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_Stanley_Cup_Final), [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Stanley_Cup_Final) and [1990](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_Stanley_Cup_Final) and the [New York Rangers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Rangers) in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Stanley_Cup_Final).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

This was the first major-league professional championship for the state of [North Carolina](21650.md) by a men's team (the [Carolina Courage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Courage) of the defunct [Women's United Soccer Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_United_Soccer_Association) won the 2002 [Founders Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Founders_Cup)). Until the Hurricanes' second Stanley Cup win in [2026](80157147.md), this was also the only Big 4 championship by an active North Carolina–based team in any of the [four major league sports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada). The Hurricanes made it to the [2002 Stanley Cup Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Stanley_Cup_Final), losing to the [Detroit Red Wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings) in five games; and the [Carolina Panthers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carolina_Panthers) made it to [Super Bowl XXXVIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_XXXVIII), but lost to the [New England Patriots](21719.md), and [Super Bowl 50](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_Bowl_50), losing to the [Denver Broncos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denver_Broncos).&#x20;

### Engraving notes

- \#48 [Anton Babchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anton_Babchuk) (D) played in 22 regular season games for Carolina, previously 17 for [Chicago](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicago_Blackhawks). #24 [Andrew Hutchinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Hutchinson_\(ice_hockey\)) (D) played in 36 regular season games for Carolina. They did not play in the playoffs (both were healthy scratches). As they did not automatically qualify,

  [^cite_note-qualify-6]

  &#x20;Carolina successfully requested an exemption to engrave their names.

- Eric Staal's name was misspelled ERIC STAAAL on the Stanley Cup. Engraver Louise St. Jacques was able to correct the mistake and removed the last "A."

- Frantisek Kaberle became the first player to win the Olympic bronze medal in ice hockey (with team Czech Republic) and the Stanley Cup (with Carolina) in the same year.

- Canadian [Craig Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craig_Adams_\(ice_hockey\)) was the first player born in Brunei, but he grew up in [Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary).

- Nine players remained from the Carolina team that lost to [Detroit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Detroit_Red_Wings) in the [2002](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Stanley_Cup_Final) Final: Craig Adams, Kevyn Adams, Rod Brind'Amour, Erik Cole, Bret Hedican, Josef Vasicek, Niclas Wallin, Glen Wesley, and Aaron Ward. ## Television

In the United States, this was the first Stanley Cup Final to be broadcast on [NBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_on_NBC) and [OLN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_on_Versus) (later Versus, currently [NBC Sports Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBC_Sports_Network)). Games one and two were on OLN, while the remainder of the series was on NBC. NBC's broadcast of game seven drew a 3.3 rating, a 21% drop from [ABC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NHL_on_ABC)'s 4.2 for game seven in [2004](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Stanley_Cup_Final).[^cite_note-Ratings-9] However, some NBC affiliates didn't air game seven live.[^cite_note-Ratings-9] Overall, NBC had an average rating of 2.3 for its five telecasts of the final, down 12% from ABC's 2004 average.[^cite_note-Ratings-9]

In Canada, the CBC's broadcast of game seven drew 4.739 million viewers. However, it included pre-game and post-game coverage. The game itself drew 5.553 million.[^cite_note-Ratings-9]
