The **Fête de la Musique** (French pronunciation: [\[fɛt d(ə) la myzik\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/French)), also known in English as **Music Day**, **Make Music Day**, or **World Music Day**, is an annual music [celebration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_minor_secular_observances) which usually takes place in mid-June. On Music Day, citizens and residents are urged to play music outside, in their neighborhoods or in public spaces and parks. Free concerts are also organized, where musicians play for fun.

The idea for the first all-day musical celebration on the day of the [summer solstice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Summer_solstice) came from [Jack Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Lang_\(French_politician\)), then [Minister of Culture](3354559.md) of [France](5843419.md), and [Maurice Fleuret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Fleuret); it was celebrated in Paris in 1982. Music Day later became celebrated in 120 countries around the world.

## History

In October 1981, [Maurice Fleuret](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Fleuret) became Director of Music and Dance at the French [Ministry of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Culture_\(France\)) at [Jack Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_Lang_\(French_politician\))'s request. He applied his reflections to the musical practice and its evolution: "the music everywhere and the concert nowhere". When he discovered, in a 1982 study on the cultural habits of the French, that five million people, one young person out of two, played a musical instrument, he began to dream of a way to bring people out on the streets. It first took place in 1982 in Paris as the Fête de la Musique.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-hist-2]

Ever since, the festival has become an international phenomenon, celebrated on the same day in more than 700 cities in 120 countries, including India, Germany, Italy, Greece, Russia, Australia, Peru, Brazil, Ecuador, Mexico, Canada, the United States,[^cite_note-3] the UK, and Japan.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-hist-2]

In the [Anglosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglosphere), the day has become known as Music Day,[^cite_note-5] Make Music Day[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] and World Music Day.[^cite_note-8]

## Purpose

The main purpose of Fête de la Musique is to promote music. Amateur and professional musicians are encouraged to perform in the streets, under the slogan *"Faites de la musique"* ("Make music"), a [homophone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homophone) of *Fête de la musique*.[^cite_note-9] Thousands of free concerts are staged throughout the day, making all genres of music accessible to the public.[^cite_note-10]

## France

Despite the general public exhibiting great tolerance for musical performances in public areas in evening hours, noise restrictions do apply and entertainment establishments may only extend their working hours on the day and broadcast music in the evening hours with prior authorization. Prefectures in France reserve the right to prohibit individuals, groups or establishments from installing audio equipment in the streets.[^cite_note-11]

## Reach and impact

As of 2019, a total of 120 countries[^cite_note-12] and over 1,000 cities participate in Fête de la Musique.[^cite_note-auto-13] In 2023, events were held on most continents.[^cite_note-world-14]

Italy's *Festa della Musica* began in 1985, and became national in 1994.[^cite_note-world-14]

The UK Event began as *National Music Day* in 1992.[^cite_note-15] [Make Music Day UK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make_Music_Day_UK) became an independent organization in 2022.[^cite_note-world-14]

[Ukraine](31750.md) has been holding its event in [Lviv](49968.md) since 2013. Despite the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), the event continues to be held each year.[^cite_note-world-14]

In the United States, the *Make Music Alliance* was formed in 2014 to help coordinate efforts across the country. In 2023, a total of 4,791 free concerts were held across 117 cities. [Cincinnati](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cincinnati), [ Madison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madison,_Wisconsin), [New York City](645042.md), [Philadelphia](50585.md), and [Salem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salem,_Massachusetts) each organized over a hundred different concerts on the day.[^cite_note-16]

In India, classical, contemporary and folk music performances are held throughout the country. Musical duo [Sourendro-Soumyojit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sourendro%E2%80%94Soumyojit) are known for their annual World Music Day concert in Kolkata[^cite_note-17], which brings together Indian playback singers at a ticketed event. Brands and advertisers have also adopted the event; on June 21, 2026, *[The Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Hindu)* published a digital music crossword[^cite_note-18] shaped like a [beam note](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beam_\(music\)) to mark the occasion.&#x20;

In Australia, [Make Music Day Australia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Music_Association#Make_Music_Day_Australia) was initiated in 2018 by the [Australian Music Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Australian_Music_Association) (AMA),[^cite_note-namm2018-19] and as of 2022, was co-hosted by the AMA and [National Association of Music Merchants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Association_of_Music_Merchants) (NAMM).[^cite_note-apata2021-20] In 2023, a huge international project called "Make Music, Make Friends" partnered 10 Australian schools with schools around the world to share music and greet one another on Make Music Day.[^cite_note-briggs2023-21]

In Cyprus, the festival has been held every year since 2019. While *Make Music Cyprus* is usually limited to [Nicosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicosia), the 2025 edition expanded to [Larnaca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Larnaca) as well, but it was held at an earlier date.[^cite_note-22]

[Turkey](11125639.md) and [Ghana](12067.md) held their first *Make Music Days* in 2022, and South Africa in 2023.[^cite_note-world-14]
