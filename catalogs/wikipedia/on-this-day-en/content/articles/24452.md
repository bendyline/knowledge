The **prime minister of India** ([ISO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_15919): *Bhāratīya Gaṇarājyasya Pradhānamantrī*) is the [head of government](37570.md)[^cite_note-2] of the [Republic of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_India). [Executive authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_authority) is vested in the prime minister and his chosen [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers),[^cite_note-majeed-roi-2-3]<sup>: 185 </sup>[^cite_note-dam-3-4]<sup>: 307 </sup>[^cite_note-prime-ministers-india-2-5] despite the [President of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) being the nominal [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state).[^cite_note-pillay-cri-pm-2-6]<sup>: 146–147 </sup>[^cite_note-majeed-roi-2-3]<sup>: 185 </sup>[^cite_note-dam-3-4]<sup>: 307 </sup>[^cite_note-nsingh-india-7] The prime minister has to be a member of one of the houses of bicameral [Parliament of India](410238.md), alongside heading the respective house.[^cite_note-pillay-cri-pm-2-6]<sup>: 146–147 </sup> The prime minister and the [cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers) are at all times responsible to the [Lok Sabha](275456.md), the lower house of the [Parliament](410238.md).[^cite_note-dam-3-4]<sup>: 307 </sup>[^cite_note-majeed-roi-2-3]<sup>: 185 </sup>

The sitting prime minister ranks third in the [Order of Precedence of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_precedence_in_India)[^cite_note-OPR-8] and is appointed by the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India); however, the prime minister has to [enjoy the confidence](208101.md) of the majority of [Lok Sabha](275456.md) members, who are directly elected [every five years](1435365.md#Parliamentary_general_elections_\(Lok_Sabha\)), lest the prime minister shall resign. The prime minister can be a member of the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) or the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md), the upper house of the parliament. The prime minister controls the selection and dismissal of members of the [Union Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers) and allocation of posts to members within the government.

The longest-serving prime minister was the first prime minister, [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru), whose tenure lasted 16 years and 286 days. His premiership was followed by [Lal Bahadur Shastri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Bahadur_Shastri)'s short tenure and [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi)'s 11- and 4-year-long tenures, with both politicians belonging to the [Indian National Congress](149333.md). After [Indira Gandhi's assassination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Indira_Gandhi), her son [Rajiv Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi) took charge until 1989, when a decade with five unstable governments began. This was followed by the full terms of [P. V. Narasimha Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._V._Narasimha_Rao), [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee), [Manmohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmohan_Singh), and [Narendra Modi](444222.md), who is the current prime minister of India, serving since [26 May 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_swearing-in_ceremony_of_Narendra_Modi). He is the first non-Congress leader to become PM after consecutive general elections and secure a third successive term (2014, 2019, 2024). The first prime minister to do so was [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru), who secured successive terms following the victory in the general elections of 1946, 1952, 1957, and 1962.

## Origins and history

India follows a [parliamentary system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system) in which the prime minister is the presiding [head of the government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_government) and chief of the executive of the government. In such systems, the [head of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state), or, the head of state's official representative (i.e., the monarch, president, or [governor-general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor-general)) usually holds a purely ceremonial position and acts, on most matters, only on the advice of the prime minister.

The prime minister must become a [member of parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_\(India\)) within six months of beginning their tenure, if they are not one already. A prime minister is expected to work with other central ministers to ensure the passage of bills by the parliament.

### 1947–1984

Since 1947, there have been 14 different prime ministers.[^cite_note-56o-9] The first few decades after 1947 saw the [Indian National Congress](149333.md)' (INC) near complete domination over the political map of India. India's first prime minister, [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru), took oath on 15 August 1947. Nehru went on to serve as prime minister for 17 consecutive years, winning four [general elections](1435365.md) in the process. His tenure ended in May 1964, on his death.[^cite_note-\:0-10][^cite_note-11] After the death of Nehru, [Lal Bahadur Shastri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Bahadur_Shastri), a former [home minister](1974473.md) and a leader of the Congress party, ascended to the position of prime minister. Shastri's tenure saw the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1965). Shashtri subsequently died of a reported heart attack in [Tashkent](57533.md), after signing the [Tashkent Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tashkent_Declaration).[^cite_note-12]

After Shastri, [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi), Nehru's daughter, was elected as the country's third prime minister. The first, and to date, the only, woman to hold the post, Indira's first term in office lasted 11 years, in which she took steps such as [nationalisation of banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banking_in_India#Nationalisation_in_the_1960s);[^cite_note-13] end of [allowances](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Privy_Purse_in_India) and political posts, which were received by members of the royal families of the erstwhile [princely states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Princely_state) of the [British Indian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Indian_Empire).[^cite_note-Interpretation_Act_1889-14][^cite_note-15] In addition, events such as the [Indo-Pakistani War of 1971](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indo-Pakistani_War_of_1971);[^cite_note-16] the establishment of a sovereign [Bangladesh](3454.md);[^cite_note-17] accession of [Sikkim](140372.md) to India, through a [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1975_Sikkimese_monarchy_referendum) in 1975;[^cite_note-18] and India's [first nuclear test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smiling_Buddha) in [Pokhran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran) occurred during Indira's first term. In 1975, amid growing unrest and a court order declaring Indira's election to the Lok Sabha void, President [Fakhruddin Ali Ahmed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fakhruddin_Ali_Ahmed), on Indira's advice, imposed a [state of emergency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Emergency_\(India\)), therefore bestowing the government with the power to [rule by decree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rule_by_decree); this period is known for human rights violations such as mass sterilisation and the imprisonment of Indira's political opponents.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

After widespread protests, the emergency was lifted in 1977, and a [general election](1435365.md) was held. All of the political parties of the opposition, after the conclusion of the emergency, fought together against the Congress, under the umbrella of the [Janata Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janata_Party), in the [general election of 1977](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Indian_general_election), and were successful in defeating the Congress. Subsequently, [Morarji Desai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morarji_Desai), a former [deputy prime minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_India), became the first non-Congress prime minister of India. Desai's government was composed of groups with opposite ideologies, in which unity and coordination were difficult to maintain. Ultimately, after two and a half years as PM; on 28 July 1979, Desai tendered his resignation to the president; and his government fell. Thereafter, [Charan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charan_Singh), a deputy prime minister in Desai's cabinet, with outside, conditional support from Congress, proved a majority in [Lok Sabha](275456.md) and took oath as prime minister.[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] However, Congress pulled its support shortly after, and Singh had to resign; he had a tenure of 5 months, the shortest in the history of the office.

In [1980](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1980_Indian_general_election), after a three-year absence, the Congress returned to power with an absolute majority. Indira Gandhi was elected prime minister a second time.[^cite_note-23] In June 1984, [Operation Blue Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Blue_Star), an [Indian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Army) operation against [Sikh](27964.md) militants inside the [Golden Temple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Temple), the most sacred site in Sikhism, was conducted, resulting in reportedly thousands of deaths, both of the militants and civilians.[^cite_note-24] In revenge, on 31 October of that year, Gandhi was shot dead by [Satwant Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satwant_Singh) and [Beant Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beant_Singh_\(assassin\)), two of her bodyguards, in the garden of her residence at 1, [Safdarjung Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safdarjung_Road), New Delhi.[^cite_note-25]

### 1984–1999

After Indira, [Rajiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi), her eldest son and 40 years old at the time, was sworn in on the evening of 31 October 1984, becoming the youngest person ever to hold the office of prime minister. Rajiv immediately called for a general election. In the subsequent [general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Indian_general_election), the Congress secured a [supermajority](638313.md), winning 401 of 552 seats in the [Lok Sabha](275456.md), the maximum number received by any party in the history of India.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] [Vishwanath Pratap Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._P._Singh), first [finance minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_\(India\)) and then later [defence minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_\(India\)) in Gandhi's cabinet, uncovered irregularities, in what became known as the [Bofors scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bofors_scandal), during his stint at the [Ministry of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Defence_\(India\)); Singh was subsequently expelled from Congress and formed the [Janata Dal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janata_Dal) and, with the help of several anti-Congress parties, also formed the [National Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Front_\(India\)), a coalition of many political parties.[^cite_note-28]

In the [general election of 1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Indian_general_election), the National Front, with outside support from the [Bharatiya Janata Party](149330.md) (BJP) and the [Left Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Left_Front_\(West_Bengal\)), came to power.[^cite_note-\:1-29] [V. P. Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V._P._Singh) was elected prime minister.[^cite_note-\:1-29] During a tenure of less than a year, Singh and his government accepted the [Mandal Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandal_Commission)'s recommendations.[^cite_note-30] Singh's tenure came to an end after he ordered the arrest of BJP member [Lal Krishna Advani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L._K._Advani),[^cite_note-31] as a result, BJP withdrew its outside support to the government, V. P. Singh lost the subsequent [vote-of-no-confidence](208101.md) 146–320 and had to resign.[^cite_note-\:9-32] After V. P. Singh's resignation, [Chandra Shekhar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chandra_Shekhar) along with 64 [members of parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_\(India\)) (MPs) floated the [Samajwadi Janata Party (Rashtriya)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samajwadi_Janata_Party_\(Rashtriya\)),[^cite_note-33] and proved a majority in the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) with support from Congress.[^cite_note-34] But Shekhar's premiership did not last long, Congress proceeded to withdraw its support; Shekhar's government fell as a result, and [new elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Indian_general_election) were announced.[^cite_note-35]

Rajiv Gandhi [was assassinated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assassination_of_Rajiv_Gandhi) on the campaign trail for the [general election of 1991](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1991_Indian_general_election), and the Congress, under the leadership of [P. V. Narasimha Rao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P._V._Narasimha_Rao), rode a sympathy wave to form a [minority government](271434.md); Rao became the first PM of [South Indian](678583.md) origin.[^cite_note-\:3-36] After the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union), India was on the brink of [bankruptcy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign_bankruptcy), so, Rao took steps to [liberalise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalise) the economy, and appointed [Manmohan Singh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manmohan_Singh), an economist and a former [governor of the Reserve Bank of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Governors_of_Reserve_Bank_of_India), as finance minister.[^cite_note-\:3-36] Rao and Singh then took various steps to liberalise the economy,[^cite_note-\:3-36] these resulted in unprecedented economic growth in India.[^cite_note-37] His premiership, however, was also a witness to the [demolition of the Babri Masjid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demolition_of_the_Babri_Masjid), which resulted in the death of about 2,000 people.[^cite_note-38] Rao, however, did complete five continuous years in office, becoming the first prime minister outside of the [Nehru—Gandhi family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nehru%E2%80%93Gandhi_family) to do so.[^cite_note-\:3-36]

After the end of Rao's tenure in May 1996, the nation saw four prime ministers in a span of three years, *[viz.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viz.)*, two tenures of [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee); one tenure of [H. D. Deve Gowda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._D._Deve_Gowda) from 1 June 1996 to 21 April 1997; and one tenure of [I. K. Gujral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._K._Gujral) from 21 April 1997 to 19 March 1998. The government of Prime Minister Vajpayee—elected in [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_Indian_general_election)—took some concrete steps; in May 1998, after a month in power, the government announced the [conduct of five underground nuclear explosions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran-II) in [Pokhran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pokhran).[^cite_note-39] In response to these tests, many [western countries](21208200.md), including the United States, imposed [economic sanctions](411315.md) on India,[^cite_note-40] but, due to the support received from Russia, France, the [Gulf countries](3088555.md) and some other nations, the sanctions, were largely, not considered successful.[^cite_note-41][^cite_note-42] A few months later in response to the Indian nuclear tests, Pakistan also [conducted nuclear tests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chagai-I).[^cite_note-43] Given the deteriorating situation between the two countries, the governments tried to improve bilateral relations. In February 1999, India and Pakistan signed the [Lahore Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahore_Declaration), in which the two countries announced their intention to annul mutual enmity, increase trade and use their nuclear capabilities for peaceful purposes.[^cite_note-44]

In May 1999, [All India Anna Dravida Munnetra Kazhagam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Anna_Dravida_Munnetra_Kazhagam) leader [J. Jayalalithaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._Jayalalithaa) withdrew from the ruling [National Democratic Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance_\(India\)) (NDA) coalition;[^cite_note-45] Vajpayee's government, hence, became a [caretaker](240683.md) one after losing a motion-of-no-confidence 269–270, this coincided with the [Kargil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kargil_War) with Pakistan.[^cite_note-46] In the subsequent [October 1999 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Indian_general_election), the BJP-led NDA and its affiliated parties secured a comfortable majority in the Lok Sabha, winning 299 of 543 seats in the [lower house](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_house).[^cite_note-47]

### 2000–present



Former Prime Minister Manmohan Singh with the former Prime Minister Atal Bihari Vajpayee in New Delhi on May 24, 2004



Prime minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) addressing the 69th [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md), c. 2014

Vajpayee continued the process of economic liberalisation during his reign, resulting in economic growth.[^cite_note-\:4-48] In addition to the development of infrastructure and basic facilities, the government took several steps to improve the infrastructure of the country, such as, the [National Highways Development Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Highways_Development_Project) (NHDP) and the *[Pradhan Mantri Gram Sadak Yojana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pradhan_Mantri_Gram_Sadak_Yojana)* (PMGSY; [IAST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Alphabet_of_Sanskrit_Transliteration): *Pradhānamaṃtrī Grāma Saḍaka Yojanā*; lit. Prime Minister Rural Road Scheme),[^cite_note-49] for the development of roads. But during his reign, the [2002 Gujarat communal riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Gujarat_riots) in the state of Gujarat took place; resulting in about 2,000 deaths.[^cite_note-50] Vajpayee's tenure as prime minister came to an end in May 2004, making him the first non-Congress PM to complete a full five-year tenure.[^cite_note-\:4-48]

In the [2004 election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Indian_general_election), the Congress emerged as the largest party in a [hung parliament](652214.md); the Congress-led [United Progressive Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Progressive_Alliance) (UPA), with outside support from the Left Front, the [Samajwadi Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samajwadi_Party) (SP) and [Bahujan Samaj Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bahujan_Samaj_Party) (BSP) among others, proved a majority in the Lok Sabha,[^cite_note-\:5-51] and Manmohan Singh was elected prime minister; becoming the first Sikh prime minister of the nation.[^cite_note-\:5-51] During his tenure, the country retained the economic momentum gained during Prime Minister Vajpayee's tenure.[^cite_note-52] Apart from this, the government succeeded in getting the *[National Rural Employment Guarantee Act, 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rural_Employment_Guarantee_Act,_2005)*, and the *[Right to Information Act, 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Right_to_Information_Act,_2005)* passed in the parliament.[^cite_note-53][^cite_note-54] Further, the government strengthened India's relations with nations like Afghanistan,[^cite_note-55][^cite_note-56] Russia,[^cite_note-57] the [Gulf States](3088555.md), and the United States, culminating with the ratification of [India–United States Civil Nuclear Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/India%E2%80%93United_States_Civil_Nuclear_Agreement) near the end of Singh's first term.[^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59][^cite_note-60] At the same time, the [November 2008 Mumbai terrorist attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Mumbai_attacks) also happened during Singh's first term in office.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-62] In the [general election of 2009](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Indian_general_election), the mandate of UPA increased.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64] Prime Minister Singh's second term, however, was surrounded by accusations of high-level scandals and corruption.[^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66] Singh resigned as prime minister on 17 May 2014, after Congress' defeat in the [2014 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Indian_general_election).[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69]

In the [general election of 2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Indian_general_election), the BJP-led NDA won an absolute majority, winning 336 out of 543 Lok Sabha seats. The BJP became the first party since 1984 to secure a majority in the Lok Sabha. [Narendra Modi](444222.md), then the [Chief Minister of Gujarat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_of_Gujarat), was elected prime minister, becoming the first prime minister to have been born in an independent India.[^cite_note-70]

[Narendra Modi](444222.md) was re-elected as prime minister in 2019 with a bigger mandate than that of 2014. The BJP-led [NDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Democratic_Alliance_\(India\)) won 354 seats out of which the [BJP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BJP) secured 303 seats.[^cite_note-71]

## Constitutional framework and position of Prime Minister

The [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) envisions a scheme of affairs in which the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) is the head of state; in terms of Article 53 with office of the prime minister being the head of [Council of Ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Ministers_of_the_Republic_of_India) to assist and advise the president in the discharge of their constitutional functions. To quote, Article 53, [74](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution%20of%20India/Part%20V) and 75 provide as under:

> The executive powers of the Union shall be vested in the president and shall be exercised either directly or through subordinate officers, in accordance with the Constitution.

— Article 53(1), Constitution of India

> There shall be a Council of Ministers with the Prime Minister at the head to aid and advise the president who shall, in the exercise of his functions, act in accordance with such advice.

— [Article 74(1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Article_74_\(Constitution_of_India\)), Constitution of India

> The Prime Minister shall be appointed by the President and the other Ministers shall be appointed by the President on the advice of the Prime Minister.

— Article 75(1), Constitution of India

Like most [parliamentary democracies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_system), the [president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_state)'s duties are mostly [ceremonial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_ruler) as long as the [constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) and the [rule of law](25166191.md) is obeyed by the cabinet and the legislature. The prime minister of India is the head of government and has the responsibility for executive power. The president's constitutional duty is to preserve, protect and defend the Constitution and the law per [article 60](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution%20of%20India/Part%20V). In the constitution of India, the prime minister is mentioned in only four of its articles (articles 74, 75, 78 and 366). The prime minister plays a crucial role in the [government of India](553883.md) by enjoying majority in the [Lok Sabha](275456.md).

## Appointment, tenure and removal

### Eligibility

According to Article 84 of the Constitution of India, which sets the principle qualification for member of Parliament, and Article 75 of the Constitution of India, which sets the qualifications for the minister in the Union Council of Ministers, and the argument that the position of prime minister has been described as *[primus inter pares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primus_inter_pares)* (the first among equals),[^cite_note-72] A prime minister must:

- Be a [citizen of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_nationality_law).

- Be a member of the [Lok Sabha](275456.md) or the [Rajya Sabha](232210.md). If the person chosen as prime minister is neither a member of the Lok Sabha nor the Rajya Sabha at the time of selection, he must become a member of either of the houses within six months.

- Be above 25 years of age if he is a [member of the Lok Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament,_Lok_Sabha), or, above 30 years of age if he is a [member of the Rajya Sabha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament,_Rajya_Sabha).

- Not hold any office of profit under the government of India or the government of any state or under any local or other authority subject to the control of any of the said governments.

Once a candidate is elected as the prime minister, he must vacate his posts at any private or government companies and may take up the position only on completion of his term.

### Oaths of office and secrecy



[First swearing-in ceremony of Narendra Modi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_swearing-in_ceremony_of_Narendra_Modi), 2014

The prime minister is required to make and subscribe in the presence of the [president of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_India) before entering office, the oath of office and secrecy, as per the Third Schedule of the Constitution of India.

Oath of office:

> I, (name), do swear in the name of God/solemnly affirm that I will bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India as by law established, that I will uphold the sovereignty and integrity of India, that I will faithfully and conscientiously discharge my duties as Prime Minister for the Union and that I will do right to all manner of people in accordance with the Constitution and the law, without fear or favour, affection or ill-will.

— [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India), Third Schedule, Part I

Oath of secrecy:

> I, (name), do swear in the name of God/solemnly affirm that I will not directly or indirectly communicate or reveal to any person or persons any matter which shall be brought under my consideration or shall become known to me as Prime Minister for the Union except as may be required for the due discharge of my duties as such Minister.

— Constitution of India, Third Schedule, Part II

### Tenure and removal from office

The prime minister serves at 'the pleasure of the president', hence, a prime minister may remain in office indefinitely, so long as the president has confidence in them. However, a prime minister must have the confidence of Lok Sabha, the lower house of the [Parliament of India](410238.md).

The term of a prime minister can end before the end of a Lok Sabha's term, if a [simple majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Majority) of its members no longer have confidence in them, this is called a [vote-of-no-confidence](208101.md).[^cite_note-73] Three prime ministers, [I. K. Gujral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I._K._Gujral),[^cite_note-\:9-32] [H. D. Deve Gowda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._D._Deve_Gowda) and [Atal Bihari Vajpayee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atal_Bihari_Vajpayee) have been voted out from office this way. In addition, a prime minister can resign from office; [Morarji Desai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morarji_Desai) was the first prime minister to resign while in office.

Upon ceasing to possess the requisite qualifications to be a member of Parliament subject to the *[Representation of the People Act, 1951](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representation_of_the_People_Act,_1951)*.[^cite_note-74]

### Executive powers



Agencies under the Prime Minister

The prime minister leads the functioning and exercise of authority of the government of India. The president of India, subject to eligibility, invites a person who is commanding support of majority members of Lok Sabha to form the [government of India](553883.md), also known as the central government or Union government, at the national level and exercise its powers.[^cite_note-75] In practice the prime minister nominates the members of their [council of ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Council_of_Ministers) to the president.[^cite_note-cabinet-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78] He also works upon to decide a core group of ministers (known as the cabinet),[^cite_note-cabinet-76] as in charge of the important functions and ministries of the [government of India](553883.md).

The prime minister is responsible for aiding and advising the president in distribution of work of the government to various ministries and offices and in terms of the *Government of India (Allocation of Business) Rules, 1961*.[^cite_note-79] The co-ordinating work is generally allocated to the [Cabinet Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretariat_\(India\)).[^cite_note-80] While the work of the government is generally divided into various ministries, the prime minister may retain certain portfolios if he is not allocated to any member of the cabinet.

The prime minister, in consultation with the cabinet, schedules and attends the sessions of the houses of parliament and is required to answer questions from the Members of Parliament.[^cite_note-81]

Some specific ministries/department are not allocated to anyone in the cabinet but the prime minister themself. The prime minister is usually always in charge/head of:

- [Ministry of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Personnel,_Public_Grievances_and_Pensions) (as [Minister of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Personnel,_Public_Grievances_and_Pensions))

- [Cabinet Secretariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretariat_\(India\))

- [Appointments Committee of the Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appointments_Committee_of_the_Cabinet)

- [Cabinet Committee on Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Committee_on_Security)

- Cabinet Committee on Economic Affairs

- [NITI Aayog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NITI_Aayog) (formerly [Planning Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planning_Commission_\(India\)))

- [Department of Atomic Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Atomic_Energy)

- [Department of Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Space)

- [Nuclear Command Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_Command_Authority_\(India\))



US President [Jimmy Carter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Carter) and PM [Morarji Desai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morarji_Desai) signing the New Delhi declaration, 3 January 1978



Russian president [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) meeting prime minister Narendra Modi in the [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin), Moscow, 24 December 2015

The prime minister represents the country in various delegations, high level meetings and international organisations that require the attendance of the highest government office,[^cite_note-82] and also addresses to the nation on various issues of national or other importance.[^cite_note-83]

Per [Article 78](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Constitution%20of%20India/Part%20V) of the [Constitution of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India), the union cabinet and the president officially communicate through the prime minister. Otherwise, the Constitution recognises the prime minister as a member of the union cabinet only outside the sphere of union cabinet.

#### Administrative and appointment powers

The prime minister recommends to the president, among others, names for the appointment of:

- [Chief Election Commissioner of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Election_Commissioner_of_India) (CEC) and other [Election Commissioners of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Election_Commissioners_of_India) (ECs)

- [Comptroller and Auditor General of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Comptroller_and_Auditor_General_of_India) (C\&AG)

- Chairperson and members of the [Union Public Service Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_Public_Service_Commission) (UPSC)

- [Chief Information Commissioner of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Information_Commissioner_of_India) (CIC) and [Information Commissioners of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Information_Commissioner_of_India)

- Chairperson and members of the [finance commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance_commission) (FC)

- [Attorney General of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attorney_General_of_India) (AG) and [Solicitor General of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solicitor_General_of_India) (SG)

As the chairperson of [Appointments Committee of the Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Appointments_Committee_of_the_Cabinet) (ACC), the prime minister, on the non-binding advice of the [Cabinet Secretary of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabinet_Secretary_of_India) led-Senior Selection Board (SSB), decides the postings of top civil servants, such as, [secretaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_to_Government_of_India), [additional secretaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Additional_secretary_to_Government_of_India) and [joint secretaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_secretary_to_Government_of_India) in the government of India.[^cite_note-Governance_in_India-84]<sup>: 3.16–3.17 </sup>[^cite_note-\:12-85][^cite_note-\:22-86] Further, in the same capacity, the PM decides the assignments of top military personnel such as the [Chief of the Army Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Army_Staff_\(India\)), [Chief of the Air Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Air_Staff_\(India\)), [Chief of the Naval Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_the_Naval_Staff_\(India\)) and commanders of operational and training commands.[^cite_note-87] In addition, the ACC also decides the posting of [Indian Police Service](451884.md) officers, the [All India Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_India_Services) for policing, which staffs most of the higher level law enforcement positions at federal and state level, in the [government of India](553883.md).

Also, as the [Minister of Personnel, Public Grievances and Pensions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Personnel,_Public_Grievances_and_Pensions), the PM also exercises control over the [Indian Administrative Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Administrative_Service) (IAS),[^cite_note-Governance_in_India-84]<sup>: 7.6 </sup> the country's premier civil service,[^cite_note-Profile_IAS-88][^cite_note-\:19-89] which staffs most of the senior civil service positions;[^cite_note-Profile_IAS-88][^cite_note-\:19-89] the Public Enterprises Selection Board (PESB);[^cite_note-Governance_in_India-84]<sup>: 7.37 </sup>[^cite_note-\:6-90] and the [Central Bureau of Investigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Bureau_of_Investigation) (CBI),[^cite_note-Governance_in_India-84]<sup>: 7.37 </sup>[^cite_note-\:6-90] except for the selection of its director, who is chosen by a committee of: (a) the prime minister, as chairperson; (b) the [leader of the opposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leader_of_the_Opposition_\(India\)) in Lok Sabha; and (c) the [chief justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India).[^cite_note-91]

Unlike most other countries, the prime minister does not have much influence over the selection of judges; that is done by a collegium of judges consisting of the [chief justice of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Justice_of_India), four senior most [judges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sitting_judges_of_the_Supreme_Court_of_India) of the [Supreme Court of India](395852.md) and the [chief justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_Indian_chief_justices) (or the senior-most judge) of the concerned [state high court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_high_courts_in_India).[^cite_note-Where_Angles_Fear_to_Tread-92][^cite_note-93] The executive as a whole, however, has the right to send back a recommended name to the collegium for reconsideration;[^cite_note-94] this, however, is not a full veto power, and the collegium can still put forward a rejected name.[^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

### Legislative powers

The prime minister acts as the leader of the house of the chamber of parliament that s/he belongs to, generally the [Lok Sabha](275456.md). In this role, the prime minister is tasked with representing the executive in the legislature, announces important legislation, and is further expected to respond to the opposition's concerns.[^cite_note-97] Article 85 of the [Indian constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_India) confers the president with the power to convene and end extraordinary sessions of the parliament; this power, however, is exercised only on the advice of the prime minister and their council; hence, in practice the prime minister does exercise some control over affairs of the parliament.

## Compensation and benefits

Article 75 of the Constitution of India confers the Parliament with the power to decide the remuneration and other benefits of the prime minister and other ministers are to be decided by the Parliament.[^cite_note-99] and is renewed from time to time. The original remunerations for the prime minister and other ministers were specified in the Part B of the second schedule of the constitution, which was later removed by an amendment.

In 2010, the Prime Minister's Office reported that the prime minister does not receive a formal salary; only monthly allowances.[^cite_note-100] That same year *[The Economist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Economist)* reported that, on a [purchasing power parity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Purchasing_power_parity) basis, the prime minister received an equivalent of \$4106 per year. As a percentage of the country's per-capita GDP (gross domestic product), this is the lowest of all countries *The Economist* surveyed.[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-salary-102]

### Residence



[PM Modi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PM_Modi) welcomes [JD Vance](53396477.md), 50th [Vice President of the United States](32759.md), and his family to 7, Race Course Road (2025)

The [7, Race Course Road](547114.md), officially called 7, Lok Kalyan Marg, in New Delhi, currently serves as the official place of residence for the prime minister of India.[^cite_note-103]

The residence during the tenure of the first prime minister, Jawaharlal Nehru, was [Teen Murti Bhavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teen_Murti_Bhavan). [Lal Bahadur Shastri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lal_Bahadur_Shastri) chose [10, Janpath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/10,_Janpath) as an official residence. [Indira Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indira_Gandhi) resided at [1, Safdarjung Road](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safdarjung_Road). [Rajiv Gandhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rajiv_Gandhi) became the first prime minister to use 7, Race Course Road as his residence, which was used by his successors.[^cite_note-104]

## Post-premiership

Former prime ministers are entitled to a bungalow,[^cite_note-\:7-112][^cite_note-\:8-113] and are also entitled the same facilities as those given to a serving cabinet minister,[^cite_note-\:7-112] including a fourteen-member secretarial staff, for a period of five years; reimbursement of office expenses; six domestic [executive-class](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Business_class) air tickets each year; and security cover from the armed forces and police as established by law.[^cite_note-\:7-112][^cite_note-\:8-113]

In addition, former prime ministers rank seventh on the [Indian order of precedence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_order_of_precedence), equivalent to [chief ministers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_Minister_\(India\)) of states (within their respective states) and cabinet ministers.[^cite_note-rajyasabha-114][^cite_note-OPR-8][^cite_note-115] As a former [member of the parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_\(India\)), the prime minister also receives a pension after he leaves office. In 2015, a former MP receives a minimum pension of [₹](490783.md)20,000 (US\$210) per month, plus, if he/she served as an MP for more than five years, [₹](490783.md)1,500 (US\$16) for every year served.[^cite_note-116]

Some prime ministers have had significant careers after their tenure, including [H. D. Deve Gowda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._D._Deve_Gowda), who continues to be a [member of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Member_of_Parliament_\(India\)).[^cite_note-117]

## Prime ministerial funds

The prime minister presides over various funds.

### National Defence Fund

Main article: [National Defence Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_Fund)

The [National Defence Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Defence_Fund) (NDF) was set up the Indian government in 1962, in the aftermath of [1962 Sino-Indian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Indian_War). The prime minister acts as chairperson of the fund's executive committee, while, the ministers of defence, finance and home act as the members of the executive committee, the finance minister also acts the treasurer of the committee. The secretary of the fund's executive committee is a [joint secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_secretary_to_Government_of_India) in the Prime Minister's Office, dealing with the subject of NDF.[^cite_note-119] The fund, according to its website, is "entirely dependent on voluntary contributions from the public and does not get any budgetary support.". Donations to the fund are 100% tax-deductible under section 80G of the *[Income Tax Act, 1961](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income_Tax_Act,_1961)*.[^cite_note-\:10-120][^cite_note-121]

### Prime Minister's National Relief Fund

The Prime Minister's National Relief Fund (PMNRF) was set up by the first prime minister of India, [Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru), in 1948, to assist displaced people from Pakistan. The fund, now, is primarily used to assist the families of those who are killed during natural disasters such as earthquakes, cyclones and flood and secondarily to reimburse medical expenses of people with chronic and deadly diseases.[^cite_note-122] Donations to the PMNRF are 100% tax-deductible under section 80G of the *Income Tax Act, 1961*.[^cite_note-\:10-120]

### Prime Minister's Citizen Assistance and Relief in Emergency Situations Fund (PM Cares Fund)

In March 2020, after the rapid spread of the [COVID-19](63030231.md) virus from Wuhan to countries across the world, Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md) announced the formation of a special fund to deal with any kind of emergency or distress situations like the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-123] The fund being fully voluntary without any government budgetary support, PM Modi appealed to the public to donate generously towards the fund. Following this donors from all sections of the society donated whole heartedly. Initially in FY 2019–20 the fund received Rs 3076.62 crores in just 5 days of its announcement. Initially the fund was used to procure COVID emergency products like ventilators, PPE kits for frontline workers and also funding the vaccine resaearch efforts and procurement. By FY 2020–21 the corpus of the [PM Cares Fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PM_CARES_Fund) was around Rs 10990 crore.[^cite_note-124]

## Deputy Prime Minister

Main article: [Deputy Prime Minister of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_India)

The post of [deputy prime minister of India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deputy_Prime_Minister_of_India) is not technically a constitutional post, nor is there any mention of it in an Act of Parliament.[^cite_note-\:2-125] But historically, on various occasions, different governments have assigned one of their senior ministers as the deputy prime minister. There is neither constitutional requirement for filling the post of deputy PM, nor does the post provide any kind of special powers.[^cite_note-\:2-125] Typically, senior cabinet ministers like the [finance minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Finance_\(India\)) or the [home minister](1974473.md) are appointed as deputy prime minister. The post is considered to be the senior most in the cabinet after the prime minister and represents the government in their absence. Typically, deputy prime ministers have been appointed to strengthen the coalition governments.

## Oath of office

The oath of office for [Prime Minister of India](24452.md) as follows:

> I, (name), do swear in the name of God (or, solemnly affirm) that I will bear true faith and allegiance to the Constitution of India as by law established, that I will uphold the sovereignty and integrity of India, that I will faithfully and conscientiously discharge my duties as a Prime Minister for the Union and that I will do right to all manner of people in accordance with the Constitution and the law, without fear or favour, affection or ill-will.
