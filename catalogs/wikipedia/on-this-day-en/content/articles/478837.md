The **Kyiv Pechersk Lavra**[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Києво-Печерська лавра, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Kyievo-Pecherska Lavra*), also known as the **Kyiv Monastery of the Caves**, is a historic [lavra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavra) or large [monastery](45856.md) of [Eastern Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Christianity) that gave its name to the [Pecherskyi District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pecherskyi_District) where it is located in [Kyiv](585629.md).

Since its foundation as a [cave monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cave_monastery) during the 11th century, the Lavra has been a preeminent center of Eastern Christianity in Eastern Europe.[^cite_note-3] Its printing house was an early part of the development of [printing in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Printing_in_Ukraine).

## Etymology and other names

[Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): печера, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *pechera* means lit. *cave*, which in turn derived from [Proto-Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Slavic) *\*реktera* with the same meaning. Ukrainian: лавра, romanized: *[lavra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavra)* is used to describe high-ranking male monasteries for monks of the [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md). Therefore, the name of the monastery is also translated as Kyiv Cave Monastery, Kyiv Caves Monastery or the Kyiv Monastery of the Caves (from *Ukrainian: на печерах*).

## History

### Foundation and early history



Icon of Saints Anthony and [Theodosius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodosius_of_Kiev), founders of Kyiv Pechersk Lavra

The *[Primary Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_Chronicle)* contains contradictory information as to when the monastery was founded: in 1051, or in 1074.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTECrossSherbowitz-Wetzor19537-4] [Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_of_Kiev), a [monk](419369.md) on [Mount Athos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Athos), originally from [Liubech](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liubech) of the [Principality of Chernigov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Chernigov), returned to [Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kievan_Rus') and settled in Kyiv as a [missionary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missionary) of monastic tradition to Kyivan Rus'. He chose a cave at the [Berestov Mount](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berestove) that overlooked the [Dnipro river](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dnipro_river), where metropolitan [Hilarion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hilarion_of_Kiev) had reportedly resided during his youth,[^cite_note-ency-5] and a community of disciples soon grew. [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knyaz) [Iziaslav I of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iziaslav_I_of_Kiev) (1024–1078) ceded the whole mount to the Anthonite monks who founded a monastery built by architects from [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople).

Supported by numerous princes and [boyars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boyars), the monastery served as the most important centre of religion and culture in Rus'. Its monks produced important works of literature, including the *[Primary Chronicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_Chronicle)* and *[Kyiv Caves Patericon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Caves_Patericon)*. It also housed renowned builders and artists, and the monastery life inspired numerous tales and legends. At the same time, it suffered from numerous attacks: in 1096 the monastery was plundered by the [Cumans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumans), and in 1169 sacked by prince [Andrey Bogolyubsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Bogolyubsky). In 1203 it was once again plundered by forces of prince [Rurik Rostislavich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rurik_Rostislavich) and his allies. In 1240 the monastery fell victim to [Mongol invaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Kiev_\(1240\)) commanded by [Batu Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batu_Khan), and in 1416 was burned down by forces of [Golden Horde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Horde) ruler [Edigey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edigu). After being rebuilt by prince [Simeon Olelkovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simeon_Olelkovich) in 1470, it was once again burned by the [Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tatars) in 1482.[^cite_note-meta-6][^cite_note-ency-5]

At the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra were buried some high-importance personalities from the period when Kyiv was a part of the [Grand Duchy of Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Lithuania): Prince of Kyiv [Vladimir Olgerdovich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Olgerdovich) and his son [Aleksandras Olelka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleksandras_Olelka), the Lithuanian and Ruthenian Grand Duke [Švitrigaila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0vitrigaila), [Feodor Ostrogski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feodor_Ostrogski), [Uliana Olshanska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uliana_Olshanska) (a second wife of the Lithuanian Grand Duke [Vytautas the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vytautas_the_Great)), and the Lithuanian Grand Hetman [Konstanty Ostrogski](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstanty_Ostrogski), known for commanding the [Grand Ducal Lithuanian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Ducal_Lithuanian_Army) in the victorious [Battle of Orsha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Orsha) (1514) versus the [Grand Principality of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Moscow) Army.[^cite_note-Vkpk-7] [Mayors of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Kyiv), members of the [szlachta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Szlachta) and [Cossack starshyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_starshyna), as well as church hierarchs also found their burial place in the monastery.[^cite_note-meta-6]

### Baroque era and Russian rule



The [Near Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_Caves_\(Pechersk_Lavra\)) of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra. Sketch by the Dutch artist Abraham van Westerveld made in 1651

Following reconstruction, during the second half of 16th century the monastery received rights of [stauropegion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stauropegion) from the [Patriarch of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Constantinople), and started bearing the name of Lavra. During that period, six separate monasteries were united under its leadership. After the [Union of Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Brest), the monastery became an object of dispute between the [Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox) and [Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic) communities. In 1613, the Orthodox established their control after receiving support of the princely [Ostrogski family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostrogski_family).[^cite_note-ency-5]

In the 17th century, under the leadership of [archimandrites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archimandrite) [Eliseus Pletenetsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yelysei_Pletenetskyi), [Zacharias Kopystensky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zacharias_Kopystensky) and [Peter Mohyla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_Mohyla), the monastery stood at the heart of Ukrainian national identity. [Kyiv Caves Patericon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Caves_Patericon), which was created by Lavra's monks and soon became a popular reading around the whole [Eastern Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Europe), contributed to the emergence of the symbolic image of Kyiv as a capital of [Eastern Orthodoxy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodoxy). Lavra's printing house, established by Pletenetskyi in the 1620s, started the process of Kyiv's cultural revival, and the monastery's school, founded by Mohyla, introduced European educational trends of the time, leading to a radical reform of education. During the [Baroque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baroque) era Kyiv Pechersk Lavra flourished as a centre of arts and spirituality, and pilgrimage to Kyiv was seen by some as more preferable than visiting [Jerusalem](16043.md).[^cite_note-meta-6]

According to a legend published by [polemicist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polemicist) and preacher [Joannicius Galiatovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joannicius_Galiatovsky), in 1630 the monastery was besieged by a Polish army, but the [Holy Mother of God](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virgin_Mary) protected its monks by turning a "fiery rain" against the invaders.[^cite_note-8] In 1631 a school was established at the monastery on the base of Western educational establishments; it would eventually formed with the [Brotherhood Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brotherhood_Monastery) school, creating the [Kyiv Mohyla Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Mohyla_Academy).[^cite_note-ency-5]

Despite the patronage of powerful figures, including [Ivan Mazepa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Mazepa) and [Raphael Zaborovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raphael_Zaborovsky), the [Annexation of the Metropolis of Kyiv by the Moscow Patriarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_the_Metropolis_of_Kyiv_by_the_Moscow_Patriarchate) in 1685 started a process of subjugation of the monastery to [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire) imperial authority.[^cite_note-moly-10] In 1688 Lavra became a stauropegion of the [Patriarch of Moscow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Moscow). In 1718, a major fire damaged many of the monastery's buildings, destroying its [library](17727.md) and [archive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archive), and in 1720 its printing house was subjected to strict government [censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Censorship).[^cite_note-ency-5] In 1722, by the decree of [Peter I of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_I_of_Russia), the Metropolis of Kyiv was lowered in status to an [archbishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop), which made it equal to other subdivisions of the Russian [Synodal Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Most_Holy_Synod). In the following years, Russian religious traditions, [axiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axiology) and language were imposed on the [Orthodox Church in Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Christianity_in_Ukraine).[^cite_note-moly-10] In 1786 the monastery's lands were [secularized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secularization_\(church_property\)), and its elected leadership was replaced with the metropolitan's appointees.[^cite_note-ency-5]

Under the Russian rule, Pechersk Lavra became a popular place of mass pilgrimage for both the common folk and figures of authority, including the royal family. During the late 19th century numerous guides for pilgrims visiting the monastery were published in Tsarist Russia, contributing to its inclusion into the empire's symbolic space. Among prominent figures buried in Lavra's walls under the Russian rule are [Natalia Dolgorukova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natalia_Dolgorukova), [Pyotr Rumyantsev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Rumyantsev) and [Pyotr Stolypin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyotr_Stolypin).[^cite_note-moly-10][^cite_note-meta-6] During the 19th and early 20th centuries, the monastery also served as the residence of [Metropolitans of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_of_Kyiv), and was known for producing [myrrh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myrrh) for the whole Russian Empire. By the time of [revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_1917), the Lavra housed over 1200 monks.[^cite_note-ency-5]

### Modern history



Ruins of the Dormition Cathedral in 1958

During the [Ukrainian Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_War_of_Independence) of the early 20th century attempts to [Ukrainize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainization) the Lavra failed due to political instability.[^cite_note-moly-10] On 25 January 1918 Metropolitan [Vladimir of Kiev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Bogoyavlensky) was tortured and murdered in the monastery by [Bolshevik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik) troops. In 1921-1922 many valuable objects were confiscated from the monastery by [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Ukraine) authorities.[^cite_note-ency-5] Eventually, the monastery was disbanded, and in 1926 a museum was opened on its territory.[^cite_note-meta-6] In 1934, the museum complex was disbanded, and its artifacts distributed among various institutions.[^cite_note-ency-5] Under [German occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ukraine) religion services in the monastery were resumed.[^cite_note-moly-10]

On 3 November 1941 the main [Dormition Cathedral](78734654.md) was blown up by Soviet [NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD);[^cite_note-ki-2026-11] Soviet press would falsely accuse the Germans of committing that act. The demolition of the cathedral's ruins continued into the 1960s. After a long period of reconstruction, on 24 August 2000 the reconstructed Dormition Cathedral was solemnly reopened.[^cite_note-meta-6]

Starting from the end of the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the monastery resumed its activities as part of the Russian Orthodox Church. Over 100 monks lived on Lavra's premises until its next closure by the authorities in 1961.[^cite_note-mon-12]

In 1988 activities of Kyiv Pechersk Lavra were renewed as part of celebrations dedicated to the 1000th anniversary of the [Christianization of Kievan Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christianization_of_Kievan_Rus'). During the early 1990s the monastery was headed by metropolitan [Filaret of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filaret_Denysenko), whose residence was located on its premises. However, in 1992 ownership over the Lavra was transferred to the [Ukrainian Orthodox Church (Moscow Patriarchate)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Orthodox_Church_\(Moscow_Patriarchate\)) with the support of [Kyiv's political leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_City_State_Administration).[^cite_note-moly-10] Under the management of the Moscow Patriarchate Lavra became an epicentre of several scandals connected with its leadership's love for expensive cars and other attributes of wealth, as well as its monks' connections to Russian [FSB](385990.md), veneration of Tsar [Nicholas II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_II_of_Russia) and spread of [anti-Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Ukrainian_sentiment) propaganda.[^cite_note-meta-6]



A panorama of the monastery, southward view

Together with the [Saint Sophia Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Sophia_Cathedral_in_Kyiv), the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra has been inscribed as a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md) since 1990.[^cite_note-Kyiv_Pechersk_Lavra,_St._Sophia_Cathedral_remain_on_UNESCO's_World_Heritage_List-13][^cite_note-15] The monastery complex is considered a separate national historic-cultural preserve (sanctuary), the national status to which was granted on 13 March 1996.[^cite_note-16] The Lavra is not only located in another part of the city, but is part of a different national sanctuary than Saint Sophia Cathedral. While being a cultural attraction, the monastery is once again active, with over 100 monks in residence. It was named one of the [Seven Wonders of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seven_Wonders_of_Ukraine) on 21 August 2007.

Until the end of 2022, jurisdiction over the site had been divided between the state museum, National Kyiv-Pechersk Historic-Cultural Preserve,[^cite_note-17] and the Ukrainian Orthodox Church (Moscow Patriarchate) (UOC-MP) as the site of the chief monastery of that Church and the residence of its leader, [Onufrius, Metropolitan of Kyiv and All Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onufriy_\(Berezovsky\)).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] In January 2023, the Ukrainian government terminated the UOC-MP's lease of the Dormition Cathedral and the Refectory Church (also known as the Trapezna Church), returning those properties to direct state control.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-\:0-21] It also announced that the [Orthodox Church of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orthodox_Church_of_Ukraine) (OCU) had been granted permission to celebrate a Christmas service in the Dormition Cathedral, on 7 January 2023, Orthodox Christmas by the Old Calendar,[^cite_note-\:0-21] a service which was celebrated by Metropolitan [Epiphanius of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Kyiv) at 9am that day.[^cite_note-22]



The restored Cathedral of the Dormition, in 2005

On 10 March 2023, the National Kyiv-Pechersk Historic-Cultural Preserve announced that the 2013 agreement on the free use of churches by the UOC-MP would be terminated on the grounds that the church had violated their lease by making alterations to the historic site, and other technical infractions.[^cite_note-PBSNewsHourOrthodox_leader-23][^cite_note-7393889PavloLebid-24] The UOC-MP was ordered to leave the territory by 29 March.[^cite_note-7393889PavloLebid-24] The UOC-MP answered back that there were no legal grounds for the eviction and called it "a whim of officials from the [Ministry of Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Culture_and_Information_Policy)."[^cite_note-7393889PavloLebid-24] On 17 March 2023 [Dmitry Peskov](9214683.md), the [press secretary](456013.md) for Russian President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md), stated that the decision of the Ukrainian authorities not to extend this lease to representatives of the UOC-MP "confirms the correctness" of the [(24 February 2022) Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-7393889PavloLebid-24] The UOC-MP did not fully leave Kyiv Pechersk Lavra following 29 March 2023.[^cite_note-epiphany-urges-calm-25][^cite_note-550462lavra_shcho_dali-26]

On 23 July 2025, a religious service in the [Ukrainian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language), the first of its kind in many years, was performed in the Far Caves of Kyiv Pechersk Lavra by Metropolitan Epiphanius of Kyiv.[^cite_note-moly-10]

#### 2026 drone attacks



Cathedral on fire, 15 June 2026



The cathedral roof after the attack

On 24 January 2026, a Russian drone attack shattered windows and damaged two buildings of the complex, marking the first time it sustained war-related damage since [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-27]

On 14 June 2026, a Russian drone attack caused significant damage to the complex, as part of a targeted attack that also damaged other cultural elements in Kyiv.[^cite_note-Guardian_video-28][^cite_note-Beaumont-29] The damage, likely from [Geran-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geran-2) drones ("the Russian version of the Iranian Shahed-type kamikaze drone"[^cite_note-WaPoKyivMonastery2026-30]), included destruction of a significant portion of the roof, damage to the main dome and cupola, and significant fires within the structure of the [Dormition Cathedral](78734654.md).[^cite_note-ki-2026-11] This was the first time the structure was targeted in an armed conflict since World War II. The [Security Service of Ukraine](709222.md) published photos of debris from the Geran-2 drone.[^cite_note-WaPoKyivMonastery2026-30] By 15 June, the remains of two Shahed-type drones had been found at the UNESCO World Heritage site by state security officials, suggesting at least two drones targeted the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra complex around 4\:55 a.m. Kyiv time.[^cite_note-Beaumont-29] Russia denied responsibility and blamed a US-made Patriot air-defence missile,[^cite_note-Beaumont-29][^cite_note-NOS-31] but the Kremlin spokesperson offered no evidence of this claim,[^cite_note-NOS-31] and no explanation why the remains of Russian-made Shahed-type Geran-2 drones were found at the site instead.[^cite_note-Beaumont-29]

Prime Minister [Yulia Svyrydenko](69191807.md) of Ukraine posted a photo of the burning monastery and wrote: "A brutal assault on our people ‌and our heritage. This is the true face of Russia’s Orthodox values. We ask for prayers for the salvation of the shrine from destruction. Another Russian crime against humanity, against history, against Christianity."[^cite_note-Beaumont-29] Ukraine's President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) called it "one of Russia’s most serious crimes against Christian culture to date",[^cite_note-Beaumont-29][^cite_note-NOS-31] adding: "It is vital that the world does not remain silent in the face of this latest act of Russian barbarism."[^cite_note-NOS-31] The attack on the Lavra was widely condemned by European leaders, with [European Commission](9974.md) President [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) calling it "pointless violence": 'Russia has once again shown that it is interested only in violence and destruction.'[^cite_note-NOS-31] French President [Emmanuel Macron](43671127.md) stated that "nothing justifies this attack on our universal heritage,"[^cite_note-NOS-31] with France's European and Foreign Minister [Jean-Noël Barrot](54388162.md) adding: "It is as if [Notre-Dame](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Notre-Dame_de_Paris) or the [Cathedral of Saint-Denis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_Saint-Denis) were to be bombed in France."[^cite_note-NOS-31][^cite_note-Beaumont-29] EU Foreign Chief [Kaja Kallas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaja_Kallas) issued a statement qualifying the attacks on the monastery, just like attacks on Ukrainian civilians, as [war crimes](48626.md), announcing another round of sanctions against Russia.[^cite_note-NOS-31] The [Romanian Patriarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Patriarchate) expressed "deep concern and sadness" regarding the attack that "represents a loss for all of Christianity and for the universal heritage of humanity".[^cite_note-32]

The [Ecumenical Patriarchate of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarchate_of_Constantinople) issued an official statement strongly condemning the attack, characterizing it as an "unacceptable and profane" assault against a sacred monument of Christianity's shared cultural heritage, noting that "no rational person and no argument can justify this barbaric destructive attack".[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] On 15 June 2026, [Ecumenical Patriarch Bartholomew Ι](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartholomew_I_of_Constantinople) held telephone communications with both [Metropolitan Epiphanius of Kyiv and All Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphanius_of_Kyiv) and Ukraine's President [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md). During these calls, the Ecumenical Patriarch expressed his abhorrence regarding the "act of barbarism and disrespect," conveying the complete support and solidarity of the Mother Church of Constantinople to the war-torn Ukrainian people, while also offering his condolences to the families of the victims and the injured.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

## Buildings and structures



Pechersk Lavra, Kyiv, in 1889, [Department of Image Collections](https://www.nga.gov/research/library/imagecollections/features/watsons_russia.html), [National Gallery of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Art) Library, Washington, D.C.

The Kyiv Pechersk Lavra contains numerous architectural monuments, ranging from [bell towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_tower) to cathedrals to cave systems and to strong stone [fortification walls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavra_fortification). The main attractions of the Lavra include the [Great Lavra Belltower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lavra_Belltower), and the [Dormition Cathedral](78734654.md), destroyed in fighting the Germans [World War II](32927.md), and fully reconstructed in the 1990s after the fall of Soviet Union by Ukraine.

Other churches and cathedrals of the Lavra include: the [Refectory Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refectory_Church_\(Pechersk_Lavra\)), the Church of All Saints, the [Church of the Saviour at Berestove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Saviour_at_Berestove), the Church of the Exaltation of Cross, the Church of the Trinity, the Church of the Nativity of the Virgin, the Church of the Conception of St. Anne, and the Church of the Life-Giving Spring. The Lavra also contains many other constructions, including: the St. Nicholas Monastery, the Kyiv Theological Academy and Seminary, and the Debosquette Wall.

### Great Lavra Belltower



Up-close view of the [Great Lavra Belltower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lavra_Belltower) with its four tiers in 2005

Main article: [Great Lavra Belltower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Lavra_Belltower)

The Great Lavra Belltower is one of the most notable features of the Kyiv skyline and among the main attractions of the Lavra. 96.5 meters in height, it was the tallest free-standing belltower at the time of its construction in 1731–1745, and was designed by the architect [Johann Gottfried Schädel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gottfried_Johann_Sch%C3%A4del). It is a [Classical style](52687.md) construction and consists of [tiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storey), surmounted by a [gilded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilding) [dome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dome).

### Dormition Cathedral



The restored Dormition Cathedral

Main article: [Dormition Cathedral, Kyiv Pechersk Lavra](78734654.md)

Built in the 11th century, the main church of the monastery was destroyed during the [World War II](32927.md), a couple of months after the Nazi Germany troops occupied the city of [Kyiv](585629.md), during which the Soviet Union conducted the controversial [1941 Khreshchatyk explosions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1941_Khreshchatyk_explosions). Withdrawing Soviet troops practiced the tactics of [scorched earth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scorched_earth) and blew up all the Kyiv bridges over [Dnieper](380138.md) as well as the main Khreshchatyk street and Kyiv Pechersk Lavra.[^cite_note-\:1-50] The destruction of the cathedral followed a pattern of Soviet disregard for cultural heritage, as they previously blew up the ancient [St. Michael's Golden-Domed Monastery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Michael's_Golden-Domed_Monastery) nearby in the 1930s.[^cite_note-51]

In 1928, the monastery was converted into an anti-religious museum park by the Soviet authorities and after their return no efforts were provided to restore the church. The temple was finally restored in 1995 after Ukraine obtained its independence and the construction was accomplished in two years. The new Dormition Church was consecrated in 2000.[^cite_note-\:1-50]

### Gate Church of the Trinity

Main article: [Gate Church of the Trinity, Kyiv Pechersk Lavra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gate_Church_of_the_Trinity,_Kyiv_Pechersk_Lavra)

The Gate Church of the Trinity is located atop the Holy Gates, which houses the entrance to the monastery. According to a legend, this church was founded by the [Chernihiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernihiv) [Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rulers_of_Kievan_Rus') [Sviatoslav II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatoslav_II_of_Kiev). It was built atop an [ancient stone church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Kievan_Rus') which used to stand in its place. After the fire of 1718, the church was rebuilt, its revered facades and interior walls enriched with ornate stucco work made by craftsman V. Stefaovych. In the 18th century, a new gilded pear-shaped dome was built, the facade and exterior walls were decorated with stucco-moulded plant ornaments and a vestibule built of stone attached to the north end. In the early 20th century, the fronts and the walls flanking the entrance were painted by icon painters under the guidance of V. Sonin. The interior of the Gate Trinity Church contains murals by the early 18th century painter Alimpy Galik.

### Refectory chambers with Church of the Saints Anthony and Theodosius

Main article: [Refectory Church, Kyiv Pechersk Lavra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refectory_Church,_Kyiv_Pechersk_Lavra)



Refectory Church

The refectory chambers with the Church of the Saints Anthony and Theodosius is the third in a series of temples. The original temple was built in the 12th century and no drawings or visual depictions of it remain. The second temple was built at the time of the [Cossack Hetmanate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate) and was disassembled by the Russian authorities in the 19th century. It was replaced with the current temple, often referred to as the Refectory Church of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra.

### The All Saints Church

The All Saints Church, erected in 1696–1698, is a fine specimen of Ukrainian baroque architecture. Characteristic of the church facades are rich architectural embellishments. In 1905, students of the Lavra art school painted the interior walls of the church. The carved wooden iconostasis is multi-tiered and was made for the All Saints church in the early 18th century.

### Church of the Saviour at Berestove

Main article: [Church of the Saviour at Berestove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Saviour_at_Berestove)



Side view of the [Church of the Saviour at Berestove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Saviour_at_Berestove) seen with its [campanile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campanile), designed by architect Andrei Melenskyi in the [Classical style](52687.md).

The Church of the Saviour at Berestove is located to the North of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra. It was constructed in the village of Berestove around the start of the 11th century during the reign of Prince [Vladimir Monomakh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_II_Monomakh). It later served as the mausoleum of the Monomakh dynasty, also including [Yuri Dolgoruki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuri_Dolgoruki), the founder of Moscow. Despite being outside the Lavra fortifications, the Church of the Saviour at Berestove is part of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra complex.

### Caves

Main article: [Near Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_Caves)

The Kyiv Pechersk Lavra caverns are a system of narrow underground corridors (about 1-1½ metres wide and 2-2½ metres high), along with numerous living quarters and underground chapels. In 1051, the monk [Anthony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_of_Kiev) settled in an old cave in a hill near the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra. This cave received additions including corridors and a church, and is now the [Far Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Caves?action=edit\&redlink=1). In 1057, Anthony moved to a cave near the Upper Lavra, now called the [Near Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Near_Caves).

Foreign travellers in the 16th–17th centuries wrote that the catacombs of the Lavra stretched for hundreds of kilometres, reaching as far as Moscow and [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velikiy_Novgorod),[^cite_note-Touring_Kyiv-52] spreading awareness of the Kyiv Pechersk Lavra.

### Library

The notable library of the Lavra was burned during the reign of [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great). On the night of April 21–22, 1718, Orthodox monks — tsarist agents — set fire to the premises of the Lavra, where its library and archive with unique documents and books from the historical past of Ukraine were located.

In 1988, after the restoration of the monastery's activities, library work was resumed. The funds began to be replenished with those publications that the Lavra monks and parishioners managed to save. New books began to be purchased, and some of the books that began to be published by the Lavra printing house restored in 1995 were transferred to the library.

Over 20 years of activity after the revival of the monastery, more than 10 thousand volumes were collected. In 2008, the library was moved to premises that allow the best placement and organization of library funds. Accounting and cataloging of the Lavra library funds were digitized.

## Museum



Eastern Orthodox pilgrim in Kyiv Pechersk Lavra – women must cover their head when entering the lavra.

The Kyiv Pechersk Lavra is one of the largest museums in Kyiv. The exposition is the actual ensemble of the Upper (Near Caves) and Lower ([Far Caves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far_Caves?action=edit\&redlink=1)) Lavra territories, which house many architectural relics of the past. The collection within the churches and caves includes articles of precious metal, prints, higher clergy portraits and rare church hierarchy photographs.[^cite_note-Kyiv_Sightseeing_Guide-54] The main exposition contains articles from 16th to early 20th centuries, which include [chalices](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chalice_\(cup\)), [crucifixes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crucifix), and textiles from 16th–19th centuries, with needlework and [embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery) of Ukrainian masters. The remainder of the collection consists of pieces from the Lavra's Printing House and the Lavra's Icon Painting Workshop.[^cite_note-Kyiv_Sightseeing_Guide-54]

The museum provides tours of the [catacombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catacombs), which contain remains of Eastern Orthodox saints or their relics. The Caves are of geological interest because they are excavated into [loess](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loess) ground. They form one of the most extensive occurrences of loess caves in the world.
