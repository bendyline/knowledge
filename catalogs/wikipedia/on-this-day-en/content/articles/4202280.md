**Damascus International Airport** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): مَطَار دِمَشْق الدَّوْلِيّ, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Arabic): *Maṭār Dimašq al-Duwalī*; [IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **DAM**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **OSDI**) is the [international airport](1519705.md) of [Damascus](8914.md), the capital of [Syria](7515849.md) and the home base of the national flag carrier airline, [Syrian Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Air). Damascus International Airport serves as a primary gateway to Syria and is one of the largest airports in the country along with [Aleppo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleppo_International_Airport) and [Latakia International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latakia_International_Airport). Inaugurated in the mid-1970s, it also is the country's busiest airport.[^cite_note-2]

## History



[Jawaharlal Nehru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jawaharlal_Nehru) at Damascus Airport

### Establishment and early years

In 1965, the construction of the airport was entrusted to a group of French companies ([SCB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soci%C3%A9t%C3%A9_de_construction_des_Batignolles), [CSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Compagnie_g%C3%A9n%C3%A9rale_de_la_t%C3%A9l%C3%A9graphie_sans_fil), [Spie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SPIE_SA?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Cegelec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cegelec)), led by the SCB.[^cite_note-3] Damascus Airport was opened in the late 1960s, replacing the old Mazzeh military airport that previously served as the city's main aviation hub. The airport was built to accommodate the growing air traffic and to enhance Syria's connectivity with the rest of the world. In the 1980s, the airport was served by over 30 airlines and had nonstop flights to destinations in Europe, North Africa, the Middle East, and South Asia.[^cite_note-4] It was also a stop-over on a [Pakistan International Airlines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_International_Airlines) route from [Karachi](17123.md) and [Islamabad](51153.md) to [New York](645042.md) and [Toronto](64646.md).[^cite_note-5]

In the following years, Damascus Airport underwent several expansion and modernisation projects in order to meet the increasing demands of international and domestic travellers.[^cite_note-6] New terminals, runways, and facilities were constructed to enhance passenger services and improve operational efficiency. In March 2007, [Iran Air](168635.md) inaugurated a flight from [Tehran](57654.md) to [Caracas](55289.md) via Damascus.[^cite_note-7] Its [codeshare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Codeshare) partner [Conviasa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conviasa) took over the route seven months later.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9]

### Civil war

With the onset of the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md), the airport and approach roads were closed intermittently and most international airlines ceased flights.[^cite_note-10] Conviasa ended its direct service to Caracas in August 2012.[^cite_note-11]

In June 2022, Damascus International Airport suffered major damage, including to runways, following an [Israeli](9282173.md) missile attack, targeting alleged [Iranian weapons transfers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_involvement_in_the_Syrian_civil_war).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] Flights were halted to and from the airport for two weeks due to the extensive damage to infrastructure.[^cite_note-14] On 2 January 2023, Damascus International Airport temporarily went out of service after another Israeli missile strike, which killed at least 2 soldiers, before returning to operation 7 hours later after the damage was repaired.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] It is announced that Syrian Airlines plans to establish a joint venture with a private company to invest, manage, and operate its activities and those of the airport.[^cite_note-18]

On 12 October 2023, Damascus International Airport was temporarily closed due to a damaged runway following Israeli missile attacks on both it and [Aleppo International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aleppo_International_Airport), during the [skirmishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2023_Israel%E2%80%93Syria_exchanges) which occurred across the border in connection with the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-19] The airport was put back into service on 18 October.[^cite_note-20] On 22 October, both Aleppo and Damascus airports were hit simultaneously, putting them out of service for the second time within two weeks.[^cite_note-21] Two workers were killed during the attack.[^cite_note-22] On 26 November 2023, hours after the airport was put back into service from the previous attack, Israel targeted the airport with missiles, causing material losses and putting the airport out of service again.[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

### Post-Assad regime



Aerial view



Air traffic control tower

The airport was closed during the [opposition offensive on Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Damascus_\(2024\)) on 8 December 2024.[^cite_note-25] On 18 December 2024, commercial operations resumed with the first flight taking off with 32 passengers heading to [Aleppo](159244.md).[^cite_note-26] On 4 January 2025, the [Syrian transitional government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_caretaker_government) announced that international flights would resume from 7 January, for the first time since the [fall of the Assad regime](78548066.md).[^cite_note-27] Services would later resume on 7 January, with the arrival of a [Qatar Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qatar_Airways) flight from [Doha](26214389.md).[^cite_note-28] Flights to some destinations remain suspended, although they have been progressively reinstated thanks to work done by the Syrian government[^cite_note-29]

In February 2025, with the help of the Turkish government, the airport began its renovation.[^cite_note-30] The General Authority of Civil Aviation signed a \$4 billion investment to refurbish the airport, including the construction of a new 5-star hotel.[^cite_note-31]

In January 2026, Turkey installed a new radar system to modernize civil aviation infrastructure, improve safety standards, and enhance the efficiency of airspace management around the airport.[^cite_note-32]

## Infrastructure

### Airfield

The current [runways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runways) allow the landing of virtually all types of aircraft currently in use (including [Airbus A380](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A380), [Boeing 787 Dreamliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_787_Dreamliner) and [Boeing 747-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_747-8)). The airport has two parallel runways (05R/23L and 05L/23R), both 3,600 metres (11,800 ft) in length, which were completely renovated in the 2010s.[^cite_note-33]

### Terminals

The airport is of [Islamic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture), and has two [terminals](423849.md), one for [international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_flight) flights and the other for [domestic](30208174.md) flights. The airport features two duty-free outlets. The departures hall also includes an in-house [coffee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coffee) shop, several [souvenir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Souvenir) shops, three [restaurants](26437.md), and a lounge for first and business class passengers.[^cite_note-34] The southern part of the airport has [hardened aircraft shelters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardened_aircraft_shelter) and artillery revetments.[^cite_note-35] The second terminal have a capacity to handle 25 million passengers.[^cite_note-36]

On 4 August 2025, Syria and [Qatar](8238258.md) signed a US\$4 billion agreement for the construction of a third passenger terminal. The project is funded by Qatar's [UCC Holding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCC_Holding) and aims to expand the airport's capacity and modernize its infrastructure. Its geometrical shape takes inspiration from the Damascus sword. Terminal 3 will be built adjacent to the current Terminal 1, utilizing the existing runway and apron infrastructure.

The third terminal is intended to increase the capacity of the airport to 31 million passengers per year.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

## Ground transportation

### Motorway

Located 30 kilometers (20 miles) southeast of [Damascus](8914.md), the airport is in the governate of [Rif Dimashq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rif_Dimashq). The facility is connected to the city by the [M5 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M5_motorway_\(Syria\)).

### Bus

A shuttle bus runs between the city center and the airport.[^cite_note-83][^cite_note-84] The bus ride takes approximately 30 minutes.[^cite_note-85]

[Royal Jordanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Jordanian) operates ground bus services from Damascus International Airport to Amman's [Queen Alia International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Alia_International_Airport).[^cite_note-86]

### Railway

The building of a [railway line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Syria) and a terminal [bus station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bus_station) with a [shopping center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shopping_center) at the airport is planned to connect it to the [Hejaz station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hejaz_Railway_Station).[^cite_note-87]

## Accidents and incidents

- On 20 August 1975, [ČSA Flight 540](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C4%8CSA_Flight_540) crashed while on approach to Damascus International Airport. Out of the 128 passengers and crew on board, there were only two survivors.

  [^cite_note-88]

- On 20 September 2012, Syrian Arab Airlines Flight 501, operated by an [Airbus A320-232](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbus_A320-232), was struck by a [Syrian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrian_Air_Force) [Mil Mi-17](1493374.md) helicopter, with the helicopter's rotor removing half of the airliner's vertical tail. The airliner successfully landed back at the airport, while the helicopter crashed, killing four crew members.

  [^cite_note-89]
