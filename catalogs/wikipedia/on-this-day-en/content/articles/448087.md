**Qazvin province** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): استان قزوین; IPA: [\[ɢæzˈviːn\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Persian) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/ec/Qazvin.ogg/Qazvin.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/e/ec/Qazvin.ogg/Qazvin.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>)[^cite_note-7] is one of the 31 [provinces](448160.md) of Iran. It is in the northwest of the country, with the city of [Qazvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazvin) as its capital.[^cite_note-Qazvin_Province_Establishment_Law-4]

The province was carved out of [Tehran](202698.md) and [Zanjan](448108.md) provinces in 1997.[^cite_note-Qazvin_Province_Establishment_Law-4] The province was made a part of [Region 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Region_1_\(Iran\)) upon the division of the provinces into [5 regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Iran) solely for coordination and development purposes on June 22, 2014.[^cite_note-hamshahrionline.ir-3]

## History

Qazvin was the location of a former capital of the [Persian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Empire) and contains over 2000 architectural and archeological sites. It is a provincial capital today that has been a cultural center of mass throughout history.

Archeological findings in the Qazvin plain reveal the existence of urban agricultural settlements as far back as 7000 BC. Significant ancient settlements in the area include [Tepe Sagzabad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepe_Sagzabad), [Tepe Ghabristan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tepe_Ghabristan), and [Teppe Zagheh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teppe_Zagheh).

The name "Qazvin" or "Kasbin" is derived from Cas, an ancient tribe that lived south of the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) millennia ago. The Caspian Sea itself in fact derives its name from the same origin. Qazvin geographically connects Tehran, Isfahan, and the [Persian Gulf](24761.md) to the Caspian seacoast and [Asia Minor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia_Minor), hence its strategic location throughout the ages.

Qazvin has been a hotbed of historical developments in Iranian history. In the early years of the Islamic era Qazvin served as a base for the Muslim forces. Destroyed by [Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genghis_Khan) (13th century), the [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavids) monarchs made Qazvin the capital of the Safavid empire in 1548 only to have it moved to [Isfahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isfahan_\(city\)) in 1598. During the Qajar dynasty and contemporary period, Qazvin has always been one of the most important governmental centers due to its proximity to Tehran. [Abbas Mirza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbas_Mirza), a [Crown Prince](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crown_Prince) and Minister of Commerce, was also the governor of Qazvin.

Qazvin is situated close to [Alamut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamut), where the famous [Hasan-i Sabbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hasan-i_Sabbah), founder of the secret [Ismaili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ismaili) order of the [Assassins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashshashin), operated from.

Qazvin is where the coup d'état of General [Reza Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reza_Khan), with his [Russian](25391.md)-trained [Cossack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack) brigade, was launched from – which led to the founding of the [Pahlavi dynasty](338883.md) in 1921.

[1962 Buin Zahra earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1962_Buin_Zahra_earthquake) killed 12.225 people.

## Demographics

### Language and ethnicity

The majority of people in the northeast of the province, in [Alamut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamut), are [Mazandarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandarani_people) or [Gilaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilaki_people) who speak a dialect of the [Mazandarani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandarani_language)[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] or [Gilaki language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilaki_language).[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Other sources say that the majority of people in Alamut are [Tats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tat_people_\(Iran\)).[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

### Population

At the time of the 2006 National Census, the province's population was 1,127,734 in 294,305 households.[^cite_note-2006_Qazvin_Province-15] The following census in 2011 counted 1,201,565 inhabitants living in 352,472 households.[^cite_note-2011_Qazvin_Province-16] The 2016 census measured the population of the province as 1,273,761 people in 397,165 households.[^cite_note-2016_Qazvin_Province-5]

Barajin

The province covers 15821 km<sup>2</sup> between 48–45 to 50–50 east of Greenwich Meridian of longitude and 35–37 to 36–45 north latitude of the equator. The province is bounded on the north by [Mazandaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran) and [Gilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilan), on the west by [Hamedan](43610555.md) and [Zanjan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanjan_Province), on the south by [Markazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Markazi_Province) and on the east by [Tehran Provinces](202698.md).  The famous mountains of the province are those of Siälän, Shäh Alborz, Khashchäl, Sephidkouh, Shojä e din, Alehtareh, Rämand, Ägh dägh, Kharaghän, Saridagh, Soltan pïr, and Siähkouh, in which Siälän with a height of 4,175m and Shäh Alborz which is 4,056m are the highest. All are part of the central chain of [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz). The lowest point of the province is in Tärom e Soflä.

### Climate

The climate of the province in the northern parts is cold and snowy in winters and temperate in summers. In the southern parts, the climate is mild with comparatively cold winters and warm summers.
