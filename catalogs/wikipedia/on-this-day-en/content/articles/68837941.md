**SE Trains Limited**,[^cite_note-1] [trading as](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trade_name) **Southeastern** and **GBR South Eastern**,[^cite_note-2] is a [state-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned) British [train operating company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Train_operating_company) in the south east of England. It operates services to destinations in [Kent](16766.md), London and [East Sussex](67923.md), including the domestic services on [High Speed 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High_Speed_1).

It is owned by [DfT Operator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DfT_Operator),[^cite_note-coinfo-3] which took over operating the [South Eastern franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Eastern_franchise) from privately owned [London & South Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_&_South_Eastern_Railway) (which also traded as Southeastern) on 17 October 2021.

## History

In September 2021 the [Department for Transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Transport) (DfT) announced it would be terminating the [South Eastern franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Eastern_franchise) then operated by the [Govia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govia)-owned [London & South Eastern Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_&_South_Eastern_Railway) after revenue declaration discrepancies involving £25 million of public money were discovered.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6] On 17 October 2021, an [operator of last resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operator_of_last_resort) (OLR), SE Trains Limited, took over operations under the Southeastern brand; at the time, the DfT stated the OLR would manage the franchise for a six-year period.[^cite_note-olr2021-7]

Southeastern has been one of several train operators affected by the [2022–2023 United Kingdom railway strikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%932023_United_Kingdom_railway_strikes), the first national rail strikes in the UK in three decades.[^cite_note-8] Its workers are amongst those who are participating in [industrial action](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_action) due to a dispute over pay and working conditions.[^cite_note-9] These actions have led to Southeastern occasionally being unable to provide a full service level, and due to the often abrupt nature of the industrial action, which has included [overtime bans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overtime_ban) and [strikes](155134.md), has sometimes not been able to arrange for [rail replacement buses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_replacement_bus) in time.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

On 1 June 2023, both [e-scooters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-scooter) and [electric skateboards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_skateboard) were banned from being carried onboard Southeastern trains; the reason for the ban was that the lithium-ion batteries that power these compact vehicles posed a fire safety risk. Most [electric bicycles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_bicycle) may still be taken on board, however.[^cite_note-12] Over the summer of 2023, Southeastern opened several new secure cycle hubs at railway stations across Kent as part of wider efforts to make its stations more accessible to cyclists.[^cite_note-13] These additions came amid a £4.4 million station improvement programme covering both Kent and south-east London, headed by both Southeastern and [Network Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network_Rail).[^cite_note-14]

In late 2023, Southeastern introduced digital season tickets, referred to as sTickets, enabling customers to purchase and store season tickets on their [smartphones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smartphone). They can be bought through Southeastern's website as well as its mobile [application](454746.md), incorporate anti-[fraud](58790.md) measures, and are valid for travel across south-east London, Kent, and East Sussex.[^cite_note-15] Several other fare changes were made around this time, including the abolition of some super off-peak tickets and various price rises.[^cite_note-16] Around the same time, Southeastern scrapped plans to close many of its staffed ticket offices across its network.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

In June 2025, as part of the preparations for establishing [Great British Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_British_Railways) (GBR) as the new overarching body responsible for rail infrastructure and operations, [state-owned](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State-owned) Southeastern and Network Rail (Kent area) unified their operations as South Eastern Railway.[^cite_note-19]

## Overview

Southeastern serves the main London stations of [Charing Cross](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charing_Cross_railway_station), [Waterloo East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Waterloo_East_railway_station), [Cannon Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cannon_Street_station), [London Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Bridge_station), [St Pancras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Pancras_railway_station), [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Victoria_railway_station) and [Blackfriars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blackfriars_station). The network has route mileage of 540 miles (870 km),[^cite_note-coinfo-3] covering 180 stations,[^cite_note-coinfo-3] 164 of which it manages.[^cite_note-explore-20]

## Routes

Upon its takeover of the franchise, Southeastern initially operated the same routes and services as its predecessor. During December 2022, a new timetable was introduced that reportedly aimed to reduce congestion, improve reliability and better match demand following the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md).[^cite_note-21] One year later, it was announced that Southeastern was to put on an additional 100 trains per week as a result of timetable changes based on customer feedback and travel pattern analysis across Kent, East Sussex and south-east London.[^cite_note-BBC_11Dec2013-22][^cite_note-23]

Several parties have called for the transfer of Southeastern's metro services to [Transport for London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_for_London) (TfL).[^cite_note-24] Prior to the OLR taking over operations, TfL itself had announced its intention to take over the London suburban parts of the franchise and integrate them into a wider metro network.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] Officials such as the [Mayor of London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_London) [Sadiq Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadiq_Khan) have publicly supported this move.[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

## Rolling stock

At the commencement of operations, Southeastern retained the same [electric multiple units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_multiple_unit) as its predecessor. It promptly launched a refurbishment programme covering most of its existing rolling stock; £27 million alone was allocated to the refurbishment of its 29 [Class 395 Javelin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail_Class_395) high-speed trains.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] By May 2023, Southeastern had reportedly completed work on half of its 112-strong [Class 375 Electrostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail_Class_375) fleet under a £10 million upgrade programme; internal changes included the addition of new [USB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USB) ports, at-seat [electrical sockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrical_socket), [LED](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LED) lighting, and [energy meters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_meter).[^cite_note-44]

The previous franchisee had finalised a leasing arrangement for the entire [Class 707](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail_Class_707) fleet and was in the process of launching the type into service when the OLR took over.[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-entryse-46] Southeastern has continued this deal, thus the remainder of the 30 Class 707s shall enter service as they are released by [South Western Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Western_Railway_\(train_operating_company\)).[^cite_note-MR903-47]

Various initiatives have been explored to improve train performance, capabilities and effectiveness. Southeastern formed a team with [Siemens Mobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siemens_Mobility) and [Eversholt Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eversholt_Rail) to jointly develop a fully automated vehicle inspection system to inspect its vehicles; this technology, which uses numerous high sensitivity cameras and optical laser sensors, is to be first used at the operator's Ramsgate depot, and will be active from 2025.[^cite_note-48]

Southeastern has also examined the prospects for replacing older portions of its train fleet. In November 2022, the operator launched the process of procuring new trains via the issuing of a [tender](36571608.md). Southeastern has sought between 350 and 640 new carriages for its services; these are projected to be introduced sometime during the mid-2020s.[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50]

In November 2024 it was announced that in the period between December 2024 and December 2025, Southeastern would be set to receive 13 [Class 377/1s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail_Class_377#Fleet_details) (377121–133) from [Southern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_\(Govia_Thameslink_Railway\)) to replace ageing [Class 465](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail_Class_465) units, being made available by a movement of out-of-service [Class 379](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail_Class_379) units and [Class 387/1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Rail_Class_387) units to support [Govia Thameslink Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Govia_Thameslink_Railway) services.[^cite_note-51]
