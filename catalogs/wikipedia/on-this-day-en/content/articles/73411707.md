In the context of the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md), in the time leading up to and after the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), a number of citizens of the [Russian Federation](25391.md) and of other nationalities working for Russia have been identified publicly as spies or agents of the [Federal Security Service](385990.md) (FSB), the [Russia's foreign intelligence service](614404.md) (SVR) or the third intelligence arm, the [military intelligence service](261749.md) (GRU). Each arm having their own remits.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-ee101-2]

Termed "illegals", operatives [without diplomatic accreditation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-official_cover), agents often spend years building up false identities, living a quiet life, though sometimes they then move location/country with their partner, who may or may not know of their espionage activities and any children, who will not know of any nefarious activities. They can be hard to identify, resembling normal people.[^cite_note-ee101-2]

## Legals

See also: [Diplomatic expulsions during the Russo-Ukrainian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_expulsions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War)

In addition to "illegals", over 600 Russian diplomats have been identified and been declared "persona non grata" in 2022 and 2023 and [had their accreditation cancelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_expulsions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War), with a number being described as "intelligence officers masquerading as diplomats".[^cite_note-3]

The head of Britain's [Secret Intelligence Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secret_Intelligence_Service) (MI6), Richard Moore, reported that roughly half of Russia's spies working under diplomatic cover were expelled from Europe by July 2022.[^cite_note-4]

## Illegals

Deep-cover spies, or "illegals", a term for intelligence agents operating without diplomatic cover, have been uncovered in a number of countries. Resembling normal people, many have been uncovered through a combination of their recent activities and heightened awareness by the authorities.[^cite_note-ee101-2]

In the 1920s, the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) was not recognised by many countries and was therefore not given diplomatic representation. The interior ministry of the Soviet Union ([NKVD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NKVD)) sent people overseas giving them false documentation and instructions to settle into their new country and acquire contacts that might be useful in the future. The [KGB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KGB) used the same system from the 1950s.[^cite_note-5] In Russia, they are called *razvedchik*, an approximation of the word 'scout'.

Russia is almost unique in the way it changes the nationality of its Russian born officers and gives them years to build up a false identity in another country, before being called forward to undertake a specific task that may take decades to complete. The number of Russian illegals is not thought to be high, probably less than 100, worked by GRU and SVR with their main aim to get close to political, cultural, industrial and military organisations to enable them to "talent spot", identify people who might be recruited by others.[^cite_note-6]

"Illegals" also cover locals recruited by Russia, espionage groups sent from Russia or other countries sympathetic to Russia to countries and migrant asylum-seekers who have been persuaded to collect intelligence.[^cite_note-7]

### Albania

In August 2022, three people, two Russians and one Albanian, climbed over a wall of a weapons factory before being spotted by guards, whom they attacked with a chemical spray, before being arrested. One supposedly admitted to being a Russian spy; the factory is currently used to repair weapons. The three are being held in detention. One, a woman, had asked for asylum, claiming she would be persecuted if returned to Russia.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Albania agreed to the asylum claim. Mikhail Zorin, a Russian who had used the spray, remains in prison in May 2023, the others have been released.

### Australia

In February 2023, a large spy ring whose members included those posing as diplomats, as well a deep-cover identity operatives was uncovered. Operating for 18 months, it was broken by the Australian Security Intelligence Organisation (ASIO). Those involved have had visas not renewed, or cancelled, forcing them to leave the country.[^cite_note-10]

### Austria

A 39-year-old Greek citizen was uncovered as a spy in Vienna in March 2022 when police raided a house and discovered tracking devices, bugs, a device for finding hidden listening devices and cameras as well as phones, tablets and laptops containing 10 million files and a splinter protection suit. The individual, whose father was a former Russian intelligence agent who had been a diplomat in Germany and Austria, had trained in Russia and made 65 trips to other countries since 2018. With no visible income, he owns property in Greece, Austria and Russia and whilst being monitored was seen to use drop boxes to pass information to Russian embassy staff in Vienna.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12]
