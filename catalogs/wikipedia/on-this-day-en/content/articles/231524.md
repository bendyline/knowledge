The **Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem** ([Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_language): *Patriarchatus Latinus Hierosolymitanus*) is the [Latin Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) ecclesiastical [patriarchate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarchate) in [Jerusalem](16043.md), officially [seated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedra) in the [Church of the Holy Sepulchre](7810.md). The Latin patriarch of Jerusalem is the [archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop) of Latin Church [Catholics](606848.md) of the Archdiocese of Jerusalem with jurisdiction for [all Latin Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church_in_the_Middle_East) in [Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Israel), [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Palestine), [Jordan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Jordan) and [Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Cyprus); he also holds the office of [grand prior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_prior) of the [Order of the Holy Sepulchre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre_\(Catholic\)). It is [exempt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exemption_in_the_Catholic_Church), being directly subject to the [Holy See](13393.md) (and exceptionally its [Dicastery for the Eastern Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dicastery_for_the_Eastern_Churches), which normally handles [Eastern Catholics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Catholics)). It is not within an [ecclesiastical province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecclesiastical_province), and has no [metropolitan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_see) functions.

The patriarchate was originally established in 1099, with the [Kingdom of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem) encompassing the territories in the [Holy Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land) newly conquered by the [First Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Crusade). From 1374 to 1847 it was a [titular see](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_see), with the patriarchs of Jerusalem being based at the [Basilica di San Lorenzo fuori le Mura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_di_San_Lorenzo_fuori_le_Mura) in [Rome](25458.md). [Pope Pius IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_IX) re-established a resident Latin patriarch in 1847.

The title of [patriarch](175409.md) in the Latin Church is retained by only five archbishops: the [patriarchs](175409.md#Titular_Latin_patriarchates) of Jerusalem, of [the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_the_West) (being the pope himself), of [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Venice), of [Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Lisbon) and of [the East Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_the_East_Indies). Until 1964, there had also been the honorary patriarchal titles of [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Constantinople), [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Alexandria) and [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Patriarch_of_Antioch); still others were abolished earlier. Outside the Catholic Church, the title of "Patriarch of Jerusalem" is also used by the [Greek Orthodox patriarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem), the [Armenian patriarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem), and within the Catholic Church it is also used titularly by the [Melkite patriarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melkite_Catholic_Patriarchate_of_Antioch_and_All_the_East#List_of_Melkite_Greek_Catholic_Patriarchs_of_Antioch,_Alexandria_and_Jerusalem).

## History

See also: [Latin Church in the Middle East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church_in_the_Middle_East)

Jerusalem (in Latin also Hierosolyma) was one of the Apostles' original [bishoprics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishoprics) (diocese). It was renamed Aelia Capitolina in 135 AD, again Jerusalem in 325. In 451 it was promoted as patriarchal see. After 649, [Pope Martin I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Martin_I) appointed John of Philadelphia (Amman) as patriarchal vicar of Jerusalem to replace Sergius of Jaffa.

In 1054, the [Great Schism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East%E2%80%93West_Schism) split Christianity into the [Catholic Church](606848.md), which consisted of the [pope](23056.md) of Rome and virtually all of Western Christianity; and the [Eastern Orthodox Church](10186.md)—which consisted of the four Orthodox Christian patriarchs of [Antioch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Antioch), [Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_Orthodox_Patriarch_of_Jerusalem), [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecumenical_Patriarch_of_Constantinople) and [Alexandria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patriarch_of_Alexandria)—under the stewardship of [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople).[^cite_note-3] Apart from the [Maronites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maronites), most Christians in the Holy Land came under the jurisdiction of the [Eastern Orthodox Patriarchate of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Orthodox_Patriarchate_of_Jerusalem).

### Latin Kingdom of Jerusalem

In 1099, the Western [crusaders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crusaders) [captured](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Jerusalem_\(1099\)) [Jerusalem](16043.md), set up the [Kingdom of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Jerusalem) and established a Latin hierarchy under a Latin patriarch (in communion with Rome),[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] while expelling the Orthodox patriarch. The Latin Patriarchate was divided into four [archdioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archdiocese)—their heads bearing the titles of [archbishop of Tyre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Tyre), [archbishop of Caesarea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Caesarea), [archbishop of Nazareth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Nazareth) and [archbishop of Petra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archbishop_of_Petra)—and a number of [suffragan dioceses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragan_diocese). The Latin patriarch took over control of the Latin quarter of the city of Jerusalem (the [Holy Sepulchre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Sepulchre) and the immediate surroundings) as his metropolitan see, and had as his direct suffragans the [bishops of Lydda-Ramla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lydda_\(titular_see\)), [Bethlehem](4312.md), [Hebron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hebron_\(titular_see\)) and [Gaza](55387.md), and the abbots of the [Temple](65719.md), [Mount Sion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Zion) and the [Mount of Olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_of_Olives).

The Latin patriarch resided in Jerusalem from 1099 to 1187, while Orthodox patriarchs continued to be appointed, but resided in [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople). In 1187, the crusaders were forced to flee Jerusalem, and the Latin Patriarchate moved to [Acre (Akka)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acre,_Israel),[^cite_note-OO-6] while the Orthodox patriarch returned to Jerusalem. The [Catholic Church](606848.md) continued to appoint residential Latin patriarchs. The crusader Kingdom of Jerusalem endured almost 200 years until the last vestiges of the kingdom were conquered by the Muslim [Mamluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluks) in 1291, and the Latin hierarchy was effectively eliminated in the Levant.

With the [fall of Acre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Acre), the Latin patriarch moved to the [Kingdom of Cyprus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Cyprus) in 1291.

### Titular Latin Patriarchate of Jerusalem

In 1374, the Kingdom of Cyprus underwent a severe upheaval when it was invaded by the [Genoese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Genoa), who conquered [Famagusta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famagusta) and held King [Peter II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_II_of_Cyprus) captive.

From that time on, the Latin patriarchs of Jerusalem ceased to reside in Cyprus. The Catholic Church continued to appoint [titular](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_bishop) patriarchs of Jerusalem, but from then on they were based at the [Basilica di San Lorenzo fuori le Mura](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_di_San_Lorenzo_fuori_le_Mura) in [Rome](25458.md).[^cite_note-7]

In 1342, [Pope Clement VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_VI) officially committed the care of the Holy Land to the Franciscans[^cite_note-8] and the [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) [Custos of the Holy Lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Custos_of_the_Holy_Land) (the grand masters of the [Order of the Holy Sepulchre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre_\(Catholic\))) held the title *ex officio* under the [papal bull](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Papal_bull) *[Gratiam agimus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_papal_bulls)* by [Clement VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clement_VI), unless someone was specifically appointed to the honorary office.

In 1570, it gained territories from the suppressed Archdiocese of Nicosia and Diocese of Paphos, and in 1571 it gained more territories from the suppressed Diocese of Limassol and Diocese of Famagosta, all in former crusader Kingdom of Cyprus, which had fallen to the Ottoman Turks.

### Modern Patriarchate of Jerusalem

See also: [Catholic Church in Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Israel) and [Catholic Church in Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Catholic_Church_in_Palestine)

A resident Latin patriarch was re-established in 1847 by [Pius IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pius_IX),[^cite_note-OO-6] with Bishop [Joseph Valerga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Valerga) being appointed to the office. Though officially superseding the Franciscans, Valerga was also the grand master of the Order. On Valerga's death in 1872, Vincent Braco was appointed, and following his death in 1889, the [Ottoman sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_sultan) authorised the re-establishment of a Latin hierarchy. The grand masters of the Order continued to be named as Latin patriarchs until 1905.



Church of the Most Holy Name of Jesus, the Patriarchate's co-cathedral, Jerusalem

The [Co-Cathedral of the Most Holy Name of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-Cathedral_of_the_Most_Holy_Name_of_Jesus) is the principal, or "mother" church of the Latin Patriarchate, the church in which the Latin patriarch of Jerusalem has his official chair (cathedra).[^cite_note-9] However, the [Basilica of the Holy Sepulchre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre) has the title of [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral) of the patriarchate. The residence of the patriarch is in the [Old City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_City_\(Jerusalem\)), near the Co-Cathedral, while the [seminary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seminary), which is responsible for the [liturgical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liturgical) education, is in [Beit Jala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beit_Jala), a town 10 km south of Jerusalem, where it has been since 1936.

In 1987, [Michel Sabbah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Sabbah) became the first native [Palestinian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people) to be appointed Latin patriarch.[^cite_note-10] The Latin Patriarch of Jerusalem is now the [diocesan archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocesan_archbishop) of Latin Catholics of the *Catholic Archdiocese of Jerusalem* and has jurisdiction for all [Latin Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_Church) Catholics in [Israel](9282173.md), [Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_\(state\)), [Jordan](7515964.md) and [Cyprus](5593.md).

The prerogatives of the patriarch in his relation with government authorities overlap with the prerogatives of the [apostolic nuncio to Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_nuncio_to_Israel) and the [apostolic delegate to Jerusalem and Palestine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_delegate_to_Jerusalem_and_Palestine).

In 2008, Archbishop [Fouad Twal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fouad_Twal) was appointed patriarch to succeed Michel Sabbah.[^cite_note-11] He exercised his mandate from June 21, 2008, until June 24, 2016, when he reached the canonical age of retirement and Pope Francis accepted his resignation. On 24 June 2016, [Pope Francis](1687680.md) nominated Father [Pierbattista Pizzaballa](54051059.md) as [Apostolic Administrator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Administrator) [sede vacante](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sede_vacante) of the patriarchate and appointed him [titular archbishop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titular_archbishop) of Verbe.[^cite_note-vatbio-12] On 24 October 2020, Archbishop Pizzaballa was elevated to the position of patriarch.[^cite_note-vatnews-13] He took possession of the see on 6 November 2020.

[Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) visited in January 1964, [Pope John Paul II](23805.md) in March 2000, [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) in May 2009 and [Pope Francis](1687680.md) in May 2014.

## Statistics and extent

As per 2015, it pastorally served 293,053 Catholics in 66 parishes with 464 priests (81 diocesan, 383 religious), 9 deacons, 1,652 lay religious (590 brothers, 1,062 sisters) and 55 seminarians. Since then, several parishes have been added and there are now over 70 across four areas in 2023.[^cite_note-19]

The proper archdiocese of the patriarchal see of Jerusalem has jurisdiction over all Latin Catholics (not Eastern Catholics) in the [Holy Land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Land) ([Israel](9282173.md), [Palestine](241405.md) and [Jordan](7515964.md)) as well as [Cyprus](5593.md). In [Jerusalem](16043.md), the Latin Catholic community is the largest Christian community, with some 4,500 people out of an estimated Christian population of about 11,000.[^cite_note-20]

## Special churches

In [Jerusalem](16043.md), the patriarch has his [cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cathedral) archiepiscopal see, a [minor basilica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_basilica) and [World Heritage Site](44940.md): the [Basilica of the Holy Sepulchre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Holy_Sepulchre), as well as the [Co-Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-Cathedral), also a World Heritage Site: [Co-Cathedral of the Most Holy Name of Jesus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Co-Cathedral_of_the_Most_Holy_Name_of_Jesus), and four other [Minor Basilicas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_Basilica) and World Heritage Sites: [Basilica of the Agony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Agony), [Basilica of St. Stephen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Stephen's_Basilica,_Jerusalem), [Dormition Abbey of the Virgin Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dormition_Abbey_of_the_Virgin_Mary) and [St. Anne's Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Anne's_Church).

Other cities in the archdiocese have more minor basilicas: [Basilica of the Annunciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Annunciation) in [Nazareth](21702.md), [Basilica of the Transfiguration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Basilica_of_the_Transfiguration) in [Mount Tabor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Tabor), Carmelite Monastery of Stella Maris in [Haifa](55606.md) and [St. Cleophas Church](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Cleophas_Church) in El Qubeibeh, and two other World Heritage Sites, both in [Bethlehem](4312.md): [Church of St. Catherine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_Saint_Catherine,_Bethlehem) and [Church of the Nativity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_Nativity).
