**Catherine Martina Ann Connolly**[^cite_note-ElectionResults2025-4] (born 5 November 1955) is an Irish politician serving as the [president of Ireland](24676.md) since 11 November 2025. She had been a [Teachta Dála](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teachta_D%C3%A1la) (TD) for the [Galway West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway_West) constituency from [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Irish_general_election) until [her election as president](75903783.md) in 2025.

Born in [Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway), Connolly began her political career as a member of the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Ireland\)), for which she was elected to [Galway City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway_City_Council) in [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Galway_Corporation_election), and was [Mayor of Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Galway) from 2004 to 2005. She left the party in 2006 in a dispute over candidate selection. After unsuccessfully contesting the [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Irish_general_election) and [2011](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Irish_general_election) general elections in Galway West as an independent, Connolly was elected to the [Dáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_%C3%89ireann) in [2016](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Irish_general_election). She became Ireland's first female [Leas-Cheann Comhairle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leas-Cheann_Comhairle), serving in the [33rd Dáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/33rd_D%C3%A1il) from July 2020 to November 2024. Connolly ran as an independent candidate in the 2025 presidential election, supported by [Sinn Féin](28175.md), the [Social Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democrats_\(Ireland\)), the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Ireland\)), [People Before Profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Before_Profit), the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Ireland\)), [100% Redress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100%25_Redress), and several independent [Oireachtas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oireachtas) members. She defeated [Heather Humphreys](31023259.md) and [Jim Gavin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Gavin) in a [landslide victory](41053306.md), with her 914,143 [first-preference votes](46785965.md) being the largest first preference personal mandate in the [history of the Republic of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Republic_of_Ireland).[^cite_note-5] She is the third woman to hold the office after [Mary Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Robinson) and [Mary McAleese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_McAleese).

Ideologically described as [left-wing](18499.md) to [far-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-left), Connolly describes herself as a [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist) and [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifist).[^cite_note-6] A supporter of [Irish neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_neutrality), her foreign policy views were described by *[Politico](9172097.md)* as "often [anti-Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Western_sentiment)";[^cite_note-Politico_16_July_2025-7] she is critical of [NATO](21133.md), the [European Union](9317.md)'s increased military and defence spending and general European militarisation.[^cite_note-\:3-8] Connolly has condemned the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md) and has also described NATO's [attitude toward Russia](19066574.md) as "warmongering".[^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9] An outspoken supporter of [Palestine](241405.md), Connolly is a staunch [critic of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Critic_of_Israel) and has called it a "[genocidal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_genocide_accusation) state". Connolly advocates for [Irish reunification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_reunification). Domestically, she supported the [legalisation of same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-fourth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland) and the [legalisation of abortion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland). Connolly is an [Irish speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language) and has previously worked as a [clinical psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_psychologist) and as a [barrister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrister).

## Early life and education

Catherine Martina Ann Connolly was born on 5 November 1955 and grew up in [Shantalla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shantalla), [Galway City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway_City), the ninth of 14 siblings (seven boys and seven girls).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11][^cite_note-Indo_17_August-12][^cite_note-Hot_Press_September_2025-13] Her father was a carpenter and a boatbuilder, who built [Galway hooker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway_hooker) sailing boats.[^cite_note-Indo_17_August-12] Her mother died at the age of 43, when Connolly was nine years old, her death suspected to have been caused by issues related to [asthma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asthma).[^cite_note-Hot_Press_September_2025-13] The family grew up in one of Galway's first [social housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_housing) developments.[^cite_note-Indo_17_August-12] In the 1970s, Connolly led a campaign to have tennis courts built in Shantalla.[^cite_note-\:0-14]

Connolly has stated that she "learned her socialism" growing up through early volunteering. She participated in activities with the [Legion of Mary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legion_of_Mary), including house cleaning and hospital visits, and continued volunteering in later years with the [Order of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_Malta).[^cite_note-15] Connolly earned a [bachelor's degree](188874.md) in psychology from the [University College, Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Galway) in the late 1970s and a [master's degree](170346.md) in [clinical psychology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_psychology) from the [University of Leeds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leeds) in 1981 before practising as a clinical psychologist for a number of years.[^cite_note-times_sep25-16] Connolly subsequently earned a law degree from University College, Galway in 1989 before studying at [King's Inns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King's_Inns) and becoming a [barrister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrister) in 1991.[^cite_note-RTÉ_25_Sept_2025-17]

## Pre-presidential career

Connolly practised as a barrister, mostly on the [Western Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Court_\(Ireland\)), with a general practice, mainly in the areas of [family law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_law) and [personal injury law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Personal_injury_law), from 1991 until she was elected to Dáil Éireann in 2016.[^cite_note-times_sep25-16][^cite_note-18] Connolly joined the Labour Party after the [1997 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_Irish_general_election).[^cite_note-\:0-14] She was elected to [Galway City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway_City_Council) for Labour in the west city [area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_electoral_area) at the [1999 corporation election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Galway_Corporation_election). For the [2004 council election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Galway_City_Council_election), Connolly switched to the south city local electoral area to allow her sister, Colette, to contest the election in the west area of the city;[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-elecs_irl-20] both were elected.[^cite_note-21] In the same year, she was elected [Mayor of Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_Galway), leading Labour into a power-sharing agreement with [Fine Gael](11252.md) and independent councillors.[^cite_note-\:0-14] As Mayor, Connolly successfully advocated for and presided over the awarding (*in absentia*) of the Freedom of Galway City to Myanmar political activist [Aung San Suu Kyi](2847.md) in June 2005.[^cite_note-22]

### National politics (2004–present)



Connolly speaking in the Dáil in 2024

Connolly was a supporter of [Michael D. Higgins](559753.md)'s unsuccessful campaign to run in the [2004 presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_Irish_presidential_election);[^cite_note-23] she later voted to nominate [Dana Rosemary Scallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dana_Rosemary_Scallon) for that election.[^cite_note-24] Connolly aimed to run in the [2007 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Irish_general_election) as a running mate of Higgins in [Galway West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway_West), but the party opted to only run one candidate in the constituency. Higgins had reportedly considered retiring due to health concerns, but he allowed his name to go forward to contest the seat again; Connolly criticised Higgins for "dragging his heels" on the decision,[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26] describing the decision to only run him as "crazy" and saying the party "lost out on a great opportunity".[^cite_note-27] She left the Labour Party and unsuccessfully contested the [2007 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Irish_general_election) as an independent candidate, polling just over 2,000 votes.[^cite_note-siggins-2011constituency_profile-28]

Connolly contested the [2011 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Irish_general_election) again in Galway West,[^cite_note-rte-galway-west-2011-29] where she lost out on the last seat to [Fine Gael](11252.md)'s [Seán Kyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Se%C3%A1n_Kyne) by only 17 votes. She sought a full recount,[^cite_note-30] which concluded after four days but did not change the outcome.[^cite_note-31] Connolly was elected to Dáil Éireann for the Galway West constituency at the [2016 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Irish_general_election), when the Labour Party (of which she was formerly a member) lost 30 of its 37 seats, including its seat in Galway West, following an unpopular term in government.[^cite_note-oireachtas_db-32][^cite_note-33][^cite_note-times_sep25-16] Her sister, Colette Connolly, who had lost her seat as a Labour councillor in [2014](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Galway_City_Council_election),[^cite_note-34] was co-opted as an independent to replace Catherine on Galway City Council.[^cite_note-35] Connolly voted for [Richard Boyd Barrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Boyd_Barrett) for [Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach) when the [32nd Dáil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/32nd_D%C3%A1il) first met.[^cite_note-36] At the 32nd Dáil's second meeting on 5 April 2016, she made her maiden speech in which she criticised the [Minister for the Environment, Community and Local Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Housing,_Local_Government_and_Heritage) [Alan Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Kelly_\(politician\))'s handling of Ireland's homelessness crisis.[^cite_note-37] Connolly sat on the Public Accounts Committee and was Chair of the Committee on the Irish Language, the Gaeltacht and the Islands.

In 2018 Connolly joined [Clare Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Daly), [Mick Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Wallace), and [Maureen O'Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maureen_O'Sullivan_\(politician\)) on a visit to [Damascus](8914.md), [Maaloula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maaloula), and [Aleppo](159244.md) in [Syria](7515849.md).[^cite_note-Syrian_trip_2018-38][^cite_note-IT_11_July_2025-39][^cite_note-Raymond_17_July_2025-40] When launching her 2025 presidential campaign she defended this trip, commenting that she funded the trip herself and that she did not "utter one word of support for [Assad](364813.md)".[^cite_note-\:2-41] However, *[Politico](9172097.md)* observed that Connolly also refrained from criticising Assad at that time and later called for the removal of sanctions on his regime.[^cite_note-Politico_16_July_2025-7] Connolly contested the [2020 general election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_Irish_general_election) and was re-elected on the 12th count.[^cite_note-42]

### Leas-Cheann Comhairle of Dáil Éireann (2020–2024)



Connolly hosting the "Working breakfast of Women Presidents of Parliament" in Dublin, September 2023

Connolly was elected the [Leas-Cheann Comhairle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leas-Cheann_Comhairle) of [Dáil Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%A1il_%C3%89ireann) on 23 July 2020, in a surprise victory over Fine Gael candidate [Fergus O'Dowd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fergus_O'Dowd), becoming the first woman to hold the position.[^cite_note-43] In January 2021, Connolly criticised the Government for their handling of the Final Report of the Commission of Investigation (Mother and Baby Homes and certain related matters). Referring to the [Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach) ([Micheál Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miche%C3%A1l_Martin)), [Tánaiste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T%C3%A1naiste) ([Leo Varadkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leo_Varadkar)) and [Minister for Children, Equality, Disability, Integration and Youth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_for_Children,_Disability_and_Equality) ([Roderic O'Gorman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roderic_O'Gorman)) as the "three unwise men", she criticised the Government's failure to provide survivors of mother and baby homes the report before it was released to the general public.[^cite_note-\:1-44] Connolly stated: "This document I have to hand is what the report looks like. I hold it up to show survivors because they do not have it. It is the executive summary with the recommendations and one or two other things. Not a single survivor has it. I have it since yesterday, when it was put in the pigeonholes of Deputies."[^cite_note-\:1-44]

In June 2023 Connolly appeared alongside Clare Daly, Mick Wallace, [Mairéad Farrell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mair%C3%A9ad_Farrell_\(politician\)), and [George Galloway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Galloway) in a "neutrality forum" hosted by the organisation "Galway Alliance Against War" where the topic for discussion was the [Russo-Ukrainian War](42085878.md).[^cite_note-\:4-45] In May 2024, during the campaign for the [2024 European Parliament election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_European_Parliament_election_in_Ireland), Connolly endorsed Clare Daly in the [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_\(European_Parliament_constituency\)) constituency and spoke at her campaign launch.[^cite_note-LL_9_May_2024-46]

Connolly confirmed on 11 July 2025 on [RTÉ Raidió na Gaeltachta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RT%C3%89_Raidi%C3%B3_na_Gaeltachta) that she had decided to run for [president of Ireland](24676.md) and believed she had the necessary support.[^cite_note-Connolly_confirms_11_July-48] On 16 July, Connolly officially launched her campaign outside [Leinster House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leinster_House) and confirmed she had received the minimum of twenty [Oireachtas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oireachtas) nominations required to run for the office.[^cite_note-Connolly_launch_1-49] Connolly emphasised giving a voice to ordinary people and tackling issues such as a [United Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Ireland), [climate change](5042951.md), [homelessness](19394651.md), and the normalisation of violence.[^cite_note-\:2-41] She signalled interest in scrutinising the €330,000 presidential salary, stating she would "look at it" and consider using it "for the common good" if elected.[^cite_note-50]

In the election on 24 October 2025, Connolly won receiving 63.4% of the votes cast, the highest percentage any president has received since the creation of the role in 1938, in what was widely described as a [landslide victory](41053306.md).[^cite_note-ElectionResults2025-4][^cite_note-51] With 914,143 votes, she recorded the highest number of first preference votes ever received by a candidate in Ireland.[^cite_note-52]

#### Endorsements

Connolly's campaign was backed by the [Social Democrats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Democrats_\(Ireland\)),[^cite_note-Soc_Dems_endorsement-53] [People Before Profit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People_Before_Profit), [100% Redress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100%25_Redress), and a number of [independent](61259001.md) [Oireachtas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oireachtas) members.[^cite_note-54] On 31 July, Connolly secured the backing of the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Ireland\)).[^cite_note-IT_Labour_backs_Connolly-55] On 27 August, Labour TD [Alan Kelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Kelly_\(politician\)) said he would not support Connolly due to concerns about her policies on international relations.[^cite_note-Kelly_won't_nominate-56] On 4 September, [Solidarity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solidarity_\(Ireland\)) TD [Ruth Coppinger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruth_Coppinger) backed Connolly and on 19 September the [Green Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_Party_\(Ireland\)) announced it would endorse Connolly's candidacy.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

On 20 September, [Sinn Féin](28175.md) announced its backing for Connolly's campaign.[^cite_note-rteSinnFeinConnolly-59] Connolly was also backed by both the [Workers' Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Workers'_Party_\(Ireland\)) and the [Communist Party of Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Ireland), neither of which has any elected representatives.[^cite_note-Indo_30_September_2025-60][^cite_note-61] [Holly Cairns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holly_Cairns) (Social Democrats), [Marie Sherlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marie_Sherlock) (Labour), independent senator [Eileen Flynn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eileen_Flynn_\(politician\)), [Roderic O'Gorman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roderic_O'Gorman) (Green Party), [Paul Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Murphy_\(Irish_politician\)) (People Before Profit), and [Mary Lou McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Lou_McDonald) (Sinn Féin) all spoke at Connolly's campaign launch on 22 September endorsing her candidacy.[^cite_note-62]

#### Campaign trail

Connolly made a commitment to visit every [County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_counties) in the [Republic of Ireland](14560.md) during her campaign.[^cite_note-63] By 24 October, she had visited: [Wicklow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Wicklow) on 7 August,[^cite_note-64] [Louth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Louth) on 30 August,[^cite_note-65] [Sligo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Sligo) on 5 September,[^cite_note-66] [Leitrim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Leitrim) on 6 September,[^cite_note-67] [Donegal](45576.md) on 12 and 27 September,[^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69] [Carlow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Carlow) on 22 September,[^cite_note-70] [Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Cork) on 3 October,[^cite_note-71] [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Dublin) on 5 October,[^cite_note-72] [Tipperary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Tipperary) on 10 October,[^cite_note-73] [Waterford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Waterford) and [Wexford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Wexford) on 11 October,[^cite_note-74] [Kildare](94784.md) on 14 October,[^cite_note-75] [Meath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Meath) on 15 October,[^cite_note-76] [Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Kerry) on 17 October,[^cite_note-77] [Clare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Clare) and [Limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Limerick) on 19 October,[^cite_note-78] [Cavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Cavan), [Monaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Monaghan), [Longford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Longford) and [Westmeath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Westmeath) on 22 October,[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] and [Roscommon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Roscommon) on 23 October.[^cite_note-82]

Connolly attended several agricultural shows in August: the Bonniconlon Show in [County Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Mayo), the Ballyshannon Show in County Donegal, and the [Tullamore Show](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tullamore_Show) in [County Offaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Offaly). She came back to Offaly on a higher-profile visit for the [National Ploughing Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Ploughing_Championships) in Screggan on 17 September 2025, where she was interviewed about rural and agricultural life in Ireland and her presidential candidacy.[^cite_note-83] Connolly visited [Belfast](5046.md) on 28 August 2025; she said that citizens of [Northern Ireland](21265.md) should be permitted to vote in the presidential election, and the North was a "limb" cut off from the Republic of Ireland.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

#### Nomination of Gemma O'Doherty in 2018

At her campaign launch, Connolly was questioned about her nominating [Gemma O'Doherty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gemma_O'Doherty) for the [2018 Irish presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Irish_presidential_election). Connolly was one of eleven Oireachtas members who did so.[^cite_note-86] Connolly said that she "doesn't regret her decision to sign O'Doherty's papers at the time", adding that she "did not know her personally but saw her as a journalist who had done some very good work in the past".[^cite_note-87] In September 2025, Connolly stated she would not have nominated O'Doherty if it had been a few years later.[^cite_note-Hot_Press_September_2025-13]

#### Visit to Syria

Main article: [2018 visit by Catherine Connolly to Ba'athist Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_visit_by_Catherine_Connolly_to_Ba'athist_Syria)

During Connolly's campaign, she faced scrutiny for [a visit she made to Ba'athist Syria in 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_visit_by_Catherine_Connolly_to_Ba'athist_Syria), along with [Mick Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Wallace) (then a [Member of the European Parliament](258909.md)), [Clare Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Daly) (also an MEP) and [Maureen O'Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maureen_O'Sullivan_\(politician\)) (then a TD). Members of the Irish-Syria Solidarity Movement asked the [Labour Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Labour_Party_\(Ireland\)) not to support Connolly's campaign, arguing that the trip had "provided legitimacy to the [Assad regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Assad_regime) and its narrative".[^cite_note-88] Connolly said the trip was a "fact-finding mission" to see the harm that EU sanctions were causing to Syrian civilians.[^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9] She visited a refugee camp in [Damascus](8914.md) and travelled to [Aleppo](159244.md) to meet the Chamber of Commerce and [UNICEF](19698110.md). Connolly said she did not support the Assad regime: "On no occasion had I anything to do with the government, nor did I ever utter one word of support for Assad".[^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9] She added: "Assad's dictatorship committed countless atrocities and human rights abuses, all of which I have criticised".[^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9]

In a piece for *[The Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheJournal.ie)* during her campaign, Shane Raymond noted that "the Irish tour group was shown around Aleppo by the pro-Assad commentator [Fares Al-Shehabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fares_Shehabi), who ... had been put under sanctions by the EU for supporting the Assad regime". The year before, he had called for the rape and murder of anti-Assad Syrians and journalists.[^cite_note-Raymond_17_July_2025-40] Connolly replied that meeting Al-Shehabi was a "mistake". She said she "had absolutely no respect for that man after listening to him" during the trip, and "In retrospect, when one looks back and sees the comments that he made ... this man is utterly unacceptable to me".[^cite_note-89] She also met Saed Abd Al-Aal, a commander of the pro-Assad [Free Palestine Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_Palestine_Movement) militia, a group which human rights monitors have implicated "in atrocities and starvation siege tactics against Palestinian refugees".[^cite_note-new_arab-90] When her meeting with Saed Abd Al-Aal was pointed out, Connolly claimed that "\[...] you have no control when you go to a country like that as to who will come into your presence or not. That's no endorsement of the \[Assad] regime".[^cite_note-new_arab-90]

#### Appointment of parliamentary assistant

On 1 October 2025, [theJournal.ie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TheJournal.ie) published the story that, in 2018, Connolly sought to hire Ursula Ní Shionnain, a former [Éirígí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89ir%C3%ADg%C3%AD) member who had served almost four and a half years of a six-year prison sentence for firearms offences, as an administrative support in Leinster House.[^cite_note-91] Ní Shionnain had been convicted by the Special Criminal Court in 2014 after being arrested in a stolen van carrying weapons, one of which had previously been used in a murder, though there was no suggestion she was linked to that crime. Ní Shionnain, who holds degrees in early and modern Irish and in language planning, had been working on a PhD in new Irish language communities at the time of her arrest. Connolly said she assessed Ní Shionnain's suitability through recommendations from prison visiting committees and politicians familiar with her background, and that she understood Ní Shionnain had rehabilitated herself and was no longer active in Éirígí.[^cite_note-RTÉ_3_October_2025-92] Ní Shionnain's hiring was intended to support Connolly's work on the Oireachtas committee for the Irish language. Garda vetting was required for employment in Leinster House, but Connolly stated that the process was not completed during Ní Shionnain's six-month tenure, which ended when she left of her own accord.

The hiring drew criticism from senior political figures, including Taoiseach Micheál Martin and Fine Gael's presidential candidate Heather Humphreys, who raised concerns about parliamentary security and the judgment of employing a former prisoner with past [dissident republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissident_republican) affiliations. Connolly and others, including former Fianna Fáil minister [Éamon Ó Cuív](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89amon_%C3%93_Cu%C3%ADv) and Sinn Féin leader [Mary Lou McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Lou_McDonald), defended the decision as an example of rehabilitation, noting Ní Shionnain's further education and professional work since release, with Ó Cuív stating he had in fact recommended the appointment to Connolly.[^cite_note-93] Connolly also expressed concern about the public disclosure of Ní Shionnain's identity, describing it as damaging to her privacy and questioning how and why the information was released.[^cite_note-RTÉ_3_October_2025-92][^cite_note-94]

## President of Ireland (2025–present)

### Inauguration



Connolly at her inauguration as president on 11 November 2025



Connolly meets with [First Minister of Scotland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Scotland), [John Swinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Swinney) at [Bute House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bute_House) in [Edinburgh](9602.md), September 2026

Connolly was elected [president of Ireland](24676.md) on 24 October 2025,[^cite_note-95] winning 63.4% of valid first-preference votes, becoming the third woman to hold the office. Her nearest rival, [Heather Humphreys](31023259.md), finished on 29% (424,987).[^cite_note-96] Connolly's 914,143 votes is the highest number of first-preference votes ever received by an electoral candidate in Ireland. On 11 November 2025, she was inaugurated as president of Ireland in St. Patrick's Hall, [Dublin Castle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_Castle), in a ceremony attended by [Taoiseach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taoiseach) [Micheál Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miche%C3%A1l_Martin), and former presidents [Michael D. Higgins](559753.md), [Mary McAleese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_McAleese) and [Mary Robinson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Robinson), as well as representatives of all political parties. [In her inauguration speech](https://en.wikisource.org/wiki/Inaugural%20Speech%20of%20President%20Catherine%20Connolly) she promised to focus on "peace, neutrality and climate change" during her term.[^cite_note-97] A new republic, a [united Ireland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Ireland), and the importance of the Irish language were key themes in her speech, saying that it will be the working language in [Áras an Uachtaráin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ras_an_Uachtar%C3%A1in).[^cite_note-98]

### Tenure

Connolly visited Gaelscoil Inse Chór, [Inchicore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inchicore) for her first official presidential engagement on 12 November.[^cite_note-99] On 12 June 2026, Connolly was conferred with the Freedom of Galway City in recognition of her decades of public service.[^cite_note-100]

#### Foreign policy

On 2 December, she received [President of Ukraine](20688479.md) [Volodymyr Zelenskyy](59210523.md) and [First Lady](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Lady_of_Ukraine) [Olena Zelenska](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olena_Zelenska) at Áras an Uachtaráin on a [courtesy call](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtesy_call).[^cite_note-101][^cite_note-102] Connolly received Swedish climate activist [Greta Thunberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greta_Thunberg) on 17 December.[^cite_note-103] She made her first official presidential visit to the [United Kingdom](31717.md) from 18 to 20 May 2026, inviting [King Charles](125248.md) to Ireland for a state visit the following year.[^cite_note-104] In September 2026, she engaged in a four day visit to [Scotland](26994.md), where she met with [First Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Minister_of_Scotland) [John Swinney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Swinney) at [Bute House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bute_House) in Edinburgh, before visiting [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow), [Coatbridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coatbridge) and the [Isle of Skye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Isle_of_Skye). On her visit to Scotland, Connolly said "Ireland and Scotland are bound by strong ties of friendship, culture and language, with people moving between our countries for centuries".[^cite_note-105]

## Political views

Connolly is characterised in the press as a [left-wing](18499.md),[^cite_note-106][^cite_note-107][^cite_note-IT_11_July_2025-39][^cite_note-Politico_16_July_2025-7] or [far-left](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Far-left),[^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] independent politician on the Irish political spectrum. In May 2025, *[The Phoenix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Phoenix_\(magazine\))* magazine described her as a "long-time [socialist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist)" who had been regarded as "left-wing" and an "[Irish republican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_republican)" during her time in Labour,[^cite_note-110] although Connolly clarified in September 2025 that she never supported the [Provisional IRA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provisional_IRA) during [The Troubles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Troubles) and feels that "violence was never justified" during that time period.[^cite_note-Hot_Press_September_2025-13][^cite_note-IT_23_Sept_2025-111] Connolly identifies as a [pacifist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacifist).[^cite_note-IT_23_Sept_2025-111] [Clare Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Daly) and [Mick Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Wallace) (both former TDs and MEPs) are political allies of Connolly, with Connolly endorsing Daly in her 2024 European re-election campaign;[^cite_note-Politico_16_July_2025-7][^cite_note-LL_9_May_2024-46][^cite_note-IT_20_July_2025-112][^cite_note-Hot_Press_September_2025-13] in a 2025 interview with *[Hot Press](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_Press)*, Connolly said she had "the greatest of respect" for Wallace and Daly.[^cite_note-Hot_Press_September_2025-13]

### Domestic affairs



Connolly at the exhibition "Irish Female [MEPs](258909.md) Past and Present" to mark [International Women's Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Women's_Day) 2024

Connolly supported the [2013 referendum on Irish court reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-third_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland), saying the passing of the referendum "renewed her faith in democracy".[^cite_note-113] She supported Ireland's [2015 referendum on same-sex marriage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-fourth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland).[^cite_note-114] In 2018, Connolly supported the [repeal of the eighth amendment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirty-sixth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland) which allowed the Oireachtas to legislate for abortion,[^cite_note-115] supporting the legalisation of abortion up to 12 weeks.[^cite_note-116]

In 2025, Connolly stated "Our [Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Forces_\(Ireland\)) exist to protect our people, our sovereignty, and ... to foster peace where conflict has broken out". She added: "Ireland needs our Defence Forces. We must value, respect, and support their members. But Ireland does not need an [army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Army)", arguing that "armies fight wars" and are "at odds with a nation's neutrality".[^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9]

Connolly believes that drug use and addiction should be treated primarily as health issues rather than criminal offences. She has expressed support for moves toward [drug decriminalisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Decriminalisation), highlighting the work of politicians such as [Gino Kenny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gino_Kenny) in advancing this cause. In her view, criminalisation fails to address the root causes of addiction and instead punishes vulnerable people.[^cite_note-Hot_Press_September_2025-13] Connolly has described [euthanasia](9587.md) as a complex issue, but stated that she accepts the principle of individual autonomy in end-of-life decisions. She supports allowing people to make such choices provided there are robust safeguards to prevent abuse.[^cite_note-Hot_Press_September_2025-13]

### Foreign affairs



Connolly with US President [Donald Trump](4848272.md) in Dublin, 2026

In 2025, *[Politico](9172097.md)* characterised Connolly's foreign policy views as pro-[neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_neutrality), "often [anti-Western](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Western_sentiment)", and anti-[NATO](21133.md), while also highlighting her "trenchant" criticism of [Israel](9282173.md).[^cite_note-Politico_16_July_2025-7]

#### European Union and militarism



Connolly (left) with [President of the European Commission](24468.md), [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md), during her visit to Ireland, December 2022

Connolly has expressed concerns about [Irish neutrality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_neutrality) being challenged;[^cite_note-117] she said that Ireland's peace is threatened by what she called "the war-mongering [military industrial complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_industrial_complex)" in Europe.[^cite_note-118] In 2025, Connolly remarked that "we certainly cannot trust" countries such as "The US, England and France" because they "are deeply entrenched in an arms industry which causes bloodshed across the world".[^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9] That September, Connolly linked Germany's increased military spending to the "military-industrial complex" and said there were "some parallels with the ’30s", referring to [German rearmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_rearmament) under the Nazis.[^cite_note-119] Some academics responded that the two were "fundamentally different". They said that Nazi re-armament was for [expansionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expansionism) while modern Germany's spending increase was for [deterrence](735661.md) and defence.[^cite_note-120] Germany's ambassador to Ireland, [David Gill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Gill_\(civil_servant\)),[^cite_note-121] and Taoiseach [Micheál Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miche%C3%A1l_Martin) criticised Connolly in response.[^cite_note-122]

Connolly has criticised the European Union as having a "blatant [neoliberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoliberal) agenda".[^cite_note-Indo_17_August-12] She opposed the ratification of the [Treaty of Nice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Nice) in the [2002 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Twenty-sixth_Amendment_of_the_Constitution_of_Ireland),[^cite_note-123][^cite_note-Regan_15_October-124] and the ratification of the [Treaty of Lisbon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Lisbon) in both referendums.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126][^cite_note-Regan_15_October-124] Regarding the [2016 United Kingdom European Union membership referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_United_Kingdom_European_Union_membership_referendum), in a speech in the Dáil shortly after the UK voted to [leave the EU](41688778.md), Connolly said the Irish government "took an active part in the [project of fear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Fear) that sought to scare the British electorate into remaining" and that despite trying "to force a desired result, the electorate was not fooled".[^cite_note-Regan_15_October-124] Connolly also stated during the speech that there is a "democratic deficit that is an integral part of the EU" and she felt that "dissent is not tolerated" with regards to EU membership.[^cite_note-Regan_15_October-124]

Connolly said that "The EU has become increasingly militarised under the leadership of [Ursula von der Leyen](2991932.md) and the [European People's Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_People's_Party)", and "has lost sight of its foundations: a project developed to promote and preserve peace".[^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9] Connolly considers von der Leyen's differing positions of Israel and Russia as hypocritical, saying von der Leyen strongly opposed Russia's actions in Ukraine and was passive over Israel's actions in Gaza.[^cite_note-Regan_15_October-124] On [Europe Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europe_Day) 2025, Connolly declared that she was "ashamed to be European" because she believed the current EU leadership was pro-Israel.[^cite_note-Indo_17_August-12] After launching her presidential bid later that year, Connolly said she does not consider herself a [Eurosceptic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurosceptic) and called herself a "committed European".[^cite_note-Indo_17_August-12]

#### International conflict



Connolly (first from left), [Jerry Buttimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerry_Buttimer) (second from left) and [Pat Buckley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Buckley_\(Irish_politician\)) (fourth from left) meeting [President of](466686.md) [Vietnam](202354.md) [Tô Lâm](54878346.md) (third from left) in October 2024

A vocal critic of Israel and its [war in Gaza](74998519.md), Connolly has called for solidarity with the [Palestinian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_people);[^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9] in April 2025, she said in the Dáil "I challenge all of us to stand up and stop [the genocide](75668427.md) taking place in our name, because we are complicit",[^cite_note-IT_20_July_2025-112] while in July of the same year, she called Israel a "genocidal state".[^cite_note-IT_11_July_2025-39] Connolly said the Irish government had been "dragging their feet" in imposing its [planned sanctions against Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occupied_Territories_Bill), urging it not to water down the sanctions under pressure from US corporate interests.[^cite_note-127]

Connolly has condemned both Russia and NATO over the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9] She called the invasion "illegal and unacceptable"[^cite_note-128] and said Russia "must immediately end its horrific assault".[^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9] Highlighting [Russian opposition to NATO enlargement](19066574.md), she said that "NATO has played a despicable role in [moving forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enlargement_of_NATO) to the border and engaging in warmongering. Ireland has been hypocritical on many levels".[^cite_note-IT_20_July_2025-112][^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9] She has called for continued solidarity with the Ukrainian people[^cite_note-IrishTimes16Aug25-9] and supports sanctions against Russia but would like to see a “discussion on sanctions generally and whether they’re effective or not effective”.[^cite_note-129]

While Mayor of Galway from 2004 to 2005, Connolly spoke in opposition to the [Iraq War](5043324.md).[^cite_note-130] In 2017, she opposed sanctions against [Ba'athist Syria](57134800.md) during the [Syrian civil war](30741795.md), stating that such sanctions were untargeted and only made the situation worse for Syrians.[^cite_note-131][^cite_note-Raymond_17_July_2025-40] As part of a group including [Mick Wallace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Wallace), [Clare Daly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_Daly) and [Maureen O'Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maureen_O'Sullivan_\(politician\)), Connolly visited Syria in 2018. They toured [regime-controlled](57134800.md) territory and met members of a pro-regime militia but did not meet members of the government; according to Connolly "there was no praise for the regime in the company we were in".[^cite_note-Indo_17_August-12][^cite_note-l787-132][^cite_note-j367-133][^cite_note-p111-134]

## Further Information

In September 2026, Connolly visited the "Working Over By\:Donegal Emigrant Working Lives in Scotland 1940-1990" exhibition in [Glasgow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glasgow). At a reception on the same day, she described the visit, which had featured input from relatives of the people depicted, as an "emotional experience" . She also commented "It’s not going over to live, it’s working over \[by], but so many never came back." One of those she met was former [Celtic](69708.md) and [Republic of Ireland](14560.md) [goalkeeper](5348091.md) [Packie Bonner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Packie_Bonner) [^cite_note-135]

## Personal life



Connolly and her husband Brian McEnery in December 2025

Connolly has lived in the [Claddagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claddagh) in [Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway) since 1988. She married woodwork teacher Brian McEnery in 1992 and they have two adult sons.[^cite_note-times_sep25-16][^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137][^cite_note-138] She has worked as [clinical psychologist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinical_psychologist) with the Western Health Board in [Ballinasloe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballinasloe) and [Connemara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connemara) in [County Galway](92632.md), and as a [barrister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrister). She is an [Irish speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language), having done a diploma in Irish at university[^cite_note-Hot_Press_September_2025-13] and also speaks German, having studied psychology in Germany.[^cite_note-139][^cite_note-Indo_17_August-12] She was raised Catholic, but has described herself as [irreligious](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligious).[^cite_note-Hot_Press_September_2025-13]

Connolly has completed both [marathons](49132.md) and [triathlons](31524.md), revealing in the podcast *How to Gael* that she has lost count of how many marathons she has finished. She also recounted that she was five months pregnant when completing the Streets of Galway race in 1995.[^cite_note-140][^cite_note-141] Sligo-based socialist politician [Declan Bree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declan_Bree) is Connolly's brother-in-law.[^cite_note-142] Connolly has two pet cats.[^cite_note-143]
