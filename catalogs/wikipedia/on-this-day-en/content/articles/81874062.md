# 2026 Australia gun buyback program

| Date | 19 December 2025 (announced) |
| --- | --- |
| Location | [Australia](4689264.md) |
| Type | [Gun buyback program](11825603.md) |
| Motive | Response to the [2025 Bondi Beach shooting](81839099.md) |
| Target | Surplus, newly banned and illegal firearms |
| Organised by | [Federal government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_Australia) in partnership with [state and territory governments](4689096.md) |

On 19 December 2025, Australian prime minister [Anthony Albanese](556745.md) announced a proposed national [gun buyback program](11825603.md) following a [terrorist attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terrorist_attack) at [Bondi Beach](368567.md) in [Sydney](27862.md), New South Wales on 14 December 2025.[^cite_note-PMDec19-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Background

In 1996, following the [Port Arthur massacre](39737448.md), a buyback program was put in place. The program saw the collection of more than 650,000 newly prohibited firearms.[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] During the two years when the program was carried out, 1996 and 1997, the percentage of [homicide rates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_intentional_homicide_rate) dropped the most in any two-year timeframe in [Australia](4689264.md) between 1915 and 2004,[^cite_note-6] although the rates increased slightly in 1997, and peaked in 1999 but have declined since 2007.[^cite_note-7]

On 14 December 2025, [a mass shooting occurred at Bondi Beach](81839099.md) during a [Hanukkah](7388.md) celebration with two gunmen allegedly killing fifteen people and injuring forty.[^cite_note-NSW_Police-2025-8][^cite_note-9][^cite_note-\:0-10] The fifteen victims were aged between 10 and 87.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-NSW_Police-2025-8] One of the gunmen had a [firearms licence](450955.md#Licensing) and was the registered owner of six firearms.[^cite_note-12]

## New state and territory firearms laws

On 15 December 2025, at a [National Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Cabinet_\(Australia\)) meeting, all the [state and territory](4689096.md) governments agreed to strengthen firearms laws in the wake of the mass shooting.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15] The [federal government](1222540.md) proposes that state and territory governments agree to ambitious new firearm law reforms no later than March 2026, and that reforms are legislated no later than 1 July 2026.[^cite_note-PMDec19-1]

## Buyback funding

The federal government will introduce legislation to support funding the buyback.[^cite_note-PMDec19-1] The cost of the buyback, which is expected to collect hundreds of thousands of firearms, will be split between the federal government and with the state and territory governments.[^cite_note-PMDec19-1][^cite_note-\:0-10][^cite_note-16] On 20 January, the [federal parliament](302298.md) passed laws to establish the funding scheme for the buyback.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]

## Collection of prohibited firearms

The federal government proposes that states and territories be responsible for the collection, processing and payment to individuals for surrendered firearms. The [Australian Federal Police](341221.md) will be responsible for the destruction of surrendered firearms.[^cite_note-PMDec19-1]

### Implementation

New South Wales Premier Chris Minns has announced plans to begin the buyback program there on 2 November 2026, with individuals being eligible for up to \$1,000 AUD per firearm surrendered. The state also prohibits any individual from owning more than 4 firearms in the wake of the aforementioned [Bondi Beach shooting](81839099.md).[^cite_note-19]
