The **National Assembly**, formerly known as the **Chamber of Deputies**, is the [unicameral](204304.md) [parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament) of the [Republic of Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Djibouti). Established under Title V of the [1992 Constitution](51114502.md), it consists of 65 members who are elected by direct and secret universal suffrage and cannot simultaneously serve in the [government of Djibouti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministries_of_Djibouti). Elections are scheduled to be held once every five years, normally on the last Friday of February. The most recent elections were held on [24 February 2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Djiboutian_parliamentary_election).

Djibouti’s legislative body traces its origins to the colonial era. From 1946 to 1957, the territory—then under foreign rule as [French Somaliland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Somaliland)—was governed by a Representative Council. The 1956 framework law introduced a Territorial Assembly, which functioned as the main legislative body until 1967, when it was replaced by the Chamber of Deputies of the [French Territory of the Afars and the Issas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Territory_of_the_Afars_and_the_Issas). Following Djibouti’s independence following the [1977 referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1977_Afars_and_Issas_independence_referendum), the Chamber of Deputies remained in place, eventually evolving into the modern National Assembly.

## Overview

The National Assembly consists of 65 members known as deputies – 30 [Somali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_people) (30 [Issa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Issa_\(clan\))) and 25 [Afar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afar_people). Members are elected to serve five-year terms in [multi-member constituencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Djibouti), which range from 4 to 37 seats each. Any Djiboutian citizen aged 23 or older, with full civil and political rights, is eligible to run for office. The country’s first multi-party parliamentary elections since independence in 1977 were held in [2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_Djiboutian_parliamentary_election). The ruling coalition, led by the [People's Rally for Progress](622709.md) (RPP), secured 62.7% of the vote but was awarded all legislative seats.

Since its inception, the National Assembly has been overwhelmingly dominated by the RPP, which currently heads the [Union for a Presidential Majority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_the_Presidential_Majority_\(Djibouti\)) (UMP) coalition. From 1981 to 1992, the RPP was the only legal political party. Opposition parties were legalized following the [1992 constitutional referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Djiboutian_constitutional_referendum), but the RPP continued to control the legislature. Beginning in 1997, the party ran elections as part of a coalition—rebranded in 2003 as the UMP—which maintained its firm grip on parliament. It was not until the [2013 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Djiboutian_parliamentary_election) that opposition parties finally secured representation. However, in the [2018 elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Djiboutian_parliamentary_election), which were boycotted by major opposition groups, the UMP retained a dominant two-thirds majority.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2]

Djibouti’s political system remains highly centralized, with executive authority concentrated in the presidency, a position held by [Ismaïl Omar Guelleh](392341.md) since [1999](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_Djiboutian_presidential_election), which has left little room for legislative opposition. As of 2018, a quota mandates that at least 25% of parliamentary seats be held by women.[^cite_note-3]

## Building

The National Assembly building of Djibouti, constructed with partial funding from the Iranian government, was completed on 23 November 2014. However, the debt incurred for the project remains unpaid to this day. The inauguration ceremony was attended by the [Speaker](24397880.md) of the [Islamic Consultative Assembly](383276.md), [Ali Larijani](531848.md), who formally handed over the building.
