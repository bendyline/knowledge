Colombian geography presents formidable challenges to roadbuilders, who need to integrate its largest production centers deep within the Andes with major ports in both the Atlantic and Pacific oceans. All of this creates a heavy premium to roadbuilding, compared with the cost of building highways in flat terrain. Therefore, the Colombian government is undertaking a great effort in order to improve the highway system, under the name of Fourth Generation Highways, with the intent of updating major roads to international safety and speed standards. This project will be funded through both public and private capital, with a total worth of nearly US\$23 billion, accounting to a yearly investment of 3% of national GDP, improving or building a grand total of over 8,000 km of roads.[^cite_note-1] These roads are expected to improve Colombia's competitiveness in order to successfully take advantage of the many trade agreements signed in recent years.[^cite_note-2]

Highway safety in Colombia is enforced by the [Highway Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highway_Police_\(Colombia\)), a unit of the [National Police of Colombia](7278792.md). Colombia is crossed by the [Pan-American Highway](301108.md).

## Primary routes

The main highways (with primary route numbers) include:

### Western Trunk - National Route 25

Colombia is crossed by the Western Trunk highway (part of the [Pan-American Highway](301108.md)) that connects to the south with [Ecuador](9334.md) (where the highway is called the **[Sierra Trunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highways_in_Ecuador)** or *Troncal de Sierra*) and other South American countries. This international route connects to the national highway network on the Rumichaca Bridge in [Nariño Department](549702.md), which marks the international border between Colombia and Ecuador. From there and to the north, this road connects the cities of [Pasto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasto), [Popayán](657155.md), [Cali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cali), the [Colombian Coffee-Growers Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Coffee-Growers_Axis) (*Eje Cafetero* or "Coffee Region"), [Anserma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anserma,_Caldas), [Medellín](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medell%C3%ADn), [Sincelejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sincelejo) and [Barranquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla), where it connects to the Caribbean Transverse highway.[^cite_note-TRONCAL-3] Difficulties arise in the [Darién Gap](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari%C3%A9n_Gap) between [Panama](22997.md) and Colombia as there are no connections by land in Colombia with [Central America](6121.md) and the rest of [North America](21139.md).

### Magdalena Trunk - National Route 45

The Magdalena Trunk crosses the section between the town of [San Miguel, Putumayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Miguel,_Putumayo) at the border with Ecuador and the point known as *Y de Ciénaga*, a few kilometers from the city of [Santa Marta](547214.md) on the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md). The road runs parallel to the [Magdalena River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magdalena_River), mainly on its right bank. This pathway allows the integration of central Colombia with the system of ports located on its Caribbean coast as well as the ports of [Cartagena](63927.md), Barranquilla and [Santa Marta](547214.md).[^cite_note-TRONCAL-3]

### Caribbean Transverse - National Route 90

The Caribbean Transverse highway runs parallel to the Caribbean Sea coastline and joins the cities of [Turbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbo,_Colombia) (Antioquia) and [Paraguachón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paraguach%C3%B3n) (La Guajira), where it connects to the highway network in [Venezuela](32374.md). The highway connects the capital cities of this region: [Montería](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monter%C3%ADa), Sincelejo, Cartagena, Barranquilla, Santa Marta and [Riohacha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riohacha). This route is also called Caribbean Trunk and connects to the Magdalena Trunk (National Route 45) in a place known as *Y de Ciénaga*, just 6 km from the town of [Ciénaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ci%C3%A9naga,_Magdalena) (Magdalena) and the Western Trunk at two different points: in the municipality of [Toluviejo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toluviejo) (18 km from Sincelejo) and the city of Barranquilla.[^cite_note-4]

### Buenaventura Transverse - Puerto Carreño - National Route 40

The Buenaventura Transverse highway connects the country's largest port with the Venezuelan border in [Puerto Carreño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Carre%C3%B1o).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-5] Highway sections 06 (Bogotá - [Villavicencio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villavicencio)) and 07 (Villavicencio - [Puerto López](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_L%C3%B3pez,_Meta)) are part of the Autopista al Llano ("Llano Tollway") section of the Buenaventura Transverse highway.

### Central Trunk - National Route 45A

The Central Trunk highway connects the city of Bogotá with the departments of [Boyacá](185665.md) and [Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santander_Department). Major cities on this route include [Zipaquirá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zipaquir%C3%A1), [Chiquinquirá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiquinquir%C3%A1) and [Bucaramanga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucaramanga). The highway ends in the municipality of [San Alberto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Alberto,_Cesar), where it connects with the Magdalena Trunk highway (National Route 45).

### North-Central Trunk - National Route 55

The North-Central Trunk highway begins in the village of La Caro in the town of [Chía](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ch%C3%ADa,_Cundinamarca), connecting to Bogotá and [Cundinamarca Department](189405.md) to the municipalities of [Gachancipá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gachancip%C3%A1), [Tocancipá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tocancip%C3%A1) and [Villapinzón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villapinz%C3%B3n). In Boyacá Department, the route changes to a north-south direction and later reaches [Tunja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tunja), the capital of Boyacá Department. The highway continues through the towns of [Paipa](10689213.md), [Duitama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duitama) and [Soatá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soat%C3%A1,_Boyac%C3%A1) in Boyacá Department and [Málaga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%A1laga,_Santander) in Santander Department. The highway terminates in [Puerto Santander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Santander,_Norte_de_Santander) near the border with Venezuela.

### Carretera Marginal de la Selva - National Route 65

Carretera Marginal de la Selva ("Jungle Border Highway") is an important [South American](26769.md) route originally planned in 1963 to join the [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon_River) regions of [Bolivia](3462.md), [Peru](170691.md), Ecuador, Colombia and Venezuela. In Colombia, this route begins at the San Miguel International Bridge (on the border with Ecuador) and follows the first stretch of the Magdalena Trunk highway to the municipality of [Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Villa,_Putumayo?action=edit\&redlink=1), where National Route 65 turns north toward the right side of the [Cordillera Oriental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cordillera_Oriental_\(Colombia\)) mountains. The highway is scheduled to be extended to [Saravena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saravena) on the border with Venezuela; however, the section between [Mina Blanca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mina_Blanca,_Caquet%C3%A1?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [La Uribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Uribe) is not built yet, so the highway is currently divided into two sections. The southern section is also known as the **Forest Trunk** section and the northern section is known as the **Llano Trunk** section.
