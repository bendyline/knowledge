The **Mayo county football team** ([/ˈmeɪoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English)[^cite_note-2]) represents [Mayo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_Mayo) in men's [Gaelic football](37688.md) and is governed by [Mayo GAA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_GAA), the [county board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_board_\(Gaelic_games\)) of the [Gaelic Athletic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelic_Athletic_Association). The team competes in the three major annual inter-county competitions; the [All-Ireland Senior Football Championship](885732.md), the [Connacht Senior Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship) and the [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)).

Mayo's home ground is [MacHale Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacHale_Park), [Castlebar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlebar). The team's manager is [Andy Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Moran).

Mayo was the second [Connacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connacht) county to win an [All-Ireland Senior Football Championship](885732.md) (SFC), following [Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway_county_football_team), but the first to appear in the final. The team last won the Connacht Senior Championship in [2021](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship), the All-Ireland Senior Championship in [2026](80557192.md) and the National League in [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)).

Mayo has acquired a long-term record for reaching [All-Ireland SFC finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_finals) only to fall at the ultimate hurdle. In [1989](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final), the county reached a first All-Ireland SFC final since its last previous appearance in 1951 only to lose to [Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_county_football_team). In [1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final), a freak point by [Meath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meath_county_football_team) at the end of the final forced a replay, which saw Mayo concede another late score that would deny the county victory. [Kerry](63970030.md) bridged an 11-year title gap against Mayo in the [1997 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final) with a three-point win, before torturing them by eight points in the [2004 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final) and thirteen points in the [2006 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final).[^cite_note-despair_cannot_last_forever-3] In the [2012 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final), [Donegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donegal_county_football_team) bridged a 20-year gap between titles, helped in no small part by a nightmare opening quarter for Mayo as [Michael Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Murphy_\(Gaelic_footballer\)) launched a rocket of a shot into the goal after three minutes. Then, in the eleventh minute, [Colm McFadden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colm_McFadden) seized the ball from the grasp of [Kevin Keane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Keane) and slid it into the net for a second Donegal goal. Mayo managed thirteen points to Donegal's two goals and eleven, only got on the scoresheet after sixteen minutes when already two goals behind and never led during the match.[^cite_note-despair_cannot_last_forever-3][^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5] Mayo qualified for the [2013 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final), and once more came up short, this time being seen off by a [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_county_football_team) team which won by a single point. The [2016 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final) likewise, a single point against Dublin, though this time after a replay; the drawn game featuring two [own goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal) by Mayo players, a previously unknown occurrence in the sport, which left Mayo behind by five points at half-time despite holding Dublin scoreless for the first 30 minutes of the game. The [2017 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final), and another one-point loss to Dublin. The [2020 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final) and Mayo conceded the fastest goal scored in the history of [All-Ireland SFC finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_finals) on the way to another loss to Dublin, this one by five points. Another five-point loss followed in the [2021 final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final), with [Ryan O'Donoghue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_O'Donoghue) hitting a penalty against the goalpost and numerous other goal chances being squandered. Since 1989, Mayo has played in 13 finals (including replays), losing 11 of them, with the others finishing level. Mayo managed to beat Kerry in the [2026 All-Ireland SFC final](83772902.md), achieving their first title in 75 years.&#x20;

## History

Though not affiliated with the GAA through the 1890s, there is strong evidence of local GAA activity in Mayo and [the rivalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway%E2%80%93Mayo_Gaelic_football_rivalry) with [Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway_county_football_team) which brought success to both counties from the 1930s onwards was already in evidence.

Between them, Galway and Mayo have won more than three quarters of the [Connacht Senior Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship) (SFC) titles that have been contested.

Mayo has an unequalled number of consecutive [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)) titles. The Mayo team were champions in 1934, 1935, 1936, 1937, 1938, and 1939.

### Early years: First All-Ireland SFC title

One of the great turning points in GAA history west of the [River Shannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_Shannon) was the 1935 Connacht SFC final when 26,000 spectators witnessed then league title holders Mayo defeat then All-Ireland SFC holders Galway at [Dr Hyde Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_Hyde_Park) in [Roscommon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roscommon).

Mayo was leading by a goal in the last minute of the 1936 Connacht SFC final when [Brendan Nestor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_Nestor) scored an equaliser for Galway; Nestor [raised the flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scoring_in_Gaelic_games) himself and caused a riot. However, Mayo won the replay and went on to capture their first All-Ireland SFC, defeating [Laois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laois_county_football_team) by 4–11 (23) to 0–5 (5) in [the final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1936_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final).

The following year, 1937, the county was the victim of a [Louis Blessing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Blessing) last-minute goal in the All-Ireland SFC semi-final against [Cavan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavan_county_football_team), another match that featured a pitch invasion. This ended Mayo's run of 57 matches without defeat.

Mayo dominated the [National Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)) for six years, but pulled out of the 1939–40 league in a grievance over the 1939 semi-final, a bad-tempered draw and replay with [Kerry](63970030.md). Mayo returned to the competition to win its seventh league title in 1941. Due to the [Second World War](32927.md), the league was suspended for four seasons until 1946, and Mayo was unable to add to these successes.

### Post-war: Second and third All-Ireland SFC titles

Following Mayo's 1939 Connacht SFC final victory, the team took nine years to emerge from the province again.

Mayo narrowly lost the [1948 All-Ireland SFC Final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) to Cavan and [1949 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1949_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) semi-final to [Meath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meath_county_football_team).

However, the county returned to [Croke Park](322713.md) to win the [1950 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1950_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) when [Louth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louth_county_football_team)'s [Sean Boyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Boyle_\(Gaelic_footballer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) had his kick-out charged down and [Mick Flanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Flanagan?action=edit\&redlink=1) broke through for a freak winning goal, Mayo winning the game by 2–5 (11) to 1–6 (9). Mayo retained the All-Ireland SFC [the following year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship), with [Pádraig Carney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1draig_Carney) (known as the flying doctor because he had returned from the United States to play the game) scoring three late points to give Mayo a 2–8 (14) to 0–9 (9) victory against Meath. During this period, Mayo also won the National League in 1949 and 1954, the county's eighth and ninth titles in this competition. Eight wides and a one-point defeat in the replayed [1955 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1955_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) semi-final against [Dublin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dublin_county_football_team) brought this period of success to an end.

Mayo went 12 years without winning another Connacht SFC title. The county then won the 1967 Connacht SFC, ending Galway's four-in-a-row All-Ireland SFC expectations in the process. Another Connacht SFC followed in 1969. Mayo also added a tenth National League title in 1970, defeating [Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_county_football_team) in the final by a scoreline of 4–7 (19) to 0–10 (10). Despite this success, the 1970s was arguably the least successful decade in the history of Mayo football, as the team failed to win a Connacht SFC title, coming closest when losing the 1975 Connacht SFC final to [Sligo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sligo_county_football_team) after a replay.

### 1980–1991: Return to regional prominence

While Mayo had not won an All-Ireland SFC title since 1951, nor even qualified for the final, the 1980s brought a marked improvement in the team's fortunes. The [1981 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1981_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) bridged a twelve-year gap when Mayo defeated Sligo at [MacHale Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacHale_Park) in [Castlebar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlebar) in the Connacht SFC final by a scoreline of 0–12 to 0–4; however, this was followed by a heavy defeat to the sport's then dominant Kerry team, by a scoreline of 1–6 to 2–19.

The [1982 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) featured a heavy Connacht SFC final defeat to Galway at [St Jarlath's Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St_Jarlath's_Park) in [Tuam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuam), and a rematch of that final the [following year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1983_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) in Castlebar brought another defeat to Galway, by three points on this occasion. Mayo had by then been [relegated](648049.md) to Division 2 of the National League and lost a promotion play-off to [Roscommon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roscommon_county_football_team) in early 1984. For [the third consecutive year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship), Galway and Mayo met in the Connacht SFC final, this time at [Pearse Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearse_Stadium). The result remained the same, with Galway completing the three-in-a-row, by a scoreline of 2–13 to 2–9.

Mayo and Galway were positioned on opposite sides of the [1985 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1985_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) draw, and easy victories over [Leitrim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leitrim_county_football_team) (semi-final) and Roscommon in the final, allowed Mayo to win the title. The final notably coincided with the retirement of Roscommon player [Dermot Earley Snr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dermot_Earley_Snr), whom the Mayo players chaired from the pitch at the end of the match. Mayo played Dublin in the All-Ireland SFC semi-final, the county's first championship meeting with the Dubs since 1955 ending in a draw, with a scoreline of 1–13 to 1–13. Mayo player [John Finn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Finn)'s jaw was broken in mysterious circumstances during the first game; tension was high for the replay. However, despite a goal from Padraig Brogan, Dublin easily defeated Mayo by eight points, with a scoreline of 2–12 to 1–7. Still, this was as close as Mayo had come to an All-Ireland SFC final since 1955. [Dermot Flanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dermot_Flanagan), [Willie Joe Padden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Joe_Padden) and [Kevin McStay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_McStay) received [All Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GAA_GPA_All_Stars_Awards) in recognition of their performances during the year.

Although 1986 brought [promotion](648049.md) to Division 1 of the National League, [that year's championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) campaign ended with a Connacht SFC semi-final defeat to Roscommon at McHale Park. Mayo reached the Connacht SFC final in the [1987 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1987_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship), losing to Galway in a low-scoring match which finished with a scoreline of 0–7 to 0–8. Though the team was [relegated](648049.md) to Division 2 of the National League in 1988, the county fared better in [that year's championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship). Connacht SFC wins over Sligo, Leitrim and Roscommon (the last in the final) brought Mayo to another All-Ireland SFC semi-final. However, reigning All-Ireland SFC champions Meath defeated the county, by a scoreline of 0–16 to 2–5, on its way to defend the title.

Former player [John O'Mahony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_O'Mahony_\(Mayo_politician\)) took over as manager ahead of the 1989 season. Mixed results in the league were followed by [that year's championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship). After a draw in the Connacht SFC semi-final against Galway in Tuam, Mayo won the replay by a scoreline of 2–13 to 1–8 in Castlebar, with goals from [Liam McHale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_McHale) and [Anthony "Larry" Finnerty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Finnerty). The final, against Roscommon, was held in Castlebar. This too resulted in a draw, 0–12 to 1–9. A further 70 minutes at [Dr Hyde Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dr_Hyde_Park) a week later could not separate the teams either, with Mayo winning after extra-time by a scoreline of 3–14 to 2–13. A scrappy game against [Tyrone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrone_county_football_team) in the All-Ireland SFC semi-final ended with Mayo winning by a scoreline of 0–12 to 1–6. This victory was made famous by an iconic image of a bandaged Willie Joe Padden. Mayo's first [All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final) appearance in 38 years was against [Cork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cork_county_football_team), the runner-up in the previous two competitions. Though Mayo substitute Finnerty scored a goal in the middle of the second half, Cork won by a scoreline of 0–17 to 1–11. All Stars followed that year for [Gabriel Irwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabriel_Irwin), [Jimmy Browne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jimmy_Browne), [Dermot Flanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dermot_Flanagan), Willie Joe Padden and [Noel Durkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Durkin). The team Mayo selected for the 1989 All-Ireland SFC final was full of players more accustomed to the role of midfielder than anything else, e.g. T. J. Kilgallon at centre-back and Greg Maher at wing-forward.[^cite_note-losing-formula-6]

Mayo's [1990 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) was brief, a 2–11 to 1–12 defeat to Galway in Tuam. The [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1990_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) proved little better; after big wins against London and Galway, Mayo met Roscommon in the Connacht SFC final. [Derek Duggan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derek_Duggan?action=edit\&redlink=1)'s long-range last-minute free for Roscommon led to a replay at Dr Hyde Park, a game which Mayo lost by one point on a scoreline of 0–13 to 1–9. John O'Mahony resigned as manager at the end of the season, following a dispute between himself and the county board over team [selector](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Selector_\(sports\)) appointments.

### 1991–1995: Further decline; relegation to Division 3

Though Mayo won Connacht SFC titles in 1992 and 1993, the early 1990s was a largely unsuccessful period for the county, as the standard of Connacht football declined significantly between 1990 and 1995.

Former Dublin player [Brian McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_McDonald_\(Dublin_footballer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) took over as manager for the 1992 season, which proved more successful for Mayo. Following a Connacht SFC first round replay victory over Galway, Mayo defeated Sligo in the Connacht SFC semi-final and then Roscommon in the final. This set up a first championship meeting for the county with [Donegal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donegal_county_football_team) in a [1992 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) semi-final. Donegal, however, defeated Mayo by a scoreline of 0–13 to 0–9 to qualify for [a first All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final) in team history, achieving an unanticipated victory over Dublin in that game. TJ Kilgallon received an All Star at midfield. A player revolt, over allegations concerning his training methods (which were said to include pushing a car around a car park), led to McDonald departing as manager.

Former Kerry player [Jack O'Shea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jack_O'Shea) succeeded McDonald as manager for the 1993 season. Consecutive Connacht SFC titles were secured in a poor game against Roscommon, which finished with a scoreline of 1–5 to 0–7. But humiliation followed in the [1993 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) semi-final against Cork, as Mayo lost by 20 points, 5–15 to 0–10. [Kevin O'Neill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_O'Neill_\(Mayo_footballer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) was a rare bright spot for Mayo, and the young forward ended the year with an All Star. Despite the result, O'Shea remained in place for the [1994 campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship), which ended in a Connacht SFC final to a Leitrim team then managed by John O'Mahony. This was only Leitrim's second Connacht SFC title, and prompted O'Shea to resign.

[Anthony Egan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthony_Egan) succeeded O'Shea as manager but 1995 was a low point for Mayo, with [relegation](648049.md) to Division 3 of the National League followed by a [championship campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) that ended in a seven-point defeat to Galway in the Connacht SFC final in Tuam.

### 1995–1999: Three championship finals; one draw, two losses

[John Maughan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Maughan), noted for managing [Clare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clare_county_football_team) to a [Munster Senior Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munster_Senior_Football_Championship) title in 1992, was brought in as manager in an effort to improve the team's position. Maughan, a former Mayo player and [Defence Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_Forces_\(Ireland\)) officer, was renowned for the physical fitness regime he imposed on his teams; improvements were swift. Mayo won Division 3 of the [National League in 1996](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)); then the county won its third Connacht SFC title of the decade, defeating Galway in the final. Maughan's team produced its year's best performance in the [1996 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) semi-final against Kerry. Mayo won by a scoreline of 2–13 to 1–10, with a [James Horan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Horan_\(Gaelic_footballer\)) goal lobbed from a distance of 40 metres by in the last minute of the game sealing the victorya nd giving Mayo its first championship victory against Kerry since 1951.

[Ray Dempsey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ray_Dempsey)'s 45th-minute goal gave Mayo a lead of six points in the [1996 All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final) against Meath; however, a Meath comeback, culminating in a last-minute [Colm Coyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colm_Coyle) long-range point, saw the game end in a draw on a scoreline of 1–9 to 0–12. During a bad-tempered replay – which included a brawl in which Coyle and [Liam McHale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liam_McHale), one of Mayo's most influential players, was sent off – Mayo led by four points at half-time but conceded a goal to [Tommy Dowd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Dowd_\(Gaelic_footballer\)) and lost by one-point on a scoreline of 2–9 to 1–11. [Kenneth Mortimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_Mortimer_\(Gaelic_footballer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1), Pat Holmes, [James Nallen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Nallen), Liam McHale and James Horan later received All Stars.

Mayo defeated Galway, Leitrim and Sligo (the last of these in the final) to retain the Connacht SFC title in [1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship). The defeat of Galway was Mayo's first victory in Tuam for more than 30 years. Mayo reached its second successive All-Ireland SFC final following a 0–13 to 0–7 victory over [Leinster Senior Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leinster_Senior_Football_Championship) winner [Offaly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offaly_county_football_team). However, Mayo lost the [1997 All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final) to Kerry by a scoreline of 0–13 to 1–7 in a poor game illuminated by the skill of Kerry forward [Maurice Fitzgerald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurice_Fitzgerald_\(footballer\)). Mortimer received another All Star, with [Pat Fallon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Fallon_\(Gaelic_footballer\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) also receiving one.

Mayo entered the [1998](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1998_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) Connacht SFC heavily favoured to win the competition, but John O'Mahony's Galway team ambushed the county in the preliminary round in Castlebar. Two [Ciarán McDonald](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ciar%C3%A1n_McDonald) goals could not prevent a 2–6 to 1–13 defeat.

Mayo had achieved [promotion](648049.md) in the [1997–98 National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1997%E2%80%9398_National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)) and returned to Division 1 of the National League. [Another Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1999_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) title (Mayo's fifth of the decade) followed, after wins over [New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_county_football_team), Roscommon and Galway, this last in the final. However, Mayo failed to reach another All-Ireland SFC final, losing to Cork by a scoreline of 2–12 to 0–12 in the All-Ireland SFC semi-final. James Horan received an All Star and manager John Maughan resigned after four tumultuous seasons.

### 1999–2010: Two championship finals

[Pat Holmes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pat_Holmes_\(Gaelic_footballer\)) was manager for the [2000 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2000_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship), but his managerial career got off to a poor start with a Connacht SFC first round defeat against Sligo. When National League matches resumed in the spring of 2001, Mayo won the competition for the eleventh time. In what was the only national final played between the two western rivals, Mayo defeated Galway by a scoreline of 0–13 to 0–12, courtesy of substitute [Marty McNicholas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_McNicholas?action=edit\&redlink=1)'s late point. This was Mayo's first national title since 1970. League success did not transfer to the championship and Mayo lost the Connacht SFC final to Roscommon following a last-minute [Gerry Lohan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerry_Lohan) goal. That year's introduction of the All-Ireland SFC qualifier system meant Mayo received a second chance. The county met [Westmeath](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westmeath_county_football_team) but this too ended in a Mayo defeat, albeit after [extra time](633242.md), by a scoreline of 1–14 to 0–16.

Galway defeated Mayo in the opening round of the [2002 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship). Into the qualifiers once more, Mayo fared better this time with wins against Roscommon, [Limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick_county_football_team) and [Tipperary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tipperary_county_football_team), facing the latter duo for the first time in the championship. In the All-Ireland SFC quarter-final (the quarter-final stage having been added to the championship in 2001), Cork again provided the opposition and Mayo again lost, by a scoreline of 0–16 to 1–10 on this occasion.

Mayo's failure to win a Connacht SFC title under Holmes and his departure at the end of the 2002 season paved the way for John Maughan's return for a second term as team manager. The [first season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) of Maughan's return was also a failure as Galway defeated Mayo in the Connacht SFC final and [Fermanagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fermanagh_county_football_team) defeated Mayo in the last 12 of [that year's All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2003_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship).

Mayo regained the Connacht SFC title with ease in 2004, securing facile wins over New York, Galway and Roscommon, with none of the teams able to finish its match within five points of Mayo. The county then attained an unexpected victory over reigning All-Ireland SFC champions Tyrone in the [All-Ireland SFC quarter-final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2004_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) by a scoreline of 0–16 to 1–9. However, Mayo's form rapidly deteriorated following this win. A struggle to overcome surprise All-Ireland SFC semi-finalists Fermanagh following a replay led to an eight-point loss to Kerry in the All-Ireland SFC final on a scoreline of 1–20 to 2–9. James Nallen and Ciarán McDonald received All Stars that year.

In the [2005 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship), Mayo did not retain its title, losing to Galway in the 2005 Connacht SFC final. A three-point victory over Cavan in the All-Ireland SFC qualifiers was followed by a three-point All-Ireland SFC quarter-final defeat to Kerry, bringing an end to Maughan's second term as manager.

[Mickey Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mickey_Moran) then became Mayo's first manager from outside the county since Jack O'Shea managed the team in the early 1990s. In the [2006 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship), Moran led the team to another Connacht SFC title, defeating Galway by one point in the final. Following an unconvincing replay victory over [Laois](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laois_county_football_team) in the All-Ireland SFC quarter-final, in the semi-final against Dublin, Mayo produced arguably its greatest performance since the defeat of Kerry ten years previously. Even before the game started tensions were raised by Mayo warming up into the [Hill 16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hill_16) end. They were soon joined by Dublin, and some jostling occurred between players and management teams. The match itself was of high quality and resulted in Mayo coming from seven points behind with 20 minutes remaining to defeat pre-match favourite Dublin by a scoreline of 1–16 to 2–12. Ciarán McDonald's winning point from under the [Hogan Stand](322713.md) was a particular highlight. Kerry again awaited Mayo in [the All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final). Kerry defeated Mayo at the competition's ultimate stage for the third time in ten years, this time by a scoreline of 4–15 to 3–5. [Alan Dillon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Dillon) won his first All Star award, as did [Conor Mortimer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conor_Mortimer), joining his brother Kenneth as an award winner. Despite the relative success of the 2006 season, rumours persisted of divisions between Moran and the Mayo County Board and Moran was not retained as manager for the 2007 season.

John O'Mahony returned as Mayo manager following a 16-year absence during which he had won a Connacht SFC title with Leitrim (1994) as well as two All-Ireland SFC titles with Galway (1998 and 2001). In his first season back as manager, O'Mahony led Mayo to the [National League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)) but this ended in defeat to Donegal. A seven-point defeat to Galway followed in the opening round of the 2007 Connacht SFC and Mayo exited the [2007 championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) in the second round of the All-Ireland SFC qualifiers with a defeat to [Derry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derry_county_football_team) at [Celtic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celtic_Park_\(Derry\)), a game in which Ciarán McDonald made his final appearance for Mayo as a [substitute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substitution_\(sport\)).

Mayo lost the [2008 Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship) final by one point to Galway. The county then lost narrowly to eventual All-Ireland SFC winner Tyrone in a [2008 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) qualifier by a scoreline of 0–13 to 1–9.

Mayo defeated Galway in the [2009 Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship) final by a scoreline of 2–12 to 1–14, with [Peader Gardiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peader_Gardiner?action=edit\&redlink=1)'s injury-time point giving Mayo a first (and only) Connacht SFC title of O'Mahony's second term as manager, and its 42nd title overall. However, a 2–15 to 1–15 defeat to Meath followed in the [2009 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) quarter-final.

The 2010 season was a [nadir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nadir) for Mayo. Cork defeated it in a [National League final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)), Sligo defeated it in the first round of the [2010 Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship) and [Longford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longford_county_football_team) defeated it in round one of the [2010 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) qualifiers. John O'Mahony immediately resigned as Mayo manager after this game.

### 2010–2025: Six championship finals, relegation and disposal of McStay

James Horan replaced O'Mahony as manager for the 2011 campaign. Horan had won a [Mayo Senior Football Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayo_Senior_Football_Championship) with [Ballintubber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballintubber_GAA) as manager the previous year. After nearly losing in London in the first round of the [2011 Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship), Mayo won that year's Connacht SFC title by defeating Roscommon at a rain-sodden Dr Hyde Park. Mayo was the [underdog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Underdog) ahead of its [2011 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) quarter-final against title holder Cork but won by four points. The county's championship campaign ended at the semi-final stage, with a nine-point defeat to Kerry, but the improved performances augured well for the future.[^cite_note-7] [Andy Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Moran) was subsequently selected as an All Star [full-forward](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Full-forward).

Mayo reached the [2012 National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)) final but lost to Cork by a scoreline of 2–10 to 0–11. Mayo retained the [Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship) title with wins against Leitrim and Sligo, before defeating [Down](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Down_county_football_team) in the [2012 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) quarter-final. Encountering the reigning champion Dublin in an All-Ireland SFC semi-final, Mayo overcame a Dublin rally to win by a scoreline of 0–19 to 0–16 and qualify for a first All-Ireland SFC final since 2006. Conceding two goals in the first eleven minutes of [that game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final), Mayo to Donegal by a scoreline of 2–11 to 0–13. Alan Dillon received his second All Star, while [Ger Cafferkey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ger_Cafferkey), [Keith Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Higgins) and [Lee Keegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Keegan) each received their first.

Mayo easily won its third consecutive [Connacht SFC in 2013](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship), defeating Galway by 17 points, Roscommon by 12 points and first-time finalist London by 16 points. Mayo met Donegal in the [2013 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) quarter-final, winning comprehensively. A six-point win over Tyrone returned Mayo to the [All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final). A close game was ultimately decided by a single point in favour of Mayo's opponent, with a scoreline of 2–12 to 1–14.[^cite_note-8] Higgins and Keegan received their second All Stars, while [Colm Boyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colm_Boyle) and [Aidan O'Shea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aidan_O'Shea_\(Mayo_Gaelic_footballer\)) both received their first.

Horan led Mayo to the [Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship) title in his fourth year as team manager, after wins against New York, Roscommon and a victory against Galway in the final by a scoreline of 3–14 to 0–16. A one-point win over Cork led to a [2014 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) semi-final meeting with Kerry. An improved second-half performance had Mayo leading by five points as the last five minutes approached; however, a [Kieran Donaghy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kieran_Donaghy) goal levelled the game, 1–16 apiece. The GAA fixed the replay for [Gaelic Grounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelic_Grounds) in [Limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick) as Croke Park was unavailable due to it hosting a game of [American football](18951490.md). Another eventful match occurred there and the teams were again level after 70 minutes of play. Kerry stretched its lead in [extra time](633242.md) and won by a scoreline of 3–16 to 3–13. James Horan resigned as team manager immediately afterwards. Keith Higgins won his third consecutive All Star, with Colm Boyle receiving his second and [Cillian O'Connor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cillian_O'Connor) his first.

Mayo appointed Pat Holmes and [Noel Connelly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noel_Connelly) as joint managers ahead of the 2015 season. After defeating Galway in the [2015 Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship) semi-final, a 6–25 to 2–11 victory over Sligo gave Mayo a record-breaking fifth consecutive Connacht SFC title. Another [All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) quarter-final defeat of Donegal led to Mayo meeting Dublin again in the All-Ireland SFC semi-final. Seven points down with five minutes to go, a Cillian O'Connor penalty helped Mayo force a draw, 1–15 to 2–12. The replay was played at a high tempo, but after an even first half, Dublin pulled away in the second half to win by seven points, 3–15 to 1–14, because of a bad error by management. Lee Keegan received his third All Star and Aidan O'Shea received his second. A player protest against management over the winter led to the resignation of Holmes and Connelly.

[Stephen Rochford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Rochford) succeeded them as Mayo manager for the 2016 season. Though [its league performance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)) was poor, Mayo avoided [relegation](648049.md) from Division 1 for an eighteenth consecutive year. A heavy favourite to win a sixth successive Connacht SFC title, Galway achieved an unexpected win by a scoreline of 0–12 to 1–12 against Mayo in the [semi-final of the competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship). Mayo advanced through the [2016 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) qualifiers, with unconvincing victories against Fermanagh, [Kildare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kildare_county_football_team) and Westmeath to reach an All-Ireland SFC quarter-final encounter with Tyrone. In a tight game, Mayo won by a point, 0–13 to 0–12. Tipperary awaited in the All-Ireland SFC semi-final, which Mayo won by 2–13 to 0–14 to set up an [All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) against Dublin. Mayo's [Kevin McLoughlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_McLoughlin) and Colm Boyle both scored early [own goals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Own_goal) to give Dublin a significant half-time lead (though no Dublin player scored in the first 30 minutes of the game). Cillian O'Connor scored an equalising point in the 77th minute of play to leave the score at 0–15 to 2–9. Thus, a replay, which ended in another All-Ireland SFC final single-point defeat. Cillian O'Connor had an opportunity to bring the game to [extra time](633242.md) in the 76th minute but he missed the free. The game finished with a scoreline of 1–15 to 1–14. [David Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Clarke_\(Gaelic_footballer\)) received his first All Star, Lee Keegan received his fourth All Star and was named [All Stars Footballer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Stars_Footballer_of_the_Year) for 2016, Colm Boyle received his third All Star and [Brendan Harrison](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brendan_Harrison) also received an All Star.



A Mayo flag flying on the day of the [2017 All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final)

After a [2017 Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship) semi-final loss to Galway, Mayo required [extra time](633242.md) in two games and played a total of eight games to reach the [2017 All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final) against Dublin. After conceding an early goal in that final, Mayo dominated the game and held control for most of the first half, leading by one point at half-time. However, Dublin recovered and [Dean Rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dean_Rock) scored a free late into injury time to win the game for his team by a scoreline of 1–17 to 1–16, inflicting a one-point defeat on Mayo at this stage and to the same opponent for a second consecutive year. Andy Moran received his second All Star and was named [All Stars Footballer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Stars_Footballer_of_the_Year) for 2017. Keith Higgins and Colm Boyle received their fourth All Stars, Aidan O'Shea received his third All Star, David Clarke received his second All Star and [Chris Barrett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Barrett_\(Gaelic_footballer\)) also received an All Star.

Another poor [league campaign in 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)) required a late Kevin McLoughlin point to salvage a draw against a Donegal team which that result [relegated](648049.md) instead. Galway defeated Mayo in the opening round of the [2018 Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship). This obliged Mayo to participate in the [All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) qualifiers again, and although Mayo defeated a poor [Limerick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Limerick_county_football_team) team in Round One, Mayo had to earn a hard-fought win over Tipperary to secure a place in the next game against Kildare. A rejuvenated Kildare team won the game by a scoreline of 0–21 to 0–19. Rochford resigned as team manager not long after this defeat, citing that he "didn't have the backing of the board".

James Horan returned as manager in 2019. Mayo lost to Roscommon in the [2019 Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship). The county defeated Down, [Armagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armagh_county_football_team) and Galway in [All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) qualifiers. The campaign ended in defeat (by a scoreline of 3–14 to 1–10) to Dublin at the All-Ireland SFC semi-final stage, with [Con O'Callaghan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Con_O'Callaghan_\(Gaelic_footballer\)) scoring two goals and [Brian Fenton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Fenton) scoring the other.[^cite_note-9] [Paddy Durcan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_Durcan) received an All Star.

The county was [relegated](648049.md) from Division 1 on the final day of the [2020 National League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)), its first time to exit the top flight in 23 years, with Tyrone heavily defeating the team in Castlebar.[^cite_note-10] Mayo reached the [2020 All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2020_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final), Dublin again the victors, while conceding the fastest goal scored in the history of [All-Ireland SFC finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_finals), knocking more than 20 seconds off a record which had stood for 58 years.[^cite_note-11]

In 2021, Mayo gained [promotion](648049.md) at the first attempt back to Division 1 of the National Football League with a 2–22 to 2–18 win over Clare in the final round of that competition. [Another All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_Final), another five-point loss, with [Ryan O'Donoghue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_O'Donoghue) hitting a penalty against the goalpost and numerous other goal chances being squandered.[^cite_note-12] A [2022 All-Ireland SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship) quarter-final exit to Kerry was followed within hours by the resignation of manager James Horan.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

In [2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship), under the management of [Kevin McStay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_McStay), Mayo lost to Cavan in a championship game for the first time since the [1948 All-Ireland SFC final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship_final).[^cite_note-15] Less than a week later, McStay became ill during training at MacHale Park.[^cite_note-16] On 26 May, McStay stepped back from managing Mayo to deal with his health problems and former manager [Stephen Rochford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Rochford) took his place.[^cite_note-17] Rochford was [interim manager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_management) for two games, as Mayo made an early championship exit.[^cite_note-Keys-18][^cite_note-'All_bets_are_off_now'-19][^cite_note-rte.ie-20] The Mayo County Board then decided to "relieve Kevin McStay and his management team from their roles" with the team, although their four-year term was not complete.[^cite_note-21]

### 2025–present: Fourth All-Ireland SFC title

It was [Andy Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Moran) took on the poisoned mantle of manager. He took over things in 2025.

On 14 August 2025, Mayo ratified Moran as new manager.[^cite_note-22]

Due to the [impact of the COVID-19 pandemic on Gaelic games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impact_of_the_COVID-19_pandemic_on_Gaelic_games), Mayo's [2021 Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship) game in London had not been permitted to occur for the second time in its history, after the [UK's 2001 foot-in-mouth outbreak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_United_Kingdom_foot-and-mouth_outbreak) caused to fixture to be postponed.[^cite_note-23] Mayo's first trip to London since the 1996 Connacht Senior Football Championship occurred 10 years later, and coincidentally the first trip there since [2016 Connacht SFC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship) would take place [another 10 years afterwards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_Connacht_Senior_Football_Championship). The match took place on Saturday 11 April 2026; the score was the same as previous game in 2016, with Mayo winning 0–31 to 1–15.

Mayo finished third in the [NFL Division 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2026_National_Football_League_\(Ireland\)#Division_1), qualifying for the [2026 All-Ireland SFC](80557192.md) via NFL ranking after losing to Roscommon in the semi-finals of the Connacht SFC. Mayo entered the [2026 All-Ireland SFC final](83772902.md) against [Kerry](63970030.md) having lost to Tyrone in Round 2A, but winning all other clashes in the competition. Mayo would win the final by 1–20 to 1–17, to get a 4th overall All-Ireland SFC title and the first since the previous win in [1951](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1951_All-Ireland_Senior_Football_Championship), after 75 years of emptiness.[^cite_note-SFC2026-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

[Marty Morrissey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marty_Morrissey)'s call of the final seconds and aftermath on RTÉ:

> "...Seventy-five years of heartbreak are about to end! 1936, 1950, 1951, and surely now, 2026! Listen to the roar! *Seventy-five years of heartbreak is **OVER!!*** Mayo are the All-Ireland Senior Football Champions 2026!"

## Curse

Further information: [Sports-related curses § Mayo](11347008.md#Mayo)

Since the 1990s, a [sports curse](11347008.md) story had circulated that Mayo would not win another All-Ireland until all members of the 1951 team had died.[^cite_note-it3214371-27] The supposed cause was that the 1951 champions, returning through [Foxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foxford) in a coach or lorry, passed a [funeral cortège](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funeral_cort%C3%A8ge) without respectfully suspending their boisterous celebrations, and were cursed by either the priest, the bereaved spouse, or a [Traveller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Travellers).[^cite_note-it3214371-27] Folklorist Arlene Crampsie, who conducted interviews in Mayo for the 2008–2012 GAA [Oral History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oral_History) Project, found that participants had no interest in the story and regarded it as "recently contrived nonsense" spread by the media in the wake of multiple recent near-misses.[^cite_note-it3214371-27] In a 2016 [TG4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TG4) documentary, the two surviving 1951 players, [Paddy Prendergast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paddy_Prendergast_\(Gaelic_footballer\)) and [Pádraig Carney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P%C3%A1draig_Carney) dismissed the story.[^cite_note-28] Carney died on 9 June 2019 — leaving Prendergast as the last surviving player from the 1951 team. Prendergast died on 26 September 2021, 15 days after Mayo lost in the 2021 All-Ireland Final.[^cite_note-29] The last overall member of the 1951 team — [Mick Loftus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mick_Loftus), who was a sub and did not play on the day — died at the age of 93 on 22 April 2023.[^cite_note-30] Mayo won the All-Ireland Senior Football Championship in [2026](80557192.md), their first appearance in the All-Ireland Final after all members of the 1951 team had died, ending a seventy-five year wait.[^cite_note-SFC2026-24]

## Colours and crest



County Mayo coat of arms

The team's traditional colours are green and red. The Mayo jersey will commonly be mostly green, with a thick horizontal red stripe just below chest level. These colours are inspired by "The Green Above The Red", a [rebel song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_rebel_music).[^cite_note-31]

Mayo's current crest is based on the county's [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms), which is shown on the left. It features four crosses, each representing a [diocese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diocese) of the [Catholic Church](606848.md) in Mayo. The Patriarchal or 'double' cross represents the [Archdiocese of Tuam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Archdiocese_of_Tuam), while the three smaller Passion crosses represent [Achonry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Achonry), [Killala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Killala) and [Galway/Kilmacduagh/Kilfenora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Catholic_Diocese_of_Galway,_Kilmacduagh_and_Kilfenora). The [Irish-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_language) root word of the county, *Maigh Eo*, means "plain of the yew trees", and the trees that surround the crest represent this. As well as this, the number of trees is significant, with the nine trees representing the number of [baronies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barony_\(Ireland\)) in the county. The sailing ship represents the county's maritime history, while the red sea below the green hills represents the traditional "green above the red" motif of the county.[^cite_note-32] The Mayo GAA crest also features the Irish words *Críost Linn*, which translates to "Christ be with us".

## Stadium



MacHale Park in November 2016

[MacHale Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacHale_Park) in [Castlebar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castlebar) is the largest stadium in the [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Ireland) of [Connacht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Connacht). Its pitch is much criticised.[^cite_note-33]

It can hold around ten thousand more people than [Pearse Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearse_Stadium), the home venue of [Galway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galway_county_football_team), to the south.

### All Stars

Mayo has 48 All Stars, as of 2017. 29 different players have won, as of 2017. [Lee Keegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Keegan) won five All Stars, and is the only player to win five All Stars without winning an All-Ireland medal (he also never played at minor level).[^cite_note-all-time_greatest-67]

Mayo's John Carey was among the inaugural selection in 1971. Despite winning only two awards in total during their unsuccessful 1970s period, Mayo have been one of the most successful teams nationally in terms of All Star awards gained, with Mayo players winning 42 awards to date. [Willie Joe Padden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Joe_Padden) and Dermot Flanagan became the first Mayo players to win multiple awards, both winning their second awards in 1989. Kenneth Mortimer achieved the same feat in 1997, as did James Horan in 1999 and James Nallen in 2004. Mortimer became the first Mayo player to win consecutive awards, in 1996 and 1997, a feat matched by Colm Boyle (2013, 2014 and again, 2016, 2017), Lee Keegan (2012, 2013, and again, 2015, 2016) and David Clarke (2016, 2017), and exceeded by Keith Higgins, who completed three-in-a-row in 2014. Kenneth and Conor Mortimer became the first Mayo siblings to win awards when Conor won an All Star in 2006.

### Footballer of the Year

The **GAA & GPA [All Stars Footballer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Stars_Footballer_of_the_Year)**—known for sponsorship reasons as the Vodafone Footballer of the Year—is an annual award given at the end of the Championship season to a footballer who is adjudged to have been the best in Gaelic football. Players from Mayo have won this twice, with [Lee Keegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Keegan) winning it in 2016 and [Andy Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Moran) winning it in 2017, Mayo did not win the All-Ireland Championship in either of those years.

- 2016 - [Lee Keegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Keegan)

- 2017 - [Andy Moran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Moran)

### Young Footballer of the Year

The **GAA & GPA [All Stars Young Footballer of the Year](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_Stars_Young_Footballer_of_the_Year)** (often called simply Young Footballer of the Year) is an annual award given at the end of the Championship season to a young footballer under the age of 21 who is adjudged to have been the best in Gaelic football.

Players from Mayo have won this award more times (7) than players from any other county since the award was instituted in 1997. [Cillian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cillian_O'Connor) and [Diarmuid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diarmuid_O'Connor_\(Mayo_Gaelic_footballer\)) are the only set of brothers to win the award; they and [Oisín Mullin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ois%C3%ADn_Mullin) are the only multiple winners of the award.

- 2006 - [Keith Higgins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Keith_Higgins)

### Team of the Century and Team of the Millennium

**The Team of the Century** was nominated in 1984 by *[Sunday Independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunday_Independent_\(Ireland\))* readers and selected by a panel of experts including journalists and former players.[^cite_note-70] It was chosen as part of the [Gaelic Athletic Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaelic_Athletic_Association)'s centenary year celebrations. Two Mayo players were chosen on the team. These were [Seán Flanagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Se%C3%A1n_Flanagan) at left-corner-back and [Tommy Langan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tommy_Langan) at full-forward.

Similarly **The Team of the Millennium** was a team chosen in 1999 by a panel of GAA past presidents and journalists. The goal was to single out the best ever 15 players who had played the game in their respective positions, since the foundation of the GAA in 1884 up to the [Millennium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_celebrations) year, 2000. Both Flanagan and Langan were also selected on this team, occupying the same positions.
