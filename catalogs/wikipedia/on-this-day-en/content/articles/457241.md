**Kandahar** ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): د کندهار ولايت, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Pashto): *Da Kandahār Wilāyat* and [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari_language): ولایت قندهار, romanized: *Wilāyat-e Qandahār*) is one of the southern [provinces of Afghanistan](412343.md) and is widely regarded as one of the country's most historically influential and politically significant regions. It borders [Helmand](457235.md) to the west, [Uruzgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruzgan_Province) to the north, [Zabul](362239.md) to the east, and the [Balochistan province](4687.md) of [Pakistan](23235.md) to the south. The provincial capital is the city of [Kandahar](17260.md), which functions as the main administrative, economic, and cultural center of southern Afghanistan called *[Loy Kandahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loy_Kandahar)*.

Covering an area of approximately 54,000 square kilometers and having an estimated population of about 1.5 million people, Kandahar is characterized by its arid climate, desert landscapes, and fertile agricultural zones along the [Arghandab River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghandab_River). The province plays a central role in Afghanistan's agricultural economy, particularly in the production of [pomegranates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate), [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), and other cash crops. Kandahar also contains numerous archaeological and historical sites, reflecting its long and continuous human habitation.

Historically, Kandahar has served as a crucial gateway between [South Asia](21566765.md), the [Iranian Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_plateau), and [Central Asia](6742.md). The region was part of ancient [Arachosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachosia) and later became an important center under [Achaemenid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire), [Kushan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kushan_Empire), and [Islamic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia) rule. In the modern era, Kandahar gained national prominence as the birthplace of [Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani), the founder of the [modern Afghan state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire) in the 18th century, establishing the province as the symbolic heartland of Afghan statehood and [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns) political power.

Today, Kandahar remains one of the most strategically important provinces of Afghanistan due to its geographic position, economic role, and political influence, with the city of Kandahar *de facto* serving as the seat of national power since 2021 due to the permanent presence of the [Taliban's supreme leadership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leadership_Council_of_Afghanistan) in the city. While the province continues to face significant challenges related to security, governance, and infrastructure development, it retains a strong regional identity rooted in its historical legacy, tribal traditions, and agricultural productivity.

## Etymology

There is long-standing scholarly speculation surrounding the origin of the name *Kandahar*. One theory traces the name to the *Kandhāra*, believed to be a linguistically altered form of [Gandhāra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gandh%C4%81ra_\(kingdom\)) ([Sanskrit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanskrit_language): कंधार), a historically significant region referenced as early as 2000–1700 BCE.[^cite_note-4] This interpretation suggests a deep pre-[Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire) linguistic continuity in the broader region of southern Afghanistan.

The more widely accepted explanation, however, links the name directly to [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great). Kandahar is believed to have originated as one of the many cities named after the Hellenistic conqueror across his former [Achaemenid domains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), with its ancient name being *Alexandria Arachosia* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Ἀλεξάνδρεια Ἀραχωσία). The modern form *Kandahar* ([Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto_language): کندهار) is thought to derive from the adaptation of *Iskandariya* ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الإسكندرية), itself based on *Alexándreia* ([Ancient Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek_language): Ἀλεξάνδρεια).[^cite_note-Alexander-5] Archaeological evidence supports this Hellenistic connection. A temple dedicated to the deified Alexander has been identified in the old citadel, along with a significant bilingual inscription in [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) and [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aramaic) issued by the [Mauryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire) emperor [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka), who ruled a few decades after Alexander's campaigns.[^cite_note-6]

## History

Further information: [History of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Afghanistan)

### Antiquity



Bilingual [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greek) and [Aramaic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Aramaic) inscriptions by king [Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashoka) at modern-day [Old Kandahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kandahar) from the [3rd century BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_century_BC)

Kandahar and its surrounding region have been inhabited for more than 7,000 years, making it one of the oldest continuously settled areas in the world.[^cite_note-Dupree3-7] Archaeological excavations at populated places near modern Kandahar reveal early farming villages dating to approximately 5000 BCE, featuring multiroomed [mudbrick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mudbrick) houses, [pottery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pottery) [kilns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiln), and evidence of organized agricultural production. Excavations at the nearby caves yielded [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) artifacts, including [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) and [bronze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze) tools, [horse trappings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse_tack), and stone seals, indicating early technological advancement and trade. Stylistically, these artifacts show cultural links with the [Indus Valley civilization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indus_Valley_Civilisation), the [Iranian plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_plateau), and [Central Asia](6742.md), suggesting Kandahar's integration into a wide prehistoric trade and cultural network.[^cite_note-Dupree3-7]

During antiquity, the region was known as *[Arachosia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arachosia)*, strategically situated between [South Asia](21566765.md), Central Asia, and the Iranian plateau. Its location along river valleys, such as the [Arghandab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghandab_River), and early trade routes made it a coveted territory. Arachosia became a [satrapy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satrap) of the [Achaemenid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), serving as a hub for administration, taxation, and military control in the eastern territories of the empire. In 330 BCE, [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) conquered Arachosia and founded *Alexandria Arachosia*, which functioned as both a military stronghold and a commercial center connecting [Hellenistic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hellenistic_period), [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persosphere), and [Indian spheres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indosphere). After Alexander's death, the city fell under the [Seleucid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seleucid_Empire) and later came under the [Mauryan Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maurya_Empire), highlighting the spread of [Buddhism](3267529.md) and the region's integration into South Asian cultural networks. The geographic advantages, fertile land, and strategic position established Arachosia as a crucial nexus of commerce, culture, and military power in antiquity.[^cite_note-\:1-8]

### Medieval times



A [miniature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miniature_\(illuminated_manuscript\)) from [Padshahnama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Padshahnama) depicting the surrender of the [Shia](26961.md) [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty) at what is now [Old Kandahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kandahar) in 1638 to the [Mughal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) army of [Shah Jahan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Jahan) commanded by Kilij Khan

The medieval period in Kandahar saw successive waves of conquest, settlement, and cultural transformation. After the decline of Hellenistic and Mauryan influence, the region was ruled by local dynasties, most notably the [Zunbils](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zunbil_dynasty), who maintained semi-independent control until the 7th century CE. The arrival of Arab forces under the [Umayyad Caliphate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) introduced [Islam](6037917.md), though initial control was largely temporary. By the 9th century, the [Saffarid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saffarid_dynasty) consolidated Islamic authority in modern-day southern Afghanistan, while the eastern territories remained under the [Hindushahis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hindu_Shahis), a Hindu dynasty controlling parts of Gandhara.

In the 10th and 11th centuries, Kandahar became part of the [Ghaznavid Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavids) under [Mahmud of Ghazni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_of_Ghazni), who implemented centralized administration, collected taxes, and expanded trade networks into [northern India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_India).[^cite_note-9] The Ghaznavids facilitated the spread of [Islamic culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_culture), [architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture), and [Persian administrative systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire#Government). They were succeeded by the [Ghurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghurid_dynasty), who maintained Kandahar as a strategic military and administrative center.

The 13th century brought devastation during [Mongol invasions under Genghis Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongol_invasion_of_the_Khwarazmian_Empire), resulting in widespread destruction and population displacement. Reconstruction occurred under the [Timurids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timurid_Empire), who strengthened fortifications, rebuilt urban infrastructure, and promoted trade, while delegating governance to family members such as [Pir Muhammad ibn Jahangir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pir_Muhammad_\(son_of_Jahangir\)) in the late 14th and early 15th century. Kandahar's position as a crossroads for trade and military campaigns remained central to its identity during this period.

### Early modern period



Painting by [Abdul Ghafoor Breshna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Ghafoor_Breshna) depicting the 1747 [coronation](226191.md) of [Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani), who is regarded as the [founding father](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_national_founders) of Afghanistan ([Father of the Nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Father_of_the_Nation)).

From the 16th century onward, Kandahar was at the center of repeated conflicts between regional powers. The city was contested between the [Mughal Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mughal_Empire) and the [Safavid dynasty of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid_dynasty), reflecting both strategic and religious rivalries between [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) and [Shia](26961.md) rulers. [Babur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babur) briefly captured the city, but it ultimately fell under Safavid control. During this period, Kandahar's fortifications were rebuilt multiple times to withstand sieges, and the city functioned as a provincial administrative and military center.

In 1709, [Mirwais Hotak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirwais_Hotak) successfully rebelled against the Safavids, founding the [Hotak dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotak_dynasty) and establishing Kandahar as the political heart of a [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns)-led empire. The Hotaki rulers consolidated local authority and defended the region against Persian encroachment. In 1738, [Nader Shah of Persia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nader_Shah) defeated the last Hotaki ruler, [Shah Hussain Hotak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hussain_Hotak), briefly restoring Persian influence. Soon after, in 1747, [Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Durrani) established the [Durrani Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani_Empire), often considered the foundation of modern [Afghanistan](737.md), making Kandahar its initial capital. Under Durrani, the city was the seat of imperial administration, military organization, and tribal coordination, though the capital later moved to [Kabul](16826.md) in the 1770s.



[Afghan military commanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Afghanistan) on 2 September 1880, about a month after their victory at the [Battle of Maiwand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Maiwand)

Throughout the 19th and early 20th centuries, Kandahar was influenced by broader geopolitical struggles. The city was occupied temporarily by [British-led forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Army_during_the_Victorian_Era) during the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Anglo-Afghan_War) and [Second Anglo-Afghan Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Anglo-Afghan_War) (1832–1842 and 1878–1880), highlighting its strategic significance in the [British-Russian Great Game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Game). In the mid-20th century, Kandahar also became a commercial hub along trade routes leading to [India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_India) and a notable stop for travelers, including participants in the [Hippie trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hippie_trail), reflecting its continuing economic and cultural relevance.[^cite_note-\:1-8] Infrastructure, agriculture, and local governance expanded under various Afghan governments, though the city's growth remained constrained by periodic tribal conflicts and regional instability.

### During war times (1979–2021)

The [Soviet invasion of Afghanistan in 1979](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Baikal-79) marked the beginning of a prolonged period of armed conflict in Kandahar. During the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), the province became a major center of *[mujahideen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_mujahideen)* resistance, benefiting from its rugged terrain, tribal networks, and proximity to the [Pakistan](23235.md) border, which allowed insurgent forces to operate effectively.[^cite_note-\:2-10] The city of Kandahar and its surrounding districts experienced significant destruction, population displacement, and disruptions to trade and agriculture. Following the [Soviet withdrawal from Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_withdrawal_from_Afghanistan) in 1989, governance of the province was contested among local warlords, with [Gul Agha Sherzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gul_Agha_Sherzai) emerging as a prominent authority figure in the early 1990s, amid continuing instability and localized conflict.

In 1994, Kandahar became the birthplace of the [Taliban](30635.md) movement, led by [Mullah Omar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullah_Omar), who sought to consolidate political authority and restore order following [years of civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1992%E2%80%931996\)). The city quickly became the movement's *de facto* capital, from which it extended control over much of southern and eventually all of Afghanistan.[^cite_note-\:6-11][^cite_note-\:7-12] Under [Taliban rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\)), Kandahar experienced the imposition of strict [Sharia law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharia_law), limited political pluralism, and attempts—largely unsuccessful—to regulate the [opium economy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_production_in_Afghanistan).



[Operational Detachment Alpha 574](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_Detachment_Alpha_574) of the U.S. Army Special Forces alongside [Hamid Karzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Karzai) in the province in October 2001

Following the [U.S.-led invasion of Afghanistan in 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Afghanistan), prompted by the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), [Kandahar fell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Kandahar) to the [Northern Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Alliance) in December, ending Taliban governance in the province at that time.[^cite_note-\:10-13][^cite_note-\:11-14][^cite_note-15] The city then became a hub for international military operations, reconstruction, and counterinsurgency efforts, with arrested or suspected insurgents being transferred from the Kandahar detention center to the [Guantanamo Bay naval base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_Naval_Base), while others detained by coalition forces were handed over to the Afghan intelligence service, the [National Directorate of Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Directorate_of_Security), where some reportedly faced torture during interrogation.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17] Despite the establishment of local governance under figures such as [Hamid Karzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamid_Karzai), the province remained a stronghold of [Taliban insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taliban_insurgency).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] Throughout the 2000s and 2010s, Kandahar saw recurring attacks, targeted assassinations, and ongoing challenges to infrastructure, public services, and rural governance, making it a focal point of security and political concern in southern Afghanistan.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-\:17-21][^cite_note-22]

### Today (2021–)

In 2021, as the [United states withdrew from Afghanistan](66883576.md), a [Taliban offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Taliban_offensive) advanced across the country, including Kandahar and other southern provinces.[^cite_note-\:6-11][^cite_note-23] On May 1, 2021, the U.S. completed its withdrawal from Kandahar, enabling the Taliban to capture surrounding districts and encircle the city.[^cite_note-24] The ensuing conflict displaced approximately 150,000 residents in the province.[^cite_note-\:6-11] Kandahar city fell to the Taliban on August 12, 2021, establishing their *de facto* control over the province. Since their return to power, the Taliban have implemented restrictions affecting education, employment, and public life, including bans on women and girls from participating in multiple institutions.[^cite_note-\:6-11][^cite_note-25] In 2023, the provincial office of the Taliban's spokesman was relocated from Kabul to Kandahar, further consolidating the role of the region as a regional administrative and political center.[^cite_note-\:6-11] The city maintains its symbolic status as the [Pashtun heartland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtunistan) and a historic nexus of Afghan governance, commerce, and tribal authority.

## Geography

### Landscape



The [Arghandab Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghandab_Valley) between [Kandahar](17260.md) and [Lashkar Gah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashkar_Gah)

Kandahar is situated in southern Afghanistan and shares borders with [Helmand](457235.md) to the west, [Zabul](362239.md) to the east, [Uruzgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uruzgan_Province) to the north, and [Pakistan](23235.md) to the south. The province's terrain is diverse, comprising [arid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid) [plains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plain), fertile [river valleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_valleys), and rugged [hills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hills). The [Arghandab River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghandab_River) valley is the most fertile part of the province and serves as the center of agricultural production. Extensive irrigation projects, such as the [Dahla Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahla_Dam), help channel water from the [Arghandab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghandab_River) and [Helmand rivers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmand_River) to surrounding farmland.

The western and southern parts of the province consist of [semi-desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Semi-desert) plains, characterized by sparse vegetation and sandy soils, which are suitable for grazing but less productive for intensive agriculture. To the north and east, low-lying hills and rocky [outcrops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outcrop) dominate, creating natural barriers between districts. These hills also contain mineral deposits such as gravel, clay, and marble, which support small-scale mining operations. Kandahar city itself is located on relatively flat terrain in the [Arghandab valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghandab_Valley), making it a natural hub for trade and transportation.

### Flora and fauna

Kandahar's [flora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flora) is adapted to its mostly arid and semi-arid environment. Irrigated areas along the Arghandab River and around the Dahla Dam support [orchards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orchard), [gardens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garden), and agricultural fields, where crops such as [pomegranates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate), [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape), [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond), [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), and various vegetables thrive. The dry plains and hills are covered with hardy [shrubs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrub), [grasses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grass), and scattered [acacia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acacia) and [tamarisk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamarix) trees. The [wildlife](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife) reflects its diverse habitats. Common mammals include [foxes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fox), [jackals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jackal), [wild goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wild_goat), and [hares](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hare), while bird species such as [partridges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partridge), [doves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doves), and [migratory waterfowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migratory_waterfowl) inhabit river valleys and seasonal wetlands. Rivers and small irrigation channels also support [fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fish) and [amphibians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amphibian) in localized areas, though these populations are limited due to low water availability and seasonal fluctuations.

### Climate



Kandahar typically experiences hot summers

Kandahar has a predominantly [arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arid_climate) with long, hot summers and mild winters. Average summer temperatures often exceed 40 °C in the plains, while winter temperatures can drop to near freezing at night. [Rainfall](19009110.md) is scarce, mostly occurring during the winter and spring months, averaging less than 200 mm annually, which makes irrigation essential for agriculture. Wind patterns can cause [dust storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_storm), particularly during late spring and early summer, which occasionally disrupt daily life and reduce air quality. The [Arghandab River valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghandab_Valley) and other irrigated areas benefit from slightly more moderate [microclimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microclimate), allowing for the successful cultivation of high-value crops such as pomegranates, grapes, and melons. Seasonal variations also affect water availability, with spring [snowmelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snowmelt) from surrounding hills providing temporary increases in river flow.

## Government and politics

Further information: [Politics of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Afghanistan)

### Local governance

Further information: [List of governors of Kandahar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_governors_of_Kandahar)

Kandahar's governance has historically been shaped by the interplay of tribal authority, central administration, and strategic geography. In the 19th and early 20th centuries, the province was governed through a mixture of [tribal elders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribal_elder), local *[khans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_\(title\))*, and representatives of the [Afghan monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Afghanistan), who exercised significant autonomy while recognizing formal sovereignty from [Kabul](16826.md). Early efforts by the Afghan state to consolidate power included the appointment of provincial governors and the establishment of administrative offices in [Kandahar](17260.md), aimed at centralizing decision-making and improving public services.

During periods of conflict from 1979 onwards—including the [Soviet–Afghan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet%E2%80%93Afghan_War), the [civil wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Civil_War_\(1989-2001\)), and the [Taliban's first regime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan_\(1996%E2%80%932001\))—formal governance structures were frequently replaced or supplemented by local [warlords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warlord) and tribal leaders, who controlled security, taxation, and dispute resolution. After the [U.S.-led invasion in 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_invasion_of_Afghanistan), provincial governance was restored through appointed officials, while traditional councils and community elders continued to exert influence over social and judicial matters, particularly in rural areas.

Since the [Taliban regained control in 2021](68481047.md), the provincial administration operates under the framework of the [Islamic Emirate of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Emirate_of_Afghanistan). Governors and district officials are directly appointed by the central Taliban authorities, and formal political parties no longer participate in provincial governance. Nevertheless, tribal networks, religious leaders, and local councils continue to play a role in mediating disputes and influencing decisions at the community level.

As of December 2025, the governor of Kandahar is [Mullah Shirin Akhund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mullah_Shirin_Akhund).[^cite_note-mei-1]

### Administrative divisions



Map of the districts of Kandahar as of January 2004, prior to the redrawing of provincial and district boundaries later that year

With the exception of a few districts, the district borders of Kandahar have been largely unchanged since 1914.[^cite_note-26] The province is divided into 18 districts, each managed by a district head responsible for local administration and coordination with the provincial government. The provincial capital, [Kandahar](17260.md), serves as the administrative, economic, and logistical hub for southern Afghanistan. Other key settlements include [Arghandab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghandab,_Afghanistan), [Panjwayi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panjwayi_District), [Daman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daman,_Afghanistan), [Spin Boldak](539307.md), and [Shah Wali Kot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Wali_Kot_District). District centers are typically located along major roads, river valleys, or population clusters, reflecting both geographic accessibility and demographic patterns. Local administrations are tasked with civil registration, maintenance of basic law and order, and oversight of services such as education, healthcare, and infrastructure development.

### Security



[Afghan National Security Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_National_Security_Forces) in southern Kandahar (2012)

The province's security landscape has long been influenced by its strategic location and [porous border with Pakistan](203570.md). During the [Islamic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan) era, the province was a central venue of counterinsurgency operations against the Taliban, hosting international military forces and [Afghan National Security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_National_Security_Forces) personnel. Since 2021, the [Taliban](30635.md) maintain primary control over provincial security, with the city of [Kandahar](17260.md) experiencing a comparatively stable environment, while rural districts remain vulnerable to localized unrest, criminal networks, and sporadic militant activity. The continued influence of tribal structures and local power brokers further shapes the implementation of law and order throughout the province.

## Economy

Further information: [Transport in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_Afghanistan) and [Economy of Afghanistan](9895.md)

The economy of Kandahar is predominantly based on [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), [regional trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_trade), and [transport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport)-related services. A large share of the population depends directly or indirectly on [farming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farming), [livestock breeding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Livestock_breeding), [market trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_trade), and cross-border commerce with [Pakistan](23235.md). Since 2021, public employment and international aid-related income have declined sharply, increasing reliance on subsistence agriculture, informal labor, and remittances.

### Agriculture and animal husbandry



A child herds sheep through a street in [Maiwand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maiwand) (2012)

Agriculture remains the primary economic sector of Kandahar. The fertile lands of the [Arghandab River valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghandab_River) support the cultivation of [wheat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wheat), [pomegranates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate), [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape), [melons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melon), [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetable), and [fodder crops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fodder_crops). The province is internationally known for its high-quality [pomegranate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate) production, which remains a key export commodity.[^cite_note-37] Since the Taliban return to power in 2021, [opium poppy cultivation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_production_in_Afghanistan) has been largely suppressed through strict enforcement, significantly altering rural income structures and forcing farmers to return to legal crops. [Animal husbandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_husbandry) plays a complementary role, with the breeding of [sheep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheep), [goats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goat), [cattle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cattle), and [poultry](23197.md) providing [meat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meat), [dairy products](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dairy_product), [wool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wool), and [hides](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hide_\(skin\)) for both local consumption and trade.

### Mining and industry



[Andradite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andradite) from Kandahar

Kandahar possesses limited large-scale industrial capacity, with mining activity concentrated primarily on [construction materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_materials). The province hosts licensed extraction of [gravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gravel), [river gravel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_gravel), [crushed stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crushed_stone), [marble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marble), [sand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand), [clay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clay), and minor [fluorite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorite) deposits, which are used almost entirely for local and regional construction markets.[^cite_note-38] Marble extraction exists on a small scale and is processed mainly for domestic use rather than export.

Industrial activity is dominated by small-scale manufacturing and processing, including [food processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food_processing), [brick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brick) and [concrete](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concrete) production, [carpet weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpet_weaving), [leatherwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leatherwork), [metal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metal) workshops, and [stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stone) crushing plants. Most industrial output remains artisanal or semi-mechanized, constrained by weak infrastructure, limited access to capital, fuel shortages, and unstable electricity supply. As a result, Kandahar's industrial sector continues to serve primarily local consumption and basic construction demand, rather than functioning as a major export-oriented industrial region.

### Trade

Trade is one of the most important economic functions of the province due to its location near the [border to Pakistan](203570.md). The [Spin Boldak–Chaman crossing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaman_border_crossing) serves as a major commercial gateway for imports and exports, facilitating the movement of [food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Food), [fuel](21188370.md), [construction materials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Construction_materials), [textiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Textile), and [consumer goods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consumer_goods). Kandahar acts as a distribution hub for southern Afghanistan, linking domestic markets with [South Asian](21566765.md) supply routes. Informal cross-border trade remains widespread alongside official customs operations.

### Energy and irrigation



The [Dahla Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahla_Dam) in Kandahar

Energy supply in Kandahar is limited and unreliable, relying on a mix of imported electricity, [diesel generators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diesel_generator), and small-scale [solar installations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_plant). Rural areas remain especially underserved. Irrigation is critical to the agricultural economy and depends primarily on the [Arghandab River system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghandab_River), traditional *[karez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karez)* underground canals, and seasonal [water flows](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_flow). Several districts benefit from irrigation provided by infrastructure developed and managed by the [Helmand and Arghandab Valley Authority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helmand_and_Arghandab_Valley_Authority).[^cite_note-clapp-39] The [Dahla Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahla_Dam), located north of the city of Kandahar, supplies additional water resources to the province. There are approximately 700 [greenhouses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greenhouse) in the province.[^cite_note-40] Aging infrastructure, drought cycles, and insufficient maintenance continue to constrain agricultural productivity.

### Tourism

Kandahar's tourism is centered on its historical, cultural, and religious sites, including the [mausoleum of Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Ahmad_Shah_Durrani) and the [Shrine of the Cloak of the Prophet Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirka_Sharif). Decades of conflict limited both domestic and international visitation. Since 2021, relative stabilization under the [Taliban](30635.md) has allowed a revival of local tourism and a limited number of foreign visitors.[^cite_note-41] Tourism remains restricted in scale, with facilities and infrastructure underdeveloped, and international travel constrained by security and political conditions.

### Communication

Telecommunications infrastructure expanded rapidly after 2001, with [mobile phone networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_phone_network) and limited [internet services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_access) reaching most urban areas. Since 2021, communication services continue to function, though under tighter regulation. [Internet penetration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet_penetration) remains low, especially in rural districts, and access is affected by electricity shortages and economic constraints.

### Transportation and infrastructure



A [Kam Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kam_Air) passenger plane at [Kandahar International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandahar_International_Airport) in 2012

Kandahar remains a major transportation hub in Afghanistan. The [Ahmad Shah Baba International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmad_Shah_Baba_International_Airport), located east of the city of Kandahar, serves both civilian and military flights, providing domestic connections and limited international services to the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md), [Iran](14653.md), and other regional countries. Although there is currently no [operational railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Afghanistan), plans have been proposed for a line connecting Kandahar to [Spin Boldak](539307.md) and onward to [Chaman](2288065.md) and the [Pakistan Railways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Railways).[^cite_note-42]

Ground transport relies mainly on [trucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trucks) and [cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cars), with Spin Boldak serving as a key hub for trade with Pakistan. Bus services connect major towns and village centers, while the capital city formerly had a city bus system and continues to operate taxicabs. The province is linked by major highways to [Herat](14128.md), [Kabul](16826.md), and [Quetta](93764.md) in [Pakistan](23235.md), with several key roads paved with support from the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md). Despite these improvements, many secondary and rural routes remain [unpaved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dirt_road) or in poor condition, limiting access for agricultural and commercial transport.

## Demographics

Further information: [Demographics of Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Afghanistan)

### Population



Local men sitting outside of a [forward operating base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forward_operating_base) in [Pasab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhari_District), Kandahar

Kandahar province has an estimated population of approximately 1.5 million people as of 2023, spread across urban centers, towns, and rural villages.[^cite_note-NSIA-3] The provincial capital, [Kandahar](17260.md), hosts by far the largest share of urban residents, followed by [Spin Boldak](539307.md), while the majority of the population lives in rural districts. Poverty and limited infrastructure continue to affect living conditions, particularly in remote districts, with a [multidimensional poverty index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multidimensional_Poverty_Index) of 0.428 and 48.4% of the population living in [severe poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severe_poverty) as of 2023.[^cite_note-ophi-43] Many households rely on subsistence agriculture, animal husbandry, informal labor, or remittances, reflecting the province's economic challenges.

### Ethnicity, languages, and religion

As with much of Afghanistan, ethnic, linguistic, and religious identities in Kandahar are fluid and overlapping. The province is overwhelmingly [Pashtun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtuns), with one major tribal confederation being the [Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Durrani), specifically the [Zirak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zirak) (like [Barakzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barakzai), [Achakzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achakzai), and [Alokozai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alakozai)) and [Panjpai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panjpai) (like [Noorzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nurzai)), and the other confederation being the [Ghilzai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghilji) (e.g. [Tokhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tokhi) and [Hotak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotak)).[^cite_note-NSIA-3] The [Baloch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baloch_people) and [Brahui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brahui_language) are concentrated in southern districts, while smaller populations of [Hazara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hazaras) and [Tajik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tajiks)/[Farsiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farsiwan) reside mainly in urban areas. [Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashto) serves as the main *[lingua franca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingua_franca)*, while [Dari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dari) is spoken by minorities and for administrative or formal purposes.[^cite_note-\:0-44] Many residents are bilingual or multilingual, reflecting historical trade and cultural exchange. The population is predominantly [Sunni Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam), with small [Shia](26961.md) minorities among Hazaras and Farsiwan and small [Hindu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism_in_Afghanistan) and [Sikh communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sikhism_in_Afghanistan) in the cities. Tribal affiliations remain important in politics, business, and social life, with intermarriage and overlapping identities blurring strict ethnic or religious boundaries.

### Education

Further information: [List of universities in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_Afghanistan) and [List of schools in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_schools_in_Afghanistan)



Students of a [boys school](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boys_school) in Kandahar (2011)

Education access varies widely across the province. As of 2015, Kandahar had over 200 educational institutions, including [primary](25058.md) and [secondary schools](554992.md) concentrated in urban areas. [Kandahar University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandahar_University) is the largest educational institution in the province with over 5,000 students as of 2012.[^cite_note-Embassy-60] Rural districts face significant shortages of schools, teachers, and learning materials. Literacy rates are low, with an overall rate of approximately 13%, and female literacy remains particularly limited. [Vocational training](56551.md) and [NGO](46539.md)-supported programs provide skills development in rural areas. Since 2021, the Taliban administration has expanded *[madrasas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madrasa)*, but female access to secondary and higher education is restricted.

### Health

Further information: [Health in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Afghanistan) and [Healthcare in Afghanistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Healthcare_in_Afghanistan)



Doctors treating an injured patient at the Kandahar Regional Military Hospital (2010)

There are a number of hospitals in the province, most of them in the city of Kandahar. These include Aino Mina Hospital, Al Farhad Hospital, Ayoubi Hospital, [Mirwais Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirwais_Hospital), Mohmand Hospital,[^cite_note-61] Sial Curative Hospital and Sidal Hospital.

Healthcare infrastructure is concentrated in the city of [Kandahar](17260.md), with 133 hospitals, 86 clinics, and 730 pharmacies providing primary and specialized care. Rural populations often must travel long distances to reach medical facilities. Common health challenges include [maternal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maternal_death) and [infant mortality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infant_mortality), [malnutrition](258979.md), and limited access to [clean water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clean_water) and [sanitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanitation). [NGOs](46539.md) like [Médecins Sans Frontières](20498.md) amd the [Islamic Relief Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Relief_Organization) supplement government health services, offering vaccinations, basic medical care, and health education.[^cite_note-NSIA-3]

## Culture

### Music and dances



Kandahari men performing the national dance of *[attan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attan)*

Kandahar has a rich musical and dance heritage deeply rooted in [Pashtun tribal traditions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pashtun_tribal_culture). The *[attan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attan)*, a circular group dance, is considered the most iconic cultural expression, performed at weddings, religious festivals, and communal celebrations to symbolize unity and local identity. Music often features traditional instruments such as the *[rubab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rubab_\(instrument\))*, *[tabla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabla)*, and *[sitar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sitar)*, accompanying both festive and ceremonial occasions. Folk songs recount historical events, tribal legends, and heroic deeds, preserving the oral traditions of the region. Religious chants and recitations of the [Quran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quran) are also central to cultural life, particularly during [Ramadan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramadan) and other Islamic observances. Singers from Kandahar such as [Naghma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naghma_\(singer\)) contribute to the modern [Afghan music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Afghanistan) landscape.

### Dresses and attire

Traditional clothing varies across tribal groups but remains a central part of Kandahari identity. Men typically wear *[perahan o tunban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perahan_o_tunban)*, often paired with [turbans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turban), [embroidered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery) [caps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cap), or [sashes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sash) to indicate tribal affiliation. Women wear long, colorful dresses with intricate embroidery, complemented by [scarves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scarf) or [veils](68897.md). For special occasions such as weddings, festivals, or religious celebrations, garments feature elaborate patterns, metallic threads, and decorative jewelry, reflecting both social status and regional craftsmanship. Tribal elders and leaders often wear distinct clothing that signals their authority and heritage.

### Cuisine

Kandahar's cuisine is closely tied to its agricultural wealth, particularly its [pomegranates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomegranate), [grapes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape), and [melons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Melon), which are widely used in both meals and sweets. The province is known for *[kababs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kababs)*, especially [lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_and_mutton) and [chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken), often served with rice dishes such as *[qabeli palaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qabeli_palaw)*. Seasonal vegetables like [carrots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carrot), [spinach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spinach), and [cauliflower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauliflower) are common, alongside dairy products such as [yogurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yogurt) and *[qurut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qurut)*. Traditional sweets are often accompanied by [green tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_tea), which is served throughout the day during family gatherings and communal events.

### Architecture, art, and literature



Mausoleum of Ahmad Shah Durrani in 2021

Kandahar features historic [Islamic architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_architecture), including [mosques](19894.md), [shrines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shrine), and traditional [courtyard homes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Courtyard_house). Notable sites include the [Mausoleum of Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Ahmad_Shah_Durrani) and the [Shrine of the Cloak of the Prophet Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloak_of_Muhammad), which attract pilgrims from across the country. Kandahari art includes [calligraphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calligraphy), [carpet weaving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpet_weaving), [embroidery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embroidery), and [woodwork](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodworking), reflecting millennia of local craftsmanship. [Poetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poetry) and [storytelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storytelling) remain highly valued, often performed during social gatherings or religious events, preserving tribal histories, moral lessons, and heroic tales.

### Media, entertainment, and festivities

[Radio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_in_Afghanistan) is the main source of information in rural areas, with stations such as Radio Kandahar, [BBC Pashto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Pashto), and [Radio Azadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Azadi) broadcasting news and cultural programming. Television and social media are more accessible in the city of [Kandahar](17260.md), providing entertainment and global news. Traditional festivities include [Eid al-Fitr](154944.md), [Eid al-Adha](358226.md), [Nowruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nowruz_in_Afghanistan), and weddings, which feature music, dance, and communal meals. Since 2021, Taliban restrictions have limited music events and public performances, changing the way cultural celebrations are conducted, though private family gatherings continue to uphold traditions.

### Places of interest



Front view of the [Kandahar University Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandahar_University)

Kandahar is home to numerous religious, historical, and cultural landmarks, reflecting its long-standing role as a political and spiritual center of Afghanistan. The most iconic site is the [Shrine of the Cloak of the Prophet Muhammad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloak_of_Muhammad), one of the most sacred places in the country. The [mausoleums of Ahmad Shah Durrani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mausoleum_of_Ahmad_Shah_Durrani) and [Mirwais Hotak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirwais_Hotak), founders of modern Afghanistan, are other major national monuments located in the city. Kandahar also features historic and modern mosques, [old city quarters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Kandahar), and [archaeological remains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archaeological_site) linked to ancient trade routes. Beyond urban landmarks, the province is characterized by its fertile river valleys, gardens, and orchards, especially along the [Arghandab River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arghandab_River). The [Dahla Dam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dahla_Dam) and the Arghandab canal network hold both economic and cultural importance, as they sustain traditional irrigation systems that have shaped agriculture, settlement patterns, and livelihoods for generations.

### Sports



Amateur football players in Kandahar (2011)

Historically, the most prominent traditional sport in Kandahar has been *[buzkashi](169471.md)*, an ancient [equestrian game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equestrianism) similar to [polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo). It has long been associated with tribal prestige, strength, and horsemanship, and remains culturally significant, particularly in rural districts. Other traditional activities have included *[koshti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koshti_\(wrestling\))* wrestling, [archery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archery), and physically demanding folk games practiced during festivals and community gatherings.

In the modern era, [cricket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_in_Afghanistan) has become the most popular sport in Kandahar, especially among younger generations, influenced by [Afghanistan's international success](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_national_cricket_team). In national cricket competitions such as the [Shpageeza Cricket League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shpageeza_Cricket_League), Kandahar is represented by the [Boost Defenders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boost_Defenders) franchise, which covers the southern provinces, playing its home matches at the [Kandahar International Cricket Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandahar_International_Cricket_Stadium). [Football](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Afghanistan) is also widely played due to its low equipment requirements and long-standing presence in schools and local clubs. During the period of the [Islamic Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Republic_of_Afghanistan), Kandahar was represented in national football competitions through [De Maiwand Atalan FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Maiwand_Atalan_FC), which served as the regional team for Kandahar together with [Helmand](457235.md), [Urozgan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urozgan_Province), [Nimruz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nimruz_Province) and [Zabul](362239.md) in the [Afghan Premier League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghan_Premier_League). In the successor competition, the [Afghanistan Champions League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afghanistan_Champions_League), Kandahar is represented through [Aino Mina FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aino_Mina_FC?action=edit\&redlink=1). In addition, combat sports such as [boxing](4243.md) and [taekwondo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taekwondo), along with [fitness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fitness_culture) and [bodybuilding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodybuilding), are practiced in private gyms, mainly in Kandahar.
