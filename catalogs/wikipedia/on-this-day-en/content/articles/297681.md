A **curfew** is an order that imposes certain regulations during specified hours.[^cite_note-dictionary.com-1] Typically, curfews order all people affected by them to remain indoors during the evening and nighttime hours.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3] Such an order is most often issued by public authorities, but may also be given by the owner of a house to those living in the household. For instance, children are often given curfews by their parents, and an [au pair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Au_pair) is traditionally given a curfew by which time they must return to their host family's home. Some [jurisdictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jurisdiction) have juvenile curfews which affect all persons under a certain age not accompanied by an adult or engaged in certain approved activities.

Curfews have been used as a control measure in [martial law](50477.md), as well as for public safety in the event of a disaster, [epidemic](66981.md), or crisis.[^cite_note-4] Various countries have implemented such measures throughout history, including during [World War II](32927.md). The enforcement of curfews has been found to disproportionately affect [marginalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_marginalisation) groups, including those who are [homeless](19394651.md) or have limited access to [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-6]

During the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md), curfews were implemented in several countries, including [France](5843419.md), [Italy](14532.md), [Poland](22936.md) and [Australia](4689264.md), as a measure to limit the spread of the [virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Virus).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] However, recent studies have reported negligible or no effect,[^cite_note-9] and even a potential increase in virus transmission.[^cite_note-10] The use and enforcement of curfews during the pandemic has been associated with human rights violations and [mental health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_health) deterioration, further complicating their use as a control measure.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Curfews may also impact [road safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_traffic_safety), as studies indicate a potential decrease in crashes during curfew hours but an increase in crashes before curfew due to rushing.[^cite_note-13]

## Etymology



A 17th century "curfew", used to cover a fire at night

> Between the evening twilight and the grayness before dawn one can hardly make out the walls of the houses, for there is no lighting in the medieval city as we said. At evening curfew the women cover the coals in the [hearth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hearth) with ash to reduce the fire hazard. The houses are built with beams of [oak](39696.md) and every one is a potential tinderbox waiting to blaze up, so at night the only flames left burning are the candles before the [holy images](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_images_in_Christian_theology). Why would the streets need to be lit anyway? In the evening the entrances to the dangerous neighborhoods are barred, [chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boom_\(navigational_barrier\)) are stretched across the river to prevent a surprise attack from barbarian raiders coming upstream, and the [city gates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_gate) are locked tight. The city is like one big household, with everything well secured.

— [Arsenio Frugoni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arsenio_Frugoni), Quoted in *Urban Space in the Middle Ages and the Early Modern Age*

[^cite_note-14]

The word "curfew" [/ˈkɜːr.fjuː/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) comes from the [Old French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_French) phrase "*couvre-feu*", which means "cover fire".[^cite_note-dictionary.com-1] It was later adopted into [Middle English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_English) as "curfeu", which later became the modern "curfew".[^cite_note-15] Its original meaning refers to a law by [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror) that all lights and fires should be covered to extinction at the ringing of an eight o'clock bell to prevent the spread of destructive fire within communities in timber buildings.[^cite_note-16] With the same derivation a "curfew" also refers to a device used to cover the embers of a fire at night, allowing it to be re-ignited more easily in the morning.[^cite_note-17]

## Historical

Further information: [Curfew bell § History](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curfew_bell#History)

Curfews have been used since the [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) to limit uprisings among subordinate groups, including Anglo-Saxons under [William the Conqueror](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_the_Conqueror). Prior to the [U.S. Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Civil_War), most Southern states placed a curfew on slaves.[^cite_note-Baldwin-18]<sup>: 603 </sup>

Modern curfews primarily focus on youth as well as during periods of war and other crises. In the United States, progressive reformers pushed for curfews on youth, successfully securing bans on children's nighttime presence on streets in cities such as [Louisville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louisville,_Kentucky), Kentucky and [Lincoln](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lincoln,_Nebraska), Nebraska. General curfews were also put into place after crises such as the 1871 Chicago Fire.[^cite_note-Baldwin-18]<sup>: 603–605 </sup>

Wartime curfews were also implemented during the First and Second World Wars. A formal curfew introduced by the British board of trade ordered shops and entertainment establishments to extinguish their lights by 10\:30 p.m. to save fuel during [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-19]

## Types

- An order issued by public authorities or military forces requiring everyone or certain people to be indoors at certain times, often at night. It can be imposed to maintain public order (as was the case with the [northeast blackout of 2003](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northeast_blackout_of_2003), the [2005 French riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_French_riots), the [2010 Chile earthquake](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Chile_earthquake), the [2011 Egyptian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Egyptian_revolution), and the [2014 Ferguson unrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferguson_unrest)), or suppress targeted groups. Curfews have long been directed at certain groups in many cities or states, such as [Japanese-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Americans) university students on the [West Coast of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States) during [World War II](32927.md), African-Americans in many towns during the time of [Jim Crow laws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Crow_laws), or people younger than a certain age (usually within a few years either side of 18) in many towns of the United States since the 1980s. In recent times, curfews have been imposed by many countries during disease epidemics or [pandemics](24255.md) such as the [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md); see below.

- A rule set for a child or teenager by their parents or [legal guardians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_guardian), requiring them to return home by a specific time, usually in the evening or night. This may apply daily, or vary with the day of the week, e.g., if the minor has to go to school the next day.

- An order by the head of household to a domestic assistant such as an au pair or [nanny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanny). The domestic assistant must then return home by a specific time.

- A daily requirement for guests to return to their [hostel](233996.md) before a specified time, usually in the evening or night.

- A daily requirement that a person subject to a [court order](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_order), such as [probation](406786.md) or [bail](170652.md) conditions, must return to their home before a certain hour and be inside it until a certain hour of the morning.

- In aeronautics, [night flying restrictions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Night_flying_restrictions) may restrict aircraft operations over a defined period in the nighttime, to limit the disruption of [aircraft noise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aircraft_noise) on the sleep of nearby residents. Notable examples are the London airports of [Heathrow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Heathrow_Airport), [Gatwick](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Gatwick_Airport) and [Stansted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/London_Stansted_Airport), which operate under the [Quota Count system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quota_Count_system).

- In a few locations in the UK, patrons of licensed premises may not enter after a "curfew" time, also known as "last orders". In [Inverclyde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inverclyde), for example, this is currently set at 12\:00 am.

  [^cite_note-20]

- In many boarding schools, students are usually ordered by school staff to stay in their dormitories at night.

### Sport

- In [baseball](3850.md), a time after which a game must end, or play be suspended. For example, in the [American League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_League) the curfew rule for many years decreed that no [inning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innings) could begin after 1 am local time (with the exception of international games).

- In [Formula One](10854.md), a time when team members can no longer work on their [cars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Formula_One_car). Curfews are imposed by the [FIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIA) on the Wednesday, Thursday, and Friday preceding a [Grand Prix weekend](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prix_weekend), the latter 15 hours before the start of the third [free practice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_practice) session or sprint. Teams are permitted two exemptions on each day throughout a season.

### Australia

On 17 August 2011, a nighttime curfew was imposed on children who had run amok in the streets of Victoria after repeating youth offenses.[^cite_note-22]

On 2 August 2020, following the surge of [COVID-19](63030231.md) cases in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)), especially in Melbourne, [Victorian premier](74903453.md) [Daniel Andrews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daniel_Andrews) declared a state of disaster across the state and imposed stage 4 lockdown in Metropolitan [Melbourne](17306237.md). The new measures included nighttime curfew, which was implemented across Melbourne from 20\:00 to 05\:00 ([AEST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Australia)). The restrictions came into effect at 18\:00 (6 pm) and lasted until 28 September 2020 (5 am).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

On 16 August 2021, following a surge of [COVID-19](63030231.md) cases and a drop in compliance in restrictions in [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria_\(Australia\)), especially in Melbourne, Victorian Premier Daniel Andrews reinstated the curfew in Melbourne, this time from 21\:00 to 05\:00 ([AEST](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_in_Australia)) effective midnight 17 August 2021 until at least 2 September 2021.[^cite_note-25]

On 20 August 2021, as [COVID-19](63030231.md) cases continued to surge in [New South Wales](21654.md), [NSW Premier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Premier_of_NSW) [Gladys Berejiklian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gladys_Berejiklian) imposed a curfew in the local government areas of Bayside, Blacktown, Burwood, Campbelltown, Canterbury-Bankstown, Cumberland, Fairfield, Georges River, Liverpool, Parramatta, Strathfield, and parts of Penrith, from 9\:00 pm to 5\:00 am (AEST) beginning from 23 August.[^cite_note-26]

### Belgium

Main article: [COVID-19 pandemic in Belgium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Belgium)

On 17 October 2020, due to surge of [COVID-19](63030231.md) cases and deaths in [Belgium](3343.md), Prime Minister [Alexander De Croo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_De_Croo) announced a nationwide curfew from midnight to 05\:00 am local time. The curfew was imposed on 19 October 2020 and was to last for four weeks.[^cite_note-27] The government also announced the closure of cafes, bars and restaurants for one month and alcohol sales were banned after 8\:00 pm local time.[^cite_note-28]

### Bangladesh

Main article: [July uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_uprising)

On 19 July 2024, [Sheikh Hasina](623439.md)-led [Awami League](404861.md) government declared a national curfew and announced plans to deploy the [army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bangladesh_Army) to tackle the public unrest in Bangladesh.[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] The government announced the imposition of a curfew after days of clashes at [protests against government job quotas](77355536.md) across the country.[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35] On 4 August, government declared a curfew again following the deadliest day of the mass uprising on the [non-cooperation movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-cooperation_movement_\(2024\)).

### Canada

See also: [COVID-19 pandemic in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_Canada)



Sign in a [Montréal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montr%C3%A9al) store window indicating that the store would close early due to curfew (2021).

On 6 January 2021, due to a surge of [COVID-19](63030231.md) cases and deaths in the [province of Quebec](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Quebec), a curfew was ordered by the premier of Quebec [François Legault](6899647.md). The curfew was adjusted for different areas of the province depending on the number of cases, amongst other criteria. The more populous areas, such as the urban areas of Montréal and Quebec City qualified as "red zones" and were placed under a curfew from 8 pm to 5 am while the less urban areas were either "orange zones" with a curfew from 9\:30 pm to 5 am. This curfew was expected to be in effect from 9 January up to and including 8 February 2021. "Yellow zones" did not have curfew. However, the curfew did not end in February. It ended on 28 May 2021.[^cite_note-36] On 30 December 2021, Quebec reinstated the nightly curfew this time starting at 10\:00 pm to 5\:00 am.[^cite_note-37] Following the reinstatement of the curfew, studies came out doubting its effectiveness in lowering the transmission of COVID-19.[^cite_note-38]

### Philippines

In 1565, the Spanish conquistador [Miguel López de Legazpi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_L%C3%B3pez_de_Legazpi) arrived in [Cebu](160278.md) to [colonize the islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Colonization_of_the_Philippines) that would later be known as the Philippines. Legazpi constructed a fort and instituted a curfew for those entering it at night, citing concerns that "women prostituted themselves in the camp."[^cite_note-78] During the [American colonial period in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_colonial_period_in_the_Philippines) at the turn of the 19th century, [Manila](184334.md) was under a "Curfew Order" requiring them not to go out of their houses after 7\:00 pm, and later the restriction changed to 8\:30 pm, then to 10\:00 pm, then to 11\:00 pm, and finally revoked in 1901.[^cite_note-79]

On 22 September 1972, the day after then [President](271126.md) [Ferdinand Marcos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferdinand_Marcos) declared [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martial_law_under_Ferdinand_Marcos), he issued General Order No. 4, mandating a curfew from midnight to 4\:00 a.m., and anyone who violated this curfew would be arrested and taken into custody.[^cite_note-80] In December 1972, Marcos conditionally lifted the curfew,[^cite_note-81] and in 1977, he announced the complete removal of the curfew as part of efforts to ease restrictions imposed during martial law.[^cite_note-82] The primary goal of the curfew was to reduce crime, among other reasons.[^cite_note-83]

On 23 May 2017, then President [Rodrigo Duterte](7407293.md) proclaimed [martial law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proclamation_No._216) in the entire [Mindanao](20546.md) island group as a response to the [siege of Marawi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marawi_siege), and the proclamation involved curfews.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85] 129 areas in Mindanao had curfews in 2017.[^cite_note-86] After winning the [2016 presidential elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Philippine_presidential_election) and before starting his term, Duterte proposed a nationwide curfew for minors.[^cite_note-87]

There have been local ordinances regarding curfew for minors in some cities and municipalities[^cite_note-88][^cite_note-89] but no nationwide law. Article 129 of the Presidential Decree 603 in 1974 permits city or municipal councils to implement "curfew hours for children as may be warranted by local conditions."[^cite_note-90] In 2022, a proposed bill was introduced in the [House of Representatives](293605.md) to implement a nationwide curfew.[^cite_note-91] However, the bill has been pending with the Committee on the Welfare of Children since July 2022.[^cite_note-92]

The curfew for minors in the Philippines is a debatable topic. Those in favor argue that curfews will promote the children's safety and welfare, while those against state that curfews infringe on children's right to travel in their vicinity and their parents' right to nurture them.[^cite_note-constitutionality-93] In 2017, the [Supreme Court of the Philippines](1335534.md) ruled on the constitutionality of some of these local ordinances after a group filed a case. The high court upheld the curfew for minors in Quezon City but did not support the curfews implemented in [Manila](184334.md) and [Navotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navotas).[^cite_note-constitutionality-93]

During the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_the_Philippines), several areas of the Philippines enforced curfews, including [Metro Manila](72340.md), [Bulacan](252408.md), [Cavite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavite), [Laguna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_\(province\)), [Rizal](232458.md), [Cebu](160278.md), and [Cagayan de Oro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cagayan_de_Oro).[^cite_note-94][^cite_note-95][^cite_note-96]

### United States

Curfew law in the United States is usually a matter of [local ordinance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_ordinance) (mainly applied by a municipality or county), rather than federal law. However, the [Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Constitution) guarantees certain rights, which have been applied to the states through the [14th Amendment](31666.md). Hence, any curfew law may be overruled and struck down if, for example, it violates [1st](31653.md), [4th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution), [5th](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution) or 14th Amendment rights.

Nonetheless, curfews are set by state and local governments. They vary by state[^cite_note-111] and even by county or municipality.

American military curfews are a tool used by commanders at various installations to shape the behavior of soldiers.[^cite_note-112]

#### Juvenile curfews

Local ordinances and state statutes may make it unlawful for minors below a certain age to be on public streets, unless they are accompanied by a parent or an adult or on lawful and necessary business on behalf of their parents or guardians. For example, a Michigan state law provides that "\[n]o minor under the age of 12 years shall loiter, idle or congregate in or on any public street, highway, alley or park between the hours of 10 o'clock p.m. and 6 o'clock a.m., unless the minor is accompanied by a parent or guardian, or some adult delegated by the parent or guardian to accompany the child." MCLA § 722.751; MSA § 28.342(1). Curfew laws in other states and cities typically set forth different curfews for minors of different ages.

The stated purpose of such laws is generally to deter disorderly behavior and crime, while others can include to protect youth from victimization and to strengthen parental responsibility,[^cite_note-113] but their effectiveness is subject to debate. Generally, curfews attempt to address vandalism, shootings, and [property crimes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_crime), which are believed to happen mostly at night, but are less commonly used to address [underage drinking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legal_drinking_age), [drunk driving](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driving_under_the_influence), risky driving,[^cite_note-114] and [teenage pregnancy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teenage_pregnancy). Parents can be fined, [charged](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Criminal_charge) or ordered to take parenting classes for willingly, or through insufficient control or supervision, permitting the child to violate the curfew. Many local curfew laws were enacted in the 1950s and 1960s to attack the "juvenile delinquent" problem of youth gangs. Most curfew exceptions include:

- accompanied by a parent or an adult appointed by the parent;

- going to or coming home from work, school, religious, or recreational activity;

- engaging in a lawful employment activity or;

- involved in an emergency;

Some cities make it illegal for a business owner, operator, or any employee to knowingly allow a minor to remain in the establishment during curfew hours. A business owner, operator, or any employee may be also subject to fines.[^cite_note-115]

A 2011 UC-Berkeley study looked at the 54 larger U.S. cities that enacted youth curfews between 1985 and 2002 and found that arrests of youths affected by curfew restrictions dropped almost 15% in the first year and approximately 10% in following years.[^cite_note-116] However, not all studies agree with the conclusion that youth curfew laws actually reduce crime, and many studies find no benefit or sometimes even the opposite.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] For example, one 2016 systematic review of 12 studies on the matter found that the effect on crime is close to zero, and can perhaps even backfire somewhat.[^cite_note-119]

There are also concerns about [racial profiling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racial_profiling).[^cite_note-120] In response to concerns about racial profiling, [Montgomery County, Maryland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery_County,_Maryland), passed a limited curfew, which would permit police officers to arrest juveniles in situations that appear threatening.[^cite_note-121]

#### Mall curfews

Main article: [Mall curfew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mall_curfew)

Many malls in the United States have policies that prohibit minors under a specified age from entering the mall after specified times,[^cite_note-122] unless they are accompanied by a parent or another adult or are working at the mall during curfew times.[^cite_note-123] Such policies are known as *mall curfews.* For example, the [Mall of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mall_of_America)'s Youth Supervision Policy requires all minors visiting Mall after 4 p.m. to be accompanied by someone 21 or older. One adult can chaperone up to four minors. The policy is part of the mall's broader security program, which includes the addition of metal detectors, more patrols and a [K-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_dog) unit. Malls that have policies prohibiting unaccompanied minors at any time are known as *parental escort policies.*[^cite_note-124]

#### Curfews for adults

States and municipalities in the United States have occasionally enacted curfews on the population at large, often as a result of severely [inclement weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inclement_weather) or [civil disorder](329906.md). Some such curfews require all citizens simply to refrain from driving. Others require all citizens to remain inside, with exceptions granted to those in important positions, such as [elected officials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elected_official), [law enforcement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_enforcement) personnel, [first responders](500273.md), healthcare workers, and the mass media.

However, unlike juvenile curfews, all-ages curfews have always been very limited in terms of both location and duration. That is, they are temporary and restricted to very specific areas, and generally only implemented during states of emergency, then subsequently lifted or allowed to sunset.

In 1992, a curfew was imposed in [Los Angeles](18110.md), California during the [Rodney King Riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_King_Riots).

In 2015, the city of [Baltimore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltimore) enacted a curfew on all citizens that lasted for five days and prohibited all citizens from going outdoors from 10 pm to 5 am with the exception of those traveling to or from work and those with medical emergencies. This was in response to the [2015 Baltimore protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Baltimore_protests).

During the global [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) in 2020, local curfews were used (typically in combination with daytime [lockdown policies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_lockdowns)) in the attempt to slow down the spread of the [virus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SARS-CoV-2) by limiting nonessential interactions between people from different households. Later in 2020, citywide curfews were enacted in major cities across the country due to [protests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Floyd_protests) following the [murder of George Floyd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murder_of_George_Floyd) in May. [Arizona](21883824.md) enacted a statewide curfew. Countywide curfews were enacted for [Los Angeles County](79734.md) and [Alameda County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alameda_County) in California. In spring 2021, the city of [Miami Beach, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miami_Beach,_Florida) enacted a citywide curfew due to public disorder associated with [spring break](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_break) celebrations.
