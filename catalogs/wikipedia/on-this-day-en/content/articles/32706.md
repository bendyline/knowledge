**Vancouver**[^cite_note-6] is a major city in [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada), located in the [Lower Mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Mainland) region of [British Columbia](3392.md). As the [most populous city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_British_Columbia) in the province, the [2021 Canadian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_census) recorded 662,248 people in the city, up from 631,486 in 2016. The [Greater Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Vancouver) area had a population of 2.6 million in 2021, making it the [third-largest metropolitan area in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_census_metropolitan_areas_and_agglomerations_in_Canada#List) after [Toronto](64646.md) and [Montreal](7954681.md). The region, together with the [Fraser Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Valley), makes up the Lower Mainland, which has a total population of over 3 million. Vancouver has the highest [population density](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Population_density) in Canada, with over 5,700 inhabitants per square kilometre (15,000/sq mi),[^cite_note-\:0-7] and the fourth highest in North America (after [New York City](645042.md), [San Francisco](49728.md), and [Mexico City](18987.md)).

Vancouver is one of the most [ethnically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_origins_of_people_in_Canada) and [linguistically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Canada) diverse cities in Canada: 49.3 percent of its residents are not native English speakers, 47.8 percent are native speakers of neither English nor French, and 54.5 percent of residents belong to [visible minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_minority) groups.[^cite_note-VancouverCityMinority2021-8][^cite_note-\:1-9] It has been consistently ranked one of the [most liveable cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_quality_of_life_indices) in Canada and in the world.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-BBC_News-11][^cite_note-CNBC-12] In terms of [housing affordability](3531662.md), Vancouver is also one of [the most expensive cities in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing_in_Canada) and [in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing_by_country).[^cite_note-13] [Vancouverism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouverism) is the city's [urban planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_planning) design philosophy.

Indigenous settlement of Vancouver began more than 10,000 years ago and included the [Squamish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squamish_people), [Musqueam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musqueam_Indian_Band), and [Tsleil-Waututh (Burrard)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsleil-Waututh_First_Nation) peoples. The beginnings of the modern city, which was originally named [Gastown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastown), grew around the site of a makeshift tavern on the western edges of [Hastings Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_Mill) that was built on July 1, 1867, and owned by proprietor [Gassy Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Deighton). The Gastown steam clock marks the original site. Gastown then formally registered as a [townsite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townsite) named [Granville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granville,_British_Columbia), [Burrard Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrard_Inlet). The city was renamed "Vancouver" in 1886 through a deal with the [Canadian Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway). The Canadian Pacific transcontinental railway was extended to the city by 1887. The city's large natural seaport on the [Pacific Ocean](23070.md) became a vital link in the trade between [Asia-Pacific](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific), [East Asia](19605700.md), [Europe](9239.md), and [Eastern Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Canada).[^cite_note-Morley-14][^cite_note-STEV-15]

Vancouver has hosted many international conferences and events, including the [1954 Commonwealth Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1954_British_Empire_and_Commonwealth_Games), [UN Habitat I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habitat_I), [Expo 86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_86), [APEC Canada 1997](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APEC_Canada_1997), the [World Police and Fire Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Police_and_Fire_Games) in 1989 and 2009, matches and the finals of the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup), several matches of the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md),[^cite_note-FIFA-16] and the [2010 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics) and [Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Paralympics) which were held in Vancouver and [Whistler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whistler,_British_Columbia), a resort community 125 km (78 mi) north of the city.[^cite_note-V2010-17] In 1969, [Greenpeace](12233.md) was founded in Vancouver. The city became the permanent home of [TED conferences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TED_\(conference\)) in 2014.

As of 2016, the [Port of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Vancouver) is the fourth-largest port by tonnage in the Americas,[^cite_note-18] the busiest and largest port in Canada, and the most diversified port in North America.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20] While forestry remains its largest industry, Vancouver is well known as an urban centre surrounded by nature, making [tourism its second-largest industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism_in_Vancouver).[^cite_note-21] Major film production studios in Vancouver and nearby [Burnaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnaby) have turned Greater Vancouver and nearby areas into one of the largest [film production](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principal_photography) centres in North America,[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] earning it the nickname "[Hollywood North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_North)".[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Etymology

The city takes its name from [George Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Vancouver), who explored the inner harbour of [Burrard Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrard_Inlet) in 1792 and gave various places British names.[^cite_note-27] The family name "Vancouver" itself originates from the Dutch "van Coevorden", denoting somebody from the city of [Coevorden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coevorden), Netherlands. The explorer's ancestors came to England "from Coevorden", which is the origin of the name that eventually became "Vancouver".[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

*K'emk'emeláy̓*, meaning "place of many [maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maple) trees", was the name of a village inhabited by the Squamish people, where a [sawmill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_Mill) was established by [Edward Stamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edward_Stamp), beginning the settlement that became Vancouver. This name is used to represent the city on the [Squamish-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squamish_language) road signage along the [Sea to Sky Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_to_Sky_Highway).[^cite_note-SquamishVillages-30] In hən̓q̓əmin̓əm̓ (the Downriver dialect of Halkomelem), spoken by the [Musqueam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musqueam_First_Nation), there is no specific term for Vancouver.[^cite_note-32] Rather there existed names for specific villages and landscape features that the people knew intimately in the area in which Vancouver exists currently, as opposed to larger geographic features.[^cite_note-suttles-31] The region where Vancouver is currently located was referred to by the [Stó꞉lō](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St%C3%B3%EA%9E%89l%C5%8D) in the Upriver [Halkomelem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halkomelem) dialect as *Lhq’á\:lets*,[^cite_note-33][^cite_note-34] meaning "wide at the bottom/end". Speakers of the Island dialect of Halkomelem referred to the region of Vancouver as *sqwx̌wam̓ush*[^cite_note-35] or *skwóm̓esh*, referring to the Squamish,[^cite_note-rozen-36] or as *Pankúpe7*, a transliteration of the English word *Vancouver*.[^cite_note-rozen-36]

## History

Main article: [History of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Vancouver)

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of Vancouver history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Vancouver_history).

### Before 1850

[Archaeological](18951655.md) records indicate that [Aboriginal people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada) were already living in the Vancouver area from 8,000 to 10,000 years ago.[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-Atlas-38]

The [Squamish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squamish_people), [Musqueam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musqueam_Indian_Band), and [Tsleil-Waututh (Burrard)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsleil-Waututh_First_Nation) peoples of the [Coast Salish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coast_Salish) group[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40][^cite_note-41] had villages in various parts of present-day Vancouver, such as [Stanley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Park), [False Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_Creek), [Kitsilano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsilano), [Point Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Grey) and near the mouth of the [Fraser River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_River).[^cite_note-Atlas-38]

Europeans became acquainted with the area of the future Vancouver when [José María Narváez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Narv%C3%A1ez) of [Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) explored the coast of present-day [Point Grey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Point_Grey) and parts of [Burrard Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrard_Inlet) in 1791.[^cite_note-42]



Draft map of villages and landmarks with their Indigenous names, [Burrard Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrard_Inlet) and [English Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Bay,_Vancouver), by Vancouver archivist [J. S. Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/J._S._Matthews)&#x20;

The explorer and [North West Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_West_Company) trader [Simon Fraser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fraser_\(explorer\)) and his crew became the first-known Europeans to set foot on the site of the present-day city. In 1808, they travelled from the east down the Fraser River, perhaps as far as Point Grey.[^cite_note-43]

### Early growth



View of [Gastown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastown) from Carrall and [Water Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_Street,_Vancouver) in 1886. Gastown was a settlement that quickly became a centre for trade and commerce on Burrard Inlet.

The [Fraser Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Canyon_Gold_Rush) of 1858 brought over 25,000 men, mainly from [California](5407.md), to nearby [New Westminster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Westminster) (founded February 14, 1859) on the Fraser River, on their way to the [Fraser Canyon](1897554.md), bypassing what would become Vancouver.[^cite_note-Vancouver's_past-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] Vancouver is among British Columbia's youngest cities;[^cite_note-Horizons-47] the first European settlement in what is now Vancouver was not until 1862 at McCleery's Farm on the Fraser River, just east of the ancient village of [Musqueam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musqueam_Indian_Band) in what is now [Marpole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marpole). A sawmill was established at Moodyville (now the [City of North Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_North_Vancouver)) in 1863, beginning the city's long relationship with logging. It was quickly followed by mills owned by Captain Edward Stamp on the south shore of the inlet. Stamp, who had begun logging in the [Port Alberni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Alberni) area, first attempted to run a mill at [Brockton Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brockton_Point), but difficult currents and reefs forced the relocation of the operation in 1867 to a point near the foot of Dunlevy Street. This mill, known as the [Hastings Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_Mill), became the nucleus around which Vancouver formed. The mill's central role in the city waned after the arrival of the [Canadian Pacific Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Pacific_Railway) (CPR) in the 1880s. It nevertheless remained important to the local economy until it closed in the 1920s.[^cite_note-GVB-48] The settlement, which came to be called [Gastown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastown), proliferated around the original makeshift tavern established by [Gassy Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gassy_Jack) in 1867 on the edge of the Hastings Mill property.[^cite_note-Horizons-47][^cite_note-49]

In 1870, the [colonial government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colony_of_British_Columbia_\(1866%E2%80%931871\)) surveyed the settlement and laid out a [townsite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Townsite), renamed "[Granville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granville,_British_Columbia)" in honour of the then–British [Secretary of State for the Colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_the_Colonies), [Lord Granville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granville_Leveson-Gower,_2nd_Earl_Granville). This site, with its natural harbour, was selected in 1884[^cite_note-50] as the terminus for the Canadian Pacific Railway, to the disappointment of [Port Moody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Moody), New Westminster and [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_British_Columbia), all of which had vied to be the railhead. A railway was among the inducements for British Columbia to join the [Confederation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Confederation) in 1871, but the [Pacific Scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Scandal) and arguments over the use of Chinese labour delayed construction until the 1880s.[^cite_note-51]

### Incorporation



The first [Vancouver City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_City_Council) meeting following the [Great Vancouver Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Vancouver_Fire) in 1886

The City of Vancouver was incorporated on April 6, 1886, the same year that the first transcontinental train arrived. CPR president [William Van Horne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Cornelius_Van_Horne) arrived in Port Moody to establish the CPR terminus recommended by [Henry John Cambie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_John_Cambie) and gave the city its name in honour of [George Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Vancouver).[^cite_note-Horizons-47] The [Great Vancouver Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Vancouver_Fire) on June 13, 1886, razed the entire city. The [Vancouver Fire Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Fire_and_Rescue_Services) was established that year and the city quickly rebuilt.[^cite_note-GVB-48] Vancouver's population grew from a settlement of 1,000 people in 1881 to over 20,000 by the turn of the century and 100,000 by 1911.[^cite_note-52]

Vancouver merchants outfitted prospectors bound for the [Klondike Gold Rush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Klondike_Gold_Rush) in 1898.[^cite_note-Vancouver's_past-44] One of those merchants, Charles Woodward, had opened the first [Woodward's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodward's) store at Abbott and Cordova Streets in 1892 and, along with [Spencer's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spencer's_\(department_store\)) and the [Hudson's Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hudson's_Bay_Company) department stores, formed the core of the city's retail sector for decades.[^cite_note-53]

The economy of early Vancouver was dominated by large companies such as the CPR, which fuelled economic activity and led to the rapid development of the new city;[^cite_note-54] in fact, the CPR was the main real estate owner and housing developer in the city. While some manufacturing did develop, including the establishment of the British Columbia Sugar Refinery by [Benjamin Tingley Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benjamin_Tingley_Rogers) in 1890,[^cite_note-55] natural resources became the basis for Vancouver's economy. The resource sector was initially based on logging and later on exports moving through the seaport, where commercial traffic constituted the largest economic sector in Vancouver by the 1930s.[^cite_note-56]

### 20th century



Plainclothes [RCMP](69080.md) officers attack [Relief Camp Workers' Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relief_Camp_Workers'_Union) protesters in 1938. Several protests over unemployment occurred in the city during the [Great Depression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Depression).



Downtown celebrations at the end of [World War II](32927.md)

The dominance of the economy by big business was accompanied by an often militant [labour movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trades_and_Labor_Congress_of_Canada). The first major sympathy strike was in 1903 when railway employees struck against the CPR for union recognition. Labour leader Frank Rogers was killed by CPR police while picketing at the docks, becoming the movement's first martyr in British Columbia.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-phillips-58]<sup>: 39–41 </sup> The rise of industrial tensions throughout the province led to Canada's first general strike in 1918, at the [Cumberland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cumberland,_British_Columbia) coal mines on [Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Island).[^cite_note-phillips-58]<sup>: 71–74 </sup> Following a lull in the 1920s, the strike wave peaked in 1935 when unemployed men flooded the city to protest conditions in the relief camps run by the military in remote areas throughout the province.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-Brown_1987-60] After two tense months of daily and disruptive protesting, the [relief camp strikers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relief_Camp_Workers'_Union) decided to take their grievances to the federal government and embarked on the [On-to-Ottawa Trek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/On-to-Ottawa_Trek),[^cite_note-Brown_1987-60] but their protest was put down by force. The workers were arrested near [Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission,_British_Columbia) and interned in work camps for the duration of the Depression.[^cite_note-61]

Other social movements, such as the [first-wave feminist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-wave_feminism), moral reform, and [temperance movements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_Canada), were also instrumental in Vancouver's development. [Mary Ellen Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mary_Ellen_Smith), a Vancouver [suffragist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_suffrage) and [prohibitionist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_in_Canada), became the first woman elected to a [provincial legislature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_assemblies_of_Canadian_provinces_and_territories) in Canada in 1918.[^cite_note-robin-62]<sup>: [172](https://archive.org/details/rushforspoilscom0000robi/page/172) </sup> Alcohol prohibition began in the First World War and lasted until 1921 when the provincial government established control over alcohol sales, a practice still in place today.[^cite_note-robin-62]<sup>: [187–188](https://archive.org/details/rushforspoilscom0000robi/page/187) </sup> Canada's first [drug law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prohibition_of_drugs) came about following an inquiry conducted by the federal [minister of Labour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Labour_\(Canada\)) and future prime minister, [William Lyon Mackenzie King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Lyon_Mackenzie_King). King was sent to investigate damages claims resulting from a riot when the [Asiatic Exclusion League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asiatic_Exclusion_League) led a rampage through [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Vancouver) and [Japantown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japantown,_Vancouver). Two of the claimants were [opium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium) manufacturers, and after further investigation, King found that white women were reportedly frequenting [opium dens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opium_den) as well as [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Canadians) men. A federal law banning the manufacture, sale, and importation of opium for non-medicinal purposes was soon passed based on these revelations.[^cite_note-63] These riots, and the formation of the Asiatic Exclusion League, also act as signs of a growing fear and mistrust towards the Japanese living in Vancouver and throughout BC. These fears were exacerbated by the [attack on Pearl Harbor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_on_Pearl_Harbor) leading to the eventual [internment or deportation of all Japanese-Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internment_of_Japanese_Canadians) living in the city and the province.[^cite_note-64] After the war, these Japanese-Canadian men and women were not allowed to return to cities like Vancouver causing areas, like the aforementioned [Japantown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japantown,_Vancouver), to cease to be ethnically Japanese areas as the communities never revived.[^cite_note-65]

[Amalgamation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merger_\(politics\)) with Point Grey and South Vancouver gave the city its final boundaries not long before it became the third-largest metropolis in the country. As of January 1, 1929, the population of the enlarged Vancouver was 228,193.[^cite_note-66]

During the late twentieth century, [Vancouver's stock exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Stock_Exchange) was notorious for listing many companies that were scams or fronts for criminals.[^cite_note-67] A 1989 *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* article said the city had become the "scam capital of the world".[^cite_note-68] The exchange was merged with the [Alberta Stock Exchange](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alberta_Stock_Exchange) and the Canadian Dealing Network in 1999.[^cite_note-69]

## Geography

Further information: [List of bodies of water in Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_bodies_of_water_in_Vancouver) and [Lower Mainland Ecoregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Mainland_Ecoregion)



Satellite image of Metro Vancouver (2018)

Located on the [Burrard Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrard_Peninsula), Vancouver lies between [Burrard Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrard_Inlet) to the north and the [Fraser River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_River) to the south. The [Strait of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strait_of_Georgia), to the west, is shielded from the Pacific Ocean by [Vancouver Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Island). The city has an area of 115.18 km<sup>2</sup> (44.47 sq mi), including both flat and hilly ground; it is within the [Pacific Maritime Ecozone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Maritime_Ecozone_\(CEC\)).[^cite_note-70]

Until the city's naming in 1885, "Vancouver" referred to Vancouver Island, and it remains a common misconception that the city is located on the island.[^cite_note-71][^cite_note-72] The island and the city are both named after Royal Navy Captain [George Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Vancouver) (as is the city of [Vancouver, Washington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver,_Washington), in the United States).

Vancouver has one of the largest urban parks in North America, [Stanley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Park), which covers 404.9 ha (1,001 acres).[^cite_note-73] The [North Shore Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Shore_Mountains) dominate the cityscape, and on a clear day, scenic vistas include the snow-capped volcano [Mount Baker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Baker) in the state of Washington to the southeast, Vancouver Island across the Strait of Georgia to the west and southwest, and [Bowen Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bowen_Island) to the northwest.[^cite_note-aboutvancouver-74]

### Ecology

The vegetation in the Vancouver area was originally [temperate rainforest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temperate_rainforest), consisting of [conifers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conifer) with scattered pockets of maple and alder and large areas of swampland (even in upland areas, due to poor drainage).[^cite_note-75] The conifers were a typical coastal British Columbia mix of [Douglas fir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_fir), [western red cedar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thuja_plicata) and [western hemlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsuga_heterophylla).[^cite_note-76] The area is thought to have had the largest trees of these species on the [British Columbia Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Coast). Only in [Elliott Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elliott_Bay), [Seattle](11388236.md), did the size of trees rival those of Burrard Inlet and [English Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_Bay,_Vancouver). The largest trees in Vancouver's old-growth forest were in the [Gastown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastown) area, where the first logging occurred and on the southern slopes of [False Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_Creek) and English Bay, especially around [Jericho Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jericho_Beach). The forest in Stanley Park was logged between the 1860s and 1880s, and evidence of old-fashioned logging techniques such as [springboard notches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logging#Springboards) can still be seen there.[^cite_note-77]

Many plants and trees growing throughout Vancouver and the [Lower Mainland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Mainland) were imported from other parts of the continent and points across the Pacific. Examples include the [monkey puzzle tree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Araucaria_araucana), the [Japanese maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acer_palmatum) and various flowering exotics, such as [magnolias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia), [azaleas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azalea) and [rhododendrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhododendron). Some species imported from harsher climates in Eastern Canada or Europe have grown to immense sizes. The native [Douglas maple](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acer_glabrum) can also attain a tremendous size. Many of the city's streets are lined with flowering varieties of [Japanese cherry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherry_blossom) trees donated from the 1930s onward by the government of Japan. These flower for several weeks in early spring each year, an occasion celebrated by the [Vancouver Cherry Blossom Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Cherry_Blossom_Festival). Other streets are lined with flowering chestnut, [horse chestnut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aesculus_hippocastanum) and other decorative shade trees.[^cite_note-78]

Vancouver's climate, one of the mildest and most temperate climates in Canada, is classified as an [oceanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanic_climate) climate ([Köppen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen_climate_classification): *Cfb*) bordering on a warm-summer [Mediterranean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate) climate (Köppen: *Csb*). While the city has the coolest summer average high of all major Canadian metropolitan areas, winters in Greater Vancouver are the fourth-mildest of Canadian cities, after nearby [Victoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_British_Columbia), [Nanaimo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanaimo) and [Duncan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duncan,_British_Columbia), all on Vancouver Island.[^cite_note-79]

Vancouver is one of the wettest Canadian cities. However, precipitation varies throughout the metropolitan area. Annual precipitation as measured at [Vancouver International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_International_Airport) in [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_British_Columbia) averages 1,189 mm (46.8 in), compared with 1,588 mm (62.5 in) in the downtown area and 2,044 mm (80.5 in) in North Vancouver.[^cite_note-80][^cite_note-81] The daily maximum averages 22 °C (72 °F) in July and August, with highs rarely reaching 30 °C (86 °F).[^cite_note-ccn-82] The summer months are typically dry, with only one in five days receiving precipitation during July and August. In contrast, most days from November through March record some precipitation.[^cite_note-vanprecip-83]

The highest temperature ever recorded at the airport was 34.4 °C (93.9 °F) set on July 30, 2009,[^cite_note-84] and the highest temperature ever recorded within the city of Vancouver was 35.0 °C (95.0 °F) occurring first on July 31, 1965,[^cite_note-85] again on August 8, 1981,[^cite_note-86] and also on May 29, 1983.[^cite_note-87] The coldest temperature ever recorded in the city was −17.8 °C (0.0 °F) on January 14, 1950[^cite_note-88] and again on December 29, 1968.[^cite_note-89]

On average, snow falls nine days per year, with three days receiving 5 cm (2.0 in) or more. Average yearly snowfall is 38.1 cm (15.0 in) but typically does not remain on the ground for long.[^cite_note-ccn-82]

Vancouver's [growing season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Growing_season) averages 237 days, from March 18 until November 10.[^cite_note-ccn-82] Vancouver's 1981–2010 USDA Plant Hardiness Zone ranges from 8a to 9a depending on elevation and proximity to water.[^cite_note-90]

&#x20;Due to the mild climate, Vancouver is a popular city for the cultivation of various [cold-hardy palm trees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hardy_palms). The most popular location for planting palms is English Bay, and the most popular palm is [Trachycarpus fortunei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trachycarpus_fortunei). They were first planted in the 1980s.[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]

## Cityscape

### Urban planning

Main article: [Vancouverism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouverism)



Aerial view of [Downtown Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Vancouver). Urban development in Vancouver is characterized by a large residential population living in the city centre with mixed-use developments.

As of 2021, Vancouver is the most densely populated city in Canada.[^cite_note-\:0-7] Urban planning in Vancouver is characterized by high-rise residential and mixed-use development in urban centres, as an alternative to [sprawl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_sprawl).[^cite_note-112] As part of the larger [Metro Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Vancouver) region, it is influenced by the policy direction of livability as illustrated in Metro Vancouver's Regional Growth Strategy.

Vancouver ranked high on the [Global Liveability Ranking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Liveability_Ranking) and stood at number 1 on the list for several years until 2011.[^cite_note-113] In recent years, it has dropped, ranking as low as 16 in 2021. As of 2022, Vancouver was ranked as having the fifth-highest quality of living of any city on Earth.[^cite_note-114] According to *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)*, Vancouver had the fourth-most expensive real estate market in the world in 2019.[^cite_note-115] Vancouver has also been ranked among Canada's most expensive cities in which to live. Sales in February 2016 were 56.3 percent higher than the 10-year average for the month.[^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118] *Forbes* also ranked Vancouver as the tenth-cleanest city in the world in 2007.[^cite_note-119]

Vancouver's characteristic approach to urban planning originated in the late 1950s, when city planners began to encourage the building of high-rise residential towers in Vancouver's [West End](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_End,_Vancouver),[^cite_note-120] subject to strict requirements for setbacks and open space to protect sight lines and preserve green space. The success of these dense but livable neighbourhoods led to the redevelopment of urban industrial sites, such as North False Creek and Coal Harbour, beginning in the mid-1980s. The result is a compact urban core that has gained international recognition for its "high amenity and 'livable' development".[^cite_note-121] In 2006, the city launched a planning initiative entitled [EcoDensity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EcoDensity), with the stated goal of exploring ways in which "density, design, and land use can contribute to environmental sustainability, affordability, and livability".[^cite_note-122]



Vancouver skyline and [False Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_Creek) viewed from the [Fairview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairview,_Vancouver) neighbourhood

### Architecture

Main article: [Architecture of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Architecture_of_Vancouver)

See also: [List of tallest buildings in Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Vancouver)



[Robson Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robson_Square) is a [civic centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civic_centre) and public square designed by local architect [Arthur Erickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Erickson).



Waterfront station, Vancouver

The [Vancouver Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Art_Gallery) is housed downtown in the [neoclassical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neoclassical_architecture) former courthouse built in 1906. The courthouse building was designed by [Francis Rattenbury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Rattenbury), who also designed the [British Columbia Parliament Buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Parliament_Buildings) and the [Empress Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Empress_\(hotel\)) in Victoria, and the lavishly decorated second Hotel Vancouver.[^cite_note-123] The 556-room [Hotel Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Vancouver), opened in 1939 and the third by that name, is across the street with its copper roof. The Gothic-style [Christ Church Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christ_Church_Cathedral_\(Vancouver\)), across from the hotel, opened in 1894 and was declared a heritage building in 1976.

There are several [modern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_architecture) buildings in the downtown area, including the [Harbour Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbour_Centre), the [Vancouver Law Courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Law_Courts_\(Vancouver\)) and surrounding plaza known as [Robson Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robson_Square) (designed by [Arthur Erickson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthur_Erickson)) and the [Vancouver Library Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Public_Library) (designed by [Moshe Safdie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Safdie) and [DA Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DA_Architects_+_Planners)), reminiscent of the [Colosseum](49603.md) in Rome, and the recently completed [Woodward's building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodward's_Building) Redevelopment (designed by [Henriquez Partners Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gregory_Henriquez)).

The original [BC Hydro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Hydro) headquarters building (designed by [Ron Thom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ronald_Thom) and Ned Pratt) at Nelson and Burrard Streets is a [modernist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modernism) high-rise, now converted into the Electra condominium.[^cite_note-124] Also notable is the "concrete waffle" of the [MacMillan Bloedel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MacMillan_Bloedel) building on the north-east corner of the Georgia and Thurlow intersection.



Completed in 2008, [Living Shangri-La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Shangri-La) is the [tallest building in Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_Vancouver).

A prominent addition to the city's landscape is the giant tent-frame [Canada Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Place) (designed by [Zeidler Roberts Partnership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeidler_Partnership_Architects) Partnership, MCMP & [DA Architects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DA_Architects_+_Planners)), the former Canada Pavilion from the [1986 World Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_86), which includes part of the [Convention Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Convention_Centre), the [Pan-Pacific Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Pacific_Vancouver_Hotel), and a cruise ship terminal. Two modern buildings that define the southern skyline away from the downtown area are [City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_City_Hall) and the Centennial Pavilion of [Vancouver General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_General_Hospital), both designed by [Townley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fred_Townley) and Matheson in 1936 and 1958, respectively.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-kalman-126]<sup>: 160–161 </sup>

A collection of [Edwardian buildings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edwardian_architecture) in the city's old downtown core were, in their day, the tallest commercial buildings in the [British Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Empire). These were, in succession, the Carter-Cotton Building (former home of *[The Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Province)* newspaper), the [Dominion Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominion_Building) (1907) and the [Sun Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Tower) (1911), the former two at Cambie and [Hastings Streets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hastings_Street_\(Vancouver\)) and the latter at Beatty and Pender Streets. The Sun Tower's [cupola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cupola) was finally exceeded as the Empire's tallest commercial building by the elaborate [Art Deco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Art_Deco) [Marine Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Building) in the 1920s.[^cite_note-kalman-126]<sup>: 22, 24, 78 </sup> The Marine Building is known for its elaborate ceramic tile facings and brass-gilt doors and elevators, which make it a favourite location for movie shoots.[^cite_note-127] Topping the list of tallest buildings in Vancouver is [Living Shangri-La](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Living_Shangri-La), the second tallest building in BC at 201 m (659 ft)[^cite_note-autogenerated1-128] and 62 storeys. The second-tallest building in Vancouver is the [Paradox Hotel Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paradox_Hotel_Vancouver) at 188 m (617 ft), followed by the Private Residences at [Hotel Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hotel_Georgia), at 156 m (512 ft). The fourth-tallest is [One Wall Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_Wall_Centre) at 150 m (490 ft)[^cite_note-EMP-129] and 48 storeys, followed closely by the [Rogers Tower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Tower) at 149 m (489 ft).[^cite_note-EMP-129]

In the [2021 Canadian census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Canadian_census) conducted by [Statistics Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Statistics_Canada), Vancouver had a population of 662,248 living in 305,336 of its 328,347 total private dwellings, a change of 4.9% from its 2016 population of 631,486, making it the [eighth-largest among Canadian cities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_largest_municipalities_in_Canada_by_population). More specifically, Vancouver is the fourth-largest in [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada) after [Calgary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Calgary), [Edmonton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edmonton) and [Winnipeg](100730.md). With a land area of 115.18 km<sup>2</sup> (44.47 sq mi), it had a population density of 5,749.7/km<sup>2</sup> (14,891.6/sq mi) in 2021,[^cite_note-VancouverCity-2] the most densely populated Canadian municipality with more than 5,000 residents.[^cite_note-\:0-7]

At the [census metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Census_metropolitan_area) (CMA) level in the 2021 census, the [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_area) referred to as [Greater Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Vancouver) had a population of 2,642,825 living in 1,043,319 of its 1,104,532 total private dwellings, a change of 7.3% from its 2016 population of 2,463,431, the [third-most populous metropolitan area in the country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_census_metropolitan_areas_and_agglomerations_in_Canada) and the most populous in [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada). With a land area of 2,878.93 km<sup>2</sup> (1,111.56 sq mi), it had a population density of 918.0/km<sup>2</sup> (2,377.6/sq mi) in 2021.[^cite_note-2021censusCMA-4] Approximately 75 percent of the people living in Metro Vancouver live outside Vancouver itself.

The larger Lower Mainland-Southwest economic region (which includes also the [Squamish-Lillooet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squamish-Lillooet_Regional_District), [Fraser Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fraser_Valley_Regional_District), and [Sunshine Coast Regional District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunshine_Coast_Regional_District)) has a population of over 3.04 million.[^cite_note-130]

The 2021 census reported that [immigrants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Canada) (individuals born outside Canada) comprise 274,365 persons or 42.2% of the total population of Vancouver. Of the total immigrant population, the top countries of origin were mainland China (63,275 persons or 23.1%), Philippines (29,930 persons or 10.9%), Hong Kong (25,480 persons or 9.3%), India (14,640 persons or 5.3%), United Kingdom (12,895 persons or 4.7%), Vietnam (12,120 persons or 4.4%), Taiwan (9,870 persons or 3.6%), United States of America (9,790 persons or 3.6%), Iran (8,775 persons or 3.2%), and South Korea (6,495 persons or 2.4%).[^cite_note-2021censusB-131]

Vancouver has been called a "city of neighbourhoods".[^cite_note-CityofNeighbourhoods1-137][^cite_note-CityofNeighbourhoods2-138] Each neighbourhood in Vancouver has a distinct character and ethnic mix.[^cite_note-139] People of English, Scottish, and Irish origins were historically the largest ethnic groups in the city,[^cite_note-140] and elements of British society and culture are still visible in some areas, particularly [South Granville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Granville_Rise) and [Kerrisdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerrisdale). [Germans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Canadians) are the next-largest European ethnic group in Vancouver and were a leading force in the city's society and economy until the rise of anti-German sentiment with the outbreak of [World War I](4764461.md) in 1914.[^cite_note-STEV-15] Today, [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Canadians) are the largest visible ethnic group in Vancouver; the city has a diverse [Chinese-speaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) community with speakers of several dialects, notably [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese) and [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese).[^cite_note-GVB-48][^cite_note-141] Neighbourhoods with distinct ethnic commercial areas include [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown,_Vancouver), [Punjabi Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Market,_Vancouver), [Little Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Italy,_Vancouver), [Greektown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greektown,_Vancouver), and (formerly) [Japantown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japantown,_Vancouver).

Since the 1980s, [immigration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Immigration_to_Canada) increased substantially, making the city more [ethnically](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_origins_of_people_in_Canada) and linguistically diverse; 49 percent of Vancouver's residents do not speak [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) as their first language.[^cite_note-\:1-9] Over 25 percent of the city's inhabitants are of Chinese heritage.[^cite_note-VancouverCityMinority2021-8] In the 1980s, an influx of immigrants from [Hong Kong](13404.md) in anticipation of [the transfer of sovereignty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Handover_of_Hong_Kong) from the United Kingdom to China, combined with an increase in immigrants from [mainland China](55079.md) and previous immigrants from [Taiwan](25734.md), established in Vancouver one of the highest concentrations of ethnic Chinese residents in North America.[^cite_note-142] Another significant [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_Canadians) ethnic group in Vancouver includes [South Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Asian_Canadians), forming approximately 7 percent of the city's inhabitants; while a small community had existed in the city since 1897,[^cite_note-143] larger waves of migration began in the 1950s and 1960s,[^cite_note-SikhsOfVancouver-144] prompting new [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabis) immigrants to establish a *[Little India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_India)* (known as [Punjabi Market](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Market,_Vancouver)) and preside over much of the mass construction of the *[Vancouver Special](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Special)* across the southeastern quadrant of the city,[^cite_note-145][^cite_note-146] notably within the [Sunset neighbourhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunset,_Vancouver) prior to the [suburbanization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suburbanization) of the community to outer suburbs such as Surrey or Delta.[^cite_note-147][^cite_note-148][^cite_note-149]

Other Asian-origin groups that reside in Vancouver include [Filipinos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_Canadians) (5.9%), [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_Canadians) (1.7%), [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Canadians) (1.7%), [West Asians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Asian_Canadians) (1.9%), as well as sizeable communities of [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_Canadians), [Indonesians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Canadians), and [Cambodians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_Canadians).[^cite_note-VancouverCityMinority2021-8] Despite increases in [Latin American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American_Canadians) immigration to Vancouver in the 1980s and 1990s, recent immigration has been comparatively low. However, growth in the Latino population – which largely consists of [Mexicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Canadian) and [Salvadorans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salvadoran_Canadian) – rose in the late 2010s and early 2020s. African immigration has been similarly stagnant (3.6% and 3.3% of total immigrant population, respectively).[^cite_note-150] The [black](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Canadians) population of Vancouver is small in comparison to other Canadian major cities, making up 1.3 percent of the city. [Hogan's Alley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hogan's_Alley,_Vancouver), a small area adjacent to Chinatown, just off Main Street at Prior, was once home to a significant black community. The Black population consists of [Somalis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Somali_Canadian), Jamaicans/Caribbeans, and other groups, including those who descended from African Americans. The neighbourhood of [Strathcona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strathcona,_Vancouver) was the core of the city's [Jewish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Canada) community.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-152] In 1981, approximately 24 percent of the city population belonged to a [visible minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visible_minority) group;[^cite_note-population1981-153]<sup>: 127 </sup> at the same time, this proportion was roughly 14 percent for the entire [metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Vancouver).[^cite_note-Minority1981to2001-154][^cite_note-155] By 2016, the proportion in the city had grown to 52 percent.[^cite_note-VancouverCityMinority2016-156]

Prior to the Hong Kong diaspora of the 1990s, the largest non-British ethnic groups in the city were [Irish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irish_Canadians) and [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Canadians), followed by [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavia), [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Canadians), [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Canadians), Chinese, and [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_Canadians). From the mid-1950s until the 1980s, many [Portuguese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Canadians) immigrants came to Vancouver, and the city had the third-largest Portuguese population in Canada in 2001.[^cite_note-157] Eastern Europeans, including [Russians](59510.md), [Czechs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechs), [Poles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polish_people), [Romanians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanians) and [Hungarians](17106358.md) began immigrating after the Soviet takeover of Eastern Europe after [World War II](32927.md).[^cite_note-STEV-15] [Greek](42056.md) immigration increased in the late 1960s and early '70s, with most settling in the [Kitsilano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsilano) area. Vancouver also has a significant [aboriginal](196075.md) community of about 15,000 people.[^cite_note-indigenous2021-158][^cite_note-VancouverCityMinority2021-8]

### Homelessness

Main article: [Homelessness in Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homelessness_in_Vancouver)

Homelessness is a significant and persistent issue in Vancouver. A 2019 count found that at least 2,223 people in the city were experiencing homelessness, the highest number recorded since counts began in 2005. Of those surveyed, 28 percent reported having no physical shelter. Indigenous people accounted for 39 percent of all respondents. Three-fifths of respondents said at least two health concerns, and 67 percent said an addiction to at least one substance.[^cite_note-167]

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Vancouver)

With its location on the [Pacific Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Rim) and at the western terminus of Canada's [transcontinental highway](70775.md) and rail routes, Vancouver is one of the nation's largest industrial centres.[^cite_note-aboutvancouver-74] [Port Metro Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Vancouver), Canada's largest and most diversified port, does more than \$172 billion in trade with over 160 different trading economies annually. Port activities generate \$9.7 billion in gross domestic product and \$20.3 billion in economic output.[^cite_note-168] Vancouver is also the headquarters of [forest product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry) and mining companies. In recent years, Vancouver has become a centre for [software development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_development), [biotechnology](4502.md), [aerospace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerospace), [video game development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_development), [animation studios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_animation_studios) and television production and [film industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cinema_of_Canada).[^cite_note-Economy-169] Vancouver hosts approximately 65 movies and 55 TV series annually and is the third largest film and TV production centre in North America, supporting 20,000 jobs.[^cite_note-170] The city's strong focus on lifestyle and health culture also makes it a hub for many lifestyle brands with [Lululemon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lululemon_Athletica), [Arc'teryx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arc'teryx), [Kit and Ace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kit_and_Ace), [Mountain Equipment Co-op](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_Equipment_Co-op), [Herschel Supply Co.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Herschel_Supply_Co.), [Aritzia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aritzia), [Reigning Champ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reigning_Champ), and [Nature's Path Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nature's_Path) all founded and headquartered in Vancouver. Vancouver was also the birthplace of [1-800-GOT-JUNK?](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1-800-GOT-JUNK%3F) and [Western Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Canada)'s largest online-only publication, *[Daily Hive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Hive)*.[^cite_note-171]



The [Port of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_Vancouver) is the largest port in Canada and the third-largest port in the Americas (by tonnage).

&#x20;Conversely, since the onset of the global [COVID-19 pandemic](62750956.md) in 2020, multiple media organizations and economists have continued to warn of a severe long-term economic [doom loop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_collapse) impending for Vancouver, similar to the decline noted in [San Francisco, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco,_California).[^cite_note-VancouverDoomLoop-172]

Vancouver's scenic location makes it a major tourist destination. Over 10.3 million people visited Vancouver in 2017. Annually, tourism contributes approximately \$4.8 billion to the Metro Vancouver economy and supports over 70,000 jobs.[^cite_note-173] Many visit to see the city's gardens, [Stanley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Park), [Queen Elizabeth Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_Park,_British_Columbia), [VanDusen Botanical Garden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VanDusen_Botanical_Garden) and the mountains, ocean, forest and parklands which surround the city. Each year over a million people pass through Vancouver on [cruise ship](314855.md) vacations, often bound for [Alaska](624.md).[^cite_note-Economy-169]

Vancouver is the most stressed city in the spectrum of [affordability of housing in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Affordable_housing_in_Canada).[^cite_note-RBC_2012-174] In 2012, Vancouver was ranked by Demographia as the second-most unaffordable city in the world, rated as even more severely unaffordable in 2012 than in 2011.[^cite_note-RBC_2012-174][^cite_note-Demographia2012-175][^cite_note-176][^cite_note-177] The city has adopted various strategies to reduce housing costs, including [cooperative housing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Housing_cooperative), legalized [secondary suites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_suite), increased density and [smart growth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_growth). As of April 2010, the average two-level home in Vancouver sold for a record high of \$987,500, compared with the Canadian average of \$365,141.[^cite_note-178] A factor explaining the high property prices may be policies by the Canadian government which permit [snow washing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snow_washing), which allows foreigners to buy property in Canada while shielding their identities from tax authorities, making real estate transactions an effective way to conduct [money laundering](19390.md).[^cite_note-twsEconomist1-179]

Since the 1990s, the development of high-rise [condominiums](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Condominiums_in_Canada) in the downtown peninsula has been financed, in part, by an inflow of capital from Hong Kong immigrants due to the former colony's 1997 handover to China.[^cite_note-bbcasianfuture-180] Such development has clustered in the [Yaletown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaletown) and [Coal Harbour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coal_Harbour) districts and around many of the [SkyTrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTrain_\(Vancouver\)) stations to the east of the downtown.[^cite_note-Economy-169] The city's selection to co-host the [2010 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics) was also a major influence on economic development. Concern was expressed that Vancouver's increasing [homelessness](19394651.md) problem would be exacerbated by the Olympics because owners of single-room occupancy hotels, which house many of the city's lowest-income residents, converted their properties to attract higher-income residents and tourists.[^cite_note-181] Another significant international event held in Vancouver, the [1986 World Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_86), received over 20 million visitors and added \$3.7 billion to the Canadian economy.[^cite_note-Can_Encyc_-_Expo_86-182] Some still-standing Vancouver landmarks, including the [SkyTrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTrain_\(Vancouver\)) public transit system and [Canada Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Place), were built as part of the exposition.[^cite_note-Can_Encyc_-_Expo_86-182]

## Government

Main article: [Government and politics of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_and_politics_of_Vancouver)



Map of the 22 official neighbourhoods of Vancouver

Vancouver, unlike other British Columbia municipalities, is [incorporated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Local_government_in_Canada) under the *[Vancouver Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Charter)*.[^cite_note-183] The legislation, passed in 1953, supersedes the *Vancouver Incorporation Act, 1921* and grants the city more and different powers than other communities possess under British Columbia's *Municipalities Act*.

The civic government was dominated by the centre-right [Non-Partisan Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Partisan_Association) (NPA) since [World War II](32927.md), albeit with some significant centre-left interludes until 2008.[^cite_note-GVB-48] The NPA fractured over the issue of [drug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narcotic) policy in 2002, facilitating a landslide victory for the [Coalition of Progressive Electors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_Progressive_Electors) (COPE) on a [harm reduction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harm_reduction) platform. Subsequently, North America's only legal safe injection site at the time, [Insite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insite), was opened for the significant number of intravenous heroin users in the city.[^cite_note-184]

Vancouver is governed by the eleven-member [Vancouver City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_City_Council), a nine-member [School Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_School_Board), and a seven-member [Park Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Park_Board), all of whom serve four-year terms. Unusually for a city of Vancouver's size, all municipal elections are on an [at-large](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/At-large) basis. Historically, in all levels of government, the more affluent west side of Vancouver has voted along [conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservatism_in_Canada) or [liberal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberalism_in_Canada) lines. In contrast, the city's eastern side has voted along [left-wing](18499.md) lines.[^cite_note-185] This was reaffirmed with the results of the [2005 provincial election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_British_Columbia_general_election) and the [2006 federal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_federal_election_results_in_Greater_Vancouver_and_the_Sunshine_Coast).



Opened in 1936, [Vancouver City Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_City_Hall) is home to [Vancouver City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_City_Council).

Though polarized, a political [consensus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consensus_decision-making) has emerged in Vancouver around several issues. Protection of urban parks, a focus on the development of [rapid transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_transit) as opposed to a freeway system, a harm-reduction approach to illegal drug use, and a general concern about community-based development are examples of policies that have come to have broad support across the [political spectrum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_spectrum) in Vancouver.[^cite_note-186]

In the [2008 municipal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Vancouver_municipal_election) campaign, NPA incumbent mayor [Sam Sullivan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Sullivan) was ousted as mayoral candidate by the party in a close vote, which instated Peter Ladner as the new mayoral candidate for the NPA. [Gregor Robertson](6832515.md), a former MLA for [Vancouver-Fairview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver-Fairview) and head of [Happy Planet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Happy_Planet), was the mayoral candidate for Vision Vancouver, the other main contender. Vision Vancouver candidate Gregor Robertson defeated Ladner by a considerable margin, nearing 20,000 votes. The balance of power was significantly shifted to Vision Vancouver, which held seven of the ten spots for councillor. Of the remaining three, COPE received two and the NPA one. For park commissioner, four seats went to Vision Vancouver, one to the Green Party, one to COPE, and one to NPA. For school trustees, there were four Vision Vancouver seats, three COPE seats, and two NPA seats.[^cite_note-187] In the [2018 Vancouver municipal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Vancouver_municipal_election), independent [Kennedy Stewart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Stewart_\(Canadian_politician\)) was elected [mayor of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mayors_of_Vancouver).[^cite_note-188] Stewart was later defeated as mayor in the [2022 Vancouver municipal election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Vancouver_municipal_election) by [Ken Sim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ken_Sim), the runner-up in the 2018 election.[^cite_note-189]

Vancouver's budget consists of a capital and an operating component. In 2023, the operating budget was \$1.97 billion, with a 5-year financial plan, developed in 2022, that projected the budget would increase to \$2.46 billion by 2027. The 2023 capital budget was \$580 million, with a 2023 to 2026 Capital Plan that anticipates \$3.5 billion in expenditures in those four years.[^cite_note-190] Budget increases are primarily funded through increases in property taxes and community amenity contributions imposed in exchange for increases in allowable density as part of the construction permitting process. Utility and other user fees have also been increased but represent a small portion of Vancouver's overall budget.

### Regional government



Aerial view of [Greater Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Vancouver) from the west, 2018

Along with 20 other municipalities, one electoral area and one treaty First Nation,[^cite_note-191] Vancouver is a member municipality of [Metro Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Vancouver_Regional_District), the regional government whose seat is in [Burnaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnaby). While each member of Metro Vancouver has its own separate local governing body, Metro Vancouver oversees standard services and planning functions within the area, such as providing drinking water; operating sewage and solid waste handling; maintaining regional parks; managing air quality, [greenhouse gases](21350772.md) and ecological health; and providing a strategy for regional growth and land use.

### Policing and crime



Mounted officers of the [Vancouver Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Police_Department) in [Stanley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Park)

Vancouver operates the [Vancouver Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Police_Department), with 1,327 sworn members and an operating budget of \$316.5 million in 2018.[^cite_note-193][^cite_note-2018AnnualReport-194] Over 19 percent of the city's budget was spent on police protection in 2018 and by 2023 that has increased to 20.2 percent.[^cite_note-2018AnnualReport-194][^cite_note-195]

The Vancouver Police Department's operational divisions include a [bicycle squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_bicycle), a [marine squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Water_police), and a [dog squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_dog). It also has a [mounted squad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mounted_police), used primarily to patrol Stanley Park and for crowd control.[^cite_note-196] The police work in conjunction with civilian and volunteer-run Community Police Centres.[^cite_note-197] In 2006, the police department established its own [counterterrorism unit](83392.md). In 2005, a new transit police force, the Greater Vancouver Transportation Authority Police Service (now the [Metro Vancouver Transit Police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Vancouver_Transit_Police)), was established with full police powers.

Before the legalization of marijuana, Vancouver police generally did not arrest people for possessing small amounts of [marijuana](1481886.md).[^cite_note-198] In 2000, the Vancouver Police Department established a specialized drug squad, "Growbusters", to carry out an aggressive campaign against the city's estimated 4,000 [hydroponic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroponics) marijuana growing operations (or grow-ops) in residential areas.[^cite_note-199] As with other law enforcement campaigns targeting marijuana this initiative has been sharply criticized.[^cite_note-200]



For decades, the [Downtown Eastside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Eastside) area of Vancouver has faced a complex set of social issues, including disproportionately high levels of [drug use](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Substance_abuse), [homelessness](19394651.md), [poverty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty), [crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime), and [mental illness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_illness).

Since 1982, when Vancouver's homicide rate peaked at around 9.6 per 100,000 people with a total of 40 murders, the city's overall crime rate has declined, with a few notable exceptions, one being in 1991, when the city surpassed its homicide record with 41 being reported, giving the city a slightly lower homicide rate of 8.7 per 100,000 residents than its peak. However, in 2013, Vancouver reached a record low 6 murders, resulting in a homicide rate of 1 per 100,000 residents.[^cite_note-201][^cite_note-202] As of 2018, Vancouver had the ninth-highest [crime rate](705876.md), dropping five spots since 2005, among Canada's 35 census metropolitan areas.[^cite_note-STCD-203] As with other Canadian cities, the overall crime rate has been falling "dramatically".[^cite_note-204] The rate of firearm related violence dropped from 45.3 per 100,000 in 2006, the highest of any major metropolitan region in Canada at that time, to 16.2 in 2017.[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206] A series of gang-related incidents in early 2009 escalated into what police dubbed a [gang war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_Vancouver_gang_war).

Vancouver plays host to special events, such as the [Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asia-Pacific_Economic_Cooperation) conference, the Clinton-Yeltsin Summit, and the [Symphony of Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_of_Fire) fireworks show that require significant policing. The [1994 Stanley Cup riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Vancouver_Stanley_Cup_riot) overwhelmed police and injured as many as 200 people.[^cite_note-207] A [second riot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Vancouver_Stanley_Cup_riot) took place following the [2011 Stanley Cup Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Stanley_Cup_Finals).[^cite_note-208]

To reduce the public health risk from discarded hypodermic needles commonly found on downtown and the adjacent [Downtown Eastside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_Eastside) streets, the city runs a continuous collection effort, recovering approximately 1000 needles per day from public spaces.[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210] According to [Vancouver Coastal Health](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Coastal_Health), the regional health authority and a distributor of clean needles to intravenous drug users, there has never been a documented case of disease transmission from an accidental needlestick.[^cite_note-211]

### Military

[Jericho Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jericho_Beach) in Vancouver is the location of the headquarters of [39 Canadian Brigade Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/39_Canadian_Brigade_Group) of the [Canadian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Army).[^cite_note-212] Local primary reserve units include [The Seaforth Highlanders of Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Seaforth_Highlanders_of_Canada) and [The British Columbia Regiment (Duke of Connaught's Own)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_British_Columbia_Regiment_\(Duke_of_Connaught's_Own\)), based at the [Seaforth Armoury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaforth_Armoury) and the [Beatty Street Drill Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beatty_Street_Drill_Hall), respectively, and the [15th Field Regiment, Royal Canadian Artillery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_Field_Artillery_Regiment_\(Canada\)).[^cite_note-213] The Naval Reserve Unit [HMCS *Discovery*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMCS_Discovery) is based on [Deadman's Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deadman's_Island_\(Vancouver\)) in Stanley Park.[^cite_note-214] [RCAF Station Jericho Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RCAF_Station_Jericho_Beach), the first air base in Western Canada, was taken over by the [Canadian Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Army) in 1947 when [seaplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seaplane) were replaced by long-range aircraft. Most of the base facilities were transferred to the City of Vancouver in 1969, and the area was renamed "Jericho Park".[^cite_note-215]

## Education



Headquarters of the [Vancouver School Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_School_Board). The English-language [school district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district) serves Vancouver, [UBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia_Vancouver) and the [University Endowment Lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Endowment_Lands).



Main mall of the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) (UBC). UBC is one of five [public universities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_universities) located in Vancouver.

The [Vancouver School Board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_School_Board) enrols more than 110,000 students in its elementary, secondary, and [post-secondary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Higher_education) institutions, making it the second-largest [school district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district) in the province.[^cite_note-VSB1-216][^cite_note-VSB2-217] The district administers about 76 elementary schools, 17 elementary annexes, 18 secondary schools, 7 [adult education](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adult_education) centres, 2 Vancouver Learning Network schools,[^cite_note-218] which include 18 [French immersion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_immersion) schools, a [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Mandarin) bilingual school, and fine arts (Byng Arts Mini School), gifted, and [Montessori](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montessori_method) schools.[^cite_note-VSB1-216] The *[Conseil scolaire francophone de la Colombie-Britannique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conseil_scolaire_francophone_de_la_Colombie-Britannique)* operates three Francophone schools in that city: the primary schools *école Rose-des-vents* and *école Anne-Hébert* as well as the *[école secondaire Jules-Verne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89cole_secondaire_Jules-Verne)*.[^cite_note-219] More than 46 [independent schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_school#Canada) of a wide variety are also eligible for partial provincial funding and educate approximately 10 percent of pupils in the city.[^cite_note-220]

There are five public universities in the Greater Vancouver area, the largest and most prestigious being the [University of British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) (UBC) and [Simon Fraser University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simon_Fraser_University) (SFU), with a combined enrolment of more than 90,000 [undergraduates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Undergraduates), graduates, and professional students in 2008.[^cite_note-221][^cite_note-222] UBC often ranks among the top 40 best universities in the world and is ranked among the 3 best universities in Canada.[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-224][^cite_note-225] SFU consistently ranks as the top comprehensive university in Canada and is among the 350 best universities in the world.[^cite_note-226][^cite_note-227] UBC's [main campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Grey_Campus,_University_of_British_Columbia) is located on the tip of Burrard Peninsula, on the [Point Grey campus lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia_Vancouver) just west of the [University Endowment Lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Endowment_Lands) with the city-proper adjacent to the east. SFU's main campus is in [Burnaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnaby). Both also maintain campuses in Downtown Vancouver and the southeastern suburban city of [Surrey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surrey,_British_Columbia).

The other public universities in the metropolitan area around Vancouver are [Capilano University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capilano_University) in North Vancouver, [Emily Carr University of Art and Design](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Carr_University_of_Art_and_Design), and [Kwantlen Polytechnic University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwantlen_Polytechnic_University), whose four campuses are all outside the city proper. Six private institutions also operate in the region: [Trinity Western University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trinity_Western_University) in Langley, [UOPX Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Phoenix) in Burnaby, and [University Canada West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Canada_West), [NYIT Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Institute_of_Technology), [Fairleigh Dickinson University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairleigh_Dickinson_University), [Columbia College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia_College_\(British_Columbia\)), and [Sprott Shaw College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprott_Shaw_College), all in Vancouver.

[Vancouver Community College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Community_College) and [Langara College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langara_College) are publicly funded [college](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_\(Canada\))-level institutions in Vancouver, as is [Douglas College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_College) with three campuses outside the city. The [British Columbia Institute of Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Institute_of_Technology) in Burnaby provides [polytechnic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_of_technology#Canada) education. These are augmented by private and vocational institutions and other colleges in the surrounding areas of Metro Vancouver that provide [career](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_school#Canada), [trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vocational_school), [technical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Technical_school), and university-transfer programs. In contrast, the [Vancouver Film School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Film_School) and InFocus Film School provide one-year programs in film production, animation, and other entertainment arts.[^cite_note-228][^cite_note-229][^cite_note-230]

[International students](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_student) and [English as a second language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_as_a_second_or_foreign_language) (ESL) students have been significant in the enrolment of these public and private institutions. For the 2008–2009 school year, 53 percent of Vancouver School Board's students spoke a language other than English at home.[^cite_note-VSB2-217]

### Theatre, dance, film and television

#### Theatre

Prominent theatre companies in Vancouver include the [Arts Club Theatre Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arts_Club_Theatre_Company), which operates three venues, including one on [Granville Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granville_Island),[^cite_note-231] and [Bard on the Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bard_on_the_Beach), a large summer [Shakespeare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shakespeare) festival. Smaller professional companies include [Touchstone Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Touchstone_Theatre), the Cultch, Pacific Theatre, and [Firehall Arts Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Firehall_Arts_Centre). Non-professional theatre organizations include United Players and Metro Theatre, and [Theatre Under the Stars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_Under_the_Stars_\(Vancouver\)), which produces musicals in the summer at [Malkin Bowl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malkin_Bowl) in [Stanley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Park). Annual festivals held in Vancouver include the [PuSh International Performing Arts Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PuSh_International_Performing_Arts_Festival) in January and the [Vancouver Fringe Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Fringe_Festival) in September.

The Vancouver Playhouse Theatre Company operated for fifty years, ending in March 2012.[^cite_note-232]

#### Dance

Vancouver is home to [Ballet BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet_BC), a [ballet company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballet_company) whose principal venue is the [Queen Elizabeth Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_Theatre). Ballet BC was founded in 1986 and is British Columbia's only ballet company.[^cite_note-Strate-Forzle-2015-233]

The Scotiabank Dance Centre, a converted bank building on the corner of Davie and Granville, functions as a gathering place and performance venue for Vancouver-based dancers and choreographers. Dances for a Small Stage is a semi-annual dance festival.

#### Film

See also: [Category\:Films set in Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Films_set_in_Vancouver)

The [Vancouver International Film Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_International_Film_Festival), which runs for two weeks each September, shows over 350 films and is one of North America's most prominent film festivals. The [VIFF Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VIFF_Centre) venue runs independent non-commercial films throughout the rest of the year, as do [the Cinematheque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Cinematheque) and the [Rio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Theatre) theatres.

Vancouver has become a significant film location,[^cite_note-234] known as [Hollywood North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_North), as it has stood in for several U.S. cities. However, it has started to appear as itself in several feature films. Among [films set in the city and its surroundings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Films_set_in_Vancouver) are the 1994 US thriller *[Intersection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intersection_\(1994_film\))*, starring [Richard Gere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Gere) and [Sharon Stone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sharon_Stone); the 2007 Canadian ghost thriller *[They Wait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/They_Wait)*, starring [Terry Chen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terry_Chen) and [Jaime King](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jaime_King); and the acclaimed Canadian 'mockumentary' *[Hard Core Logo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hard_Core_Logo)*, and was named the second-best Canadian film of the last 15 years, in a 2001 poll of 200 industry voters, performed by Playback. [Genie Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genie_Awards)-winning filmmaker [Mina Shum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mina_Shum) has filmed and set several of her internationally released features in Vancouver, including the [Sundance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sundance_Film_Festival)-screened *[Long Life, Happiness & Prosperity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Life,_Happiness_&_Prosperity)* (2002).

### Libraries and museums

See also: [List of museums in British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_museums_in_British_Columbia)



[Science World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_World_\(Vancouver\)) is an interactive [science centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_centre). The building was originally constructed for [Expo 86](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_86).

Libraries in Vancouver include the [Vancouver Public Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Public_Library), with its main branch at Library Square, designed by [Moshe Safdie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Safdie). The central branch contains 1.5 million volumes. Altogether, twenty-two branches contain 2.25 million volumes.[^cite_note-238] The [Vancouver Tool Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Tool_Library) is Canada's original tool lending library.

The [Vancouver Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Art_Gallery) has a permanent collection of nearly 10,000 items and is the home of a significant number of works by [Emily Carr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emily_Carr).[^cite_note-239] However, little or none of the permanent collection is ever on view. Downtown is also home to the [Contemporary Art Gallery (Vancouver)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contemporary_Art_Gallery_\(Vancouver\)), which showcases temporary exhibitions by up-and-coming Vancouver artists. The [Morris and Helen Belkin Art Gallery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morris_and_Helen_Belkin_Art_Gallery), with a small collection of contemporary works, is part of the University of British Columbia.

In the [Kitsilano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsilano) district are the [Vancouver Maritime Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Maritime_Museum), the [H. R. MacMillan Space Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/H._R._MacMillan_Space_Centre), and the [Vancouver Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Vancouver), the largest civic museum in Canada. The [Museum of Anthropology at UBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Anthropology_at_UBC) is a leading museum of [Pacific Northwest Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Northwest) [First Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Nations_in_Canada) culture. A more interactive museum is [Science World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Science_World_\(Vancouver\)) at the head of [False Creek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/False_Creek). The city also features a diverse collection of Public Art.

### Music and nightlife

See also: [Music of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Vancouver)

Musical contributions from Vancouver include performers of classical, folk and popular music. The [Vancouver Symphony Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Symphony_Orchestra) is the professional orchestra based in the city. The [Vancouver Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Opera) is a major opera company in the city, and [City Opera of Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Opera_of_Vancouver) is the city's professional chamber opera company. The city is home to several [Canadian composers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_composers), including [Rodney Sharman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rodney_Sharman), [Jeffrey Ryan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeffrey_Ryan), and [Jocelyn Morlock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jocelyn_Morlock).



The [Granville Entertainment District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granville_Entertainment_District), downtown, can attract large crowds to the street's many bars and nightclubs.

The city produced a number of notable [punk rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punk_rock) bands, including [D.O.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D.O.A._\(band\)) Other early Vancouver punk bands included the [Subhumans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subhumans_\(Canadian_band\)), the [Young Canadians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Young_Canadians), the [Pointed Sticks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pointed_Sticks), and [U-J3RK5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U-J3RK5).[^cite_note-243] When [alternative rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alternative_rock) became popular in the 1990s, several Vancouver groups rose to prominence, including [54-40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/54-40_\(band\)), [Odds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odds_\(band\)), [Moist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moist_\(Canadian_band\)), the [Matthew Good Band](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Good_Band), [Sons of Freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sons_of_Freedom_\(band\)) and [Econoline Crush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Econoline_Crush). Recent successful Vancouver bands include [Gob](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gob_\(band\)), [Marianas Trench](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marianas_Trench_\(band\)), [Theory of a Deadman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theory_of_a_Deadman) and [Stabilo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stabilo_\(band\)). Today, Vancouver is home to several popular independent bands such as [The New Pornographers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_New_Pornographers), [Japandroids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japandroids), [Destroyer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destroyer_\(band\)), [In Medias Res](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/In_Medias_Res_\(band\)), [Tegan and Sara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tegan_and_Sara), and independent labels including [Nettwerk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nettwerk) and [Mint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mint_Records). Vancouver also produced influential metal band [Strapping Young Lad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strapping_Young_Lad) and pioneering [electro-industrial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electro-industrial) bands [Skinny Puppy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Skinny_Puppy), [Numb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numb_\(band\)) and [Front Line Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Front_Line_Assembly); the latter's [Bill Leeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Leeb) is better known for founding ambient pop super-group [Delerium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delerium). Other popular musical artists who made their mark from Vancouver include [Carly Rae Jepsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carly_Rae_Jepsen), [Bryan Adams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Adams), [Sarah McLachlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sarah_McLachlan), [Heart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heart_\(band\)), [Prism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prism_\(band\)), [Trooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trooper_\(band\)), [Chilliwack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilliwack_\(band\)), [Payolas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payolas), [Moev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moev), [Images in Vogue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Images_in_Vogue), [Michael Bublé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Bubl%C3%A9), [Stef Lang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DYLN) and [Spirit of the West](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_of_the_West).[^cite_note-244]

More significant musical performances are usually held at venues such as [Rogers Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Arena), [Queen Elizabeth Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_Elizabeth_Theatre), [BC Place Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Place) or the [Pacific Coliseum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Coliseum). In contrast, more minor acts are held at places such as the [Commodore Ballroom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commodore_Ballroom), the [Orpheum Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orpheum_\(Vancouver\)) and the [Vogue Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vogue_Theatre_\(Vancouver\)). The [Vancouver Folk Music Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Folk_Music_Festival) and the [Vancouver International Jazz Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_International_Jazz_Festival) showcase music from around the world in their respective genres. Vancouver's Hong Kong Chinese population has produced several [Cantopop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantopop) stars across the Hong Kong entertainment industry. Similarly, various Indo-Canadian artists and actors have a profile in [Bollywood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bollywood) or other aspects of [India](14533.md)'s entertainment industry.

Vancouver has a vibrant nightlife scene, whether food and dining or bars and nightclubs. The [Granville Entertainment District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granville_Entertainment_District) has the city's highest concentration of bars and nightclubs with closing times of 3 am, in addition to various after-hours clubs open until late morning on weekends. The street can attract large crowds on weekends and is closed to traffic on such nights. [Gastown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastown) is also a popular area for nightlife with many upscale restaurants and nightclubs, as well as the [Davie Village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Davie_Village), which is the centre of the city's [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT) community.

## Media

Main article: [Media in Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_in_Vancouver)



[Granville Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granville_Square) (centre building) houses the two major daily newspapers of the city, *[The Vancouver Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vancouver_Sun)* and *[The Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Province)*.

Vancouver is a centre for film and television production. Nicknamed [Hollywood North](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hollywood_North), a distinction it shares with Toronto,[^cite_note-Gasher2002-245][^cite_note-McPhail2010-246][^cite_note-Lavery2010-247] the city has been used as a film making location for nearly a century, beginning with the [Edison Manufacturing Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edison_Studios).[^cite_note-248] In 2021, \$3.6 billion was spent on film production in Vancouver. This ranks Vancouver as the largest production hub in Canada and the 3rd largest in North America, behind [Los Angeles](18110.md) and [New York City](645042.md).[^cite_note-249]

A wide mix of local, national, and international newspapers are distributed in the city. The two major [English-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) daily newspapers are the *[Vancouver Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Sun)* and *[The Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Province)*. Also, there are two national newspapers distributed in the city, including *[The Globe and Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Globe_and_Mail)*, which began publication of a "national edition" in BC in 1983 and recently expanded to include a three-page BC news section, and the *[National Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Post)*, which centres on national news. Other local newspapers have included *[24H](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/24_Hours_\(newspaper\))* (a local free daily), the Vancouver franchise of the national free daily *[Metro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_International)*, the twice-a-week *[Vancouver Courier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Courier)*, and the independent newspaper *[The Georgia Straight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Georgia_Straight)*. Three Chinese-language daily newspapers – *[Ming Pao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_Pao)*, *[Sing Tao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sing_Tao_Daily_\(Canada\))* and *[World Journal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Journal)* – cater to the city's large Cantonese- and Mandarin-speaking population. Several other local and international papers serve other multicultural groups in the Lower Mainland.



[750 Burrard Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/750_Burrard_Street) houses [Bell Media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Media)'s West Coast headquarters and the regional offices for *[The Globe and Mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Globe_and_Mail)*.

Some of the local television stations include [CBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBUT-DT), [Citytv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CKVU-DT), [CTV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIVT-DT) and [Global BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHAN-DT). [OMNI British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CHNM-DT) produces daily newscasts in [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese), [Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_Chinese), [Punjabi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punjabi_language) and [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_language), and weekly newscasts in [Tagalog](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tagalog_language), as well as programs aimed at other cultural groups. [Fairchild Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_Group) also has two television stations: [Fairchild TV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fairchild_TV) and [Talentvision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talentvision), serving Cantonese- and Mandarin-speaking audiences, respectively.

Radio stations with news departments include [CBC Radio One](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBU_\(AM\)), [CKNW](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CKNW) and [News 1130](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CKWX). The [Franco-Columbian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Columbian) community is served by [Radio-Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Broadcasting_Corporation) outlets [CBUFT-DT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBUFT-DT) channel 26 ([Ici Radio-Canada Télé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ici_Radio-Canada_T%C3%A9l%C3%A9)), [CBUF-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBUF-FM) 97.7 ([Première Chaîne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ici_Radio-Canada_Premi%C3%A8re)) and [CBUX-FM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CBUX-FM) 90.9 ([Espace musique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ici_Musique)). The multilingual South Asian community is served by Spice Radio on 1200 AM, which was established in 2014.[^cite_note-250]

Media dominance is a frequently discussed issue in Vancouver as newspapers the *Vancouver Sun*, *The Province*, the *Vancouver Courier* and other local newspapers such as the *Surrey Now*, the *Burnaby Now* and the *Richmond News*, are all owned by [Postmedia Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Postmedia_Network).[^cite_note-251] The [concentration of media ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_of_media_ownership) has spurred alternatives, making Vancouver a centre for independent online media including *[The Tyee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Tyee)*, *[The Vancouver Observer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Vancouver_Observer)*, and [NowPublic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NowPublic),[^cite_note-252] as well as [hyperlocal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyperlocal) online media, like *[Daily Hive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_Hive)* and *Vancouver Is Awesome*,[^cite_note-253] which provide coverage of community events and local arts and culture.

## Transportation

Main article: [Transportation in Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_Vancouver)

See also: [List of roads in Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_roads_in_Vancouver)



A [SeaBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaBus) crosses [Burrard Inlet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burrard_Inlet) between Vancouver and the neighbouring [city of North Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vancouver_\(city\)).

Vancouver's [streetcar](30733.md) system began on June 28, 1890, and ran from the (first) [Granville Street Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granville_Street_Bridge) to Westminster Avenue (now [Main Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Main_Street_\(Vancouver\)) and [Kingsway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingsway_\(Vancouver\))). Less than a year later, the Westminster and Vancouver Tramway Company began operating Canada's first interurban line between the two cities (extended to [Chilliwack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chilliwack) in 1910). Another line (1902), the Vancouver and Lulu Island Railway, was leased by the Canadian Pacific Railway to the British Columbia Electric Railway in 1905 and ran from the Granville Street Bridge to [Steveston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steveston,_British_Columbia) via [Kerrisdale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerrisdale), which encouraged residential neighbourhoods outside the central core to develop.[^cite_note-254] After 1897, the [British Columbia Electric Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Electric_Railway) (BCER) became the operator of the urban and [interurban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interurban) rail systems until 1958, when its remaining lines were dismantled in favour of trackless [electric trolleys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) and gasoline or diesel [buses](4146.md).[^cite_note-255] BCER later became the core of the newly created, publicly owned [BC Hydro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Hydro), established in 1962.[^cite_note-256][^cite_note-257][^cite_note-258] Vancouver currently has the second-largest [trolleybus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trolleybus) fleet in North America, after [San Francisco](49728.md).[^cite_note-259]



Off- and on-ramps leading to [British Columbia Highway 1](633679.md) in Vancouver. Highway 1 is the only [controlled-access highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Controlled-access_highway) within the city limits.

Successive city councils in the 1970s and 1980s prohibited the construction of freeways as part of a long-term plan.[^cite_note-260] As a result, the only major freeway within city limits is [Highway 1](633679.md), which passes through the north-eastern corner of the city. While the number of cars in Vancouver proper has been steadily rising with population growth, the rate of car ownership and the average distance driven by daily commuters have fallen since the early 1990s.[^cite_note-VanMag-261][^cite_note-262] Vancouver is the only major Canadian city with these trends. Even though the journey time per vehicle has increased by one-third and growing traffic mass, there are 7 percent fewer cars making trips into the downtown core.[^cite_note-VanMag-261] In 2012, Vancouver had the worst traffic congestion in Canada and the second-highest in [North America](21139.md), behind [Los Angeles](18110.md).[^cite_note-263] As of 2013, Vancouver had the worst traffic congestion in North America.[^cite_note-264] Residents have been more inclined to live in areas closer to their interests, or use more energy-efficient means of travel, such as mass transit and cycling. This is, in part, the result of a push by city planners for a solution to traffic problems and pro-environment campaigns. [Transportation demand management](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_demand_management) policies have imposed restrictions on drivers, making commuting more difficult and expensive while introducing more benefits for non-drivers.[^cite_note-VanMag-261]

A two car train follows rail tracks under and bridge. In the background can be seen a domed sports stadium and high-rise buildings.

Vancouver's [SkyTrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTrain_\(Vancouver\)) in the Grandview Cut, with downtown Vancouver in the background. The white dome-like structure is the old roof of [BC Place Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Place_Stadium).

[TransLink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TransLink_\(British_Columbia\)) is responsible for roads and public transportation within [Metro Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Vancouver_Regional_District) (in succession to [BC Transit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Transit), which had taken over the transit functions of BC Hydro). It provides bus service, including the [RapidBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RapidBus_\(TransLink\)) express service, a foot passenger and bicycle ferry service (known as [SeaBus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaBus)), an automated rapid transit service called [SkyTrain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SkyTrain_\(Vancouver\)), and [West Coast Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_Express) commuter rail. Vancouver's SkyTrain system is currently running on three lines, the [Millennium Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennium_Line), the [Expo Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_Line_\(SkyTrain\)) and the [Canada Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Line)[^cite_note-Translink10-265] with a total of 53 stations as of 2017. Only 20 stations are within the City of Vancouver borders, with the remainder in the adjacent suburbs. A number of the city's biggest tourist attractions – such as English Bay, Stanley Park, the Vancouver Aquarium, the Museum of Anthropology, and the Kitsilano neighbourhood – are not connected by this rapid transit system.

Changes are being made to the regional transportation network as part of Translink's 10-Year Transportation Plan. The [Canada Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canada_Line), opened on August 17, 2009, connects [Vancouver International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_International_Airport) and the neighbouring city of [Richmond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richmond,_British_Columbia) with the existing SkyTrain system. The [Evergreen Extension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evergreen_Extension), which opened on December 2, 2016, links the cities of [Coquitlam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coquitlam) and [Port Moody](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Moody) with the SkyTrain system.[^cite_note-bcmot_rfp-266] As of January 2019, plans to extend the SkyTrain Millennium Line west to [UBC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia) as a subway under [Broadway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_\(Vancouver\)) have been approved and there are plans for capacity upgrades and an extension to the [Expo Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expo_Line_\(SkyTrain\)).[^cite_note-267] Several road projects will be completed within the next few years, as part of the Provincial Government's [Gateway Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gateway_Program_\(Vancouver\)).[^cite_note-Translink10-265]

Other modes of transport add to the diversity of options available in Vancouver. Inter-city passenger rail service is operated from [Pacific Central Station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Central_Station) by [Via Rail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Via_Rail) to points east, [Amtrak Cascades](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_Cascades) to [Seattle](11388236.md) and [Portland](23503.md), and [Rocky Mountaineer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocky_Mountaineer) rail tour routes. Small passenger ferries in False Creek provide commuter service to Granville Island, Downtown Vancouver and Kitsilano. Vancouver has a citywide network of bicycle lanes and routes, supporting an active cyclist population year-round. Cycling has become Vancouver's fastest-growing mode of transportation.[^cite_note-268] The [bicycle-sharing system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bicycle-sharing_system) [Mobi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobi_\(bike_share\)) was introduced to the city in June 2016.[^cite_note-269]

Vancouver is served by [Vancouver International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_International_Airport) (YVR), located on [Sea Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Island_\(British_Columbia\)) in the city of Richmond, immediately south of Vancouver. Vancouver's airport is Canada's second-busiest airport,[^cite_note-270] and the second-largest gateway on the west coast of North America for international passengers.[^cite_note-271] [HeliJet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helijet) and [float plane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floatplane) companies operate scheduled air service from Vancouver harbour and YVR south terminal. Two [BC Ferry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Ferries) terminals also serve the city. One is to the northwest at [Horseshoe Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe_Bay_ferry_terminal) in [West Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Vancouver), and the other is to the south at [Tsawwassen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsawwassen_ferry_terminal) in [Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta,_British_Columbia).[^cite_note-272]

## Sports and recreation

Main article: [Sports in Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_Vancouver)



[Third Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Beach) is one of many beaches located in Vancouver. Given the city's proximity to the ocean and mountains, the area is a popular destination for outdoor recreation.

The city's mild climate and proximity to the ocean, mountains, rivers and lakes make the area a popular destination for outdoor recreation. Vancouver has over 1,298 ha (3,210 acres) of parks, of which [Stanley Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_Park), at 404 ha (1,000 acres), is the largest.[^cite_note-273] The city has several large beaches, many adjacent to one another, extending from the shoreline of Stanley Park around False Creek to the south side of English Bay, from Kitsilano to the [University Endowment Lands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Endowment_Lands), (which also has beaches that are not part of the city proper). The 18 km (11 mi) of beaches include Second and Third Beaches in Stanley Park, English Bay (First Beach), Sunset, [Kitsilano Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitsilano_Beach), Jericho, Locarno, [Spanish Banks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Banks), Spanish Banks Extension, Spanish Banks West, and [Wreck Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wreck_Beach). There is also a freshwater beach at Trout Lake in [John Hendry Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Hendry_Park). The coastline provides for many types of water sports, and the city is a popular destination for boating enthusiasts.[^cite_note-274]

Within a 20- to 30-minute drive from downtown Vancouver are the [North Shore Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Shore_Mountains), with three ski areas: [Cypress Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cypress_Mountain_Ski_Area), [Grouse Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouse_Mountain), and [Mount Seymour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Seymour). [Mountain bikers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_biking) have created world-renowned trails across the North Shore. The [Capilano River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capilano_River), Lynn Creek and Seymour River, also on the North Shore, provide opportunities to [whitewater](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Whitewater) enthusiasts during periods of rain and spring melt. However, the canyons of those rivers are more utilized for hiking and swimming than whitewater.[^cite_note-275]

Running races include the [Vancouver Sun Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Sun_Run) (a 10 km (6.2 mi) race) every April; the [Vancouver Marathon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Marathon), held every May; and the Scotiabank Vancouver Half-Marathon held every June. The [Grouse Grind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grouse_Mountain) is a 2.9 km (1.8 mi) climb up Grouse Mountain, open throughout the summer and fall months, including the annual Grouse Grind Mountain Run. Hiking trails include the [Baden-Powell Trail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baden-Powell_Trail), an arduous 42 km-long (26 mi) hike from [West Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Vancouver)'s [Horseshoe Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horseshoe_Bay,_West_Vancouver) to [Deep Cove](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deep_Cove,_North_Vancouver) in the [District of North Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Vancouver_\(district_municipality\)).[^cite_note-276]



[BC Place](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Place) is a multi-purpose stadium that is home to the [BC Lions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Lions) of the [CFL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League) and the [Vancouver Whitecaps FC](22060729.md) of [MLS](71802.md).

Vancouver is also home to notable [cycling races](857311.md). During most summers since 1973, the [Global Relay Gastown Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastown_Grand_Prix) has been held on the cobblestone streets of [Gastown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gastown). This race and the UBC Grand Prix are part of BC Superweek, an annual series of professional cycling races in Metro Vancouver.

The [British Columbia Derby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Columbia_Derby) is a nine-furlong horse race held at the Hastings Racecourse in the third week of September.[^cite_note-277]

In 2009, Metro Vancouver hosted the [World Police and Fire Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Police_and_Fire_Games). [Swangard Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swangard_Stadium), in the neighbouring city of [Burnaby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burnaby), hosted games for the [2007 FIFA U-20 World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_FIFA_U-20_World_Cup).[^cite_note-V2010-17][^cite_note-278]

Vancouver, along with Whistler and Richmond, was the host city for the [2010 Winter Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Olympics) and the [2010 Winter Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Winter_Paralympics). On June 12, 2010, it played host to [Ultimate Fighting Championship 115](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UFC_115) (UFC 115), which was the fourth [UFC](169660.md) event to be held in Canada (and the first outside [Montreal](7954681.md)).

In 2011, Vancouver hosted the [Grey Cup](230107.md), the [Canadian Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Football_League) (CFL) championship game, which is awarded every year to a different city that has a CFL team. The [BC Titans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Titans) of the [International Basketball League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Basketball_League) played their inaugural season in 2009, with home games at the [Langley Event Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langley_Events_Centre).[^cite_note-279] Vancouver is a centre for the fast-growing sport of [ultimate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultimate_\(sport\)). During the summer of 2008 Vancouver hosted the World Ultimate Championships.[^cite_note-280]

The [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks) are an [ice hockey](14790.md) team in the [National Hockey League](21809.md) and have played there since 1970.

The [National Basketball Association](22093.md) (NBA) expanded into Vancouver in 1995 with the establishment of the [Vancouver Grizzlies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Grizzlies). They played their games at GM Place (now [Rogers Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Arena)). After six years in Vancouver, the team relocated to [Memphis, Tennessee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Memphis,_Tennessee) in 2001.



The [Vancouver Canucks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver_Canucks) are an [NHL](21809.md) team who play their home games in [Rogers Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogers_Arena).

In 2015, Vancouver was one of six host cities for the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup) and hosted the [Final game](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women's_World_Cup_Final) between the United States and Japan. Eleven years later, Vancouver was one of two Canadian cities that hosted matches during the [2026 FIFA World Cup](36581929.md).[^cite_note-281]

Vancouver is Canada's fittest major city, with an obesity rate of only 17.4%, compared to the national average of 24.8%. It is only surpassed by [Kelowna, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelowna) with a rate of 17% and followed by [Victoria, British Columbia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victoria,_British_Columbia) at 19.6%.[^cite_note-282] Overall, the province of British Columbia has the [lowest obesity rate in Canada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Obesity_in_Canada), followed by Quebec at 2nd and Ontario at 3rd.

## Sustainability



Container recycling, paper recycling and garbage bins in Vancouver

The City of Vancouver is a member of [Metro Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metro_Vancouver), which provides sustainable regional services[^cite_note-283] to the [Greater Vancouver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Vancouver) area. The city electrical grid is serviced by [BC Hydro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BC_Hydro), which claims 97.8% of the energy it generates is clean owing to the extensive use of [hydroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric) power generation.[^cite_note-284] The City of Vancouver is the greenest city in Canada according to an independent ongoing urban ecological footprint study.[^cite_note-285]

The [Greenest City action plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vancouver's_greenest_city_action_plan) (GCAP) is a City of Vancouver urban sustainability initiative. Its primary mission was to ensure Vancouver would become the greenest city in the world by 2020. The GCAP originated based on the 2009 work of the Greenest City Action Team, a committee co-chaired by Vancouver mayor [Gregor Robertson](6832515.md).[^cite_note-286] The GCAP was approved by Vancouver city council in July 2011.[^cite_note-287]

In May 2018, the [Zero Waste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zero_Waste) 2040 Strategy was passed[^cite_note-288] The city began work the same year on decreasing the amount of single-use items distributed in the city. It intended to ban these items by 2021 if businesses do not meet reduction targets. As part of the plan, a ban on [plastic straws](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drinking_straw), [polystyrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polystyrene) food packaging and free shopping bags was to go into effect in mid-2019.[^cite_note-289]

In January 2022, the city council passed a regulation mandating that businesses charge a \$0.25 fee on single-use cups. This decision was criticized because the fees stayed within the business and were not re-invested in city-wide environmental efforts. On March 28, 2023, the council enacted a by-law that repealed all single-use cup fees.[^cite_note-290]
