The **White House Correspondents' Association** (**WHCA**) is an organization of journalists who cover the [White House](33057.md) and the [president of the United States](24113.md). The WHCA was founded on February 25, 1914, by journalists in response to an unfounded rumor that a [United States congressional committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_congressional_committee) would select which journalists could attend press conferences of President [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson).[^cite_note-WHCA_History-4]

The WHCA operates independently of the White House. Application for membership is made online and granted by the association on the basis of criteria. Historically, notable issues handled by the WHCA were the credentialing process, access to the president and physical conditions in the White House press briefing rooms.[^cite_note-officers-2][^cite_note-Compton-5] Its most high-profile activity is the annual [White House Correspondents' Dinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Correspondents'_Dinner), which is traditionally attended by the president and covered by the news media.

In February 2025, the White House announced that the WHCA would no longer determine which outlets are allowed access to the president.[^cite_note-6]

## White House press room

The White House Correspondents' Association is responsible for assigning the seats and the standing room in the [James S. Brady Press Briefing Room](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_S._Brady_Press_Briefing_Room) in the [White House](33057.md). Even though the press office issues the passes for each session of press briefing, the correspondents' association decides who sits where. The board of directors assigns news sources to certain positions, which change about each 3–4 years.

Most media providers have their own seat, but some share seats with other providers.

The association usually publishes the seating chart on its website every time a new one is decided and comes into effect.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

## White House Correspondents' Dinner

Main article: [White House Correspondents' Dinner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_House_Correspondents'_Dinner)

The association's annual dinner, begun in 1921, has become a [Washington, D.C.](108956.md), tradition, and is traditionally attended by the [president](24113.md) and [vice president of the United States](32759.md). The dinner is traditionally held on the evening of the last Saturday in April at the [Washington Hilton](11732002.md).

### The Aldo Beckman Memorial Award

The Aldo Beckman Award for Journalistic Excellence is an annual award sponsored by the WHCA. Established in 1981, it recognizes "a correspondent who personifies the journalistic excellence and personal qualities of [Aldo Beckman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldo_Beckman)"[^cite_note-16] for overall excellence in White House coverage.[^cite_note-awards-17] Described by [CBS News](359551.md) as "one of journalism's most prestigious awards", past recipients include [David E. Sanger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_E._Sanger), [Mark Knoller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Knoller), [Glenn Thrush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Glenn_Thrush), [Kenneth T. Walsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenneth_T._Walsh), and [Carl M. Cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_M._Cannon).[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

### Discontinued awards

#### The Edgar A. Poe Memorial Award

Main article: [Edgar A. Poe Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_A._Poe_Award)

Named in honor of the distinguished correspondent [Edgar Allen Poe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edgar_Allen_Poe_\(journalist\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) (1906–1998),[^cite_note-Bustle-90] a former WHCA president unrelated to the American fiction writer of the nearly identical name.[^cite_note-91] Funded by the *[New Orleans Times-Picayune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Orleans_Times-Picayune)* and [Newhouse Newspapers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newhouse_Newspapers),[^cite_note-Bustle-90] the award honored excellence in news coverage of subjects and events of significant national or regional importance to the American people.[^cite_note-92] The Edgar A. Poe Memorial Award was presented from 1990 to 2019, when it was replaced by the **Katharine Graham Award for Courage and Accountability** and the **Award for Excellence in Presidential News Coverage by Visual Journalists**.

Notable past winners of the award include [Rochelle Sharpe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rochelle_Sharpe), [Marjie Lundstrom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marjie_Lundstrom), [Michael Tackett](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Tackett), [Russell Carollo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russell_Carollo), [Cheryl Reed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheryl_Reed), [Michael Isikoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Isikoff), [Sam Roe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sam_Roe), [Sean Naylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sean_Naylor), [Lance Williams and Mark Fainaru-Wada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lance_Williams_and_Mark_Fainaru-Wada), [Marcus Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Stern_\(journalist\)), [Megan Twohey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Megan_Twohey), [David Fahrenthold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Fahrenthold), and [Norah O'Donnell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norah_O'Donnell).

#### Raymond Clapper Memorial Award

Main article: [Raymond Clapper Memorial Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Clapper_Memorial_Award)

Named in honor of [Raymond Clapper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Raymond_Clapper) (1892-1944) and given "to a journalist or team for distinguished Washington reporting."[^cite_note-JournoInstitue-93] The award was presented from 1944 to 2003, usually at the WHCA dinner[^cite_note-94] (although in the period 1951–1965 it was presented at the [American Society of News Editors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Society_of_News_Editors) annual dinner).[^cite_note-NYT-Fleeson-95][^cite_note-NYT-Landauer-96]

In 2004, the award passed to the Scripps Howard [National Journalism Awards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Journalism_Awards).[^cite_note-JournoInstitue-93] Under Scripps Howard, the [Washington Reporting Raymond Clapper Award](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Howard_Awards#Washington_Reporting_Raymond_Clapper_Award) was presented until 2011, at which point it was discontinued.[^cite_note-97]

Notable past winners of the Raymond Clapper Award included [Ernie Pyle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernie_Pyle), [Nicholas Lemann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Lemann), [Clark R. Mollenhoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clark_R._Mollenhoff), [James Reston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Reston), [Joseph Albright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Albright_\(journalist\)), [Morton Mintz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morton_Mintz), [Adam Liptak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adam_Liptak), [Helene Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helene_Cooper), [Jean Heller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean_Heller), [Newbold Noyes Jr.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newbold_Noyes_Jr.), [Thomas Lunsford Stokes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Lunsford_Stokes), [Tom Squitieri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Squitieri), [Marcus Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcus_Stern_\(journalist\)), [Susan Feeney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Susan_Feeney), [Doris Fleeson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doris_Fleeson), [James Polk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Polk_\(journalist\)), [James V. Risser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_V._Risser), and [William Neikirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Neikirk).
