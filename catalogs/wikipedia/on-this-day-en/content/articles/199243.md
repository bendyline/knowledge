The **Republic of Karelia**,[^cite_note-9] or simply **Karelia** or **Karjala**[^cite_note-12] is a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_Russia) of [Russia](25391.md) situated in the [northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwest_Russia) of the country.[^cite_note-britannica-11] The republic is a part of the [Northwestern Federal District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Federal_District), and covers an area of 172,400 square kilometres (66,600 square miles), with a population of 533,121 residents.[^cite_note-2021census-5] Its [capital](181337.md) is [Petrozavodsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrozavodsk).&#x20;

The modern Karelian Republic was founded as an autonomous republic within the [Russian SFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic), by the Resolution of the Presidium of the [All-Russian Central Executive Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All-Russian_Central_Executive_Committee) (VTsIK) on 27 June 1923 and by the Decree of the VTsIK and the Council of People's Commissars of 25 July 1923, from the [Karelian Labour Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Labor_Commune). From 1940 to 1956, it was known as the [Karelo-Finnish Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelo-Finnish_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), one of the [republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union) of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). In 1956, it was once again made an autonomous republic and remained part of Russia following the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union) in 1991.&#x20;

## Etymology

"Karelia" derives from the name of the ethnic group — [Karelians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelians). The name "Karjala" has unknown origins, but it is theorised that it may come from the Proto-Finnic word *karja*, meaning "herd", which was borrowed from the [Proto-Germanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proto-Germanic_language) *harjaz* ("army"); the ending *-la* means "earth".[^cite_note-13]&#x20;

## Geography

The Republic of Karelia is in the northwestern part of Russia, between [Lake Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ladoga) and the [White Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea), which has a shoreline of 630 kilometers (390 mi). The republic has an area of 172,400 km<sup>2</sup> (66,600 sq mi). It shares internal borders with [Murmansk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk_Oblast) (north), [Arkhangelsk Oblast](472779.md) (east/south-east), [Vologda Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vologda_Oblast) (south-east/south), and [Leningrad Oblast](480845.md) (south/south-west), and it also borders [Finland](10577.md) ([Kainuu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kainuu), [Lapland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lapland_\(Finland\)), [North Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Karelia), [Northern Ostrobothnia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ostrobothnia), and [South Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Karelia)); the borders measure 723 km. The main bodies of water next to Karelia are the White Sea (an inlet of the [Barents Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barents_Sea)) to the north-east and [Lake Onega](374540.md) and Lake Ladoga both shared with neighboring Oblasts to the south. Its highest point is the [Nuorunen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuorunen) peak at 576 m (1,890 ft).&#x20;

As a part of the [Fennoscandian Shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Shield)'s ancient Karelian [craton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craton), most of the Republic of Karelia's surficial geology is [Archaean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archean) or [Paleoproterozoic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleoproterozoic), dated up to 3.4 billion years in the Vodlozero block. This area is the largest contiguous Archaean outcrop in [Europe](9239.md) and one of the largest in the world.&#x20;

Since [deglaciation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deglaciation), the rate of [post-glacial rebound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post-glacial_rebound) in the Republic of Karelia has varied. Since the [White Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea) connected to the World's oceans uplift along the southern coast of [Kandalaksha Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kandalaksha_Gulf) has totaled 90 m. In the interval 9,500–5,000 years ago the uplift rate was 9–13 mm/[yr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Year). Before the [Atlantic period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_\(period\)), uplift rate had decreased to 5–5.5 mm/yr, to then rise briefly before arriving at the present uplift rate is 4 mm/yr.[^cite_note-14]&#x20;

### Natural resources

The majority of the republic's territory (148,000 km<sup>2</sup> (57,000 sq mi), or 85%) is composed of state forest stock. The total growing stock of timber resources in the forests of all categories and ages is 807 million m³. The mature and over-mature tree stock amounts to 411.8 million m³, of which 375.2 million m³ is coniferous.&#x20;

Fifty useful minerals are found in Karelia, located in more than 400 deposits and ore-bearing layers. Natural resources of the republic include [iron ore](145352.md), [diamonds](8082.md), [vanadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vanadium), [molybdenum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Molybdenum), and others.&#x20;

### Climate

The Republic of Karelia is located in the Atlantic [continental climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continental_climate) zone. The average temperature in January is −8.0 °C (17.6 °F) and +16.4 °C (61.5 °F) in July. Average annual [precipitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precipitation_\(meteorology\)) is 500–700 mm.[^cite_note-allregions-17]&#x20;

## International cooperation

### Northern Dimension Project

In 2000, the EU Summit approved the [Northern Dimension](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Dimension) project, which aimed to gradually develop a consolidated strategy for intercountry and interregional cooperation in Northern Europe, including the Russian northern regions of Karelia,[^cite_note-19] the Komi Republic, Murmansk, Kaliningrad, Arkhangelsk, Vologda, Pskov, and Leningrad Oblasts, St. Petersburg, and the [Nenets Autonomous Okrug](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nenets_Autonomous_Okrug).&#x20;

The Republic of Karelia promotes international cultural and economic cooperation[^cite_note-20] with the provinces and unions of communes of [Finland](10577.md),[^cite_note-21] the [Swedish](5058739.md) province ([Västerbotten](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4sterbotten)),[^cite_note-22] the [Norwegian](21241.md) municipality ([Tromsø](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troms%C3%B8_Municipality)),[^cite_note-23] the state of the [United States of America](3434750.md) ([Vermont](32578.md)), the voivodeship of [Poland](22936.md) (Chekhanovo), the autonomous republic of [Georgia](48768.md) ([Adjara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adjara)), the prefecture of [Greece](12108.md) (Ilia),[^cite_note-24] the [Republic of Belarus](3457.md),[^cite_note-25] the province of [Fujian](88585.md) (Eastern [China](5405.md)),[^cite_note-26] and the region of [Abai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abai_Region) in the Republic of Kazakhstan.[^cite_note-27]&#x20;

## History

Main article: [History of Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Karelia)

### Middle ages



[Korela Fort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korela_Fortress)

The [Karelian people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelians) and culture developed during the [Viking Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_Age) in the region to the west of [Lake Ladoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Ladoga). Karelians were first mentioned in [Swedish sagas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viking_saga) around the 10th century. Russians first mentioned Karelians in 1143, they called Karelians *["Korela"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korela_Fortress).*[^cite_note-28]&#x20;

[Sweden](5058739.md)'s interest in Karelia began a centuries-long struggle with [Novgorod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novgorod_Republic) (later [Russia](25391.md)) that resulted in numerous border changes following the many wars fought between the two, the most famous of which is the [Pillage of Sigtuna of 1187](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pillage_of_Sigtuna). In 1137 the oldest documented settlement was established, the modern-day city of [Olonets (Aunus)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olonets).[^cite_note-29] Karelians converted to [Orthodox Christianity](10186.md) in 1227.[^cite_note-30] The Karelians' alliance with Novgorod developed into domination by the latter in the 13th century, when Karelia became a part of Novgorod under the name of Obonezhie pyatina as an autonomy. Later Karelia had anti-Novgorod [revolts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolt) in the 13th and 14th centuries. Later Karelia became a part of [Muscovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Moscow) when Novgorod was annexed in the second half of the 15th century.&#x20;

### Modern era

During the [Great Northern War (1700–1721)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Northern_War) the modern-day capital of Karelia, the city of Petrozavodsk, was founded as a cannon factory by [Peter the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_the_Great).[^cite_note-31]&#x20;

### 19th century

On 9(21) September 1801 [Olonets Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olonets_Governorate) was created by order of [Alexander I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Russia).[^cite_note-32]&#x20;

After [Russian emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_emperor) [Alexander I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_I_of_Russia) became the [Grand Duke of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duke_of_Finland) following the [Finnish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_War), he transferred [Old Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Finland) to the newly formed [Grand Duchy of Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Duchy_of_Finland) in 1812.[^cite_note-33] This move was regarded as a means of securing support of the [Finnish nobility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_nobility) for the Russian emperor.[^cite_note-34]&#x20;

### Early 20th century



Union of White Sea Karelians

In 1906, the [Union of White Sea Karelians](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Union_of_White_Sea_Karelians\&action=edit\&redlink=1) (Vienan karjalaisten liitto) was created. The Union's main goal was to improve the life of the common Karelians and additionally develop their own [national](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_identity) identity.[^cite_note-35] The union was temporarily dissolved in 1911 after series of repressions done by the local government.[^cite_note-36]&#x20;

In 1917, the [Murmansk Railroad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirov_Railway) was built, leading to the Karelian lands becoming more strategically important. This led much of [intelligentsia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intelligentsia) to believe that the Russian tourism and [immigration](4599312.md) into the region would rise, leading to further [assimilation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_assimilation) of the Karelians to the Russian culture.[^cite_note-37]&#x20;

### Civil War

Main article: [Karelian Labor Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Labor_Commune)

During the [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Civil_War) and the [Russian Civil Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Civil_War) the local peasantry rebelled against the new Soviet State due its *[Prodrazverstka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prodrazverstka)* policy, causing several squads of the "[Whiteguard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_movement)" to cross into the [Karelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Karelia) lands,[^cite_note-38] where then was organized a government that later swiftly declared independence from the [Russian Soviet Federative Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic), creating the [Uhtua Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Uhtua). Later in 1920 Finnish forces occupied Olonets, creating another [puppet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olonets_Government_of_Southern_Karelia), which then merged with the other Karelian state into the [United Karelian Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_United_Government). The regions were reclaimed by the [Red Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) later the same year, the Tartu peace was signed and the Karelian United Government was dissolved.&#x20;

Similar to other ethnically non-Russian states within [RSFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Soviet_Federative_Socialist_Republic), the Karelia would receive autonomy within RSFSR, establishing the [Karelian Labour Commune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Labor_Commune) on 8 June 1920, which enjoyed a large de-facto autonomy approved by [Lenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_Lenin) in early 1921.[^cite_note-39]&#x20;

In 1921, an [uprising was started](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Karelian_uprising) by the *[Forest Guerrillas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forest_Guerrillas)* in an attempt to gain control over Karelia yet again, but it was defeated by the Soviets shortly after.[^cite_note-40]&#x20;

During the years of its existence, the Commune was actively educating the people, opening the schools and libraries as of the [Likbez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likbez) policy were open and maintained, the Commune was later expanded in 1923 by transferring the Kolezhemskaya, Lapinskaya, Navodnitskaya and many other [posads](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Posads\&action=edit\&redlink=1) from [Arkhangelsk Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkhangelsk_Governorate).[^cite_note-41]&#x20;

In 1923, the Karelian Labour Commune became the [Karelian Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Autonomous_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) due its de-facto large autonomy, with the government of the region directly managing the local economy without having to pay its taxes to the RSFSR's state budget. The formal increase of the autonomy was first [vetoed](74661.md) by [People's Commissar for Nationalities of the RSFSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People%27s_Commissariat_for_Nationalities), but it was later accepted by the [Central Committee of the Communist Party of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_Committee_of_the_Communist_Party_of_the_Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43]&#x20;

### Karelian Autonomous Soviet Socialist Republic

After the Likbez policy was fulfilled, the Republic now shifted its goal from educating the people to expanding the production and [electrifying](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrifying) the Republic according to the [GOELRO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GOELRO) plan. The first steps were the creation of Mevezegorsky and Pudozsky tree-cutting factories, the [Kondopoga Paper Factory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OAO_Kondopoga), and the launch of the Kem and the Uhta [hydroelectrostations](381399.md).&#x20;

In the 1930s, the goal yet again shifted, now to improving the cultural and physical development and well-being of the locals by creation of many free [clinics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinic) and [hospitals](18994221.md), "Houses of Physical Culture", Theaters etc.[^cite_note-44]&#x20;



Sandarmokh forest

Many of the Finns who fled to Karelia were detained and most likely shot during [The Great Purge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Great_Purge) of 1937, with the Karelian ethnic Finns' population dropping to 21%.[^cite_note-45] Karelia has one of the biggest burial sites of Stalinist purges in Russia, [Sandarmokh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandarmokh), where possibly thousands of victims were executed.&#x20;

### Winter War

Main article: [Winter War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_War)

During the Winter War, a Soviet [puppet government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Democratic_Republic) was created in occupied territories. The Finnish Democratic Republic was to incorporate Finland, plus some western parts of the KASSR. Some members of the FDP government were also members of the KASSR government.[^cite_note-46]&#x20;



Evacuation from [Kurkijoki](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Kurkijoki\&action=edit\&redlink=1), near [Lahdenpohja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lakhdenpokhya)

After the [Moscow Peace Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Peace_Treaty) territories of the [Karelian Isthmus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_Isthmus) were transferred to the newly created Karelo-Finnish Soviet Socialist Republic. After the [evacuation of Finnish Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Evacuation_of_Finnish_Karelia), the new territories were left unpopulated, so migrants from [Belarus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byelorussian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), [Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Soviet_Socialist_Republic), Russia, and other [Soviet republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republics_of_the_Soviet_Union) moved in. To this day, this area has one of the lowest percentages of Karelian and Finnish populations in the Republic.&#x20;

### World War II

After the beginning of [World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_II\)), mass rallies were held on the territory of the republic, at which the inhabitants of Karelia declared their readiness to stand up for the defense of the Soviet Union. Workers of the [Onega Tractor Plant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Onega_Tractor_Plant) wrote "We will work only in such a way as to fully meet the needs of our Red Army. We will double, triple our forces and crush, destroy the [German fascists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wehrmacht)".[^cite_note-\:5-47]&#x20;

On 24 June 1941, after the German army crossed [Zapadnaya Dvina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zapadnaya_Dvina), [Finnish president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Finland) [Risto Ryti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Risto_Ryti) announced declaration of war on the Soviet Union.[^cite_note-\:5-47] The [Finnish army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Army) crossed the Soviet border on 1 July.[^cite_note-\:6-48]&#x20;

Soon after the evacuation of border regions began, On 3 July, a republican evacuation commission was created. At its first meeting, it was decided to evacuate children under 14 out of Petrozavodsk. The same decision also refers to the evacuation of 150 families of leading party and Soviet workers in Karelia. Those residents who could work had to remain in the harvest and defense work.[^cite_note-\:7-49]&#x20;



In grey, Finnish occupied territories

By September the Finnish army already reached Petrozavodsk and captured Olonets.[^cite_note-\:6-48] Petrozavodsk offensive began on 20 September. To protect the city, the [7th Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Army_\(Soviet_Union\)) under the command of General [K.A. Meretskov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirill_Meretskov) was directly subordinated to the Headquarters of the Supreme Commander.[^cite_note-50]&#x20;

On 30 September, the position of the defenders of the city deteriorated sharply. The Finnish army managed to break through Soviet defenses and cut the highway to Kondopoga in the area of the Sulazhgorsky brick factory. In the south Finns came close to the city outskirts. On 1 October, due to the threat of encirclement, an order was received from the command to withdraw the main units defending the city.&#x20;

The fighting near Petrozavodsk allowed the authorities to evacuate most of the civilian population and a significant part of the production capacities. In total, more than 500 thousand people were evacuated from the republic to the east. [Petrozavodsk University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrozavodsk_State_University) was temporarily relocated to [Syktyvkar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syktyvkar).[^cite_note-\:6-48]&#x20;



[Wartime concentration camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Karelian_concentration_camps) in Petrozavodsk under the [Finnish military administration in Eastern Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_military_administration_in_Eastern_Karelia)

After the capture of Petrozavodsk, the capital of Soviet Karelia was transferred first to Medvezhyegorsk, then to Belomorsk. Less than 90 thousand people remained in the occupied territory, half of which are representatives of the Finno-Ugric peoples: Karelians, Vepsians, and Finns. [The Finnish administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_military_administration_in_Eastern_Karelia) has officially recognized them as a "[kindred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heimosodat)" population. The rest received the status of "unrelated" people.[^cite_note-\:6-48] Most of them have been put into [concentration camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Karelian_concentration_camps), along with communists and people who could not speak Finnish or Karelian.&#x20;

Former prisoners of the camps recalled that the staff often treated them more harshly than was supposed to according to the instructions. According to them, the Finns, in the presence of children, shot prisoners and beat women, children, and the elderly. One of the prisoners told the Finnish historian Helga Seppel that before leaving Petrozavodsk, the invaders shot several young people for unknown reasons.[^cite_note-\:6-48]&#x20;

During the occupation, Petrozavodsk was renamed to [Äänislinna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrozavodsk).&#x20;

Only a few territories of the K-FSSR managed to escape the Finnish occupation: the [Belomorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belomorsky_District), [Loukhsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loukhsky_District), [Kemsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kemsky_District), [Pudozhsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pudozhsky_District) okrugs, as well as part of the [Medvezhiegorsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvezhyegorsk), Tungudsky and [Ukhta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalevalsky_District) okrugs. By 1942, about 70 thousand people lived here.[^cite_note-\:7-49]&#x20;

After the end of the [Siege of Leningrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Leningrad) Soviet army was ordered to liberate Karelia.&#x20;

On 21 June 1944 [Svir-Petrozavodsk operation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyborg%E2%80%93Petrozavodsk_offensive) started. On 27 June the Finnish army left Petrozavodsk. By August the Soviet army reached pre-war borders.&#x20;

The [Moscow Armistice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscow_Armistice) was signed on 19 September 1944, ending the [Continuation War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Continuation_War), resulting in Finland ceding the Karelian Isthmus and [Ladoga Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladoga_Karelia) to the USSR.&#x20;

### Post-war



KFSSR building on [VDNkH](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exhibition_of_Achievements_of_National_Economy)

After the end of [World War II](32927.md), the Karelian Isthmus was incorporated into the [Leningrad Oblast](480845.md) and the city of [Alakurtti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alakurtti_\(rural_locality\)) was transferred to [Murmansk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk_Oblast).&#x20;

After [normalization of diplomatic relations between USSR and Finland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finlandization) the status of the Karelo-Finnish SSR was changed back to the Karelian ASSR in 1956. After this Karelian, Veps, and Finnish languages began a decline in usage due to the lack of support from the state and lack of education.[^cite_note-\:2-51]&#x20;

The transformation of the KFSSR into the Karelian ASSR was supposed to show that the USSR did not have aggressive goals against Finland.[^cite_note-52]&#x20;

In 1978, a [Korean Air Lines Boeing 707-321B](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_Air_Lines_Flight_902) was shot down over Murmansk Oblast and landed near Louhi.&#x20;

## Politics



The building of the [Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_the_Republic_of_Karelia) in [Petrozavodsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrozavodsk)

The highest executive authority in the Republic of Karelia is the Head of the Republic. The acting Head of the Republic is [Artur Parfenchikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Parfenchikov), who was elected in February 2017 and later [re-elected in 2022](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Karelia_head_election).&#x20;

The parliament of the Republic of Karelia is the [Legislative Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_the_Republic_of_Karelia) comprising fifty deputies elected for a four-year term.&#x20;

The Constitution of the Republic of Karelia was adopted on 12 February 2001.&#x20;

### Legislature

The [Legislative Assembly of the Republic of Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legislative_Assembly_of_the_Republic_of_Karelia) is a permanent representative and the only legislative body of state power in the Republic of Karelia.[^cite_note-55] Since 2016, it consists of 36 deputies elected by the inhabitants of the republic according to a mixed electoral system: 18 deputies according to party lists (proportional system), and 18 in single-member districts (majority system) based on universal, equal and direct suffrage by secret ballot. The term of office of deputies of one convocation is five years.&#x20;

The 7th convocation was [elected in September 2021](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%92%D1%8B%D0%B1%D0%BE%D1%80%D1%8B_%D0%B2_%D0%97%D0%B0%D0%BA%D0%BE%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B4%D0%B0%D1%82%D0%B5%D0%BB%D1%8C%D0%BD%D0%BE%D0%B5_%D1%81%D0%BE%D0%B1%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B8%D0%B5_%D0%A0%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D1%83%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%8F_\(2021\)) and will last until 2026. Of the 36 deputies, 22 are from [United Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Russia), 4 from the [Communist Party of the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_the_Russian_Federation), 2 from the [Liberal Democratic Party of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democratic_Party_of_Russia), 4 from [A Just Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Just_Russia), 2 from [Yabloko](429579.md), 1 from [New People](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_People_\(political_party\)), and 1 from the [Party of Pensioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Party_of_Pensioners_for_Social_Justice). [Elissan Shandalovich](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%A8%D0%B0%D0%BD%D0%B4%D0%B0%D0%BB%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87,_%D0%AD%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%81%D1%81%D0%B0%D0%BD_%D0%92%D0%BB%D0%B0%D0%B4%D0%B8%D0%BC%D0%B8%D1%80%D0%BE%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%87) (United Russia) was elected Chairman. [Igor Zubarev](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igor_Zubarev) (United Russia) was elected representative of the Legislative Assembly in the Federation Council.[^cite_note-56]&#x20;

### Executive



Government building in [Petrozavodsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrozavodsk)

Executive power is exercised by:[^cite_note-57]&#x20;

- The [Head of the Republic of Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Head_of_the_Republic_of_Karelia) – the highest official of the Republic of Karelia

- the [Government of the Republic of Karelia](https://ru.wikipedia.org/wiki/%D0%9F%D1%80%D0%B0%D0%B2%D0%B8%D1%82%D0%B5%D0%BB%D1%8C%D1%81%D1%82%D0%B2%D0%BE_%D0%A0%D0%B5%D1%81%D0%BF%D1%83%D0%B1%D0%BB%D0%B8%D0%BA%D0%B8_%D0%9A%D0%B0%D1%80%D0%B5%D0%BB%D0%B8%D1%8F), headed by the Head of the Republic – the permanent supreme executive body of state power of the Republic of Karelia

- other executive authorities

The Head of the Republic is elected by the republic's inhabitants on the basis of universal, equal, and direct suffrage by secret ballot. The term of office is 5 years and one person cannot hold office for more than two consecutive terms.&#x20;

The current head of the republic is [Artur Parfenchikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artur_Parfenchikov) (appointed by President [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) on 15 February 2017; on 10 September 2017, he was elected in the [elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Karelia_head_election) from the [United Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Russia) party). [Alexander Rakitin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Rakitin) has been appointed as the representative in the [Federation Council](1268578.md).&#x20;

### Languages



A [bilingual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multilingualism) street sign in [Petrozavodsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrozavodsk)

Currently Russian is the only [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) of the republic. [Karelian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelian_language), [Veps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veps_language), and [Finnish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_language) have been officially recognized languages of the republic since 2004, and they are *[de jure](43594.md)* supported by the government.[^cite_note-repu-7] In early 2000s Karelian and Veps [language nests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Language_nest) were created in Petrozavodsk, [Kalevala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalevala,_Russia), Tuksa and [Sheltozero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shyoltozero),[^cite_note-73] but were later shut down.[^cite_note-74] Now native languages of Karelia have little support from the government.[^cite_note-\:2-51]&#x20;

Finnish was the second [official language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_language) of Karelia from the creation of the Karelian Labour Commune up until the [dissolution of the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dissolution_of_the_Soviet_Union).[^cite_note-75] Thereafter there were suggestions to raise Karelian as the second official language, but they were repeatedly turned down.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-repu-7]&#x20;

The Karelians have traditionally been [Eastern Orthodox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Orthodox_Church). [Lutheranism](23371382.md) was brought to Karelia during [Sweden's conquest of Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingrian_War) and was common in regions that then belonged to Finland. Nowadays Lutherans can be found in most big settlements but they remain a minority.[^cite_note-79]&#x20;

Catholics have one parish in Petrozavodsk.[^cite_note-80]&#x20;

The Petrozavodsk [Jewish](25955086.md) Religious Community was registered in 1997.[^cite_note-81]&#x20;

Karelian Muslims were organized into Karelian [muftiate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muftiate) in 2001.[^cite_note-82]&#x20;

In addition, 44% of the population declared to be "spiritual but not religious", 18% is [atheist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atheism), and 8% follow other religions or did not answer the question.[^cite_note-2012ArenaAtlas-77]&#x20;

## Economy



The remains of the Onega Tractor Plant

Karelia's economy is based on [forestry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forestry), [mining](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mining), [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism), [agriculture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Agriculture), [fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fishing)[^cite_note-\:3-83] and [the paper industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pulp_and_paper_industry).&#x20;

Despite being 0,4% of Russia's population, 65–70% of all Russian [trout](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trout) is grown in the Republic, 26% of [iron ore pellets](145352.md), 20% of paper, 12% of wood pulp and cellulose.&#x20;

Karelia's [gross regional product](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gross_regional_product) (GRP) in 2007 was 109.5 billion rubles.[^cite_note-hse_2007-84] The Karelian economy's GRP in 2010 was estimated at 127733.8 million rubles. Karelia's GRP in 2021 was 176 billion rubles.[^cite_note-\:3-83] This amounts to 291,841 rubles per capita, which is lower than [national average](25705.md).&#x20;

The largest companies in the region include [Karelsky Okatysh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelsky_Okatysh_mine) (\$1319755601 of revenue in 2021), [Segezha Pulp and Paper Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segezha_Pulp_and_Paper_Mill) (\$86897488 of revenue in 2021), [OAO Kondopoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OAO_Kondopoga) (\$20366599 of revenue in 2021).[^cite_note-regioncompanies-85]&#x20;

In the structure of the gross regional product in 2017, the main types of economic activity were\:mining – 17.6%; manufacturing industries – 16.9%; transportation and storage – 11.8%; wholesale and retail trade; repair of motor vehicles and motorcycles – 9.8%; public administration and military security; social security – 8.7%.[^cite_note-86]&#x20;

A fast [fiber-optic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fibre-optic) cable link connecting Finnish [Kuhmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuhmo) and Karelian [Kostomuksha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostomuksha) was built in 2007, providing fast telecommunications.[^cite_note-hse_2007-84]&#x20;

### Budget sector

In 2022, the republic's budget received 75 billion 198 million rubles of revenue. At the same time, expenses amounted to 82 billion 202 million rubles.[^cite_note-87]&#x20;

Tax revenues make up the majority of budget revenues and in 2008 amounted to 64% of operating income. The tax concentration is relatively high: the 10 largest taxpayers, mainly industrial enterprises, provided about 38% of all tax revenues in 2008.[^cite_note-88]&#x20;

### Industry

#### Forestry



The Segezha Pulp and Paper Mill

The forest and [wood processing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wood_processing) sector dominates industrial activity in Karelia. A large number of small enterprises carry out timber logging whereas pulp and paper production is concentrated in five large enterprises, which produce about a quarter of Russia's total output of paper.[^cite_note-hse_characteristics-89] Three largest companies in the pulp and paper sector in 2021 were: [OAO Kondopoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OAO_Kondopoga) (sales of \$369314325), [Segezha Pulp and Paper Mill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Segezha_Pulp_and_Paper_Mill) (\$221317040) and [RK-Grand (*Pitkäranta* Pulp Factory)](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=OAO_Pitkjaranta_Pulp_Factory\&action=edit\&redlink=1) (\$78750849).[^cite_note-regioncompanies-85]&#x20;

The timber industry complex of Karelia produces 28% of the republic's industrial output.[^cite_note-90]&#x20;

#### Mining



Karelsky okatysh

Karelia is a region with a lot of natural resources, including metals such as [gold](12240.md), [silver](27119.md), [platinum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Platinum), [copper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copper) and [palladium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palladium).[^cite_note-91][^cite_note-92]&#x20;

In 2007, extractive industries (including extraction of metal ores) amounted to 30% of the republic's industrial output.[^cite_note-hse_2007-84] There are about 53 mining companies in Karelia, employing more than 10,000 people.[^cite_note-mining2009-93] One of the most important companies in the sector is [AO Karelian Pellet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelsky_Okatysh_mine), which is the 5th largest of Russia's 25 mining and [ore dressing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ore_dressing) enterprises involved in ore extraction and [iron ore concentrate](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Iron_ore_concentrate\&action=edit\&redlink=1) production. Other large companies in the sector were [OAO Karelnerud](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=OAO_Karelnerud\&action=edit\&redlink=1), [Mosavtorod State Unitary Enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Mosavtorod_State_Unitary_Enterprise\&action=edit\&redlink=1), and [Pitkjaranta Mining Directorate State Unitary Enterprise](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Pitkjaranta_Mining_Directorate_State_Unitary_Enterprise\&action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-allregions-17]&#x20;

#### Energy



The Harlu hydroelectric plant

As of 2021, there were 29 [powerplants](212141.md), of them 21 were [hydroplants](381399.md) and 8 [thermal power plants](2549543.md).[^cite_note-94]&#x20;

#### Agriculture

Due to Karelia's climate, only 1,2% of the land is used for farming. Most of the farmland is located on [podzol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podzol).[^cite_note-95]&#x20;

20 agricultural organizations employing 2.3 thousand people. [Animal husbandry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Animal_husbandry) is the leading branch of agriculture in the Republic, the main areas of which are dairy cattle breeding, pig breeding, broiler poultry farming, and fur farming.[^cite_note-\:4-96]&#x20;

Annually agricultural enterprises of the region produce up to 59 thousand tons of milk. Based on its natural and climatic conditions, the plant growing industry is focused on the production of feed for livestock, the bulk of potatoes and vegetables are grown in small forms of management.[^cite_note-\:4-96]&#x20;

#### Fishing

Fishing enterprises of Karelia produced 91.9 thousand tons of aquatic biological resources in 2021.&#x20;

In the Barents Sea and the Atlantic Ocean, 89.9 thousand tons of aquatic biological resources were caught, of them 34.6 thousand tons of [cod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cod) and haddock, 34.1 thousand tons of [blue whiting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_whiting), 18 thousand tons of [mackerel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mackerel) and 1.1 thousand tons of [northern shrimp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Shrimp). 306 tons of fish were caught in the White Sea and 612 tons of [kelp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelp) and [fucus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fucus) were harvested. The catch of [freshwater fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freshwater_fish) amounted to 1.1 thousand tons.[^cite_note-97]&#x20;

### Tourism



[Ruskeala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruskeala) museum express

Karelia is popular for [international](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_tourism) and [domestic tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_tourism).&#x20;

Traditional, active, [cultural](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_tourism) and [ecological](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ecotourism) types of tourism are popular among tourists.[^cite_note-98]&#x20;

Karelia attracts ecotourists with its nature and wilderness[^cite_note-99] and low population density. During the summer water tourism is also popular among many tourists.&#x20;

Cultural tourism is also a big part of Karelia's tourism economy. The region attracts many tourists with its wooden architecture, local culture, and traditions.&#x20;

Karelia also has the first Russian [health resort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Destination_spa) – Martial Waters (1719).&#x20;

### Foreign trade

The economy of Karelia is export-orientated. By the volume of exports per capita, Karelia is among the leading regions of Russia. More than 50% of manufactured products (and up to 100% in several industries) are exported.[^cite_note-\:3-83]&#x20;

The Republic's main export partners in 2001 were Finland (32% of total exports), [Germany](11867.md) (7%), [Netherlands](21148.md) (7%), and the [United Kingdom](31717.md) (6%).[^cite_note-allregions-17] Main export products were lumber (over 50%), iron ore pellets (13–15%) paper and cardboard (6–9%) and sawn timber with (5–7%). Many of Karelia's companies have received investments from Finland.[^cite_note-allregions-17]&#x20;

## Transportation

### Railroad



Train station in [Medvezhyegorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvezhyegorsk)

Karelia is a strategically important railroad region due to the fact that it connects [Murmansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk) with the rest of Russia by the [Kirov Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kirov_Railway), which was [electrified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrified_\(rail\)) in 2005.[^cite_note-100]&#x20;

There are also rail connections with Finland in [Värtsilä](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4rtsil%C3%A4_\(Russia\)) and [Kostomuksha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kostomuksha), but they are not electrified.&#x20;

Most of Karelia's railway lines are served by the [Petrozavodsk branch of the Oktyabrskaya Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrozavodsk_Railway_Division), which is one of the largest budget-forming enterprises of the Republic.&#x20;

All Karelian district capitals are connected by railroad, except for the Kalevalsky district and Prionezhsky district.&#x20;

In total, Karelia has 1915 km of railways.[^cite_note-101]&#x20;

### Water communications



[White Sea-Baltic Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea%E2%80%93Baltic_Canal)

Water communications connect Karelia with the [Barents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barents_Sea), [Baltic](3335.md), [Black](3386.md), White and [Caspian Seas](19653787.md).&#x20;

The [White Sea–Baltic Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_Sea%E2%80%93Baltic_Canal) was built in the 1930s to connect the Baltic and White seas. The 227 km long canal was built by prisoners. Even though it has 19 [locks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lock_\(water_navigation\)), the canal cannot pass vessels with a [draft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draft_\(hull\)) of more than 5 meters.[^cite_note-102] The canal is a part of the [Volgo-Baltic Waterway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga%E2%80%93Baltic_Waterway).&#x20;

There are also [river ports](1341936.md) on the coast of the White Sea, there were plans to upgrade them to [ocean ports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port#Seaport) but they were deemed too expensive.[^cite_note-103]&#x20;

### Highways



[R-21 Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R21_highway_\(Russia\))

Automobile [highway R-21 "Kola"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R21_highway_\(Russia\)) crosses Karelia and connects the [Murmansk region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk_region) and Murmansk seaport with [St. Petersburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Petersburg) and [Moscow](19004.md).&#x20;

[European route E105](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E105) also goes through Karelia.&#x20;

Other highways connect with Finland in Louhsky district Värtsilä and Kostomuksha.&#x20;

Many of Karelian roads are still [unimproved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_surface).[^cite_note-104]&#x20;

### Air transportation



[Petrozavodsk Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrozavodsk_Airport)

[Petrozavodsk Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrozavodsk_Airport) is the only working airport in Karelia as of 2022.[^cite_note-105]&#x20;

There are other airports, such as Kalevala or Kostomuksha, but they are either not in use or are only used by [firefighters](1143974.md).[^cite_note-106]&#x20;

## Healthcare

In 2024, the incidence of cancer in Karelia was 684 cases per 100,000 population, which is 36 more than in 2023 and is the highest rate among Russian regions.[^cite_note-107]&#x20;

According to Olga Ruotselainen, Deputy head of the Karelian Ministry of Health, today more than 20,000 people with a diagnosis of oncology are registered.&#x20;

Women in Karelia most often suffer from breast cancer. Cancer of other skin growths is in second place, and colon cancer is in third place. Among men, the most common type of oncology is prostate cancer, second being cancer of the bronchi, trachea, lung, and third being skin cancer.[^cite_note-108]&#x20;

The healthcare system of the Republic of Karelia has 24 hospital institutions (republican and district hospitals), 5 dispensaries, the Republican Center for the Prevention and Control of [AIDS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HIV/AIDS) and Infectious Diseases, the Republican Blood Transfusion Station, 3 maternity and childhood care institutions, 10 outpatient clinics, 5 special type healthcare institutions, 7 social service institutions, 18 district social protection institutions, the autonomous educational institution of secondary vocational education of the Republic of Karelia Petrozavodsk Basic Medical College.[^cite_note-109]&#x20;

The regional target program Improvement of the Demographic Situation of the Republic of Karelia for the period 2008–2010 and up to 2015 has been adopted.[^cite_note-110]&#x20;

## Culture

See also: [Finnish mythology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_mythology), [Runic song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runic_song), and [Kalevala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalevala)

Karelia is very culturally diverse region that was influenced by Finno-Ugric, [Slavic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavs) and [Scandinavian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scandinavian_culture) cultures. The main unifying factor in the formation of the culture of the region was the Orthodox religion.[^cite_note-111]&#x20;

A lot is being done in the Republic of Karelia today to support the interests of more than 100 nationalities inhabiting it, including Karelians, Veps and Finns. More than 60 national public associations have been registered: unions, congresses, popular movements, autonomies, friendship societies, cultural societies.[^cite_note-112] There is a regional target program Karelia – the Territory of Consent, a republican target program State support of Karelian, Vepsian and Finnish languages, a public council has been established to coordinate the implementation of these programs.[^cite_note-113]&#x20;

### Literature



Kalevala rune singers

Karelia is sometimes called "the songlands", as Karelian poems constitute most of the Karelo-Finnish epic *[Kalevala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalevala)* and many of [Russian Bylinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bylina) were documented in [Pudozh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pudozh).[^cite_note-114]&#x20;

The written literature of Karelia was formed at the beginning of the 20th century. In the 1930s Karelian and Veps languages gained a writing system, but during the Stalinist repressions many books in Veps and Karelian were burned and cultural figures were deported.[^cite_note-115]&#x20;

After the creation of the Karelian Labour Commune many [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Finnish) and [Canadian finns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finnish_Canadians) moved to Karelia and began creating new literature. Many Karelians could understand Finnish so some authors, such as one of the most famous Karelian writers [Antti Timonen](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=Antti_Timonen\&action=edit\&redlink=1) , started to write in Finnish.[^cite_note-116]&#x20;

Writers of the Republic of Karelia are united in public organizations:&#x20;

- Karelian regional branch of the [Union of Writers of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Writers_of_Russia)

- Karelian Writers' Union

- Representation of the [Union of Russian Writers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_of_Russian_Writers) in Karelia

### Art

Karelian art history begun with [Petroglyphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petroglyphs_of_Lake_Onega_and_the_White_Sea), which were created around 6,500 years ago.[^cite_note-117] They became a [UNESCO](21786641.md) [World Heritage Site](44940.md), listed in 2021.[^cite_note-118]&#x20;

Icon painters were the first professional artists of Karelia.[^cite_note-119]&#x20;

Karelia has become a source of inspiration for many famous artists of the 19th–20th century such as: [Ivan Shishkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Shishkin), [Arkhip Kuindzhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkhip_Kuindzhi), and [N. K. Roerich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicholas_Roerich).&#x20;

The formation of professional painting in Karelia is associated with the name of the People's Artist of the [KFSSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karelo-Finnish_Soviet_Socialist_Republic) V. N. Popov (1869–1945). In 1934, the Union of Artists of the Autonomous Karelian SSR was established, the first chairman of which was elected Yu. O. Rautanen, since 2010, the Karelian branch of the Union of Artists of Russia. As part of the Karelian department, there is an Association of Young artists and Art Historians.&#x20;

### Architecture



Church of the Transfiguration, Kizhi

Karelia is famous for its wooden architecture. Karelian architecture developed under the strong influence of [Novgorod architecture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_architecture).[^cite_note-120] Examples of Karelian architecture are collected in the [Kizhi Pogost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kizhi_Pogost) Museum.&#x20;

Later Karelian architecture was influenced by Finns, especially after the creation of the Karelian Labour Commune.&#x20;

### Music

See also: [Music in the Republic of Karelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_in_the_Republic_of_Karelia)

[Kantele](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kantele) is the most famous traditional Karelian musical instrument. In [Kalevala](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalevala) the mage [Väinämöinen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C3%A4in%C3%A4m%C3%B6inen) makes the first kantele from the jawbone of a giant [pike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pike_\(fish\)) and a few hairs from [Hiisi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hiisi)'s stallion.&#x20;

In 1933, the Karelian State Philharmonic Symphony Orchestra was founded. The orchestra belonged to the Karelian Radio and Television, part of the Ministry of Culture of Karelia. However, since 1997, the orchestra has been a part of the Karelian State Philharmonic Society.[^cite_note-121]&#x20;

In 1935, the Karelian Folk Segozer Choir (Padans), the Karelian Folk Olonets Choir Karelian birch was founded.&#x20;

In 1936, the National Song and Dance Ensemble of Karelia (*Kantele*), the Veps Folk Choir, and the Karelian Folk Petrovsky Choir were founded.&#x20;

The Pomeranian Folk Choir ([Medvezhyegorsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medvezhyegorsk)) was founded in 1937, and the Karelian Folk Vedlozersky Choir (*Vedlozero*) was founded in 1938.&#x20;

In 1937, the Union of Karelian Composers was founded.&#x20;

In 1938, the Petrozavodsk Music College (now the Petrozavodsk Music College named after K. E. Rautio) was opened.[^cite_note-122]&#x20;

In 1939, the Symphony Orchestra of the Karelo-Finnish State Philharmonic was founded.[^cite_note-123]&#x20;



Karelian Rune singers

In 1967, the Petrozavodsk branch of the [Leningrad State Conservatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Petersburg_Conservatory) (now the Petrozavodsk Glazunov State Conservatory) was opened.&#x20;

In 1973, Honored Artist of the Republic of Karelia L. P. Budanov founded the Karelia-Brass ensemble.&#x20;

Throughout the years, many Karelian, Russian, Veps, Finnish and [Pomor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pomor_dialects) choirs were created, such as the Karelian choir "Oma pajo" in 1990, which is still active.[^cite_note-124]&#x20;
