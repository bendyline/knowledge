The **Shanghai Masters** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 上海大师赛), known as the **Rolex Shanghai Masters** for [sponsorship reasons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolex), is an annual tennis tournament for male professional players held in Shanghai, China in the month of October. It is played on outdoor [hard courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt) at the [Qizhong Forest Sports City Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qizhong_Forest_Sports_City_Arena) in the [Minhang District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minhang_District). The tournament is part of the nine [ATP 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_1000) events on the [ATP Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Tour), and is the only one not played in Europe or North America.[^cite_note-Profile-1] The tournament was not held from 2020 to 2022 due to Chinese travel restrictions related to the [COVID-19 pandemic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic_in_mainland_China).

## Venue



The Qizhong Arena main court, during the [2008 Tennis Masters Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Tennis_Masters_Cup)

Main article: [Qizhong Forest Sports City Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qizhong_Forest_Sports_City_Arena)

All matches are played in outdoor conditions at the Qizhong Arena, after the venue held the [Tennis Masters Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_World_Tour_Finals) indoors from 2005 to 2008. The surface is [hard court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardcourt), specifically [DecoTurf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DecoTurf).[^cite_note-Profile-1][^cite_note-DecoTurf-2]

The venue was originally built in 2004 and 2005 to host the Tennis Masters Cup, after the ATP awarded the tournament to Shanghai for a three-year contract (2005–2007), later extended to a fourth year.[^cite_note-Shanghai20061-3][^cite_note-Shanghai20062-4] The site was conceived to become the largest tennis venue in Asia, with a 15,000-seats main stadium featuring a retractable roof of eight steel panels representing Shanghai's city flower, the [magnolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magnolia). As of 2013, the Arena's Grand Stand Court 1 stands in fifth place in the [list of tennis stadiums by capacity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tennis_stadiums_by_capacity), alongside Beijing's [National Tennis Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Tennis_Stadium) (built for the [2008 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics)) and [Wimbledon's Centre Court](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wimbledon_Centre_Court).

In preparation of the first edition of the Shanghai Masters, the venue was expanded with several new stadiums and courts constructed by August 2009, including a Grand Stand Court 2, with a seating capacity of 5,000 spectators, and a Grand Stand Court 3, with a seating capacity of 3,000 spectators.[^cite_note-Launch-5]

## History

The Shanghai ATP Masters 1000 was established to fulfill the desire of the ATP World Tour and the [Chinese Tennis Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Tennis_Association) to develop the market for [tennis in China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tennis_in_China) and Asia in general.[^cite_note-People-6] In 2010 following a sponsorship deal the tournament was renamed the Shanghai Rolex Masters.[^cite_note-7]

In 1996, a professional tournament was held for the first time in Shanghai, the [largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_the_People's_Republic_of_China_by_population) city in China. The inaugural [Shanghai Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingfisher_Airlines_Tennis_Open) was won by Russian [Andrei Olhovskiy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrei_Olhovskiy) over [Mark Knowles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Knowles) of [the Bahamas](3451.md).[^cite_note-Shanghai1996-8] In 2002 the year-end championships, then called the [Tennis Masters Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_World_Tour_Finals).[^cite_note-Shanghai2001-9][^cite_note-Shanghai2002-10] were held in the city. The success of the [2002 Tennis Masters Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Tennis_Masters_Cup), won by [World No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_players) [Lleyton Hewitt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lleyton_Hewitt) from Australia, prompted the ATP, which had abandoned the idea of a touring Tennis Masters Cup, to award Shanghai the right to hold the tournament from 2005 to 2007.

While the [ATP International Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_International_Series) tournament of Shanghai was held two more years in 2003 and 2004 at the [Shanghai New International Expo Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_New_International_Expo_Center) created for the [2002 Tennis Masters Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Tennis_Masters_Cup), a new facility, the [Qizhong Forest Sports City Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qizhong_Forest_Sports_City_Arena), was built to host the year-end championships starting from 2005.[^cite_note-Shanghai2005-11] The ATP eventually extended the three-year deal to a fourth year in 2006, allowing the Tennis Masters Cup to increase its success in Shanghai.[^cite_note-Shanghai20061-3][^cite_note-Shanghai20062-4] Over the four years spent at the Qizhong venue, the tournament saw Swiss [World No. 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ATP_number_1_ranked_players) [Roger Federer](262376.md) reach three finals, losing the first in [2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2005_Tennis_Masters_Cup) to [Argentine](18951905.md) [David Nalbandian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Nalbandian) before winning the following two in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Tennis_Masters_Cup) and [2007](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Tennis_Masters_Cup), and [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) of [Serbia](29265.md) taking the [2008](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Tennis_Masters_Cup) title.

In March 2007, the ATP announced that their 2009 rebranding[^cite_note-Changes-12] would also be the occasion to use the Qizhong facility and the Shanghai Tennis Masters Cup organisation to host an [ATP World Tour Masters 1000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_World_Tour_Masters_1000) event in the city, the equivalent of what were then the [ATP Masters Series](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_Masters_Series).[^cite_note-Shanghai2007-13] Shanghai was eventually given the October spot in the calendar, previously held by the Mutua Madrileña Masters Madrid indoor hard courts event, but was to be held as an outdoor hard surface tournament, thereby reducing the number of indoor Masters events to one, that being the Paris Masters. The new [Mutua Madrileña Madrid Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutua_Madrile%C3%B1a_Madrid_Open) moved to outdoor [red clay courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_clay_court) during the spring European clay court season. The Tennis Masters Cup became the [ATP World Tour Finals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_World_Tour_Finals) and moved to [the O2 arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_O2_arena_\(London\)) in London, United Kingdom.[^cite_note-Changes-12]

Organized by Juss International Sports Event Management Company directed by Jiang Lan, the event was formally presented in a press conference on November 13, 2008, during the season's Tennis Masters Cup tournament, where the choice of the draw sizes, of the surface, and the building of [additional courts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_ATP_Masters_1000#Venue) were announced. [Rolex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rolex), the Swiss watch company, was also revealed as the official sponsor of the event.[^cite_note-ESPN-14] The promotional campaign for the tournament started in early 2009, with the presentation of its slogan, "Simply The Best",[^cite_note-SimplyTheBest-15] and the event was officially launched on May 5, 2009.[^cite_note-Launch-5] Expecting nearly 150,000 spectators during the tournament, the Shanghai Rolex Masters was introduced as the flagship of an Asian swing in the [2009 ATP World Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2009_ATP_World_Tour) calendar after the late September [ATP World Tour 250](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_World_Tour_250) [Thailand Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PTT_Thailand_Open_\(ATP\)) of [Bangkok](56615.md) and [Malaysian Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malaysian_Open_\(tennis\)) of [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md), and then early October [ATP World Tour 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATP_World_Tour_500_series) [Japan Open Tennis Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan_Open_Tennis_Championships) of Tokyo and [China Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_Open_\(tennis\)) of Beijing.[^cite_note-Launch-5]

## Past finals

In singles, [Novak Djokovic](16100029.md) (winner in 2012–13, 2015, and 2018) holds the record for most titles (four). Djokovic and [Andy Murray](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Murray) share the records for most consecutive titles (two victories in a row each). In doubles, [Marcelo Melo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marcelo_Melo) (winner in 2013, 2015, and 2018) holds the record for most titles (three), and no player has collected back-to-back titles yet.
