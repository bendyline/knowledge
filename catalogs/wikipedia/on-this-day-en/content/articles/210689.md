**Gilan Province** ([Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language): استان گیلان)[^cite_note-13] is one of the 31 [provinces of Iran](448160.md), in the northwest of the country and southwest of the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md). Its capital is the city of [Rasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasht).[^cite_note-Gilan_Province_Structure-14] The province lies along the Caspian Sea, in Iran's [Region 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Iran), west of the province of [Mazandaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran), east of the province of [Ardabil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ardabil_province), and north of the provinces of [Zanjan](448108.md) and [Qazvin](448087.md).[^cite_note-15] It borders [Azerbaijan](746.md) ([Astara District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astara_District)) in the north.

The northern section of the province is part of the territory of South (Iranian) [Talysh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talysh_\(region\)). At the center of the province is [Rasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasht). Other cities include [Astaneh-ye Ashrafiyeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astaneh-ye_Ashrafiyeh), [Astara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astara,_Iran), [Fuman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fuman,_Iran), [Hashtpar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashtpar), [Lahijan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lahijan), [Langarud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langarud), [Masuleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masuleh), [Manjil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manjil), [Rudbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudbar), [Rudsar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudsar), [Shaft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaft,_Iran), [Siahkal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siahkal), and [Sowme'eh Sara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sowme'eh_Sara). The main port is [Bandar-e Anzali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Anzali), formerly known as Bandar-e Pahlavi.

## History

Main article: [History of Gilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Gilan)

### Paleolithic

Early humans were present at Gilan since [Lower Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lower_Paleolithic). [Darband Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darband_Cave) is the earliest known human habitation site in Gilan province; it is located in a deep tributary canyon of the [Siah Varud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siah_Varud) and contains evidence for the earliest prehistoric human cave occupation during the Lower Paleolithic in Iran.

Stone artifacts and animal [fossils](10958.md) were discovered by a group of Iranian archaeologists that dates back to the late [Chibanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chibanian).[^cite_note-ReferenceA-16] Yarshalman is a [Middle Paleolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Paleolithic) shelter that was probably occupied by [Neanderthals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neanderthal) about 40,000 to 70,000 years ago.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-16] Later Paleolithic sites in Gilan are [Chapalak Cave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chapalak_Cave)[^cite_note-17] and [Khalvasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khalvasht) shelter.[^cite_note-18]

### Early history

See also: [Caspians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspians) and [Gelae (Scythian tribe)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelae_\(Scythian_tribe\))

It seems that the [Gelae](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelae_\(Scythian_tribe\)), or Gilites, entered the region south of the Caspian coast and west of the Amardos River (now called the [Sefid-Rud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sefid-Rud)) in the second or first century BCE, [Pliny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliny_the_Elder) identifies them with the [Cadusii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cadusii) who were living there previously. It is more likely that they were a separate people, had come from the region of [Dagestan](751099.md), and taken the place of the Kadusii. That the native inhabitants of Gilan have some originating roots in the [Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caucasus) is supported by genetics and language, as the Y-DNA of [Gilaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilaks) most closely resemble that of [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgians) and other [South Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Caucasus) peoples, while their mtDNA closely resembles other [Iranian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iranian_peoples) groups.[^cite_note-sciencedirect.com-19] Their languages shares typologic features with the [languages of the Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_the_Caucasus).[^cite_note-20]

### Medieval history

Gilan province was the place of origin of the [Ziyarid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ziyarid_dynasty) and [Buyid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyid_dynasty) in the mid-10th century. Previously, the people of the province had a prominent position during the [Sassanid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sassanid) through the 7th century, so that their political power extended to [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia).

The first recorded encounter between Gilak and [Deylamite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deylamite) warlords and [invading Muslim armies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_Persia) was at the [Battle of Jalula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jalula) in 637 AD. Deylamite commander Muta led an army of Gils, Deylamites, [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people) and people of the [Rey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rey,_Iran) region. Muta was killed in the battle, and his defeated army managed to retreat in an orderly manner.

However, this appears to have been a Pyrrhic victory for the Arabs, since they did not pursue their opponents. Muslim Arabs never managed to conquer Gilan as they did with other provinces in Iran. Gilanis and Deylamites successfully repulsed all Arab attempts to occupy their land or to convert them to Islam.

In fact, it was the Deylamites under the Buyid king [Mu'izz al-Dawla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mu'izz_al-Dawla) who finally shifted the balance of power by conquering [Baghdad](4492.md) in 945. Mu'izz al-Dawla, however, allowed the [Abbasid caliphs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Abbasid_caliphs) to remain in comfortable, secluded captivity in their palaces.[^cite_note-21]

The [Church of the East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Church_of_the_East) began evangelizing Gilan in the 780s, when a [metropolitan bishopric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_bishop) was established under [Shubhalishoʿ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shubhalisho%CA%BF).[^cite_note-22] In the 9th and 10th centuries AD, Deylamites and later Gilanis gradually converted to [Zaydi Shiʿism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zaidism).

Several Deylamite commanders and soldiers of fortune who were active in the military theaters of Iran and [Mesopotamia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesopotamia) were openly [Zoroastrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zoroastrian) (for example, [Asfar Shiruyeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asfar_Shiruyeh?action=edit\&redlink=1) a warlord in central Iran, and Makan, son of Kaki, the warlord of Rey) or were suspected of harboring pro-Zoroastrian (for example [Mardavij](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mardavij)) sentiments. Muslim chronicles of [Varangian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Varangian) (Rus', pre-Russian Norsemen) invasions of the littoral Caspian region in the 9th century record Deylamites as non-Muslim. These chronicles also show that the Deylamites were the only warriors in the Caspian region who could fight the fearsome Varangian Vikings as equals. Deylamite mercenaries served as far away as [Egypt](8087628.md), [al-Andalus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Andalus), and in the [Khazar Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khazars).

The [Buyids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buyids) established the most successful of the Deylamite dynasties of Iran.

In the 9th–11th century AD, there were repeated military raids undertaken by the Rus' between 864 and 1041 on the [Caspian Sea](19653787.md) shores of Iran, Azerbaijan, and Dagestan as part of the [Caspian expeditions of the Rus'](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_expeditions_of_the_Rus').[^cite_note-Logan201-23] Initially, the Rus' appeared in [Serkland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serkland) in the 9th century traveling as merchants along the [Volga trade route](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volga_trade_route), selling furs, honey, and slaves. The first small-scale raids took place in the late 9th and early 10th century. The Rus' undertook the first large-scale expedition in 913; having arrived on 500 ships, they pillaged the westernmost parts of [Gorgan](84622.md) as well as Gilan and [Mazandaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran_Province), taking slaves and goods.

The [Turkish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkic_peoples) invasions of the 10th and 11th centuries CE, which saw the rise of [Ghaznavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghaznavid) and [Seljuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Seljuq_Turks) dynasties, put an end to Deylamite states in Iran. From the 11th century CE to the rise of [Safavids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavids), Gilan was ruled by local rulers who paid tribute to the dominant power south of the [Alborz range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz) but ruled independently.

In 1307 the [Ilkhan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilkhan) [Öljeitü](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96ljeit%C3%BC) conquered the region.[^cite_note-24] This was the first time the region came under the rule of the [Mongols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols) after the Ilkhanid Mongols and their [Georgian allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Georgia) failed to do it in the late 1270s.[^cite_note-25] After 1336, the region seemed to be independent again.

Before the introduction of silk production (date unknown but a pillar of the economy by the 15th century AD), Gilan was a poor province. There were no permanent trade routes linking Gilan to Persia. There was a small trade in smoked fish and wood products. It seems that the city of [Qazvin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qazvin) was initially a fortress-town against marauding bands of Deylamites, another sign that the economy of the province did not produce enough on its own to support its population. This changed with the introduction of the silk worm in the late Middle Ages.

### Early modern and modern history

Gilan recognized twice, for brief periods, the [suzerainty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suzerainty) of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) without rendering [tribute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribute) to the [Sublime Porte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sublime_Porte), in 1534 and 1591.[^cite_note-26]

The [Safavid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safavid) emperor, [Shah Abbas I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah_Abbas_I) ended the rule of [Khan Ahmad Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khan_Ahmad_Khan) (the last semi-independent ruler of Gilan) and annexed the province directly to his empire. From this point onward, rulers of Gilan were appointed by the Persian [Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah). In the Safavid era, Gilan was settled by large numbers of [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_people), [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians), [Armenians](387816.md), and other [peoples of the Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peoples_of_the_Caucasus) whose descendants still live or linger across Gilan. Most of these [Georgians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgians) and [Circassians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circassians) are assimilated into the mainstream Gilaks. The history of Georgian settlement is described by [Iskandar Beg Munshi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iskandar_Beg_Munshi), the author of the 17th century *[Tarikh-e Alam-Ara-ye Abbasi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Alam_Aray_Abbasi)*, and the Circassian settlements by [Pietro Della Valle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pietro_Della_Valle), among other authors.[^cite_note-27]

The Safavid empire became weak towards the end of the 17th century CE. By the early 18th century, the once-mighty empire was in the grips of civil war and uprisings. The ambitious [Peter I of Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peter_I_of_Russia) (Peter the Great) sent a force that captured Gilan and many of the Iranian territories in the [North Caucasus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Caucasus), [Transcaucasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transcaucasia), as well as other territories in northern mainland Iran, through the [Russo-Persian War (1722–1723)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1722%E2%80%931723\)) and the resulting [Treaty of Saint Petersburg (1723)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint_Petersburg_\(1723\)).[^cite_note-28] Gilan and its capital of Rasht, [which was conquered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capture_of_Rasht) between late 1722 and late March 1723, stayed in [Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_Russia) possession for about ten years.[^cite_note-29]



Map of the Province of Gilan in Qajar Iran.

[Qajars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) established a central government in Persia (Iran) in the late 18th century CE. They lost a series of wars to Russia (Russo-Persian Wars [1804–1813](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1804%E2%80%931813\)) and [1826–28](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_War_\(1826%E2%80%931828\))), resulting in an enormous gain of influence by the Russian Empire in the [Caspian region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_region), which would last up to [1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_crisis_of_1946). The Gilanian cities of [Rasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasht) and [Anzali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Anzali) were all but occupied and settled by [Russians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russians_in_Iran) and Russian forces. Most major cities in the region had Russian schools and significant traces of [Russian culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_culture) can be found today in Rasht. Russian class was mandatory in schools and the significant increase of Russian influence in the region lasted until [1946](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran_crisis_of_1946) and had a major impact on Iranian history, as it directly led to the [Persian Constitutional Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Constitutional_Revolution).

Gilan was a major producer of silk beginning in the 15th century CE. As a result, it was one of the wealthiest provinces in Iran. Safavid annexation in the 16th century was at least partially motivated by this revenue stream. The silk trade, though not the production, was a monopoly of the Crown and the single most important source of trade revenue for the imperial treasury. As early as the 16th century and until the mid 19th century, Gilan was the major exporter of silk in Asia. The [Shah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shah) farmed out this trade to [Greek](42056.md) and [Armenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armenian_people) merchants and, in return, received a handsome portion of the proceeds.

In the mid-19th century, a fatal epidemic among the silk worms paralyzed Gilan's economy, causing widespread economic distress. Gilan's budding industrialists and merchants were increasingly dissatisfied with the weak and ineffective rule of the [Qajars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty). Re-orientation of Gilan's agriculture and industry from silk to production of [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) and the introduction of [tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tea) plantations were a partial answer to the decline of silk in the province.

After [World War I](4764461.md), Gilan came to be ruled independently of the central government of [Tehran](57654.md) and concern arose that the province might permanently separate. Before the war, [Gilanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilani_people) had played an important role in the [Constitutional Revolution of Iran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitutional_Revolution_of_Iran). [Sepahdar-e Tonekaboni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sepahdar-e_Tonekaboni) (Rashti) was a prominent figure in the early years of the revolution and was instrumental in defeating [Mohammad Ali Shah Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammad_Ali_Shah_Qajar).

In the late 1910s, many Gilanis gathered under the leadership of [Mirza Kuchik Khan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirza_Kuchik_Khan), who became the most prominent revolutionary leader in northern Iran in this period. Khan's movement, known as the [Jangal movement of Gilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jangal_movement_of_Gilan), had sent an armed brigade to Tehran that helped depose the [Qajar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qajar_dynasty) ruler Mohammad Ali Shah. However, the revolution did not progress the way the constitutionalists had strived for, and Iran came to face much internal unrest and foreign intervention, particularly from the British and Russian empires.

During and several years after the [Bolshevik Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution), the region saw another massive influx of Russian settlers (the so-called *[White émigrées](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_%C3%A9migr%C3%A9)*). Many of the descendants of these refugees are in the region. During the same period, Anzali served as the main trading port between Iran and Europe.

The Jangalis are glorified in Iranian history and effectively secured Gilan and [Mazandaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran) against foreign invasions. However, in 1920 British forces invaded [Bandar-e Anzali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Anzali), while being pursued by the [Bolsheviks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolshevik). In the midst of this conflict, the Jangalis entered into an alliance with the Bolsheviks against the British. This culminated in the establishment of the [Persian Socialist Soviet Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Socialist_Soviet_Republic) (commonly known as the Socialist Republic of Gilan), which lasted from June 1920 until September 1921.

In February 1921 the Soviets withdrew their support for the Jangali government of Gilan and signed the [Russo-Persian Treaty of Friendship (1921)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russo-Persian_Treaty_of_Friendship_\(1921\)) with the central government of Tehran. The Jangalis continued to struggle against the central government until their final defeat in September 1921 when control of Gilan returned to Tehran.

## Demographics

### Language and ethnicity

See also: [Gilaki language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilaki_language) and [Talysh language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talysh_language)



Masuleh

Gilaks and Talysh form the majority of the population, while Azerbaijanis, Kurds and Persians are minorities in the province.

[Gilaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilaks) live in most of the cities and villages in the province, except Astara and Talesh counties. They are the majority in most of the counties and the province is named after them.

[Talysh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talysh_people) people live in the northern districts of the province, constituting a majority in the counties of [Astara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astara_County), [Talesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talesh_County), [Rezvanshahr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rezvanshahr_County) and [Masal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masal_County). Overall, in Astara county the Talysh are a slight majority, although they are a significant minority in urban areas like [Astara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astara,_Iran) and [Lavandevil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavandevil). In the other three counties, they are a clear majority.

[Azerbaijanis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Azerbaijanis), Turkic speakers of the province, are concentrated primarily in urban areas of [Astara county](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astara_County), as well as the Haviq and Lisar districts of [Talesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talesh_County) county. Also, there is a sizeable Azerbaijani community in the port city of [Bandar-e Anzali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bandar-e_Anzali).

There are four groups of [Kurds](17068.md) in the province with different origins. Amarlou in [Rasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasht) and [Rudbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rudbar), Reshvand in [Rasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasht), Jalalvand in [Langroud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langroud), and Kormanj in [Hashtpar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashtpar).[^cite_note-iranicaonline.org-30]

Persians are concentrated in the city of [Rasht](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rasht) and are divided into two: the immigrants from [Tehran](57654.md) and other central Iranian cities, and the local people who have adopted the [Persian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language) in favor of their heritage language.[^cite_note-iranicaonline.org-30]

The [Gilaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilaki_language) language is a [Caspian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caspian_languages), and a member of the [northwestern Iranian language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northwestern_Iranian_language) branch, spoken in Iran's Gilan, [Mazandaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran_province) and [Qazvin](448087.md) provinces.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32] Gilaki is the main heritage language in the province of Gilan and is divided into three dialects: Western Gilaki, Eastern Gilaki, and Galeshi (in the mountains of Gilan and Mazandaran).[^cite_note-33] The western and eastern dialects are separated by the Sefid Roud.[^cite_note-34]

Although Gilaki is the most widely spoken heritage language in [Gilan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilan), the [Talysh language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Talysh_language) is also spoken. There are only two counties in Gilan where Talyshi is exclusively spoken: [Masal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masal) and [Masuleh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masuleh). In other cities Talyshi is spoken alongside other languages.

The [Kurdish language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdish_language) is used by Kurds who have moved to the Amarlu region.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38]

[Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_language)[^cite_note-39] is also spoken in the province of Gilan as it is Iran's official language, requiring everyone to know Persian.

### Population

At the time of the 2006 National Census, the province's population was 2,381,063 people in 669,221 households.[^cite_note-2006_Gilan_Province-41] The following census in 2011 counted 2,480,874 in 777,316 households.[^cite_note-2011_Gilan_Province-42] The 2016 census measured the population of the province as 2,530,696 people in 851,382 households.[^cite_note-2016_Gilan_Province-3]

Gilan has a [humid subtropical climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humid_subtropical_climate) with, by a large margin, the heaviest rainfall in Iran: reaching as high as 1,900 millimetres (75 in) in the southwestern coast and generally around 1,400 millimetres (55 in). Rasht, the capital of the province, is known internationally as the "City of Silver Rains" and in Iran as the "City of Rain".

Rainfall is heaviest between September and December because the onshore winds from the [Siberian High](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siberian_High) are strongest, but it occurs throughout the year though least abundantly from April to August. Humidity is very high because of the marshy character of the coastal plains and can reach 90 percent in summer for [wet bulb temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wet_bulb_temperature) of over 26 °C (79 °F). The [Alborz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alborz) range provides further diversity to the land in addition to the Caspian coasts.

The coastline is cooler and attracts large numbers of domestic and international tourists. Large parts of the province are mountainous, green and forested. The coastal plain along the Caspian Sea is similar to that of [Mazandaran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mazandaran) and mainly used for [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) paddies. Due to successive cultivation and selection of rice by farmers, several cultivars including Gerdeh, Hashemi, Hasani, and Gharib have been bred.[^cite_note-pazuki-48]
