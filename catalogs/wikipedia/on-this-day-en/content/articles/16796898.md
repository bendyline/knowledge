**HD 80606 b** (also known as **Struve 1341 Bb** or **HIP 45982 b**) is an [eccentric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eccentric_Jupiter) [hot Jupiter](961349.md) 217 [light-years](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light-year) from the Sun in the [constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constellation) of [Ursa Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ursa_Major). HD 80606 b was discovered orbiting the star [HD 80606](4461932.md) in April 2001 by a team led by [Michel Mayor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michel_Mayor) and [Didier Queloz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Queloz).[^cite_note-eso0114-2] With a mass 4 times that of [Jupiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jupiter), it is a [gas giant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gas_giant). Because the planet [transits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_transit) the host star its radius can be determined using the [transit method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transit_method#Transit_photometry), and was found to be about the same as Jupiter's. Its density is slightly less than Earth's.[^cite_note-fossey2009-6] It has an extremely eccentric orbit like a comet, with its orbit taking it very close to its star and then back out very far away from it every 111 days.[^cite_note-7]

## Discovery

The variable radial velocity of HD 80606 was first noticed in 1999 from observations with the 10-m Keck 1 telescope at the [W. M. Keck Observatory](203314.md) in Hawaii by the G-Dwarf Planet Search, a survey of nearly 1000 nearby G dwarfs to identify extrasolar planet candidates. The star was then followed up by the [Geneva Extrasolar Planet Search](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva_Extrasolar_Planet_Search) team using the [ELODIE spectrograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ELODIE_spectrograph) mounted on the 1.93-m telescope at the [Haute-Provence Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haute-Provence_Observatory). The discovery of HD 80606 b was announced on 4 April 2001.[^cite_note-eso0114-2][^cite_note-Naef2001-1]

The transit was detected using a [Celestron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestron) 35-cm [Schmidt–Cassegrain telescope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schmidt%E2%80%93Cassegrain_telescope) at the [UCL Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UCL_Observatory).[^cite_note-fossey2009-6] Prior to the large data release of the [Kepler Mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kepler_Mission) in February 2011, HD 80606 b had the longest orbital period of any known transiting planet. It takes 12.1 hours to transit its star.

The transit of 14 January 2010 was partially observed by MOST; but there were equipment failures over part of this time, and the 8 January secondary transit was entirely lost.[^cite_note-roberts2012-8] The midpoint of the next transit was 1 February 2013 11\:37 [UT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Universal_Time).[^cite_note-9]

## Physical properties



Orbital motion of HD 80606 b.

HD 80606 b – animation (01\:28) (28 March 2016).

HD 80606 b has the most eccentric orbit of any known planet after [HD 20782 b](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HD_20782_b). Its eccentricity is \~0.9318, comparable to [Halley's Comet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halley's_Comet). The eccentricity may be a result of the [Kozai mechanism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kozai_mechanism), which would occur if the planet's orbit is significantly inclined to that of the binary stars. This interpretation is supported by measurements of the [Rossiter–McLaughlin effect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rossiter%E2%80%93McLaughlin_effect), which indicate that the planet's orbit may be significantly inclined (by 42±8°)[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11] to the rotational axis of the star, a configuration which would be expected if the Kozai mechanism were responsible for the orbit.[^cite_note-moutou2009-12]

As a result of this high eccentricity, the planet's distance from its star varies from 0.03 to 0.88 [AU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomical_unit). At [apastron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apsis) it would receive an [insolation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Insolation) similar to that of Earth, while at periastron the insolation would be around 800 times greater, far more than that experienced by [Mercury](19694.md) in the Solar System. In 2009, the eclipse of HD 80606 b by its parent star was detected, allowing measurements of the planet's temperature to be made as the planet passed through periastron. These measurements indicated that the temperature rose from around 800 K (527 °C; 980 °F) to 1,500 K (1,230 °C; 2,240 °F) in just 6 hours.[^cite_note-laughlin2009-13]

An observer above the cloud tops of the gas giant would see the parent star swell to 30 times the [apparent size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apparent_size) of the Sun in our own sky.[^cite_note-RAS-14]

The planet's [rotation period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rotation_period) has been measured to be 93+85\
−35 hours, longer than the predicted [pseudo-synchronous rotation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pseudo-synchronous_rotation) period of 40 hours.[^cite_note-deWit2016-5]

## Weather

The planet has wild variations in its weather as it orbits its parent star. Computer models predict the planet heats up 555 [K](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelvin) (280 [°C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celsius); 540 [°F](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit)) in just a matter of hours, triggering "[shock wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_wave) storms" that ripple out from the point facing its star, with winds that move at around 5 kilometres per second (3.1 mi/s; 11,000 mph).[^cite_note-laughlin2009-13][^cite_note-hothothot-15]
