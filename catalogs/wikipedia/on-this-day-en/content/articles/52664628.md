The **Russian occupation of Ukraine** is the [military occupation](816214.md) by [Russia](25391.md) of parts of [Ukraine](31750.md) during the [Russo-Ukrainian war](42085878.md). The areas currently under Russian control lie in southern and eastern Ukraine. In Ukrainian law, they are defined as the "temporarily occupied territories". As of 2024, Russia occupies almost 20% of Ukraine and about 3 to 3.5 million Ukrainians are estimated to be living under occupation;[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-SWP_occupation_2024-2] since the invasion, the occupied territories lost roughly half of their population. The [United Nations Human Rights Office](22463.md) reports that Russia is committing severe [human rights](13831.md) violations in occupied Ukraine, including [arbitrary detentions](13435705.md), [enforced disappearances](686148.md), torture, crackdown on peaceful [protest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_protests_in_Russian-occupied_Ukraine) and [freedom of speech](21401843.md), enforced [Russification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification), [passportization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passportization), indoctrination of children, and suppression of Ukrainian language and culture.[^cite_note-UN_report_March_24-3]

The occupation began in 2014 with [Russia's invasion and annexation](42228673.md) of [Crimea](163045.md), and its *de facto* takeover of Ukraine's [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas)[^cite_note-4] during a [war in eastern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)).[^cite_note-5] In 2022, Russia launched a full-scale invasion. However, due to fierce Ukrainian resistance and logistical challenges[^cite_note-6] (*e.g.* the stalled [Russian Kyiv convoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Kyiv_convoy)), the [Russian Armed Forces](25709.md) retreated from northern Ukraine in early April.[^cite_note-7] In September 2022, Ukrainian forces launched the [Kharkiv counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kharkiv_counteroffensive) and liberated most of [that oblast](494901.md).[^cite_note-8] Another [southern counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kherson_counteroffensive) resulted in the [liberation of Kherson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberation_of_Kherson) that November.

On 30 September 2022, Russia announced the [annexation of Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson oblasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_annexation_of_Donetsk,_Kherson,_Luhansk_and_Zaporizhzhia_oblasts), despite only occupying part of the claimed territory. The UN General Assembly passed [a resolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_ES-11/4) rejecting this annexation as illegal and upholding Ukraine's right to territorial integrity.[^cite_note-9]

As of 2024, Ukraine's [peace terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_negotiations_in_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine#Overview_of_key_negotiation_points) call for Russian forces to leave the occupied territories. Russia's terms call for it to keep all the land it occupies, and be given all of the oblasts that it claims but does not fully control.[^cite_note-Putin_terms-10] Several Western-based analysts say that allowing Russia to keep the land it seized would "reward the aggressor while punishing the victim" and encourage further [Russian expansionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_neo-imperialism).[^cite_note-Chatham_end-11][^cite_note-Council_on_Foreign_Relations-12]

## Background

Main article: [Russian irredentism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_irredentism)

See also: [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan) and [Revolution of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity)



Pro-[EU](9317.md) demonstration in Kyiv, 27 November 2013, during the [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan) protests

With the [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan) and [Revolution of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity) since November 2013, popular protests across [Ukraine](31750.md) led to the dismissal of pro-Russian Ukrainian president [Viktor Yanukovych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viktor_Yanukovych) by the Verkhovna Rada (Ukraine's parliament), as he fled to Russia.[^cite_note-13] The growing pro-European sentiment at the center of this period of upheaval caused unease in [the Kremlin](9787373.md), and [Russian president](239646.md) [Vladimir Putin](32817.md) immediately mobilized Russian army and airborne forces to invade [Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Republic_of_Crimea), and they swiftly took control of major government buildings and blockaded the Ukrainian military in their bases across the [peninsula](163045.md).[^cite_note-14] Soon after, Russian-installed officials announced and carried out a [referendum for the region to join Russia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Crimean_status_referendum), which western and independent organizations labeled as illegitimate.[^cite_note-15] The Kremlin rejected these claims and soon officially [annexed Crimea into Russia](42228673.md), with western nations issuing [sanctions against Russia in response](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War#First_round:_March/April_2014).[^cite_note-16] In addition, with [pro-Russian counter-protests across Eastern and Southern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_pro-Russian_unrest_in_Ukraine) in response to the [ousting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_of_Dignity) of Yanukovych,[^cite_note-17] Russia allegedly supported Russian and pro-Russian [militant separatists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_separatist_forces_in_Donbas) in the [Donbas region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) in taking control of major government buildings.[^cite_note-18] These separatists eventually created the [Donetsk](42425747.md) and [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic) People's Republics,[^cite_note-19] and have since been at conflict with the now-pro-European Ukrainian government, known as the [war in Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/War_in_Donbas_\(2014%E2%80%932022\)) (Russia announced their "annexation" after the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine)).

In response to [Russian military intervention](42085878.md), the [Parliament of Ukraine](844993.md) adopted government laws (with further updates and extensions) to qualify the [Autonomous Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Republic_of_Crimea) and parts of [Donetsk](494889.md) and [Luhansk](494904.md) regions as temporarily occupied and uncontrolled territories:

1. [Autonomous Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_Republic_of_Crimea):&#x20;

   - Law of Ukraine No. 1207-VII (15 April 2014) "Assurance of Citizens' Rights and Freedom, and Legal Regulations on Temporarily Occupied Territory of Ukraine".

     [^cite_note-20]

2. Separate [Raions](6719452.md) of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts:&#x20;

   - Order of the Cabinet of Ministers of Ukraine No. 1085-р (7 November 2014) "A List of Settlements on Territory Temporarily Uncontrolled by Government Authorities, and a List of Landmarks Located at the Contact Line".

     [^cite_note-21]

   - Law of Ukraine No. 254-19-VIII (17 March 2015) "On Recognition of Separate Raions, Cities, Towns and Villages in Donetsk and Luhansk Regions as Temporarily Occupied Territories".

     [^cite_note-22]

[Petro Poroshenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petro_Poroshenko), one of the opposition leaders during [Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euromaidan), won a landslide victory in the [election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Ukrainian_presidential_election) to succeed interim president Turchynov, three months after the ousting of Yanukovych.[^cite_note-23]

## Before February 2022

Further information: [Russian occupation of Crimea](71944314.md), [Russian occupation of Donetsk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_occupation_of_Donetsk_Oblast), and [Russian occupation of Luhansk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_occupation_of_Luhansk_Oblast)

[Ukraine](31750.md) (2014 – February 2022), with the annexed [Crimea](163045.md) at bottom and [two self-proclaimed separatist republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_separatist_forces_in_Donbas) in [Donbas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donbas) at right

Since [Russia annexed Crimea in March 2014](42228673.md), it administers the peninsula under two [federal subjects](701438.md): the [Republic of Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_Crimea_\(Russia\)) and the [federal city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Federal_cities_of_Russia) of [Sevastopol](51587.md). Ukraine continues to claim the peninsula as an integral part of its territory, which is [supported by most foreign governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_reactions_to_the_annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) through the [United Nations General Assembly Resolution 68/262](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_68/262),[^cite_note-49] even though Russia and [some other UN member states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_status_of_Crimea#Others) have expressed support for the 2014 Crimean referendum, implying recognition of [Crimea](163045.md) as part of the Russian Federation. In 2015, the [Ukrainian parliament](844993.md) officially set 20 February 2014 as the date of "the beginning of the temporary occupation of Crimea and Sevastopol by Russia".[^cite_note-50]

The uncontrolled portions of the [Donetsk](42425747.md) and [Luhansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luhansk_People's_Republic) Oblasts are commonly abbreviated as "**ORDLO**" from [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language), especially among Ukrainian news media. ("certain areas of Donetsk and Luhansk Oblasts", [Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Окремі райони Донецької та Луганської областей, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Ukrainian): *Okremi raiony Donetskoi ta Luhanskoi oblastei*)[^cite_note-51] The term first appeared in Law of Ukraine No.1680-VII (October 2014).[^cite_note-52] Documents of the [Minsk Protocol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk_agreements) and the OSCE refer to them as "certain areas of Donetsk and Luhansk regions" (CADLR) of Ukraine.[^cite_note-53]

The [Ministry of Reintegration of Temporarily Occupied Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Reintegration_of_Temporarily_Occupied_Territories) is the Ukrainian government ministry that oversees government policy towards the regions.[^cite_note-NMC20416-54] As of 2019, the government considered 7% of Ukraine's territory to be under occupation.[^cite_note-55] The [United Nations General Assembly resolution 73/194](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_resolution_73/194), adopted on 17 December 2018, designated Crimea as under "temporary occupation".[^cite_note-A73-56]

The Ukrainian army was concerned in 2019 about the deployment of [3M-54 Kalibr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kalibr_\(missile_family\)) cruise missiles on Russian naval and coast guard vessels operating in the [Sea of Azov](78019.md), which is adjacent to the temporarily occupied territories. As a result, [Mariupol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mariupol) and [Berdiansk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berdiansk), two main [Pryazovian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pryazovia) seaports, suffer from an increase in insecurity[^cite_note-ahedm-57] (both cities were captured in 2022).

[Temryuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Temryuk) and [Taganrog](1288065.md), two other ports on the Sea of Azov, have allegedly been used to disguise the provenance of [anthracite coal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anthracite) and [liquefied natural gas](832128.md) (LNG) from the temporarily occupied territories.[^cite_note-ahedm-57]

## Since the 2022 invasion

Main articles: [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), [Southern Ukraine campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Ukraine_campaign), [Eastern Ukraine offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Ukraine_offensive), and [Northern Ukraine campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ukraine_campaign)

See also: [Annexation of Southern and Eastern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Southern_and_Eastern_Ukraine)



Regions of Ukraine annexed by Russia, with a red line marking the area of actual control by Russia on 30 September 2022



2024 United Nations map of Russian-occupied Ukraine in December 2023

After Russia's full-scale invasion in February 2022, the Russian military and [Russian proxy forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_people's_militias_in_Ukraine) further occupied additional Ukrainian territory. By early April, Russian forces [withdrew from Northern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_offensive_\(2022\)), including the capital [Kyiv](585629.md),[^cite_note-69] after stagnating progress amid fierce Ukrainian resistance in order to focus on consolidating control over Eastern and Southern Ukraine. On 2 June 2022, Zelenskyy announced that Russia occupied approximately 20% of Ukrainian territory.[^cite_note-Zelenskiy-38]

Before 2022, Russia occupied 42,000 km<sup>2</sup> (16,000 sq mi) of Ukrainian territory (Crimea, and parts of Donetsk and Luhansk), and occupied a further 119,000 km<sup>2</sup> (46,000 sq mi) after its full-scale invasion by March 2022, a total of 161,000 km<sup>2</sup> (62,000 sq mi) or almost 27% of Ukraine.[^cite_note-cnn-37] By 11 November 2022, the [Institute for the Study of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Study_of_War) calculated that Ukrainian forces had liberated an area of 74,443 km<sup>2</sup> (28,743 sq mi) from Russian occupation,[^cite_note-70] leaving Russia with control of about 18% of Ukraine.[^cite_note-71] During the whole of 2023, Russian forces captured an estimated net 487 km<sup>2</sup> (188 sq mi) of Ukrainian territory.[^cite_note-NY_Times_230928-42]

In 2024, Russian forces captured an estimated 4,168 km<sup>2</sup> (1,609 sq mi) in both Ukraine and Russia's [Kursk Oblast](480916.md), where Ukraine had previously [launched a cross-border offensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kursk_offensive_\(2024%E2%80%93present\)). The majority of the Russian advances took place in the months of September, October, and November 2024.[^cite_note-72]

As of 2024, Ukraine's [peace terms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peace_negotiations_in_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine) include Russia withdrawing its troops from the occupied territories. Russia's terms include Russia keeping all the land it occupies, and being given all of the oblasts that it claims but does not fully control.[^cite_note-Putin_terms-10]

Several Western-based analysts say that allowing Russia to keep the land it seized would "reward the aggressor while punishing the victim" and set a dangerous precedent.[^cite_note-Chatham_end-11] They predict that this would encourage Russia "to continue its [imperialist campaign of expansionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_neo-imperialism)" against Ukraine and its other neighbors, and embolden other expansionist regimes.[^cite_note-Chatham_end-11][^cite_note-Council_on_Foreign_Relations-12][^cite_note-73][^cite_note-74][^cite_note-75] Zelenskyy commented: "It's the same thing [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler) did, when he said '[give me a part of](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Munich_Agreement) [Czechoslovakia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Czechoslovakia) and it'll end here'."[^cite_note-76] Leo Litra of the [European Council on Foreign Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Council_on_Foreign_Relations) pointed out that allowing Russia to [annex Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation) in 2014 did not stop further Russian aggression. Opinion polls show that the majority of Ukrainians oppose giving up any of their country for peace.[^cite_note-77]

### Kharkiv Oblast

Further information: [Russian occupation of Kharkiv Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_occupation_of_Kharkiv_Oblast)

The occupation began on 24 February 2022, immediately after Russian troops invaded Ukraine and began seizing parts of the Kharkiv Oblast. Since April, Russian forces tried to consolidate control in the region and capture the major city of [Kharkiv](2788558.md) after their [withdrawal from Northern Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_offensive_\(2022\)). However, by mid-May, the Ukrainian forces pushed the Russians back towards the periphery of the Russian border,[^cite_note-78] indicating that Ukrainians continue to garner stiff resistance against Russian advances. In early September 2022, Ukrainian forces began a major [counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Kharkiv_counteroffensive) and by 11 September 2022, Russia had retreated from most of the settlements it previously occupied in the oblast,[^cite_note-79] and the Russian Ministry of Defense announced a formal withdrawal of Russian forces from nearly all of Kharkiv Oblast stating that an "operation to 'curtail and transfer troops' was underway."[^cite_note-meduza20220911-80][^cite_note-81]

## Occupation administration and policies

Main article: [Russian temporary administrative agencies in occupied Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_temporary_administrative_agencies_in_occupied_Ukraine)

Following the [annexation of Crimea in 2014](42228673.md), Russia imposed Russian citizenship, laws and institutions across the peninsula.[^cite_note-103] Following the 2022 invasion, Russian occupation authorities similarly replaced or subordinated Ukrainian systems of governance, administration, justice and education in newly occupied areas, while imposing Russian laws, courts and administrative systems.[^cite_note-UN_report_March_24-3]

Russia established occupation administrations in Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson oblasts and, following its [claimed annexation of the four regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_annexation_of_Donetsk,_Kherson,_Luhansk_and_Zaporizhzhia_oblasts), sought to integrate them into the Russian political and administrative system. Russian regional and local elections were extended to the occupied territories in September 2023. Economic integration has included requiring businesses to register under Russian law, introducing Russian banking and social-security systems, infrastructure programmes, and a system under which regions of Russia are assigned patronage over occupied Ukrainian cities and districts.[^cite_note-SWP_occupation_2024-2]

## Violations and war crimes

The [United Nations Human Rights Office](22463.md) reports that Russia is committing severe [human rights](13831.md) violations in occupied Ukraine. These include [arbitrary detentions](13435705.md), [torture](47702.md), [looting](209811.md), and [enforced disappearances](686148.md) by Russian soldiers acting with "impunity". Peaceful protests and [freedom of speech](21401843.md) have been suppressed, while freedom of movement is severely restricted.[^cite_note-UN_report_March_24-3] Anyone suspected of opposing the occupation has been targeted, while people have been "encouraged to inform on one another, leaving them afraid even of their own friends and neighbours".[^cite_note-UN_report_March_24-3] OHCHR later found Russia guilty of enforced disppearances and torture as [crimes against humanity](61099.md), as well as arbitrary arrest and detention. [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) also found Russia guilty of forcible conscription of people under occupation to force them to fight against their own country, [deportation](412990.md) and [forced displacement](477535.md).[^cite_note-104]

Ukrainians have been [coerced into taking Russian passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passportization) and becoming Russian citizens. Those who refuse are denied healthcare, freedom of movement, public sector employment and social security benefits.[^cite_note-UN_report_March_24-3] From July 2024, anyone in occupied Ukraine who does not have a Russian passport can be imprisoned as a "foreign citizen". Ukrainian men who take a Russian passport are then drafted to fight against the Ukrainian army.[^cite_note-105]

The UN reports that Ukrainian children are the worst affected. Schools are forced to teach the Russian curriculum, with textbooks that seek to justify the invasion.[^cite_note-UN_report_March_24-3] Children are also enlisted into youth groups that indoctrinate them with Russian nationalism.[^cite_note-UN_report_March_24-3] There are reports of parents who refuse Russian passports having their children taken away from them.[^cite_note-106] The [Parliamentary Assembly of the Council of Europe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliamentary_Assembly_of_the_Council_of_Europe) recognized Russia's [abduction and deportation of Ukrainian children](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Child_abductions_in_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War) as genocidal.[^cite_note-ombud-107]

Ukrainian language and media has been replaced by Russian language and media.[^cite_note-UN_report_March_24-3]

Russia has been accused of [neo-colonialism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neocolonialism#Russia) and colonization in Crimea by enforced [Russification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russification), [passportization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passportization), and by settling Russian citizens on the peninsula and forcing out Ukrainians and [Crimean Tatars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_Tatars).[^cite_note-108]

### Suppression of Ukrainian culture

Further information: [Ukrainian culture during the Russian invasion of Ukraine](70287102.md)

[United Nations special rapporteurs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_special_rapporteur) have condemned the Russian occupation authorities for attempting "to erase local \[Ukrainian] culture, history, and language" and to forcibly replace it with Russian language and culture. Monuments and places of worship have been razed, while Ukrainian history books and literature deemed to be "extremist" have been seized from public libraries and destroyed. Civil servants and teachers have been detained for their refusal to implement Russian policy.[^cite_note-109] The [International Court of Justice](14918.md) ruled that Russia had broken the [Convention on the Elimination of Racial Discrimination](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Convention_on_the_Elimination_of_All_Forms_of_Racial_Discrimination) by restricting school classes in the Ukrainian language in occupied Crimea.[^cite_note-110]

## Resistance

Main articles: [Ukrainian resistance in Russian-occupied Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_resistance_in_Russian-occupied_Ukraine) and [2022 protests in Russian-occupied Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_protests_in_Russian-occupied_Ukraine)

During the first months of the 2022 invasion, residents of a number of newly occupied cities held public demonstrations against the Russian occupation. Open protest subsequently became increasingly difficult as Russian authorities detained and abused people suspected of opposing the occupation.[^cite_note-UN_report_March_24-3] By late 2022, open protest had largely given way to more clandestine forms of resistance.[^cite_note-RUSI_resistance_2023-111]

Clandestine resistance has included gathering and passing intelligence to Ukrainian forces, pro-Ukrainian messaging, and attacks on Russian military targets and occupation officials. A 2023 analysis by the [Royal United Services Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_United_Services_Institute) described intelligence gathering and assistance with the targeting of Russian forces as the principal function of resistance networks, with direct action playing a more limited role.[^cite_note-RUSI_resistance_2023-111] A 2025 study of resistance networks documented acts of sabotage, ambushes and attacks on occupation officials, particularly in occupied parts of Zaporizhzhia and Kherson oblasts and in Crimea.[^cite_note-112]

## Collaboration

Main article: [Collaboration with Russia during the Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Collaboration_with_Russia_during_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine)

Russian occupation authorities have relied on local collaborators as well as officials and other personnel sent from Russia. At the same time, the UN Human Rights Office has documented cases in which Russian forces used threats, detention and violence to coerce Ukrainian public-sector workers into cooperating with occupation authorities.[^cite_note-UN_report_March_24-3][^cite_note-SWP_occupation_2024-2]

Following the liberation of occupied territories, thousands of civilians were accused of collaboration. They are tried by a single judge without a jury. The offense is punished by up to ten years of prison, with some of those convicted getting three or five years of prison. The accused include people who worked as volunteers and held administrative positions during the occupation.[^cite_note-113]

## International reactions

See also: [International reactions to the Euromaidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_reactions_to_the_Euromaidan), [International sanctions during the Russo-Ukrainian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions_during_the_Russo-Ukrainian_War), [International reactions to the annexation of Crimea by the Russian Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_reactions_to_the_annexation_of_Crimea_by_the_Russian_Federation), and [International recognition of the Donetsk People's Republic and the Luhansk People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_recognition_of_the_Donetsk_People's_Republic_and_the_Luhansk_People's_Republic)



On 12 October 2022, the [UN General Assembly](31957.md) adopted [Resolution ES 11/4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_ES-11/4) declaring that the staged referendums and attempted annexation are invalid and illegal under [international law](8195726.md).\

&#x20; Against: 5

On 20 April 2016 Ukraine officially established government Ministry of Temporarily Occupied Territories and Internally Displaced Persons.[^cite_note-NMC20416-54] It was subsequently renamed the Temporarily Occupied Territories, IDPs and veterans and then the [Ministry of Reintegration of Temporarily Occupied Territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Reintegration_of_Temporarily_Occupied_Territories). The current minister is [Iryna Vereshchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iryna_Vereshchuk), appointed on 4 November 2021.[^cite_note-114]

In March 2014, in a vote at the United Nations, 100 member states out of 193[^cite_note-115] did not recognize the [annexation](18950961.md) of the [Crimea](163045.md) by Russia, with only Armenia, Belarus, Bolivia, Cuba, Nicaragua, North Korea, Russia, Sudan, Syria, Venezuela, Zimbabwe voting against the resolution[^cite_note-116] (see [United Nations General Assembly Resolution 68/262](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_68/262)).

The United Nations passed three resolutions regarding the issue of "human rights in the Autonomous Republic of Crimea and the city of Sevastopol", first in December 2016,[^cite_note-117] then again a year later in December 2017,[^cite_note-118] and lastly yet another in December 2018.

The UN's position according to the [resolution adopted in 2018](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_resolution_73/194):

> Condemning the ongoing temporary occupation of part of the territory of Ukraine, namely, the Autonomous Republic of Crimea and the city of Sevastopol (hereinafter referred to as "Crimea"), by the Russian Federation, and reaffirming the non-recognition of its annexation[^cite_note-A73-56]

In April 2018, PACE's emergency assembly recognized occupied regions of Ukraine as "territories under effective control by the Russian Federation".[^cite_note-119][^cite_note-120] Chairman of the Ukrainian delegation to PACE, MP Volodymyr Aryev mentioned that recognition of the fact that part of the occupied Donbas is under Russia's control is so important for Ukraine. "The responsibility for all the crimes committed in the uncontrolled territories is removed from Ukraine. Russia becomes responsible", Aryev wrote on Facebook.[^cite_note-121]

In early March 2022, in response to Russia's invasion, the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md) convened an emergency special session to discuss the latest developments regarding the peace situation in Ukraine, and adopted the [United Nations General Assembly Resolution ES-11/1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_ES-11/1) to condemn Russia's invasion and [Belarus's involvement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belarusian_involvement_in_the_Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-122]
