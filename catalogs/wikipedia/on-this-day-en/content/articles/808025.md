**Westdeutscher Rundfunk Köln** (pronounced [\[ˈvɛstˌdɔʏtʃɐ ˈʁʊntfʊŋk ˈkœln\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German); "West German Broadcasting Cologne"), shortened to **WDR** (pronounced [\[ˌveːdeːˈʔɛʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/2c/De-WDR.ogg/De-WDR.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/2c/De-WDR.ogg/De-WDR.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), is a German [public-broadcasting](178808.md) institution based in the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/States_of_Germany) of [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md) with its main office in [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne). WDR is a constituent member of the consortium of German public-broadcasting institutions, [ARD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARD_\(broadcaster\)). As well as contributing to the output of the national television channel [Das Erste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Das_Erste), WDR produces the regional television service [WDR Fernsehen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDR_Fernsehen) (formerly known as WDF and West3) and six regional radio networks.

## History

### Origins

The [Westdeutsche Funkstunde AG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westdeutsche_Funkstunde_AG?action=edit\&redlink=1) (*WEFAG*) was established on 15 September 1924.

There was a substantial purge of left wing staff following the National-Socialist seizure of power in 1933. This included [Ernst Hardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ernst_Hardt), [Hans Stein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Stein), and [Walter Stern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_Stern_\(art_critic\)).



One of WDR's buildings in Cologne

WDR was created in 1955, when [Nordwestdeutscher Rundfunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordwestdeutscher_Rundfunk) (NWDR) was split into [Norddeutscher Rundfunk](2917610.md) (NDR) – covering [Lower Saxony](18435.md), Schleswig-Holstein, and Hamburg – and Westdeutscher Rundfunk, responsible for North Rhine-Westphalia, [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany). WDR began broadcasting on two radio networks (one produced jointly with NDR) on 1 January 1956. WDR constitutes the most prominent example of regional broadcasting in Germany.[^cite_note-1]

## Funding

WDR is in part funded by the limited sale of on-air commercial advertising time; however, its principal source of income is the revenue derived from viewer and listener [licence fees](48551.md). As of 2023 the monthly fee due from each household for radio and television reception was €18.36.[^cite_note-5] These fees are collected not directly by WDR but by a [joint agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beitragsservice_von_ARD,_ZDF_und_Deutschlandradio) of [ARD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARD_\(broadcaster\)) (and its member institutions), [ZDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZDF), and [Deutschlandradio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutschlandradio).

WDR began its regional television service, Westdeutsches Fernsehen (WDF), on 17 December 1965. On 27 August 1967, when West Germany broadcast its first color TV program, WDF used a live broadcast originating from a Bosch [outside broadcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outside_broadcast) van to start broadcasting in color. In 1988, the channel was renamed West 3; since 1994, it has been known as [WDR Fernsehen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDR_Fernsehen).

While the programmes are mainly run from their [Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) headquarters, they also have a number of sub-regional studios contributing to a regular broadcast called *Lokalzeit* with the opt-outs "aus Aachen" ([Aachen](1520.md)), "OWL" ([Bielefeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bielefeld)), "aus Bonn" ([Bonn](3295.md)), "aus Dortmund" ([Dortmund](149349.md)), "aus Düsseldorf" ([Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf)), "aus Duisburg" ([Duisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duisburg)), "Ruhr" ([Essen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Essen)), "aus Köln" ([Cologne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cologne) and [Bonn](3295.md)), "Münsterland" ([Münster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCnster_\(Westfalen\))), "Südwestfalen" ([Siegen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siegen)) and "Bergisches Land" ([Wuppertal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuppertal)) for each respective region. WDR has its current affairs and regional politics studios in Düsseldorf.

It has served as the production entity for shows on Das Erste, such as *[Verbotene Liebe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verbotene_Liebe)* ('Forbidden Love'), which, over the years, has introduced many young actors to the German audience, such as [Andreas Stenschke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Stenschke), [Jo Weil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jo_Weil), Luca Zamperoni and Kay Böger. The *[Sportschau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportschau)* is produced for ARD in Cologne, and WDR contributes to [ARD Digital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARD_Digital), *3sat* and *arte*.

*[Hallo Ü-Wagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallo_%C3%9C-Wagen)* with [Carmen Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen_Thomas) in 1982

A long-running talk show on wheels was *[Hallo Ü-Wagen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hallo_%C3%9C-Wagen)* ("Hello Radio Van"), which ran from 1974 to 2010 with [Carmen Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmen_Thomas) as the original host.

WDR's main radio channels are available on FM and digital (DAB+), as well as via cable and satellite:

- [1LIVE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1LIVE) is a popular music channel modelled on [BBC Radio 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BBC_Radio_1) and aimed at a young audience. Its schedules include such non-mainstream night-time programmes as *Heimatkult*, focusing on pop music from Germany, and *Lauschangriff*, a series of audio-books.

- [WDR 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDR_2), featuring adult-oriented popular music, focuses strongly on national and regional news, current affairs, and sport.

- [WDR 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDR_3), the cultural channel, offers mostly classical, jazz and world music as well as [radio drama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_drama) and spoken-word features dealing with literature and the performing arts.

- [WDR 4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDR_4) (motto: *Melodien für ein gutes Gefühl*, "Melodies for a good feeling") is a channel aimed chiefly towards an older audience. Its focus is on tuneful music – in particular, [oldies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oldies) and [classic hits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classic_hits): popular music of the 1960s to the 1980s or later – with more specialized programming (operetta, country, folk) in the evenings. Around 30–40% of WDR 4's musical output is made up of [German-language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German-language) songs.

- [WDR 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDR_5) features spoken-word programming with the focus on present-day culture and society. Between 6.05 and 9.45 each Monday to Saturday morning the channel offers news, background briefing, interviews, and correspondents' reports in a sequence entitled *Morgenecho*. The main lunchtime and early-evening news and current affairs programmes *Mittagsecho* (at 13.05–14.00 on Mondays to Fridays and 13.30–14.30 on Sundays) and *Echo des Tages* (at 18.30–19.00 daily) are both co-productions with [Norddeutscher Rundfunk](2917610.md) (NDR) in [Hamburg](13467.md). Additionally, WDR 2's 30-minute round-up of the day's most important news reports, *Berichte von heute*, is [simulcast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulcast) by WDR 5 on Monday to Friday evenings at 23.30. WDR 5 also carries children's radio programming from [KiRaKa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KiRaKa) at 19.05–20.00 each evening as well as on Sundays at 7.05–8.00 and 14.05–15.00.

- [COSMO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/COSMO_\(German_radio_station\)) (earlier WDR 5 Funkhaus Europa – an offshoot of WDR 5 – and now a joint production of [Radio Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Bremen), [RBB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rundfunk_Berlin-Brandenburg), and WDR) is a channel principally aimed at serving immigrants and promoting integration. It features a wide selection of [world music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_music). It is not available over-the-air in every part of WDR's broadcasting area.

Broadcast only via DAB (Digital Radio) as well as DVB-S and partly DVB-C:

- [1LIVE diggi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1LIVE_diggi) – off-mainstream music and news from 1LIVE

- [WDR Event](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDR_Event) – program of events for special occasions

- WDR Maus – radio for children aged 5 to 16

Former radio channels are:

- [WDR 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDR_1) – Predecessor of 1 Live

- [WDR 2 Klassik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDR_2_Klassik) – Contributions from WDR 2 with classical music, briefly replaced by the online service Mein WDR Radio after it was discontinued

- [1LIVE Kunst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1LIVE_Kunst) – web radio with cultural themes and sophisticated pop music

- [Radio Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Dortmund?action=edit\&redlink=1) – The first local radio station in North Rhine-Westphalia, which was started in 1985 as part of a cable pilot project. Radio Dortmund ran for nine years until WDR needed the frequency for a new WDR 2 "regional window".

- [KiRaKa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KiRaKa) – web radio for children aged 5 to 16

- WDR Info – WDR data and news service with visual information

- [WDR VERA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WDR_VERA) – full-length traffic news spoken non-stop by a computer
