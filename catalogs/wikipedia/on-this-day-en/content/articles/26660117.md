# Papal travel



Pope Francis at the [Western Wall](75225.md) in [Jerusalem](16043.md), 2014

**Papal travel** outside [Rome](25458.md) has been historically rare, and voluntary travel of the [pope](23056.md) was non-existent for the first 500 years of the Papacy. [Pope John Paul II](23805.md) (1978–2005) undertook more pastoral trips than all his predecessors combined. [Pope Francis](1687680.md) (2013–2025), [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) (1963–1978) and [Pope Benedict XVI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_XVI) (2005–2013) also travelled globally, the latter to a lesser extent due to his advanced age. The current pope, [Leo XIV](54520372.md), has traveled internationally as well.&#x20;

Popes resided outside Rome—primarily in [Viterbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viterbo_Papacy), [Orvieto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orvieto_Papacy), and [Perugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perugia_Papacy)—during the 13th century, and then moved to France during the [Avignon Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon_Papacy) (1309–1378). [Pope Vigilius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Vigilius) (537–555) in 547, [Pope Agatho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Agatho) (678–681) in 680, and [Pope Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Constantine) in 710 visited [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople), whereas [Pope Martin I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Martin_I) (649–655) was abducted there for trial in 653. [Pope Stephen II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Stephen_II) (752–757) became the first pope to cross the [Alps](981.md) in 752 to crown [Pepin the Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepin_the_Short); [Pope Pius VII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_VII) repeated the feat over a millennium later for the [coronation of Napoleon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_Napoleon).&#x20;

## Travel before the Second Vatican Council

### Outside Rome, within Italy



[Pope Clement I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_I), the first pope recorded to have left Rome as pope

[Pope Clement I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_I) was exiled to [Chersonesos Taurica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chersonesos_Taurica) by Roman emperor [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan) and then [martyred](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyr) into the [Black Sea](3386.md), according to apocryphal accounts circa 99. [Pope Pontian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pontian) (230–235) died in exile in [Sardinia](29376.md), but resigned his pontificate before leaving the city. [Pope Cornelius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Cornelius) (251–253) died after a year of exile in [Civitavecchia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civitavecchia), 80 km from Rome. [Pope Liberius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Liberius) (352–366) was the first pope to get far from the city as pope when he was exiled to [Beroea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veria) in [Thrace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrace) by Roman Emperor [Constantius II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantius_II).[^cite_note-1] [Pope John I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_I) (523–526) became the first pope to travel willingly outside Rome when he sailed for Constantinople in 523.[^cite_note-2]&#x20;

[Pope Clement II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Clement_II) (1046–1047) was the first pope [consecrated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consecrated) outside Rome.[^cite_note-3] [Pope Urban II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_II) (1088–1099) became the first pope to travel extensively outside Rome.[^cite_note-4] Elected in [Terracina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracina), Urban II held synods in [Amalfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amalfi), [Benevento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benevento), and [Troia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troia,_Apulia). He preached the [First Crusade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Crusade) at the [Council of Clermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Clermont) (1095) in [Clermont-Ferrand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clermont-Ferrand). Prior to this, [Pope Leo IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX) (1049–1054) had been the last pope to cross the Alps for 50 years.[^cite_note-5]&#x20;

Although the [cardinals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cardinal_\(Catholicism\)) have historically gathered at a handful of other locations within Rome and beyond, only [six elections since 1455](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_papal_elections) have been held outside the [Apostolic Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_Palace), Twenty-eight papal elections have been held outside Rome, in: [Terracina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terracina) (1088), [Cluny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cluny) (1119), [Velletri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Velletri) (1181), [Verona](263991.md) (1185), [Ferrara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrara) (Oct. 1187), [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) (Dec. 1187), [Perugia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perugia) (1216, 1264–1265, 1285, 1292–1294, 1304–1305), [Anagni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anagni) (1243), [Naples](55880.md) (1254, 1294), [Viterbo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viterbo) (1261, 1268–1271, July 1276, Aug.–Sept. 1276, 1277, 1281–1282), [Arezzo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arezzo) (Jan. 1276), [Carpentras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carpentras)/[Lyon](8638634.md) (1314–1316), [Avignon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avignon) (1334, 1342, 1352, 1362, 1370), [Konstanz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Konstanz) (1417) and [Venice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venice) (1799–1800).[^cite_note-6]&#x20;

### Outside Italy, within Europe

#### Constantinople

Main article: [Byzantine Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Byzantine_Papacy)

[Pope John I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_I) (523–526) in 523 (as a delegate of [Theodoric the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodoric_the_Great)), [Pope Vigilius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Vigilius) (537–555) in 547 (called by [Justinian I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_I) to account for his refusal to sign on to the canons of the [Council of Chalcedon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Chalcedon)), [Pope Agatho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Agatho) (678–681) in 680 (attending the [Third Council of Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Council_of_Constantinople)), and Pope Constantine in 710 visited Constantinople (called by [Justinian II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justinian_II)), whereas [Pope Martin I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Martin_I) (649–653) was abducted there for trial in 653 following the [Lateran Council of 649](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lateran_Council_of_649). [Constantine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Constantine) was the last pope to visit Constantinople until [Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) in 1967.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]&#x20;

#### France

Main article: [Frankish Papacy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankish_Papacy)

[Pope Stephen II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Stephen_II) (752–757) became the first pope to cross the [Alps](981.md) in 752 to crown [Pepin the Short](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pepin_the_Short). This made him the first pope to visit the Frankish empire.[^cite_note-9] [Pope John VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_John_VIII) (872–882) visited France in 878, and [Pope Leo IX](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Leo_IX) (1049–1054) travelled to France on September 29, 1049.[^cite_note-10] The next pope to enter France was [Pope Urban II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Urban_II) (1088–1099), who stopped at [Valence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valence,_Dr%C3%B4me) and [Le Puy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Le_Puy-en-Velay) on his way to the [Council of Clermont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council_of_Clermont) (1095).[^cite_note-11]&#x20;

Pope Pius VII (1800–1823) was in Paris in 1804 for the [Coronation of Napoleon I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronation_of_Napoleon_I).[^cite_note-12]&#x20;

#### Holy Roman Empire

Main article: [History of the Papacy (1048–1257)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Papacy_\(1048%E2%80%931257\))

[Pope Benedict VIII](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Benedict_VIII) (1012–1024) visited [Bamberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bamberg) on 14 April 1020; no pope had visited the borders of modern Germany for 150 years.[^cite_note-13] Pope Leo IX (1049–1054) also travelled through the modern borders of Germany.[^cite_note-14] Probably the last papal visit to the Holy Roman Empire was in 1782, when [Pope Pius VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Pius_VI) visited [Joseph II, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), in [Vienna](55866.md), and [Munich](19058.md) in [Bavaria](3764.md).&#x20;

## Travel since the 1960s

[Pope Paul VI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Paul_VI) (1963–1978) became the first pope to leave Europe; no pope ever left Europe before the [Second Vatican Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Vatican_Council).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] He was the first to travel by airplane as pope, the first to leave Italy since 1809, and the first to visit [North America](21139.md), [South America](26769.md), [Africa](5334607.md), [Oceania](22621.md), and [Asia](689.md) as pope.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]&#x20;

[Pope John Paul II](23805.md) travelled more often as pope than all his predecessors combined, and as a result he was seen—in person—by more people than anyone else in history.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] He traveled "721,052 miles (1,160,421 km), which is the equivalent of nearly 31 trips around the planet.[^cite_note-25]&#x20;

Beginning with Pope Paul VI, the popes used [Alitalia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alitalia) as their primary airline,[^cite_note-allen-26] flying on charter flights,[^cite_note-27] until Alitalia went out of business in 2021; after then, Italy's [ITA Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITA_Airways) was the main airline used by Pope Francis.[^cite_note-28] Pope John Paul II instituted a tradition of returning to Rome on a [flag carrier](465816.md) airline of the last country visited, when possible.[^cite_note-allen-26]&#x20;
