The **100 metres hurdles**, or **100-meter hurdles**, is a [track and field](194904.md) event run mainly by women (the male counterpart is the [110 metres hurdles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/110_metres_hurdles)). For the race, ten [hurdles](179325.md) of a height of 83.8 centimetres (33 in) are placed along a straight course of 100 metres (109.36 yd). The first hurdle is placed after a run-up of 13 metres from the starting line. The next 9 hurdles are set at a distance of 8.5 metres from each other, and the home stretch from the last hurdle to the finish line is 10.5 metres long. The hurdles are set up so that they will fall over if bumped into by the runner, but weighted so this is disadvantageous. Fallen hurdles do not count against runners provided that they do not run into them on purpose. Like the [100 metres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/100_metres) sprint, the 100 m hurdles begins with athletes in [starting blocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starting_blocks).

The fastest 100 m hurdlers run the distance in well under 13 seconds. The [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_record) set by [Masai Russell](73770605.md) stands at 12.09 seconds.

## History

See also: [Women's 100 metres hurdles world record progression](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_100_metres_hurdles_world_record_progression)



[Cornelia Oschkenat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cornelia_Oschkenat) (nearest camera), Heike Theele, and [Kerstin Knabe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kerstin_Knabe) (1986)

The race started back in the 1830s in England where wooden barriers were placed along a 100-yard stretch. The hurdles event was included as part of the inaugural Women's World Games in 1922, and made its first appearance in the [Olympic Games](22576.md) in 1932 as 80m hurdles.

Starting with the [1972 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Summer_Olympics), the women's race was lengthened to 100m hurdles.[^cite_note-1]

The hurdles sprint race has been run by women since the beginning of women's athletics, just after the end of World War I. The distances and hurdle heights varied widely in the beginning. While the men had zeroed in on the [110 m hurdles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/110_m_hurdles), the [International Women's Sport Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F%C3%A9d%C3%A9ration_Sportive_F%C3%A9minine_Internationale) had registered records for eight different disciplines by 1926 (60 [yards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yards)/75 cm height, 60 yards/61 cm, 65 yards/75 cm, 83 yards/75 cm, 100 yards/75 cm, 100 yards/61 cm, 120 yards/75 cm, 110 metres/75 cm). At the first [Women's World Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_World_Games) in 1922, a 100 m hurdles race was run.

From 1926 until 1968, the distance was 80 metres: women had to clear eight hurdles placed at a distance of 8 metres from each other and a height of 76.2 centimetres (30 in).

Just like with the men's races, until 1935 no more than three hurdles could be knocked over, or the runner was disqualified, and records were only officially registered if the runner had cleared all her hurdles clean.

In 1935, this rule was abandoned, and L-shaped hurdles were introduced that fell over forward easily and greatly reduced the risk of injury to the runner. Hurdles are weighted, so when properly set for the height (for women, closer to the fulcrum of the "L"), they serve as a consistent disadvantage to making contact with the barrier.

The [80 m hurdles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/80_m_hurdles) was on the list of women's sports demanded by the International Women's Sport Federation for the [Olympic Summer Games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olympic_Summer_Games) in 1928, but was not included as an Olympic discipline until 1932. Starting with 1949, the 80 m hurdles was one of the disciplines included in the [women's pentathlon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Women's_pentathlon).

During the 1960s, some experimental races were run over a distance of 100 metres using hurdles with a height of 76.2 centimetres (30 in). During the [1968 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Summer_Olympics), a decision was made to introduce the 100 m hurdles from 1969, using hurdles with a height of 83.8 centimetres (33 in).

The first international event in the 100 m hurdles occurred at the European Athletics Championships, which were won by [Karin Balzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karin_Balzer) of the [GDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany).

The modern 100 m race has an extra two hurdles compared to the 80 m race, which are higher and spaced slightly further apart. The home stretch is shorter by 1.5 m.

Currently, women run 110 m hurdles at the [World Athletics Relays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Athletics_Relays), a mixed team event, which was instituted in [2019](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2019_IAAF_World_Relays_%E2%80%93_Mixed_shuttle_hurdles_relay).

## Masters athletics

A version of the 100 metres hurdles is also used for 50- to 59-year-old men in [Masters athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Masters_athletics). They run the same spacing as women, which coordinates with existing markings on most tracks, but run over 91.4 cm (36 in) hurdles. In the 60-69 age range, the spacings are changed. Women over age 40 and men over age 70 run 80 metre versions with different heights and spacings.[^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3]

## Milestones

100 m hurdles:

- First official time registered with hurdles of reduced height of 76.2 centimetres (30 in): [Pamela Kilborn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pam_Kilborn), [AUS](4689264.md), 26 November 1961

- First official time with hurdles of standard height of 83.8 centimetres (33 in): 15.1 seconds, Connie Pettersson, USA, 28 May 1966

- First official world record: 13.3 seconds, [Karin Balzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karin_Balzer), [GDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), 20 June 1969

- First runner under 13 seconds: 12.9 seconds, [Karin Balzer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karin_Balzer), [GDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), 5 September 1969

- First runner under 12.5 seconds:&#x20;

  - 12.3 seconds, [Annelie Ehrhardt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Annelie_Ehrhardt) [GDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany), 20 July 1973 (last hand timed world record; electronically timed at 12.68 seconds)

  - 12.48 seconds, [Grażyna Rabsztyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gra%C5%BCyna_Rabsztyn), [POL](22936.md), 10 June 1978

- First runner under 12.3 seconds: 12.29 seconds, [Yordanka Donkova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yordanka_Donkova) [BUL](3415.md), 17 August 1986

- First runner under 12.2 seconds: 12.12 seconds, [Tobi Amusan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tobi_Amusan) NGR, 24 July 2022. 12.06 seconds (wind aided) Tobi Amusan NGR, 24 July 2022.
