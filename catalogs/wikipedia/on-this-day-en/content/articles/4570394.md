**Shebekino** ([Russian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_language): Шебе́кино) is a [town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Types_of_inhabited_localities_in_Russia) in [Belgorod Oblast](478857.md), Russia, located on the [Nezhegol River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nezhegol_River), 30 kilometers (19 mi) southeast of [Belgorod](478861.md). Population: 39,680 ([2021 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Russian_census));[^cite_note-2021Census-8] 44,277 ([2010 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2010_Russian_census));[^cite_note-2010Census-2] 45,119 ([2002 census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Russian_census));[^cite_note-PopCensus-9] 44,552 ([1989 Soviet census](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1989_Soviet_census)).[^cite_note-Census1989-10] It is the administrative center, though not part of, [Shebekinsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shebekinsky_District).

In June 2023, during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Shebekino was the site of [ground raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Belgorod_Oblast_incursions) by pro-Ukrainian militias, with residents describing it as "destroyed" after heavy shelling.

## Geography

Shebekino is located at the confluence of the [Koren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koren_\(river\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Korocha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korocha_\(river\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) rivers, 35 kilometres (22 mi) from the oblast center [Belgorod](478861.md). Additionally, the [Nezhegol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nezhegol_\(river\)) river flows through the town, dividing it into two parts.[^cite_note-bel-11] It is located a little over 4 miles (6.4 km) from the [Russia–Ukraine border](36430484.md).[^cite_note-12]

Within the [framework of administrative divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Administrative_divisions), Shebekino serves as the [administrative center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_center) of [Shebekinsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shebekinsky_District),[^cite_note-OKATO2-4] even though it is not a part of it.[^cite_note-OKATO1-3] As an administrative division, it is incorporated separately as the **[town of oblast significance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_federal_subject_significance) of Shebekino**—an administrative unit with the status equal to that of the [districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_Belgorod_Oblast).[^cite_note-Ref16-1] As a [municipal division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subdivisions_of_Russia#Municipal_divisions), the town of oblast significance of Shebekino is incorporated within Shebekinsky Municipal District as **Shebekino Urban Settlement**.[^cite_note-Ref24-5]

## History

### Early history

In 1654, near the site of modern Shebekino, the [Nezhegolsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nezhegolsk?action=edit\&redlink=1) fortress was built, part of the [Belgorod defensive line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belgorod_defensive_line?action=edit\&redlink=1). Since then, the site of Shebekino began to be populated by workers, peasants, and [Ukrainians](145144.md).[^cite_note-bel-11]

Shebekino proper first appears in Russian maps in 1713. The name of the settlement originates from the original owner of the land, Ivan Dmitrievich Shibeko. Shibeko was a soldier who fought in the [Battle of Poltava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Poltava), who bought the land and settled there with his [serfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serf). Shibeko died childless in 1716, leading to protracted ownership disputes over the land.[^cite_note-bel-11]

### Industrial development



Old merchant house, now post office

In 1836, Shebekino was bought by military officer [Alexei Rehbinder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexei_Rehbinder?action=edit\&redlink=1). Rehbinder built several factories in the area, including a sugar factory on the river [Nezhegol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nezhegol_\(river\)), which specifically is credited with beginning the industrial development of Shebekino.[^cite_note-bel-11] In 1890, construction began on a railway line from Shebekino to [Kupiansk](5156384.md).[^cite_note-13] In 1905, Alexei Rehbinder's son Alexander Rehbinder built an electric power plant near the sugar factory, which provided the factory as well as residential houses with electricity.[^cite_note-14]

In 1928, Shebekino became the center of the newly created [Shebekinsky District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shebekinsky_District).[^cite_note-bel-11] It received town/city status in 1938.[^cite_note-led-15] During [World War II](32927.md), the town was located on the frontline between opposing forces and was heavily damaged. It was occupied by [Nazi Germany](21212.md) between 14 June 1942 and 9 February 1943. By the end of the occupation, the industry of the town was reduced to ruins.[^cite_note-bel-11]

In the postwar period, Shebekino developed a chemical industry.[^cite_note-bel-11] On 6 January 1954, Shebekinsky District was transferred to the newly created [Belgorod Oblast](478857.md).[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-bel-11] By 1993, Shebekino had a population of 45,100 inhabitants.[^cite_note-led-15]

### Russian invasion of Ukraine

Further information: [2023 Belgorod Oblast incursions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Belgorod_Oblast_incursions)

On June 1, 2023, during the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), Shebekino was reportedly [hit by shelling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%932023_western_Russia_attacks) from Ukrainian territory.[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Shebekino was described by journalists as effectively becoming a "frontline city", with little-to-no help from Russian authorities. Residents of the town described it as "destroyed" by heavy shelling. Mass evacuations took place.[^cite_note-19] The shelling was the prelude to [a full ground raid into Russian territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Belgorod_Oblast_incursions) by two pro-Ukrainian militias: the [Russian Volunteer Corps](71652852.md) (RVC) and the [Freedom of Russia Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_of_Russia_Legion) (FRL).[^cite_note-20]

According to the [Institute for the Study of War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Institute_for_the_Study_of_War), the FRL and RVC reached Shebekino and [Novaya Tavolzhanka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Novaya_Tavolzhanka) on 1 June. The ISW described the attacks as raids, defining them as "small scale assault operations involving swift entry into hostile territory to secure information, disrupt hostile forces, or destroy installations". Belgorod Oblast Governor [Vyacheslav Gladkov](67064358.md) said residents were being evacuated from the settlements and claimed that all "Ukrainian" forces had withdrawn from Belgorod Oblast by the end of the day.[^cite_note-ISW_June_1-21][^cite_note-ISW_June_4-22]

On 4 June, the FRL posted a video online calling for Governor Gladkov to meet them "to discuss the current situation in the region and the future of Russia," in exchange for releasing two Russian soldiers they had captured. Gladkov said he would be willing to meet the group in Shebekino.[^cite_note-23] The FRL leadership arrived at the checkpoint outside Shebekino at the agreed time, but Gladkov did not, claiming that the POWs had been executed. In response, the FRL said it would send the POWs to Kyiv.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-ISW_June_4-22]

As late as 17 June, there were still reports of anti-government forces sporadically operating in Belgorod Oblast, shelling pro-government positions. The number of evacuations of civilians from border regions was estimated at 6,500, with most of the civilians from Shebekino having left.[^cite_note-RFL_June_17-25]
