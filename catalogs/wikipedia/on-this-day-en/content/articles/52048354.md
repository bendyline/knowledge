**Karel Sadsuitubun Airport** ([IATA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IATA_airport_code): **LUV**, [ICAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ICAO_airport_code): **WAPF**) is a domestic airport located in Ibra, [Southeast Maluku Regency](31153285.md), [Kai Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kai_Islands), [Maluku](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maluku_\(province\)), [Indonesia](14579.md). It serves the town of Langgur, the regency seat, as well as the nearby city of [Tual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tual). The airport is named after [Karel Satsuit Tubun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karel_Satsuit_Tubun) (1928–1965), a police officer from Tual who was killed during the [attempted coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/30_September_Movement) by the [Indonesian Communist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Indonesia) on 30 September 1965 and is recognized as a [National Hero of Indonesia](38552936.md). The airport replaced the former [Dumatubun Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominicus_Dumatubun_Air_Force_Base), which was located in the center of Langgur and is now used exclusively by the [Indonesian Air Force](8842734.md). Karel Sadsuitubun Airport is situated approximately 12 km (7.5 miles) from Langgur and 14 km (8.7 miles) from Tual. It serves as the main gateway to the Kai Islands, with scheduled flights to [Ambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambon,_Maluku), the provincial capital of Maluku, operated by [Lion Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Air) and [Wings Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wings_Air), as well as pioneer routes to Larat and [Timika](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timika), operated by Smart Aviation.

## History

[Dumatubun Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominicus_Dumatubun_Air_Force_Base), the former principal airport of the Kai Islands, which had been in operation since the [Japanese occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_occupation_of_the_Dutch_East_Indies) during [World War II](32927.md), was deemed no longer viable for continued use, primarily because its 1,300-metre runway could not be extended.[^cite_note-\:0-3] Due to its short runway, Dumatubun Airport can only accommodate smaller aircraft such as the [CASA C-212 Aviocar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASA_C-212_Aviocar), [Fokker 27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_F27_Friendship), and [ATR 42](346378.md).[^cite_note-\:1-4] Furthermore, the airport is located in the middle of Langgur’s town center and is surrounded by residential areas, limiting any further expansion. Consequently, the construction of a new, larger airport was proposed. Construction of the new airport began in 2006 on a 5,000 m × 2,000 m site spanning two villages, Ibra and Sathean, located approximately 15 km from Dumatubun Airport.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-\:1-4] After significant delays—mainly due to land clearing issues, particularly disputes over compensation with landowners—the airport was completed in 2013.[^cite_note-\:0-3] The inauguration and commencement of operations at the airport were postponed multiple times due to unresolved land ownership disputes with local residents,[^cite_note-\:2-5] to the extent that landowners and residents blocked access to the airport, claiming that the local government had not fully paid the compensation agreed upon in the original agreement.[^cite_note-6]

The airport began operations on 24 February 2014, marked by the first landing of a [Trigana Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trigana_Air) aircraft.[^cite_note-7] It was finally inaugurated by the then [Minister of Transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Transportation_\(Indonesia\)), [Ignasius Jonan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ignasius_Jonan), on 19 December 2014. The inauguration was held simultaneously with the opening of 20 ports and 10 airports across Indonesia.[^cite_note-8] Following the opening of the new airport, all flights to and from Dumatubun Airport were transferred there, with Dumatubun subsequently serving primarily as an [Indonesian Air Force](8842734.md) base.[^cite_note-\:2-5]

In 2016, an Indonesian Air Force [Boeing 737-200](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737) landed at the airport as part of testing to verify its capability to handle such aircraft, marking the first time a Boeing 737 had landed at the airport.[^cite_note-9] The following year, [Sriwijaya Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sriwijaya_Air) became the first airline to operate a Boeing 737 on a commercial flight to the airport, using a [Boeing 737-500](15103676.md) as part of the launch of the Langgur–[Makassar](389464.md) route.[^cite_note-10] In 2022, [Lion Air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lion_Air) launched the Langgur–[Ambon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambon,_Maluku) route using a [Boeing 737-800](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737_Next_Generation).[^cite_note-11]

## Facilities and development

The airport originally had a runway measuring 1,650 m × 30 m, which initially could only accommodate aircraft such as the [ATR-72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ATR_72). To support larger [narrow-body aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Narrow-body_aircraft), including the [Boeing 737](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_737), [Fokker 100](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fokker_100), and [Airbus A320](164933.md), the runway was extended to 2,100 m in 2015 and further lengthened to 2,350 m later that same year.[^cite_note-\:3-12] The expansion also included widening the apron and constructing an additional taxiway, with the runway extension costing approximately Rp 9 billion and the new taxiway about Rp 11 billion.[^cite_note-\:3-12] Following the expansion, the airport has a single apron measuring 208 m × 124 m and two taxiways, each measuring 132 m × 23 m. There are plans to further extend the runway to 2,500 m in the future.[^cite_note-13] Navigation facilities are also planned to be upgraded to enable night landings and takeoffs at the airport.[^cite_note-14]

On the landside, it features a single terminal building with an area of 3,694 m², including a departure lounge of 876 m² and an arrival lounge of 1,079 m². The terminal is capable of accommodating 50,000 passengers annually.[^cite_note-kemhub-1]
