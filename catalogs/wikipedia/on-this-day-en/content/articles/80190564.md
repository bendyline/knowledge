**Ra'ad Sa'ad** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): رائد سعد‎; 1972 – 13 December 2025) was a Palestinian militant and senior commander in the [Al-Qassam Brigades](1703333.md), the military wing of [Hamas](13913.md). Sa'ad joined Hamas in 2005, where he helped to develop the group's military strategy. While removed from his position as head of operations in 2021, Sa'ad was a major contributor to the initial attacks that lead to the [Gaza war](74998519.md). After an initial failed assassination attempt in 2024, Israeli forces killed Sa'ad in a drone strike the following year.

## Militant career

Sa'ad was born in 1972, and joined Hamas in 2005. He participated in the [First](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Intifada) and [Second Intifadas](71717.md), and was imprisoned by both Israel and the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md).[^cite_note-4] He was appointed the commander of Hamas's [Gaza City](55387.md) brigade in 2005. He was responsible for arms manufacturing and building Hamas's rocket arsenal.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-\:0-6]

He founded the group's "military academy", and helped develop its military strategy, such as cross-border attacks, artillery usage, and [urban warfare](319027.md).[^cite_note-\:0-6] He developed the ["Jericho Wall" invasion](74998317.md) plan of Israel in response to the construction of [an underground barrier](478337.md) on the Gaza border, which intended to deploy almost all of Hamas's 40,000 fighters, although it was dismissed by Hamas as unrealistic.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

Prior to the outbreak of the [Gaza war](74998519.md), Sa'ad was a political rival to Hamas leader [Yahya Sinwar](53195047.md). He backed [Nizar Awadallah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nizar_Awadallah) against Sinwar during the 2021 Gaza political bureau elections, which Awadallah narrowly lost. During the [2021 Israel–Palestine crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Israel%E2%80%93Palestine_crisis), Sa'ad was removed as Hamas's head of operations due to his perceived poor performance, and he was replaced by [Mohammed Sinwar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Sinwar).[^cite_note-\:0-6]

Sa'ad was a part of the "inner circle" of Hamas leaders who planned the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md), which led to the outbreak of the Gaza war. At the beginning of the war, he was widely identified as No. 4 in the Hamas military hierarchy following [Mohammed Deif](92534.md), [Marwan Issa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marwan_Issa), and Yahya Sinwar.[^cite_note-9] By December 2025, he became Hamas's de facto chief of staff after much of its leadership was killed by Israel, with Israeli intelligence officials considering him the second-most senior Hamas military official.[^cite_note-10] He played a large role in developing and adapting to military strategies and doctrines, such as [guerrilla warfare](12720.md), and was also responsible for replacing slain brigade commanders and training their replacements. He was close to Hamas military leader [Izz al-Din al-Haddad](79997859.md), whom he assisted in directing daily military operations.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

## Failed assassination attempt

On 22 June 2024, the [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) executed a major airstrike on [Al‑Shati refugee camp](16296150.md), Gaza City, targeting Sa'ad. At least 42 people were reportedly killed by Israeli attacks in the city.[^cite_note-11] No immediate confirmation of Sa'ad's status emerged. Multiple initial reports suggested he was likely killed,[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] but it was later reported that he survived.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

## Assassination

On 13 December 2025, at 2\:49 pm, an Israeli drone strike targeted Sa'ad's car while he was driving on the Al-Rashid road, west of Gaza City, killing five people and injuring at least 25 others, including civilians.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] Three Hamas members were killed alongside Sa'ad,[^cite_note-17] including his aide and a low-ranking Hamas official.[^cite_note-18] *[The Jerusalem Post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Jerusalem_Post)* reported that Sa'ad was targeted around an hour after he emerged from a [tunnel](43413500.md), in which he had operated for months.[^cite_note-19] The IDF later confirmed his death, saying that he "led Hamas's force buildup" and that his death would significantly hinder the group's efforts to rearm.[^cite_note-EF25a-20] Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) and Defense Minister [Israel Katz](19041378.md) called Sa'ad an architect of the 7 October attacks, and said that his killing was in response to an earlier explosion in southern Gaza that injured two IDF soldiers.[^cite_note-EF25a-20] On 14 December 2025, Hamas confirmed his death. It also said that he was killed "alongside his brothers who were with him."[^cite_note-21] [Khalil al-Hayya](43349601.md), the deputy chairman of the Hamas Political Bureau, stated that the attack is a violation of the ceasefire.[^cite_note-22]

[Axios](52436164.md) reported that the strike angered US officials, and that the US privately informed Netanyahu that it viewed the killing as a violation of the [ceasefire agreement in Gaza](81215079.md).[^cite_note-23]
