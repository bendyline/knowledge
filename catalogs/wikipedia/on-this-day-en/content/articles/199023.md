**Colonel** ([/ˈkɜːrnəl/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/81/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst\_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-colonel.wav/LL-Q1860\_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst\_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-colonel.wav.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/8/81/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-colonel.wav/LL-Q1860_%28eng%29-Naomi_Persephone_Amethyst_%28NaomiAmethyst%29-colonel.wav.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*KUR-nəl*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); abbreviated as **Col.**, **Col**, or **COL**) is the highest ranking non-General grade officer used in many countries. It is also used in some police forces and [paramilitary](146730.md) organizations.

In the 17th, 18th, and 19th centuries, a colonel was typically in charge of a [regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiment) in an army. Modern usage varies greatly, and in some cases, the term is used as an [honorific title](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_\(title\)) that may have no direct military relationship. In some smaller military forces, such as those of [Monaco](19261.md) or the [Vatican](13393.md), colonel is the highest [rank](150237.md).

Equivalent naval ranks may be called [captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Captain_\(naval\)) or [ship-of-the-line captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ship-of-the-line_captain). In the [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Nations)'s air force ranking system, the equivalent rank is [group captain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_captain).

## History and origins

By the end of the [late medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_medieval) period, a group of "companies" was referred to as a "column" of an army. According to Raymond Oliver, c. 1500, the Spanish began explicitly reorganizing part of their army into 20 *colunelas* or columns of approximately 1,000–1,250 soldiers. Each *colunela* was commanded by a *cabo de colunela* or column head. Because they were crown units who are directly under the control of the [monarch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarch) or [sovereign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sovereign) of a country, the units were also confusingly called *coronelas*, and their commanders *coronels*.[^cite_note-\:0-1] Evidence of this can be seen when [Gonzalo Fernández de Córdoba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gonzalo_Fern%C3%A1ndez_de_C%C3%B3rdoba), nicknamed "the Great Captain", divided his armies in *coronelías*, each led by a *coronel*, in 1508.[^cite_note-\:1-2]

Later, in the 16th century, the French army adopted this organizational structure, renaming *colunelas* regiments. Even so, they simply Gallicized *colunela* to the French *colonel* and pronounced it as written. The English then copied the unit and rank from the French. However, for reasons unknown, the English adopted the Spanish pronunciation of *coronel*, and after several decades of use shortened it to its current two-syllable pronunciation "kernel". *Colonel* is linked to the word *column* (from [Latin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin): *columna*; Italian: *colonna*; French: *colonne*) in a similar way that *brigadier* is linked to *brigade*, although in English this relationship is not immediately obvious.[^cite_note-\:0-1]

With the shift from primarily mercenary to primarily national armies in the course of the 17th century, a colonel (normally a member of the aristocracy) became a holder (German *Inhaber*) or proprietor of a military contract with a sovereign. The colonel purchased the regimental contract—the right to hold the regiment—from the previous holder of that right or directly from the sovereign when a new regiment was formed or an incumbent was [killed](368513.md).

As the office of colonel became an established practice, the colonel became the senior captain in a group of companies that were all sworn to observe his personal authority—to be ruled or regimented by him. This regiment, or governance, was to some extent embodied in a contract and set of written rules, also referred to as the colonel's regiment or standing regulation(s). By extension, the group of [companies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Company_\(military\)) subject to a colonel's regiment (in the foregoing sense) came to be referred to as his regiment (in the modern sense) as well.

In French usage of this period, the senior colonel in the army or, in a field force, the senior military contractor, was the [colonel general](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_general), and, in the absence of the sovereign or his designate, the colonel general might serve as the commander of a force. The position, however, was primarily contractual and it became progressively more of a functionless [sinecure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinecure). The head of a single regiment or [demi-brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demi-brigade) would be called a '[mestre de camp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mestre_de_camp)' or, after the [Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution), a '[chef de brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chef_de_brigade)'.

By the late 19th century, colonel had evolved to a professional military rank that was still held typically by an officer in command of a regiment or equivalent unit. Along with other ranks, it has become progressively more a matter of ranked duties, qualifications, and experience, as well as of corresponding titles and pay scale, than of functional office in a particular organization.

As European military influence expanded throughout the world, the rank of colonel became adopted by nearly every nation (albeit under a variety of names).

During the 20th century, with the rise of [communism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism), some of the large communist militaries saw fit to expand the colonel rank into several grades, resulting, for example, in the unique [senior colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senior_colonel) rank, which was found and is still used in such nations as China and [North Korea](21255.md).

### Colonel-in-chief

Main article: [Colonel-in-chief](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel-in-chief)

In many modern armies, the regiment has more importance as a ceremonial unit or a focus of members' loyalty than as an actual battle formation. Troops tend to be deployed in battalions (commanded by a [lieutenant colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant_colonel)) as a more convenient size of military unit and, as such, colonels have tended to have a higher profile in specialist and command roles than as actual commanders of regiments. However, in Commonwealth armies, the position of the colonel as the figurehead of a regiment is maintained in the honorary role of "colonel-in-chief", usually held by a member of the [royal family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Royal_family),[^cite_note-3] the nobility, or a retired senior military officer. The colonel-in-chief wears a colonel's uniform and encourages the members of the regiment, but takes no active part in the actual command structure or in any operational duties.[^cite_note-4]

### Colonel of the Regiment

The title **Colonel of the Regiment** (to distinguish it from the military rank of colonel) continues to be used in the modern British Army. The ceremonial position is often conferred on retired [general officers](148537.md), [brigadiers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brigadier) or colonels who have a close link to a particular regiment. Non-military personnel, usually for positions within the [Army Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Reserve\(United_Kingdom\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) may also be appointed to the ceremonial position. When attending functions as "Colonel of the Regiment", the titleholder wears the regimental uniform with rank insignia of (full) colonel, regardless of their official rank. A member of the [Royal Family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Family) is known as a [Royal Colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Colonel). A Colonel of the Regiment is expected to work closely with a regiment and its [Regimental Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regimental_Association?action=edit\&redlink=1).

## Colonel as highest-ranking officer

Some military forces have a colonel as their highest-ranking officer, with no 'general' ranks, and no superior authority (except, perhaps, the head of state as a titular commander-in-chief) other than the respective national government. Examples include the following (arranged alphabetically by country name):

- [Antigua and Barbuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antigua_and_Barbuda_Defence_Force) (170 personnel)

- [Costa Rica](5559.md) (about 8,000 personnel)

- [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iceland_Crisis_Response_Unit) (100 personnel, employed only for peacekeeping duties)

- [Libya](35021184.md) (commands all the Armed Forces – [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) until 2011)

- [Monaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_Monaco) (two branches, with a total of about 250 personnel)

## Other uses of colonel ranks

Further information: [Colonel (disambiguation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_\(disambiguation\))

The term *colonel* is also used as a title for auctioneers in the United States; there are a variety of theories or [folk etymologies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folk_etymologies) to explain the use of the term.[^cite_note-6] One of these is the claim that during the [American Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Civil_War) goods seized by armies were sold at auction by the colonel of the division.[^cite_note-Doyle-7]

[Kentucky colonel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kentucky_colonel) is the highest title of honor bestowed by the [Commonwealth of Kentucky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_of_Kentucky). Commissions for Kentucky colonels are given by the Governor and the Secretary of State to individuals in recognition of noteworthy accomplishments and outstanding service to a community, state or the nation. This is the equivalent to a full colonel in the militia. The sitting governor of the Commonwealth of Kentucky bestows the honor of a colonel's Commission, by issuance of letters patent. Perhaps the best known Kentucky colonel is [Harland Sanders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colonel_Sanders) of *[Kentucky Fried Chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KFC)* fame.

The rank of colonel is also used by some police forces and paramilitary organizations.
