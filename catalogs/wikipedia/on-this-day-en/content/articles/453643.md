**Central Kalimantan** ([Indonesian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_language): *Kalimantan Tengah*) is a [province of Indonesia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Indonesia). It is one of five provinces in [Kalimantan](165470.md), the Indonesian part of [Borneo](4518.md). It is the largest province in Indonesia by area since 2022, bordered by [West Kalimantan](453636.md) to the west, [South Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kalimantan) and [East Kalimantan](453651.md) to the east, [Java Sea](467143.md) to the south and is separated narrowly from [North Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Kalimantan) and [Malaysia](3607937.md) by East Kalimantan's [Mahakam Ulu Regency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahakam_Ulu_Regency). Its provincial capital is [Palangka Raya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palangka_Raya) and in 2010 its population was over 2.2 million,[^cite_note-Biro_Pusat_Statistik_2011-10] while the 2020 Census showed a total of almost 2.67 million;[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2021-11] the official estimate as at mid 2025 was 2,844,992 (comprising 1,467,139 males and 1,377,853 females).[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2026-1]

The population growth rate was almost 3.0% per annum between 1990 and 2000, one of the highest provincial growth rates in Indonesia during that time; in the subsequent decade to 2010 the average annual growth rate slowed markedly to around 1.8%, but it rose again in the decade beginning 2010. More than is the case in other provinces on Kalimantan, Central Kalimantan is populated by the [Dayaks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayak_people), the indigenous inhabitants of Borneo.

## History

Since the eighteenth century the central region of Kalimantan and its Dayak inhabitants were ruled by the Muslim [Sultanate of Banjar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultanate_of_Banjar). Following Indonesian independence after [World War II](32927.md), Dayak tribes demanded a province separate from South Kalimantan province.[^cite_note-12]

In 1957 [South Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kalimantan) was divided to provide the Dayak population with greater autonomy from the [Muslim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim) population in the province. The change was approved by the Indonesian Government on 23 May 1957 under Presidential Law No. 10 Year 1957, which declared Central Kalimantan the seventeenth province of Indonesia. President Sukarno appointed the Dayak-born national hero [Tjilik Riwut](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tjilik_Riwut) as the first Governor and designated [Palangkaraya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palangkaraya) as the provincial capital.[^cite_note-13]

## Geography

Central Kalimantan is the largest Indonesian province by area ([Papua Province](15199.md) held this position until its 2022 partition) with a size of 153,430.36 km<sup>2</sup> (59,239.79 sq mi), about 1.5 times the size of the island of [Java](69336.md), and it is slightly larger than [Bangladesh](3454.md) or the U.S. state of [Illinois](14849.md) or [Murmansk Oblast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murmansk_Oblast). It is bordered by [West Kalimantan](453636.md) and [East Kalimantan](453651.md) provinces to the north, by the [Java Sea](467143.md) to the south, by [South Kalimantan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Kalimantan) and East Kalimantan provinces to the east, and by West Kalimantan province to west.

The [Schwaner Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwaner_Mountain?action=edit\&redlink=1) stretch from the north-east of the province to the south-west, 80% of which is covered in dense forest, [peatland swamps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borneo_peat_swamp_forests), [mangroves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mangrove), rivers and traditional agriculture land. Highland areas in the north-east are remote and not easily accessible. Non-volcanic mounts are scattered in this area including [Kengkabang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kengkabang?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Samiajang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samiajang?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Liang Pahang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liang_Pahang?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Ulu Gedang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulu_Gedang?action=edit\&redlink=1).

The centre of the province is covered with tropical forest, which produces [rattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rattan), [resin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Resin) and valuable timber such as [Ulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ulin) and [Meranti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shorea). The southern lowlands are dominated by peatland swamps that intersect with many rivers. [Sabangau National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabangau_National_Park) is a protected peatland area internationally acknowledged as sanctuary for the endangered [Orangutan](22433.md). Recently the peat swamp forests have been damaged by the [Mega Rice Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mega_Rice_Project_\(Kalimantan\)), which unsuccessfully sought to turn large areas into [rice paddies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice_paddies).

The province's climate is [wet weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_rainforest_climate) equatorial zone with an eight-month rainy season, and 4 months of dry season. Rainfall or precipitation is 2,776—3,393 mm per year with an average of 145 rainy days annually.

See also: [2021 Central Kalimantan floods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_Central_Kalimantan_floods)

### Rivers

Central Kalimantan has numerous rivers, generally flowing southwards from the catchment areas in the north in the [Schwaner Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwaner_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1), down to the [Java Sea](467143.md). The major rivers (with their lengths) include:

- [Barito River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barito_River) (900 km)

- [Kapuas River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kapuas_River_\(Barito_River\)) (600 km)

- [Kahayan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kahayan_River) (600 km)

- [Katingan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mendawai_River) (600 km)

- [Mentaya (Sampit) River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sampit_River_\(Indonesia\)) (400 km)

- [Seruyan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pembuang_River) (350 km)

- [Lamandau River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamandau_River) (300 km)

- [Arut River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arut_River) (250 km)

- [Sabangau River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabangau_River) (200 km)

- [Kumai River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumai_River) (179 km)

- [Jelai River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jelai-Bila_River) (100 km)

Note: (a) Not to be confused with the Kapuas River in West Kalimantan province.

Rivers are an important mode of [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_transport) and a primary location for settlement. With relatively undeveloped infrastructure, the province's economy relies heavily on the rivers.

## Ecology

Map of Central Kalimantan showing tree-cover loss years, with forest remaining since 2000 in green and loss years shaded yellow through purple.

Tree-cover loss year in Central Kalimantan, 2001-2024, from the [Global Forest Change dataset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Forest_Change_dataset).

Based upon the research of the Indonesian Ministry of Environment and Forestry, Central Kalimantan comprises approximately ten different ecological regions. The two main ecological regions are the **peat ecoregion** which covers 26% of the total area of Central Kalimantan and the **fluvial plain ecoregion** which covers 21% of the whole area of Central Kalimantan.[^cite_note-ecoregion-14] Due to its high concentration of the peat ecological region, Central Kalimantan is prone to drought and peat fires during the dry season and inundation during the rainy season within each episodes of the [ENSO](317311.md) and the [IOD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Ocean_Dipole). These situations have worsened because of massive deforestation in the region and climate change.

Central Kalimantan is also home for many endemic floras and faunas like [orangutans](22433.md), [proboscis monkeys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Proboscis_monkey), [hornbill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hornbill) birds, [rattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rattan), [Bornean iron wood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eusideroxylon), etc. This province currently has three national parks i.e. [Sabangau National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabangau_National_Park), [Tanjung Puting National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tanjung_Puting_National_Park), and [Bukit Baka Bukit Raya National Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukit_Baka_Bukit_Raya_National_Park) which are home to the endemic floras and faunas of Kalimantan.[^cite_note-ecoregion-14]

## Government and administrative divisions

Central Kalimantan Province, when separated from South Kalimantan in 1958, comprised three [regencies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regencies_of_Indonesia) (*kabupaten*) - **Barito**, **Kapuas** and **Kotawaringin**. However on 26 June 1959 two of these were split - Barito Regency was divided into a **South Barito** Regency and a **North Barito** Regency, while Kotawaringin Regency was divided into a **West Kotawaringin** Regency and an **East Kotawaringin** Regency. On 14 June 1965 the provincial capital of **[Palangka Raya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palangka_Raya)** was split off from Kapuas Regency to form an independent city (*kotamadya*).

On 10 April 2002 an additional eight regencies were created by splitting existing regencies - **Sukamara** and **Lamandau** from parts of West Kotawaringin Regency, **Seruyan** and **Katingan** from parts of East Kotawaringin Regency, **Pulang Pisau** and **Gunung Mas** from parts of Kapuas Regency, **East Barito** from part of South Barito Regency, and **Murung Raya** from part of North Barito Regency. Thus the province now is administratively divided into thirteen regencies (each headed by a regent) and the single city. The six western regencies (covering the original pre-1958 Kotawaringin Regency, have a combined area of 71,552.48 km<sup>2</sup> and had a total population of 1,264,451 persons as at mid 2025. The seven eastern regencies (plus the municipality of Palangka Raya) have a combined area of 81,877.89 km<sup>2</sup> and had a total population of 1,580,541 persons as at mid 2025.[^cite_note-Badan_Pusat_Statistik_2025-16] There has been a campaign in Kotawaringin for the six western regencies to be split away to form a separate province.[^cite_note-17]

In addition to the civil service, Central Kalimantan also recognises a traditional governing system led by traditional leaders known as *Damang*. The province is divided into 67 traditional law areas known as *Kedamangan*, headed by *Damang*. The system is intended to culturally recognise and preserve the customs and heritage of the Dayak tribes.[^cite_note-18]

The province now forms one of Indonesia's 84 national electoral districts to elect members to the [People's Representative Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indonesian_Parliament). The **Central Kalimantan Electoral District** consists of all of the 13 regencies in the province, together with the city of Palangkaraya, and elects 6 members to the People's Representative Council.[^cite_note-19]

## Railways

In 2026, Indonesia is evaluating plans to build a 2,772-kilometre railway network across all the Kalimantan provinces to improve logistics and unlock resource-driven growth, though the project remains at an early planning stage. A principal line will stretch across Central Kalimantan from West Kalimantan to South Kalimantan.

Central Kalimantan is predominantly inhabited by the Dayak people that would further be divided into three major Dayak sub-ethnics. The three major Dayak tribes in Central Kalimantan are the [Ngaju](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngaju_people), Ot Danum and Dusun [Ma'anyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ma'anyan_people) Ot Siang. The three major tribes extended into several branches of prominent Dayak tribes in Central Kalimantan such as [Lawangan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lawangan_people), Taboyan, Dusun Siang, Boyan, Bantian, Dohoi and Kadori.

In addition to the indigenous Dayak tribes, there are also ethnic groups from other areas of Indonesia, including [Malays](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malays_\(ethnic_group\)), [Javanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Javanese_people), [Madurese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madurese_people), [Batak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Batak), [Toraja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toraja), [Ambonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambonese_people), [Bugis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bugis), Palembang, [Minang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minangkabau_people), [Banjarese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banjar_people), Makassar, Papuan, [Balinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balinese_people), [Acehnese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acehnese_people) and also [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Indonesians).

## Culture



A Karungut Music Group Performance in Isen Mulang Cultural Festival in 2018. To the left of the group it can be seen that the group played three main musical instruments of Dayak people i.e. Japen or Kacapi, Gandang Manca, and Garantung.

### Traditional music

The most well-known traditional musical instrument from Central Kalimantan is Japen, Garantung, and Gandang Manca which are traditional Dayak musical instruments. These musical instruments are usually played during traditional procession or ritual.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-Gandang-27]

Japen or Kecapi is a lute-like instrument that is honored by [Dayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ngaju_people) people. This traditional musical instrument is usually played as an accompaniment for Central Kalimantan traditional music such as Karungut recitation, a Dayak oral literature in the form of musicalized [rhyme scheme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhyme_scheme). Japen is mainly made of wood and nylon rope for its strings.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]

Garantung is a [percussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percussion) instrument that is fairly similar to [kempul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kempul) in [gamelan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamelan). It is a type of hanging gong that is normally played ensemble with Gandang Manca or Gandang Kembar. Gandang Manca itself is a two-headed drum of Dayak traditional musical instrument that is mainly made of wood for the body of the drum and leather membranes to cover the cavity at both ends that are tied by [rattan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rattan) rope.[^cite_note-Gandang-27] Both Garantung and Gandang Manca are commonly played ensembly as an accompaniment for Dayak traditional processions and rituals.[^cite_note-30]

### Traditional architecture

The most notable [fine arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fine_arts) of Dayak people is the traditional house. The traditional house of Dayak people in Central Kalimantan is commonly called Huma Betang. Huma Betang or simply called Betang like many other traditional houses in Indonesia is built on stilts. This traditional house structure is mainly made of iron wood which is commonly anti-termite and resistant to high humidity, thus the house can stand up to hundreds of years. Huma Betang is very well-known for its huge and long size. The length of this house structure is usually between 30 and 150 metres, the width is somewhere between 5 and 30 metres, and the height is approximately 3 to 6 metres. Due to its huge and long size, this house can be occupied by up to 150 people and usually inhabited by one big family.[^cite_note-31]

### Traditional carving

Talawang shield used as a dance instrument

Central Kalimantan is well-known for its unique traditional carving. The three most famous carving from this province are the three dimensional wood carving sculpture of Sapundu and the traditional weapons i.e. Mandau and Talawang.

Sapundu sculpture is a three dimensional wood carving in the form of humanoid statue. This sculpture is commonly adorned with decorative motifs or even colors. Sapundu sculpture is mainly made of Bornean iron wood and usually presents during Tiwah procession. In ancient times, Sapundu sculpture symbolized the social status of its owner and was believed to possess magical powers.[^cite_note-32]

The *[Mandau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandau)* and *Talawang* are the main traditional weapon of Dayak people in Central Kalimantan. The *Mandau* is a type of sword with a single edge blade i.e. one side is convex and the other side is concave. The *Mandau* consists of three different parts, which are (i) the *Bilah* or the blade that made of *Mantikei* iron plate with a sharp edge and is 70 cm long, (ii) the *Pulang Gagang* or the hilt that is usually made of wood, deer's horns, or even human bones, and (iii) the *Sarung* or the sheath which covers the blade and is typically made of wood and usually covered with bracelet-shaped bone on the upper part and wrapped by rattan rope.[^cite_note-33]

The *Talawang* is a shield that mainly made of Bornean iron wood that is carved with certain motifs and adorned with some bright colors. In ancient times, this weapon was used as a protection against wild animals and enemy's arrows and sword slashes. However, the function currently shifts to become a decorative ornament of [Kaharingan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaharingan) temples, public buildings, or even people's houses mostly in Central Kalimantan.[^cite_note-34]

### Traditional dance

Central Kalimantan is home for various sub-ethnics of [Dayak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayak_people) tribe. Each of those Dayak sub-ethnics live spread out across all regencies in Central Kalimantan. This makes every regency have their own unique traditional dances. These are some of the traditional dances from Central Kalimantan:[^cite_note-35]

1. Kinyah Mandau Hatue Dance, originating from Kapuas Regency and depicting theatrical war and martial arts of Dayak tribes in ancient time.

   [^cite_note-36]

2. Giring-Giring Dance, coming from East Barito Regency and usually performed in group of 6 to 10 people.

   [^cite_note-37]

3. Walian Dadas Dance, originating from South Barito Regency and used to be performed as a way to heal a region from negativity.

   [^cite_note-38]

4. [Babukung Dance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Babukung), originally from Lamandau Regency and performed during funeral especially among Dayak Tomun people.

   [^cite_note-39]

5. Tambun Bungai Dance, originating from Palangka Raya city and depicting heroic action of Tambung and Bungai protecting the territory from enemy who tried to loot people's crops.

   [^cite_note-40]
