The **Met Office**, previously known as the **Meteorological Office** until November 2000,[^cite_note-2] is the United Kingdom's national weather and climate service. It is an [executive agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_agency) and [trading fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trading_fund) of the [Department for Science, Innovation and Technology ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Science,_Innovation_and_Technology) and is led by CEO[^cite_note-CEO2018-3] [Penny Endersby](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Penny_Endersby), who took on the role as Chief Executive in December 2018 and is the first woman to do so.[^cite_note-Endersby-4] The Met Office makes meteorological predictions across all timescales from weather forecasts to [climate change](5042951.md).

Although an executive agency of the UK Government, the Met Office supports the [Scottish Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Government), [Welsh Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welsh_Government) and [Northern Ireland Executive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Ireland_Executive) in their functions and preparations ahead of intense weather and planning for extreme weather alerts. Met Office policies can be used by each government to inform their planning and decision making processes. The Met Office has an office located in the Scottish capital, [Edinburgh](9602.md), and a forecasting centre in Aberdeen in the north-east of Scotland, which are some of the services used to help the Scottish Government with objectives such as [climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Scotland).[^cite_note-5]

## History



[Vice Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Admiral_\(Royal_Navy\)) [Robert FitzRoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_FitzRoy), founder of the Met Office

&#x20;The Met Office was established on 1 August 1854[^cite_note-6] as a small department within the [Board of Trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Board_of_Trade) under [Vice Admiral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice_Admiral_\(Royal_Navy\)) [Robert FitzRoy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_FitzRoy) as a service to [mariners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sailor). The loss of the passenger vessel, the [*Royal Charter*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Charter_\(ship\)), and 459 lives off the coast of [Anglesey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglesey) in a violent storm in October 1859 led to the first gale warning service. FitzRoy established a network of 15 coastal stations from which visual gale warnings could be provided for ships at sea.

The new [electric telegraph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electric_telegraph) enabled rapid dissemination of warnings and also led to the development of an observational network which could then be used to provide synoptic analysis. The Met Office started in 1861 to provide weather forecasts to newspapers. FitzRoy requested the daily traces of the photo-[barograph](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barograph) at [Kew Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kew_Observatory) (invented by [Francis Ronalds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_Ronalds)) to assist in this task and similar barographs and as well as instruments to continuously record other meteorological parameters were later provided to stations across the observing network.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] Publication of forecasts ceased in May 1866 after FitzRoy's death but recommenced in April 1879.

### Connection with the Ministry of Defence



Former Met Office building in [Bracknell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bracknell), [Berkshire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkshire), before relocation to [Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter), since demolished

Following the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War), the Met Office became part of the [Air Ministry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Ministry) in 1919, the weather observed from the top of [Adastral House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adastral_House) (where the Air Ministry was based) giving rise to the phrase "The weather on the Air Ministry roof". As a result of the need for weather information for aviation, the Met Office located many of its observation and data collection points on [RAF airfields](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Royal_Air_Force_stations), and this accounts for the large number of military airfields mentioned in weather reports even today. In 1936 the Met Office split with services to the [Royal Navy](26061.md) being provided by its own forecasting services.

It became an [executive agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Executive_agency) of the [Ministry of Defence](203793.md) in April 1990, a quasi-governmental role, being required to act commercially.

### Changes of ministry

Following a [machinery of government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Machinery_of_government) change, the Met Office became part of the [Department for Business, Innovation and Skills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Business,_Innovation_and_Skills) on 18 July 2011,[^cite_note-9] and subsequently part of the [Department for Business, Energy and Industrial Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Business,_Energy_and_Industrial_Strategy) following the merger of BIS and the [Department of Energy and Climate Change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Energy_and_Climate_Change) on 14 July 2016.[^cite_note-10]

Although no longer part of the MOD, the Met Office maintains strong links with the military through its front line offices at RAF and Army bases both in the UK and overseas and its involvement in the Joint Operations Meteorology and Oceanography Centre (JOMOC) with the Royal Navy. The [Mobile Met Unit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobile_Meteorological_Unit) (MMU) are a unit consisting of Met Office staff who are also [RAF reservists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Reserve) who accompany forward units in times of conflict advising the armed forces of the conditions for battle, particularly the RAF.

## Locations



The 2003 headquarters building on the edge of Exeter

In September 2003 the Met Office moved its headquarters from [Bracknell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bracknell) in Berkshire to a purpose-built £80m structure at [Exeter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exeter) Business Park, near junction 29 of the [M5 motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M5_motorway). The new building was officially opened on 21 June 2004 – a few weeks short of the Met Office's 150th anniversary – by [Robert May, Baron May of Oxford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_May,_Baron_May_of_Oxford).

It has a worldwide presence – including a forecasting centre in [Aberdeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aberdeen), and offices in [Gibraltar](7607314.md) and on the [Falklands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falklands). Other outposts lodge in establishments such as the MetOffice\@Reading (formerly the Joint Centre for Mesoscale Meteorology) at [University of Reading](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Reading) in Berkshire, the Joint Centre for Hydro-Meteorological Research (JCHMR) site at [Wallingford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallingford,_Oxfordshire) in Oxfordshire, and there is a Met Office presence at Army and Air Force bases within the UK and abroad (including frontline units in conflict zones).[^cite_note-11] [Royal Navy](26061.md) weather forecasts are often provided by naval officers, not Met Office personnel.

## Forecasts

### Weather forecasting and warnings

The Met Office issues Severe Weather Warnings for the United Kingdom through the [National Severe Weather Warning Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Severe_Weather_Warning_Service) (NSWWS). These warn of weather events that may affect transport infrastructure and endanger people's lives. In March 2008, the system was improved and a new stage of warning was introduced, the 'Advisory'.[^cite_note-12]

The Met Office along with Irish counterpart [Met Éireann](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Met_%C3%89ireann) introduced a [storm naming system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winter_storm_naming_in_the_United_Kingdom_and_Ireland) in September 2015 to provide a single authoritative naming system for the storms that affect the UK and Ireland.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The first named storm under this system, [Abigail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Abigail) was announced on 10 November 2015.[^cite_note-15] In 2019, the Met Office and Met Éireann were joined by [Dutch](21148.md) national weather forecasting service the [Royal Netherlands Meteorological Institute](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Netherlands_Meteorological_Institute) (KNMI).[^cite_note-met2019-16]

### Weather prediction models

The [Unified Model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Model), or UM, is the principal modelling suite used by the Met Office. Released in 1990, it is named as such for its ability to be run over a range of scales in space and time. The UM forms the basis of all operational [Numerical Weather Prediction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numerical_weather_prediction) at the Met Office, for both deterministic and [ensemble forecasts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_forecasting).[^cite_note-17]

Deterministic forecasts include the [UKV model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Model#UKV_model) which runs at 1.5 km (0.93-mile) resolution over the UK and 4 km (2.5-mile) in surrounding areas out to a maximum of 120 hours. In addition is global deterministic forecast at 1.0 km (0.62-mile) resolution out to six days, which then provides the boundary conditions for the UKV model.[^cite_note-18]

Ensemble forecasts are also run for both the UK and globally. The UK ensemble (MOGREPS-UK) has 18 members and operates at a 2.2 km (1.4-mile) resolution out to five days, while the global ensemble (MOGREPS-G) runs at 20 km (12-mile) out to seven days. The global model also has 18 members, which then provide interpolated [physics perturbations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ensemble_forecasting#Stochastic_parametrisations) and necessary boundary conditions for the corresponding members of the UK ensemble.[^cite_note-19]

### Flood Forecasting Centre

Main article: [Flood Forecasting Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_Forecasting_Centre)

Formed in 2009, the [Flood Forecasting Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flood_Forecasting_Centre) (FFC) is a joint venture between the [Environment Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Environment_Agency) and the Met Office to provide flood risk guidance for England and Wales. The Centre is jointly staffed from both parent organisations and is based in the Operations Centre at the Met Office headquarters in Exeter.[^cite_note-20]

#### Scottish Flood Forecasting Service

In Scotland this role is performed by the Scottish Flood Forecasting Service, a joint venture between the [Scottish Environment Protection Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scottish_Environment_Protection_Agency) (SEPA) and the Met Office.[^cite_note-21]

### Seasonal forecasts

The Met Office makes seasonal and long range forecasts and distributes them to customers and users globally.[^cite_note-22] The Met Office was the first climate and weather forecast provider to be recognised as a Global Producing Centre of long range forecasts by the [World Meteorological Organisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Meteorological_Organisation) and continues to provide forecasts to the WMO for dissemination to other national meteorological services worldwide.[^cite_note-23]

Met Office research has broken new ground in seasonal forecasting for the [extratropics](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/extratropics) and has demonstrated its abilities in its seasonal predictions of the [North Atlantic Oscillation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Atlantic_Oscillation) and winter climate for Europe and North America.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### Supply of forecasts for broadcasting companies

One of the main media companies, [ITV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_\(TV_network\)) produce forecasts for [ITV Weather](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ITV_Weather) using the Met Office's data and animated weather symbols.

The BBC used to use Met Office forecasts for all of its output, but on 23 August 2015, it was announced that the BBC would be replacing the Met Office with [MeteoGroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MeteoGroup), a competing provider, as part of the corporation's legal obligation to provide best value for money for the [licence fee](48551.md) payers.[^cite_note-26] The BBC still uses some Met Office data for certain forecasts, particularly severe weather warnings and the *[Shipping Forecast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shipping_Forecast)*. In July 2025, the BBC announced that it would be partnering with the Met Office again in future.[^cite_note-\:0-27]

### World Area Forecast Centre

Main article: [World Area Forecast Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Area_Forecast_Centre)

The Met Office is one of only two World Area Forecast Centres or WAFCs, and is referred to as WAFC London. The other WAFC is located in [Kansas City, Missouri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kansas_City,_Missouri), and known as WAFC Washington. WAFC data is used daily to safely and economically route aircraft, particularly on long-haul journeys. The data provides details of wind speed and direction, air temperature, cloud type and tops, and other features.

### Volcanic Ash Advisory Centre

Main article: [Volcanic Ash Advisory Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_Ash_Advisory_Center)

As part of its aviation forecast operation the Met Office operates the London [Volcanic Ash Advisory Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_Ash_Advisory_Centre) (VAAC).[^cite_note-LVAAC-28] This provides forecasts to the aviation industry of [volcanic ash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanic_ash) clouds that could enter aircraft flight paths and impact aviation safety. The London VAAC, one of nine worldwide, is responsible for the area covering the [British Isles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Isles), the north east Atlantic and [Iceland](14531.md). The VAAC were set up by the [International Civil Aviation Organization](14985.md) (ICAO), an agency of the United Nations, as part of the International Airways Volcano Watch (IAVW).[^cite_note-IAVW-29] The London VAAC makes use of satellite images, plus seismic, radar and visual observation data from [Iceland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volcanism_of_Iceland),[^cite_note-Witham-30] the location of all of the active volcanoes in its area of responsibility. The [NAME dispersion model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAME_\(dispersion_model\)) developed by the Met Office is used to forecast the movement of the ash clouds 6, 12 and 18 hours from the time of the alert at different flight levels.

### Air quality

Main article: [UK Dispersion Modelling Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UK_Dispersion_Modelling_Bureau)

The Met Office issues [air quality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_quality_index#United_Kingdom) forecasts made using [NAME](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NAME_\(dispersion_model\)), the Met Office's medium-to-long-range [atmospheric dispersion model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmospheric_dispersion_modelling). It was developed as a nuclear accident model following the [Chernobyl accident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chernobyl_accident) in 1986, but has since evolved into an all-purpose dispersion model capable of predicting the transport, transformation and deposition of a wide class of airborne materials. NAME is used operationally by the Met Office as an emergency response model as well as for routine air quality forecasting. Aerosol dispersion is calculated using the [United Kingdom Chemistry and Aerosols model](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_Chemistry_and_Aerosols_model).

**Decadal Predictions**

The Met Office coordinates the production and collation of decadal climate prediction from climate centres around the world as part of its responsibilities as World Meteorological Organisation [Lead Centre for Annual to Decadal Climate Prediction](https://hadleyserver.metoffice.gov.uk/wmolc/). These predictions are updated each year and a summary, the Global Annual to Decadal Climate Update is published each year.

## Weather stations

Reports (observations) from [weather stations](206018.md) can be [automatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Automatic_weather_station) (totally machine produced), semi-automatic (part-machine and part manual), or manual. Some stations produce manual observations during business hours and revert to automatic observations outside these times. Many stations feature "present weather" sensors, [CCTV](87179.md), etc. There is also a network of 'upper air' stations, using [radiosondes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiosondes). The six main radiosonde stations in the UK are Camborne, Lerwick, Albemarle, Watnall, Castor Bay and Herstmonceux.

Some stations have limited reporting times, while other report continuously, mainly [RAF](25679.md) and [Army Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Air_Corps_\(United_Kingdom\)) stations where a staffed met office is provided for military operations. The "standard" is a once-hourly reporting schedule, but automatic stations can often be "polled" as required, whilst stations at airfields report twice-hourly, with additional (often frequent in times of bad weather) special reports as necessary to inform airfield authorities of changes to the weather that may affect aviation operations.

Some stations report only [CLIMAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CLIMAT) data (e.g. maximum and minimum temperatures, rainfall totals over a period, etc.) and these are usually recorded at 0900 and 2100 hours daily. Weather reports are often performed by observers not specifically employed by the Met Office, such as [Air traffic control](48563.md) staff, coastguards, university staff and so on.

## Meteorological Research Unit and the Facility for Airborne Atmospheric Measurements (FAAM)

Main article: [RAF Cardington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Cardington)

Meteorological Research was carried out at [RAE Bedford](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAE_Bedford) with instruments being carried by barrage balloons until the RAE facility closed in the 1980s.

The Met Office association with [Cardington](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Cardington) continued by maintaining a Meteorological Research Unit (MRU) until 2025. This was responsible for conducting research into part of the atmosphere called the [boundary layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_layer) by using a tethered balloon which was kept in a small portable hangar.[^cite_note-autogenerated1-38][^cite_note-39]

### FAAM

Main article: [Facility for Airborne Atmospheric Measurements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Facility_for_Airborne_Atmospheric_Measurements)



FAAM BAe146-300 takes off at [RIAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RIAT), [RAF Fairford](475013.md), England

The Facility for Airborne Atmospheric Measurements (FAAM), part of the [National Centre for Atmospheric Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Centre_for_Atmospheric_Science), is based at [Cranfield Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cranfield_Airport). It is a collaboration with the [Natural Environment Research Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Natural_Environment_Research_Council).[^cite_note-autogenerated1-38]

The FAAM was established as part of the National Centre for Atmospheric Science (NCAS),[^cite_note-40] itself part of NERC, to provide aircraft measurement for use by UK atmospheric research organisations on worldwide campaigns. The main equipment is a modified [BAe 146 type 301](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAe_146) aircraft, registration G-LUXE, owned and operated by [BAE Systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BAE_Systems) on behalf of Directflight Limited.[^cite_note-41]

Areas of application include:[^cite_note-42]

- [Radiative transfer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiative_transfer) studies in clear and cloudy air;

- [Tropospheric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troposphere) chemistry measurements;

- [Cloud physics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cloud_physics) and dynamic studies;

- Dynamics of [mesoscale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesoscale_meteorology) weather systems;

- [Boundary layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boundary_layer) and turbulence studies;

- [Remote sensing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Remote_sensing): verification of ground-based instruments;

- Satellite ground truth: [radiometric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiometric) measurements and winds;

- Satellite instrument test-bed;

- Campaigns in the UK and abroad.
