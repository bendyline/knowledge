The **Myanmar Air Force**, officially the **Defence Services (Air)** ([Burmese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_language): တပ်မတော် (လေ), [romanised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Burmese): *Tatmadaw (Lay)*, pronounced [\[taʔmədɔ̀ (le)\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Burmese)),[^cite_note-2] is the aerial branch of the Myanmar Defence Services ([Tatmadaw](20394.md)), the combined military of [Myanmar](19457.md). Its official name was **Burma Air Force** (Burmese: မြန်မာ့လေတပ်မတော်, romanized: *Myanma Lay Tatmadaw*) before being renamed to Defence Services (Air Force) (Burmese: တပ်မတော် (လေတပ်), romanized: *Tatmadaw (Lay Tat)*) in 1956.[^cite_note-3] The primary mission of the Myanmar Air Force (MAF) since its inception has been to provide air bases force protection, anti-aircraft warfare, close air support (CAS), logistical, and transport to the [Myanmar Army](9503548.md) in counterinsurgency operations.[^cite_note-Myoe-4] It is mainly used in [internal conflicts in Myanmar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_conflict_in_Myanmar), and, on a smaller scale, in relief missions,[^cite_note-5] especially after the deadly [Cyclone Nargis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Nargis) of May 2008.

## History

### Post-independence era (1948–1990)

The Myanmar Air Force (MAF) was formed as the [Burmese Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burmese_Air_Force) on 16 January 1947, while [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) (as [Myanmar](19457.md) was known until 1989) was still under [British rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_rule_in_Burma). By 1948, the fleet of the new air force included 40 [Airspeed Oxfords](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airspeed_Oxford), 16 [de Havilland Tiger Moths](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Tiger_Moth), four [Austers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auster_Aircraft), and three [Supermarine Spitfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Spitfire) transferred from the [Royal Air Force](25679.md), and had a few hundred personnel.[^cite_note-Myoe-4]

The Mingaladon Air Base HQ, the main air base in the country, was formed on 16 June 1950. No.1 Squadron, Equipment Holding Unit and Air High Command - Burma Air Force, and the Flying Training School, were placed under the jurisdiction of the base. A few months later, on 18 December 1950, No. 2 Squadron was formed with nine [Douglas Dakotas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douglas_C-47_Skytrain) as a transport squadron. In 1953, the Advanced Flying Unit was formed under the Mingaladon Air Base with [de Havilland Vampire T55s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Vampire), and by the end of 1953 the Burmese Air Force had three main airbases, at [Mingaladon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mingaladon_Township), [Hmawbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmawbi_Township), and [Meiktila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiktila), in central Burma.[^cite_note-Myoe-4]

In 1953, the Burmese Air Force bought 30 [Supermarine Spitfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Spitfire) from [Israel](9282173.md) and 20 [Supermarine Seafires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supermarine_Seafire) from the [United Kingdom](31717.md), and in 1954 it bought 40 [Percival Provost](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Percival_Provost) T-53s and 8 [de Havilland Vampire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Vampire) Mark T55s from the [United Kingdom](31717.md). In late 1955, the Burmese Air Force formed a Maintenance Air Base in Mingaladon, No. 501 Squadron Group (Hmawbi Airbase) and No. 502 Squadron Group (Mingaladon Air Base). In 1956, the Burmese Air Force bought 10 [Cessna 180](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_180) aircraft from the [United States](3434750.md). The same year, 6 [Kawasaki Bell 47Gs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_47) formed its first helicopter unit. The following year, the Burmese Air Force procured 21 [Hawker Sea Fury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Sea_Fury) aircraft from the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and 9 [de Havilland Canada DHC-3 Otters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Canada_DHC-3_Otter) from [Canada](5042916.md). In 1958, it procured 7 additional Kawasaki Bell 47Gs and 12 [Vertol H-21 Shawnees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vertol_H-21_Shawnee) from the [United States](3434750.md).[^cite_note-Myoe-4] Five years later, No. 503 Squadron Group was formed with No. 51 Squadron ([de Havilland Canada DHC-3 Otters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_Havilland_Canada_DHC-3_Otter) and [Cessna 180s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_180)) and No. 53 Squadron (Bell 47Gs, [Kaman HH-43 Huskies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaman_HH-43_Huskie), and [Aérospatiale Alouettes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_Alouette_II)) in [Meiktila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiktila).[^cite_note-Myoe-4]

On 15 February 1961, an unmarked [Republic of China Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_Air_Force) [Consolidated PB4Y Privateer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_PB4Y_Privateer) came into Burmese air space carrying supplies for Chinese [Kuomintang](16903.md) forces fighting in northern Burma, and was intercepted by three [Hawker Sea Fury](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hawker_Sea_Fury) fighters of the Burmese Air Force. The intruding bomber and one Burmese fighter crashed in [Thailand](30128.md) during the incident.[^cite_note-ColdList-6] On 17 February, a team from Burmese 9th Front Brigade left for the crash site. A 12.7mm bullet was fired into the fuselage of UB-466, hitting pilot officer Peter as well, breaking five of his ribs. Peter was recorded in the history of Burmese Air Force as an airman who gave his life for the country and the people.[^cite_note-7] In 1962, a new radar station in Mingaladon and a mobile radar station in Lwemwe (near Tachileik) were put into operation. By December 1964, the Burmese Air Force had 323 officers and 5,677 other ranks and it acquired [Lockheed T-33 Shooting Star](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_T-33_Shooting_Star) jet trainers and a new radar station, which could operate within a 120-mile (193-km) radius, was opened in [Namsang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Namsang). In 1966, the radar arm of the air force underwent a complete overhaul and upgrade, with new radar stations being operated. The Namsang Radar station was upgraded to cover about a 200-mile (322-km) radius and renamed No.71 Squadron. In the same year, the Burmese Air Force formed the No. 1 [Airborne Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_forces) with 26 officers and 750 other ranks.

On 1 January 1967, the Burmese Air Force reorganised its command structure. No. 501 Squadron Group in [Hmawbi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmawbi_Township) became No. 501 Air Base HQ; No. 502 Squadron Group in Mingalardon became No. 502 Air Base HQ; and No. 503 Squadron Group in [Meiktila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meiktila) became No. 503 Air Base HQ in Meiktila. It also maintained airfield detachments in [Lashio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashio) and [Kengtung](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kengtung) to cope with the insurgency of [Communist Party of Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communist_Party_of_Burma) in the northeast border region of the country.[^cite_note-Myoe-4]

In 1975, the Burmese Air Force took delivery of 18 [Bell 205](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_205)A and seven [Bell 206](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_206)B helicopters from the United States under the International Narcotic Control Program (INCP). In March 1975, it bought 20 [SIAI-Marchetti SF.260](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIAI-Marchetti_SF.260) trainers from [Italy](14532.md).[^cite_note-Myoe-4]

Between 1976 and 1987, the Burmese Air Force bought seven [Pilatus PC-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_PC-6) Turbo porter STOL aircraft; and 16 [Pilatus PC-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_PC-7) and 10 [Pilatus PC-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pilatus_PC-9) turboprop trainers from [Switzerland](26748.md). These aircraft were deployed in [Lashio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lashio) for close air support in counter-insurgency operations.[^cite_note-Myoe-4]

In 1989, the Burmese Air Force was renamed the Myanmar Air Force in accordance with the country changing its name from [Burma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burma) to [Myanmar](19457.md).

### Modernisation Programme (1990–present)



Myanmar Air Force Chengdu F-7M

In the early 1990s, the Burmese Air Force upgraded its facilities and introduced two new air base headquarters and existing air base headquarters were renamed. It also significantly upgraded its radar and electronic warfare facilities. The Burmese Air Force bought more than 100 aircraft from the [People's Republic of China](5405.md), which included [F7 IIK](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-7) interceptors, [FT-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-7#Variants) trainers, [A-5C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang_A-5) ground attack aircraft, [FT-6M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shenyang_FT-6) trainers, [K-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hongdu_JL-8) trainers and [Y-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi_Y-8) transport aircraft.

By 2000, the Myanmar Air Force had received 62 [F7 IIK interceptors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-7) from [China](5405.md).[^cite_note-Myoe-4] Assistance from [Israel](9282173.md) was sought to refurbish and upgrade all operational [F-7s and FT-7s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-7): these were to get the [Elta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elta_Systems) [EL/M-2032](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EL/M-2032) air-to-air radar,[^cite_note-8] [Rafael Python](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rafael_Python) Mk. III and even [Litening](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litening) laser-designator pods. The same equipment was then installed on the two-seater FT-7 fighter trainers as well. In a related deal, Israel transferred at least one consignment of laser-guided bombs, but no deliveries of any other weapons are known. Since the [Elbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbit) contract was won in 1997, the air force had acquired at least one more squadron of F-7 and FT-7 aircraft from China, but these were not upgraded.

Between 1992 and 2000, the Myanmar Air Force took delivery of 36 [A-5C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanchang_A-5) ground attack aircraft. In addition, the Myanmar Air Force also bought 20 [Soko G-4 Super Galeb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soko_G-4_Super_Galeb) armed jet trainers from [Yugoslavia](34244.md) in 1991, but only approximately 6 aircraft were delivered due to the breakup of Yugoslavia.



President [Htin Kyaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Htin_Kyaw) and Indian Prime Minister [Narendra Modi](444222.md), pass in front of the MAF Honour Guards during an arrival ceremony at the [Presidential Palace, Naypyidaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidential_Palace,_Naypyidaw) in 2017.

The Myanmar Air Force procured a range of helicopters from [Russia](25391.md) and [Poland](22936.md) between 1991 and 1997; it bought 20 PZL-Swidnik [Mil Mi-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-2) and 13 [PZL W-3 Sokol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PZL_W-3_Sokol) helicopters from Poland and 13 [Mil Mi-17](1493374.md) from [Russia](25391.md). These helicopters were put into counter-insurgency operations against ethnic rebels in the [Irrawaddy River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrawaddy_River) delta. Four [Mil Mi-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-2), four [PZL W-3 Sokol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PZL_W-3_Sokol), and two [Bell 205](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_205) helicopters were grouped as an air detachment stationed in [Bogalay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogalay) for "Operation Monediang" in October 1991. During this operation, Mil Mi-2 helicopters were fitted with a wide range of weapons to provide ground attack and air cover for heliborne [air assault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_assault) operations. Four Mi-2s of the air detachment made a total of 80 sorties over 17 targets with nearly 82 flying hours. Four PZL W-3 Sokol helicopters, unarmed and used for troop transport carrying 20 airborne commandos, each flew 443 missions with 197 flying hours. [Bell 205](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_205) helicopters carried out [search and rescue](172599.md), and they flew 263 missions with over 114 flying hours.[^cite_note-Myoe-4]

In 2001, the Myanmar Air Force bought 12 [MiG-29](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-29) fighter aircraft (10 MiG-29Bs and two MiG-29UB two seats trainers)[^cite_note-Myoe-4] from [Belarus](3457.md). This was followed by an additional order of 20 MiG-29 (10 MiG-29B, 6 MiG-29SE and 4 MiG-29UB) as part of a \$570 million defence package in December 2009. 10 MiG-29B were upgraded to SM (mod) standard in 2017.[^cite_note-Myanmar_MiG-29_Upgrade_Revealed-9] The air force also ordered 10 [Mil Mi-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-35) gunship helicopters as part of a \$71 million defence package signed in December 2009.[^cite_note-mizzima-10]

Despite these modernisation measures, the capability of the Myanmar Air Force remained questionable, due to its absence during the [Battle of Border Post 9631](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Border_Post_9631?action=edit\&redlink=1) with [Thailand](30128.md) and the rescue missions related to [Cyclone Nargis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyclone_Nargis) in May 2008.

A contract had been signed in December 2015 with [Pakistan](23235.md) for the purchase of [JF-17 Thunder](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JF-17_Thunder) multirole fighter, which was jointly developed by [Chengdu Aircraft Industry Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_Aircraft_Industry_Group) and [Pakistan Aeronautical Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pakistan_Aeronautical_Complex), to Myanmar Air Force. However, in March 2018 it was reported that the deal for the purchase of JF-17 Thunder has been suspended by Pakistan.[^cite_note-11] However, four JF-17 Block-IIs were seen at Air Force Day celebrated in December 2018. Under a bilateral contract, the MAF ordered six [Su-30](552808.md)SM fighters from [Russia](25391.md) in 2018.[^cite_note-12] Six [HAL HJT-16 Kiran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HAL_HJT-16_Kiran) trainers were delivered as a gift from [ex-Indian Airforce](403355.md) stocks partly to counter Chinese clout and partly as reciprocity for co-operation from the [Tatmadaw](20394.md) in Indian Operation against [Naga insurgents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naga_Conflict).[^cite_note-\:1-13]

### Myanmar civil war (2021-present)

Since the [military coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myanmar_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) in February 2021, Myanmar Air Force aircraft have been used in airstrikes on villages,[^cite_note-14] killing noncombatant civilians including elders, humanitarian workers and children while forcing thousands of others to flee their homes.

On 11 April 2023, the Myanmar air force conducted a relentless airstrike on Pazigyi village in Kantbalu Township, Sagaing Region, killing at least 100 people, including children. This was the junta's deadliest airstrike after the coup. The attack targeted an opening ceremony of a local administration office. The junta spokesperson reasoned that high casualties were due to the secondary explosion of munitions stored in the area. However, ground sources confirmed that most of the casualties are civilians, including children. The airstrike was conducted by a fighter jet and later by a Mi-35 gunship.[^cite_note-15]

On the morning of January 7, 2024, the Myanmar Air Force conducted an airstrike on Kanan village, Tamu Township, Sagaing Region, hitting a civilian area, with bombs landing near St Peter Baptist Church and a nearby school compound while residents were gathered for Sunday service. The strike killed at least 17 civilians, including nine children, and injured over 20 people.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17]

On 12 May 2025, Myanmar air force carried out an airstrike on Ohe Htein Twin village in [Tabayin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabayin) Township, Sagaing Region, that struck a school building. According to local resistance members, aid workers, and media reports, the attack killed up to 20 students and two teachers, and wounded dozens of other draftees. The incident occurred in the morning and added to the mounting civilian toll of the conflict in central Myanmar.[^cite_note-18]

On 25 August 2025, Myanmar's air force carried out an airstrike on the Daing Kyi quarter of [Mrauk-U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mrauk-U), a historic town in Rakhine State known for its temples and pagodas. The attack killed at least 12 people, including children, according to the Arakan Army (AA), which has controlled the town since January 2024.[^cite_note-19]

On 12 September 2025, Myanmar's air force conducted an airstrike on a private boarding school in Thayat Tabin village, [Kyauktaw](15160309.md) Township, Rakhine State. According to local media and residents, two 500-pound bombs struck the Pyinnya Pan Khinn High School and its surroundings at around 1 a.m., killing 22 people, most of them students.[^cite_note-20] Kyauktaw town has been under the control of the Arakan Army since January 2024.[^cite_note-21]

On 10 December 2025 at midnight, Myanmar's air force [bombed the Mrauk-U General Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Mrauk-U_hospital_airstrike), leaving more than 30 deaths and multiple injuries.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23]

On 23 February 2026, a bazaar in Yoe-ngu Village, Ponnagyun Township, was reportedly bombed by Myanmar's Air Force, leaving 15 people dead and several others injured.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

### Air defence

Main article: [Office of the Chief of Air Defence (Myanmar)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Chief_of_Air_Defence_\(Myanmar\))

The Office of the chief of Air Defence is one of the major branches of the [Tatmadaw](20394.md). It was established as the Air Defence Command in 1997 but was not fully operational until late 1999. It was renamed the Bureau of Air Defence in the early 2000s. In the early 2000s, the Tatmadaw established the Myanmar Integrated Air Defence System (MIADS) with help from [Russia](25391.md), [Ukraine](31750.md) and [China](5405.md). It is a tri-service bureau with units from all three branches of [Myanmar Armed Forces](20394.md). All Air Defence assets except Anti-Aircraft Artillery are integrated into MIADS.[^cite_note-IndraStra_Global_Editorial_Team-26]

### Radars

The Air Force has several radar installations including the three-dimensional surveillance [YLC-2 Radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/YLC-2_Radar), the [P-37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P-35_radar) [Early-warning radar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early-warning_radar) system, the [JLP-40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/JLP-40_Radar) defensive radar, and the Galaxy [EWR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early-warning_radar) system, which is linked with [Integrated Air Defence office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_the_Chief_of_Air_Defence_\(Myanmar\)).[^cite_note-IndraStra_Global_Editorial_Team-26][^cite_note-42][^cite_note-archive.org-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-45][^cite_note-SIPRI-40]

## Markings

Myanmar [national insignia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_aircraft_insignia) (white triangle with yellow field in the centre and borders in blue) is usually applied on six positions. The serialling system of Myanmar Air Force aircraft is suggested to serve as both – unit and individual aircraft identity, this could not be confirmed so far, however. Most of the older aeroplanes carried the serials with the prefix "UB" and the numbers in Burmese. Sometimes the serials were outlined in white. Combat aircraft generally carry serials in black.

## Accidents and incidents

On 24 January 1980, a Burma Air Force [FH-227](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FH-227) crashed when an engine failed shortly after take-off, killing all but one of the 44 people on board. One person on the ground was injured.[^cite_note-46]

On 11 June 2014, a [MiG-29UB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-29) caught fire and crashed on to farmland near [Myothit township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Myothit_Township) of [Magway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Magway,_Myanmar) at 8\:30 a.m. (local time). The two pilots safely ejected.[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48][^cite_note-49]

On 10 February 2016, a [Beech 1900](2192224.md) aircraft crashed after taking off from [Naypidaw Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nay_Pyi_Taw_International_Airport), killing 5 military personnel.[^cite_note-50]

On 14 June 2016, an [Mil Mi-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-2) helicopter crashed near the [Yangon–Mandalay Expressway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangon%E2%80%93Mandalay_Expressway) at being refuelled at the [Taungngu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taung-Ngu) airbase, killing three military servicemen on board.[^cite_note-51]

[2017 Myanmar Air Force Shaanxi Y-8 crash](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2017_Myanmar_Air_Force_Shaanxi_Y-8_crash): On 7 June 2017, a [Shaanxi Y-8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shaanxi_Y-8) was reported missing 30 nautical miles (56 km) west to [Dawei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dawei). The aircraft was carrying 122 people. There were no survivors.

On 3 April 2018, an F-7 fighter aircraft of Taungoo Air Base crashed into a farm near KyunKone Village in [Taungoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taungoo). The aircraft was on a training route during the crash while trying to land on the ground at around 11\:30 am, killing its pilot.[^cite_note-52]

On 16 October 2018, two F-7Ms crashed near [Magway](493360.md), [Myanmar](19457.md), killing both pilots and a civilian on the ground. Both aircraft struck a broadcast tower. One plummeted into a rice paddy, while the other nose-dived near a Buddhist pagoda in the [Magway region](493360.md) of central Myanmar.[^cite_note-nyt-53]

On 3 May 2021, one Mi-35 helicopter was shot down near the town of [Moemauk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Momauk_Township) in Kachin province by the [Kachin Independence Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_Independence_Army) in response to the MAF's air raid. There was no confirmation from the MAF nor the [KIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_Independence_Army) on which AA system was used by the KIA in the incident.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55]

On 11 June 2021, a [Beechcraft 1900](2192224.md) crashed on its landing approach to [Pyin Oo Lwin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyin_Oo_Lwin)'s airport, killing 12 people including a senior Buddhist monk, the abbot of Zay Kone Monastery in [Pyinmana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pyinmana).[^cite_note-56]

On 16 February 2022, an A-5 ground-attack jet crashed near Ohn Taw village in [Sagaing Region](493361.md).[^cite_note-A5_crash-57]

On 29 March 2022, an [Mi-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mi-17) helicopter crashed and injured five people on board near [Hakha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakha), [Chin State](493366.md).[^cite_note-58]

On 11 November 2023, a K-8W trainer aircraft of the Myanmar Air Force crashed in [Hpruso Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hpruso_Township), [Karenni State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karenni_State). Local rebels claimed to have shot it down, while the Myanmar Air Force claimed that it was a mechanical failure, and the pilot was later captured.[^cite_note-59]

On 3 January 2024, an Mi-17 was shot down by the [Kachin Independence Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_Independence_Army) using [FN-6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FN-6) MANPADS in Waimaw Township, [Kachin State](493369.md), killing all seven people on board.[^cite_note-60]

On 16 January 2024, the [Kachin Independence Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_Independence_Army) successfully shot down an FTC-2000G trainer/fighter of the Myanmar Air Force in the Namhpatkar area of [northern Shan State](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Shan_State).[^cite_note-61]

In January 2024, a Myanmar Air Force Y-8 on a mission to evacuate troops who had sought refuge in [Mizoram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mizoram), [India](14533.md), overshot its landing in [Lengpui Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lengpui_Airport). There were no deaths but the plane was badly damaged.

On 29 January 2024, the [Karen National Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karen_National_Liberation_Army) shot down a MAF helicopter above [Myawaddy Township](15144429.md) near the Thai border. During the incident, Brigadier General Aye Min Naung, the 44th LI Division commander, Colonel Soe Tun Lwin, LI Battalion 9's acting commander, pilot Colonel Toe Oo and two army captains were supposedly killed according to military sources.[^cite_note-62]

On 29 February 2024, a MiG-29SMT fighter of Myanmar Air Force crashed in the southwest of Salin District, Magway Region. This aircraft crashed when it was on its way to a combat mission. The Myanmar military blamed the crash on a technical failure. One pilot ejected successfully and escaped the crash.[^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64]

On 2 August 2024, a Myanmar Air Force Eurocopter AS365 Dauphin crashed after takeoff near [Hmawbi Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmawbi_Air_Base), Yangon. The aircraft was on a training flight and the cause of the crash was blamed on an engine failure. 2 were injured and 2 were killed. The deceased were identified as Maj-Gen [Soe Tin Latt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soe_Tin_Latt) and Copilot Col Myo Thaung.[^cite_note-65]

On 20 May 2025, a Myanmar Air Force [Mil Mi-17](1493374.md) was shot down by an [FPV drone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First-person_view_\(radio_control\)) of the [Kachin Independence Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kachin_Independence_Army) while transporting reinforcements to the city of [Bhamo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bhamo_\(2024\)), and crashed 14 miles from [Shwegu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shwegu). Reportedly, all 18 passengers on board were killed, including the pilot.[^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67]

On 10 June 2025, an [F-7M](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chengdu_J-7) fighter jet crashed into a house in Sabarsae village in Pearl township during an air operation to assist defending of battle against Myanmar pro-Democracy Alliance Forces. [People's Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Liberation_Army_\(Myanmar\)) (PLA), one of the rebel militia group, participating in operation, claims that they shot down the jet. However, spoke person from other rebel group denied and said it crashed itself.&#x20;

On 1 July 2025, a [Guizhou FTC-2000G](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guizhou_FTC-2000G) trainer/fighter jet lost contact due to severe weather or technical malfunction during night flying mission.[^cite_note-68] On 3 Jul 2025, a video of the member of [KNDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karenni_Nationalities_Defence_Force) forces celebrating on the wreckage of crashed jet appear on social media. Both pilots were killed.[^cite_note-69]

A Mil Mi-17 helicopter was struck by a suicide drone on 23 June 2026 by the insurgent forces. The aircraft was struck while preparing for takeoff. [^cite_note-70]

Two unidentified aircraft were damaged beyond repair on 10 September 2026 following an FPV drone attack conducted by the [People's Defense Forces](67819275.md) (PDF) at [Mandalay International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandalay_International_Airport). Two others were damaged.[^cite_note-71]
