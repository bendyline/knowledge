**Libya**,[^cite_note-6] officially the **State of Libya**,[^cite_note-9] is a country in the [Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maghreb) region of [North Africa](21714.md). It borders the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md) to the north, [Egypt](8087628.md) to [the east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Libya_border), [Sudan](27421.md) to [the southeast](62950857.md), [Chad](5488.md) to [the south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad%E2%80%93Libya_border), [Niger](21373.md) to [the southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya%E2%80%93Niger_border), [Algeria](358.md) to [the west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria%E2%80%93Libya_border), and [Tunisia](30188.md) to [the northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya%E2%80%93Tunisia_border). With an area of almost 1.8 million km<sup>2</sup> (700,000 sq mi), Libya is the [fourth-largest country in Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_African_countries_by_area) and the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world), and the [16th-largest in the world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_outlying_territories_by_total_area).[^cite_note-10] The country claims 32,000 square kilometres of southeastern Algeria, south of the Libyan town of [Ghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghat,_Libya).[^cite_note-CIA-1][^cite_note-11] The capital and [largest city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_cities_in_Libya) is [Tripoli](31232.md), located in the northwest and containing over a million of Libya's seven million people.[^cite_note-12]

Libya has been inhabited by [Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_people) since the late [Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bronze_Age) as descendants from [Iberomaurusian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iberomaurusian) and [Capsian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capsian) cultures. In classical antiquity, the [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) established city-states and trading posts in western Libya, while several [Greek cities](75451.md) were established in the East. Parts of Libya were variously ruled by [Carthaginians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Carthage), [Numidians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numidia), [Persians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Achaemenid_Empire), and [Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Kingdom) before the entire region became a part of the [Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Empire). Libya was an [early centre of Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Early_Christianity#Libya). After the [fall of the Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_the_Western_Roman_Empire), the area of Libya was mostly occupied by the [Vandals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandal_Kingdom) until the 7th century when [invasions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Muslim_conquest_of_the_Maghreb) brought [Islam](6037917.md) to the region. From then on, centuries of [Arab migration to the Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_migration_to_the_Maghreb) shifted the demographic scope of Libya in favour of [Arabs](2185.md). In the 16th century, the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) and the [Knights Hospitaller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitaller) occupied Tripoli until [Ottoman](22278.md) rule [began in 1551](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Tripolitania). Libya was involved in the [Barbary Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_Wars) of the 18th and 19th centuries. Ottoman rule continued until the [Italo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Turkish_War) in 1911, which resulted in [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) occupying Libya and establishing two [colonies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_colonization_of_Libya): [Italian Tripolitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Tripolitania) and [Italian Cyrenaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Cyrenaica), later unified in the [Italian Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libya) colony from 1934 to 1943.[^cite_note-13]

During [World War II](32927.md), Libya was an area of warfare in the [North African Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_Campaign). The [Italian population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libyans) then went into decline and Libya became independent as a [kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Libya) in 1951. A [bloodless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nonviolent_revolution) [military coup in 1969](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Libyan_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), initiated by a coalition led by Colonel [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md), overthrew [King Idris I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idris_of_Libya) and created a [republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic). Gaddafi was often described by critics as a [dictator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dictator), and was one of the world's longest serving non-royal leaders. He [ruled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Libya_under_Muammar_Gaddafi) for 42 years until being overthrown and [killed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Death_of_Muammar_Gaddafi) in the [2011 civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Libyan_Civil_War), which was part of the wider [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring), with authority transferred to the [National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council) then to the elected [General National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_National_Congress).

Since 2011, Libya has been involved in a [political and humanitarian crisis](44791215.md), and by 2014, two rival authorities claimed to govern Libya, which led to a [second civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Libyan_Civil_War), with parts of Libya split between separate governments, based in [Tripoli](31232.md) and [Tobruk](405871.md), as well as various tribal and [Islamist](15012.md) militias. The two main warring sides signed a permanent ceasefire in 2020, and a [unity government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\)) took authority to plan for democratic elections, though political rivalries continue to delay this.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-rival-15][^cite_note-guardian-20140909-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] In March 2022, the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Libya\)) ceased recognising the [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\)) and proclaimed an alternative government, the [Government of National Stability](71621370.md) (GNS). Both governments have been functioning simultaneously since then, which has led to dual power in Libya. The international community continues to recognise the unity government as the legitimate government of the country.

Libya is a developing country ranking 115th by HDI, and has the [10th-largest proven oil reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_by_proven_oil_reserves) in the world. Libya has the highest level of greenhouse gas emissions per person in Africa, but has made little progress toward developing climate commitments. Libya is a member of the [United Nations](31769.md), the [Non-Aligned Movement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-Aligned_Movement), the [African Union](10634933.md), the [Arab League](52625.md), the [Organisation of Islamic Cooperation](47725.md), and [OPEC](166346.md). The country's official religion is [Islam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islam_in_Libya), with 96.6% of the Libyan population being [Sunni Muslims](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam).[^cite_note-CIA-1] The official language of Libya is [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Standard_Arabic), with vernacular [Libyan Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Arabic) being spoken most widely. The majority of Libya's population is [Arab](2185.md).[^cite_note-Encyclopaedia_Britannica,_Inc.-2014-22][^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24]

## Etymology

Further information: [Ancient Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Libya) and [Libu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libu)



Archaeological site of [Sabratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabratha), Libya

The origin of the name "Libya" first appeared in an inscription of [Ramesses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramesses_II), written as *[rbw](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/rbw)* in [hieroglyphic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hieroglyphic). The name derives from a generalised identity given to a large confederacy of ancient east "Libyan" [Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers), [North African](21714.md) people(s) and tribes who lived around the lush regions of [Cyrenaica](75451.md) and [Marmarica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marmarica). An army of 40,000 men[^cite_note-3.8_MERENPTAH_(1236-1230_BC)-25] and a confederacy of tribes known as "Great Chiefs of the [Libu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libu)" were led by King [Meryey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meryey) who fought a war against [pharaoh](23294.md) [Merneptah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merneptah) in year 5 (1208 BC). This conflict was mentioned in the [Great Karnak Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Karnak_Inscription) in the western delta during the 5th and 6th years of his reign and resulted in [a defeat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Perire) for Meryey. According to the [Great Karnak Inscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Karnak_Inscription), the military alliance comprised the [Meshwesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meshwesh), the [Lukka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lukka), and the "Sea Peoples" known as the [Ekwesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ekwesh), [Teresh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_Peoples), [Shekelesh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shekelesh), and the [Sherden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sherden).

The Great Karnak Inscription reads:

> "... the third season, saying: 'The wretched, fallen chief of Libya, Meryey, son of Ded, has fallen upon the country of Tehenu with his bowmen—Sherden, Shekelesh, Ekwesh, Lukka, Teresh. Taking the best of every warrior and every man of war of his country. He has brought his wife and his children—leaders of the camp, and he has reached the western boundary in the fields of Perire."

The name "Libya" was brought back into use in 1903 by Italian geographer Federico Minutilli. It was intended to supplant terms applied to [Ottoman Tripolitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Tripolitania), the coastal region of what is today Libya, having been ruled by the Ottoman Empire from 1551 to 1911 as the Eyalet of Tripolitania.[^cite_note-be177-26]

Libya gained independence in 1951 as the [United Libyan Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Libya) (المملكة الليبية المتحدة *_al-Mamlakah al-Lībiyyah al-Muttaḥidah_*), changing its name to the Kingdom of Libya (المملكة الليبية *_al-Mamlakah al-Lībiyyah_*), literally "Libyan Kingdom", in 1963.[^cite_note-LoC1987-27] Following a coup d'état led by [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) in 1969, the name of the state was changed to the Libyan Arab Republic (الجمهورية العربية الليبية *_al-Jumhūriyyah al-'Arabiyyah al-Lībiyyah_*). The official name was "Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriya" from 1977 to 1986 (الجماهيرية العربية الليبية الشعبية الاشتراكية), and "Great Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriya"[^cite_note-name-28] (الجماهيرية العربية الليبية الشعبية الاشتراكية العظمى,[^cite_note-29] *_al-Jamāhīriyyah al-'Arabiyyah al-Lībiyyah ash-Sha'biyyah al-Ishtirākiyyah al-'Udmá_* [listen](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/2/2a/Al-Jamahiriyyah_al-Arabiyyah_al-Libiyyah_ash-shabiyyah_al-Ishtirakiyyah_al-Udhma.ogg/Al-Jamahiriyyah_al-Arabiyyah_al-Libiyyah_ash-shabiyyah_al-Ishtirakiyyah_al-Udhma.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>) from 1986 to 2011.

The [National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council), established in 2011, referred to the state as simply "Libya". The [United Nations](31769.md) (UN) formally recognised the country as "Libya" in September 2011[^cite_note-30] based on a request from the Permanent Mission of Libya citing the [Libyan interim Constitutional Declaration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_interim_Constitutional_Declaration) of 3 August 2011. In November 2011, the [ISO 3166-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISO_3166-1) was altered to reflect the new country name "Libya" in [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language), *"Libye (la)"* in [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language).[^cite_note-31]

In December 2017, the Permanent Mission of Libya to the United Nations informed the UN that the country's official name was henceforth the "State of Libya"; "Libya" remained the official short form, and the country continued to be listed under "L" in alphabetical lists.[^cite_note-32]

## History

Main article: [History of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Libya)

### Ancient Libya

Main articles: [Ancient Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Libya) and [Libu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libu)



[Leptis Magna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leptis_Magna)

The coastal plain of Libya was inhabited by [Neolithic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neolithic) peoples from as early as 8000 BC. The [Afroasiatic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afroasiatic_languages) ancestors of the [Berber people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_people) are assumed to have spread into the area by the [Late Bronze Age](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Late_Bronze_Age). The earliest known name of such a tribe was the [Garamantes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Garamantes), based in [Germa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germa). The [Phoenicians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phoenicia) were the first to establish trading posts in Libya.[^cite_note-33] By the 5th century BC, the greatest of the Phoenician colonies, [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage), had extended its [hegemony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hegemony) across much of North Africa, where a distinctive civilisation, known as [Punic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Punic), came into being.

In 630 BC, the [ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) colonised the area around [Barca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barca_\(ancient_city\)) in Eastern Libya and founded the city of [Cyrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrene,_Libya).[^cite_note-34] Within 200 years, four more important Greek cities were established in the area that became known as [Cyrenaica](75451.md).[^cite_note-35] The area was home to the renowned philosophy school of the [Cyrenaics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrenaics). In 525 BC the [Persian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_people) army of [Cambyses II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambyses_II) overran Cyrenaica, which for the next two centuries remained under Persian or Egyptian rule. [Alexander the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_the_Great) ended Persian rule in 331 BC and received tribute from Cyrenaica. Eastern Libya again fell under the control of the Greeks, this time as part of the [Ptolemaic Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemaic_Kingdom).



[Septimius Severus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Septimius_Severus), the first Roman emperor native to [Roman Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Africa), was born in Leptis Magna.

After the fall of [Carthage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carthage) the Romans did not immediately occupy [Tripolitania](664792.md) (the region around Tripoli), but left it instead under control of the kings of [Numidia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Numidia), until the coastal cities asked and obtained its protection.[^cite_note-be202-36] [Ptolemy Apion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ptolemy_Apion), the last Greek ruler, bequeathed Cyrenaica to Rome, which formally annexed the region in 74 BC and joined it to Crete as [a Roman province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Creta_et_Cyrenaica). As part of the [Africa Nova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Africa_Nova) province, Tripolitania was prosperous,[^cite_note-be202-36] and reached a golden age in the 2nd and 3rd centuries, when the city of [Leptis Magna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leptis_Magna), home to the [Severan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Severan_dynasty), was at its height.[^cite_note-be202-36]

On the eastern side, Cyrenaica's first Christian communities were established by the time of the Emperor [Claudius](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Claudius).[^cite_note-be417-37] It was heavily devastated during the [Kitos War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitos_War)[^cite_note-ro364-38] and almost depopulated of Greeks and Jews alike.[^cite_note-39] Although repopulated by [Trajan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trajan) with military colonies,[^cite_note-ro364-38] from then started its decline.[^cite_note-be417-37] Libya was early to convert to [Nicene Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicene_Christianity) and was the home of [Pope Victor I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pope_Victor_I); however, Libya was also home to many non-Nicene varieties of early Christianity, such as [Arianism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arianism) and [Donatism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donatism).

### Islamic Libya: 647–1556

Main article: [Islamic Tripolitania and Cyrenaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Islamic_Tripolitania_and_Cyrenaica)



The [Atiq Mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atiq_Mosque,_Awjila) in [Awjila](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awjila) is the oldest mosque in the [Sahara](325363.md).

Under the command of [Amr ibn al-As](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amr_ibn_al-As), the [Rashidun army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashidun_army) conquered [Cyrenaica](75451.md).[^cite_note-be278-40] In 647 an army led by [Abdullah ibn Saad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_ibn_Saad) took Tripoli from the Byzantines definitively.[^cite_note-be278-40] The [Fezzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fezzan) was conquered by [Uqba ibn Nafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uqba_ibn_Nafi) in 663. The Berber tribes of the hinterland accepted Islam, however they resisted Arab political rule.[^cite_note-41] For the next several decades, Libya was under the purview of the [Umayyad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Umayyad_Caliphate) [Caliph of Damascus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caliph_of_Damascus) until the [Abbasids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abbasid_Caliphate) overthrew the Umayyads in 750, and Libya came under the rule of Baghdad. When Caliph [Harun al-Rashid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harun_al-Rashid) appointed [Ibrahim ibn al-Aghlab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibrahim_I_ibn_al-Aghlab) as his governor of [Ifriqiya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ifriqiya) in 800, Libya enjoyed considerable local autonomy under the [Aghlabid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aghlabid) dynasty. By the 10th century, the Shiite [Fatimids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatimids) controlled Western Libya, and ruled the entire region in 972 and appointed [Bologhine ibn Ziri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bologhine_ibn_Ziri) as governor.[^cite_note-be202-36]

Ibn Ziri's Berber [Zirid dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zirid_dynasty) ultimately broke away from the Shiite Fatimids, and recognised the Sunni Abbasids of Baghdad as rightful Caliphs. In retaliation, the Fatimids brought about the migration of thousands from mainly two Arab Qaisi tribes, the [Banu Sulaym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Sulaym) and [Banu Hilal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Hilal) to [North Africa](21714.md). This act drastically altered the fabric of the Libyan countryside, and cemented the cultural and linguistic Arabisation of the region.[^cite_note-be202-36]

Zirid rule in Tripolitania was short-lived though, and already in 1001 the Berbers of the [Banu Khazrun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Khazrun) broke away. Tripolitania remained under their control until 1146, when the region was overtaken by the [Normans of Sicily](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Normans_of_Sicily).[^cite_note-be203-42] For the next 50 years, Tripolitania was the scene of numerous battles among [Ayyubids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ayyubid_dynasty), the [Almohad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almohad_Caliphate) rulers and insurgents of the [Banu Ghaniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Ghaniya). Later, a general of the Almohads, Muhammad ibn Abu Hafs, ruled Libya from 1207 to 1221 before the later establishment of the Tunisian [Hafsid Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hafsid_dynasty)[^cite_note-be203-42] independent from the Almohads. In the 14th century, the [Banu Thabit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Thabit) dynasty ruled Tripolitania before reverting to direct Hafsid control. By the 16th century, the Hafsids became increasingly caught up in the power struggle between Spain and the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).

After Abbasid control was weakened, Cyrenaica was under Egypt-based states such as the [Tulunids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tulunids), [Ikhshidids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ikhshidid_dynasty), Ayyubids, and [Mamluks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mamluk_Sultanate) before Ottoman conquest in 1517. [Fezzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fezzan) acquired independence under Awlad Muhammad dynasty after [Kanem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kanem%E2%80%93Bornu_Empire) rule. Ottomans finally conquered Fezzan between 1556 and 1577.

### Ottoman Tripolitania: 1556–1911

Main article: [Ottoman Tripolitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Tripolitania)



The [siege of Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Tripoli_\(1551\)) in 1551 allowed the [Ottomans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Navy) to capture the city from the [Knights of St. John](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitaller).

After a successful invasion of Tripoli by [Habsburg Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habsburg_Spain) in 1510,[^cite_note-be203-42] and its handover to the [Knights Hospitaller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knights_Hospitaller), the [Ottoman](22278.md) admiral [Sinan Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinan_Pasha_\(Ottoman_admiral\)) took control of Libya in 1551.[^cite_note-be203-42] His successor [Turgut Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turgut_Reis) was named the [Bey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bey) of Tripoli and later [Pasha of Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasha_of_Tripoli) in 1556. By 1565, administrative authority as regent in Tripoli was vested in a *[pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasha)* appointed directly by the *[sultan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sultan)* in [Constantinople](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantinople)/[Istanbul](3391396.md). In the 1580s, the rulers of [Fezzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fezzan) gave their allegiance to the sultan, and although Ottoman authority was absent in [Cyrenaica](75451.md), a *bey* was stationed in Benghazi late in the next century to act as agent of the government in Tripoli.[^cite_note-be417-37] European [slaves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbary_slave_trade) and large numbers of enslaved Blacks transported from Sudan were also a feature of everyday life in Tripoli. In 1551, [Turgut Reis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turgut_Reis) enslaved almost the entire population of the Maltese island of [Gozo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gozo), some 5,000 people, sending them to Libya.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44]

In time, real power came to rest with the pasha's corps of [janissaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Janissaries).[^cite_note-be203-42] In 1611 the *[deys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dey)* staged a coup against the pasha, and Dey Sulayman Safar was appointed as head of government. For the next hundred years, a series of *deys* effectively ruled Tripolitania. The two most important Deys were [Mehmed Saqizli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mehmed_Saqizli) (r. 1631–49) and [Osman Saqizli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Osman_Saqizli) (r. 1649–72), both also Pasha, who ruled effectively the region.[^cite_note-be204-45] The latter conquered also Cyrenaica.[^cite_note-be204-45]



A view of Tripoli, c. 1766

Lacking direction from the Ottoman government, Tripoli lapsed into a period of military anarchy during which coup followed coup and few deys survived in office more than a year. One such coup was led by Turkish officer [Ahmed Karamanli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Karamanli).[^cite_note-be204-45] The [Karamanlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karamanli_dynasty) ruled from 1711 until 1835 mainly in Tripolitania, and had influence in Cyrenaica and Fezzan as well by the mid-18th century. Ahmed's successors proved to be less capable than himself, however, the region's delicate balance of power allowed the Karamanli. The [1793–95 Tripolitanian civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1793%E2%80%9395_Tripolitanian_civil_war) occurred in those years. In 1793, Turkish officer [Ali Pasha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trabluslu_Ali_Pasha) deposed Hamet Karamanli and briefly restored Tripolitania to Ottoman rule. Hamet's brother [Yusuf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yusuf_Karamanli) (r. 1795–1832) re-established Tripolitania's independence. In 1816, Yusuf ordered the [massacre of the al-Jawazi tribe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al-Jawazi_massacre).[^cite_note-46]



U.S. naval officer [Stephen Decatur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_Decatur) boarding a Tripolitan gunboat during the [First Barbary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Barbary_War), 1804

In the early 19th century war broke out between the United States and Tripolitania, and a series of battles ensued in what came to be known as the [First Barbary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Barbary_War) and the [Second Barbary War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Barbary_War). By 1819, the various treaties of the [Napoleonic Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Napoleonic_Wars) had forced the Barbary states to give up piracy almost entirely, and Tripolitania's economy began to crumble. As Yusuf weakened, factions sprung up around his three sons. Civil war soon resulted.[^cite_note-be205-47]

Ottoman Sultan [Mahmud II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_II) sent in troops ostensibly to restore order, marking the end of both the Karamanli dynasty and an independent Tripolitania.[^cite_note-be205-47] Order was not recovered easily, and the revolt of the Libyan under Abd-El-Gelil and Gûma ben Khalifa lasted until the death of the latter in 1858.[^cite_note-be205-47] The second period of direct Ottoman rule saw administrative changes, and greater order in the governance of the three provinces of Libya. Ottoman rule finally reasserted to Fezzan between 1850 and 1875 for earning income from Saharan commerce.

### Italian colonisation and Allied occupation (1911–1951)

Main articles: [Italian Tripolitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Tripolitania), [Italian Cyrenaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Cyrenaica), and [Italian Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libya)

See also: [Italian colonization of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_colonization_of_Libya)



[Omar Mukhtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Mukhtar) was a prominent leader of Libyan resistance in Cyrenaica against Italian colonisation.

After the [Italo-Turkish War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Turkish_War) (1911–1912), Italy simultaneously turned the three regions into colonies.[^cite_note-BBCLibyaTimeline-48] From 1912 to 1927, the territory of Libya was known as [Italian North Africa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libya). From 1927 to 1934, the territory was split into two colonies, [Italian Cyrenaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Cyrenaica) and [Italian Tripolitania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Tripolitania), run by Italian governors. Some 150,000 Italians settled in Libya, constituting roughly 20% of the total population.[^cite_note-49]

[Omar Mukhtar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omar_Mukhtar) rose to prominence as a [resistance leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_resistance_movement) against Italian colonisation and became a national hero despite his capture and execution on 16 September 1931.[^cite_note-BBCLibyaTimeline-48] His face is currently printed on the Libyan ten dinar note in memory and recognition of his patriotism. Another prominent resistance leader, Idris al-Mahdi as-Senussi (later [King Idris I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idris_of_Libya)), Emir of Cyrenaica, continued to lead the Libyan resistance until the outbreak of the [Second World War](32927.md).

The so-called "[pacification of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacification_of_Libya)" by the Italians resulted in mass deaths of the indigenous people in Cyrenaica, killing approximately one quarter of Cyrenaica's population of 225,000.[^cite_note-50] [Ilan Pappé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilan_Papp%C3%A9) estimates that between 1928 and 1932 the Italian military "killed half the Bedouin population (directly or through disease and starvation in [Italian concentration camps in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_concentration_camps_in_Libya))."[^cite_note-51]



Italian propaganda postcard depicting the [Italian invasion of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_invasion_of_Libya) in 1911

In 1934, Italy combined [Cyrenaica](75451.md), [Tripolitania](664792.md) and [Fezzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fezzan) and adopted the name "Libya" (used by the [Ancient Greeks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ancient_Greece) for all of [North Africa](21714.md) except [Egypt](8087628.md)) for the unified colony, with [Tripoli](31232.md) as its [capital](181337.md).[^cite_note-52] The Italians emphasised infrastructure improvements and public works. In particular, they greatly expanded Libyan railway and road networks from 1934 to 1940, building hundreds of kilometres of new roads and railways and encouraging the establishment of new industries and dozens of new agricultural villages. In 1939, the coastal areas of Libya began to be administered as an integral part of Italy known as the [Fourth Shore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fourth_Shore).

In June 1940, [Italy entered World War II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_history_of_Italy_during_World_War_II). Libya became the setting for the hard-fought [North African Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_African_Campaign) that ultimately ended in defeat for Italy and its [German ally](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Afrika_Korps) in 1943. From 1943 to 1951, Libya was under [Allied occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_occupation_of_Libya). The British military [administered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Military_Administration_\(Libya\)) the two former Italian Libyan provinces of Tripolitana and Cyrenaïca, while the French [administered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Fezzan) the province of Fezzan. In 1944, Idris returned from exile in [Cairo](6293.md) but declined to resume permanent residence in Cyrenaica until the removal of some aspects of foreign control in 1947. Under the terms of the [1947 peace treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Peace_with_Italy,_1947) with the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II), Italy relinquished all claims to Libya.[^cite_note-53]

### Independent Kingdom (1951–1969)

Main article: [Kingdom of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Libya)



[King Idris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Idris) of Libya

A national assembly crafted a constitution that established a [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy) and extended an offer for the throne to [Sayyid Idris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idris_of_Libya), the [Emir of Cyrenaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emir_of_Cyrenaica).[^cite_note-History_Today-54] Sayyid Idris held the esteemed position as the leader of the influential [Senussi religious brotherhood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senusiyya), which was founded by his grandfather in the preceding century as a response to Western influence in the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world).[^cite_note-History_Today-54] This devout Islamic movement garnered significant support from the [desert Bedouin](61770.md) and became a major political force in Libya. During the declining years of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md), it effectively governed the Libyan interior.[^cite_note-History_Today-54] Born in an oasis in [Cyrenaica](75451.md) in 1890, Sayyid Idris assumed leadership of the Senussi at a young age. He spent a considerable period of exile in [Egypt](8087628.md) under Italian rule and returned to Libya after the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers) were ousted in 1943.[^cite_note-History_Today-54] On 24 December 1951, as King Idris I, he addressed the nation via radio from [Benghazi](204114.md).[^cite_note-History_Today-54] On 21 November 1949, the [UN General Assembly](31957.md) passed a resolution stating that Libya should become independent before 1 January 1952.[^cite_note-BBC_News-2021b-55] Idris represented Libya in the subsequent UN negotiations. By 24 December 1951, Libya declared its independence as the [United Kingdom of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Libya), a constitutional and hereditary [monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monarchy) under King [Idris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idris_I_of_Libya).[^cite_note-Schiller-2009-56]

However, the new kingdom faced challenging prospects.[^cite_note-History_Today-54] It lacked significant industry and agricultural resources.[^cite_note-History_Today-54] The kingdom's primary exports consisted of hides, wool, horses, and ostrich feathers.[^cite_note-History_Today-54] It had one of the lowest per capita incomes in the world and also one of the highest illiteracy rates.[^cite_note-History_Today-54] King Idris I, already in his sixties, had no direct heir to succeed him.[^cite_note-History_Today-54] His cousin, whom he had married in 1932, reportedly experienced numerous miscarriages, and their son, born in 1953, died shortly after birth.[^cite_note-History_Today-54] Crown Prince Rida, Idris's brother, was the designated heir, but the royal family was riddled with incessant disputes.[^cite_note-History_Today-54] King Idris's devout Muslim piety, which solidified his support among the [Bedouin population](61770.md), clashed with the modernising and urban intellectual currents in Libya.[^cite_note-History_Today-54] To address the rivalry between [Cyrenaica](75451.md) and [Tripolitania](664792.md), [Benghazi](204114.md) and [Tripoli](31232.md) alternated as the capital every two years.

The swift emergence of a large number of bureaucrats resulted in a costly royal government.[^cite_note-History_Today-54] The discovery of significant [oil reserves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_reserves) in 1959 and the subsequent income from [petroleum](23195.md) sales enabled one of the world's poorest nations to establish an extremely wealthy state.[^cite_note-Schiller-2009-56] Although oil drastically improved the Libyan government's finances, popular resentment began to build over the increased concentration of the nation's wealth in the hands of King Idris and the national elite.[^cite_note-BBC_News-2021b-55][^cite_note-Schiller-2009-56] This discontent continued to mount with the rise of [Nasserism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nasserism) and [Arab nationalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_nationalism) throughout [North Africa](21714.md) and the [Middle East](19323.md), that inspire many anti-royal coups across the [Arab world](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_world).[^cite_note-Schiller-2009-56]

### Libya under the Gaddafi regime (1969–2011)

Main article: [History of Libya under Muammar Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Libya_under_Muammar_Gaddafi)

On 1 September 1969, a group of rebel military officers led by [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) launched a [coup d'état against King Idris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Libyan_coup_d'%C3%A9tat), which became known as the Al Fateh Revolution.[^cite_note-SalakLibya-57][^cite_note-Bearman-58] Gaddafi was referred to as the "[Brother Leader and Guide of the Revolution](32860582.md)" in government statements and the official Libyan press.[^cite_note-59][^cite_note-Bearman-58] He began dominating history and politics of Libya for the next four decades.[^cite_note-Bearman-58] Moving to reduce Italian influence, in October 1970 all Italian-owned assets were expropriated and the 12,000-strong [Italian community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libyans) was [expelled](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_expulsion_of_Italians_from_Libya) from Libya alongside the smaller community of [Italian Libyan Jews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_the_Jews_in_Libya).[^cite_note-Bearman-58] The day became a [national holiday](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_holiday) known as "[Day of Revenge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day_of_Revenge)";[^cite_note-Bearman-58] it was later renamed the "Day of Friendship" because of an improvement in [Italy–Libya relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italy%E2%80%93Libya_relations).[^cite_note-Zoubir2009-60]<sup>: 403, 411 </sup>



[Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md), leader of Libya (r. 1969–2011)

Libya's increase in prosperity was accompanied by increased internal political repression, and political dissent was made illegal under Law 75 of 1973.[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] Widespread surveillance of the population was carried out through Gaddafi's Revolutionary Committees.[^cite_note-Gaddafiunrepentant-62][^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61][^cite_note-63] Gaddafi also wanted to ease the strict social restrictions imposed on women by the previous regime, establishing the [Revolutionary Women's Formation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Women's_Formation) to encourage reform.[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] In 1970, a law was introduced affirming equality of the sexes and wage parity.[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] In 1971, Gaddafi sponsored the creation of a Libyan General Women's Federation.[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] In 1972, a law was passed criminalising the marriage of girls under the age of sixteen and making the woman's consent a necessary prerequisite for a marriage.[^cite_note-64]

&#x20;On 25 October 1975, a coup attempt was launched by a group of 20 military officers, mostly from the city of [Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misrata).[^cite_note-65] This resulted in the arrest and executions of the coup plotters.[^cite_note-66] In March 1977, Libya officially became the "Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriya".[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] Gaddafi officially passed power to the [General People's Committees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_People's_Committee) and henceforth claimed to be no more than a symbolic figurehead.[^cite_note-67][^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] The new *jamahiriya* (Arab for "republic") governance structure he established was officially referred to as "[direct democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Direct_democracy)".[^cite_note-68]&#x20;



F-4J of VF-74 with Libyan MiG-23 over Gulf of Sidra in 1981

In February 1977, Libya started delivering military supplies to [Goukouni Oueddei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goukouni_Oueddei) and the [People's Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/People's_Armed_Forces) in Chad.[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] The [Chadian–Libyan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chadian%E2%80%93Libyan_War) began in earnest when Libya's support of rebel forces in northern Chad escalated into an [invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toyota_war).[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] Later that same year, Libya and [Egypt](8087628.md) fought a four-day border war that came to be known as the [Egyptian–Libyan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egyptian%E2%80%93Libyan_War).[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] Both nations agreed to a [ceasefire](592591.md) under the mediation of the Algerian president [Houari Boumédiène](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houari_Boum%C3%A9di%C3%A8ne).[^cite_note-69] Hundreds of Libyans lost their lives in the country's support for [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin)'s [Uganda](31816.md) in [its war against Tanzania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uganda%E2%80%93Tanzania_War).[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] Gaddafi financed various other groups from anti-nuclear movements to [Australian](4689264.md) trade unions.[^cite_note-70] On 2 March 1977, Libya officially became the [Great Socialist People's Libyan Arab Jamahiriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Libya_under_Muammar_Gaddafi#Great_Socialist_People's_Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya_\(1977%E2%80%932011\)).[^cite_note-71]

Libya adopted its plain green national flag on 19 November 1977.[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] The country had the only plain-coloured flag in the world until 2011, when Libya adopted [its current flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Libya). From 1977 onward, per capita income in the country rose to more than US\$11,000, the fifth-highest in Africa,[^cite_note-72] while the [Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_Development_Index) became the highest in Africa and greater than that of [Saudi Arabia](349303.md).[^cite_note-dailynews-73] This was achieved without borrowing any foreign loans, keeping Libya [debt-free](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_by_public_debt).[^cite_note-74] The [Great Manmade River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Manmade_River) was also built to allow free access to fresh water across large parts of the country.[^cite_note-dailynews-73] In addition, financial support was provided for university scholarships and employment programs.[^cite_note-75] Much of Libya's income from oil, which soared in the 1970s, was spent on arms purchases and on sponsoring dozens of paramilitaries and terrorist groups around the world.[^cite_note-76][^cite_note-77][^cite_note-78]

[An American airstrike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_United_States_bombing_of_Libya) led by then U.S. president [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) intended to kill Gaddafi failed in 1986.[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61] Libya was finally put under sanctions by the [United Nations](31769.md) after [the bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockerbie_bombing) of a commercial flight at [Lockerbie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockerbie) in 1988 killed 270 people.[^cite_note-79] In the 1990s, the government's rule was threatened by militant Islamism and an unsuccessful attempt to assassinate Gaddafi.[^cite_note-Martínez_2007_71-80] The government responded with repressive measures.[^cite_note-Martínez_2007_71-80] Riots and Islamic activisim were crushed by [Revolutionary Guard Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolutionary_Guard_Corps_\(Libyan_paramilitary_unit\)).[^cite_note-Martínez_2007_71-80] Nevertheless, [Cyrenaica](75451.md) between 1995 and 1998 was politically unstable, due to the tribal allegiances of the local troops.[^cite_note-Martínez_2007_71-80] In 2003, Gaddafi announced that all of his regime's [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_and_weapons_of_mass_destruction) were disassembled, and that Libya was transitioning toward [nuclear power](22153.md).[^cite_note-GlobalEdge-61]

### First Libyan Civil War and the fall of Gaddafi (2011)

Main articles: [First Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Libyan_Civil_War) and [2011 military intervention in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_military_intervention_in_Libya)



[U.S. Ambassador](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_the_United_States_to_Libya) [Cretz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gene_A._Cretz) Stands by Fist Crushing a US Fighter Plane Sculpture which was captured after the [fall of Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_Battle_of_Tripoli)

The first civil war came during the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) movements which overturned the rulers of [Tunisia](30188.md) and [Egypt](8087628.md). Libya first experienced protests against Gaddafi's regime on 15 February 2011, with a full-scale revolt beginning on [17 February](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revolution_Day).[^cite_note-81] Libya's authoritarian regime led by Muammar Gaddafi put up much more of a resistance compared to the regimes in Egypt and Tunisia. While overthrowing the regimes in Egypt and Tunisia was a relatively quick process, Gaddafi's campaign posed significant stalls on the uprising in Libya.[^cite_note-82] The first announcement of a competing political authority appeared online and declared the [Interim Transitional National Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interim_Transitional_National_Council) as an alternative government. One of Gaddafi's senior advisors responded by posting a tweet, wherein he resigned, defected, and advised Gaddafi to flee.[^cite_note-83] By 20 February, the unrest had spread to [Tripoli](31232.md). On 27 February 2011, the [National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council) was established to administer the areas of Libya under rebel control. On 10 March 2011, the [United States](3434750.md) and many other nations recognised the council headed by [Mahmoud Jibril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Jibril) as acting prime minister and as the legitimate representative of the Libyan people and withdrawing the recognition of Gaddafi's regime.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]



A protest against the anti-Gaddafi supporters in Tripoli

Pro-Gaddafi forces were able to respond militarily to rebel pushes in [Western Libya](664792.md) and launched a counterattack along the coast toward Benghazi, the *de facto* centre of the uprising.[^cite_note-86] The town of [Zawiya](3105551.md), 48 kilometres (30 mi) from Tripoli, was bombarded by [air force planes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Air_Force_\(1951%E2%80%932011\)) and army tanks and seized by [Jamahiriya troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya), "exercising a level of brutality not yet seen in the conflict."[^cite_note-87] Organizations of the United Nations, including [United Nations Secretary General](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Secretary_General) [Ban Ki-moon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Ki-moon)[^cite_note-88] and the [United Nations Human Rights Council](635790.md), condemned the crackdown as violating international law, with the latter body expelling Libya outright in an unprecedented action.[^cite_note-89][^cite_note-90] On 17 March 2011, the UN Security Council passed [Resolution 1973](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1973),[^cite_note-91] with a 10–0 vote and five abstentions including [Russia](25391.md), [China](5405.md), [India](14533.md), [Brazil](3383.md) and [Germany](11867.md). The resolution sanctioned the establishment of a [no-fly zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-fly_zone) and the use of "all means necessary" to protect civilians within Libya.[^cite_note-92] On 19 March, the first act of [NATO](21133.md) allies to secure the no-fly zone began by destroying Libyan air defences when French military jets entered Libyan airspace on a [reconnaissance](146911.md) mission heralding attacks on enemy targets.[^cite_note-libyrate1-93]

In the weeks that followed, U.S. American forces were in the forefront of NATO operations against Libya. More than 8,000 US personnel in warships and aircraft were deployed in the area. At least 3,000 targets were struck in 14,202 strike sorties, 716 of them in Tripoli and 492 in [Brega](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brega).[^cite_note-94] The U.S. air offensive included flights of [B-2 stealth bombers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_B-2_Spirit), each bomber armed with sixteen 2000-pound bombs, flying out of and returning to their base in [Missouri](19571.md).[^cite_note-95] The support provided by the NATO air forces contributed to the ultimate success of the revolution.[^cite_note-96] By 22 August 2011, [rebel fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Liberation_Army_\(Libya\)) had entered Tripoli and occupied [Green Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martyrs'_Square,_Tripoli),[^cite_note-Richburg-97] which they renamed Martyrs' Square in honour of those killed since 17 February 2011. On 20 October 2011, the last heavy fighting of the uprising came to an end in the city of [Sirte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirte). The [Battle of Sirte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Sirte_\(2011\)) was both the last decisive battle and the last one in general of the [First Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Libyan_Civil_War) where Gaddafi was captured and killed by NATO-backed forces on 20 October 2011. Sirte was the last Gaddafi loyalist stronghold and his place of birth. The defeat of [loyalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Loyalist) forces was celebrated on 23 October 2011, three days after the fall of Sirte. At least 30,000 Libyans died in the civil war.[^cite_note-98] In addition, the [National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council) estimated 50,000 wounded.[^cite_note-99]

### Interwar period and the Second Libyan Civil War (2011–2020)

Main articles: [Aftermath of the First Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_the_First_Libyan_Civil_War) and [Second Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Libyan_Civil_War)

Following the defeat of loyalist forces, Libya was torn among numerous rival, armed militias affiliated with distinct regions, cities and tribes, while the central government had been weak and unable to effectively exert its authority over the country. Competing militias pitted themselves against each other in a political struggle between [Islamist](15012.md) politicians and their opponents.[^cite_note-100] On 7 July 2012, Libyans held their first parliamentary elections since the end of the former regime. On 8 August, the [National Transitional Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Transitional_Council) officially handed power over to the wholly elected [General National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_National_Congress), which was then tasked with the formation of an interim government and the drafting of a new [Libyan Constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_interim_Constitutional_Declaration) to be approved in a general [referendum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Next_Libyan_referendum).[^cite_note-Esam-8-Aug-101] On 25 August 2012, in what Reuters reported as "the most blatant sectarian attack" since the end of the civil war, unnamed organised assailants bulldozed a [Sufi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sufism) mosque with graves in the centre of the Libyan capital [Tripoli](31232.md). It was the second such razing of a Sufi site in two days.[^cite_note-102] Numerous acts of vandalism and destruction of heritage were carried out by suspected Islamist militias, including the removal of the Nude Gazelle Statue and the destruction and desecration of [World War II](32927.md)–era British grave sites near [Benghazi](204114.md).[^cite_note-103][^cite_note-104] Many other cases of heritage vandalism were reported to be carried out by Islamist-related radical militias and mobs that either destroyed, robbed, or looted a number of historic sites.



Libya has emerged as a major transit point for [people trying to reach Europe](46415102.md).

On 11 September 2012, Islamist militants mounted an [attack on the American diplomatic compound in Benghazi](37018641.md),[^cite_note-105] killing the U.S. ambassador to Libya, [J. Christopher Stevens](36993318.md), and three others. The incident generated outrage in the [United States](3434750.md) and Libya.[^cite_note-106] On 7 October 2012, Libya's Prime Minister-elect [Mustafa A.G. Abushagur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_A._G._Abushagur) was ousted after failing a second time to win parliamentary approval for a new cabinet.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108][^cite_note-109] On 14 October 2012, the General National Congress elected former GNC member and human rights lawyer [Ali Zeidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Zeidan) as prime minister-designate.[^cite_note-110] Zeidan was sworn in after his cabinet was approved by the GNC.[^cite_note-111][^cite_note-112] On 11 March 2014, after having been ousted by the GNC for his inability to halt a rogue oil shipment,[^cite_note-nyt-113] Prime Minister Zeidan stepped down, and was replaced by Prime Minister [Abdullah al-Thani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdullah_al-Thani).[^cite_note-114]

The [Second Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Libyan_Civil_War) began in May 2014 following fighting between rival parliaments with tribal militias and [jihadist](16203.md) groups soon taking advantage of the power vacuum. Most notably, radical Islamist fighters seized [Derna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derna,_Libya) in 2014 and [Sirte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirte) in 2015 in the name of the [Islamic State](9087364.md). In February 2015, neighbouring [Egypt](8087628.md) [launched airstrikes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2015_Egyptian_airstrikes_in_Libya) against IS in support of the Tobruk government.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116][^cite_note-117] In June 2014, [elections were held](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_Libyan_parliamentary_election) to the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Libya\)), a new legislative body intended to take over from the [General National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_National_Congress). The elections were marred by violence and low turnout, with voting stations closed in some areas.[^cite_note-Jawad-118] Secularists and liberals did well in the elections, to the consternation of Islamist lawmakers in the GNC, who reconvened and declared a continuing mandate for the GNC, refusing to recognise the new House of Representatives.[^cite_note-119] Armed supporters of the General National Congress occupied Tripoli, forcing the newly elected parliament to flee to [Tobruk](405871.md).[^cite_note-WPost_Aug._24-120][^cite_note-guardian-20140909-16]



[Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md), the head of the [Libyan National Army](35457665.md), one of the main factions in the [2014 civil war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_civil_war_\(2014%E2%80%932020\))

In January 2015, meetings were held with the aim to find a peaceful agreement between the rival parties in Libya. The so-called Geneva-Ghadames talks were supposed to bring the GNC and the Tobruk government together at one table to find a solution of the internal conflict. However, the GNC actually never participated, a sign that internal division not only affected the "Tobruk Camp", but also the "Tripoli Camp". Meanwhile, terrorism within Libya steadily increased, also affecting neighbouring countries. The [terrorist attack against the Bardo Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bardo_National_Museum_attack) in [Tunisia](30188.md) on 18 March 2015 was reportedly carried out by two Libyan-trained militants.[^cite_note-121] During 2015 an extended series of diplomatic meetings and peace negotiations were supported by the United Nations, as conducted by the Special Representative of the Secretary-General (SRSG), Spanish diplomat [Bernardino León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardino_Le%C3%B3n).[^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123] UN support for the SRSG-led process of dialogue carried on in addition to the usual work of the United Nations Support Mission in Libya (UNSMIL).[^cite_note-124] In July 2015 SRSG Leon reported to the UN Security Council on the progress of the negotiations, which at that point had just achieved a political agreement on 11 July setting out "a comprehensive framework... includ\[ing] guiding principles... institutions and decision-making mechanisms to guide the transition until the adoption of a permanent constitution." Talks, negotiations and dialogue continued on during mid-2015 at various international locations, culminating at Skhirat in Morocco in early September.[^cite_note-125][^cite_note-126]

Also in 2015, as part of the ongoing support from the international community, the UN Human Rights Council requested a report about the Libyan situation[^cite_note-127][^cite_note-128] and the High Commissioner for Human Rights, [Zeid Ra'ad Al Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeid_bin_Ra'ad), established an investigative body (OIOL) to report on human rights and rebuilding the Libyan justice system.[^cite_note-129] Chaos-ridden Libya emerged as a major transit point for [people trying to reach Europe](46415102.md). Between 2013 and 2018, nearly 700,000 migrants reached Italy by boat, many of them from Libya.[^cite_note-130][^cite_note-131] In May 2018 Libya's rival leaders agreed to hold parliamentary and presidential elections following a meeting in [Paris](22989.md).[^cite_note-132] In April 2019, [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md) launched [Operation Flood of Dignity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Libya_campaign), in an offensive by the [Libyan National Army](35457665.md) aimed to seize Western territories from the [Government of National Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Accord) (GNA).[^cite_note-133] In June 2019, forces allied to Libya's UN-recognised Government of National Accord successfully captured [Gharyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharyan), a strategic town where military commander Khalifa Haftar and his fighters were based. According to a spokesman for GNA forces, Mustafa al-Mejii, dozens of LNA fighters under Haftar were killed, while at least 18 were taken prisoner.[^cite_note-134]

In March 2020, Prime Minister of the GNA [Fayez Al-Sarraj](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fayez_al-Sarraj) commenced Operation Peace Storm. The government initiated the bid in response to the state of assaults carried by [Field Marshal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Field_Marshal) [Haftar](31227894.md)'s [LNA](35457665.md). "We are a legitimate, civilian government that respects its obligations to the international community, but is committed primarily to its people and has an obligation to protect its citizens," Sarraj said in line with his decision.[^cite_note-135] On 28 August 2020, the *[BBC](19344654.md) Africa Eye* and *BBC Arabic Documentaries* revealed that a drone operated by the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) (UAE) killed 26 young cadets at a military academy in Tripoli, on 4 January. Most of the cadets were teenagers and none of them were armed. The Chinese-made drone [Wing Loong II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAIG_Wing_Loong_II) fired Blue Arrow 7 missile, which was operated from UAE-run Al-Khadim Libyan air base. In February, these drones stationed in Libya were moved to an air base near [Siwa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siwa_Oasis) in the western Egyptian desert.[^cite_note-136] *[The Guardian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Guardian)* probed and discovered the blatant violation of [UN](31769.md) arms embargo by the UAE and [Turkey](11125639.md) on 7 October 2020. As per the reporting, both the nations sent large-scale military cargo planes to Libya in support of their respective parties.[^cite_note-137] On 23 October 2020, [a permanent ceasefire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_peace_process) was signed to end the war.[^cite_note-138]

In December 2021, the [country's first presidential election](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022_Libyan_presidential_election) was scheduled, but was delayed to June 2022[^cite_note-139] and later postponed further.

[Fathi Bashagha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fathi_Bashagha) was appointed prime minister by the parliament in February 2022 to lead a transitional administration, but standing prime minister [Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_Dbeibeh) refused to hand over power as of April 2022. In protest against the Dbeibah government, tribal leaders from the desert town of [Ubari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ubari) shut down the [El Sharara oil field](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Sharara_oil_field), Libya's largest oil field, on 18 April 2022. The shut down threatened to cause oil shortages domestically in Libya, and preclude the state-run National Oil Corp. from exploiting the increase in oil prices resulting from the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md).[^cite_note-140] On 2 July, protesters stormed the parliament building in Tobruk and lit fires.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142]

On 10 September 2023, catastrophic floods due to [dam failures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derna_dam_collapses) generated by [Storm Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Daniel) devastated the port city of [Derna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derna,_Libya), killing more than 5,900 and possibly as many as 24,000.[^cite_note-ALMONITOR-143] The floods were the worst natural disaster in Libya's modern history.[^cite_note-October_3_update-144]

In November 2024, the [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\)) announced it would instate a morality police to crack-down on "weird haircuts", enforce "modest" clothing, and require male guardians for women.[^cite_note-145]

## Geography

Main articles: [Geography of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geography_of_Libya), [Wildlife of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wildlife_of_Libya), and [Climate change in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Libya)



Libya map of Köppen climate classification

Libya extends over 1,759,540 square kilometres (679,362 sq mi), making it the [16th-largest nation in the world by size](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_countries_and_dependencies_by_area). Libya is bounded to the north by the [Mediterranean Sea](19006.md), [the northwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya%E2%80%93Tunisia_border) by [Tunisia](30188.md), [the west](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria%E2%80%93Libya_border) by [Algeria](358.md), [the southwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya%E2%80%93Niger_border) by [Niger](21373.md), [the south](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad%E2%80%93Libya_border) by [Chad](5488.md), [the southeast](62950857.md) by [Sudan](27421.md), and [the east](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Egypt%E2%80%93Libya_border) by [Egypt](8087628.md), as well as [maritime borders](29613061.md) with [Greece](12108.md), [Italy](14532.md) and [Malta](19137.md) to the north. Libya lies between latitudes [19°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_parallel_north) and [34°N](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/34th_parallel_north), and longitudes [9°](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_meridian_east) and [26°E](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/26th_meridian_east). Libya comprises three historical regions: [Tripolitania](664792.md), [Fezzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fezzan), and [Cyrenaica](75451.md).&#x20;

At 1,770 kilometres (1,100 mi), Libya's coastline is the longest of any [African country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_sovereign_states_and_dependent_territories_in_Africa) bordering the Mediterranean.[^cite_note-146][^cite_note-147] The portion of the Mediterranean Sea north of Libya is often called the [Libyan Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Sea). The climate is mostly extremely dry and desertlike in nature. However, the northern regions enjoy a milder [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate).[^cite_note-148]

Six ecoregions lie within Libya's borders: [Saharan halophytics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saharan_halophytics), [Mediterranean dry woodlands and steppe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_dry_woodlands_and_steppe), [Mediterranean woodlands and forests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_woodlands_and_forests), [North Saharan steppe and woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Saharan_steppe_and_woodlands), [Tibesti-Jebel Uweinat montane xeric woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibesti-Jebel_Uweinat_montane_xeric_woodlands), and [West Saharan montane xeric woodlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Saharan_montane_xeric_woodlands).[^cite_note-DinersteinOlson2017-149]

Natural hazards come in the form of hot, dry, dust-laden [sirocco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirocco) (known in Libya as the *gibli*). This is a southern wind blowing from one to four days in [spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spring_\(season\)) and [autumn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autumn). There are also [dust storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_storm) and sandstorms. [Oases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oasis) can also be found scattered throughout Libya, the most important of which are [Ghadames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadames) and [Kufra](3337477.md).[^cite_note-WorldCulturalHeritage-150]

Libya is highly vulnerable to the effects of [climate change](5042951.md) and underprepared to deal with them.[^cite_note-151][^cite_note-COP_27-2022-152] The effects of [climate change in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_in_Libya), such as [desertification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desertification), sea level rise, flooding, and irregular weather patterns are already noticeable and are expected to increase.[^cite_note-COP_27-2022-152] These pose significant threats to Libya's agriculture, food and water security and economic development and sustainability.[^cite_note-UNDPClimate-153][^cite_note-154] For example [Storm Daniel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storm_Daniel) in 2023 caused catastrophic flooding that not only resulted in thousands of deaths in Libya but also destroyed agricultural infrastructure and contaminated productive lands with debris and pollutants that will affect farming for years.[^cite_note-155]

Libya was a pioneer state in North Africa in species protection, with the creation in 1975 of the El Kouf protected area. The fall of Muammar Gaddafi's regime favoured intense [poaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poaching): "Before the fall of Gaddafi even hunting rifles were forbidden. But since 2011, poaching has been carried out with weapons of war and sophisticated vehicles in which one can find up to 200 gazelle heads killed by militiamen who hunt to pass the time. We are also witnessing the emergence of hunters with no connection to the tribes that traditionally practice hunting. They shoot everything they find, even during the breeding season. More than 500,000 birds are killed in this way each year, when protected areas have been seized by tribal chiefs who have appropriated them. The animals that used to live there have all disappeared, hunted when they are edible or released when they are not," explains zoologist Khaled Ettaieb.[^cite_note-156]

### Libyan Desert

Main article: [Libyan Desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Desert)



Libya is a predominantly [desert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert) country. Over 95% of the land area is covered in desert.

[^cite_note-157]

The Libyan Desert, which covers most of Libya, is one of the most arid and sun-baked places on Earth.[^cite_note-SalakLibya-57] In places, decades may pass without seeing any rainfall at all, and even in the [highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Highland_\(geography\)) rainfall seldom happens, once every 5–10 years. At [Uweinat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gabal_El_Uweinat), as of 2006 the last recorded rainfall was in September 1998.[^cite_note-Libdesert-158]

Likewise, the temperature in the Libyan Desert can be extreme; on 13 September 1922, the town of ['Aziziya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/'Aziziya), which is located southwest of [Tripoli](31232.md), recorded an air temperature of 58 °C (136.4 °F), considered to be a world record.[^cite_note-159][^cite_note-Death_Valley-160][^cite_note-El_Azizia-161] In September 2012, however, the world record figure of 58 °C was determined to be invalid by the [World Meteorological Organization](33584.md).[^cite_note-Death_Valley-160][^cite_note-El_Azizia-161][^cite_note-162]

There are a few scattered uninhabited small oases, usually linked to the major depressions, where water can be found by digging to a few feet in depth. In the west there is a widely dispersed group of oases in unconnected shallow depressions, the Kufra group, consisting of Tazerbo, Rebianae and [Kufra](3337477.md).[^cite_note-Libdesert-158] Aside from the scarps, the general flatness is only interrupted by a series of [plateaus](538005.md) and massifs near the centre of the Libyan Desert, around the convergence of the Egyptian-Sudanese-Libyan borders.

Slightly further to the south are the [massifs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Massif) of Arkenu, Uweinat, and Kissu. These [granite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granite) mountains are ancient, having formed long before the sandstones surrounding them. Arkenu and Western Uweinat are ring complexes very similar to those in the [Aïr Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%AFr_Mountains). Eastern Uweinat (the highest point in the Libyan Desert) is a raised sandstone plateau adjacent to the granite part further west.[^cite_note-Libdesert-158]

The plain to the north of Uweinat is dotted with eroded volcanic features. With the discovery of oil in the 1950s also came the discovery of a massive [aquifer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aquifer) underneath much of Libya. The water in the [Nubian Sandstone Aquifer System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_Sandstone_Aquifer_System) pre-dates the last [ice ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ice_age) and the [Sahara Desert](325363.md) itself.[^cite_note-163] This area also contains the [Arkenu structures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arkenu_structures), which were once thought to be two impact craters.[^cite_note-CigoliniOthers2012a-164]

The Libyan Desert, despite its extreme conditions, supports a variety of adapted wildlife, mostly found near oases, rocky areas, and occasional rain zones. Animals survive through traits like being nocturnal, burrowing, conserving water, and tolerating heat. Mammals include species such as the [fennec fox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fennec_fox), [sand cat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sand_cat), and [dorcas gazelle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dorcas_gazelle), while larger animals like the addax and [scimitar-horned oryx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scimitar_oryx) have declined due to hunting. Reptiles are especially common, including vipers, cobras, monitor lizards, and geckos, all adapted to intense heat and sheltering during the day. Birds are less frequent but appear near water sources, and invertebrates like scorpions, beetles, and ants form a major part of the ecosystem, often being active at night.[^cite_note-165][^cite_note-166]

[Gaddafi](53029.md) was the leader of Libya until [2011 Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_civil_war_\(2011\)).

The politics of Libya has been in a tumultuous state since the start of the [Arab Spring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Spring) and the [NATO](21133.md) intervention related [Libyan crisis](44791215.md) in 2011. The crisis resulted in the collapse of the [Libyan Arab Jamahiriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya) and the [killing of Muammar Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Muammar_Gaddafi), amidst the [First Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Libyan_Civil_War) and 2011 [foreign military intervention](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011_military_intervention_in_Libya).[^cite_note-167][^cite_note-168][^cite_note-169] The crisis was deepened by [factional violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Factional_violence_in_Libya_\(2011%E2%80%932014\)) in the [aftermath of the First Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aftermath_of_the_First_Libyan_Civil_War), resulting in the outbreak of the [Second Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Libyan_Civil_War) in 2014.[^cite_note-170] The control over the country is currently split between the [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Libya\)) (HoR) in [Tobruk](405871.md) and the [Government of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\)) (GNU) in [Tripoli](31232.md) and their respective supporters, as well as various [jihadist groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category\:Jihadist_groups_in_Libya) and [tribal elements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_groups_in_Libya) controlling different parts of the country.[^cite_note-171][^cite_note-172]

The former legislature was the [General National Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_National_Congress), which had 200 seats.[^cite_note-cialegbr-173] The [General National Congress (2014)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_National_Congress_\(2014\)), a largely unrecognised rival parliament based in the *de jure* capital of Tripoli, claims to be a legal continuation of the GNC.[^cite_note-174][^cite_note-175] On 7 July 2012, Libyans voted in [parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_General_National_Congress_election,_2012), the first free elections in almost 40 years.[^cite_note-rfi-elections-176] Around thirty women were elected to become members of parliament.[^cite_note-rfi-elections-176] Early results of the vote showed the [National Forces Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Forces_Alliance), led by former interim prime minister [Mahmoud Jibril](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmoud_Jibril), as front runner.[^cite_note-177] The [Justice and Construction Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justice_and_Construction_Party), affiliated with the [Muslim Brotherhood](20742.md), has done less well in Libya than similar parties in [Egypt](8087628.md) and [Tunisia](30188.md).[^cite_note-econ-knack-178] It won 17 out of 80 seats that were contested by parties; about 60 independents had joined its caucus by 2013.[^cite_note-econ-knack-178] As of January 2013, there was mounting public pressure on the National Congress to set up a constitution-drafting body. Congress had not yet decided whether the members of the body would be elected or appointed.[^cite_note-179] On 30 March 2014, the General National Congress voted to replace itself with a new [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Libya\)). The new legislature allocated 30 seats for women, would have 200 seats overall (with individuals able to run as members of political parties), and allowed Libyans of other nationalities to run for office.[^cite_note-lh30march-180]

Following the 2012 elections, [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) improved Libya's rating from Not Free to Partly Free and considered the country an [electoral democracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_democracy).[^cite_note-181] Gaddafi merged civil and [sharia courts](28840.md#Court_procedures) in 1973. As of 2013, civil courts employed sharia judges in regular [courts of appeal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Court_of_appeal) as specialists in sharia appellate cases.[^cite_note-182] Laws regarding [personal status](28840.md#Personal_status_and_child_marriage) are derived from Islamic law as of 2011.[^cite_note-183] At a meeting of the [European Parliament Committee on Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Parliament_Committee_on_Foreign_Affairs) on 2 December 2014, UN Special Representative [Bernardino León](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernardino_Le%C3%B3n) described Libya as a non-state.[^cite_note-184] An agreement to form a [national unity government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Unity_\(Libya\)) was signed on 17 December 2015.[^cite_note-theguardian.com-185] Under the terms of the agreement, a nine-member [Presidency Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_Council_\(Libya\)) and a seventeen-member interim [Government of National Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Accord) would be formed, with a view to holding new elections within two years.[^cite_note-theguardian.com-185] The [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Libya\)) would continue to exist as a legislature, and an advisory body called the [State Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_Council_\(Libya\)) would have members nominated by the [General National Congress (2014)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_National_Congress_\(2014\)).[^cite_note-186] The formation of an interim unity government was announced on 5 February 2021, after its members were elected by the [Libyan Political Dialogue Forum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Political_Dialogue_Forum) (LPDF).\[^cite_note-[www.aljazeera.com-187](http://www.aljazeera.com-187/)] 74 members of the LPDF cast ballots for four-member slates, which would fill positions including prime minister and the head of the Presidential Council.\[^cite_note-[www.aljazeera.com-187](http://www.aljazeera.com-187/)] After no slates reached a 60% vote threshold, the two leading groups competed in a run-off election.\[^cite_note-[www.aljazeera.com-187](http://www.aljazeera.com-187/)] [Mohamed al-Menfi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohamed_al-Menfi), a former ambassador to [Greece](12108.md), became head of the Presidential Council.[^cite_note-BBC_News-2021a-188]

Meanwhile, the LPDF confirmed that [Abdul Hamid Dbeibeh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Hamid_Dbeibeh), a businessman, would be the transitional prime minister.[^cite_note-BBC_News-2021a-188] All of the candidates who ran in the election, including the members of the winning slate, promised to appoint women to 30% of all senior government positions.[^cite_note-BBC_News-2021a-188] The politicians elected to lead the interim government initially agreed not to stand in the national elections scheduled for 24 December 2021.[^cite_note-BBC_News-2021a-188] However, Dbeibeh announced his candidacy for president despite the ban in November 2021.[^cite_note-189] The appeals court in Tripoli rejected appeals for his disqualification and allowed Dbeibeh back on the candidates' list, along ### Foreign relations

Main article: [Foreign relations of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Libya)

Libya's foreign policies have fluctuated since 1951. As a kingdom, Libya maintained a definitively pro-Western stance and was recognised as belonging to the conservative traditionalist bloc in the League of Arab States (the present-day [Arab League](52625.md)), of which it became a member in 1953.[^cite_note-LoC1987-27] The government was also friendly towards Western countries such as the United Kingdom, United States, [France](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Fifth_Republic), [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Italian_Republic), and [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Greece_\(Gl%C3%BCcksburg\)), and established full diplomatic relations with the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) in 1955.[^cite_note-Zoubir2009-60] Although the government supported Arab causes, including the Moroccan and Algerian independence movements, it took little active part in the [Arab–Israeli conflict](7960202.md) or the tumultuous inter-Arab politics of the 1950s and early 1960s. The kingdom was noted for its close association with the West, while it steered a conservative course at home.[^cite_note-197]

After the 1969 [coup d'état](25914154.md), [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) closed American and British bases and partly [nationalised](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationalization) foreign oil and commercial interests in Libya. Gaddafi was known for backing a number of leaders viewed as anathema to [Westernisation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westernization) and [political liberalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_liberalism), including [Ugandan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Uganda_\(1971%E2%80%9379\)) president [Idi Amin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Idi_Amin),[^cite_note-198] [Central African](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_African_Empire) emperor [Jean-Bédel Bokassa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-B%C3%A9del_Bokassa),[^cite_note-stanik23-199][^cite_note-Davis1990-200]<sup>: 16 </sup> [Ethiopian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derg) strongman [Haile Mariam Mengistu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haile_Mariam_Mengistu),[^cite_note-Davis1990-200]<sup>: 16 </sup> Liberian president [Charles Taylor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Taylor_\(Liberian_politician\)),[^cite_note-economistfall-201] and [Yugoslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro#History) president [Slobodan Milošević](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobodan_Milo%C5%A1evi%C4%87).[^cite_note-202]



Libyan National Security Advisor [Mutassim Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutassim_Gaddafi) and US Secretary of State [Hillary Clinton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillary_Clinton), April 2009



UK Foreign Secretary [William Hague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Hague) with Libyan Prime Minister [Ali Zeidan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Zeidan) and U.S. Secretary of State [John Kerry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Kerry), November 2013

Relations with the West were strained for most of Gaddafi's rule,[^cite_note-203][^cite_note-Davis1990-200]<sup>: 183 </sup>[^cite_note-204] with incidents including the killing of [London](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_London_Police) policewoman [Yvonne Fletcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yvonne_Fletcher), the [bombing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Berlin_discotheque_bombing) of a [West Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Berlin) nightclub frequented by U.S. servicemen, and the bombing of [Pan Am Flight 103](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan_Am_Flight_103) leading to UN sanctions in the 1990s; by the late 2000s, the United States and other Western powers had normalised relations with Libya.[^cite_note-SalakLibya-57] Gaddafi's decision to abandon the pursuit of [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_of_mass_destruction) after the [Iraq War](5043324.md) saw [Iraqi dictator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_Iraq_under_Ba'athist_rule) [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) overthrown and put on trial led to Libya being hailed as a success for Western [soft power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soft_power) initiatives in the [war on terror](13425800.md).[^cite_note-205][^cite_note-206][^cite_note-207] In October 2010, Gaddafi apologised to African leaders on behalf of Arab nations for their involvement in the [trans-Saharan slave trade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-Saharan_slave_trade).[^cite_note-France_24_2010-208]

Libya is included in the [European Union](9317.md)'s [European Neighbourhood Policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Neighbourhood_Policy) (ENP), which aims at bringing the EU and its neighbours closer. Libyan authorities rejected European Union's plans aimed at stopping [migration from Libya](15497819.md).[^cite_note-209][^cite_note-210] In 2017, Libya signed the UN [treaty on the Prohibition of Nuclear Weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_the_Prohibition_of_Nuclear_Weapons).[^cite_note-211]

### Military

Libya's [previous national army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya) was defeated in the [First Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Libyan_Civil_War) and disbanded. The [Tobruk](405871.md)-based [House of Representatives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Representatives_\(Libya\)), which claims to be the legitimate government of Libya, has attempted to reestablish a military known as the [Libyan National Army](35457665.md). Led by [Khalifa Haftar](31227894.md), it controls much of eastern Libya.[^cite_note-212] In May 2012, an estimated 35,000 personnel had joined its ranks.[^cite_note-nytimes-213] The internationally recognised [Government of National Accord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_National_Accord) established in 2015 had [its own army](35021571.md) that replaced the LNA, consisting largely of undisciplined and disorganised militia groups.

As of November 2012, it was deemed to be still in the embryonic stage of development.[^cite_note-214] President [Mohammed Magariaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mohammed_Magariaf) promised that empowering the army and police force was the government's biggest priority in 2012.[^cite_note-215] President Megarif also ordered that all of the country's militias to come under government authority or disband.[^cite_note-216]

Militias had refused to be integrated into a central security force by February 2013.[^cite_note-hangover-217] Many of the militias were disciplined, but the most powerful of them answered only to the executive councils of various Libyan cities.[^cite_note-hangover-217] The militias made up the so-called [Libya Shield Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_Shield_Force), a parallel national force, which operated at the request, rather than at the order, of the defence ministry.[^cite_note-hangover-217]

### Human rights

According to [Human Rights Watch](46530.md) annual report 2016, journalists are still being targeted by the armed groups in Libya. ## Economy

Main article: [Economy of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_Libya)



Change in per capita GDP of Libya, 1950–2018. Figures are inflation-adjusted to 2011 International dollars.

The Libyan economy depends primarily upon revenues from the [oil sector](994887.md), which account for over half of GDP and 97% of exports.[^cite_note-star-oil-223] Libya holds the largest proven oil reserves in Africa and is an important contributor to the global supply of light, [sweet crude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_crude).[^cite_note-eia-libya-224] During 2010, when oil averaged at \$80 a barrel, oil production accounted for 54% of GDP.[^cite_note-225] Apart from petroleum, the other natural resources are natural gas and [gypsum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gypsum).[^cite_note-opec-libya-226] The [International Monetary Fund](15251.md) estimated Libya's real GDP growth at 122% in 2012 and 16.7% in 2013, after a 60% plunge in 2011.[^cite_note-star-oil-223]

In 2023 Libya emitted around 23.77 million tonnes of greenhouse gases, about 0.17% of the world's total emissions. However, the nation ranked as the 21st highest emitter per capita globally and the highest in Africa, at just over 13 tonnes per person.[^cite_note-227] Libya was slow to join climate efforts, signing the [United Nations Framework Convention on Climate Change](31898.md) (UNFCCC) in 2016 and the [Paris Climate Agreement](30242372.md) in 2021.[^cite_note-228] However, as of 2024, Libya has not ratified the Paris Agreement and submitted its [Nationally Determined Contributions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nationally_determined_contribution) (NDCs), and has made little progress toward the development of [climate change adaptation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Climate_change_adaptation) plans.[^cite_note-229][^cite_note-UNDPClimate-153]

The [World Bank](36752.md) defines Libya as an 'Upper Middle Income Economy', along with only seven other African countries.[^cite_note-230] Substantial revenues from the energy sector, coupled with a small population, give Libya one of the highest per capita GDPs in Africa. Although the government supported Arab causes, including the Moroccan and Algerian independence movements, it took little active part in the [Arab–Israeli conflict](7960202.md) or the tumultuous inter-Arab politics of the 1950s and early 1960s. The kingdom was noted for its close association with the West, while it steered a conservative course at home.[^cite_note-opec-libya-226] This allowed the [Libyan Arab Jamahiriya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Arab_Jamahiriya) state to provide an extensive level of [social security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_security), particularly in the fields of housing and education.[^cite_note-231]



An [oil platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_platform) off the Libyan coast

Libya faces many structural problems including a lack of institutions, weak governance, and chronic [structural unemployment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Structural_unemployment).[^cite_note-imf-survey-232] The economy displays a lack of economic diversification and significant reliance on immigrant labour.[^cite_note-ilo-libya-233] Libya has traditionally relied on unsustainably high levels of public sector hiring to create employment.[^cite_note-african-outlook-234] In the mid-2000s, the government employed about 70% of all national employees.[^cite_note-ilo-libya-233]

Unemployment rose from 8% in 2008 to 21% in 2009, according to the census figures.[^cite_note-235] According to an [Arab League](52625.md) report, based on data from 2010, unemployment for women stands at 18% while for the figure for men is 21%, making Libya the only Arab country where there are more unemployed men than women.[^cite_note-236] Libya has high levels of social inequality, high rates of youth unemployment and regional economic disparities.[^cite_note-african-outlook-234] Water supply is also a problem, with some 28% of the population not having access to safe drinking water in 2000.[^cite_note-237]



[Pivot irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pivot_irrigation) in [Kufra](3337477.md), southeast Libya, 2008

Two trans-African automobile routes pass through Libya, the [Cairo–Dakar Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cairo%E2%80%93Dakar_Highway) and [Tripoli–Cape Town Highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tripoli%E2%80%93Cape_Town_Highway). These routes have further contributed in the economic development of Libya.

Libya imports up to 90% of its cereal consumption requirements, and imports of wheat in 2012/13 was estimated at 1 million tonnes.[^cite_note-fao_giews-238] The 2012 wheat production was estimated at 200,000 tonnes.[^cite_note-fao_giews-238] The government hopes to increase food production to 800,000 tonnes of cereals by 2020.[^cite_note-fao_giews-238] However, natural and environmental conditions limit Libya's agricultural production potential.[^cite_note-fao_giews-238] Before 1958, agriculture was the country's main source of revenue, making up about 30% of GDP. With the discovery of oil in 1958, the size of the agriculture sector declined rapidly, accounting for less than 5% GDP by 2005.[^cite_note-voa-olive-239]

The country joined the [OPEC](166346.md) cartel in 1962.[^cite_note-opec-libya-226][^cite_note-g417-240] Libya is not a [WTO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTO) member, but negotiations for its accession started in 2004.[^cite_note-241] In the early 1980s, Libya was one of the wealthiest countries in the world; its [GDP per capita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GDP_per_capita) was higher than some developed countries.[^cite_note-242]



Oil is the major natural resource of Libya, with [estimated reserves of 43.6 billion barrels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_reserves_in_Libya).

[^cite_note-243]

[UN sanctions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_sanctions) were lifted in September 2003,[^cite_note-244] and Libya announced in December 2003 that it would abandon programs to build weapons of mass destruction.[^cite_note-245] Other steps have included applying for membership of the [World Trade Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Trade_Organization), reducing [subsidies](146719.md), and announcing plans for [privatisation](24661.md).[^cite_note-246]

Authorities privatised more than 100 government-owned companies after 2003 in industries including oil refining, tourism and real estate, of which 29 were 100% foreign owned.[^cite_note-247] Many international oil companies returned to the country, including oil giants [Shell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Dutch_Shell) and [ExxonMobil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ExxonMobil).[^cite_note-248] After sanctions were lifted there was a gradual increase of air traffic, and by 2005 there were 1.5 million yearly air travellers.[^cite_note-249] Libya had long been a notoriously difficult country for Western tourists to visit due to stringent visa requirements.[^cite_note-250]

In 2007, [Saif al-Islam Gaddafi](1196902.md), the second-eldest son of Muammar Gaddafi, was involved in a [green development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Green_development) project called the Green Mountain Sustainable Development Area, which sought to bring tourism to [Cyrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrene,_Libya) and to preserve [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Classical_Greece) ruins in the area.[^cite_note-251] In August 2011, it was estimated that it would take at least 10 years to rebuild Libya's infrastructure. Even before the 2011 war, Libya's infrastructure was in a poor state due to "utter neglect" by Gaddafi's administration, according to the NTC.[^cite_note-252] By October 2012, the economy had recovered from the 2011 conflict, with oil production returning to near normal levels.[^cite_note-star-oil-223] Oil production was more than 1.6 million barrels per day before the war. By October 2012, the average oil production has surpassed 1.4 million bpd.[^cite_note-star-oil-223] The resumption of production was made possible due to the quick return of major Western companies, like [TotalEnergies](804161.md), [Eni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eni), [Repsol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Repsol), [Wintershall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wintershall) and [Occidental](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Occidental_Petroleum).[^cite_note-star-oil-223] In 2016, an announcement from the company said the company aims 900,000 barrel per day in the next year. Oil production has fallen from 1.6 million barrel per day to 900,000 in four years of war.[^cite_note-253]

The [Great Man-Made River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_Man-Made_River) is the world's largest [irrigation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irrigation) project.[^cite_note-254] The project utilises a pipeline system that pumps [fossil water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fossil_water) from the [Nubian Sandstone Aquifer System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nubian_Sandstone_Aquifer_System) from down south in Libya to cities in the populous Libyan northern Mediterranean coast including Tripoli and Benghazi. The water provides 70% of all freshwater used in Libya.[^cite_note-255] During the second Libyan civil war, lasting from 2014 to 2020, the water infrastructure suffered neglect and occasional breakdowns.[^cite_note-256] By 2017, 60% of the Libyan population were [malnourished](258979.md). Since then, 1.3 million people are waiting for emergency humanitarian aid, out of a total population of 7.1 million.[^cite_note-257]

As of March 2024, Libya was actively promoting business development and encouraging both domestic and foreign investment. This strategic initiative is aimed at securing long-term economic stability and prosperity for Libya by diversifying its economic foundation. Embracing green industries like renewable energy, energy efficiency, sustainable agriculture, and eco-tourism holds the potential to generate fresh employment prospects across a spectrum of sectors, thereby addressing unemployment challenges, particularly among the youth demographic.[^cite_note-258]

## Demographics

Main article: [Demographics of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Libya)



Ethnic composition of the Libyan population in 1974 (CIA map):&#x20;

&#x20; [Arab](2185.md) and [Arab-Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab-Berber)

&#x20; [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_people)

&#x20; [Tuareg](103054.md)

&#x20; [Toubou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toubou_people)

&#x20; Uninhabited

Libya is a large country with a relatively small population concentrated very narrowly along the coast.[^cite_note-259] Its population density is about 50 inhabitants per square kilometre (130/sq mi) in the two northern regions of [Tripolitania](664792.md) and [Cyrenaica](75451.md), but falls to less than 1 inhabitant per square kilometre (2.6/sq mi) elsewhere. 90% of the people live along the coast in less than 10% of the area.

About 88% of the population is urban, mostly concentrated in the three largest cities, [Tripoli](31232.md), [Benghazi](204114.md) and [Misrata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misrata). Libya has a population of about 6.7 million,[^cite_note-UN_WPP-260][^cite_note-UN_WPP_2022-261] 27.7% of whom are under the age of 15.[^cite_note-CIA2021-262] In 1984 the population was 3.6 million, an increase from the 1.54 million reported in 1964.[^cite_note-263]

The population of Libya is primarily of [Arab](2185.md) ancestry.[^cite_note-264] Arabs account for 92% of the population, while [Berbers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) account for 5%, though other estimates put this percentage at 10%, representing approximately 600,000 people.[^cite_note-Yakan2017-2][^cite_note-265] Among the Berber groups are the minority Berber populations of [Zuwarah](2010687.md) and the [Nafusa Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nafusa_Mountains). Southern Libya, primarily [Sebha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebha), [Kufra](3337477.md), [Ghat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghat,_Libya), [Ghadamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadamis) and [Murzuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Murzuk), is also inhabited by two other ethnic groups; the [Tuareg](103054.md) and [Toubou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toubou_people). Libya is one of the world's most [tribal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribalism) countries. There are about 140 [tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tribe) and [clans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clan) in Libya.[^cite_note-266] Also living in Libya are an estimated 750,000 Egyptian workers, down from more than 2 million prior to the overthrow of [Muammar Gaddafi](53029.md) in 2011.[^cite_note-267]

Family life is important for Libyan families, the majority of whom live in [apartment blocks](118767.md) and other independent housing units, with modes of housing depending on their income and wealth. Although the Arab Libyans traditionally lived nomadic [Bedouin](61770.md) lifestyles in tents, they have predominately settled in towns and cities.[^cite_note-268] Old ways of life are gradually fading out. A small number of Libyans still live in the desert as their families have done for centuries. Most of the population has occupations in industry and [services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_sector_of_the_economy), and a small percentage is in agriculture.

According to the UNHCR, there were around 8,000 registered refugees, 5,500 unregistered refugees, and 7,000 asylum seekers of various origins in Libya in January 2013. Additionally, 47,000 Libyan nationals were internally displaced and 46,570 were internally displaced returnees.[^cite_note-269]

### Health

Main article: [Health in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_in_Libya)

In 2010, spending on healthcare accounted for 3.88% of the country's GDP. In 2009, there were 18.71 physicians and 66.95 nurses per 10,000 inhabitants.[^cite_note-270] The life expectancy at birth was 74.95 years in 2011, or 72.44 years for males and 77.59 years for females.[^cite_note-271]

In 2023, the Libyan health ministry announced the launch of the National Strategy for Primary Healthcare 2023–2028 to improve services provided by group clinics and health centres. A unique health number allotted to each citizen will facilitate access to medical records. Before Storm Daniel, the UN Office for the Coordination of Humanitarian Affairs estimated that around 60,000 people were in need of humanitarian aid in Derna and environs. Since the storm many of the hospitals and primary health facilities in Derna and eastern Libya have been rendered partially or completely nonfunctional.[^cite_note-272]

Libya's 2024 Global Hunger Index (GHI) score is 19.2, which indicates a moderate level of hunger. Libya ranks 83rd out of 127 countries.[^cite_note-273]

### Education

Main article: [Education in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Education_in_Libya)



Al Manar Royal Palace in central [Benghazi](204114.md) – the location of the [University of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Libya)'s first campus, founded by royal decree in 1955

Libya's population includes 1.7 million students, over 270,000 of whom study at the [tertiary level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tertiary_education).[^cite_note-Libedu-274] Basic education in Libya is free for all citizens,[^cite_note-275] and is compulsory up to the [secondary level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education). The adult literacy rate in 2010 was 89.2%.[^cite_note-unescolit-276]

After Libya's independence in 1951, its first university, the [University of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Libya), was established in [Benghazi](204114.md) by royal decree.[^cite_note-Libedu2-277] In the 1975–76 academic year the number of university students was estimated to be 13,418. As of 2004, this number has increased to more than 200,000, with another 70,000 enrolled in the higher technical and vocational sector.[^cite_note-Libedu-274] The rapid increase in the number of students in the higher education sector has been mirrored by an increase in the number of institutions of higher education.

Since 1975 the number of public universities has grown from two to twelve and since their introduction in 1980, the number of higher technical and vocational institutes has grown to 84.[^cite_note-Libedu-274] Since 2007 some new private universities such as the [Libyan International Medical University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_International_Medical_University) have been established. Although before 2011 a small number of private institutions were given accreditation, the majority of Libya's higher education has always been financed by the public budget. In 1998 the budget allocation for education represented 38.2% of Libya's national budget.[^cite_note-Libedu2-277]

In 2024, the Ministry of Education announced the launch of the Full-Day School Project in which 12 schools in different parts of the country will have longer school days. The project aims to provide 800 hours of instruction per year to 3,300 elementary school students.[^cite_note-278]

### Ethnicity

The original inhabitants of Libya belonged predominantly to [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_people) ethnic groups; however, the long series of foreign invasions and migrations – particularly by [Arabs](2185.md) – had a profound and lasting ethnic, linguistic, and cultural influence on Libyan demographics. Centuries of large-scale [Arab migration to the Maghreb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_migration_to_the_Maghreb) since the 7th century shifted the demographics of Libya in favour of Arabs. Some [Turks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turkish_people) settled in Libya during the rule of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md).

Most of Libya's inhabitants are [Arab](2185.md),[^cite_note-Encyclopaedia_Britannica,_Inc.-2014-22] with many tracing their ancestry to Bedouin Arab tribes like [Banu Sulaym](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Sulaym) and [Banu Hilal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Banu_Hilal), plus Turkish and Berber minorities. The [Turkish minority](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turks_in_Libya) are often called "[Kouloughlis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kouloughlis)" and are concentrated in and around villages and towns.[^cite_note-279] There are some ethnic minorities, such as the Berber [Tuareg](103054.md) and the Black African [Tebou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toubou_people).[^cite_note-Dupree_1958_loc=33-44-280]

Most [Italian settlers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libyans), at their height numbering over half a million, left after Italian Libya's independence in 1947. More repatriated in 1970 after the accession of Muammar Gaddafi, but a few hundred returned in the 2000s.[^cite_note-281]

### Foreign labour

As of 2023, the [International Organization for Migration (IOM)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Organization_for_Migration_\(IOM\)_Mission_in_Ukraine) estimates that approximately 10% of Libya's population (upwards of 700,000 people) constituted foreign labour.[^cite_note-282] Prior to the 2011 revolution, official and unofficial figures of migrant labour ranged from 25% to 40% of the population (between 1.5 and 2.4 million people). Historically, Libya hosted millions of low- and high-skilled Egyptian migrants, in particular.[^cite_note-283]

It is difficult to estimate the total number of immigrants in Libya because the census figures, official counts and typically more accurate unofficial estimates all differ. In the 2006 census, around 359,540 foreign nationals were resident in Libya out of a population of over 5.5 million (6.35% of the population). Almost half of these were [Egyptians](31912046.md), followed by [Sudanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Sudan) and [Palestinian](23267.md) immigrants.[^cite_note-EU_migrant_policy_Centre-284] During the 2011 revolution, 768,362 immigrants fled Libya as calculated by the [IOM](36893.md), around 13% of the population at the time, although many more stayed on in the country.[^cite_note-EU_migrant_policy_Centre-284][^cite_note-285]

If consular records prior to the revolution are used to estimate the immigrant population, as many as 2 million Egyptian migrants were recorded by the Egyptian embassy in [Tripoli](31232.md) in 2009, followed by 87,200 [Tunisians](5639337.md), and 68,200 [Moroccans](5639315.md) by their respective embassies. [Turkey](11125639.md) recorded the evacuation of 25,000 workers during the 2011 uprising.[^cite_note-286] The number of Asian migrants before the revolution were just over 100,000 (60,000 Bangladeshis, 20,000 Filipinos, 18,000 Indians, 10,000 Pakistanis, as well as Chinese, Korean, Vietnamese, Thai and other workers).[^cite_note-287][^cite_note-288] This would put the immigrant population at almost 40% before the revolution and is a figure more consistent with government estimates in 2004 which put the regular and irregular migrant numbers at 1.35 to 1.8 million (25–33% of the population at the time).[^cite_note-EU_migrant_policy_Centre-284]

Libya's native population of Arabs-Berbers as well as Arab migrants of various nationalities collectively make up 97% of the population as of 2014.

### Languages

Main article: [Languages of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Languages_of_Libya)

According to the [CIA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CIA), the official language of Libya is [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language).[^cite_note-CIA-1] The local [Libyan Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_Arabic) variety is spoken alongside [Modern Standard Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Modern_Standard_Arabic). Various [Berber languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages) are also spoken, including [Tamasheq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tamasheq_language), [Ghadamis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadamis), [Nafusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nafusi), [Suknah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suknah) and [Awjilah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Awjilah_\(language\)).[^cite_note-CIA2021-262] The Libyan Amazigh High Council (LAHC) has declared the Amazigh ([Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) or Tamazight) language to be official in the cities and districts inhabited by the Berbers in Libya.[^cite_note-289] In addition, [English](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language) is widely understood in the major cities, while the former colonial language of [Italian](14708.md) is also used in commerce and by the remaining Italian population.[^cite_note-CIA2021-262][^cite_note-290]

### Religion

Main article: [Religion in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religion_in_Libya)



Mosque in [Ghadames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadames), close to the [Tunisian and Algerian border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Algeria%E2%80%93Tunisia_border)

About 97% of the population in Libya are [Muslims](19541.md), most of whom belong to the [Sunni branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam).[^cite_note-CIA2021-262][^cite_note-Chivvis-2014-291] Small numbers of [Ibadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ibadi) Muslims live in the country.[^cite_note-292][^cite_note-293]

Before the 1930s, the [Senussi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senussi) Sunni Sufi movement was the primary Islamic movement in Libya. This was a religious revival adapted to desert life. Its *zawaaya* (lodges) were found in [Tripolitania](664792.md) and [Fezzan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fezzan), but Senussi influence was strongest in [Cyrenaica](75451.md). Rescuing the region from unrest and anarchy, the Senussi movement gave the Cyrenaican tribal people a religious attachment and feelings of unity and purpose.[^cite_note-senussi-294] This Islamic movement was eventually destroyed by the [Italian invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italo-Turkish_War). [Gaddafi](53029.md) asserted that he was a devout Muslim, and his government was taking a role in supporting [Islamic](6037917.md) institutions and in worldwide proselytising on behalf of Islam.[^cite_note-295]

The [International Religious Freedom Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Religious_Freedom_Report) 2004 noted that "[bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishops), [priests](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priests) and [nuns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuns) wear religious dress freely in public and report virtually no discrimination," while also "enjoying good relations with the Government". The report also indicated that members of minority religions said "they do not face harassment by authorities or the Muslim majority on the basis of their religious practices". The [International Christian Concern](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Christian_Concern) does not list Libya as a country where there is "persecution or severe discrimination against Christians".[^cite_note-296] Since the [fall of Gaddafi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Libyan_Civil_War), ultra-conservative strains of Islam have reasserted themselves in places. [Derna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Derna,_Libya) in eastern Libya, historically a hotbed of [jihad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadist) thought, came under the control of militants aligned with the [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) in 2014.[^cite_note-297] Jihadist elements have also spread to [Sirte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sirte) and [Benghazi](204114.md), among other areas, as a result of the [Second Libyan Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Libyan_Civil_War_\(2014%E2%80%93present\)).[^cite_note-298][^cite_note-299]

Prior to independence, Libya was home to more than 140,000 [Christians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian) (mostly of [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libyans) and Maltese ancestry). Many Christian settlers left to [Italy](14532.md) or [Malta](19137.md) after the independence.[^cite_note-300] Small foreign communities of Christians remained. [Coptic Orthodox Christianity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church), the predominant Christian church of Egypt, is the [largest and most historic Christian denomination in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coptic_Orthodox_Church_in_Africa#Libya,_Tunisia,_Algeria_and_Morocco). There are about 60,000 Egyptian [Copts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copt) in Libya.[^cite_note-301] There are three Coptic Churches in Libya, one in [Tripoli](31232.md), one in [Benghazi](204114.md), and one in [Misurata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misurata).

The Coptic Church has grown in recent years in Libya, due to the growing immigration of [Egyptian Copts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copts_in_Egypt) to Libya. There are an estimated 40,000 [Roman Catholics](606848.md) in Libya who are served by two bishops, one in Tripoli (serving the Italian community) and one in Benghazi (serving the [Maltese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maltese_people) community). There is also a small [Anglican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican) community, made up mostly of African immigrant workers in Tripoli which is part of the [Anglican Diocese of Egypt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglican_Diocese_of_Egypt). People have been arrested on suspicion of being [Christian missionaries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_missionaries), as proselytising is illegal.[^cite_note-302] Christians have also faced the threat of violence from radical Islamists in some parts of the country, with a well-publicised video released by the Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant in February 2015 depicting the mass beheading of Christian Copts.[^cite_note-303][^cite_note-304] Libya was ranked fourth on [Open Doors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Doors)' 2022 World Watch List, an annual ranking of the 50 countries where Christians face the most extreme persecution.[^cite_note-305]

Libya was once the home of one of the oldest [Jewish communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jewish_ethnic_divisions) in the world, dating back to at least 300 BC.[^cite_note-306] In 1942, the Italian Fascist authorities set up forced labour camps south of Tripoli for the [Jews](25955086.md), including [Giado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giado) (about 3,000 Jews), [Gharyan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gharyan), Jeren, and Tigrinna. In Giado, some 500 Jews died of weakness, hunger, and disease. In 1942, Jews who were not in the concentration camps were heavily restricted in their economic activity and all men between 18 and 45 years were drafted for forced labour. In August 1942, Jews from Tripolitania were interned in [a concentration camp at Sidi Azaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sidi_Azaz_labor_camp). In the three years after November 1945, more than 140 Jews were murdered, and hundreds more wounded, in a series of [pogroms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pogrom).[^cite_note-harris-307] By 1948, about 38,000 Jews remained in the country. Upon Libyan independence in 1951, most of the Jewish community emigrated.

## Culture

Main article: [Culture of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_Libya)

Further information: [Music of Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Music_of_Libya) and [Libyan literature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_literature)



Temple of Zeus in [Cyrene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cyrene,_Libya)



Ancient Roman mosaic in [Sabratha](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sabratha)



[Al-Fatah Revolution day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1969_Libyan_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) decorations in [Tripoli](31232.md) 2008

Many Arabic speaking [Libyans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Libya) consider themselves as part of a wider [Arab community](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_diaspora). This was strengthened by the spread of [Pan-Arabism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pan-Arabism) in the mid-20th century, and their reach to power in Libya where they instituted [Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language) as the only official language of the state. Under [Gaddafi](53029.md)'s rule, the teaching and even use of indigenous [Berber language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_language) was strictly forbidden.[^cite_note-308] In addition to banning foreign languages previously taught in academic institutions, leaving entire generations of Libyans with limitations in their comprehension of the [English language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/English_language). Both the spoken Arabic dialects and Berber, still retain words from [Italian](14708.md), that were acquired before and during the *[Libia Italiana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Libya)* period.

Libyans have a heritage in the traditions of the previously nomadic [Bedouin](61770.md) Arabic speakers and sedentary [Berber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berbers) tribes. Most Libyans associate themselves with a particular family name originating from tribal or conquest based heritage.[^cite_note-309]

Reflecting the "nature of giving" ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic_language): الاحسان *_Ihsan_*, [Berber languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berber_languages): ⴰⵏⴰⴽⴽⴰⴼ Anakkaf), amongst the Libyan people as well as the sense of hospitality, the state of Libya made it to the top 20 on [Charities Aid Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charities_Aid_Foundation)'s World Giving Index in 2013.[^cite_note-310] According to [Charities Aid Foundation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charities_Aid_Foundation) (CAF), in a typical month, almost three-quarters (72%) of all Libyans helped somebody they did not know – the third highest level across all 135 countries surveyed.

There are few theatres or art galleries due to the decades of cultural repression under the Gaddafi regime and lack of infrastructure development under the regime of dictatorship.[^cite_note-311] For many years there have been no public theatres, and only very few cinemas showing foreign films. The tradition of [folk culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Folklore) is still alive and well, with troupes performing music and dance at frequent festivals, both in Libya and abroad.[^cite_note-312]

A large number of [Libyan television](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Television_in_Libya) stations are devoted to political review, Islamic topics and cultural phenomena. A number of TV stations air various styles of traditional Libyan music. [Tuareg music](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuareg_music) and dance are popular in [Ghadames](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghadames) and the south. Libyan television broadcasts air programs mostly in Arabic though usually have time slots for English and French programs. A 1996 analysis by the [Committee to Protect Journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Committee_to_Protect_Journalists) found Libya's media was the most tightly controlled in the Arab world during the country's dictatorship.[^cite_note-mediacont-313] As of 2012 hundreds of TV stations have begun to air due to the collapse of censorship from the old regime and the initiation of "free media".

Many Libyans frequent the country's beach and they also visit Libya's archaeological sites – especially [Leptis Magna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leptis_Magna), which is widely considered to be one of the best preserved Roman archaeological sites in the world.[^cite_note-314] The most common form of public transport between cities is the bus, though many people travel by automobile. There are no railway services in Libya, but these are planned for construction in the near future (see [rail transport in Libya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Libya)).[^cite_note-315]

Libya's capital, [Tripoli](31232.md), has many museums and archives. These include the Government Library, the Ethnographic Museum, the Archaeological Museum, the National Archives, the Epigraphy Museum and the Islamic Museum. The [Red Castle Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Castle_Museum) located in the capital near the coast and right in the city centre, built in consultation with [UNESCO](21786641.md), may be the country's most famous.[^cite_note-316]

### Cuisine

Main article: [Libyan cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libyan_cuisine)



[Bazeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazin_\(bread\)), a communal bread dish

Libyan cuisine is a mixture of the different [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_cuisine), [Bedouin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_cuisine#Bedouin_cuisine) and traditional [Arab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_cuisine) culinary influences.[^cite_note-317] [Pasta](23871.md) is the staple food in the Western side of Libya, whereas [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice) is generally the staple food in the east.

Common Libyan foods include several variations of [red tomato sauce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tomato_sauce) based pasta dishes (similar to the Italian [Sugo all'arrabbiata](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrabbiata_sauce) dish), rice, usually served with [lamb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lamb_and_mutton) or [chicken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_as_food) (typically stewed, fried, grilled, or boiled in-sauce), and [couscous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Couscous), which is steam cooked whilst held over boiling red tomato sauce and meat (sometimes also containing [zucchini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zucchini)/courgettes and [chickpeas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chickpea)), and typically served along with [cucumber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cucumber) slices, [lettuce](57079.md) and [olives](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive).

[Bazeen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bazeen), a dish made from [barley flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barley_flour) and served with red tomato sauce, is customarily eaten communally, with several people sharing the same dish, usually by hand. This dish is commonly served at traditional weddings or festivities. [Asida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asida) is a sweet version of Bazeen, made from [white flour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flour) and served with a mix of [honey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honey), [ghee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghee) or [butter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Butter). Another popular way to serve Asida is with [rub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rub_\(syrup\)) (fresh date syrup) and [olive oil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olive_oil). [Usban](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Usban) is animal tripe stitched and stuffed with rice and [vegetables](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vegetables) cooked in tomato based soup or steamed. [Shurba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chorba) is a red tomato sauce-based soup, usually served with small grains of pasta.[^cite_note-318]

A very common snack eaten by [Libyans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demographics_of_Libya) is known as *khubs bi' tun*, literally meaning "bread with tuna fish", usually served as a baked [baguette](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baguette) or [pita bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pita) stuffed with [tuna fish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tuna_dishes) that has been mixed with [harissa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harissa) (chili sauce) and olive oil. Many snack vendors prepare these [sandwiches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandwich) and they can be found all over Libya. Libyan restaurants may serve international cuisine, or may serve simpler fare such as lamb, chicken, vegetable stew, [potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potato) and [macaroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macaroni). Due to severe lack of infrastructure, many under-developed areas and small towns do not have restaurants and instead food stores may be the only source to obtain food products. [Alcohol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcoholic_beverage) consumption is illegal.[^cite_note-319]

There are four main ingredients of traditional Libyan food: olives (and olive oil), [dates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Date_palm), [grains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grain) and [milk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Milk).[^cite_note-Libyan_Food-320] Grains are roasted, ground, sieved and used for making [bread](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bread), [cakes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cake), [soups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soup) and Bazeen. Dates are harvested, dried and can be eaten as they are, made into [syrup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syrup) or slightly fried and eaten with [bsisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bsisa) and milk. After eating, Libyans often drink [black tea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_tea). This is normally repeated a second time (for the second glass of tea), and in the third round of tea, it is served with roasted [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut) or roasted [almonds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almond) known as *shay bi'l-luz* (mixed with the tea in the same glass).[^cite_note-Libyan_Food-320]

### Sport

[Football](10568.md) is the most popular sport in Libya. The country hosted the [1982 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1982_African_Cup_of_Nations) and almost qualified for the [1986 FIFA World Cup](157231.md). The [national team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Libya_national_football_team) almost won the 1982 AFCON; they lost to [Ghana](1029620.md) on penalties 7–6. In 2014, Libya won the African Nations Championship after beating Ghana in the finals. Although the national team has never won a major competition or qualified for a World Cup, there is still lots of passion for the sport and the quality of football is improving.[^cite_note-321] It also participated in many Summer Olympics, such as the [2016 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2016_Summer_Olympics), the [2008 Summer Olympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_Summer_Olympics) and more.

[Horse racing](74711.md) is also a popular sport in Libya. It is a tradition of many special occasions and holidays.[^cite_note-322]
