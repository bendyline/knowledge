# Progression of the British football transfer fee record



[Bryan Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Robson) (pictured in 1992) was the subject of a record transfer in 1981.



[Alexander Isak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Isak) (pictured) was the subject of the current British football transfer fee record, set in 2025.

The **progression of the British football transfer fee record** tracks the increases in the record for the highest [transfer fee](2643292.md) paid or received by [British](31717.md) [association football](10568.md) clubs. A transfer fee is the sum of money paid by one club to purchase the contract, and therefore the playing services, of a professional footballer. Fees are not generally formally disclosed by the clubs involved, and discrepancies can occur in figures quoted in the press. [Trevor Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Francis), for example, is regarded as Britain's first [£](270673.md)1m player but was officially transferred for £975,000. The generally reported figure of £1,180,000 included [Value Added Tax](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Value_Added_Tax), fees to [the Football League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Football_League) and Francis' signing fee.[^cite_note-1] Discrepancies may also occur due to deals which involve additional sums to be paid at a later date after a player has made a certain number of appearances, joint fees for two or more players, or deals in which one player is exchanged for a sum of money plus another player.

The first three-figure transfer fee was the £100 paid by [Aston Villa](42173.md) in 1893 for [Willie Groves](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willie_Groves). Eleven years later, [Alf Common](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alf_Common) joined [Middlesbrough](298603.md) for the first four-figure fee, a sum which caused a national sensation and outrage amongst the football authorities.[^cite_note-Davies158-2] The £5,000 mark was first reached in 1922 when [Falkirk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Falkirk_F.C.) paid that amount for [West Ham United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ham_United_F.C.)'s [Syd Puddefoot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Syd_Puddefoot), and six years later [Arsenal](2174.md) paid the first £10,000 fee to acquire [David Jack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Jack_\(footballer\)) of [Bolton Wanderers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolton_Wanderers_F.C.). After the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), the spending power of clubs in mainland Europe outstripped that of British clubs for the first time, resulting in several substantial jumps in the transfer record. [John Charles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Charles) became the first player from Britain to command a fee of £50,000 when he joined [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus_FC) in 1957 from [Leeds United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leeds_United_F.C.), and four years later [Denis Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Denis_Law) joined [Torino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torino_F.C.) in the first £100,000 transaction involving a British club.

The 1970s saw a rapid increase in transfer fees. [Martin Peters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martin_Peters) became the first £200,000 player in 1970, but by 1977 [Kevin Keegan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kevin_Keegan)'s move to [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany)'s [Hamburger SV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hamburger_SV) had taken the record to £500,000. In January 1979 [David Mills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/David_Mills_\(footballer\)) became the first player to be purchased for £500,000 by a British club, but just one month later [Nottingham Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nottingham_Forest_F.C.) paid twice that amount to acquire [Birmingham City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Birmingham_City_F.C.)'s [Trevor Francis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trevor_Francis). In 1981 [Bryan Robson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bryan_Robson) cost [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United_F.C.) £1,500,000, but fees paid by British clubs lagged behind those paid by clubs in [Italy](14532.md), [France](5843419.md) and [Spain](26667.md). The fees paid by the [Premier League](11250.md)'s top clubs began to increase at a rapid rate, with [Alan Shearer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Shearer) commanding the first £15,000,000 fee in 1996, paid by [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.) and the new millennium heralding the first £30,000,000 transfer, although sources differ as to whether this barrier was broken by [Rio Ferdinand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rio_Ferdinand)'s move to Manchester United in 2002 or [Andriy Shevchenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriy_Shevchenko)'s transfer to [Chelsea](7473.md) four years later.

On 1 September 2008, [Manchester City](165813.md) agreed a reported £32,500,000 fee for [Robinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robinho),[^cite_note-3] which remained the record amount paid by a British club until 31 January 2011, when [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) paid £35,000,000 for [Andy Carroll](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andy_Carroll) from [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.), which was also a new record amount paid for a British player. A few hours later, the record amount paid by a British club was broken again when [Chelsea](7473.md) paid £50,000,000 for [Fernando Torres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Torres) from Liverpool.

On 11 June 2009, Manchester United announced that they had accepted an £80,000,000 bid from [Real Madrid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_Madrid_C.F.) for [Cristiano Ronaldo](623737.md). The transfer was completed on 1 July 2009, setting not only a new British transfer record, but also a new [world record](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_football_transfer_record) (either in pounds or [euros](9472.md)).[^cite_note-Ronaldo_completes_£80m_Real_move-4] In turn, that record was broken on 1 September 2013 when Real announced that their £85.3 million (€100 million) purchase of [Gareth Bale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gareth_Bale) from [Tottenham Hotspur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tottenham_Hotspur_F.C.) had been completed.[^cite_note-5] This record was broken on 8 August 2016, when [Manchester United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchester_United) signed [Paul Pogba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Pogba) from [Juventus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juventus) for a fee of £89 million, again on 6 January 2018, when [Philippe Coutinho](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Philippe_Coutinho) moved to Barcelona from Liverpool for a reported initial fee of £105 million, which could rise to £142 million with various clauses being met.&#x20;

On 31 January 2023, the record was broken when [Chelsea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chelsea_F._C.) signed Argentine player [Enzo Fernández](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Fern%C3%A1ndez) for a reported deal of £106.8 million. It was broken again on 1 September 2025 when Swedish striker [Alexander Isak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Isak) joined [Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) from [Newcastle United](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newcastle_United_F.C.) for a reported £125 million.
