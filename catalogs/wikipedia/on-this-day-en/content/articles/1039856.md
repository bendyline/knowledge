The **Ivory Coast national football team** ([French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_language): *Équipe de football de Côte d'Ivoire*, recognized as the **Côte d'Ivoire** by [FIFA](11049.md)[^cite_note-2]) represents [Ivory Coast](57744.md) in men's international [football](10568.md). Nicknamed *the Elephants*, the team is managed by the [Ivorian Football Federation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivorian_Football_Federation) (FIF). The team has won the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md) three times, in [1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_African_Cup_of_Nations), [2015](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and [2023](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), and has qualified for the [FIFA World Cup](11370.md) four times, in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), [2010](168079.md), [2014](656933.md) and [2026.](36581929.md)

Ivory Coast's home colours are all orange. Since 2020 their home games have been played at [Alassane Ouattara Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alassane_Ouattara_Stadium), in Abidjan. Prior to this their home ground was [Felix Houphouet Boigny Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Houphouet_Boigny_Stadium), also in Abidjan. [Didier Zokora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Zokora) holds the record for number of caps, with 123. The nation's leading goalscorer is [Didier Drogba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Drogba), who scored 65 goals for *the Elephants* in 105 appearances.

## History

### Early history: 1960s–1980s

The team played its first international match against [Dahomey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benin_national_football_team), now known as [Benin](3459.md), which they won 3–2 on 13 April 1960 in [Madagascar](18964.md).[^cite_note-3]

The team achieved an 11–0 victory against the [Central African Republic national football team](5478.md).[^cite_note-4] In 1961 the team made their first appearance in the [Africa Cup of Nations](250680.md).[^cite_note-5] After gaining independence from [France](5843419.md), the team finished third in the [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) and [1965](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) tournaments.

Ivory Coast's performances in the 1970s were mixed. In the [1970 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1970_African_Cup_of_Nations), the team finished top of their group, but lost to Ghana – the powerhouses of African football at the time – in the semi-finals, and went on to finish 4th after losing the third-place play-off to the [United Arab Republic](1022953.md) (now Egypt).[^cite_note-6] They failed to qualify for [the 1972 edition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1972_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), losing 4–3 to [Congo-Brazzaville](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congo_national_football_team) in the final qualifying round,[^cite_note-7] and then qualified in [1974](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1974_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) but finished bottom of their group with only a single point.[^cite_note-8] Missing the [1976](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1976_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) tournament,[^cite_note-9] the team initially qualified for [1978](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), beating [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team) 2–1 on aggregate, but were disqualified for fielding an ineligible player in the second leg. Mali were also disqualified, due to police and stadium security assaulting the match officials during the first leg, resulting in [Upper Volta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burkina_Faso_national_football_team), who Ivory Coast had beaten in the first qualifying round, inheriting their place.[^cite_note-10]

In [1984](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1984_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), the team hosted the African Cup of Nations for the first time, but failed to get out of their group.[^cite_note-11] In [1986](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1986_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), they narrowly qualified from their group on goals scored, and went on to finish third once more, beating Morocco 3–2 in the third-place play-off.[^cite_note-12]

### 1990s

At the [1992 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1992_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), Ivory Coast beat [Algeria](1029528.md) 3–0[^cite_note-13] and drew 0–0 with Congo to finish top of their group.[^cite_note-14] An extra-time victory over Zambia[^cite_note-15] and a penalty shoot-out win over Cameroon[^cite_note-16] took them to the final for the first time, where they faced Ghana. The match again went to a penalty shoot-out, which became, at the time, the highest-scoring in international football; Ivory Coast eventually triumphed 11–10 to win the title for the first time.[^cite_note-17] They were unable to defend their title in [1994](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994_Africa_Cup_of_Nations), losing to [Nigeria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nigeria_national_football_team) in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-18]

### 2000s and World Cup debut

In October 2005, Ivory Coast secured qualification for the [2006 FIFA World Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_FIFA_World_Cup), their first-ever appearance at the tournament. Having been drawn into a "[group of death](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_of_death)" that also featured Cameroon and Egypt, Ivory Coast went into the final match second behind Cameroon, but qualified after beating Sudan 3–1 while Cameroon could only draw with Egypt.[^cite_note-19]

Ivory Coast finished runners-up at the [2006 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_African_Cup_of_Nations), with the tournament including another lengthy penalty shootout, where Ivory Coast defeated [Cameroon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameroon_national_football_team) 12–11.[^cite_note-20]

In the tournament itself, Ivory Coast were drawn into another group of death, against [Argentina](454699.md), [Netherlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netherlands_national_football_team), and [Serbia and Montenegro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serbia_and_Montenegro_national_football_team).[^cite_note-21] They lost 2–1 to Argentina[^cite_note-22] – with [Didier Drogba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didier_Drogba) scoring the team's first-ever World Cup goal in the 82nd minute – and then 2–1 to the Netherlands,[^cite_note-23] meaning they had already been eliminated by the time they played Serbia and Montenegro. After going 2–0 down after just 20 minutes, Ivory Coast came back to win 3–2, with [Bonaventure Kalou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bonaventure_Kalou) scoring an 86th-minute penalty to give Ivory Coast their first-ever World Cup victory.[^cite_note-24]

After [Uli Stielike](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uli_Stielike) left before the [2008 African Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2008_African_Cup_of_Nations) due to his son's health, co-trainer Gerard Gili took his position.[^cite_note-25] To compensate of the lack of another co-coach, Didier Drogba acted as a player-coach. This was only the second time that a player had also acted as a coach at the tournament, after [George Weah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_Weah) was both player and coach for [Liberia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberia_national_football_team) during the [2002 tournament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_African_Cup_of_Nations).

### 2010s



The national football team of the Côte d'Ivoire before a match against Poland in 2010

Ivory Coast qualified for the [2010 FIFA World Cup](168079.md) in [South Africa](17416221.md), and were again drawn in a group of death, against five-time champions [Brazil](149286.md), [Portugal](576328.md), and [North Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Korea_national_football_team).[^cite_note-26] Having managed a 0–0 draw against Portugal,[^cite_note-27] a 3–1 defeat to Brazil meant that in order to qualify from their group,[^cite_note-28] they would have to beat North Korea,[^cite_note-\:0-29] while Brazil needed to beat Portugal, and thanks to Portugal's 7–0 win over North Korea,[^cite_note-30] there needed to be a substantial swing in goal difference. Ivory Coast won 3–0,[^cite_note-\:0-29] but Portugal held Brazil to a 0–0 draw[^cite_note-31] and Ivory Coast were once again eliminated in the group stage.[^cite_note-32]

The team made a third appearance in the [2014 FIFA World Cup](656933.md) in [Brazil](3383.md), where they were drawn into [Group C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014_FIFA_World_Cup_Group_C) against [Colombia](680175.md), [Greece](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greece_national_football_team), and [Japan](993546.md).[^cite_note-33] After coming from behind to beat Japan 2–1,[^cite_note-34] Ivory Coast then lost 2–1 to Colombia,[^cite_note-35] leaving their qualification in the balance. In their final match against Greece, the score was 1–1 going into stoppage time, and with Japan losing 4–1 to Colombia, Ivory Coast looked set to qualify. However, in the 93rd minute, [Giovanni Sio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giovanni_Sio) gave away a penalty which [Georgios Samaras](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgios_Samaras) converted, giving Greece both the victory and the place in the last 16;[^cite_note-36] Ivory Coast, meanwhile, went out in the group stage for the third tournament in a row.[^cite_note-37]

In 2015, the national team won the [Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) for the second time in [Equatorial Guinea](9366.md), defeating [Ghana](1029620.md) in a 22-shot penalty shootout, winning 9–8 after a scoreless game.[^cite_note-38]

Ivory Coast failed to qualify for the [2018 FIFA World Cup](13327177.md). After needing a win in their final match against [Morocco](1024865.md), they lost 2–0, meaning Morocco qualified instead.[^cite_note-39]

### 2020s



The national football team of the Côte d'Ivoire in 2022

In early 2024, Ivory Coast hosted the [Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations) for the second time.[^cite_note-40] Following a 4–0 defeat to [Equatorial Guinea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Equatorial_Guinea_national_football_team) and third-placed finish in the group stage, coach [Jean-Louis Gasset](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jean-Louis_Gasset) was dismissed, and assistant coach [Emerse Faé](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emerse_Fa%C3%A9) was hired as caretaker in the knockout stages, as the national team qualified as one of the best third-placed teams.[^cite_note-41] Later on, Ivory Coast managed to defeat the defending champions [Senegal](1017436.md) in the penalty shoot-outs, [Mali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mali_national_football_team) after extra-time, and [DR Congo](1030243.md) in the semi-finals.[^cite_note-42] They eventually won the title, defeating Nigeria 2–1 in the final, marking their third victory.[^cite_note-43] For the first time in the history of the African Cup of Nations, the tournament was won by a team that changed coaches during the tournament.[^cite_note-44]

## Home stadium

From 1964 to 2020, [Felix Houphouet Boigny Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Felix_Houphouet_Boigny_Stadium), a 50,000-seater stadium in [Abidjan](21371767.md) was the main venue used to host home matches. In 2020, the 60,000-seat [Alassane Ouattara Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alassane_Ouattara_Stadium), also in Abidjan, was opened ahead of the [2023 Africa Cup of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Africa_Cup_of_Nations).[^cite_note-45]

## Supporters

Supporters of the Elephants are known to be among the most colorful in Africa. At Ivory Coast matches, the Elephants supporter sections typically include a percussion band that mimics the sounds of an [elephant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elephant) traveling through a forest.[^cite_note-46]
