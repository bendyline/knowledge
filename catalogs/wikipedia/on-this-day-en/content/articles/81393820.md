**Hurricane Melissa** was an extremely powerful [tropical cyclone](8282374.md) that made a catastrophic landfall in [Jamaica](15660.md) in late October 2025. By [maximum sustained winds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maximum_sustained_winds), it is tied with [Hurricane Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Allen) as the strongest [Atlantic hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_hurricane), and with [Hurricane Dorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dorian) and the [1935 Labor Day hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Labor_Day_hurricane) as the strongest landfalling Atlantic hurricane. Allen and Melissa are the only Atlantic hurricanes to attain maximum sustained winds of 190 mph (305 km/h). In terms of minimum [central pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Central_pressure), it is tied with the 1935 Labor Day hurricane as the [third-most intense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_most_intense_tropical_cyclones#North_Atlantic_Ocean) Atlantic hurricane on record, and was the second-most intense at landfall. Climate scientists analyzing Melissa concluded that human-driven [climate change](5042951.md), which raises ocean temperatures, intensified the hurricane's destructive winds and rainfall.

The thirteenth and final [named storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclone_naming), fifth [hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane), fourth [major hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_hurricane), and third [Category 5 hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Category_5_hurricane) of the [2025 Atlantic hurricane season](65411671.md), Melissa formed from a [tropical wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wave) that originated from [West Africa](67393.md) on October 13. It traveled from the central Atlantic to the [Windward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_Islands), and then moved quickly westward into the Caribbean Sea, where it slowed down and [developed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclogenesis) into Tropical Storm Melissa on October 21. Weak steering currents and moderate wind shear kept Melissa meandering and disorganized for the next few days as it slowly moved northwest. Over the following days, Melissa became better organized, and from October 25 to 27, [rapidly intensified](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_intensification) into a Category 5 hurricane before making [landfall](5016402.md) near [New Hope](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Hope,_Jamaica), Jamaica, just below peak intensity, on October 28. Melissa emerged from the north coast of Jamaica later that day, weakened, and made landfall near [Chivirico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chivirico), Cuba, the next day. Melissa weakened to a Category 1 hurricane after landfall, approached Bermuda as a Category 2 hurricane, then again weakened before becoming a hurricane-force [extratropical low](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_low) on October 31 northeast of [Bermuda](3460.md), dissipating the following day.

Melissa was the strongest recorded hurricane to strike Jamaica, surpassing [Hurricane Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Gilbert) in [1988](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_Atlantic_hurricane_season); numerous structures were completely destroyed. In [Hispaniola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispaniola), even though Melissa itself stayed far to the southwest, its outer [rainbands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainband) produced heavy rains over the island, leading to severe and deadly flash floods and [landslides](37208.md). Jamaica saw catastrophic damage from Melissa, with several locations near the point of landfall experiencing almost total destruction, especially around the [Westmoreland](1439909.md) and [Saint Elizabeth parishes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Elizabeth,_Jamaica). Significant agricultural and infrastructure damage occurred in these regions due to [storm surge](449744.md), flooding, and high wind gusts from Melissa's eyewall passing over. Many towns in western Jamaica were also significantly flooded as a result, which also hampered recovery efforts. Telecommunications were also severely affected, especially in the western parts of Jamaica. In all, 95 deaths were attributed to Melissa, including 45 in Jamaica, 43 in [Haiti](13373.md), 4 in the [Dominican Republic](8060.md), and 1 in Cuba. Estimated monetary losses in Jamaica were over US\$12 billion. Owing to its catastrophic impacts in Jamaica, the name *Melissa* was retired from the rotating name list in March 2026 by the [World Meteorological Organization](33584.md), and will never again be used for an Atlantic hurricane. It was replaced with the name *Molly,* which will first appear in the list for the 2031 season.

Melissa developed from a [tropical wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_wave) that first moved off the coast of Africa on October 13.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] On October 16, the [National Hurricane Center](255317.md) (NHC) began monitoring the wave for potential development.[^cite_note-2] By October 19, the wave moved through the [Windward Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Windward_Islands) and into the [Caribbean Sea](7822.md).[^cite_note-98L_Melissa-3] The disturbance moved quickly westward, then slowed significantly, providing an opportunity for development of a well-defined center and organized deep [convection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convection_\(meteorology\)) early on October 21, becoming Tropical Storm Melissa.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] On account of weak steering currents between [ridges of high pressure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ridge_\(meteorology\)) to its northwest and southeast, Melissa moved slowly and erratically west to northwest over the central Caribbean, and was unable to strengthen appreciably due to westerly [wind shear](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind_shear) from a broad upper level [trough](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trough_\(meteorology\)) moving over the [Gulf of Mexico](21076367.md); the circulation would constantly reform to the east during this period.[^cite_note-4] The trough then bypassed Melissa, resulting in a gradual decrease in the shear.[^cite_note-5] This allowed Melissa's core to become vertically aligned.[^cite_note-6] Later, increased [outflow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outflow_\(meteorology\)) became apparent in the eastern half of the system as it slowly drifted north on October 25.[^cite_note-7] By this time, the northern motion halted as Melissa was increasingly being steered by the ridge to its northwest.[^cite_note-8]

Enabled by very favorable environmental conditions, consisting of [sea surface temperatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_surface_temperature) (SSTs) of 86–88 °F (30–31 °C), very high [ocean heat content](13165796.md), wind shear decreasing to lighter values, and very high levels of mid-level [relative humidity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Relative_humidity), Melissa commenced a period of [rapid intensification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_intensification) late on October 25 and became a hurricane that afternoon. Its maximum sustained winds nearly doubled from 70 mph (110 km/h) to 130 mph (215 km/h) in 24 hours.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] While moving generally westward, Melissa attained Category 5 status early on October 27; its winds reached 180 mph (285 km/h) by 18\:00 UTC and stayed at that level for about 18 hours.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] Unusually for a storm of such high intensity, Melissa never underwent an [eyewall replacement cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eyewall_replacement_cycle), allowing it to sustain its high intensity for a prolonged period, according to several meteorologists.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] After turning north-northeastward, the system attained maximum sustained winds of 190 mph (305 km/h), and a minimum central pressure of 892 mbar ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPa)) (26.34 [inHg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inch_of_mercury)) at 12\:00 UTC on October 28.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] Land interaction imparted slight weakening, and at 17\:25 UTC, the storm made [landfall](5016402.md) near New Hope in Westmoreland Parish just below peak intensity, with sustained winds of 185 mph (295 km/h) and a pressure of 897 mbar ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPa)) (26.49 [inHg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inch_of_mercury)).[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] The hurricane weakened inland over the mountains, and emerged off Jamaica's north coast into the Caribbean at Category 3 strength, headed toward [Cuba](5042481.md).[^cite_note-Mtcr-1]

The storm continued to weaken as it headed towards Cuba and the hurricane made landfall in eastern Cuba at 07\:10 UTC on October 29 about 20 mi (30 km) east of [Chivirico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chivirico), with sustained Category 3 winds of 115 mph (185 km/h) and a pressure of 954 mbar ([hPa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HPa)) (28.17 [inHg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inch_of_mercury)).[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] Inland, the rugged terrain of Cuba led to additional weakening, and the storm's sustained winds fell to high-end Category 1 strength by the time Melissa moved back offshore, into the [Atlantic Ocean](698.md), eight hours later.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] The storm accelerated northeast, maintaining that intensity before making landfall in [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island,_Bahamas) at 22\:15 UTC on 29 October, followed by another landfall at [San Salvador Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Salvador_Island), Bahamas with a slightly lower pressure at 02\:00 UTC on 30 October.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1][^cite_note-CC_strengthened-12] Late that day, the storm re-strengthened once more to Category 2 intensity,[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] attaining sustained winds of 105 mph (165 km/h), as it headed towards [Bermuda](3460.md) on the morning of October 30. Increasing wind shear and decreasing sea surface temperatures caused Melissa to undergo [extratropical transition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extratropical_transition) early on October 31,[^cite_note-13] and to weaken again to Category 1 strength, as it passed near Bermuda.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] Melissa completed its transition to a hurricane-force extratropical cyclone that day,[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] dissipating fully on November 1 as it opened into a trough and merged into a larger trough.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1]

### Records

Globally, Hurricane Melissa is the strongest [tropical cyclone of 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclones_in_2025)[^cite_note-strongest_earth-14] and the strongest tropical cyclone to make landfall in Jamaica on record.[^cite_note-15] Melissa is tied with [Hurricane Allen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Allen) as the most intense Atlantic hurricane in terms of one-minute sustained winds and is tied with the [1935 Labor Day hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935_Labor_Day_hurricane) as the [third-most intense Atlantic hurricane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_the_most_intense_tropical_cyclones) on record in terms of lowest barometric pressure.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1][^cite_note-sky-16][^cite_note-17] Melissa is the second most intense Atlantic hurricane at landfall in terms of lowest barometric pressure after the 1935 Labor Day hurricane and is tied with [Hurricane Dorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Dorian) and the 1935 Labor Day hurricane for the strongest Atlantic hurricane at landfall in terms of maximum sustained winds.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1][^cite_note-sky-16][^cite_note-18] Melissa also holds the record for the highest wind gust ever measured by [dropsonde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dropsonde) – 252 mph (406 km/h), surpassing the previous record of 241 mph (387 km/h) by [Typhoon Megi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon_Megi_\(2010\)) in 2010.[^cite_note-dropsonde-verif-19][^cite_note-20]

### Effect of climate change

Further information: [Tropical cyclones and climate change](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tropical_cyclones_and_climate_change)

Melissa experienced extreme rapid intensification as it drifted slowly over waters that were 2.5 °F (1.4 °C) warmer than average, sea conditions that two [rapid attribution studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extreme_event_attribution)[^cite_note-CC_strengthened-12][^cite_note-WWA_202511-21] estimated to be 500–900 times more likely to be that warm because of [human-caused climate change](5042951.md).[^cite_note-ClimateCentral_20251027-22][^cite_note-ClimateCentralStudy_20251031-23] Additionally, climate change is estimated by these studies to have strengthened the hurricane's top wind speed by about 10 mph (16 km/h), thus increasing its potential to inflict damage by up to 50%.[^cite_note-ClimateCentral_20251027-22][^cite_note-ClimateCentralStudy_20251031-23] Also, rainfall was estimated to be up to 10% higher due to climate change.[^cite_note-24]

## Preparations

### Greater Antilles



Satellite imagery of Melissa from October 21 through 31

On October 21, a hurricane watch was issued for southern portions of [Haiti](13373.md), while a tropical storm watch was issued for Jamaica.[^cite_note-25] Jamaica's watch was upgraded to a hurricane watch and tropical storm warning on October 23.[^cite_note-26] The next day, it was upgraded to a hurricane warning.[^cite_note-\:1-27] A tropical storm warning was added to the southern portions of Haiti, also on October 23.[^cite_note-28] The *[Margaritaville at Sea Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margaritaville_at_Sea_Islander)* rerouted from stops on [Grand Cayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Cayman) and Jamaica to [Belize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belize) and [Honduras](13394.md).[^cite_note-29] The *[Disney Treasure](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Treasure)* was rerouted from [Tortola](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortola) and [Saint Thomas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Thomas,_U.S._Virgin_Islands) to [Cozumel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cozumel) and *[Disney Wish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Disney_Wish)* changed its schedule at [Castaway Cay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Castaway_Cay) to allow for the *Treasure*'s route change.[^cite_note-30] *[Celebrity Beyond](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity_Beyond)* and *[Icon of the Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Icon_of_the_Seas)* saw a complete change to their schedules, moving to a western Caribbean-focused route.[^cite_note-31][^cite_note-\:14-32] *[Carnival Celebration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_Celebration)* and *[Carnival Vista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_Vista)* scrapped stops in the Dominican Republic.[^cite_note-\:14-32] *[Carnival Dream](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_Dream)* and *[Carnival Liberty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_Liberty)* cancelled stops to [Montego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montego_Bay) and *Liberty* also cancelled a stop in the Cayman Islands.[^cite_note-\:14-32] Airlines cancelled 167 flights in airports from the Greater Antilles and other nearby regions such as in the Bahamas, Barbados, Haiti, Jamaica, and the [US Virgin Islands](32135.md).[^cite_note-33]

The U.S. [National Weather Service](286989.md) in [Puerto Rico](23041.md) issued a flood warning for several municipalities due to potential heavy rains from the outer bands of Melissa on October 22.[^cite_note-\:3-34]

In the Dominican Republic, four shelters were opened in [San Juan de la Maguana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Juan_de_la_Maguana), [San Cristóbal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Crist%C3%B3bal,_Dominican_Republic), and the [National District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Distrito_Nacional) and 61 people sought sheltering.[^cite_note-\:0-35] Twelve [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_the_Dominican_Republic) were placed under red alert.[^cite_note-36] Around 90 people evacuated [Saona Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saona_Island).[^cite_note-\:2-37]

The [Government of the Cayman Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Cayman_Islands) provided sandbags for citizens.[^cite_note-38] [Cayman Airways](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Airways) waived change-of-flight fees in relation to Melissa's approach.[^cite_note-39] All [touristic diving operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuba_diving_tourism) ceased on the islands of [Little Cayman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Cayman) and [Cayman Brac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cayman_Brac) on October 24.[^cite_note-40]

In Cuba, hurricane warnings were issued for the provinces of [Granma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Granma_Province), [Santiago de Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Cuba_Province), [Guantánamo](18952312.md), [Holguín](460435.md), and [Las Tunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Tunas_Province) while a tropical storm warning was issued for [Camagüey Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camag%C3%BCey_Province).[^cite_note-41] [First Secretary of the Communist Party of Cuba](38776884.md) [Miguel Díaz-Canel](38630938.md) reported that 735,000 people were evacuated in the country's eastern regions.[^cite_note-42][^cite_note-43] Additionally, the [United States Navy](20518076.md) began evacuating several hundred people, including all non-mission-essential U.S. citizens, from [Guantanamo Bay Naval Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guantanamo_Bay_Naval_Base) to [Florida](18933066.md).[^cite_note-44]

#### Jamaica



Satellite animation of Hurricane Melissa making landfall in Jamaica on October 28

The Ministry of Transportation of Jamaica deployed 30 buses for the emergency situation in [Portmore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portmore,_Saint_Catherine), [Spanish Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Town), [Rockfort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rockfort_\(Kingston,_Jamaica\)), and [Montego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montego_Bay).[^cite_note-45] On October 25, all airports were shut down.[^cite_note-\:2-37] By October 27, regular services with the [Jamaica Urban Transit Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_Urban_Transit_Company) were suspended.[^cite_note-46] The National Water Commission mobilized teams and equipment.[^cite_note-47] The [University of the West Indies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_the_West_Indies) ordered the evacuation of its students in [Mona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mona,_Jamaica),[^cite_note-48] and graduation ceremonies at the Mona campus were postponed.[^cite_note-49] Sandy Gully in [Saint Andrew](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Andrew_Parish,_Jamaica) was [de-silted](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siltation) as the storm approached.[^cite_note-50] On October 24, public institutions including the [National Gallery of Jamaica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Gallery_of_Jamaica) and the [Bob Marley Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Marley_Museum) closed.[^cite_note-51] Shelters were opened at various locations, and by October 27, 1,700 people had evacuated. Some shelters were closed as residents were arriving, forcing them to wait outside. The government also reported that misinformation was dissuading people from using shelters.[^cite_note-\:15-52] [Black River High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_River_High_School_\(Black_River,_Jamaica\)) was confused as a shelter and some people went there.[^cite_note-\:13-53]

### Elsewhere

On October 17, a yellow adverse weather alert was issued for [Trinidad and Tobago](3565457.md) as the precursor of Melissa approached.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] On October 18, a yellow alert was also issued in [Martinique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martinique).[^cite_note-56] That same day, a flash flood warning was issued in [Barbados](3455.md).[^cite_note-57] In [Curaçao](65205.md), Prime Minister [Gilmar Pisas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gilmar_Pisas) closed schools on the island to allow for greater flexibility in the operation of emergency services.[^cite_note-58] [Colombia](5222.md) issued yellow alerts along its Atlantic coast for rough seas.[^cite_note-59]

Hurricane warnings were made for the central and southwest Bahamas while a tropical storm warning was made for the [Turks and Caicos Islands](30217.md).[^cite_note-60] Evacuation orders were made for [Acklins](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Acklins), [Crooked Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crooked_Island,_Bahamas), [Inagua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inagua), [Long Cay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Cay), [Mayaguana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayaguana), and [Ragged Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ragged_Island,_Bahamas).[^cite_note-61] Almost 1,500 residents evacuated prior to Melissa.[^cite_note-\:22-62] Residents on the southern portion of [Long Island](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Long_Island,_Bahamas) were bussed to a community center.[^cite_note-\:22-62] The [British Ministry of Defence](203793.md) deployed eight people and the [HMS *Trent*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HMS_Trent_\(P224\)) to the Turks and Caicos Islands to aid with preparations and relief. Several members of the [Parliament of the Turks and Caicos Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parliament_of_the_Turks_and_Caicos_Islands) and the [Governor of the Turks and Caicos Islands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Governor_of_the_Turks_and_Caicos_Islands) distributed food and sandbags to their constituents.[^cite_note-\:8-63] Generators were filled and tested prior to the storm and two pump stations were activated.[^cite_note-64] *Carnival Celebration*, *[Carnival Sunrise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnival_Sunrise)*, and *[Freedom of the Seas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MS_Freedom_of_the_Seas)* scrapped stops to the Turks and Caicos Islands.[^cite_note-\:14-32]

A hurricane warning was issued for Bermuda.[^cite_note-65] [The Causeway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Causeway,_Bermuda), buses, and [L.F. Wade International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L.F._Wade_International_Airport) were closed on the evening of October 30 and reopened October 31. Several ferry routes were closed on October 30 and all were closed the next day. [Cedarbridge Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedarbridge_Academy) opened as a shelter on October 30 and government offices were closed on the same day.[^cite_note-\:10-66]

After the storm, outbreaks of [mosquito-borne illnesses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosquito-borne_illness) occurred, with reports of 700 cases of [chikungunya](366112.md) per day and over 50,000 hospitalized due to the disease.[^cite_note-\:26-68]

### Hispaniola

Hispaniola received the most rainfall from Melissa,[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] with parts of the [Tiburon Peninsula](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tiburon_Peninsula) receiving over 35 inches (890 mm) of rain.[^cite_note-69] [Polo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polo,_Dominican_Republic), in the Dominican Republic, received 29 inches (740 mm) of rain.[^cite_note-Mtcr-1]

In Haiti, 43 people were killed and 13 were missing;[^cite_note-70] deaths include three from a landslide near [Port-au-Prince](57391.md),[^cite_note-\:12-71][^cite_note-72][^cite_note-73] another by a falling tree in [Marigot](18453241.md),[^cite_note-74] and 25 after the [La Digue River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Digue_River?action=edit\&redlink=1) burst its banks in [Petit-Goâve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petit-Go%C3%A2ve),[^cite_note-75][^cite_note-\:25-76] while 15 others were injured by a collapsing wall in [Artibonite Department](489220.md).[^cite_note-hl-77] Petit-Goâve was submerged by dirt and debris. The La Digue River also swept away cars and homes.[^cite_note-\:25-76] Over 645,000 people were affected.[^cite_note-78] Six medical facilities and at least 840,000 structures were damaged across the country.[^cite_note-hl-77][^cite_note-\:30-79][^cite_note-80] By November 1, 15,800 people were still in shelters.[^cite_note-81] A survey of farming households after the storm indicated agricultural losses as high as \$5,854 per household in the most affected department.[^cite_note-82]

Across the Dominican Republic, more than 1.1 million[^cite_note-83] people were without water supply after heavy rainfall and flooding disrupted water supply systems, including in [Pedro Brand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Brand), [Los Alcarrizos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Alcarrizos), and [Santo Domingo Oeste](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Domingo_Oeste).[^cite_note-84] It was reported that 56 [aqueducts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aqueduct_\(water_supply\)) were out of service.[^cite_note-\:0-35] A lane of a road in [María Trinidad Sánchez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mar%C3%ADa_Trinidad_S%C3%A1nchez_Province) collapsed and 53 bridges were damaged.[^cite_note-\:0-35][^cite_note-efe1028-67] A façade fell off of a hospital in [Constanza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constanza,_Dominican_Republic) due to rains, leading to the hospital suspending operations.[^cite_note-85] A 71-year-old man died after being swept away by a swollen river in [Santo Domingo Norte](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santo_Domingo_Norte),[^cite_note-86] while a child in [Los Mameyes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Mameyes) went missing during the heavy rainfall.[^cite_note-87] A total of four people were reported dead across the country.[^cite_note-88] Damage in the country totaled [RD\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominican_peso)1.5 billion (\$23.36 million USD).[^cite_note-efe1028-67]

### Jamaica

Melissa was the strongest as well as costliest hurricane on record to strike Jamaica, with damage estimated to be [J\$](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaican_dollar)1.95 trillion (US\$12.2 billion).[^cite_note-Mtcr-1][^cite_note-89] At least 45 people were killed, 13 were missing[^cite_note-\:31-90] and 96 others were injured across Jamaica.[^cite_note-91]

### Cuba

Earlier in the season, Cuba had experienced flooding, causing immense damage, from [Hurricane Imelda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Imelda).[^cite_note-133] Melissa also impacted the same areas that were still recovering from [Hurricane Oscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hurricane_Oscar_\(2024\)) and its associated damage to power infrastructure the year prior.[^cite_note-134] Prior to Melissa's landfall on Cuba, the hurricane caused flooding and landslides in the southeastern province of [Santiago de Cuba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santiago_de_Cuba_Province).[^cite_note-nyt-105] Melissa made landfall near [Chivirico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chivirico) in Santiago de Cuba at around 07\:20 UTC on October 29, with sustained winds of 115 mph (185 km/h). The highest reported winds in Cuba were a gust of 136 mph (219 km/h) at an elevated weather station and a gust to 116 mph (187 km/h) in the town of [Contramaestre.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contramaestre,_Cuba)[^cite_note-Mtcr-1] First Secretary [Miguel Díaz-Canel](38630938.md) reported "considerable damage" from the hurricane in Cuba, with widespread flooding and streets buried in debris across Santiago de Cuba.[^cite_note-bbc2-106] Around 992,000 houses were damaged or destroyed in [Guantánamo](18952312.md), [Holguín](460435.md), and Santiago de Cuba provinces; the region lost power and cellular service.[^cite_note-135] In the nation, 642 medical facilities and 2,100 school were damaged.[^cite_note-\:30-79][^cite_note-\:5-136] An oil refinery was damaged in Santiago de Cuba.[^cite_note-\:16-137] Infrastructure to the plant, which was necessary for repairs, and a refinery tower were the most severely damaged areas of the plant.[^cite_note-138] Crews from [Havana](49719.md) and [Sancti Spíritus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sancti_Sp%C3%ADritus) were sent to aid with its reconstruction.[^cite_note-\:16-137] [Antonio Maceo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antonio_Maceo_Airport) was damaged by Melissa and would not reopen to domestic flights until November 15.[^cite_note-139] In [El Cobre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cobre,_Cuba), in Santiago de Cuba, one death was reported.[^cite_note-140] Seventeen people were injured and three million others were affected across the country, with 735,000 in shelters.[^cite_note-141][^cite_note-142] The [Cauto River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cauto_River) overflowed, with some regions receiving around 20 inches (510 mm) of rainfall.[^cite_note-\:11-143] Multiple dams reported over 20 in (500 mm) of rain.[^cite_note-144] Flooding was recorded in [Veguitas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Veguitas) after excess water was removed from a nearby dam.[^cite_note-\:11-143] After the storm, over 400,000 people lacked access to [potable water](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potable_water).[^cite_note-\:5-136]

At the U.S. Guantanamo Bay Naval Base, downed trees led to 16 homes being considered uninhabitable and the air traffic control tower at the base's air strip was partially flooded.[^cite_note-145]

Two weeks after landfall, 60% of power infrastructure in Santiago de Cuba province was without power. About 158,000 hectares of crops were damaged or destroyed by the hurricane.[^cite_note-146] Additionally, 53,000 people remained displaced.[^cite_note-147] Due to standing water, a lack of medicine, and a general decline in Cuban quality of life, outbreaks of diseases, like [dengue fever](39669.md) and chikungunya, occurred following Melissa.[^cite_note-148] Additionally, it was reported that as of November 26, almost a month after the storm's passage, thousands remained without water or proper shelter in Cuba, in addition to continued lack of electrical power.[^cite_note-149]

### Lucayan Archipelago



Flooding caused by Melissa in the Bahamas

The [United States](32090.md) and [Jamaican Air Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaican_Air_Force) worked to deliver supplies to isolated communities in Jamaica.[^cite_note-\:17-118] The Jamaican [National Housing Trust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Housing_Trust) bought 5,000 [container homes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Container_homes) to distribute and sell among Jamaicans. were made free for the people of Jamaica with the goal of helping recovery efforts.[^cite_note-180]

On December 1, the [CAF – Development Bank of Latin America and the Caribbean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CAF_%E2%80%93_Development_Bank_of_Latin_America_and_the_Caribbean), the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), the [World Bank](36752.md), the [Caribbean Development Bank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caribbean_Development_Bank) and the [Inter-American Development Bank](382592.md) jointly announced a support package of up to US\$6.7 billion over three years to aid Jamaica's recovery from an estimated total damage of US\$8.8 billion.[^cite_note-181] Two concerts, Jamaica Strong and Nice Up Jamaica, were hosted in support of Jamaica in December, headlined by several Caribbean singers like [Johnny Osbourne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Osbourne).[^cite_note-182][^cite_note-183]

Speaking in mid-November at the [2025 United Nations Climate Change Conference](75227876.md), Jamaica's economic growth minister, [Matthew Samuda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Matthew_Samuda), told delegates, "Hurricane Melissa changed the life of every Jamaican in less than 24 hours." He also identified the hurricane as evidence of "the new phase of climate change," and called on the global community to fulfill their commitments to mitigating the worst effects of climate change.[^cite_note-Guardian_cop30-184][^cite_note-185] The Minister of Tourism stated that the nation lost US\$62 million in tourism in the first week after the storm.[^cite_note-\:29-121]

On November 10, a [Beechcraft King Air](729142.md) bound for a hurricane relief mission in Jamaica crashed in [Coral Springs](109021.md), Florida, shortly after taking off from [Fort Lauderdale Executive Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Lauderdale_Executive_Airport). The two occupants of the plane, a Christian missionary and his adult daughter, were killed in the crash.[^cite_note-186][^cite_note-187] Within relief shipments, four firearms were found by the [Jamaica Customs Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jamaica_Customs_Agency), which are under investigation.[^cite_note-188]

In January 2026, the Jamaican forestry department announced an initiative to plant 300,000 trees to help recovery of forest ecosystems damaged by Melissa.[^cite_note-189] The program, known as the "Reforestation, Ecological Enhancement, and Landscape Framework initiative" or RE-LEAF, officially began in March 2026.[^cite_note-190]

### Elsewhere

The U.S. State Department would later announce it was sending \$24 million to help nations affected by Melissa (including Jamaica), with \$8.5 million to Haiti, \$500,000 to the Bahamas, and \$3 million to Cuba in coordination with the [Catholic Church](606848.md).[^cite_note-\:18-177] Additionally, the [United Nations](31769.md) appealed for \$74 million on November 5 to help aid those impacted by Melissa in Cuba.[^cite_note-191] Three humanitarian flights with supplies from the [World Food Programme](33582.md), [United Nations Population Fund](63952.md), and [Caritas Internationalis](47756384.md) were orchestrated to deliver supplies to the eastern [provinces of Cuba](239171.md).[^cite_note-\:19-178] The [Pan American Health Organization](1246706.md) delivered 23,000 pounds (10,000 kg) of medical supplies to Cuba.[^cite_note-\:30-79] China sent supplies to Cuba along with 5,000 [photovoltaic panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Photovoltaic_panels).[^cite_note-192] Starlink services were made free for the people of the Bahamas with the goal of helping recovery efforts.[^cite_note-193] Japan donated a \$1 million to Haiti after Melissa.[^cite_note-194]

Due to other islands being damaged from Hurricane Melissa, the Dominican Republic saw a burst in tourism later in 2025. The Dominican Republic approved of hundreds of flights to manage the surge.[^cite_note-195]

### Retirement

Due to the widespread destruction and loss of life in the Caribbean from the storm, particularly in Jamaica, the name *Melissa* was retired by the [World Meteorological Organization](33584.md) in March 2026, and it will never again be used for an Atlantic tropical cyclone. It was replaced with *Molly*, which will first appear on the 2031 season list.[^cite_note-196]
