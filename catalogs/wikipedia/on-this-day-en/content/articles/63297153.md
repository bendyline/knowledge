***Perseverance***[^cite_note-2] is a [NASA](18426568.md) [rover](421366.md) that has been exploring [Mars](14640471.md) since February 18, 2021,[^cite_note-landingtime-3][^cite_note-NYT-20210219-4] as part of the [Mars 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_2020) mission. Built and managed by the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory), the car-sized rover was launched on July 30, 2020,[^cite_note-Mars2020timeline-5] from [Cape Canaveral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral) aboard an [Atlas V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_V) rocket and landed in [Jezero Crater](14045083.md), a site chosen for its ancient [river delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/River_delta) that may preserve evidence of past [microbial life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microorganism).

The rover's main goals are to search for signs of ancient life, study the planet's geology and climate, and collect rock and [regolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regolith) samples for possible return to Earth by a future mission. *Perseverance* also tests technologies intended to support later [human exploration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_mission_to_Mars), including an experiment that successfully produced oxygen from the thin [carbon-dioxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbon_dioxide) atmosphere.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

*Perseverance* carries seven primary scientific instruments, 19 cameras, and two microphones.[^cite_note-presskit-7] It also deployed the experimental helicopter [*Ingenuity*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingenuity_\(helicopter\)), which in April 2021 performed the first powered and controlled flight on another planet.[^cite_note-NYT-20210419-8] Originally intended for up to five flights, *Ingenuity* completed dozens of sorties before being retired in 2024.[^cite_note-NASA_20240125-9][^cite_note-10]

Powered by a [radioisotope thermoelectric generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-mission_radioisotope_thermoelectric_generator), *Perseverance* has an expected mission duration of over a decade.[^cite_note-11] It has provided high-resolution panoramas, drilled and cached samples for later retrieval, and identified rocks which may have been habitable for ancient microbial life in Jezero Crater. In July 2024, it discovered the [Cheyava Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheyava_Falls) rock containing "possible [biosignature](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosignature)." As of 29 September 2026, *Perseverance* has been active on Mars for 1994 [sols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sol_\(day_on_Mars\)) (2049 [total days](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Day); *5 years, 223 days*) since its landing.

## Mission

Despite the high-profile success of the [*Curiosity* rover](36645032.md) landing in August 2012, NASA's [Mars Exploration Program](32506368.md) was in a state of uncertainty in the early 2010s. Budget cuts forced NASA to pull out of a planned collaboration with the [European Space Agency](10363.md) which included a rover mission.[^cite_note-12] By the summer of 2012, the program that had been launching a mission to Mars every two years suddenly found itself with no missions approved after 2013.[^cite_note-13]

In 2011, the [Planetary Science Decadal Survey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Planetary_Science_Decadal_Survey), a report from the [National Academies of Sciences, Engineering, and Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Academies_of_Sciences,_Engineering,_and_Medicine) containing an influential set of recommendations made by the planetary science community, stated that the top priority of NASA's planetary exploration program in the decade between 2013 and 2022 should be to begin a [NASA-ESA Mars Sample Return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA-ESA_Mars_Sample_Return) campaign, a four-mission project to cache, retrieve, launch, and safely return samples of the Martian surface to Earth. The report stated that NASA should invest in a sample-caching rover as the first step in this effort, with the goal of keeping costs under US\$2.5 billion.[^cite_note-14]

After the success of the *Curiosity* rover and in response to the recommendations of the decadal survey, [NASA](18426568.md) announced its intent to launch a new Mars rover mission by 2020 at the [American Geophysical Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Geophysical_Union) conference in December 2012.[^cite_note-Space_Wall_first-15]

Though initially hesitant to commit to an ambitious sample-caching capability (and subsequent follow-on missions), a NASA-convened science definition team for the Mars 2020 project released a report in July 2013 that the mission should "select and store a compelling suite of samples in a returnable cache."[^cite_note-16]

### Science objectives

The *Perseverance* rover has four main science objectives[^cite_note-17] that support the [Mars Exploration Program](32506368.md)'s science goals:[^cite_note-\:0-6]

- Looking for habitability: identify past environments that were capable of supporting [microbial life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbial_life).

- Seeking [biosignatures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biosignature): seek signs of possible past microbial life in those habitable environments, particularly in specific rock types known to preserve signs over time.

- Caching samples: collect core rock and [regolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regolith) (unconsolidated and loose "soil") samples and store them within the rover and on the [Martian surface](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martian_surface) (as a backup) for delivery to a future sample return rocket.

  [^cite_note-18]

- Preparing for humans: test [oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxygen) production from the [Martian atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martian_atmosphere).

In the first science campaign, dubbed "Crater Floor", *Perseverance* performed an arching drive southward from its landing site to the Séítah unit to perform a "toe dip" into the unit to collect remote-sensing measurements of geologic targets. After that it returned to the Crater Floor Fractured Rough to collect the first core sample there. Passing by the Octavia E. Butler landing site concluded the first science campaign.

The second campaign, "Fan Front", included several months of travel towards the "Three Forks" where *Perseverance* accessed geologic locations at the base of the ancient delta of Neretva river, as well as ascending the delta by driving up a valley wall to the northwest.[^cite_note-19]

The third and fourth campaigns were called "Upper Fan", and "Margin Unit", and the fifth campaign, "Northern Rim", in progress as of December 2024, is exploring "the northern part of the southwestern section of Jezero's rim" to study "rocks from deep down inside Mars that were thrown upward to form the crater rim" after the impact 3.9 billion years ago that formed Jezero Crater.[^cite_note-20]

#### Results

&#x20;The scientific results, as of 2025, are as follows. According to NASA, the mission has made "discoveries about the volcanic history, habitability, and role of water in Jezero Crater."[^cite_note-NASAhlts-21] Specifically, they reported that instead of all the rocks in Jezero crater being sedimentary, being "transported into the crater by wind or water," "several types of [igneous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Igneous) rock" were discovered, which "showed evidence of interaction with water."[^cite_note-NASAhlts-21] Additionally,&#x20;

> At a rock named "Wildcat Ridge" located within Jezero's well-preserved sedimentary fan deposit, Perseverance found evidence for an ancient lake environment. Not only were these sediments likely deposited in a standing body of water, but they also continued to interact with water long after they were formed. The environments recorded within the rocks at Wildcat Ridge would have been habitable for ancient microbial life, and this type of rock is ideal for preserving possible signs of ancient life.[^cite_note-NASAhlts-21]

They also found that "sediments entering Jezero's lake were deposited in a delta" and "evidence for late-stage, high-energy flooding that carried large boulders into the crater."[^cite_note-NASAhlts-21] The MOXIE experiment produced 122 grams of oxygen from carbon dioxide.[^cite_note-NASAhlts-21] The microphone studies showed that the speed of sound is slower and the volumes of sounds transmitted through the atmosphere is lower, than on Earth.[^cite_note-NASAhlts-21] PIXL found that the Seitah formation and a rock at "Otis Peak" contained olivine, phosphates, sulfates, clays, carbonate minerals, silicate minerals, "augite pyroxene, feldspathic mesostasis, various Fe,Cr,Ti-spinels, and merrillite", perchlorate, feldspar, magnesite, siderite, oxides, as well as minerals with composition including magnesium, iron, chlorine, and sodium.[^cite_note-22][^cite_note-23] SHERLOC revealed findings of high concentrations of [macromolecules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macromolecule) filled with carbon on the Cheyava Falls and Apollo Temple rocks, though it was incapable of determining whether they were of biotic origin or not.[^cite_note-Murphy_et_al_2026-24] RIMFAX revealed findings "consistent with a subsurface dominated by solid rock and mafic material"[^cite_note-25] and that "the crater floor experienced a period of erosion before the deposition of the overlying delta strata. The regularity and horizontality of the basal delta sediments observed in the radar cross sections indicate that they were deposited in a low-energy lake environment."[^cite_note-26]

## Design



*&#x20;Perseverance* in the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory) near [Pasadena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasadena), California

The *Perseverance* design evolved from its predecessor, the [*Curiosity* rover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Curiosity_rover). The two rovers share a similar body plan, landing system, cruise stage, and power system, but the design was improved in several ways for *Perseverance*. Engineers designed the rover wheels to be more robust than *Curiosity*'s wheels, which had [sustained some damage](36645032.md#Mobility_systems).[^cite_note-planetary20140819-27] *Perseverance* has thicker, more durable [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminum) wheels, with reduced width and a greater diameter, 52.5 cm (20.7 in), than *Curiosity*'s 50 cm (20 in) wheels.[^cite_note-bgr-28][^cite_note-Body-29] The aluminum wheels are covered with cleats for traction and curved [titanium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Titanium) spokes for springy support.[^cite_note-30] The [heat shield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heat_shield) for the rover was made out of [phenolic-impregnated carbon ablator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phenolic-impregnated_carbon_ablator) (PICA), to allow it to withstand up to 2,400 °F (1,320 °C) of heat.[^cite_note-31] Like *Curiosity*, the rover includes a [robotic arm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robotic_arm), although *Perseverance*'s arm is longer and stronger, measuring 2.1 m (6 ft 11 in). The arm hosts an elaborate [rock-coring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Core_sample) and sampling mechanism to store geologic samples from the Martian surface in [sterile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asepsis) caching tubes.[^cite_note-32] There is also a secondary arm hidden below the rover that helps store the chalk-sized samples. This arm is known as the Sample Handling Assembly (SHA), and is responsible for moving the soil samples to various stations within the Adaptive Caching Assembly (ACA) on the underside of the rover. These stations include volume assessment (measuring the length of sample), imaging, seal dispensing, and hermetic seal station, among others.[^cite_note-33] Owing to the small space in which the SHA must operate, as well as load requirements during sealing activities, the Sample Caching System "is the most complicated, most sophisticated mechanism that we have ever built, tested and readied for spaceflight."[^cite_note-34]

The combination of larger instruments, new sampling and caching system, and modified wheels makes *Perseverance* heavier, weighing 1,025 kg (2,260 lb) compared to *Curiosity* at 899 kg (1,982 lb), a 14% increase.[^cite_note-35]

The rover's [multi-mission radioisotope thermoelectric generator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-mission_radioisotope_thermoelectric_generator) ([MMRTG](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MMRTG)) has a mass of 45 kg (99 lb) and uses 4.8 kg (11 lb) of [plutonium-238](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plutonium-238) [oxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxide) as its power source. The radioactive decay of plutonium-238, which has a [half-life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Half-life) of 87.7 years, gives off heat which is [converted to electricity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radioisotope_thermoelectric_generator)—approximately 110 [watts](21347693.md) at launch.[^cite_note-Power-36] This will decrease over time as its power source decays.[^cite_note-Power-36] The MMRTG charges two [lithium-ion rechargeable batteries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lithium-ion_battery) which power the rover's activities, and must be recharged periodically. Unlike [solar panels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_panel), the MMRTG provides engineers with significant flexibility in operating the rover's instruments even at night, during [dust storms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dust_storm), and through winter.[^cite_note-Power-36]

The rover's computer uses the BAE Systems [RAD750](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAD750) [radiation-hardened](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radiation-hardened) single board computer based on a ruggedized [PowerPC G3 microprocessor (PowerPC 750)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PowerPC_7xx). The computer contains 128 megabytes of volatile [DRAM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dynamic_random-access_memory), and runs at 133 MHz. The flight software runs on the [VxWorks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VxWorks) [operating system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operating_system), is written in [C](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C_\(programming_language\)) and is able to access 4 gigabytes of [NAND non-volatile memory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flash_memory) on a separate card.[^cite_note-37] *Perseverance* relies on three antennas for [telemetry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Telemetry), all of which are relayed through craft currently in orbit around Mars. The primary [UHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_high_frequency) antenna can send data from the rover at a maximum rate of two megabits per second. Having multiple antennas provides communications redundancy.[^cite_note-marscom-38]

### Mars *Ingenuity* helicopter experiment

The [*Ingenuity*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingenuity_\(helicopter\)) helicopter, powered by solar-charged batteries, was sent to Mars in the same bundle with *Perseverance*. With a mass of 1.8 kg (4.0 lb), the helicopter demonstrated the reality of flight in the rarefied [Martian atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martian_atmosphere) and the potential usefulness of aerial scouting for rover missions. It carried two cameras but no scientific instruments[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-NYT-20180511-65][^cite_note-VRG-20180511-66] and communicated with Earth via a base station onboard *Perseverance*.[^cite_note-67] Its pre-launch experimental test plan was three flights in 45 days, but it far exceeded expectations and made [72 flights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ingenuity_flights) in nearly three years. After its first few flights, it made incrementally more ambitious ones, several of which were recorded by *Perseverance*'s cameras. The first flight was April 19, 2021, at 07\:15 UTC, with confirmation from data reception at 10\:15 UTC.[^cite_note-Ginny-flight-68][^cite_note-MH-status-290-69][^cite_note-Ingenuity_passes_full_speed_test-70][^cite_note-Tech_Demo-71][^cite_note-MarsDaily-72] It was the first powered flight by any aircraft on another planet.[^cite_note-NYT-20210419-8] On January 18, 2024 (UTC), it made its [72nd and final flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Ingenuity_flights), suffering the loss of a rotor blade (imaged, by *Perseverance*, lying on the sand roughly 15 m (49 ft) distant from the upright body of *Ingenuity*), causing NASA to retire it.[^cite_note-NASA_20240125-9][^cite_note-AT-20240226-73]

### Name

About twenty K-12 student finalists are standing on a stage, all smiling, and holding a banner that reads "NASA's perseverance rover". In front of them on the stage is a miniature rover.

NASA's [Thomas Zurbuchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Zurbuchen) announced the rover's official name, *Perseverance*, on March 5, 2020, at [Lake Braddock Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Braddock_Secondary_School) in [Burke, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burke,_Virginia). Zurbuchen made the final selection following a 2019 nationwide naming contest that drew more than 28,000 essays by [K-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-12) students from every U.S. state and territory.

Associate Administrator of [NASA](18426568.md)'s Science Mission Directorate [Thomas Zurbuchen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thomas_Zurbuchen) selected the name *Perseverance* following a nationwide [K-12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K-12) student "name the rover" contest that attracted more than 28,000 proposals. A [seventh-grade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secondary_education_in_the_United_States#Public_schools) student, Alexander Mather from [Lake Braddock Secondary School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Braddock_Secondary_School) in [Burke, Virginia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burke,_Virginia), submitted the winning entry at the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory). In addition to the honor of naming the rover, Mather and his family were invited to NASA's [Kennedy Space Center](16421.md) to watch the rover's July 2020 launch from [Cape Canaveral Air Force Station](306778.md) (CCAFS) in [Florida](18933066.md).[^cite_note-name_the_rover-74] He was also joined at the launch by 11th grade student Vaneeza Rupani from [Tuscaloosa County High School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuscaloosa_County_High_School) in [Northport, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northport,_Alabama), who named the [*Ingenuity*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ingenuity_\(helicopter\)) helicopter that would fly with *Perseverance*.

&#x20;Mather wrote in his winning essay:

> [Curiosity](36645032.md). [InSight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/InSight). [Spirit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_\(rover\)). [Opportunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Opportunity_\(rover\)). If you think about it, all of these names of past Mars rovers are qualities we possess as humans. We are always curious, and seek opportunity. We have the spirit and insight to explore the Moon, Mars, and beyond. ### Twin rover



The full-scale twin engineering model of *Perseverance*, the *OPTIMISM* rover is used at the JPL [Mars Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Yard) for testing procedures and solving problems.

[^cite_note-diff-75]

JPL built a copy of the *Perseverance*; a twin rover used for testing and problem solving, *OPTIMISM* (Operational Perseverance Twin for Integration of Mechanisms and Instruments Sent to Mars), a vehicle system test bed (VSTB). It is housed at the JPL [Mars Yard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Yard) and is used to test operational procedures and to aid in problem solving should any issues arise with *Perseverance*.[^cite_note-CNET-20200905-76]

In June 2026, it was reported that NASA is considering launching the spare rover to the Moon.[^cite_note-77] The mission is called Polar Rover for Observation, Mapping and In-Situ Exploration, or [PROMISE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PROMISE_\(rover\)).[^cite_note-78]

## Operational history

Further information: [Timeline of Mars 2020](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_Mars_2020)

### Mars transit

The *Perseverance* rover lifted off successfully on July 30, 2020, at 11\:50\:00 UTC aboard a [United Launch Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Launch_Alliance) [Atlas V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_V) [launch vehicle](1306157.md) from [Space Launch Complex 41](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral_Space_Launch_Complex_41), at [Cape Canaveral Air Force Station](306778.md) (CCAFS) in [Florida](18933066.md).[^cite_note-79]

The rover took 29 weeks to travel to Mars and made its landing in [Jezero Crater](14045083.md) on February 18, 2021, to begin its science phase.[^cite_note-80]

After May 17, 2022, the rover moved uphill and examined rocks on the surface for evidence of past life on [Mars](14640471.md). On its return downhill, it will collect sample rocks to be retrieved and examined by future expeditions.[^cite_note-81]

### Landing

The successful landing of *Perseverance* in [Jezero Crater](14045083.md) was announced at 20\:55 UTC on February 18, 2021,[^cite_note-landingtime-3] the signal from Mars taking 11 minutes to arrive at Earth. The rover touched down at [18°26′41″N 77°27′03″E  /  18.4446°N 77.4509°E  / 18.4446; 77.4509](https://geohack.toolforge.org/geohack.php?pagename=Perseverance_\(rover\)\&params=18.4446_N_77.4509_E_globe\:Mars),[^cite_note-82] roughly 1 km (0.62 mi) southeast of the center of its 7.7 km × 6.6 km (4.8 mi × 4.1 mi)[^cite_note-\:1-83] wide landing ellipse. It came down pointed almost directly to the southeast,[^cite_note-84] with the RTG on the back of the vehicle pointing northwest. The descent stage ("[sky crane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sky_crane_\(landing_system\))"), parachute and heat shield all came to rest within 1.5 km of the rover (see satellite image). Having come within five meters (16 ft) of its target, the landing was more accurate than any previous Mars landing; a feat enabled by the experience gained from *[Curiosity](36645032.md)*'s landing and the use of new steering technology.[^cite_note-\:1-83]

One such new technology is Terrain Relative Navigation (TRN), a technique in which the rover compares images of the surface taken during its descent with reference maps, allowing it to make last minute adjustments to its course. The rover also uses the images to select a safe landing site at the last minute, allowing it to land in relatively unhazardous terrain. This enables it to land much closer to its science objectives than previous missions, which all had to use a landing ellipse devoid of hazards.[^cite_note-\:1-83]

The landing occurred in the late afternoon, with the first images taken at 15\:53\:58 on the [mission clock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timekeeping_on_Mars#Perseverance) (local mean solar time).[^cite_note-85] The landing took place shortly after Mars passed through its [northern vernal equinox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernal_equinox_\(Northern_Hemisphere\)) ([Ls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solar_longitude) = 5.2°), at the start of the astronomical spring, the equivalent of the end of March on Earth.[^cite_note-86]

The parachute descent of the *Perseverance* rover was photographed by the [HiRISE](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/HiRISE) high-resolution camera on the *[Mars Reconnaissance Orbiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Reconnaissance_Orbiter)* (MRO).[^cite_note-87]

Jezero Crater is a paleolake basin.[^cite_note-\:2-88][^cite_note-\:3-89] It was selected as the landing site for this mission in part because paleolake basins tend to contain [perchlorates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perchlorate).[^cite_note-\:2-88][^cite_note-\:3-89] Astrobiologist [Dr. Kennda Lynch's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennda_Lynch) work in analog environments on Earth suggests that the composition of the crater, including the bottomset deposits accumulated from three different sources in the area, is a likely place to discover evidence of perchlorate-reducing microbes, if such bacteria are living or were formerly living on Mars.[^cite_note-\:2-88][^cite_note-\:3-89]

A few days after landing, *Perseverance* released the first audio recorded on the surface of Mars, capturing the sound of Martian [wind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wind).[^cite_note-90][^cite_note-91]

During its travels on Mars, NASA scientists had observed around Sol 341 (February 4, 2022) that a small rock had dropped into one of its wheels while the rover was studying the Máaz rock formation. The rock was visible from one of the hazard avoidance cameras, and was determined not to be harmful to the rover's mission. The rock has since stayed on *Perseverance*'s wheel for around 427 sols (439 days) as the rover traveled over 6 miles (9.7 km) on the Martian surface. NASA deemed that *Perseverance* had adopted a [pet rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pet_rock) for its journey.[^cite_note-92][^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] Later, by May 2024, the rover picked up another pet rock named "Dwayne".[^cite_note-95]

### Samples cached for the Mars sample-return mission



***Perseverance* rover's sampling bits**\


- The pointed one with two windows on the left is the regolith drill

- the two shorter ones on the right are abrasion tools

- the others in the center are rock drills



*Perseverance* rover - cored rock sample collection at 1574 sols (July 24, 2025)

In support of the [NASA-ESA Mars Sample Return](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NASA-ESA_Mars_Sample_Return), rock, [regolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regolith) ([Martian soil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martian_soil)), and atmosphere samples are being cached by *Perseverance*. As of July 2025, 33 out of 43 sample tubes have been filled,[^cite_note-99] including 8 igneous rock samples, 13 sedimentary rock sample tubes, 3 Igneous/[Impactite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Impactite) rock sample tubes, a [Serpentinite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpentinite) rock sample tube, a [Silica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silica)-[cemented](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cementation_\(geology\)) [carbonate rock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carbonate_rock) sample tube,[^cite_note-100] two regolith sample tubes, an atmosphere sample tube,[^cite_note-101] and three witness tubes.[^cite_note-102] Before launch, 5 of the 43 tubes were designated "witness tubes" and filled with materials that would capture particulates in the ambient environment of Mars. Out of 43 tubes, 3 witness sample tubes will not be returned to Earth and will remain on rover as the sample canister will only have 30 tube slots. Further, 10 of the 43 tubes are left as backups at the Three Forks Sample Depot.[^cite_note-103]

However, after a project review critical of its cost and complexity, NASA announced that the Mars Sample Return project was "paused" as of November 13, 2023.[^cite_note-104] In a teleconference on April 15, 2024, NASA Administrator [Bill Nelson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bill_Nelson) emphasized continuing the commitment to retrieving the samples. However, the \$11 billion cost was deemed infeasible. NASA turned to industry and the [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory) (JPL) to form a new, more fiscally feasible mission profile to retrieve the samples.[^cite_note-NASA_April_15,_2024-105][^cite_note-106]

### Possible biosignature

Main article: [Cheyava Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cheyava_Falls)

In July 2024, *Perseverance* discovered "leopard spots" on a reddish rock nicknamed "Cheyava Falls" in Mars' [Jezero Crater](14045083.md), that has some indications it may have hosted microbial life billions of years ago, but further research is needed.[^cite_note-107][^cite_note-108] Some of the additional research using the SHERLOC instrument, which included comparisons with the nearby Apollo Temple rock, was summarized in a June 2026 paper, though the spots still could not be determined to be of biotic origin.[^cite_note-Murphy_et_al_2026-24]

## Cost

NASA plans to invest roughly US\$2.75 billion in the project over 11 years, including US\$2.2 billion for the development and building of the hardware, US\$243 million for launch services, and US\$291 million for 2.5 years of mission operations.[^cite_note-presskit-7][^cite_note-cost-109]

Adjusted for inflation, *Perseverance* is NASA's sixth-most expensive robotic planetary mission, though it is cheaper than its predecessor, *Curiosity*.[^cite_note-110] *Perseverance* benefited from spare hardware and "build-to print" designs from the *Curiosity* mission, which helped reduce development costs and saved "probably tens of millions, if not 100 million dollars" according to Mars 2020 Deputy Chief Engineer Keith Comeaux.[^cite_note-111]

### Geocaching in Space Trackable



SHERLOC's calibration target aboard the *Perseverance* Mars rover with Mars Meteorite in the center of the top row and the trackable in the bottom left corner

Part of *Perseverance*'s cargo is a [geocaching](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geocaching) [trackable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trackable_\(Geocaching\)) item viewable with the SHERLOC's WATSON camera.[^cite_note-115]

In 2016, NASA SHERLOC co-investigator Dr. Marc Fries — with help from his son Wyatt — was inspired by Geocaching's 2008 placement of a cache on the International Space Station to set out and try something similar with the rover mission. After floating the idea around mission management, it eventually reached NASA scientist Francis McCubbin, who would join the SHERLOC instrument team as a collaborator to move the project forward. The Geocaching inclusion was scaled-down to a trackable item that players could search for from NASA camera views and then log on to the site.[^cite_note-116] In a manner similar to the "Send Your Name to Mars" campaign, the geocaching trackable code was carefully printed on a one-inch, polycarbonate glass disk serving as part of the rover's calibration target. It will serve as an optical target for the WATSON imager and a spectroscopic standard for the SHERLOC instrument. The disk is made of a prototype astronaut helmet visor material that will be tested for its potential use in crewed missions to Mars. Designs were approved by the mission leads at NASA's [Jet Propulsion Laboratory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory) (JPL), NASA Public Affairs, and NASA HQ, in addition to Groundspeak Geocaching HQ.[^cite_note-117][^cite_note-118]

### Parachute with coded message



*Perseverance's* parachute&#x20;

[^cite_note-NASA-2021-114]

The orange-and-white parachute used to land the rover on Mars contained a coded message that was deciphered by Twitter users. NASA's systems engineer Ian Clark used binary code to hide the message "dare mighty things" in the parachute color pattern. The 21-meter-wide (70 ft) parachute consisted of 80 strips of fabric that form a hemisphere-shape canopy, and each strip consisted of four pieces. Dr. Clark thus had 320 pieces with which to encode the message. He also included the [GPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) coordinates for the Jet Propulsion Laboratory's headquarters in [Pasadena, California](92408.md) (34°11'58" N 118°10'31" W). Clark said that only six people knew about the message before landing. The code was deciphered a few hours after the image was presented by *Perseverance*'s team.[^cite_note-121][^cite_note-122][^cite_note-123]

"Dare mighty things" is a quote attributed to U.S. president [Theodore Roosevelt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt) and is the unofficial motto of the Jet Propulsion Laboratory.[^cite_note-124] It adorns many of the [JPL center's](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_Propulsion_Laboratory#Location) walls.
