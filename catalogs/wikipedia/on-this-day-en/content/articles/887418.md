**Blue Origin Enterprises, L.P.** is a private American [space technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_technology) company headquartered in [Kent, Washington](138005.md). The company operates the suborbital [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard) rocket and the heavy-lift [New Glenn](51585988.md) rocket. In addition to producing engines for its own rockets, Blue Origin supplies engines for other vehicles, including [United Launch Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Launch_Alliance)'s [Vulcan Centaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcan_Centaur). It is also working on the [Blue Moon](54252087.md) human lunar lander for [NASA](18426568.md)'s [Artemis program](60758751.md), the [Blue Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ring) spacecraft platform, and the [Orbital Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_Reef) space station in partnership with other organizations.

Founded in 2000 by [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos), Blue Origin initially operated with a [very low profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stealth_startup), funded by Bezos's private investments. In 2015, the company achieved a significant milestone with the first uncrewed launch and landing of the New Shepard and announced plans for New Glenn. In 2021, New Shepard [first flew crewed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-16), with Bezos himself on board. Another key achievement came in January 2023 when the company delivered its first [BE-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-4) rocket engine to United Launch Alliance.[^cite_note-2] Despite these milestones, Blue Origin has faced criticism for its perceived slow progress, particularly when compared to [SpaceX](832774.md). Addressing these challenges, the company underwent a leadership change in September 2023, appointing [Dave Limp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Limp) as CEO to succeed Bob Smith.[^cite_note-3]

On January 16, 2025, Blue Origin reached orbit with the first launch of the New Glenn vehicle.[^cite_note-4] On April 14, 2025, Blue Origin completed its 11th human spaceflight and its 31st spaceflight for the New Shepard Program with an all-female crew of six.[^cite_note-5]

In January 2026, the company decided to pause tourism launches of its New Shepard rocket for two years or more, in order to focus resources on lunar landing efforts of the Artemis program.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7]

## History

The company was founded in 2000 by [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos), the founder of [Amazon](90451.md).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] [Rob Meyerson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rob_Meyerson) joined the company in 2003 and served as the CEO before leaving the company in 2018.[^cite_note-\:1-10] Bob Smith served as CEO from 2018 to 2023.[^cite_note-11] The current CEO is Dave Limp.[^cite_note-12] Little is known about the company's activities in its early years. In 2006, the company purchased land for its [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard) missions 30 miles north of [Van Horn, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Horn,_Texas), United States called [Launch Site One (LS1)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_Ranch). In November 2006, the first test vehicle was launched, the Goddard rocket, which reached an altitude of 285 feet.[^cite_note-13]

After initiating the development of an orbital rocket system prior to 2012, and stating in 2013 on their website that the first stage would perform a powered vertical landing and be reusable, the company publicly announced their orbital [launch vehicle](1306157.md) intentions in September 2015. In January 2016, the company indicated that the new rocket would be many times larger than [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard). The company publicly released the high-level design of the vehicle and announced its name in September 2016 as "[New Glenn](51585988.md)". The [New Glenn](51585988.md) [heavy-lift launch vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy-lift_launch_vehicle) can be configured in both [two-stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two-stage-to-orbit) and [three-stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three-stage-to-orbit) variants. [New Glenn](51585988.md) first successfully launched in January, 2025.[^cite_note-\:7-14]

On July 20, 2021, [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard) performed its [first crewed mission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-16) to sub-orbital space called [Blue Origin NS-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-16). The flight lasted approximately 10 minutes and crossed the [Kármán line](738149.md). The passengers were [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos), his brother [Mark Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mark_Bezos), [Wally Funk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wally_Funk), and [Oliver Daemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oliver_Daemen), after the unnamed auction winner (later revealed to have been [Justin Sun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Justin_Sun)) dropped out due to a scheduling conflict. Subsequent New Shepard passenger and cargo missions were: [Blue Origin NS-17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-17), [Blue Origin NS-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-18), [Blue Origin NS-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-19), [Blue Origin NS-20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-20), [Blue Origin NS-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-20) and [Blue Origin NS-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_NS-23).[^cite_note-15]

The company primarily employs an incremental approach from [sub-orbital](455295.md) to [orbital flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_flight),[^cite_note-A-N1-16] with each developmental step building on its prior work. The company moved into the [orbital spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_spaceflight) technology [development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_product_development) business in 2014, initially as a [rocket engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_engine) supplier via a contractual agreement to build the [BE-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-4) rocket engine, for major US launch system operator [United Launch Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Launch_Alliance) (ULA). [United Launch Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Launch_Alliance) (ULA) has said that the first flight of its [Vulcan Centaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcan_Centaur) [heavy-lift launch vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy-lift_launch_vehicle) is scheduled to launch in Q4 of 2023. The [heavy-lift launch vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy-lift_launch_vehicle) main power is supported by two [BE-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-4) engines. On June 7, 2023, [United Launch Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Launch_Alliance) (ULA) performed a Flight Readiness Firing of the [Vulcan Centaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcan_Centaur) [rocket](26301.md) at launch pad 41 at the [Cape Canaveral Space Force Station](306778.md) in [Cape Canaveral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral), Florida, United States. The two [BE-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-4) [rocket engines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_engine) worked as expected.[^cite_note-17]

In 2024, the company won its first [National Security Space Launch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Space_Launch) (NSSL) contract. The New Glenn vehicle is to be used on the launches.[^cite_note-18]

On November 13, 2025 Blue Origin launched its first NASA mission, [ESCAPADE](73006261.md), deploying two NASA science satellites to study Mars' atmosphere. The company utilized its New Glenn rocket and successfully landed the booster on a barge [Landing Platform Vessel 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landing_Platform_Vessel_1) named [Jacklyn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacklyn_\(ship\)) in the Atlantic Ocean.[^cite_note-19]

On May 28, 2026, during a static fire of New Glenn a catastrophic explosion occurred which industry experts described as generating the largest explosion in launch vehicle history, second only to the Russian N1 rocket explosion in 1969. The blast destroyed and crippled core infrastructure at [LC-36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral_Launch_Complex_36), the custom-built launch site specifically for New Glenn.[^cite_note-20]

In June 2026, Blue Origin announced plans to modify Launch Complex 36 rather than rebuild it in its previous configuration following the May 28 New Glenn hot-fire anomaly. The company said the anomaly had damaged the launch complex's lightning tower, transporter-erector, and hydraulic cylinders, while other infrastructure remained usable. Blue Origin stated that it intended to return New Glenn to flight later in 2026.[^cite_note-21]

In July 2026, NASA and Blue Origin agreed to conduct second-stage hot-fire testing for New Glenn at NASA's B-2 test stand at the Stennis Space Center in Mississippi. The testing was intended to support development of New Glenn and future Artemis missions.[^cite_note-22]

In August 2026, Blue Origin reported that its investigation into the May 28 anomaly had determined that the failure originated at the main oxygen valve on one of the New Glenn's BE-4 engines. The company said inspections and component-level testing had confirmed the finding and that modified valve hardware was being manufactured.[^cite_note-23]

### New Shepard

Main article: [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard)

[New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard) is a [fully reusable](336014.md) [sub-orbital](455295.md) [launch vehicle](1306157.md) developed for [space tourism](67896.md). The vehicle is named after [Alan Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Shepard), the first American astronaut in space. The vehicle is capable of [vertical takeoff and landings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VTVL) and can carry humans and customer payloads to the [edge of space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edge_of_space).[^cite_note-24]

The New Shepard is a [rocket](26301.md) that consists of a [booster rocket](373603.md) and a crew capsule. The capsule can be configured to house up to six [passengers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Passenger), [cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo), or a combination of both. The [booster](373603.md) rocket is powered by one [BE-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-3)PM engine, which sends the capsule to an apogee of 100.5 kilometres (62.4 mi) and flies above the [Kármán line](738149.md), where passengers and cargo can experience a few minutes of [weightlessness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weightlessness) before the capsule returns to [Earth](9228.md).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

The launch vehicle is designed to be fully reusable, with the capsule returning to Earth via three [parachutes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parachute) and a [solid rocket motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-propellant_rocket). The booster lands vertically on the same launchpad it took off from. The company has successfully launched and landed the [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard) [launch vehicle](1306157.md) 26 times with one partial failure (deemed successful) and one failure. The launch vehicle has a length of 19.2 metres (63 ft), a diameter of 3.8 metres (12 ft) and a launch mass of 75 short tons (150,000 lb; 68,000 kg). The BE-3PM engine produces 490 kN of thrust at [takeoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Takeoff). The New Shepard allows the company to significantly reduce the cost of [space tourism](67896.md).[^cite_note-27][^cite_note-28]

On the morning of February 25, 2025, Blue Origin launched its 10th space tourism mission and sent six paying passengers to suborbital space. This was the company's tenth human flight, and its 30th overall flight of New Shepard.[^cite_note-29]

### New Glenn

Main article: [New Glenn](51585988.md)



New Glenn maiden launch, January 16, 2025



New Glenn rocket as designed in 2018

[New Glenn](51585988.md) is a [heavy-lift](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy-lift_launch_vehicle) [launch vehicle](1306157.md), first successfully launched in January 2025.[^cite_note-\:7-14] The initial launch date had been set back by numerous delays. Named after NASA astronaut [John Glenn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Glenn), design work on the vehicle began in early 2012. Illustrations of the vehicle, and the high-level specifications, were initially publicly unveiled in September 2016. The full vehicle was first unveiled on a launch pad on February 21, 2024.[^cite_note-30] The [rocket](26301.md) has a diameter of 7 meters (23 ft), and its first stage is powered by seven [BE-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-4) engines. The fairing is claimed to have twice the payload volume of "any commercial launch system" and to be the biggest [payload fairing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payload_fairing) in the world.[^cite_note-31]

Like the [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard), New Glenn's first stage is also designed to be [reusable](336014.md). In 2021, the company initiated conceptual design work on approaches to potentially make the second stage reusable as well, with the project codenamed "[Project Jarvis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Jarvis)".[^cite_note-32]

In 2024, Blue Origin received funding from the [USSF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force) to assess New Glenn's ability to launch national security payloads.[^cite_note-33] On January 16, 2025, Blue Origin achieved its first successful launch of New Glenn from [Cape Canaveral Space Force Station](306778.md)'s [Launch Complex 36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Launch_Complex_36).[^cite_note-34] The mission's primary objective was to deploy the Blue Ring Pathfinder test satellite, which was successfully placed into orbit.[^cite_note-35]

NASA announced in February 2023 that it had selected the New Glenn [heavy-lift launch vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy-lift_launch_vehicle) for the launch of two [Escape and Plasma Acceleration and Dynamics Explorers (ESCAPADE) spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/EscaPADE). The New Glenn heavy-lift launch vehicle launched ESCAPADE[^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] on November 13, 2025 with the ESCAPADE [spacecraft](37910.md) entering [Mars](14640471.md)'s orbit approximately one year after launch, and the first stage booster returning successfully.[^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39]

Since 2016, Blue Origin has referred to a [super heavy-lift launch vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Super_heavy-lift_launch_vehicle) successor to New Glenn named **New Armstrong**, although nothing is publicly known about its design.[^cite_note-40][^cite_note-p129-41][^cite_note-42]

## Blue Moon

Main article: [Blue Moon (spacecraft)](54252087.md)

In May 2019, Bezos unveiled plans for a crew-capable [lunar lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_lander) named [Blue Moon](54252087.md).[^cite_note-cnbc20190509-43] The lander is designed to transport up to 3,600 kilograms (7,900 lb) to the lunar surface in its standard configuration, while a stretched-tank variant can carry up to 6,500 kilograms (14,300 lb). Both versions are engineered for soft landings on the Moon.

In 2020, Blue Origin, in collaboration with [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin), [Northrop Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman), and [Draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draper_Laboratory), submitted the Blue Moon lunar lander proposal for NASA's Artemis program. After NASA awarded the human landing system (HLS) contract to SpaceX in 2021 for its [Starship HLS](64020556.md), Blue Origin [contested the decision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_Federation,_LLC_v._United_States). This challenge ultimately led to NASA awarding a separate contract for Blue Moon in 2023.

The lander is powered by the [BE-7](#BE-7) [hydrolox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrolox) engine.[^cite_note-sfn20190509-44] On May 19, 2023, NASA contracted Blue Origin to develop, test, and deploy the Blue Moon landing system for the Artemis V mission. This mission will support lunar exploration and lay the groundwork for future crewed missions to Mars. The \$3.4 billion contract includes an uncrewed test mission followed by a crewed Moon landing planned for 2029.[^cite_note-\:6-45][^cite_note-cnbc.com-46]

In mid-2024, Blue Origin announced the successful completion of initial acceptance testing on the thrusters for the MK1 variant of the Blue Moon lander.[^cite_note-47]

## Rocket engines

### BE-1

Blue Origin's first engine is a "simple, single-propellant engine" called the Blue Engine-1 (**BE-1**) which uses [peroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-test_peroxide) propellant and generates 8.9 kN (2,000 lbf) of [thrust](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust).[^cite_note-blue20161025-48]

### BE-2

The Blue Engine-2 (**BE-2**) which is a bipropellant engine using [kerosene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RP-1) and peroxide, produces 140 kN (31,000 lbf) of thrust.[^cite_note-blue20161025-48]

### BE-3 (BE-3U and BE-3PM)

Main article: [BE-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-3)

The [BE-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-3) is a family of rocket engines made by Blue Origin with two variants, the BE-3U and BE-3PM. The rocket engine is a [liquid hydrogen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_hydrogen)/[liquid oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_oxygen) (LH2/LOX) [cryogenic engine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cryogenic_rocket_engine) that can produce 490 kN (110,000 lbf) and 710 kN (160,000 lbf) of thrust, respectively. Early thrust chamber testing began at [NASA](18426568.md) Stennis[^cite_note-nsj20130117-49] in 2013.[^cite_note-pa20131203vid-50] By late 2013, the [BE-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-3) had been successfully tested on a full-duration [sub-orbital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sub-orbital) burn, with simulated coast phases and engine relights, "demonstrating deep throttle, full power, long-duration and [reliable restart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_engine_restart) all in a single-test sequence."[^cite_note-pa20131203-51] NASA has released a video of the test.[^cite_note-pa20131203vid-50] As of December 2013, the engine had demonstrated more than 160 starts and 9,100 seconds (2.5 h) of operation at the company's test facility near [Van Horn, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Horn,_Texas).[^cite_note-pa20131203-51][^cite_note-52]

1. The [BE-3U](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-3U) is an open [expander cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expander_cycle) variant of the BE-3. Two of these engines are used to power the [New Glenn](51585988.md) heavy-lift launch vehicle's second stage. The amount of thrust the BE-3U produces is 710 kilonewtons (160,000 lbf).

   [^cite_note-\:5-53]

2. The [BE-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-3)PM uses a [pump-fed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pump-fed_engine) engine design, with a [combustion tap-off cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combustion_tap-off_cycle) to take a small amount of combustion gases from the main [combustion chamber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combustion_chamber) to power the engine's [turbopumps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbopump). One engine is used to power the Propulsive Module (PM) of [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard). The amount of thrust the [BE-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-3)PM produces is 490 kilonewtons (110,000 lbf).

   [^cite_note-\:5-53]

   &#x20;The rocket engine can be throttled to as low as 110 kN (25,000 lbf) for use in controlled vertical landings.

### BE-4

Main article: [BE-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-4)

The BE-4 is a [liquid oxygen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liquid_oxygen)/liquified natural gas (LOX/LNG) rocket engine that can produce 2,400 kN (550,000 lbf) of thrust.[^cite_note-54]

In late 2014, the company signed an agreement with [United Launch Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Launch_Alliance) (ULA) to develop the BE-4 engine, for ULA's [upgraded](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_V#Replacement_with_Vulcan) [Atlas V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_V) and [Vulcan Centaur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vulcan_Centaur) rockets replacing the [RD-180](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RD-180) Russian-made rocket engine. The newly developed heavy-lift launch vehicle will use two of the 2,400 kN (550,000 lbf) BE-4 engines on each [first stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_stage_\(rocketry\)). The engine development program for the BE-4 began in 2011.[^cite_note-wp201409-55]

On October 31, 2022, a Twitter post by the official Blue Origin account announced that the first two BE-4 engines had been delivered to ULA and were being integrated on a Vulcan rocket. In a later tweet, ULA CEO [Tory Bruno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tory_Bruno) said that one of the engines had already been installed on the booster, and that the other would be joining it momentarily.[^cite_note-56] On June 7, 2023, the two BE-4 rocket engines performed as expected when ULA performed a Flight Readiness Firing of the Vulcan Rocket at launch pad 41 at the [Cape Canaveral Space Force Station](306778.md) in [Cape Canaveral, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral,_Florida).[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]

Vulcan Centaur launched for the first time on January 8, 2024, successfully carrying [Astrobotic Technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrobotic_Technology)'s [*Peregrine* lunar lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peregrine_Mission_One), the first mission on NASA's [Commercial Lunar Payload Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Lunar_Payload_Services) (CLPS) program using the BE-4 engine.[^cite_note-spacenews-launch-59]

### BE-7

Main article: [BE-7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BE-7)

The BE-7 engine is a liquid oxygen/liquid hydrogen [dual expander cycle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Expander_cycle#Dual_expander) engine currently under development, designed for use on [Blue Moon](54252087.md).[^cite_note-60] The engine produces 44 kN (10,000 lbf) of thrust. Its first ignition tests were performed in June 2019, with thrust chamber assembly testing continuing through 2023.[^cite_note-61]

### Pusher escape motor

The company partnered with [Aerojet Rocketdyne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerojet_Rocketdyne) to [develop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_product_development) a pusher [launch escape system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Launch_escape_system) for the [New Shepard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Shepard) suborbital crew capsule. Aerojet Rocketdyne provides the Crew Capsule Escape [Solid Rocket Motor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solid-propellant_rocket) (CCE SRM) while the [thrust vector control](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thrust_vectoring) system that steers the capsule during an abort is designed and manufactured by Blue Origin.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63]

### Launch Site One



Entrance to Launch Site One

[Corn Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corn_Ranch), commonly referred to as Launch Site One (LSO) is the company's launch site 30 miles (48 km) north of [Van Horn, Texas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Van_Horn,_Texas).[^cite_note-69]

The site hosts the company's sub-orbital launch pad for its New Shepard rocket along with a landing pad. Additionally, LSO has a number of [rocket engine test stands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_engine_test_facility) and engine test cells are to support the [hydrolox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydrolox), [methalox](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Methalox) and [storable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Storable_propellant) propellant engines. There are three test cells for the BE-3 and BE-4 engines. The test cells support full-thrust and full-duration burns, and one supports short-duration, high-pressure [preburner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Preburner) tests.

### Blue Engine

Engine production is located in [Huntsville, Alabama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huntsville,_Alabama), at a 600,000-square-foot (56,000 m<sup>2</sup>) facility called, "Blue Engine". The company's Web site states, "The world-class engine manufacturing facility in The Rocket City conduct\[s] high rate production of the BE-4 and BE-3U engines.

The company is planning a third major expansion in Huntsville and the company purchased 14.83 acres (6 ha) adjacent to its already sprawling campus at the price of \$1.427 million.[^cite_note-70]

### Orbital Launch Site (OLS)

The Orbital Launch Site (OLS) at the [Cape Canaveral Space Force Station](306778.md), develops rockets and conducts extensive testing. The company converted [Launch Complex 36](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceport_Florida_Launch_Complex_36) (LC-36) to launch [New Glenn](51585988.md) into orbit[^cite_note-S-BG-2016-71] at [Cape Canaveral Space Force Station](306778.md). The facility was initially completed in 2020 and is being used for the construction of New Glenn prototypes, rocket testing, and designs.[^cite_note-72]

The company facility is situated on 306 acres (124 ha) of land assembled from the former Launch complexes [11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral_Launch_Complex_11), [12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral_Launch_Complex_12), 36A and 36B. The land parcel used to build a rocket engine test stand for the BE-4 engine, a launch mount, called the Orbital Launch Site, (hence its name) and a reusable [booster](373603.md) [refurbishment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refurbishment_\(electronics\)) facility for the New Glenn launch vehicle, which is expected to land on a [drone ship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_spaceport_drone_ship) and return to [Port Canaveral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_Canaveral) for refurbishment. Manufacturing of "large elements, such as New Glenn's first and second stages as well as the [payload fairings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Payload_fairing) and other large components will be made nearby in [Exploration Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exploration_Park), which is near the entrance to the [Kennedy Space Center Visitor Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center_Visitor_Complex) on [Merritt Island, Florida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merritt_Island,_Florida).[^cite_note-73]

In addition to their Florida operations, they have also been leased the greenfield of [Space Launch Complex 9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandenberg_Space_Launch_Complex_9) (SLC-9) at [Vandenberg Space Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vandenberg_Space_Force_Base), where they plan to construct a New Glenn launch pad to give the launch vehicle [polar orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polar_orbit) and [Sun-synchronous orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun-synchronous_orbit) capabilities.[^cite_note-74]

## Other projects

### Blue Ring

Main article: [Blue Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ring)

The Blue Ring vehicle was announced in October 2023 by Blue Origin. It will have its own engine and is meant to handle orbital logistics and delivery. In March 2024, in partnership with the [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force), it was announced that the Blue Ring's capabilities will be tested soon on a mission called DarkSky-1.[^cite_note-75]

### Orbital Reef (commercial space station)

Main article: [Orbital Reef](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orbital_Reef)

The company and its partners [Sierra Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sierra_Space), [Boeing](18933266.md), [Redwire Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Redwire_Space) and Genesis Engineering Solutions won a \$130 million award to jump-start the design of their Orbital Reef commercial space station. The project is envisioned as an expandable business park, with Boeing's [Starliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Starliner) and Sierra Space's [Dream Chaser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Chaser) transporting passengers to and from [low Earth orbit](47568.md) (LEO) for [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism), research and in-space manufacturing projects.[^cite_note-76]

Orbital Reef's design will be modular in nature, to provide the greatest amount of customization and compatibility. It will reportedly be designed to accept docking from almost every spacecraft in operation, such as [SpaceX Dragon 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SpaceX_Dragon_2), [Soyuz (spacecraft)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_\(spacecraft\)), [Dream Chaser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dream_Chaser), and [Boeing Starliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_Starliner). The initial modules will be: Life, Node, Core, and Research Modules.[^cite_note-77]

In 2024 NASA increased funding for Orbital Reef by \$42 million, bringing the total award to \$172 million.[^cite_note-78]

### Nuclear rocket program

Main article: [Demonstration Rocket for Agile Cislunar Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonstration_Rocket_for_Agile_Cislunar_Operations)

[NASA](18426568.md) plans to test [spacecraft](37910.md), engines and other propellent systems powered by [nuclear fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fission) no later than 2027 as part of the agency's effort to demonstrate more efficient methods of traveling through outer space for [space exploration](28431.md).[^cite_note-79] One benefit to using [nuclear fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fission) as a propellent for spacecraft is that nuclear-based systems can have less mass than solar cells which means a [spacecraft](37910.md) could be smaller while using the same amount of energy more efficiently. [Nuclear fission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_fission) concepts that can power both life support and propulsion systems could greatly reduce the cost and flight time during space exploration.[^cite_note-80]

The [Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA) awarded [General Atomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Atomics), [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin) and Blue Origin contracts to fund and build [nuclear](22153.md) spacecraft under the agency's [Demonstration Rocket for Agile Cislunar Operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Demonstration_Rocket_for_Agile_Cislunar_Operations) program or DRACO program. The company was awarded \$2.9 million to develop [spacecraft](37910.md) component designs.[^cite_note-81]

In partnership with Blue Origin, Ultra Safe Nuclear Corporation, [GE Vernova Hitachi Nuclear Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Vernova_Hitachi_Nuclear_Energy), [GE Research](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GE_Research), [Framatome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Framatome) and [Materion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Materion), USNC-Tech won a \$5 million contract from [NASA](18426568.md) and the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Department_of_Energy) (DOE) to develop a long range nuclear propulsion system called the Power Adjusted Demonstration Mars Engine, or PADME.[^cite_note-82]

### Space technology

NASA awarded \$35 million to the company in 2023 for the company's work on [lunar regolith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_regolith) to be used for solar powered systems on the moon.[^cite_note-83] The company's website states that "Blue Alchemist is a proposed end-to-end, scalable, autonomous, and commercial solution that produces solar cells from lunar regolith, which is the dust and crushed rock abundant on the surface of the Moon. Based on a process called molten regolith electrolysis, the breakthrough would bootstrap unlimited electricity and power transmission cables anywhere on the surface of the Moon. This process also produces oxygen as a useful byproduct for propulsion and life support."

Gary Lai, chief architect of the New Shepard rocket said during the pathfinder awards at the Seattle Museum of Flight that \[The company] "aims to be the first company that harvests natural resources from the Moon to use here on Earth". He also mentioned that the company is building a novel approach to extract outer space's vast resources.

### TeraWave

The company announced a communications satellite system called TeraWave in January, 2026. The system would involve a constellation of over 5,000 space vehicles in [low Earth orbit](47568.md) (LEO) and 128 [free-space optical communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free-space_optical_communication) satellites in [medium Earth orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medium_Earth_orbit) (MEO) with multiple [terabit per second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terabit_per_second) interlink, providing 144 [gigabit per second](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gigabit_per_second) data rates on the ground via radio frequency link from LEO.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

### Project Sunrise

In March 2026, Blue Origin filed a request to deploy a constellation of 51,600 satellites to form an [orbital AI data center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space-based_data_center) system.[^cite_note-86]

## NASA partnerships and funding

The company has contracted to do work for [NASA](18426568.md) on several development efforts. The company was awarded \$3.7 million in funding by NASA in 2009 via a [Space Act Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Act_Agreement)[^cite_note-nasa20131205a-122][^cite_note-nasa20131205b-123] under the [first](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CCDev1) [Commercial Crew Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commercial_Crew_Development) (CCDev) program for development of concepts and technologies to support future human [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight) operations.[^cite_note-124][^cite_note-125] NASA co-funded risk-mitigation activities related to ground testing of (1) an innovative 'pusher' escape system, that lowers cost by being reusable and enhances safety by avoiding the jettison event of a traditional 'tractor' Launch Escape System, and (2) an innovative composite pressure vessel cabin that both reduces weight and increases safety of astronauts.[^cite_note-nasa20131205a-122] This was later revealed to be a part of a larger system, designed for a bionic capsule, that would be launched atop an [Atlas V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlas_V) rocket.[^cite_note-126] On November 8, 2010, it was announced that the company had completed all milestones under its CCDev Space Act Agreement.[^cite_note-127]

In April 2011, The company received a commitment from NASA for \$22 million of funding under the [CCDev phase 2 program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CCDev2).[^cite_note-aw20110422-128] Milestones included (1) performing a Mission Concept Review (MCR) and System Requirements Review (SRR) on the orbital Space Vehicle, which utilizes a bionic shape to optimize its launch profile and atmospheric reentry, (2) further maturing the pusher escape system, including ground and flight tests, and (3) accelerating development of its BE-3 LOX/LH2 440 [kN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newton_\(unit\)) (100,000 [lbf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pound_\(force\))) engine through full-scale thrust chamber testing.[^cite_note-129]

In 2012, NASA's Commercial Crew Program released its follow-on CCiCap solicitation for the development of crew delivery to [ISS](15043.md) by 2017. The company did not submit a proposal for CCiCap, but reportedly continued work on its development program with private funding.[^cite_note-130] The company had a failed attempt to lease a different part of the [Space Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Coast), when they submitted a bid in 2013 to lease [Launch Complex 39A](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kennedy_Space_Center_Launch_Complex_39) (LC39A) at the [Kennedy Space Center](16421.md) – on land to the north of, and adjacent to, [Cape Canaveral AFS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cape_Canaveral_Air_Force_Station) – following NASA's decision to lease the unused complex out as part of a bid to reduce annual operation and maintenance costs. The companies bid was for shared and non-exclusive use of the LC39A complex such that the launchpad was to have been able to interface with multiple [vehicles](1306157.md), and costs for using the launch pad were to have been shared across multiple companies over the term of the lease. One potential shared user in the companies proposed plan was [United Launch Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Launch_Alliance) (ULA). [Commercial use](752732.md) of the LC39A launch complex was awarded to [SpaceX](832774.md), which submitted a bid for exclusive use of the launch complex to support their [crewed missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_spaceflight).[^cite_note-os20130818-131]

The company completed work for NASA on several small development contracts, receiving total funding of \$25.7 million by 2013.[^cite_note-nasa20131205a-122][^cite_note-aw20110422-128] In September 2013 – before completion of the bid period, and before any public announcement by NASA of the results of the process – [Florida Today](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Florida_Today) reported that the company had filed a protest with the [U.S.](195149.md) [General Accounting Office (GAO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Office) "over what it says is a plan by NASA to award an exclusive commercial lease to [SpaceX](832774.md) for use of mothballed space shuttle launch pad 39A".[^cite_note-pa20130910-132] NASA had originally planned to complete the bid award and have the pad transferred by October 1, 2013, but the protest delayed a decision until the [U.S. General Accounting Office (GAO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Office) reached a decision on the protest.[^cite_note-pa20130910-132][^cite_note-pa20131212-133] [SpaceX](832774.md) said that they would be willing to support a multi-user arrangement for pad 39A.[^cite_note-sp20130921-134] In December 2013, the [U.S.](195149.md) [General Accounting Office (GAO)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_Accountability_Office) denied the companies protest and sided with NASA, which argued that the solicitation contained no preference on the use of the facility as either multi-use or single-use. "The \[solicitation] document merely \[asked] bidders to explain their reasons for selecting one approach instead of the other and how they would manage the facility".[^cite_note-pa20131212-133] NASA selected the [SpaceX](832774.md) proposal in late 2013 and signed a 20-year lease contract for Launch Pad 39A to [SpaceX](832774.md) in April 2014.[^cite_note-ft20140415-135]

The company placed their first bid via the NASA Sustaining Lunar Development (SLD) competition to fund and develop a lunar lander capable of transporting [astronauts](664.md) to and from the lunar surface. The Blue Origin led team called the "National Team" included, [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin), [Northrop Grumman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northrop_Grumman), and [Draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draper_Laboratory). On April 30, 2020, the company and its partners won a \$579 million contract to start developing and testing an integrated Human Landing System (HLS) for the [Artemis program](60758751.md) to return humans to the [Moon](19331.md).[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137] However, the Blue Origin led team lost their first bid to work for NASA's Artemis program[^cite_note-138] and on April 16, 2021, NASA officially selected the [Space Exploration Technologies Corp. (SpaceX)](832774.md) to develop, test and build their version of the Human Landing System (HLS) for [Artemis](60758751.md) missions 2 (II), 3 (III) and 4 (IV).[^cite_note-139]

In early 2021, the company received over \$275 million from NASA for [lunar lander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunar_lander) projects and [sub-orbital](455295.md) research flights.[^cite_note-140]

The company then announced on December 6, 2022, that it had submitted a second bid via the NASA Sustaining Lunar Development (SLD) competition to fund and develop a second lunar lander capable for transporting [astronauts](664.md) to and from the lunar surface. The announcement fell within NASA's deadline for Sustaining Lunar Development (SLD) proposals. As with their first bid, the company is leading another team called the "National Team" which includes [Draper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draper_Laboratory), [Boeing](18933266.md), [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin), [Astrobotic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astrobotic_Technology), [Honeybee Robotics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Honeybee_Robotics) and Blue Origin.[^cite_note-141]

On May 19, 2023, NASA contracted the company to develop, test and deploy its Blue Moon landing system for the agency's [Artemis V](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artemis_V) mission, which explores the [Moon](19331.md) and prepares future crewed missions to [Mars](14640471.md). The project includes an uncrewed test mission followed by a crewed [Moon](19331.md) landing in 2029. The contract value is \$3.4 billion.[^cite_note-\:6-45][^cite_note-cnbc.com-46]

On August 12, 2025, Blue Origin announced a proposed Mars Telecommunications Orbiter (MTO) mission that would fulfill NASA's new requirements of a [Mars Telecommunications Network](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mars_Telecommunications_Network) (MTN) that can be dually-used for both a Mars Sample Return mission and future crewed Mars missions.[^cite_note-142] It would be based on Blue Origin's [Blue Ring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Ring) Satellite Platform supplemented by a yet unclarified small number of deployable [UHF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ultra_high_frequency) relay satellites that would enable it to provide wide-area coverage. It is envisioned by Blue Origin to be operational by 2028.[^cite_note-143]

## Internal and additional U.S government funding

By July 2014, [Jeff Bezos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jeff_Bezos) had invested over \$500 million into the company [^cite_note-sn20140718-144] and the vast majority of further funding into 2016 was to support [technology development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_product_development) and operations where a majority of funding came from Jeff Bezos' [private investment fund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Private_investment_fund). In April 2017, an annual amount was published showing that Jeff Bezos was selling approximately \$1 billion in [Amazon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amazon.com) stock per year to [invest](191280.md) in the company.[^cite_note-nyt20170405-145] Jeff Bezos has been criticized for spending excessive amounts of his fortune on [spaceflight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceflight).[^cite_note-146]

The company received \$181 million from the United States Air Force for launch vehicle development in 2019. The company was also eligible to benefit from further grants totaling \$500M as part of the U.S. Space Force [Launch Services Agreement competition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Space_Launch#Next_generation_vehicle_competition).[^cite_note-sn20190408-147] On November 18, 2022, the [U.S. Space Systems Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Systems_Command) announced that an agreement with the company that "paves the way" for the company's [New Glenn](51585988.md) [rocket](26301.md) to compete for [national security](240468.md) launch contracts once it completes its required flight certifications for [Top Secret](252857.md) [military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military) payloads.

In an interview with Bob Smith by the financial Times in 2023, Smith said that the company had "hundreds of millions in revenue as well as billions of dollars in orders".[^cite_note-148]

The company is part of the [DARPA Lunar Programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DARPA_lunar_programs), specifically Luna10, an architecture study for lunar surface operations.[^cite_note-149]

## Early test vehicles

### Charon



*Charon* on display at the [Museum of Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Flight) in Seattle, Washington.

The company's first flight test vehicle, called *[Charon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charon_\(moon\))* after [Pluto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pluto)'s moon,[^cite_note-150] was powered by four vertically mounted [Rolls-Royce Viper Mk. 301](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armstrong_Siddeley_Viper) jet engines rather than rockets. The low-altitude vehicle was developed to test autonomous guidance and control technologies, and the processes that the company would use to develop its later rockets.[^cite_note-151] *Charon* made its only test flight at Moses Lake, Washington on March 5, 2005.[^cite_note-152] It flew to an altitude of 96 m (316 ft) before returning for a controlled landing near the liftoff point.[^cite_note-Blue_Origin_Charon_Test_Vehicle-153][^cite_note-154] As of 2016, Charon is on display at the [Museum of Flight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Flight) in Seattle, Washington.[^cite_note-155]

### Goddard

The next test vehicle, named *[Goddard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_Goddard)* (also known as PM1), first flew on November 13, 2006. The flight was successful. A test flight for December 2 never launched.[^cite_note-156][^cite_note-157] According to [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) records, two further flights were performed by Goddard.[^cite_note-158] Blue Engine 1, or BE-1, was the first rocket engine developed by the company and was used in the company's [Goddard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blue_Origin_Goddard) development vehicle.

### PM2

Another early suborbital test vehicle, PM2, had two flight tests in 2011 in west Texas. The vehicle designation may be short for "Propulsion Module".[^cite_note-nsj20110902-159] The first flight was a short hop (low altitude, VTVL takeoff and landing mission) flown on May 6, 2011. The second flight, August 24, 2011, failed when ground personnel lost contact and control of the vehicle. The company released its analysis of the failure nine days later. As the vehicle reached a speed of [Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mach_number) 1.2 and 14 km (46,000 ft) altitude, a "flight instability drove an [angle of attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_of_attack) that triggered \[the] [range safety system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Range_Safety_and_Telemetry_System) to terminate thrust on the vehicle". The vehicle was lost.[^cite_note-PM2-160] Blue Engine 2, or BE-2, was a [pump-fed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turbopump) bipropellant engine burning [kerosene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RP-1) and [peroxide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-test_peroxide) which produced 140 kN (31,000 lbf) of thrust.[^cite_note-BlueEngines-161][^cite_note-BlueEngineChart-162] Five BE-2 engines powered the company's PM-2 development vehicle on two test flights in 2011.[^cite_note-163]
