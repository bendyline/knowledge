The **Taipei Economic and Cultural Representative Office** (**TECRO**), also known as **Taipei Economic and Cultural Office** (**TECO**), **Taipei Representative Office** (**TRO**) or **Taipei Mission**, is an alternative [diplomatic institution](8970.md) serving as a [*de facto* embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/De_facto_embassy) or a [consulate](6770059.md) of the Republic of China (ROC, commonly referred to as [Taiwan](25734.md)) to exercise the foreign affairs and consular services in specific countries which have established formal diplomatic relations with the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) (PRC, commonly referred to as China). The PRC denies the legitimacy of the ROC as a [sovereign state](23604120.md) and claims the [ROC-controlled territories](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_area_of_the_Republic_of_China) as an integral part of its territory. An [exclusive mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclusive_mandate), namely [One-China principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_China_principle), requires that any country wishing to establish a diplomatic relationship with the PRC must first sever any formal relationship with the ROC. According to *[The Fletcher Forum of World Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fletcher_Forum_of_World_Affairs)*, "non-recognition of the Taiwanese government is a prerequisite for conducting formal diplomatic relations with the PRC—in effect forcing other governments to choose between Beijing and Taipei."[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] As a result, these countries only allow the ROC to establish representative offices instead of a fully-fledged embassy or consulate for the purpose of conducting practical bilateral relations without granting full [diplomatic recognition](524626.md).

Except in [Lithuania](17675.md)[^cite_note-3] and [Somaliland](51425.md) (opened in 2020), all offices use the capital city "[Taipei](57648.md)" and refrain from using the name "Taiwan" or the "Republic of China", since the term "Taipei" avoids implying that Taiwan is a different country on par with the PRC or that there are "[Two Chinas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Chinas)", the PRC and the ROC, in order to diminish the obstacles of building pragmatic diplomacy and sidestep the [political status of Taiwan](66555.md). The first attempt of using the name "Taiwan" in a Taiwanese diplomatic mission within a non-diplomatic state was when Lithuania broke the tradition with the name [Taiwanese Representative Office in Lithuania](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwanese_Representative_Office_in_Lithuania) in 2021. In response the PRC expelled Lithuania's embassy staff from Beijing and downgraded diplomatic relations to [charge d'affaires](856252.md) status.[^cite_note-4] Attempts by other countries, including [Fiji](10707.md), to allow ROC government to change the representative office to indicate "Taiwan" later failed and were reversed under pressure from PRC government.[^cite_note-5]

On the other side, offices located in Taiwan usually also use the term "Taipei", though the United States, Japan, and Somaliland (but not Lithuania) use "Taiwan" (for example the [American Institute in Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_in_Taiwan)).

TECROs state that their aim is "to promote bilateral trade, investment, culture, science and technology exchanges and cooperation, as well as better understanding", and provide common consular services towards [overseas Taiwanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Overseas_Taiwanese), such as issuing visas and [passports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_passport).

TECROs in the [United States](3434750.md) enjoy many diplomatic privileges such as [extraterritoriality](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Extraterritoriality), providing [consular protection](9587132.md) and their staff have [diplomatic immunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Diplomatic_immunity).[^cite_note-6] Other countries also establish reciprocal representative offices in Taiwan, such as the [American Institute in Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_in_Taiwan), [Canadian Trade Office in Taipei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian_Trade_Office_in_Taipei) and [Japan–Taiwan Exchange Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japan%E2%80%93Taiwan_Exchange_Association).

## History

See also: [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War)

Following the admission of the PRC to the [United Nations](31769.md) in 1971, many countries began to establish diplomatic relations with the government in [Beijing](18603746.md), and as a consequence, ended diplomatic relations with the [Nanjing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanjing)-based ROC Government stationed in [Taipei](57648.md).[^cite_note-Taiwan_Review-7] In order to maintain trade and cultural ties with countries with which it no longer had diplomatic relations, Taiwan established representative offices in these countries, often replacing its former embassies.

Before the 1990s, the names of these offices would vary considerably from country to country, usually omitting any reference to "Taiwan" or "Republic of China", instead referring to "East Asia", "Far East" or "Free China".[^cite_note-8] They would also describe themselves as "centres" or "offices", concerned with trade, tourism, culture or information, thereby emphasising their private and unofficial status, despite being staffed by [Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Taiwan\)) personnel.

For example, in Japan, the former ROC Embassy was replaced by the "[Association of East Asian Relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_of_East_Asian_Relations)" (AEAR) in 1972.[^cite_note-Mosher-9] In Malaysia, following the closure of the consulate general in [Kuala Lumpur](16854.md) in 1974, an office known as the Far East Travel and Trade Centre was established.[^cite_note-Jie_Chen-10] In the [Philippines](23440.md), the former embassy in [Manila](184334.md) was replaced by the "Pacific Economic and Cultural Center", established in 1975.[^cite_note-Mosher-9] In [Thailand](30128.md), the former embassy in [Bangkok](56615.md) was replaced by the "Office of the Representative of China Airlines" in 1975.[^cite_note-Samuel_C_Y_Ku-11] This was later renamed the Far East Trade Office in 1980.[^cite_note-Jie_Chen-10]

In the United States, Taipei's mission, established in 1979, was known as the "Coordination Council for North American Affairs" (CCNAA).[^cite_note-12] As of 2019, it has been renamed "Taiwan Council for US Affairs."[^cite_note-13]

In the United Kingdom, Taiwan was represented by the "Free Chinese Centre", established in 1963.[^cite_note-books.google.co.uk-14] In [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany), it was represented by a *Büro der Fernost-Informationen* ("Far East Information Office") established in 1972.[^cite_note-Vertretung-15] In [Spain](26667.md), the office, established in 1973, was known as the *Centro Sun Yat-sen* ("[Sun Yat-sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen) Centre").[^cite_note-hibou-16] In the [Netherlands](21148.md), the office was known as the "Far East Trade Office".[^cite_note-hibou-16]

However, in the late 1980s, these offices began using the name "Taipei" in their titles due to confusion on their functions, particular those needing visa to travel to ROC areas. Using the "Taipei" is more familiar as it was recently used in the Olympics after "Chinese Taipei" Olympics team rejoined in 1981. In May 1992, the AEAR offices in Japan became Taipei Economic and Cultural Representative Offices.[^cite_note-17] The "Free Chinese Centre" in London was similarly renamed the "Taipei Representative Office".[^cite_note-18] In September 1994, the [Clinton Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Administration) announced that the CCNAA office in Washington could similarly be called the Taipei Economic and Cultural Representative Office.[^cite_note-Chi_Su-19]



The building hosting TECO branch office in [Sydney](27862.md), [Australia](4689264.md)

Earlier in 1989, the "Pacific Economic and Cultural Center" in [Manila](184334.md) became the "[Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in the Philippines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Economic_and_Cultural_Office_in_the_Philippines)".[^cite_note-Enduring_Dynamics-20] In 1991, the "Taiwan Marketing Service" office in [Canberra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canberra), Australia, established in 1988, also became a "Taipei Economic and Cultural Office", along with the "Far East Trading Company" offices in Sydney and [Melbourne](17306237.md).[^cite_note-21]

Other names are still used elsewhere; for example, the mission in Moscow is formally known as the "Representative Office in Moscow for the Taipei-Moscow Economic and Cultural Coordination Commission",[^cite_note-22] the mission in New Delhi is known as the "Taipei Economic and Cultural Center".[^cite_note-23] The mission in [Pretoria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pretoria) is known as the "Taipei Liaison Office".[^cite_note-24]

The two most recent ones to change their official names, in Papua New Guinea and in Jordan, both use the name *Taipei Economic and Cultural Office* (Chinese: 臺北經濟文化辦事處).

Prior to 1979, the Republic of China (with [government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Republic_of_China) on [Taiwan](25734.md) since 1949) was represented in [Washington, D.C.](108956.md) by its embassy, occupying the building now used by the [Haitian embassy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Haiti,_Washington,_D.C.).[^cite_note-25] After the transfer of recognition to the People's Republic of [China](5405.md), the [government of the Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Republic_of_China) was no longer recognized by the United States, and therefore no longer entitled to use the former embassy, with its diplomatic mission replaced by a non-diplomatic-titled representative office. The mission serves as the office of the [Coordination Council for North American Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Council_for_U.S._Affairs) (CCNAA) in Washington, D.C., established in March 1979 as the counterpart to the [American Institute in Taiwan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Institute_in_Taiwan), after the United States established diplomatic relations with the People's Republic of [China](5405.md).[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] The council was renamed [Taiwan Council for U.S. Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Council_for_U.S._Affairs) in 2019.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29]



The ROC embassy in the US from 1944 to 1978, on [Embassy Row](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_Row) (now the [Embassy of Haiti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Embassy_of_Haiti,_Washington,_D.C.))

In 1994, as a result of the [Clinton Administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clinton_Administration)'s Taiwan Policy Review, the name of the CCNAA office in Washington, D.C. (which functioned as an [embassy](8970.md)) was changed to *Taipei Economic and Cultural Representative Office* (TECRO).[^cite_note-30] Similarly, the names of the twelve other CCNAA offices (which functioned as [consulates](6770059.md)) were changed to *Taipei Economic and Cultural Office* (TECO).[^cite_note-31]

In September 2020, the [US Ambassador to the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/US_Ambassador_to_the_United_Nations) [Kelly Craft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kelly_Craft) met with Amb. James K.J. Lee, Director-General of the Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in New York, who was secretary-general in [Taiwan's Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Taiwan\)) until July, for lunch in New York City in what was the first meeting between a top Taiwan official and a United States ambassador to the United Nations.[^cite_note-Taipei_Economic_and_Cultural_Representative_Office,_Washington,_D.C._auto-32] Craft said she and Lee discussed ways the US can help Taiwan become more engaged within the U.N.[^cite_note-Taipei_Economic_and_Cultural_Representative_Office,_Washington,_D.C._auto-32]

## TECRO in Japan

Main article: [Taipei Economic and Cultural Representative Office in Japan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Economic_and_Cultural_Representative_Office_in_Japan)



TECRO in [Japan](15573.md)

Diplomatic relations between the Republic of China and Japan were broken off in September 1972. For practical reasons, the Association of East Asian Relations (AEAR), was established two months after the Japan-China Joint Communique was signed. EARA had offices in Taipei, Tokyo, Osaka, and Fukuoka.[^cite_note-33] In 1992, Japan authorized the change in name of AEAR to TECRO.[^cite_note-34]

## Representations in the PRC special administrative regions

### Hong Kong

Main article: [Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in Hong Kong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Economic_and_Cultural_Office_in_Hong_Kong)

In Hong Kong, from 1966, Taiwan was represented by the 'Chung Hwa Travel Service', a name chosen to avoid upsetting Beijing.[^cite_note-35] On 20 July 2011, as a result of warming relations between Taiwan and Beijing, the name was formally changed to the Taipei Economic and Cultural Office, bringing it into line with other Republic of China representative offices around the world.[^cite_note-36]

### Macau

Main article: [Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in Macau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Economic_and_Cultural_Office_in_Macau)

In Macau, from 1989 to 1999, Taiwan was represented by the 'Taipei Trade and Tourism Office', Taiwan's first-time representation in Macau after Kuomintang's expulsion from Macau as the consequence of the [December 3rd Incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12-3_incident) in 1966. From 1999 to 2011, Taiwan was represented by the 'Taipei Economic and Cultural Center'. On 13 May 2012, the name was formally changed to the Taipei Economic and Cultural Office.[^cite_note-37]

The **Taipei Representative Office in Norway** ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language): 駐挪威代表處; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Zhù Nuówēi Dàibiǎo Chù*) was established in 1980.[^cite_note-hibou2-38]

In July 2017, the [ROC Ministry of Foreign Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Taiwan\)) announced that the office will be suspended on 30 September 2017 and affairs related to Taiwanese in the country will be handled by [Taipei Mission in Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Mission_in_Sweden). The decision was made to improve the efficiency of the [foreign diplomatic missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_relations_of_Taiwan) of Taiwan.[^cite_note-39]

## Former representative offices

-  [Bangladesh](3454.md) — Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in Bangladesh (closed in 2009, transferred to [Taipei Economic and Cultural Center in India](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Economic_and_Cultural_Center_in_India) and [in Thailand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Economic_and_Cultural_Office_in_Thailand))

-  [Belarus](3457.md) — de facto embassy in [Minsk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minsk) (closed in 2006, transferred to [Representative Office in Moscow for the Taipei-Moscow Economic and Cultural Coordination Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Representative_Office_in_Moscow_for_the_Taipei-Moscow_Economic_and_Cultural_Coordination_Commission))

-  [Bolivia](3462.md) — Taipei Economic Office in [La Paz](42821.md) (closed in 2009, transferred to [Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in Peru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Economic_and_Cultural_Office_in_Peru))

-  [Cambodia](334751.md) — de facto embassy in [Phnom Penh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phnom_Penh) (closed in 1997, transferred later to [Taipei Economic and Cultural Office, Ho Chi Minh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Economic_and_Cultural_Office,_Ho_Chi_Minh_City))

-  [Libya](17633.md) — [Taiwan Commercial Office in Tripoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiwan_Commercial_Office_in_Tripoli) (closed in 2011, transferred to [Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in Jordan](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%A7%90%E7%B4%84%E6%97%A6%E8%87%BA%E5%8C%97%E7%B6%93%E6%BF%9F%E6%96%87%E5%8C%96%E8%BE%A6%E4%BA%8B%E8%99%95))

-  [Norway](21241.md) — [Taipei Representative Office in Norway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Representative_Office_in_Norway) (closed in 2017, transferred to [Taipei Mission in Sweden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Mission_in_Sweden))

-  [Papua New Guinea](22943.md) — [Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in Papua New Guinea](https://zh.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E9%A7%90%E5%B7%B4%E5%B8%83%E4%BA%9E%E7%B4%90%E5%B9%BE%E5%85%A7%E4%BA%9E%E8%87%BA%E5%8C%97%E7%B6%93%E6%BF%9F%E6%96%87%E5%8C%96%E8%BE%A6%E4%BA%8B%E8%99%95) (closed in 2026)

-  [Uruguay](31843.md) — Taipei Economic Office in [Montevideo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montevideo) (closed in 2002 temporary, transferred to [Taipei Economic and Cultural Office in Argentina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Economic_and_Cultural_Office_in_Argentina))

-  [Venezuela](32374.md) — Taipei Economic Office in [Caracas](55289.md) (closed in 2009, transferred to [Taipei Commercial Office in Bogotá, Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taipei_Commercial_Office_in_Bogot%C3%A1,_Colombia))
