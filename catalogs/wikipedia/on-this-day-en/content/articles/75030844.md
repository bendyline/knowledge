The **Israeli war cabinet** was formed on 11 October 2023, five days after the beginning of the [Gaza war](74998519.md).[^cite_note-1] The opposition party [National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Unity_\(Israel\)) joined the [thirty-seventh government](72545811.md) led by [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) as [prime minister](24898.md). Negotiations began upon the outbreak of the war.[^cite_note-2]

On 13 June 2024, former [Chiefs of the General Staff](3011266.md) [Benny Gantz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benny_Gantz) and [Gadi Eisenkot](5249926.md) exited the war cabinet,[^cite_note-3] leaving Netanyahu, [Minister of Defense](3226446.md) [Yoav Gallant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoav_Gallant), [Ron Dermer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Dermer) and [Aryeh Deri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aryeh_Deri) as its only members.[^cite_note-4] The cabinet was subsequently dissolved on 17 June.[^cite_note-tjp17june-5]

## Background

Main article: [Gaza war](74998519.md)

See also: [Israeli government response to the October 7 attacks](75523996.md)

An armed conflict between [Israel](9282173.md) and [Hamas](13913.md)-led [Palestinian militant groups](2646043.md)[^cite_note-6] has been taking place since 7 October 2023. Part of the broader [Gaza–Israel conflict](16346266.md) and following an uptick of violence in the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md), the war began with a [militant incursion into Israel](74998317.md) from the [Gaza Strip](12047.md),[^cite_note-\:122-7][^cite_note-8] while the responding Israeli [counteroffensive](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Counterattack) was named "Operation Swords of Iron" by the [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) (IDF).[^cite_note-9]

Hostilities were initiated early in the morning with [a rocket barrage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestinian_rocket_attacks_on_Israel) of at least 3,000 missiles against Israel and vehicle-transported incursions into its territory.[^cite_note-scmp8oct22-10] Palestinian militants broke through the [Gaza–Israel barrier](478337.md) and forced their way through Gaza border crossings, attacking nearby Israeli communities and [military installations](843566.md). At least 1,200 Israelis were killed, including [a massacre at a music festival](75007485.md) where at least 260 civilians were killed. Israeli soldiers and civilians, including children and elderly, were taken hostage to the Gaza Strip.[^cite_note-guardian7oct22-11] Hamas abducted at least 199 people, taking hostage both Israelis and persons of several other nationalities.[^cite_note-12]

The war represents a tipping point in the Israeli–Palestinian conflict and the Gaza–Israel conflict, which followed a violent year that saw increased expansion of [Israeli settlements](15123.md) and clashes in [Jenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/July_2023_Jenin_incursion), [Al-Aqsa mosque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Al-Aqsa_clashes), [and Gaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/May_2023_Gaza%E2%80%93Israel_clashes), which killed almost 250 Palestinians and 36 Israelis;[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-apn122-16] Hamas cited these events as justification for the attack and called on Palestinians to join the fight to "expel the occupiers and demolish the walls".[^cite_note-TOI122-17][^cite_note-auto13222-18][^cite_note-19] In response, Israeli Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu declared states of emergency and war, vowing a "mighty vengeance for this dark day".[^cite_note-20]

## Negotiations toward emergency unity government

Among opposition parties, [Yesh Atid](35515608.md) leader and former prime minister [Yair Lapid](2448072.md), National Unity chairman Benny Gantz, [Yisrael Beiteinu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yisrael_Beiteinu) party leader [Avigdor Lieberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avigdor_Lieberman) and [Labor Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Labor_Party) leader [Merav Michaeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merav_Michaeli) issued a joint statement expressing full backing for the IDF and unity with the government, saying: "In times like these, there is no opposition and coalition in Israel."[^cite_note-unity-22-21][^cite_note-unity-12-22]

Netanyahu proposed that Yesh Atid and National Unity enter an emergency unity government with his [Likud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Likud)-led coalition,[^cite_note-unity-32-23] after Lapid urged Netanyahu put "aside our differences and form an emergency, narrow, professional government". Lapid said that Israel could not effectively manage the war with "the extreme and dysfunctional composition of [the current cabinet](72545811.md)" and called upon Netanyahu to eject the far-right [Religious Zionist Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Religious_Zionist_Party) and [Otzma Yehudit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otzma_Yehudit) parties as a condition for Yesh Atid to join an emergency unity government.[^cite_note-Keller-Lynn2-24]

The National Unity party met with Likud on 9 October to discuss a possible unity government, with National Unity likely to join such an arrangement.[^cite_note-25] Likud said the emergency unity government would be similar to the one formed before the [Six-Day War](29328.md) in 1967. [Levi Eshkol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Levi_Eshkol) and then-opposition leader [Menachem Begin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Menachem_Begin) joined hands for the duration of the War in the [thirteenth government of Israel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thirteenth_government_of_Israel).[^cite_note-26]

The National Unity party agreed to join the government on 11 October.[^cite_note-27] Lapid's Yesh Atid party ultimately did not join the war cabinet,[^cite_note-KraussShurafa-28] with Lapid citing three issues: the inclusion of officials who failed to prevent the Hamas onslaught that precipitated the war, the continued inclusion of "extremists" in the government (a reference to National Security Minister [Itamar Ben Gvir](54504115.md) and Finance Minister [Bezalel Smotrich](46196834.md), who are both on the far-right and sit on the [Security Cabinet of Israel](22256400.md)), and the simultaneous existence of a War Cabinet and Security Cabinet that Lapid predicted would be unworkable and insufficient. Lapid said he would support the war effort from outside the government.[^cite_note-toi12oct-29]

## Approval by the Knesset and scope of agreement

The formation of the war cabinet was approved by the Knesset on 12 October.[^cite_note-toi12oct-29] The composition of the preexisting government was modified: MKs voted, 66–4, to approve the addition of five National Unity ministers (Gantz, [Gadi Eisenkot](5249926.md), [Gideon Sa'ar](485357.md), [Hili Tropper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hili_Tropper), and [Yifat Shasha-Biton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yifat_Shasha-Biton)) to the government as [ministers without portfolio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_without_portfolio), and unanimously voted to remove the health portfolio from Interior Minister [Moshe Arbel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moshe_Arbel) and elevate [Uriel Buso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uriel_Buso) of the [Shas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shas) party to the post of health minister.[^cite_note-toi12oct-29]

As part of the deal, Netanyahu and Gantz also agreed to freeze all new non-war, non-emergency legislation, including the highly controversial [judicial overhaul legislation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Israeli_judicial_reform), and agreed that the war cabinet would meet at least once every 48 hours.[^cite_note-toi12oct-29] The war cabinet had the authority to "update, as necessary, military and strategic aims for the conflict" but its decisions were subject to approval from the Security Cabinet of Israel.[^cite_note-30]

On 16 October, Netanyahu's Likud party announced that [Yisrael Beiteinu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yisrael_Beiteinu), led by [Avigdor Lieberman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avigdor_Lieberman), had agreed to join the emergency government. However, later the same day, Lieberman denied reaching an agreement with the government, saying that the offer to join the Security Cabinet was insufficient. Lieberman said that he wanted a seat on the smaller war cabinet instead. He said his party would "continue to support the government's actions that are meant to eliminate Hamas and Hamas leaders" but that he had "no intention of being the 38th minister in the government and be used as a [fig leaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fig_leaf)."[^cite_note-LiebermanDenies-31]

## Members of the war cabinet



US secretary of state [Antony Blinken](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antony_Blinken) with Israeli prime minister [Benjamin Netanyahu](56472.md) and the Israeli war cabinet in Tel Aviv, Israel, 22 March 2024

In the original configuration, there were three members and two observers[^cite_note-toi11octng-32][^cite_note-toi25march-33] with only the members having voting rights,[^cite_note-30Apr-34] though the size of the war cabinet was reduced to four members in June 2024,[^cite_note-35] until its dissolution later that month.

Dermer and Deri are considered to be "confidants" of Netanyahu.[^cite_note-30Apr-34]

## Departures and dissolution

Sa'ar announced in March that he was leaving the National Unity alliance and called to be appointed to the war cabinet, to which Gantz expressed opposition.[^cite_note-38] In addition, though Netanyahu was "open" to Sa'ar's appointment, Ben-Gvir demanded that he also be appointed. On 25 March 2024, Sa'ar quit the coalition.[^cite_note-toi25march-33]

On 9 June 2024, Gantz and Eisenkot resigned from the cabinet because Netanyahu did not present a post-war plan for Gaza by the previous day, meeting a previously announced deadline.[^cite_note-39] On 17 June 2024, the dissolution of the war cabinet was announced, which, according to observers, was done in order to quash the demands of Ben-Gvir and Finance Minister [Bezalel Smotrich](46196834.md) that they be given seats in the cabinet.[^cite_note-tjp17june-5]
