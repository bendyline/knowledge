The **AGM-114 Hellfire** is an American [missile](19594.md) developed for [anti-armor](37517.md) use,[^cite_note-militaryDotCom-6] later developed for [precision](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Precision-guided_munition)[^cite_note-AGM114Hellfire-7] [drone strikes](38212231.md) against other target types, especially [high-value targets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-value_target).[^cite_note-8] It was originally developed under the name "[Heliborne](8286923.md) laser, [fire-and-forget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-and-forget) missile", which led to the colloquial name "Hellfire" ultimately becoming the missile's formal name.[^cite_note-9] It has a multi-mission, multi-target precision-strike ability and can be launched from multiple air, sea, and ground platforms, including the [MQ-1 Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Atomics_MQ-1_Predator) and [MQ-9 Reaper](4769988.md). The Hellfire missile is the primary 100-pound (45 kg) class air-to-ground precision weapon for the armed forces of the United States and many other countries. It has also been fielded on surface platforms in the surface-to-surface and surface-to-air roles.[^cite_note-Breaking_Defense-10]

## Description

Most variants are [laser-guided](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_guidance), with one variant, the AGM-114L "Longbow Hellfire", being radar-guided.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] Laser guidance can be provided either from the launcher, such as the nose-mounted opto-electronics of the [AH-64 Apache](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AH-64_Apache) attack helicopter, other airborne target designators or from ground-based observers,[^cite_note-WeaponSystems-13] the latter two options allowing the launcher to break line of sight with the target and seek cover.[^cite_note-14]

Cockpit video showing a Hellfire missile being fired at two people in Afghanistan (at 1\:42)

The development of the Hellfire Missile System began in 1974 with the [United States Army](32087.md) requirement for a "[tank-buster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_warfare)", launched from [helicopters](8286923.md) to defeat [armored fighting vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armored_fighting_vehicle).[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16]

The Hellfire II, developed in the early 1990s is a modular missile system with several variants, and entered service with the U.S. Army in 1996.[^cite_note-17] Hellfire II's semi-active laser variants—AGM-114K [high-explosive anti-tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-explosive_anti-tank) (HEAT), AGM-114KII with external blast fragmentation sleeve, AGM-114M (blast fragmentation), and AGM-114N [metal augmented charge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermobaric_weapon) (MAC)—achieve pinpoint accuracy by homing in on a reflected laser beam aimed at the target. The [General Atomics MQ-1 Predator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Atomics_MQ-1_Predator) and [MQ-9 Reaper](4769988.md) [unmanned combat aerial vehicles](337755.md) (UCAVs) carry the Hellfire II, but the most common platform is the helicopter gunship, such as the [AH-64 Apache](37746.md) or [AH-1 Cobra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_AH-1_Cobra), which can carry up to 16 of them each. The AGM-114L, or Longbow Hellfire, is a [fire-and-forget](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fire-and-forget) weapon: equipped with millimeter-wave (MMW) [active radar homing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Active_radar_homing), it requires no further guidance after launch—even being able to lock on to its target after launch[^cite_note-18]—and can hit its target without the launcher or other friendly unit being in line of sight of the target. It also works in adverse weather and battlefield obscurants, such as smoke and fog, which can mask the position of a target or prevent a designating laser from forming a detectable reflection. Each Hellfire weighs 104 pounds (47 kg), including the 20-pound (9 kg) warhead, and has a range of 4.4–6.8 miles (7.1–11 km) depending on trajectory.[^cite_note-19] The Hellfire has a [Circular Error Probable](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circular_Error_Probable) (CEP) of less than 3 feet (0.91 m).[^cite_note-20]

The AGM-114R "Romeo" Hellfire II entered service in late 2012. It uses a semi-active laser homing guidance system and a K-charge multipurpose warhead[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-22] to engage targets that formerly needed multiple Hellfire variants. It will replace AGM-114K, M, N, and P variants in U.S. service.[^cite_note-23]

In October 2012, the U.S. ordered 24,000 Hellfire II missiles, for both the U.S. armed forces and foreign customers.[^cite_note-24]

A possible new JCM successor called the [Joint Air to Ground Missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Air_to_Ground_Missile) (JAGM) is under consideration. Due to budget reductions, JAGM development was separated into increments, with increment 1 focusing on adding a millimeter-wave radar to the Hellfire-R to give it a dual-mode seeker, enabling it to track moving targets in bad weather.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

## Operational history

See also: [Drone strikes in Pakistan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_strikes_in_Pakistan) and [Drone strikes in Yemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Drone_strikes_in_Yemen)



[M1A1 Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1A1_Abrams) main battle tank destroyed by [friendly fire](202484.md) in 1991 Gulf War; one Abrams is thought to have been accidentally set on fire by a Hellfire missile fired from an [Apache helicopter](37746.md).

[^cite_note-27]

In 2009, the [British Ministry of Defence](203793.md) (MoD) acknowledged that [Army Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Air_Corps_\(United_Kingdom\)) (AAC) [AgustaWestland Apaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AgustaWestland_Apache) had used AGM-114N Hellfire missiles against [Taliban](30635.md) forces [in Afghanistan](19666611.md). The MoD stated that 20 missiles were used in 2008 and a further 20 in 2009. In the [British Parliament](13964.md), [Liberal Democrat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liberal_Democrats_\(UK\)) politician [Nick Harvey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nick_Harvey) argued that the "Parliament must be reassured these are a weapon of last resort."[^cite_note-28]

AGM-114 Hellfire missiles were used to kill [Hamas](13913.md) leader [Ahmed Yassin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Yassin) by the [Israeli Air Force](177619.md) (IAF) in 2004,[^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30] and by the [US military](32212.md) to kill American-born Islamic cleric [Anwar al-Awlaki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anwar_al-Awlaki) in [Yemen](350939.md) in 2011,[^cite_note-31] [Al-Qaeda](1921.md) operative [Abu Yahya al-Libi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abu_Yahya_al-Libi) in [Pakistan](23235.md) in 2012, [al-Shabaab](8726463.md) militant [Mukhtar Abu Zubair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Abdi_Godane) in [Somalia](27358.md) in 2014,[^cite_note-godane-32] and British [ISIL](9087364.md) executioner [Mohammed Emwazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jihadi_John) (also known as "Jihadi John") in [Syria](7515849.md) in 2015.[^cite_note-33] They were also used in the [assassination of Qasem Soleimani](62726668.md) as well as the [killing of Ayman al-Zawahiri](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Killing_of_Ayman_al-Zawahiri).[^cite_note-34]

The AGM-114 has occasionally been used as an [air-to-air missile](320568.md). The first operational air-to-air kill with a Hellfire took place on 24 May 2001, after a civilian [Cessna 152](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cessna_152) aircraft entered Israeli airspace from Lebanon, with unknown intentions and refusing to answer or comply with [ATC](48563.md) repeated warnings to turn back. An Israeli Air Force AH-64A Apache helicopter fired on the Cessna, resulting in its complete disintegration.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36]

In January 2016, *[The Wall Street Journal](173070.md)* reported that one training missile without a warhead was accidentally shipped to Cuba in 2014 after a training mission in Europe;[^cite_note-37] it was later returned.[^cite_note-38] A US official said that this was an inert "dummy" version of the Lockheed system stripped of its warhead, fuse, guidance equipment and motor, known as a "Captive Air Training Missile".[^cite_note-39][^cite_note-40]

During the [Russo-Ukrainian War](70149799.md), Ukraine used a V2X Tempest vehicle equipped with a Hellfire missile launcher to conduct counter-uncrewed aerial systems missions.[^cite_note-V2X-41]

AGM-114 Hellfire missiles were used by the US in combat in the [2026 Iran war](82533582.md).[^cite_note-42] The US Navy fired Hellfire missiles at an unladen oil tanker in the Persian Gulf as it approached the port at Kharg Island. They fired Hellfire missiles at the smokestacks, disabling the oil tanker in an effort to enforce a blockade against Iranian ports.[^cite_note-43]

### Experimental platforms

IFPC Longbow vs [MQM-170 Outlaw](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffon_Aerospace_MQM-170_Outlaw) 25 March 2016

The system has been tested for use on the [Humvee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humvee) and the [Improved TOW Vehicle (ITV)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M901_ITV). Test shots have also been fired from a [C-130 Hercules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/C-130_Hercules). Sweden and Norway use the Hellfire for coastal defense and have conducted tests with Hellfire launchers mounted on the [Combat Boat 90](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_Boat_90) coastal assault boat.[^cite_note-80]

The US Navy evaluated the missile for use on the *Freedom*-class littoral combat ship and *Independence*-class littoral combat ship from 2014.[^cite_note-81] The missile was successfully fired from a LCS in early 2017.[^cite_note-82] This system was set to deploy by late 2019.[^cite_note-83]

In 2016 the Longbow Hellfire was tested by the US Army using a 15-tube [Multi-Mission Launcher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-Mission_Launcher) mounted on a [Family of Medium Tactical Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Family_of_Medium_Tactical_Vehicles) (FMTV) truck. The MML is an Army-developed weapon system capable of deploying both surface-to-surface and [surface-to-air missiles](182664.md).[^cite_note-84]

The Longbow Hellfire initially equipped the Maneuver Short-Range Air Defense (M-SHORAD) version of the [Stryker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stryker) to serve as a surface-to-air counter-drone missile,[^cite_note-Breaking_Defense-10] but in 2024 the U.S. Army prohibited its use on the platform after discovering that prolonged placement of the missiles on a ground vehicle created wear and tear that lead to potential safety concerns.[^cite_note-85]
