**Sport-Club Freiburg e.V.**, commonly known as **SC Freiburg** (German pronunciation: [\[ʔɛs ˈtseː ˈfʁaɪbʊɐ̯k\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German)), is a [German professional football club](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Football_in_Germany), based in the city of [Freiburg im Breisgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg_im_Breisgau), Baden-Württemberg. It plays in the [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga), having been promoted as champions from the [2. Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2._Bundesliga) in 2016.&#x20;

Between 1954 and 2021, Freiburg's stadium was the [Dreisamstadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreisamstadion); the club moved to the newly built [Europa-Park Stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europa-Park_Stadion) in 2021.

## History

### Early history

The club traces its origins to a pair of clubs founded in 1904: Freiburger Fußballverein 04 was organised in March of that year; FC Schwalbe Freiburg just two months later.[^cite_note-geschichte1904-1912-2] Both clubs underwent name changes, with Schwalbe becoming FC Mars in 1905, Mars becoming Union Freiburg in 1906, and FV 04 Freiburg becoming Sportverein Freiburg 04 in 1909.[^cite_note-geschichte1904-1912-2] Three years later, SV and Union formed Sportclub Freiburg, at the same time incorporating the [griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Griffin) head.[^cite_note-geschichte1904-1912-2]

In 1918, after [World War I](4764461.md), SC Freiburg entered a temporary arrangement with Freiburger FC to be able to field a full side called KSG Freiburg. The next year, SC Freiburg associated themselves with [FT 1844 Freiburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburger_Turnerschaft_von_1844) as that club's football department,[^cite_note-3] until 1928 when they left to enter into a stadium-sharing arrangement with PSV (Polizeisportverein) Freiburg 1924 that lasted until 1930 and the failure of PSV. SC Freiburg then started again with FT 1844 Freiburg in 1938. The club played first in the *[Bezirksliga Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bezirksliga_Baden)* in 1928, then in the *[Gauliga Baden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauliga_Baden)*, from which they were relegated in 1934.

At the end of [World War II](32927.md), [Allied occupation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied-occupied_Germany) authorities disbanded most existing organizations in Germany, including football and sports clubs. The clubs reconstituted themselves after about a year, but were required to take on new names in an attempt to disassociate them from Nazis. SC Freiburg was therefore briefly known as VfL Freiburg.[^cite_note-Geschicte1946-1963-4] By 1950, French-occupation authorities allowed the clubs to reclaim their old identities.[^cite_note-Geschicte1946-1963-4] Finally, in 1952, SC Freiburg left FT Freiburg behind again.[^cite_note-Geschicte1946-1963-4]



Historical chart of Freiburg league performance

Through the 1930s, SC Freiburg played in the Bezirkliga (II), in the Gauliga Baden (I), winning local titles. After World War II, they resumed playing in the [Amateurliga Südbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amateurliga_S%C3%BCdbaden) (III).

### The Finke era with ten Bundesliga seasons (1991–2007)

SC Freiburg were promoted to the [2. Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2._Bundesliga) in [1978–79](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1978%E2%80%9379_2._Bundesliga), which they would compete in for a decade-and-a-half before making the breakthrough to the top-flight [Bundesliga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bundesliga) in [1993–94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1993%E2%80%9394_Bundesliga) under the management of [Volker Finke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volker_Finke).[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-geschichte1990-2007-6] In their first Bundesliga season, Freiburg narrowly avoided relegation.[^cite_note-geschichte1990-2007-6] They made a third-place finish in their [second season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1994%E2%80%9395_Bundesliga) at the top level, just three points behind champions [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund). It was at this time that they were first nicknamed *Breisgau-Brasilianer* (literally *Breisgau-Brazilians*), due to their attractive style of play.[^cite_note-geschichte1990-2007-6]

The club reached the [UEFA Cup](232175.md) in [1995](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1995%E2%80%9396_UEFA_Cup), where they were knocked out in the first round by [Slavia Prague](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slavia_Prague).[^cite_note-geschichte1990-2007-6][^cite_note-7] In [2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_UEFA_Cup) they reached the UEFA Cup for a second time, where they were knocked out by [Feyenoord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feyenoord).[^cite_note-geschichte1990-2007-6][^cite_note-8]

Freiburg's first Bundesliga relegation was in 1997 after they finished in 17th position.[^cite_note-geschichte1990-2007-6] While they have been relegated four times since first making the Bundesliga, they have thrice won immediate promotion back to the top league. It was the first time since 1992 that Freiburg played in the 2. Bundesliga for two consecutive seasons.

Freiburg finished the [2006–07 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006%E2%80%9307_2._Bundesliga) in fourth place in the 2. Bundesliga, missing out on the third automatic-promotion spot on goal difference to [MSV Duisburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MSV_Duisburg), although they won 12 of their last 16 league games. They were knocked out of the [DFB-Pokal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DFB-Pokal) in the second round by [VfL Wolfsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfL_Wolfsburg) on 24 October 2006.[^cite_note-geschichte1990-2007-6]

On 20 May 2007, [Volker Finke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volker_Finke) resigned as the club's coach after 16 years in the job.[^cite_note-geschichte1990-2007-6] He was succeeded by [Robin Dutt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robin_Dutt), who himself left the club for [Bayer Leverkusen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayer_04_Leverkusen) in 2011.[^cite_note-bleacherreport.com_2011-07-26-9]

On 10 May 2009, Freiburg secured promotion into the Bundesliga once again, beating [TuS Koblenz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TuS_Koblenz) in an away game 5–2.[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-bleacherreport.com_2011-07-26-9]

### Streich era



SC Freiburg against [Borussia Dortmund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borussia_Dortmund) in 2012

In the [2011–12 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2011%E2%80%9312_Bundesliga), a coaching change by appointing [Christian Streich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christian_Streich), with the club finishing 12th. Under Christian Streich, the [2012–13 Bundesliga season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2012%E2%80%9313_Bundesliga) saw the club finish in fifth place, their best league standing since 1994–95. The fifth-place finish secured a position in the [2013–14 UEFA Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013%E2%80%9314_UEFA_Europa_League). Had Freiburg defeated [Schalke 04](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Schalke_04) on the final matchday of the season, Freiburg would have advanced further in the league table against Schalke and qualified for the [UEFA Champions League](44220.md) for the first time in club history. The 1–2 defeat to Schalke, however, saw Schalke secure fourth place in the league and qualify for the tournament instead.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-12] During the 2012–13 season, Freiburg also advanced to the semi-finals of the [DFB-Pokal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DFB-Pokal) for the first time in the club's history, but lost to local rivals [VfB Stuttgart](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfB_Stuttgart) 1–2, and missed the chance to play [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) in the [final](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2013_DFB-Pokal_Final).[^cite_note-13]

In the [2014–15 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2014%E2%80%9315_Bundesliga), after six years in the top flight, Freiburg was relegated to the 2. Bundesliga by a single point after a final-day defeat at [Hannover 96](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hannover_96). This was despite beating [Bayern Munich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FC_Bayern_Munich) in the second-last game. In the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2015%E2%80%9316_2._Bundesliga), however, the club earned its fifth promotion to the Bundesliga, with two matches to spare. The first season back in the Bundesliga saw them end seventh. This saw Freiburg qualify for the [Europa League](232175.md), as German cupwinners Borussia Dortmund were already qualified for the Champions League. The side were eliminated in the third qualification round against [NK Domžale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NK_Dom%C5%BEale) from Slovenia. Freiburg stayed in the top flight, finishing 15th.

In the [2021–22 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021%E2%80%9322_Bundesliga) Freiburg reached the final of the DFB-Pokal for the first time.[^cite_note-2022pokal-14] In the league Freiburg finished sixth to qualify to the next season's [Europa League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_UEFA_Europa_League), where they reached the round of 16.[^cite_note-15] In the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_Bundesliga), they finished fifth in the league to achieve another direct qualification to the [Europa League group stage](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023%E2%80%9324_UEFA_Europa_League#Group_stage), despite being in the Champions League spots most of the season; however, two consecutive losses against rivals [RB Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RB_Leipzig) and [Union Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1._FC_Union_Berlin) had them drop down in the league table with two games remaining. In the [DFB-Pokal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2022%E2%80%9323_DFB-Pokal) of the same season, defeated Bayern Munich 2–1 in the quarter-finals, in an away match for the first time in their history,[^cite_note-16] before losing in the semi-finals at home 1–5 to RB Leipzig.[^cite_note-17]

### Post-Streich years

In March 2024, it was announced that Streich's assistant coach, [Julian Schuster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Julian_Schuster), would take over as head coach of the club.[^cite_note-18] During the [2024–25 season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024%E2%80%9325_Bundesliga), Freiburg reached the Champions League spots, climbing to fourth place after a crucial away win against [Wolfsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VfL_Wolfsburg) on matchday 31.[^cite_note-19] However, a 3–1 home defeat to [Eintracht Frankfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eintracht_Frankfurt) on the final matchday saw them drop to fifth place and [Europa League](78224540.md) qualification, narrowly missing out on the Champions League for the fifth time following 2001, 2013, 2022, and 2023.[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] In Schuster's second season, the club reached the [Europa League final](76987231.md), the first European final in the club's history.[^cite_note-22] In the final in [Istanbul](3391396.md), they however lost 3-0 to English [Aston Villa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aston_Villa).[^cite_note-23]

## Reserve team

Main article: [SC Freiburg II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Freiburg_II)

The club's reserve team, formerly the **SC Freiburg Amateure**, now **[SC Freiburg II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SC_Freiburg_II)**, has, for the most part of its history played in the lower amateur leagues. It made a three-season appearance in the tier four [Verbandsliga Südbaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verbandsliga_S%C3%BCdbaden) from 1983 to 1986, but then took until 1994 to return to this league. In 1998 the team won promotion to the [Oberliga Baden-Württemberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oberliga_Baden-W%C3%BCrttemberg) after a league championship in the Verbandsliga. Freiburg II spent the next ten seasons at this level as an upper table side before another league championship took the team to the [Regionalliga Süd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regionalliga_S%C3%BCd_\(1994%E2%80%932012\)). After four seasons at this league the team became part of the new [Regionalliga Südwest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fu%C3%9Fball-Regionalliga_S%C3%BCdwest) in 2012. After a seventh place in its first season in the league the team finished runner-up in 2013–14.

A [South Baden Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Baden_Cup) win in 2001 qualified it for the first round of the [2001–02 DFB-Pokal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001%E2%80%9302_DFB-Pokal), the German Cup, where it lost to Schalke 04.

## Stadium



Dreisamstadion interior in 2011

SC Freiburg formerly played its home games at the [Dreisamstadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreisamstadion), named after the [Dreisam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dreisam) River which flows through Freiburg. Because of sponsorship agreements, the stadium was known as the Schwarzwald-Stadion. The stadium has an approximate capacity of 24,000 spectators, and was built in 1953. Forty years later, then manager [Volker Finke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volker_Finke) began an initiative to transform the Dreisamstadion into Germany's first [solar powered](13690575.md) football stadium. There are solar modules on the north, south, and main tribunes. These panels generate 250,000 [kWh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KWh) of energy per year.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25]

The new [Europa-Park Stadion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Europa-Park_Stadion)[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27] designed by [HPP Architekten](https://www.hpp.com/en/), was completed in October 2021. Located in the west of the city in a part of the city called Brühl — immediately to the west of [Freiburg Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freiburg_Airport) — it has a capacity of 34,700.[^cite_note-28][^cite_note-29][^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31][^cite_note-32]

## Sponsorship

In April 2022, the team announced their sponsorship with car retailer [Cazoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cazoo) starting in July 2022. The Cazoo brand is visible on the front of the new jerseys as the team's main sponsor. In addition to the Bundesliga professionals, Cazoo appeared as shirt sponsor and advertising partner of the second team of SC Freiburg in the third division and as co-sponsor of the Freiburg Football School, and became visible at all matches of the SC junior teams. Cazoo also became a co-sponsor and sleeve sponsor of SC Freiburg's Bundesliga women.
