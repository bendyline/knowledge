[Iga Świątek](51838558.md) defeated [Amanda Anisimova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amanda_Anisimova) in the final, 6–0, 6–0 to win the ladies' singles tennis title at the [2025 Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Wimbledon_Championships).[^cite_note-1] It was her first [Wimbledon](91195.md) title and sixth [major](197638.md#Tournaments) title overall. Świątek became the first Pole to win a Wimbledon singles title.[^cite_note-2] This was only the third major final, and the second in the [Open Era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_Era), to end in a "[double bagel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bagel_\(tennis\)#Double_bagel)" scoreline (after the [1911 Wimbledon Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1911_Wimbledon_Championships_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles) and [1988 French Open](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1988_French_Open_%E2%80%93_Women's_singles)).[^cite_note-3][^cite_note-4] Świątek was the eighth woman in the Open Era to win major singles titles on all three surfaces – clay, grass and hardcourt.[^cite_note-5] She was the fifth player overall, and fourth in the Open Era (after [Ann Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ann_Jones_\(tennis\)), [Martina Hingis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Martina_Hingis), [Amélie Mauresmo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Am%C3%A9lie_Mauresmo) and [Ashleigh Barty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ashleigh_Barty)) to win the women's singles title after previously winning the girls' singles title.[^cite_note-6][^cite_note-7] By reaching the final, Anisimova made her debut in the top ten of the [WTA rankings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/WTA_rankings).[^cite_note-8]

[Barbora Krejčíková](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbora_Krej%C4%8D%C3%ADkov%C3%A1) was the defending champion,[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] but lost in the third round to [Emma Navarro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Navarro).[^cite_note-11] The losses of [Elena Rybakina](55541443.md) and Krejčíková in the third round guaranteed a first-time Wimbledon champion for the eighth consecutive edition. For the first time in the Open Era, none of the quarterfinalists had previously reached the Wimbledon final.[^cite_note-12]

With the losses of second seed [Coco Gauff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coco_Gauff) and third seed [Jessica Pegula](32944701.md) in the first round, this was the first time in the Open Era that two of the top three women's seeds lost in the first round of a major.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14]

[Zeynep Sönmez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeynep_S%C3%B6nmez) became the first Turkish player in the Open Era to reach the third round of a major.[^cite_note-15] [Solana Sierra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solana_Sierra) became the first [lucky loser](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucky_loser#tennis) to reach the women's singles fourth round at Wimbledon in the Open Era, and the seventh woman overall to do so at any major.[^cite_note-16]

This marked the final Wimbledon appearance of former world No. 2 and two-time Wimbledon champion [Petra Kvitová](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petra_Kvitov%C3%A1).[^cite_note-17] She lost in the first round to Navarro.[^cite_note-18]
