The [United States](3434750.md) is a [charter member](31969.md) of the [United Nations](31769.md) and one of [five permanent members](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council) of the [UN Security Council](31956.md).

The United States boasts the [headquarters of the United Nations](188955.md), which includes the usual meeting place of the [General Assembly](31957.md) in [New York City](645042.md), on the northeast coast of the country. The United States is the largest provider of financial contributions to the United Nations, providing 27.89[^cite_note-1] percent of the UN assessed peacekeeping budget of \$6.38 billion for fiscal year 2020 ([China](5405.md) and [Japan](15573.md) contributed 15.2 percent and 8.5 percent, respectively). The assessed peacekeeping budget is separate from voluntary contributions and the assessed regular budget.[^cite_note-2] The assessed regular budget of the UN for fiscal year 2022 in \$3.12 billion.[^cite_note-3] From July 2016 to June 2017, 28.6 percent of the budget used for peacekeeping operations was provided by the United States.[^cite_note-4] The United States had a pivotal role in establishing the UN.

## Role in establishing the UN

Further information: [Foreign policy of the Franklin D. Roosevelt administration § Founding the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign_policy_of_the_Franklin_D._Roosevelt_administration#Founding_the_United_Nations), and [Four Policemen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_Policemen)

The UN is an outgrowth of the [Atlantic Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atlantic_Charter). It appeared in the [Declaration by United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Declaration_by_United_Nations) on January 1, 1942, in which 26 nations pledged to continue fighting the [Axis powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers).

Their main inspiration was the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations); however, their goals were to rectify the League's imperfections[^cite_note-5] in order to create an organization that would be "the primary vehicle for maintaining peace and stability."[^cite_note-Jerry_Pubantz_2006-6] Roosevelt's main role was to convince the different allies, especially [Winston Churchill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winston_Churchill) of the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and [Joseph Stalin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Stalin) of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), to join the new organization.[^cite_note-Jerry_Pubantz_2006-6] The negotiations mainly took place during the [Dumbarton Oaks Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumbarton_Oaks_Conference) and the [Yalta Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yalta_Conference), where the three world leaders tried to reach a consensus concerning the United Nations' structure, purposes and principles.[^cite_note-7] "Roosevelt saw the United Nations as the crowning achievement of his political career."[^cite_note-8] Roosevelt's envoy [Wendell Willkie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wendell_Willkie) played a key role in promoting the idea of the United States joining the new organization, publishing [*One World*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_World_\(Willkie_book\)) in April 1943. In September 1943, 81 percent of Americans—up from 63 percent in February—supported joining a "union of nations" after the war.[^cite_note-9]

In 1945, representatives from 50 countries met in [San Francisco](49728.md) for the [United Nations Conference on International Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Conference_on_International_Organization). They deliberated on proposals that had been drafted by representatives of the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), the [United Kingdom](31717.md) and the [United States](3434750.md) at the [Dumbarton Oaks Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumbarton_Oaks_Conference) between August and October 1944. Roosevelt, Churchill and Stalin reviewed the Dumbarton Oaks proposal during the Yalta Conference in February 1945. The purpose of the conference was to discuss post-war settlements[^cite_note-10] and to reach a final agreement concerning "the UN's structure and membership and set the date of the San Francisco organizing conference."[^cite_note-ReferenceA-11] The world leaders eventually agreed on Roosevelt's proposal to give certain members a veto power[^cite_note-12] so "that the Organization could take no important action without their joint consent."[^cite_note-13] Though the veto power question created a lot of disagreement among the different signatories,[^cite_note-14] its inclusion in the charter was never a matter of negotiation for Roosevelt and his allies.[^cite_note-15] Finally, during the Yalta conference, Stalin agreed to make the USSR a member of the United Nations.[^cite_note-ReferenceA-11]

An important American contribution, prior to the formation of the United Nations, was made at the [Bretton Woods Conference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bretton_Woods_Conference). This conference took place in 1944 and its goal was "to create a new international monetary and trade regime that was stable and predictable."[^cite_note-16] Over subsequent decades, this new system opened world markets and promoted a liberal economy. It was implemented through different institutions, such as the [World Bank](36752.md) and the [International Monetary Fund](15251.md), which went on to work with the United Nations but remained independent from it.[^cite_note-17]

On July 28, 1945, the [United States Senate](24909346.md) (as part of the [79th United States Congress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/79th_United_States_Congress)) ratified the [United Nations Charter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Charter) by a vote of 89–2.[^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19] (By comparison, the Senate had voted 49–35 in favor of joining the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations), short of the required two-thirds majority for ratification.[^cite_note-20]) The [United Nations](31769.md) officially came into existence on October 24, 1945, when all of the [Permanent Five](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Five) (the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)), [France](5843419.md), the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union), the [United Kingdom](31717.md), and the [United States](3434750.md)) as well as a majority of other signatories had ratified the Charter.

The United Nations was the first [international governmental organization](18201224.md) to receive significant support from the United States. Its forerunner, the [League of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/League_of_Nations), had been championed by [Woodrow Wilson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Woodrow_Wilson) after [World War I](4764461.md) to prevent future conflicts. While it was supported by most [nations of Europe](9239.md), it was never ratified by the [United States Congress](31756.md) due to the inability to reach a compromise regarding the [Lodge Reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodge_Reservations) or the [Hitchcock Reservations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitchcock_Reservations?action=edit\&redlink=1).

Shortly after the establishment of the United Nations, the United States came into conflict with another member of the Security Council. Since the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) was a [permanent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council) [member](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union_and_the_United_Nations) of the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md), it had the power to [veto](1900272.md) any binding UN resolution. In fact, Soviet [foreign minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Foreign_Affairs_\(Soviet_Union\)) and UN [ambassador](68651.md) [Vyacheslav Molotov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vyacheslav_Molotov) used veto power twice as often as any other permanent member, earning him the title "Mr. Veto".

Relations between the United States and the Soviet Union (later Russia) within the UN have evolved in step with the larger geopolitical situation between the two powers. While the Soviet Union was boycotting the Security Council and [China's seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_and_the_United_Nations) was represented by U.S.-friendly [Republic of China](25734.md) (instead of the [communist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communism) [People's Republic of China](5405.md) which would replace the ROC in the UN in 1971), the U.S. and UN jointly condemned the [invasion of South Korea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pokpung) by [North Korean troops](21263.md), leading to the UN sanctioned [Korean War](16772.md). Later, the U.S. persuaded all permanent members of the Security Council to authorize force against [Ba'athist Iraq](26215470.md) in the [Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) of 1991. This was a major step toward U.S. and Russian reconciliation in the [post–Cold War era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Post%E2%80%93Cold_War_era).

## Sources of conflict

Since 1991, the [United States](3434750.md) has been the world's dominant military, economic, social, and political power (not to mention hosting the [UN Headquarters](188955.md) itself in [New York City](645042.md)); the [United Nations](31769.md) was not designed for such a unipolar world with a single [superpower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower), and conflicts between an ascendant U.S. and other UN members have increased.

### China question

Main article: [Cross-strait conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross-strait_conflict)

Conflict between the U.S. and the UN predates the collapse of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union). In 1971, the UN adopted [Resolution 2758](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_2758)—which affected the [admission of the People's Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Accession_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China_to_the_United_Nations) and the removal of the [Republic of China](25734.md)—despite objections by the U.S. government (see [China and the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/China_and_the_United_Nations)). The U.S. government changed its own China policy shortly afterward, however, so the conflict between the UN and US foreign policy was short-lived.[^cite_note-21]

### Israeli–Palestinian conflict

Main articles: [Israel–United States military relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israel%E2%80%93United_States_military_relations), [Arab–Israeli conflict](7960202.md), [Israel and the United Nations](479389.md), and [Palestine and the United Nations](13444990.md)

The U.S. government's repeated opposition to Arab military actions has created much more tension between the U.S. government and the United Nations. The [General Assembly](31957.md) [Resolution 3379](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_3379) (determining that [Zionism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zionism) is a form of [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism) and racial discrimination) of 1975 was strongly opposed by U.S. officials. In 1991, the General Assembly adopted [Resolution 4686](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_3379#Revocation), which effectively negated Resolution 3379. Use of its veto power to prevent the Security Council from issuing resolutions condemning [Israel Defense Forces](39237.md) action has frequently divided the U.S. from the Soviet Union, China and France in the Security Council; since 1989, the U.S. government has dissented against Security Council resolutions on 12 occasions out of 17 total instances when a permanent member vetoed. Of these 12 occasions, only two related to issues other than the [Israeli–Palestinian conflict](46216.md). In 2009, the U.S. government abstained from [Security Council Resolution 1860](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1860), which called for a halt to [Israel's military response to Hamas rocket attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaza_War_\(2008%E2%80%932009\)), and the opening of the border crossings into the [Gaza Strip](12047.md).[^cite_note-22]

### Dues

Under the [Reagan administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_Ronald_Reagan), the U.S. withdrew from [UNESCO](21786641.md), and withheld its dues to encourage the UN to repeal [Resolution 3379](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_General_Assembly_Resolution_3379), which it did in 1991. The U.S. was—and continues to be—the member state levied most heavily by the UN, so U.S. policymakers expected this strategy to be an effective way to oppose Soviet and Arab influence over the UN. When the UN repealed Resolution 3379, the U.S. resumed dues payments, but not before the U.S. had accumulated significant and controversial arrears.[^cite_note-23]

### Vetoing second term of the Secretary-General

Main article: [1996 United Nations Secretary-General selection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1996_United_Nations_Secretary-General_selection)

In 1996 [Boutros Boutros-Ghali](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boutros_Boutros-Ghali) ran unopposed for the customary second term as UN Secretary-General. US ambassador [Madeleine Albright](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Madeleine_Albright) denounced him as "disengaged" and "neglect\[ful]" of [genocide in Rwanda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Genocide_in_Rwanda) and demanded Boutros-Ghali resign. He refused and seemingly had the votes. He won 14 of the 15 votes in the Security Council, but the sole opposing vote was a US veto.[^cite_note-24][^cite_note-25] After four deadlocked meetings of the Security Council, France offered a compromise in which Boutros-Ghali would be appointed to a short term of two years, but the United States rejected the French offer. Finally, Boutros-Ghali suspended his candidacy, becoming the second Secretary-General ever to be denied re-election by a veto, with [Kurt Waldheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurt_Waldheim) being the first.[^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

## Public opinion

Polls have repeatedly gauged public opinion in the U.S. regarding the UN. In the 1950s and 1960s more supportive attitudes were found among the college-educated, more prosperous, and the politically orthodox. Distrust was more common among other groups.[^cite_note-28] According to Gregory G. Holyk, "Approval of UN performance dropped to an all-time low between 2003 and 2007, after the contentious debate over the use of force against Iraq. Nonetheless, support for the UN has remained strong because the U.S. public differentiates between criticism of UN performance and support for the general purpose and aims of the UN."[^cite_note-29]

## Arrears&#x20;

The UN has always had problems with [members refusing to pay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_arrears) the assessment levied upon them under the [United Nations Charter](88805.md), but the most significant refusal in recent times has been that of the United States.[^cite_note-30]

After prolonged negotiations, the U.S. and the UN in 1999 negotiated an agreement whereby the United States would pay a large part of the money it owes, and in exchange the UN would reduce the assessment rate ceiling from 25% to 22%. The reduction in the assessment rate ceiling was among the reforms contained in the United Nations Reform Act of 1999 or Helms-Biden Act, which links payment of \$926 million in U.S. arrears to the UN and other international organizations to a series of reform benchmarks.[^cite_note-31]

Of the U.S. arrears to the UN totaling over \$1.3 billion, \$612 million was payable under Helms-Biden. The remaining \$700 million resulted from various legislative and policy withholdings.

Under Helms-Biden, the U.S. paid \$100 million in arrears to the UN in December 1999; release of the next \$582 million awaited a legislative revision to Helms-Biden, necessary because the benchmark requiring a 25 percent peacekeeping assessment rate ceiling was not quite achieved. The U.S. also sought elimination of the legislated 25 percent cap on U.S. peacekeeping payments in effect since 1995, which continued to generate additional UN arrears. Of the final \$244 million under Helms-Biden, \$30 million was payable to the UN and \$214 million to other international organizations.[^cite_note-32]

In February 2001, Congress authorized the second Helms-Biden payment, but because of an eight-month delay in appropriations, the U.N. chose not to re-elect the United States to the [United Nations Commission on Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Commission_on_Human_Rights). Following the [September 11 attacks](5058690.md), on 6 October 2001, Bush signed the second Helms-Biden appropriation into law and in March 2002, the U.S. was voted back onto the commission. In October 2002, Congress lifted the 25% cap on peacekeeping assessments and in November 2002, the U.S. made the final Helms-Biden appropriation.[^cite_note-33]

## Iraq War

Main articles: [United Nations Security Council and the Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_and_the_Iraq_War), [Iraq disarmament crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_disarmament_crisis), and [Iraq War](5043324.md)



George W. Bush addressed the General Assembly on September 12, 2002 on Iraq prior to the passage of Resolution 1441.

Further conflict between the U.S. and some UN members arose in 2002 and 2003 over the issue of Iraq. [George W. Bush](3414021.md) maintained that Iraqi President [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) had not fulfilled the obligations he had entered into at the end of the Gulf War in 1991, namely to rid Iraq of all [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon_of_mass_destruction) (WMDs) and to renounce their further use. A series of inspections by the [IAEA](14984.md) failed to find conclusive evidence that proved allegations that Iraq was continuing to develop or harbour such weapons. The findings were conveyed by the leading weapons inspector, [Hans Blix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hans_Blix), who noted Iraq's failure to cooperate with the inspections on several counts.[^cite_note-34] The U.S. replied by saying that the responsibility of proof of disarmament was upon Iraq, not on the UN or the U.S.

In November 2002, the [United Nations Security Council](31956.md) adopted [Resolution 1441](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Security_Council_Resolution_1441), giving Iraq an ultimatum to co-operate in disarmament within an unstated timeframe of a few months. However, in March 2003, the U.S., supported by fifty countries (including the [United Kingdom](31717.md), [Spain](26667.md), [Australia](4689264.md), the [Czech Republic](5321.md), [Denmark](76972.md), the [Netherlands](21148.md) and [Poland](22936.md)) which the [Bush administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_W._Bush) referred to as the "[coalition of the willing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_of_the_willing)" [launched military operations against Iraq](201936.md). On April 9, Saddam Hussein's regime was overthrown and Iraq was placed under occupation, marked by the [fall of Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baghdad_\(2003\)). The U.S. argued that this action was authorized by Resolution 1441, since Iraq had failed to comply by co-operating fully in the identification and destruction of its weapons programs, and since Resolution 1441 promised 'serious consequences' for lack of full compliance and achievement of its objective.

Other countries, led by [France](5843419.md), [Germany](11867.md) and [Russia](25391.md), maintained that Resolution 1441 did not authorize the use of force without passage of a further Resolution. [French President](24899.md) [Jacques Chirac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacques_Chirac) stated "My position is that, whatever the circumstances, France will vote 'no' because this evening it considers that it is not necessary to make war to achieve the stated goal of the disarmament of Iraq".[^cite_note-35]

Rightly or wrongly the "this evening" qualification was ignored, perhaps because the implications of its English translation are ambiguous. The statement was widely interpreted in the English-speaking world as meaning that France would exercise its right as a [Permanent Member of the Security Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_members_of_the_United_Nations_Security_Council) to veto any resolution at any time ("whatever the circumstances") to use force against Iraq.

Following the overthrow of the former Iraqi government, the [Iraq Survey Group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Survey_Group) led an exhaustive search of Iraq for WMD. Ultimately, while over 500 "degraded" chemical warheads were found,[^cite_note-36] no deployable WMD of any kind were found and all WMD production facilities had been found to be inactive since 1991.

## Reform of the United Nations

Main articles: [Reform of the United Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reform_of_the_United_Nations) and [United Nations Reform Act of 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Reform_Act_of_2005)

The [United States Congress](31756.md) has shown particular concern with reforms related to UN effectiveness and efficiency. In November 2004, the bill H.R. 4818 mandated the creation of a bipartisan Task Force to report to Congress on how to make the UN more effective in realizing the goals of its Charter. The Task Force came into being in January 2005, co-chaired by former [House Speaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speaker_of_the_United_States_House_of_Representatives) [Newt Gingrich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Newt_Gingrich) and former [Senate Majority Leader](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Party_leaders_of_the_United_States_Senate), [George J. Mitchell](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_J._Mitchell). In June 2005, the task force released "American Interests and UN Reform: Report of the Task Force on the United Nations,"[^cite_note-37] with numerous recommendations on how to improve the UN.

On June 17, 2005, the [United States House of Representatives](19468510.md) passed the [United Nations Reform Act of 2005](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Reform_Act_of_2005)[^cite_note-38] to slash funds to the UN in half by 2008 if it does not meet certain criteria. This reflects years of complaints about [anti-American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Americanism) and [anti-Israeli](30074296.md) bias in the UN, particularly the exclusion of Israel from many decision-making organizations. The U.S. is estimated to contribute about 27.89%[^cite_note-39] of the UN's yearly budget due to the UN's ability-to-pay scale, making this bill potentially devastating to the United Nations. The [Bush administration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidency_of_George_W._Bush) and several former U.S. ambassadors to the UN have warned that this may only strengthen anti-American sentiment around the world and serve to hurt current UN reform movements. The bill passed the House in June 2005, and a parallel bill was introduced in the Senate by [Gordon H. Smith](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_H._Smith) on July 13, 2005.[^cite_note-40] However, a number of leading [Senate Republicans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senate_Republican_Conference) objected to the requirement that the U.S. contributions be halved if the UN failed to meet all of the criteria. The UN Management, Personnel, and Policy Reform Act of 2005 (S. 1394), introduced on July 12, 2005 into the Senate by Sen. [Norm Coleman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norm_Coleman) \[R-MN] and Sen. [Richard Lugar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Lugar) \[R-IN], called for similar reforms but left the withholding of dues to the discretion of the [President](24113.md).[^cite_note-41] Neither piece of legislation made it into law.

## Visa refusal controversies

### Abkhazia

In April 2007, the U.S. government refused to give an entry visa to the foreign minister of the *[de facto](43594.md)* independent Republic of [Abkhazia](18933375.md) (*de jure* part of [Georgia](48768.md)) [Sergei Shamba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sergei_Shamba) who was due to speak at the UN headquarters in New York City. The incident caused an international dispute as [Russian Permanent Representative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permanent_Representative_of_Russia_to_the_United_Nations) [Vitaly Churkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vitaly_Churkin) accused the U.S.[^cite_note-42] of not letting one side of the conflict speak before UN. Security Council president, British Ambassador [Emyr Jones Parry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emyr_Jones_Parry), backed the Russian demand for Shamba's visa.[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44] But the U.S. Ambassador, [Alejandro Daniel Wolff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alejandro_Daniel_Wolff), accused the Russian side of "a mischievous effort" to raise "false analogies" between Abkhazia and Kosovo, thus "complicating the discussion."[^cite_note-45] The U.S. stated that such airport to UNHQ visa access was not guaranteed to countries seeking international recognition; [Kosovo](17391.md) president [Fatmir Sejdiu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fatmir_Sejdiu) had been given a visa.[^cite_note-46] Sergei Shamba himself described the situation as "dual standards".[^cite_note-47][^cite_note-48]

### Palestine

In August 2025, the [second Trump administration](78298108.md) barred [Palestinian](241405.md) officials from attending the UN General Assembly in New York the following month. The visa ban applied to as many as 90 Palestinian officials from the [Palestinian Authority](24093.md) and the [Palestine Liberation Organization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palestine_Liberation_Organization), including Palestinian president [Mahmoud Abbas](232595.md). U.S. Secretary of State [Marco Rubio](5502549.md) cited the need to hold the two bodies "accountable for not complying with their commitments, and for undermining the prospects for peace" and demanded that they "consistently repudiate terrorism" such as the [October 7 attacks](74998317.md), "end incitement to terrorism in education," end legal warfare against [Israel](9282173.md) through the [International Criminal Court](14880.md) and [International Court of Justice](14918.md), and stop pushing countries to recognize a "conjectural Palestinian state."[^cite_note-49][^cite_note-50] Despite this, the UN voted 145 in favor to allow Mahmoud Abbas to attend the annual gathering by video, with five votes against including the United States and Israel.[^cite_note-51]

## Future



US Secretary of State [Mike Pompeo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Pompeo) (left) with UN Secretary-General [António Guterres](471806.md) (right) in 2019

Further information: [Proposals for the United States to withdraw from the United Nations](14863992.md)

In the US, complaints about the UN surface regularly in the domestic mainstream media. Some critics who oppose international constraints on US foreign policy contend that the US should withdraw from the UN, claiming that the United States is better equipped to manage the global order unilaterally.[^cite_note-52] More frequently, critics argue that the UN should be reformed to bring it more in line with US policy and leadership.[^cite_note-53]

Despite criticisms, the majority of Americans (88%) support active engagement in the United Nations, as evidenced by a non-partisan poll conducted after the 2016 election.[^cite_note-54][^cite_note-55] While most agree that the UN could be improved, [Noam Chomsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Noam_Chomsky), a leading critic of U.S. foreign policy, proposes that measures such as the US relinquishing its veto power in the Security Council and submitting to the rulings of the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) could significantly improve the UN's ability to foster the growth of democracy and promote global peace and the protection of human rights.[^cite_note-56] However, some American organizations and individuals, such as the [John Birch Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Birch_Society) and [Wayne LaPierre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wayne_LaPierre) of the [National Rifle Association of America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Rifle_Association_of_America), oppose the United Nations on the basis of its perceived failures or alleged interference with domestic affairs.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58] LaPierre wrote the book "The Global War on Your Guns," in which he alerts the U.S. to the supposed threat of the [Arms Trade Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arms_Trade_Treaty).[^cite_note-59]

When the [United States Government](195149.md) released its [National Security Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Security_Strategy_\(United_States\)) for 2010 in May 2010, this quote was found embedded on the 46th page in regards to the United Nations:

***Enhance Cooperation with and Strengthen the United Nations:** We are enhancing our coordination with the U.N. and its agencies. We need a U.N. capable of fulfilling its founding purpose—maintaining international peace and security, promoting global cooperation, and advancing human rights. To this end, we are paying our bills. We are intensifying efforts with partners on and outside the U.N. Security Council to ensure timely, robust, and credible Council action to address threats to peace and security. We favor Security Council reform that enhances the U.N.'s overall performance, credibility, and legitimacy. Across the broader U.N. system we support reforms that promote effective and efficient leadership and management of the U.N.'s international civil service, and we are working with U.N. personnel and member states to strengthen the U.N.'s leadership and operational capacity in peacekeeping, humanitarian relief, post-disaster recovery, development assistance, and the promotion of human's rights. And we are supporting new U.N. frameworks and capacities for combating transnational threats like proliferation of weapons of mass destruction, infectious disease, drug-trafficking, and counter terrorism.*

On January 3, 2017, a bill entitled the [American Sovereignty Restoration Act](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Sovereignty_Restoration_Act) of 2017 (ASRA) was introduced in the House to withdraw the US from the U.N.[^cite_note-60] This bill was nearly identical to the first such bill, the ASRA of 1997, introduced by [Ron Paul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ron_Paul).[^cite_note-61] Since then, a version of the ASRA has been introduced in every congressional session.[^cite_note-62][^cite_note-63][^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65][^cite_note-66][^cite_note-67][^cite_note-68][^cite_note-69][^cite_note-70] To date, each such attempt has been met with minimal congressional support and died in committee.
