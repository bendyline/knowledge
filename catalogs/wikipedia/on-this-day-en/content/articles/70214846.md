**Cristian Westemaier Ribera** (born 13 November 2002) is a Brazilian [Para cross-country skier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Para_cross-country_skiing). At the [2021 World Para Snow Sports Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_World_Para_Snow_Sports_Championships), he won the silver medal in the men's sprint sitting event. At the [2026 Winter Paralympics](41045653.md), he also won the silver medal in the men's sprint sitting event, becoming the first Brazilian in history to win a medal of any kind at the [Winter Paralympics](18670284.md).[^cite_note-\:0-3]

## Background

Ribera was born with [arthrogryposis multiplex congenita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthrogryposis_multiplex_congenita), which causes bent and stiff joints in various parts of the body. He has undergone 21 leg surgeries to improve his condition. He started playing sports when he was four years old, and he started parallel skiing in 2015.[^cite_note-IPC-4]

## Career

Ribera made his international debut in the World Cup in 2017–18, and came in seventh place in the class for sitting athletes.[^cite_note-5] He participated in the [2018 Winter Paralympics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Winter_Paralympics), where he was the youngest participant at 15 years old, finishing in sixth place in the 15 km event, ninth place and 15th place in the 7.5 km and sprint events respectively. In addition, he participated in the mixed relay, where Brazil finished in 13th place. [^cite_note-6]

Ribera came in 13th place in the World Cup in 2018–19, and he came in second place in the World Cup in 2019–20, only beaten by [Ivan Golubkov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Golubkov).[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8] The following season, he finished in 13th place in the World Cup in 2020–21.

At the [2021 World Para Snow Sports Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2021_World_Para_Snow_Sports_Championships) held in [Lillehammer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lillehammer), Norway, Ribera won his first medal ever in World Championships – a silver medal in the sprint event. In the 18 km event, he finished in sixth place, and in the 10 km event, he finished in eighth place.[^cite_note-9]



&#x20;Cristian Ribera in 2021

At the [2023 World Para Nordic Skiing Championships](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_World_Para_Nordic_Skiing_Championships) held in [Östersund](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%96stersund), Sweden, Ribera won the bronze medal in the sprint event. [^cite_note-10]

Ribera won 3 medals at the [FIS Nordic World Ski Championships 2025](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Nordic_World_Ski_Championships_2025). His first gold medal ever was at the [sprint](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Nordic_World_Ski_Championships_2025_%E2%80%93_Men's_para_cross-country_sprint) in [Trondheim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trondheim), Norway[^cite_note-11]. He also won two bronze medals in the events held in [Toblach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toblach), Italy – in the 20km freestyle [^cite_note-12] and the 10 km Classical [^cite_note-13]. That month, Ribera earned the Men's Para cross-country Crystal Globe of the 2024/25 season – he became the first para-sportsman from Brazil and [South America](26769.md) to win that title.[^cite_note-14]

At the [2026 Winter Paralympics](41045653.md), Ribera also won the silver medal in the men's sprint sitting event, becoming the first Brazilian and [Latin American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latin_American) in history to win a medal of any kind at the [Winter Paralympics](18670284.md). He dominated much of the final of that event in that discipline, but was overtaken in the final stretch by China's [Zixu Liu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zixu_Liu), who clocked a time of 2\:28.9 to take the gold; the Brazilian finished in 2\:29.6, closely followed by Kazakhstan's [Yerbol Khamitov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yerbol_Khamitov) (2\:29.9), who took the bronze.[^cite_note-\:0-3]
