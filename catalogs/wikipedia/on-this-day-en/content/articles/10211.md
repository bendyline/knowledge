# Economic security

For financial securities such as [stocks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Common_stock) and [bonds](60737.md), see [security (finance)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Security_\(finance\)).

**Economic security** or financial security is the condition of having stable income or other resources to support a [standard of living](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard_of_living) now and in the foreseeable future. It includes:

- probable continued [solvency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Solvency)

- predictability of the future [cash flow](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cash_flow) of a [person](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Person) or other [economic entity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Entity), such as a country

- employment security or [job security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_security)

Without such security, people may experience its opposite: [economic insecurity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_insecurity) and resulting [economic anxiety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_anxiety).

Financial security more often refers to individual and family money management and savings.[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2] Economic security tends to include the broader effect of a society's production levels and monetary support for non-working citizens.

## Components of individual economic security

In the [United States](3434750.md), children's economic security is indicated by the [income](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Income) level and [employment security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Job_security) of their families or organizations.[^cite_note-3] Economic security of people over 50 years old is based on [Social Security benefits](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Security_\(United_States\)), [pensions](43613.md) and savings, earnings and employment, and [health insurance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Health_insurance) coverage.[^cite_note-4]

The Federal Reserve's 2025 Survey of Household Economics and Decisionmaking reported that 73 percent of U.S. adults said they were doing okay financially or living comfortably.[^cite_note-5] In the same survey, 63 percent said they would cover a hypothetical \$400 emergency expense entirely with cash, savings, or a credit card paid in full at the next statement, while 55 percent reported enough emergency savings to cover three months of expenses.[^cite_note-6]

### Arizona

In 1972, the state legislature of [Arizona](21883824.md) formed a Department of Economic Security with a mission to promote "the safety, well-being, and self sufficiency of children, adults, and families". This department combines state government activities previously managed by the Employment Security Commission, the State Department of Public Welfare, the Division of Vocational Rehabilitation, the State Office of Economic Opportunity, the [Apprenticeship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apprenticeship) Council, and the State Office of Manpower Planning. The State Department of [Mental Retardation](18567040.md) (renamed the Division of Developmental Disabilities, House Bill 2213) joined the department in 1974 . The purpose in creating the department was to provide an integration of direct services to people in such a way as to reduce duplication of administrative efforts, services and expenditures. Family Connections became a part of the department in January 2007.[^cite_note-7]

### Minnesota

The [Minnesota](19590.md) Department of Economic Security was formed in 1977 from the departments of Employment Services and Vocational Rehabilitation, the Governor's Manpower Office, and the Economic Opportunity Office, which administered [anti-poverty programs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poverty_reduction). In 1985, State Services for the Blind was included in this department. In 2003, the Minnesota Department of Economic Security and Minnesota Department of Trade and Economic Development were merged to form The Minnesota Department of Employment and [Economic Development](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_Development).[^cite_note-8]

## National economic security

In the context of [domestic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Domestic_politics) [politics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics) and [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations), **national economic security** is the ability of a [country](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nation-state) to follow its choice of policies to develop the national economy in the manner desired. Historically, conquest of nations have made conquerors rich through plunder, access to new resources and enlarged trade through controlling of the economies of conquered nations. Today's complex system of international trade is characterized by multi-national agreements and mutual inter-dependence. Availability of natural resources and capacity for production and distribution are essential under this system, leading many experts to consider economic security to be as important a part of national security as military policy.

Economic security has been proposed as a key determinant of [international relations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_relations), particularly in the [geopolitics](163225.md) of [petroleum](23195.md) in [American](3434750.md) foreign policy after [1973 oil crisis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1973_oil_crisis) and [September 11, 2001](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/September_11,_2001_attacks).[^cite_note-9]

In [Canada](5042916.md), threats to the country's overall economic security are considered **economic espionage**, which is "illegal, clandestine or coercive activity by a foreign government in order to gain unauthorized access to economic intelligence, such as proprietary information or technology, for economic advantage."[^cite_note-10]

In January 2021, the [United States Department of Homeland Security](58236.md) (DHS) issued Strategic Action Plan to Counter the Threat Posed by China.[^cite_note-11]

In October 2021 in Japan, prime minister [Fumio Kishida](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fumio_Kishida) created [the first-ever ministerial post](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Kishida_Cabinet) for economic security.[^cite_note-12] And in April 2022, Japan's [National Diet](163847.md) passed an economic security bill aimed at guarding technology and reinforcing critical supply chains, while also imposing tighter oversight of Japanese firms working in sensitive sectors or critical infrastructure. Measures in the legislation, which is primarily aimed at warding off [risks from China](2440214.md), will be implemented over two years once it is enacted, according to the bill.[^cite_note-13]

In March 2023, Japan and Germany agreed to strengthen cooperation on economic security in the aftermath of tensions over global supply chains and the economic impact of [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine). In the first high-ministerial government consultations held between the two countries, German Chancellor [Olaf Scholz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olaf_Scholz) reached out to Tokyo to seek to reduce Germany's dependence on China for imports of raw materials.[^cite_note-14]

On April 4, 2023, a G7 Trade Ministers' Meeting via video conference was held to discuss on enhancing economic security, and a G7 Trade Ministers' Statement was issued on the day.[^cite_note-15] Also in April 2023, Japan's [Public Security Intelligence Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_Security_Intelligence_Agency) (PSIA) launched a division dedicated to economic security. The agency also plans to set up such dedicated units in its regional bureaus nationwide to step up efforts to prevent cutting-edge technology and data from being leaked out of the country.[^cite_note-16] On 20 May 2023 on occasion of the [G7 Hiroshima summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/49th_G7_summit), economic security was discussed for the first time as the G7 agenda,[^cite_note-17] and "G7 Leaders' Statement on Economic Resilience and Economic Security" was issued based on the discussion.[^cite_note-G7-ER\&ES-18]

On 20 June 2023, the [European Commission](9974.md) and the High Representative proposed a Joint Communication on a European Economic Security Strategy which will be discussed by EU leaders at their meeting.[^cite_note-EC_2023-06-20-19]

On 12 December 2025 the U.S. announced [Pax Silica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pax_Silica), an initiative for strengthening and coordinating "trusted" [supply chains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supply_chain) for advanced technologies, and using those strong economic ties to increase national security.[^cite_note-20]

## Other

It is widely believed that there is a [tradeoff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tradeoff) between economic security and [economic opportunity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economic_opportunity?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-21]
