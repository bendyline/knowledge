On 25 September 2026, a [Let L-410 Turbolet](2486921.md) operated by [Tracep Congo Aviation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tracep_Congo_Aviation?action=edit\&redlink=1) had a technical problem during a flight from [Kikwit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikwit) to [N'Dolo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Dolo_Airport), [Kinshasa](45773.md). It attempted to land at [Kenge](21192344.md) in [Kwango Province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kwango_Province) but crashed and caught on fire, killing all 17 occupants.[^cite_note-ASN-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-actcd-4]

## Background

### Airline

The certificate of Tracep Congo Aviation was suspended on Sep. 26. Like other airlines based in the DRC, it had been [banned from flying in the European Union](6130249.md).[^cite_note-actcd-4][^cite_note-5] The crash was the second involving the airline in just over two months, with another L-410 crash near Buluku in July resulting in a fatality.[^cite_note-6]

### Aircraft

The aircraft involved was registered 9S-GUC, a Let L-410UVP operated by Tracep Congo Aviation. The aircraft was built in 1983 and had a [Walter M601](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Walter_M601) twin-engine turboprop.[^cite_note-ASN-1] The aircraft sustained damage to the nose gear door in June 2014 after striking a pig on a runway at [Shabunda Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shabunda_Airport) while operating under [Goma Express](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goma_Express?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-7]

### Passengers and Crews

The aircraft had four crew members and 13 passengers.[^cite_note-8] The passenger list was a mix of a high-level military justice delegation and a few civilians.[^cite_note-MiGFlug-9] Among the military personnel were Lieutenant General Joseph Mutombo Katalayi Tiende, the First President of the High Military Court, and Lieutenant General Lucien-René Likulia Bakumi, the Auditor General of the [Armed Forces of the Democratic Republic of the Congo](8029.md) (FARDC).[^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

## Accident

The flight was en route from [Kikwit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kikwit) to [N'Dolo Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N'Dolo_Airport) in [Kinshasa](45773.md), carrying a military delegation and civilians. During the flight, the aircraft encountered severe in-flight technical difficulties.[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-MiGFlug-9]

Witnesses on the ground reported seeing the plane circle over [Kenge](21192344.md) multiple times as the crew desperately looked for a safe place to land.[^cite_note-13] The pilots attempted an emergency landing at the local Kenge aerodrome, but the runway had been decommissioned in 2023 and largely built over with houses and streets due to urban encroachment. At around 2\:15 p.m., the aircraft crashed into a residential compound and burst into flames.[^cite_note-BBC-14][^cite_note-15] There were no casualties among the residents on the ground.[^cite_note-16] Every person on the aircraft except the pilot were burnt beyond recognition.[^cite_note-BBC-14][^cite_note-17]

## Aftermath

Following the accident the [Prime Minister of the Democratic Republic of the Congo](8699891.md) and the Minister of National Defense called an emergency meeting of the military command on September 26. Congolese authorities also declared three days of national mourning and stated that an investigation into the accident has been commenced.[^cite_note-18]

Online speculation hypothesised that sabotage could have occurred since the aircraft was carrying high-ranking officials. But, for now, congolese authorities have dismissed these claims.[^cite_note-19]

## Investigation

The aircraft black box was recovered by investigators and brought to Kinshasa were it is being analysed.[^cite_note-20]
