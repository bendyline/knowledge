The **Presidential Palace** ([Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language): საქართველოს პრეზიდენტის სასახლე, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Georgian): sakartvelos p'rezident'is sasakhle), the **Orbeliani Palace** ([Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language): ორბელიანის სასახლე, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Georgian): orbelianis sasakhle) or the **Atoneli Residence** ([Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_language): ათონელის რეზიდენცია, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Georgian): atonelis rezidentsia) is the [official residence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_residence) of the [president of Georgia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_Georgia) since November 2018. It is located on Atoneli Street in Central [Tbilisi](42298.md).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-AgendaGE_Zourabichvili_relocate_2018-2][^cite_note-3] It was once home to [Elizabeth Orbeliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Orbeliani), a Georgian poet and the country's first female academic.&#x20;

## History



[Elizabeth Orbeliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Orbeliani)

The Orbeliani Palace dates back to the end of the 19th century.[^cite_note-4] Its residents included [Grigol Orbeliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grigol_Orbeliani), a Georgian Romanticist poet, and [Elizabeth Orbeliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elizabeth_Orbeliani), a poet and Georgia's first woman professor. To this day, a room in the palace dedicated to Elizabeth Orbeliani[^cite_note-5] is used to receive foreign dignitaries.[^cite_note-6]&#x20;

The building also served as the U.S. Embassy in Georgia in the 1990s and early 2000s. In 2013, about 25 million [Georgian lari](904025.md) was spent renovating the palace.[^cite_note-7]&#x20;

The palace is sometimes called the "Atoneli Residence" because it is located on Atoneli Street, named after the [medieval](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages) Georgian monk [George of Athos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_the_Hagiorite).[^cite_note-8]&#x20;

### Presidential residence

Georgian politician and former French diplomat [Salome Zourabichvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salome_Zourabichvili) announced during her [2018 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2018_Georgian_presidential_election) that she would not work from the [Avlabari Presidential Palace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avlabari_Presidential_Palace) if elected. That palace, which opened in July 2009, was built during the presidency of [Mikheil Saakashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikheil_Saakashvili), with whom Zourabichvili had a fallout. Zourabichvili stated that she preferred Orbeliani Palace because it was more understated and that members of the [House of Orbeliani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Orbeliani) and [Baratashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baratashvili), who once resided there, were her ancestors.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10]&#x20;

After her election, she met with the outgoing President, [Giorgi Margvelashvili](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Giorgi_Margvelashvili), in the Avlabari Palace before moving into the Orbeliani Palace on 18 December 2018.[^cite_note-11]&#x20;
