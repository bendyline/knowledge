**Liverpool John Moores University** (abbreviated **LJMU**) is a [public](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_university) [research university](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Research_university) in the city of [Liverpool](18081.md), United Kingdom. The university can trace its origins to the Liverpool Mechanics' School of Arts, established in 1823.[^cite_note-3] This later merged to become **Liverpool Polytechnic**. In 1992, following an [Act of Parliament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Further_and_Higher_Education_Act_1992), the Liverpool Polytechnic became what is now Liverpool John Moores University.[^cite_note-4] It is named after [Sir John Moores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Moores), a local businessman and philanthropist, who donated to the university's precursor institutions.

The university had 25,050 students in 2024/25, of which 20,105 are undergraduate students and 4,945 are postgraduate,[^cite_note-HESA_citation-2] making it the [30th largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_universities_in_the_United_Kingdom_by_enrollment) university in the UK by total student population.

It is a member of the [AACSB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Association_to_Advance_Collegiate_Schools_of_Business), [MillionPlus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MillionPlus), the [Northern Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCUK) and the [European University Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_University_Association).

## History

### Origins

Founded as a small mechanics institution ([Liverpool Mechanics' School of Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Institute_High_School_for_Boys)) in 1823, the institution grew over the centuries by converging and amalgamating with different colleges, including the [F. L. Calder School of Domestic Science](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fanny_Calder),[^cite_note-5] the C. F. Mott College of Education,[^cite_note-6] before eventually becoming Liverpool Polytechnic in 1970.[^cite_note-ljmu2-7] The university also has a long history of providing training, education and research to the maritime industry, dating back to the formation of the Liverpool Nautical College in 1892.

A predecessor called the *Liverpool Gymnasium College* was founded in 1900 as a college for training physical education teachers by [Irene Marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irene_Marsh), an early advocate for women's physical education.[^cite_note-8] Initially located at 110 Bedford Street, near the city centre, the college expanded over time. In 1947 the institution was renamed the I.M. Marsh College of Physical Education, becoming the first state-maintained specialist college for women's physical education in the United Kingdom. In 1981, the college became part of Liverpool Polytechnic. The [I.M. Marsh Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.M._Marsh_Campus) remained in operation until its closure in July 2021, following the relocation of academic activities to LJMU's city centre campuses.

The institution then became a university under the terms of the [Further and Higher Education Act 1992](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Further_and_Higher_Education_Act_1992) under the new title of "Liverpool John Moores University". This new title was approved by the [Privy Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Her_Majesty's_Most_Honourable_Privy_Council) on 15 September 1992. The university took its name from [Sir John Moores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Moores_\(merchant\)), the founder of the [Littlewoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Littlewoods) empire. Moores was a great believer in the creation of opportunity for all, which embodies the ethos of LJMU in providing educational routes for people of all ages and from all backgrounds. This belief led Sir John Moores to invest in the institution and facilities, such as the [John Foster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Foster_\(architect,_born_1786\)) Building (housing the Liverpool Business School), designed by and named after leading architect John Foster.[^cite_note-ljmu2-7] With the institution's backgrounds dating back as far as 1823, many of the university buildings date back also, with aesthetically pleasing [Georgian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Georgian_architecture) and [Victorian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Victorian_architecture) buildings found on a few of the campuses.[^cite_note-push-1]

### Present day



The [James Parsons Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_Parsons_Building) at LJMU's City Campus

LJMU now has more than 27,000 students[^cite_note-9] from over 100 countries world-wide, 2,400 staff and 250 degree courses.[^cite_note-10] LJMU was awarded the [Queen's Anniversary Prize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen's_Anniversary_Prize) in 2005.[^cite_note-11]

Currently, Liverpool John Moores University is receiving more applications than previously seen; according to data in 2009, the total number of applications submitted to LJMU was 27,784.[^cite_note-12]

On 28 March 2022, former student and founder of [Mowgli Street Food](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mowgli_Street_Food), [Nisha Katona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nisha_Katona) was installed as chancellor of the university.[^cite_note-13] Previously, in 2008, astrophysicist and [Queen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queen_\(band\)) lead guitarist [Brian May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_May) was appointed the fourth [Chancellor of Liverpool John Moores University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chancellor_of_Liverpool_John_Moores_University). He replaced outgoing Chancellor [Cherie Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cherie_Blair), wife of former Prime Minister [Tony Blair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tony_Blair). Honorary fellows in attendance at the ceremony included astronomer Sir [Patrick Moore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patrick_Moore) and actor [Pete Postlethwaite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pete_Postlethwaite).[^cite_note-14] May was succeeded as Chancellor by judge Sir [Brian Leveson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Leveson) in 2013.

LJMU is a founding member of the [Northern Consortium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NCUK), an educational charity owned by 11 universities in northern England.

## Campuses

The university is separated into two campuses in Liverpool:

- City Campus, mainly situated on [Byrom Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scotland_Road#Liverpool_John_Moores_University) and surrounding Great Crosshall Street up to [Liverpool Exchange railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Exchange_railway_station), houses buildings such as the James Parsons Building, City Campus Library (formerly known as the [Avril Robarts Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avril_Robarts_Library)) and the Faculty of Health, Innovation, Technology and Science.

- Mount Pleasant Campus, next to the [Liverpool Metropolitan Cathedral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Metropolitan_Cathedral) and the University of Liverpool, contains the [John Lennon Art and Design Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Lennon_Art_and_Design_Building), Mount Pleasant Campus Library (formerly known as the [Aldham Robarts Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldham_Robarts_Library)) and is home to the Faculty of Society and Culture. The [Knowledge Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_Quarter,_Liverpool) is also regarded to be part of this campus.

Between the two campuses is the Copperas Hill Site, opened in summer 2021, and home to the Student Life and LJMU Sports Buildings. Its location between the two sites has been described by the university to help connect both of its campuses together, and is not regarded to be part of either. It is however closer to the Mount Pleasant Campus and separated from the City Campus by the [A5047](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A5047_road), and [Liverpool Lime Street railway station](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_Lime_Street_railway_station).[^cite_note-15]

### Libraries



Mount Pleasant Campus Library as viewed from Mount Pleasant campus

There are currently two libraries operated by LJMU, one for either campus:[^cite_note-16]

- The Mount Pleasant Campus Library (formerly known as the [Aldham Robarts Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aldham_Robarts_Library)) provides for students studying at the Mount Pleasant Campus or otherwise residing in the central [Knowledge Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Knowledge_Quarter,_Liverpool) area. The library is situated on Maryland Street and caters mainly for the Faculty of Society and Culture.

- The City Campus Library (formerly known as the [Avril Robarts Library](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Avril_Robarts_Library)) mainly provides services to students studying in the City Campus. It is located on [Tithebarn Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tithebarn_Street), and covers the Faculty of Health, Innovation, Technology and Science. The library hosts the [Superlambanana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superlambanana) sculpture at its front.

There is an LRC present in the Learning Commons of the Student Life Building on the Copperas Hill site between the two campuses.

Students of the university can use any library in term-time and some non-term time periods within the library's opening hours. The Student Life Building is open 24/7 in term time. Students need their student identification card for entry to all buildings.

There are more than 68,500 books in the Libraries' collections, with 1,630 work spaces available for students 24 hours a day. In addition to this there are over 16,000 e-books and 5,000 e-journals available.[^cite_note-17] It is a member of the Libraries Together: Liverpool Learning Partnership (evolved from Liverpool Libraries Group) which formed in 1990. Under which, a registered reader at any of the member libraries can have access rights to the other libraries within the partnership.[^cite_note-liv-18]

### Tom Reilly Building

The Tom Reilly Building houses the School of Sports and Exercise Sciences and the School of Psychology, which are both part of the Faculty of Health, Innovation, Technology and Science.[^cite_note-reilly1-19] Some 8,000 students use the building which is located at LJMU's City Campus on Byrom Street. The five storey, 6,493 m<sup>2</sup> (69,890 sq ft) building was completed in November 2009[^cite_note-reilly1-19] and opened in March 2010 by [Liverpool F.C.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liverpool_F.C.) captain [Steven Gerrard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steven_Gerrard).[^cite_note-reilly2-20] The building provides sports and science facilities including; appetite laboratories, psychology testing labs, neuroscience labs, an indoor 70 m (230 ft) running track, [force plates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Force_platform), caren disc, physiology suites, a [DEXA scanner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dual_energy_X-ray_absorptiometry), a [driving simulator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Driving_simulator) and a [chronobiology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chronobiology) lab.[^cite_note-reilly1-19]

### James Parsons Building

The James Parsons Building is located at the City Campus and was built in 1960 as part of Liverpool Polytechnic. Later additions were made in 1966 and 1970, an extensive modernisation took place internally and externally in 2010.[^cite_note-stats-21] The James Parsons Building has a gross floor area of 29,833 m<sup>2</sup> (321,120 sq ft) and at its tallest is 13 storeys high.[^cite_note-stats-21] Several schools within the Faculty of Health, Innovation, Technology and Science are based in the building – which has laboratories, ICT suites and lecture theatres throughout as well as two cafés.[^cite_note-stats-21]

The entire third floor of the James Parsons Building was the first internal aspect to be renovated, whilst [cladding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cladding_\(construction\)) worth £3.4 million was applied to the external facade during 2009 and 2010.[^cite_note-cladding-22]

## Academic profile

LJMU is highly ranked for teaching and research in Sports and Exercise Sciences.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-ljmu3-26] The Higher Education Funding Council for England (HEFCE) awarded LJMU £4.5 million over five years for the establishment of a Centre for Excellence in Teaching and Learning (CETL). The CETL award recognises LJMU's record for Physical Education, Dance, Sport and Exercises Sciences. LJMU is the only United Kingdom university to be awarded an Ofsted Grade A in Physical Education and it is also the premier institution for both teaching and research in Sport and Exercise Sciences.[^cite_note-ljmu3-26]

### Business School

Liverpool Business School (LBS) is located in the Redmonds Building on the Mount Pleasant Campus and has over 2,500 students and 100 academics.[^cite_note-Welcome-27]

LBS offers undergraduate, postgraduate (including an Executive MBA) and research based programmes.[^cite_note-Welcome-27] Research areas include International Banking, Economics and Finance, Sustainable Enterprise, Public Service Management, Development of Modern Economic Thought, Performance Management, Marketing, Project Management, and Market Research.[^cite_note-28]

### Research



Mount Pleasant Campus Centre

In the 2001 Research Assessment Exercise (RAE), LJMU reported notable research strengths in general engineering and sports-related sciences. By the 2008 RAE, LJMU was the top-performing post-92 university for Anthropology, Electrical and Electronic Engineering, General Engineering, Physics (Astrophysics) and Sports-Related Studies. According to the UK Research Assessment Exercise 2014 (RAE 2014), LJMU every unit of assessment submitted was rated as at least 45% internationally excellent or better.[^cite_note-LJMU_RAE-29] In 2012, the university's scientist published notable research suggesting that the dinosaur's extinction may have been caused by increased methane production from the dinosaurs, with some informally saying that dinosaurs "farted" their way to extinction.[^cite_note-30]

## Student life

### Students' Union

Main article: [John Moores Students' Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Moores_Students'_Union)

Students at the university are represented by the [John Moores Students' Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Moores_Students'_Union).

Representation for all students is central and is conducted by executive officers elected annually. In most cases, these students will be on a sabbatical from their studies. The election process is normally contested in mid April, successful candidates assuming office the following academic year.

### Sports



[I. M. Marsh Campus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/I.M._Marsh_Campus)' swimming pool and sports hall

Liverpool John Moores University has [BUCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Universities_&_Colleges_Sport)-registered teams in [badminton](3956.md), [basketball](3921.md), [cricket](25675557.md), [football](10568.md), [cycling](857311.md), [hockey](10886.md), [netball](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netball), [rugby league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rugby_league), [rugby union](25405.md), [tennis](29773.md), [volleyball](32558.md), snowriders racers team, [swimming](27999.md), and [American football](18951490.md). Many of the sports teams compete in [BUCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Universities_&_Colleges_Sport) competitions. Liverpool Students' Union has 15 [BUCS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Universities_&_Colleges_Sport) sports, from which 36 teams run, catering for over 800 athletes. In recent years, LJMU students have competed for BUCS representative squads, in national finals and at World University Championships.[^cite_note-ljmu4-38] In addition, the Students' Union also runs intramural sports leagues.



LJMU Fury, the university's American football team

The university also enjoys success at national and world level. Gymnast [Beth Tweddle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beth_Tweddle) studied at LJMU and has achieved national, [Commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth_Games), European, and World medals whilst also competing at the Olympic Games.

Every year the university sports compete for '[The Varsity Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_British_and_Irish_varsity_matches)' in the inter-university derby, Liverpool John Moores University Vs. [University of Liverpool](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Liverpool). The competing sports include: badminton, basketball, hockey, football, netball, volleyball, swimming, tennis, and the snowriders racing team.
