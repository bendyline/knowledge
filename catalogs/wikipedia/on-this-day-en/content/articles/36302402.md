**Ukrainians in Germany** ([Ukrainian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_language): Українці в Німеччині, [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Ukrainer in Deutschland*) currently form one of the largest ethnic minorities in the country. Despite the long history of contacts between the two groups, significant Ukrainian presence in Germany started only after the [First World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_World_War). Following the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), the number of Ukrainians in Germany experienced a major growth due to the arrival of refugees.

## History

### Middle Ages

Political and trade connections between German lands and territory of today's Ukraine are recorded starting from the 9th century. In 959 an [embassy](8970.md) of [Olga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olga_of_Kiev), [Princess of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Prince_of_Kiev), arrived to the court of [Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Emperor) [Otto I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otto_I). In 973 ambassadors of [Yaropolk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaropolk_I_of_Kiev), son of [Sviatoslav of Kyiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatoslav_I_of_Kiev), took part in a congress in [Quedlinburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quedlinburg). Princes [Volodymyr the Great](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_I_of_Kiev) and [Yaroslav the Wise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslav_the_Wise) also exchanged ambassadors with [German](152735.md) rulers.[^cite_note-ency-3]

In 1056 [Henry IV, Holy Roman Emperor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_IV,_Holy_Roman_Emperor), gave refuge to [Anastasia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anastasia_of_Kiev), daughter of Yaroslav, and wife of king [Andrew I of Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_I_of_Hungary). In 1073 [Iziaslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iziaslav_I_of_Kiev), son of Yaroslav, also sought refuge at Henry's court after being deposed from the Kyivan throne by his brother [Sviatoslav](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sviatoslav_II_of_Kiev). In 1089 Henry IV married [Eupraxia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eupraxia_of_Kiev), the daughter of Kyivan prince [Vsevolod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vsevolod_I_of_Kiev). However, due to intrigues and political conflicts, their marriage eventually collapsed, and Eupraxis returned to [Kyiv](585629.md). In 1189, prince [Volodymyr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vladimir_II_Yaroslavich), son of [Yaroslav Osmomysl](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yaroslav_Osmomysl), visited the residence of [Frederick Barbarossa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frederick_Barbarossa), seeking alliance in order to restore his rule in [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Principality_of_Galicia).[^cite_note-ency-3]

### Modern era

Starting from the 16th century, students from Ukrainian lands are recorded in documents of German universities. Among them was the son of [Kyiv Voivode](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyiv_Voivodeship) [Fryderyk Proński](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fryderyk_Pro%C5%84ski?action=edit\&redlink=1), who studied at the [University of Heidelberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Heidelberg), as well as numerous [Ruthenian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ruthenians) students from [Volhynia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volhynia), [Galicia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galicia_\(Eastern_Europe\)) and [Podolia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Podolia). Notable Ukrainian alumni of the [University of Leipzig](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_Leipzig) included [Meletius Smotrytsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meletius_Smotrytsky) and [Alexander Bezborodko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Bezborodko).[^cite_note-ency-3]

After the deposition of [Ukrainian Cossack hetman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetman_of_the_Zaporizhian_Host) [Ivan Mazepa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ivan_Mazepa), his allies [Pylyp Orlyk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pylyp_Orlyk) and [Andriy Voynarovsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andriy_Voynarovsky) spent some time in Germany as political [émigrés](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%89migr%C3%A9). Following the liquidation of [Ukrainian autonomy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cossack_Hetmanate), in 1791 [Vasily Kapnist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasily_Kapnist) secretly travelled to [Berlin](3354.md) in order to seek help for a possible uprising against the [Russian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Empire).[^cite_note-ency-3]

Among prominent Ukrainian figures who were active in Germany during the 19th century were [Mykola Ziber](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykola_Ziber) and [Serhiy Podolynsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serhiy_Podolynsky). Notable Ukrainian students at German universities during that period included [Mykola Lysenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykola_Lysenko) and [Andrey Sheptytsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrey_Sheptytsky). Starting from the late 19th century, inhabitants of [Western Ukraine](3258511.md) travelled to Germany as seasonal workers.[^cite_note-ency-3]

### 20th century



Monument to Ukrainian prisoners of World War I in Rastatt

During [World War I](4764461.md) Berlin became the seat of the [Union for the Liberation of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Union_for_the_Liberation_of_Ukraine). Many ethnic Ukrainian [prisoners of war](25008.md) were kept in German camps in [Rastatt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rastatt), [Wetzlar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wetzlar) and [Salzwedel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salzwedel), and later formed the core of the Bluecoat division of the [Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_People's_Republic). Following the signing of the [Treaty of Brest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Brest-Litovsk_\(Ukraine-Central_Powers\)), Ukraine established its diplomatic mission in [German Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Empire), headed by Oleksandr Sevriuk, and later [Theodor von Steinheil](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teodor_Shteingel). In September 1918 Germany was visited by [Pavlo Skoropadsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Skoropadsky), [Hetman of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetman_of_Ukraine).[^cite_note-ency-3]

Following the establishment of the [Directorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directorate_of_Ukraine), Ukraine was represented in Germany by [Mykola Porsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykola_Porsh), later replaced by Mykola von Wassilko. During the same period, [West Ukrainian People's Republic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Ukrainian_People's_Republic) had its own representation in Germany, headed by [Roman Smal-Stocki](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_Smal-Stocki). In 1919-1922 a [consulate](6770059.md) of the Ukrainian People's Republic was active in [Munich](19058.md), headed by [Vasyl Orenchuk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vasyl_Orenchuk). Between 1921 and 1923 [Ukrainian SSR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_SSR) was represented in Germany by [Voldemar Aussem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voldemar_Aussem). Following the [Treaty of Rapallo (1922)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Rapallo_\(1922\)), a trade representation of Soviet Ukraine was opened in Berlin. During the 1920s, Germany was visited by [Pavlo Tychyna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pavlo_Tychyna), [Mykola Khvylyovy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mykola_Khvylyovy), [Alexander Dovzhenko](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexander_Dovzhenko) and other Ukrainian authors. In 1926 a Ukrainian scientific institute was established in Berlin.[^cite_note-ency-3]

Starting from 1919, Germany became a centre of Ukrainian political emigration. Among notable figures of the Ukrainian community in Germany during the [Interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period) were [Zenon Kuzelia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zenon_Kuzelia), [Bohdan Lepky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bohdan_Lepky) and [Volodymyr Stakhiv](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Volodymyr_Stakhiv). Political leaders such as Pavlo SKoropadsky, [Yevhen Konovalets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevhen_Konovalets) and [Yevhen Petrushevych](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yevhen_Petrushevych) also resided in Germany during that time. In 1940 a [Ukrainian Greek Catholic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Greek_Catholic) [apostolic visitation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Apostolic_visitation) was founded in Berlin.[^cite_note-ency-3]



A group of Ukrainian *Ostarbeiter* in Germany, 1943

During the [Second World War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_World_War), up to 3 million Ukrainians ended up in Germany as [refugees](45547.md), prisoners of war, deportees or forced labourers ([Ostarbeiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ostarbeiter)). In 1944, a central representation of Ukrainians from the [Reichskommissariat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichskommissariat_Ukraine) was established in Berlin. Ukrainians from the [General Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Governorate) were represented separately. During that period, two major Ukrainian civic organizations were permitted to function in [Nazi Germany](21212.md): the [pro-nationalist](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_nationalism) Ukrainian National Union and the [pro-hetman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hetmanite_movement) Ukrainian Hromada. Separate organizations served in order to represent the interest of [stateless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stateless_person) Ukrainians and Ukrainian students.[^cite_note-ency-3]

Following [World War II](32927.md), the majority of Ukrainians residing in Germany were [repatriated](1481067.md), with approximately 180,000 remaining in [displaced persons camps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Displaced_persons_camp) in [West Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Germany). Due to gradual resettlement of the latter to [Western Europe](33800.md), [Americas](29833.md) and [Australia](4689264.md), by the early 1950s the Ukrainian population in Germany had decreased to approximately 20,000. in 1946 the [Ukrainian Free University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Free_University) resumed its activities in [Munich](19058.md). [Shevchenko Scientific Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shevchenko_Scientific_Society) and Ukrainian Free Academy of Sciences also established themselves in postwar Germany.[^cite_note-ency-3]

In terms of religion, the majority of the [Ukrainian diaspora](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_diaspora) in the country belonged to the Greek Catholic and [Ukrainian Autocephalous Orthodox Churches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ukrainian_Autocephalous_Orthodox_Church). Numerous Ukrainian educational establishments had appeared during the first postwar years, but their number significantly decreased by 1970. Ukrainian cultural establishments and publishing houses also resumed their activities. For a period of time, the [Government of the Ukrainian People's Republic in exile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_the_Ukrainian_People's_Republic_in_exile) resided in Munich, and the city also became the coordinational centre of the [Anti-Bolshevik Bloc of Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-Bolshevik_Bloc_of_Nations).[^cite_note-ency-3]

In 1999, a conflict arose involving the [German Foreign Minister](1329748.md) [Joschka Fischer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joschka_Fischer), who eased conditions for citizens of the [former Soviet states](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Former_Soviet_states) to get German visas. Many people opposed this claim that it enabled thousands to enter Germany illegally using the abuse of visas granted to them. The majority of Ukrainians that are in Germany on scholarship are there on such visas, adding to the controversy.

### Modern history



A demonstration in support of the [Orange Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Orange_Revolution) in [Frankfurt am Main](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frankfurt_am_Main), 2004

The [Russo-Ukrainian war](42085878.md) has led to millions of Ukrainians fleeing their country, many of whom ended up in [Germany](11867.md). In 2021, before the start of the full-scale [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), it was of 155,310.[^cite_note-destatis.de-4] At the end of 2022 there were approximately 1,164,200 [Ukrainians](145144.md) in [Germany](11867.md).[^cite_note-statista-1][^cite_note-destatis.de-4] Germany's Ukrainians have created a number of institutions and organizations, such as the Central Association of Ukrainians in Germany and Association of Ukrainian Diaspora in Germany.

Some Ukrainian organizations in Germany have accused Germans of [racism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racism_in_Germany) and prejudice, and of the belief that Ukrainians are only in Germany to work illegally.[^cite_note-www\.dw\.de|/''-5]
