# Hypersonic weapon

This article is about the category of missiles. For the music by Sam Fender, see [Hypersonic Missiles (album)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_Missiles_\(album\)) and [Hypersonic Missiles (song)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_Missiles_\(song\)).

A **hypersonic weapon** is a weapon that can travel and make significant sustained maneuvers during atmospheric flight at [hypersonic speed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_speed), which is defined as above [Mach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mach_number#High-speed_flow_around_objects) 5 (five times the [speed of sound](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speed_of_sound)).[^cite_note-AC-1] These typically fall into two main categories: [hypersonic glide vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_glide_vehicle) (boost-glide weapons), and hypersonic cruise missiles (airbreathing weapons).[^cite_note-twz20250619-2][^cite_note-\:0-3]

Below Mach 1, weapons would be characterized as [subsonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subsonic_ammunition), and above Mach 1, as [supersonic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supersonic_aircraft). At extremely high speeds, air in the [shock wave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shock_wave) is [ionized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ionized) into a [plasma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Plasma_\(physics\)), which makes control and communication difficult.[^cite_note-4]

## Categorization

There are two main categories of hypersonic weapon:

1. [Boost-glide](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boost-glide) hypersonic weapons, which glide and maneuver at hypersonic speeds following boosting by [rocket propulsion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rocket_propulsion). Typical examples are ballistic missiles fitted with [hypersonic glide vehicle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_glide_vehicle) warheads.

   [^cite_note-Stone2020-5]

   [^cite_note-AC-1]

   [^cite_note-\:0-3]

2. [Airbreathing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airbreathing_jet_engine) hypersonic weapons, typically [hypersonic cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hypersonic_cruise_missile) which maintain hypersonic speed by engines such as [scramjets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scramjet).

   [^cite_note-Stone2020-5]

   [^cite_note-AC-1]

   [^cite_note-\:0-3]

Gun-launched weapons, projectiles fired from either a conventional [artillery](2508.md) or prospective [railguns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Railgun) may also be considered a type of hypersonic weapon, though they are less common than the two main types.[^cite_note-AC-1]

Existing weapon systems such as [ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_missiles) already travel at hypersonic speeds (and may actually reach their target sooner when on depressed trajectories)[^cite_note-6] but are not typically classified as hypersonic weapons due to lacking the use of aerodynamic lift to allow their reentry vehicles to maneuver under guided flight within the atmosphere.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-Schumann-7][^cite_note-Brookings-8] [Maneuverable reentry vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maneuverable_reentry_vehicle), such as employed on the [Pershing II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pershing_II#Flight), [Fattah-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fattah-1) and [DF-21D](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DF-21#DF-21D_\(CSS-5_Mod-4\)_Anti-ship_ballistic_missile), are generally excluded from this definition as they maneuver aerodynamically only for short periods of time during the terminal phase, and lack the significant cross-range maneuverability expected from hypersonic weapons.[^cite_note-twz20250619-2][^cite_note-\:0-3] Additionally, air-to-air missiles can temporarily reach hypersonic speeds in certain atmospheric envelopes and launch regimes, but generally are not considered as hypersonic weapons as they do not conduct significant maneuvers at these speeds nor have the ability to evade anti-missile defenses.[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-Schumann-7][^cite_note-twz20250619-2][^cite_note-\:0-3]

The [Silbervogel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Silbervogel) was the first design for a hypersonic weapon and was developed by German scientists in the 1930s, but was never constructed.[^cite_note-SA-10]

The [ASALM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASALM) (Advanced Strategic Air-Launched Missile) was a medium-range strategic missile program developed in the late 1970s for the [United States Air Force](32090.md); the missile's development reached the stage of propulsion-system testing, test-flown to Mach 5.5[^cite_note-11] before being cancelled in 1980.

In the [2022 Russian invasion of Ukraine](79400268.md), [Russia](25391.md) was seen to have fielded operational weapons and used them for combat. The [Kremlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kremlin) presents new hypersonic weapons as supposedly capable of overcoming "any" foreign [missile defense](1099196.md) systems, with the "pre-nuclear deterrence" concept contained in its 2014 iteration of the official [Russian Military Doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Military_Doctrine).[^cite_note-rmrh-12] A volley of Russian hypersonic missiles were launched at Kyiv in January 2023.[^cite_note-13]
