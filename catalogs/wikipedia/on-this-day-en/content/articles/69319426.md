During 2021–2023, Somalia confronted its most severe drought in forty years that affected over 7.8 million people —about half of Somalia’s population.[^cite_note-3] As of 2024, drought-related impacts still continue to affect 4.4 million individuals across Somalia, who face including acute food insecurity. This includes an estimated 724,000 people at [Integrated Food Security phase 4 (Emergency)](16431152.md#phase4).[^cite_note-4][^cite_note-5]

Over 80% of the country was suffering a severe drought. In affected areas up to 20% of the population have experienced shortages of water, food and [pasture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pasture).[^cite_note-AJ-6] More than one million Somalis were displaced in 2022,[^cite_note-7] and over 300,000 people were still displaced in 2023.[^cite_note-8]

## Causes

Main article: [Climate change in Somalia](59045670.md)

Climate change is a major cause with climate related hazards on the increase.[^cite_note-UN-9]

## Impact

- The drought has had a devastating impact on Somalia's agriculture sector, which constitutes up to 26% of the country's GDP, employs 90% of the informal workforce, and contributes 90% of its exports. Since mid-2021, one-third of the livestock in the most severely affected regions have died.

  [^cite_note-10]

- Reduced access to surface water for both livestock and human use, alongside a shortage of available pasture, has severely impacted agricultural communities across the region. Some regions have seen up to a 70% reduction in crop yields.

  [^cite_note-11]

- Particularly in central regions and in [Puntland](233569.md), communities are relying on [boreholes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borehole) as most shallow wells and [berkads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berkad) have dried up,

- The below-average [Deyr](27358.md#seasons) rains, coupled with insufficient rainfall in the upper river catchments of the [Ethiopian highlands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethiopian_Highlands), have led to a decline in water levels of the Juba and Shabelle rivers.

  [^cite_note-12]

## Humanitarian situation

In 2022, 71% of people in Somalia were living beneath the poverty threshold.[^cite_note-13] The number of people requiring assistance peaked in 2023 and reached about 8.25 million.[^cite_note-14] While situation improved since then, in February 2024:[^cite_note-needs2023-15]

- 6.9M are still in need of humanitarian assistance.

- 4M people are facing acute food insecurity

- 1.7M of children are facing acute malnutrition

## Humanitarian Response Plan for Somalia

The Humanitarian Response Plan (HRP) for Somalia has undergone significant revisions over the years to address the evolving needs of its population. Here's a summary of the calls and responses from 2021 to 2023:

- **2021 HRP:** The 2021 plan aimed to support 4 out of the 5.9 million people in need of humanitarian assistance, requiring a budget of \$1.09 billion.

  [^cite_note-16]

  &#x20;The total funding received was about 79% of the requested \$1.09 billion.

  [^cite_note-17]

- **2022 HRP:** The needs for the 2022 HRP grew significantly, driven by ongoing drought conditions and increasing displacement. It sought to assist 5.5 million people, with initial funding request of \$1.46 billion.

  [^cite_note-18]

  &#x20;The response was scaled up to \$2.1 billion,

  [^cite_note-19]

  &#x20;reaching 7.3 million individuals despite challenges in funding and security.

  [^cite_note-plan2023-20]

- **2023 HRP:** In 2023, the number of people needing urgent assistance soared further, with 8.25 million people requiring humanitarian aid.

  [^cite_note-needs2023-15]

  &#x20;The plan for 2023 sought \$2.6 billion to respond to the needs, as famine risk remained high.

  [^cite_note-plan2023-20]

  &#x20;However, only 46% of the requested budget were eventually allocated.

  [^cite_note-21]
