A [Ground-Based Interceptor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ground-Based_Interceptor), designed to destroy incoming [Intercontinental ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile), is lowered into its [silo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_silo) at the missile defence complex at [Fort Greely](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Greely), Alaska, July 22, 2004.

The **militarisation of space** involved the placement and development of weaponry and military technology in [outer space](177602.md).[^cite_note-1][^cite_note-2][^cite_note-3][^cite_note-th-4] The early exploration of space in the mid-20th century had, in part, a military motivation, as the [United States](3434750.md) and the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) used it as an opportunity to demonstrate [ballistic-missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic-missile) technology and other technologies having the potential for military application.[^cite_note-5] Outer space has since been used as an operating location for [military spacecraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_satellite) such as [imaging](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imaging_satellite) and [communications satellites](45207.md), and some ballistic missiles pass through outer space during their flight. As of 2018, known deployments of weapons stationed in space only included the [Almaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almaz#Defense_measures) space-station armament, and [pistols](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pistol) such as the [TP-82 Cosmonaut survival pistol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TP-82_Cosmonaut_survival_pistol) (for post-landing, pre-recovery use). On September 14th 2026 the [United States Secretary of the Air Force](305210.md), [Troy Meink](78903908.md), announced at the annual Air, Space, and Cyber Conference that the United States has created and launched a "highly classified" weapon into orbit, becoming the first nation to openly admit to possessing a functional weapon in space[^cite_note-6]. No further information was given regarding the weapons function or when exactly it was put into orbit.

## History

### The Cold War

Main article: [Cold War](325329.md)

During the Cold War, the world's two great superpowers—the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and the [United States of America](3434750.md)—spent large proportions of their [GDP](12594.md) on developing military technologies. The drive to place objects in orbit stimulated space research and started the [Space Race](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Race). In 1957, the USSR launched the first [artificial satellite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_satellite), *[Sputnik 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sputnik_1)*.[^cite_note-7][^cite_note-8]

By the end of the 1960s, both countries regularly deployed satellites. [Reconnaissance satellites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reconnaissance_satellite) were used by militaries to take accurate pictures of their rivals' military installations. As time passed, the resolution and accuracy of orbital reconnaissance alarmed both sides of the [Iron Curtain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron_Curtain). Both the United States and the Soviet Union began to develop [anti-satellite weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-satellite_weapon) to blind or destroy each other's satellites. [Directed-energy weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Directed-energy_weapon), [kamikaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamikaze)-style satellites, as well as orbital nuclear explosives were researched with varying levels of success. Spy satellites were, and continue to be, used to monitor the dismantling of military assets by arms control treaties signed between the two superpowers. To use spy satellites in such a manner is often referred to in treaties as "national technical means of verification".[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10][^cite_note-11]

The superpowers developed [ballistic missiles](38025.md) to enable them to use [nuclear weaponry](21785.md) across great distances. As rocket science developed, the range of missiles increased and [intercontinental ballistic missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) (ICBM) were created, which could strike virtually any target on Earth in a timeframe measured in minutes rather than hours or days. To cover large distances ballistic missiles are usually launched into [sub-orbital spaceflight](455295.md).[^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13]



Test of the [LG-118A Peacekeeper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG-118A_Peacekeeper) missile, each one of which could carry 10 independently targeted [nuclear warheads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_warhead) along trajectories outside of the [Earth's atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Earth's_atmosphere)

As soon as intercontinental missiles were developed, military planners began programmes and strategies to counter their effectiveness.[^cite_note-14][^cite_note-15]

#### United States

Main articles: [Strategic Defense Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defense_Initiative), [Project Nike § Nike Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Nike#Nike_Zeus), [Project Defender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Defender?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Sentinel Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentinel_Program?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Safeguard Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safeguard_Program), [Project A119](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_A119), [Project Horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Horizon), and [Lunex Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunex_Project)

Early American efforts included the [Nike-Zeus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Nike#Nike_Zeus) Program, [Project Defender](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_national_missile_defense#Project_Defender), the [Sentinel Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sentinel_program) and the [Safeguard Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safeguard_Program). The late 1950s Nike-Zeus programme involved firing Nike nuclear missiles against oncoming ICBMs, thus exploding nuclear warheads over the North Pole. This idea was soon scrapped and work began on Project Defender in 1958.[^cite_note-NYtimes-16] Project Defender attempted to destroy Soviet ICBMs at launch with satellite weapon systems, which orbited over Russia. This programme proved infeasible with the technology from that era.[^cite_note-NYtimes-16] Work then began on the Sentinel Program which used [anti-ballistic missiles](1791.md) (ABM) to shoot down incoming ICBMs.

In the late 1950s [United States Air Force](32090.md) considered detonating an [atomic bomb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atomic_bomb) on the [Moon](19331.md) to display U.S. superiority to the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) and the rest of the world ([Project A119](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_A119)). In 1959, a feasibility study of a possible military base on the Moon ([Project Horizon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Project_Horizon)) was conducted. In 1958, a plan for a 21-airman underground Air Force base on the Moon by 1968 was developed ([Lunex Project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lunex_Project)).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18][^cite_note-19]

The [Safeguard Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safeguard_Program) was deployed in the mid-1970s and was based on the Sentinel Program. Since the ABM treaty only allowed for the construction of a single ABM facility to protect either the nation's capital city or an ICBM field, the [Stanley R. Mickelsen Safeguard Complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stanley_R._Mickelsen_Safeguard_Complex) was constructed near [Nekoma, North Dakota](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nekoma,_North_Dakota) to protect the [Grand Forks ICBM facility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/321st_Missile_Wing_LGM-30_Minuteman_Missile_Launch_Sites).[^cite_note-20][^cite_note-21] Though it was only operational as an ABM facility for less than a year, the Perimeter Acquisition Radar (PAR), one of Safeguard's components, was still operational as of 2005. One major problem with the Safeguard Program, and past ABM systems, was that the interceptor missiles, though state-of-the-art, required nuclear warheads to destroy incoming ICBMs. Future ABMs will likely be more accurate and use [hit-to-kill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hit-to-kill) or conventional warheads to knock down incoming warheads. The technology involved in such systems was shaky at best, and deployment was limited by the [ABM treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ABM_treaty) of 1972.[^cite_note-22]

In 1983, American president [Ronald Reagan](25433.md) proposed the [Strategic Defense Initiative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Defense_Initiative) (SDI), a space-based system to protect the United States from attack by strategic nuclear missiles. The plan was ridiculed by some as unrealistic and expensive, and Dr. Carol Rosin nicknamed the policy "Star Wars", after [the popular science-fiction movie franchise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_Wars).[^cite_note-23][^cite_note-24] Astronomer [Carl Sagan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carl_Sagan) pointed out that in order to defeat SDI, the Soviet Union had only to build more missiles, allowing them to overcome the defence by sheer force of numbers. Proponents of SDI said the [strategy of technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategy_of_technology) would hasten the Soviet Union's downfall. According to this doctrine, Communist leaders were forced to either shift large portions of their GDP to counter SDI, or else watch as their expensive nuclear stockpiles were rendered obsolete.[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26]

[United States Space Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Command) (USSPACECOM), a [unified command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Combatant_Command) of the [United States military](32212.md), was created in 1985 to help institutionalise the use of [outer space](177602.md) by the United States Armed Forces. The Commander in Chief of U.S. Space Command (CINCUSSPACECOM), with headquarters at [Peterson Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peterson_Air_Force_Base), [Colorado](5399.md) was also the Commander in Chief of the bi-national U.S.-Canadian *[North American Aerospace Defense Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aerospace_Defense_Command)* (CINCNORAD), and for the majority of time during USSPACECOM's existence also the Commander of the U.S. Air Force major command [Air Force Space Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Space_Command). Military space operations coordinated by USSPACECOM proved to be very valuable for the U.S.-led coalition in the 1991 [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War).[^cite_note-27]

The U.S. military has relied on communications, intelligence, navigation, missile warning and weather satellite systems in areas of conflict since the early 1990s, including the Balkans, Southwest Asia and Afghanistan. Space systems are considered indispensable providers of tactical information to U.S. war-fighters.

As part of the ongoing initiative to transform the U.S. military, on 26 June 2002, Secretary of Defense [Donald Rumsfeld](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Rumsfeld) announced that U.S. Space Command would merge with [USSTRATCOM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USSTRATCOM). The UCP directed that [Unified Combatant Commands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Unified_Combatant_Command) be capped at ten, and with the formation of the new [United States Northern Command](1685700.md), one would have to be deactivated in order to maintain that level. Thus the USSPACECOM merger into USSTRATCOM.

On December 10, 2019, the [United States Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Force) was formed as the world's only independent space force, with 8600 military personnel[^cite_note-28] and 77 spacecraft.[^cite_note-29]

**Operation Hardtack I**

[Operation Hardtack I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Hardtack_I) was a series of nuclear tests carried out by the United States Government in 1958. A major facet of these tests was three high-altitude nuclear tests: YUCCA, ORANGE, and TEAK. YUCCA was detonated April 28 at an altitude of 86,000 feet and had a comparatively small yield of 1.7 kilotons. YUCCA is notable as the first nuclear test carried via balloon. Following tests ORANGE and TEAK were carried out July 31 and August 11 at altitudes of 252,000 feet and 141,000 feet, respectively. The bombs were delivered via rocket and their yields were in the megaton range.[^cite_note-30]

##### Starfish Prime



Image of [Starfish Prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starfish_Prime) nuclear test in space (1962). Such tests in space and [high-altitudes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-altitude_nuclear_explosion) stopped completely with the [Partial Test Ban Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partial_Test_Ban_Treaty) (1963).

[Starfish Prime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Starfish_Prime) was a nuclear test carried out in 1962 over Johnston Atoll by the United States as part of Operation Fishbowl. The 1.4 megaton bomb was detonated at an altitude of 400 km (250 miles), in the ionosphere and was the highest altitude nuclear test ever demonstrated. The test is notable for its Electromagnetic Pulse (EMP) effect, which was felt as far as 1400 km (800 miles) away in Hawaii.[^cite_note-\:0-31]

#### USSR/Russia

Main articles: [Fractional Orbital Bombardment System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_Orbital_Bombardment_System) and [Almaz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Almaz)



[Polyus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyus_\(spacecraft\)) (1987)

The Soviet Union was also researching innovative ways of gaining space supremacy. Two of their most notable efforts were the [R-36ORB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R-36_\(missile\)#R-36ORB) [Fractional Orbital Bombardment System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fractional_Orbital_Bombardment_System) (FOBS) and [Polyus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyus_\(spacecraft\)) orbital weapons system.

The R-36ORB was a [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) [ICBM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intercontinental_ballistic_missile) in the 1960s that, once launched, would go into a [low Earth orbit](47568.md) whereupon it would de-orbit for an attack. This system would approach North America over the [South Pole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Pole), thereby striking targets from the opposite direction from that to which [NORAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Aerospace_Defense_Command) early warning systems are oriented. The missile was phased out in January 1983 in compliance with the [SALT II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SALT_II) treaty.

The [SALT II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SALT_II) treaty (1979) prohibited the deployment of FOBS systems:

*&#x20;Each Party undertakes not to develop, test, or deploy:&#x20;*

*(...)*

*(c) systems for placing into Earth orbit [nuclear weapons](21785.md) or any other kind of [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_of_mass_destruction), including fractional orbital missiles;*

On May 15, 1987, an [Energia rocket](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energia_\(rocket\)) flew for the first time. The payload was a prototype orbital weapons platform [Polyus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polyus_\(spacecraft\)) (also known as Polus, Skif-DM or 17F19DM), the final version of which according to some reports could be armed with nuclear space mines and defensive cannon. The Polyus weapons platform was designed to defend itself against anti-satellite weapons with recoilless cannon. It was also equipped with a sensor blinding laser to confuse approaching weapons and could launch test targets to validate the fire control system. The attempt to place the satellite into orbit failed.

The [Russian Space Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Space_Forces) was the first independent space force, formed in 1992, independent from 1992 to 1997 and 2001 to 2011, however it currently now part of the [Russian Aerospace Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Aerospace_Forces).

##### Soviet high-altitude nuclear tests

The Soviet Union executed their own high-altitude tests for the purpose of studying and developing High-Altitude Electromagnetic Pulse (HEMP) weapons. The most notable of these is the 1962 Nuclear Test 184 in which a nuclear bomb was detonated at an altitude of 290 km.[^cite_note-32] The ensuing HEMP damaged a 1000 km long line in Kazakhstan which was designed to be protected from such damage. The electrical damage is comparable to the strongest naturally occurring geomagnetic disturbances recorded.[^cite_note-33]

### Post-Cold War



A [Lightweight Exo-Atmospheric Projectile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightweight_Exo-Atmospheric_Projectile) (LEAP), which attaches to a modified SM-2 Block IV missile used by the [U.S. Navy](20518076.md)

As the Cold War ended with the implosion of the Soviet Union the space race between the two superpowers ended. The United States of America was left as the only superpower on Earth with a large concentration of the world's wealth and technological advancement. Despite the United States' new status in the world, the monopoly of space militarisation is in no way certain. Countries such as [China](5405.md), [Japan](15573.md), and [India](14533.md) have begun their own space programmes, while the [European Union](9317.md) collectively works to create satellite systems to rival those of the United States.

The USSR Space Forces were established as the Ministry of Defense Space Units in 1982. In 1991, the Soviet Union disintegrated. The [Russian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armed_Forces_of_the_Russian_Federation) were established on 7 May 1992, enabling the creation of [Russian Space Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Space_Forces) later that year on 10 August. In July 1997, the Space Force was dissolved as a separate service arm and incorporated to the [Strategic Rocket Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strategic_Rocket_Forces) along with the Space Missile Defense Forces, which previously were part of the [Troops of Air Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Air_Defense_Forces). The Russian Space Forces were officially reborn on June 1, 2001, as an independent section of the Russian military.

Post Cold War space militarisation seems to revolve around three types of applications. (The word "seems" is used because much of this subject matter is inconclusively verifiable, due to the high level of secrecy that exists among the [great powers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_powers) with regard to the details of space sensing systems.) The first application is the continuing development of "spy" or reconnaissance satellites which began in the Cold War era, but has progressed significantly since that time. Spy satellites perform a variety of missions such as high-resolution photography ([IMINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IMINT)) and communications eavesdropping ([SIGINT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SIGINT)). These tasks are performed on a regular basis both during peacetime and war operations. Satellites are also used by the nuclear states to provide early warning of missile launches, locate nuclear detonations, and detect preparations for otherwise clandestine or surprise nuclear tests (at least those tests or preparations carried out above-ground); this was the case when, in 1998, [India](14533.md) and [Pakistan](23235.md) both conducted a series of nuclear tests; in addition, a nuclear-detection satellite of the [Vela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vela_\(satellite\)) type was also reported to have detected a nuclear detonation in the [Indian Ocean](14580.md) in 1978 that was believed to be a [South African](17416221.md) nuclear test in what was famously called the [Vela incident](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vela_incident). Early-warning satellites can also be used to detect tactical missile launches; this capability was used during [Desert Storm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Storm), when America was able to provide advance warning to [Israel](9282173.md) of Iraqi [SS-1 SCUD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SS-1_SCUD) missile launches.

#### Global Positioning System (GPS)

Main articles: [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System), [GLONASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLONASS), [Galileo (satellite navigation)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_\(satellite_navigation\)), [BeiDou Navigation Satellite System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BeiDou_Navigation_Satellite_System), and [Indian Regional Navigation Satellite System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_Regional_Navigation_Satellite_System)



Artist's conception of a [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System) satellite in Earth orbit

The second application of space militarisation currently in use is GPS or [Global Positioning System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Positioning_System). This [satellite navigation system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_navigation_system) is used for determining one's precise location and providing a highly accurate [time reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Time_transfer) almost anywhere on [Earth](9228.md) or in [Earth orbit](568972.md). It uses an [intermediate circular orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Intermediate_circular_orbit) (ICO) [satellite constellation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Satellite_constellation) of at least 24 satellites. The GPS system was designed by and is controlled by the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) and can be used by anyone, free of charge. The cost of maintaining the system is approximately US\$400 million per year, including the replacement of ageing satellites. The first of 24 satellites that form the current GPS constellation (Block II) was placed into orbit on February 14, 1989. The 52nd GPS satellite since the beginning in 1978 was launched November 6, 2004 aboard a [Delta II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Delta_II) rocket. The primary military purposes are to allow improved command and control of forces through improved location awareness, and to facilitate accurate targeting of [smart bombs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Smart_bombs), [cruise missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cruise_missiles), or other munitions, and spoofing or jamming location data to civilian navigation receivers during wartime. The satellites also carry nuclear detonation detectors, which form a major portion of the [United States Nuclear Detonation Detection System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Nuclear_Detonation_Detection_System). [European](9239.md) concern about the level of control over the GPS network and commercial issues has resulted in the planned [Galileo positioning system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galileo_positioning_system). Russia already operates an independent system called [GLONASS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/GLONASS) (global navigation system); the system operates with 24 satellites that are deployed in 3 orbital planes as opposed to the 4 in which GPS is deployed.[^cite_note-34] The Chinese "Beidou" system provides China a similar regional (not global) navigation capability.

#### Military communication systems

The third current application of militarisation of space can be demonstrated by the emerging military doctrine of [network-centric warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Network-centric_warfare). Network-centric warfare relies heavily on the use of high-speed communications, which allows all soldiers and branches of the military to view the battlefield in real-time. Real-time technology improves the [situational awareness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Situational_awareness) of all of the military's assets and commanders in a given theatre. For example, a [soldier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soldier) in the battle zone can access satellite imagery of enemy positions two blocks away, and if necessary [e-mail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/E-mail) the coordinates to a bomber or [weapon platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon_platform) hovering overhead while the commander, hundreds of miles away, watches as the events unfold on a monitor. This high-speed communication is facilitated by a separate [internet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internet) created by the military for the military.[^cite_note-35][^cite_note-36][^cite_note-37] Communication satellites hold this system together by creating an informational grid over the given theatre of operations. The [Department of Defense](7279897.md) is currently working to establish a [Global Information Grid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Global_Information_Grid) to connect all military units and branches into a computerised network in order to share information and create a more efficient military.

#### Military spaceplanes

Main article: [spaceplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceplane)

It was revealed that Soviet officials were concerned that the US [Space Shuttle program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_Shuttle_program) had such military objectives such as to make a sudden dive into the atmosphere to drop bombs on Moscow.[^cite_note-38] Although it is a popular myth that these concerns were part of the motivation behind pursuing their own [Buran programme](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Buran_program),[^cite_note-BBC_Buran-39] the actual study concerning the potential for US Space Shuttles to launch nuclear munitions into Soviet territory was released after the Buran program had already been approved.[^cite_note-40]

The NASA uncrewed [spaceplane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaceplane) project [X-37](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Boeing_X-37) was transferred to the US Department of Defense in 2004. It is unclear what its military mission is, although speculation ranges from the testing of experimental [reconnaissance](146911.md) and spy sensors and how they hold up against [radiation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_radiation) and other hazards of orbit. [The Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon) has denied claims that the X-37 has been, or will be, used in the development or testing of space-based weapons.[^cite_note-41] The USAF has confirmed that Hall thruster electric propulsion tests have been carried out using the X-37, utilizing Aerojet Rocketdyne's AEHF satellites' Hall thrusters.[^cite_note-42] These thrusters are 4.5 kilowatt units that utilize electricity and xenon to produce thrust by ionizing and accelerating xenon gas particles. The X-37 is akin to a space version of [Unmanned aerial vehicle](58900.md).

## Weapons in space

Main article: [Space weapon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_weapon)



Triple-barreled [TP-82 Cosmonaut survival pistol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TP-82_Cosmonaut_survival_pistol) in Saint-Petersburg Artillery museum

Space weapons are [weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon) used in [space warfare](956982.md). They include weapons that can attack space systems in orbit (i.e. [anti-satellite weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-satellite_weapon)), attack targets on the earth from space or disable missiles travelling through space.[^cite_note-th-4] In the course of the militarisation of space, such weapons were developed mainly by the contesting [superpowers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superpower) during the [Cold War](325329.md), and some remain under development today. Space weapons are also a central theme in [military science fiction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_science_fiction) and sci-fi [video games](5363.md).

### Terrestrial-type weapons in space

The Soviet space station [Salyut 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salyut_3) was fitted with a 23mm cannon, which was successfully test fired at target satellites, at ranges from 500 to 3,000 metres (1,600 to 9,800 ft).[^cite_note-RSW-43][^cite_note-Olberg-44][^cite_note-45]

As of 2008, it was reported that Russian cosmonauts have regularly carried the [TP-82 Cosmonaut survival pistol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/TP-82_Cosmonaut_survival_pistol) on Soyuz spacecraft, as part of the emergency landing survival kit. The intent of the weapon is to protect cosmonauts from wild animals in the event of an off-course wilderness landing. The specially designed gun is capable of firing bullets, shotgun shells, or flares.[^cite_note-46]

### High-Altitude Electromagnetic Pulses (HEMP)

A high-altitude electromagnetic pulse is a result of an atmospheric nuclear explosion, as demonstrated by the United States' Starfish Prime and the Soviet Union's Nuclear Test 184. Though such explosions lack the usual damage caused by nuclear explosions such as physical damage and radioactive fallout, ensuing HEMPs have far-reaching effects on unprotected electronics.

The 1962 Starfish Prime test produced an HEMP which caused electronics to fail 1400 km (800) away in Hawaii where about 300 streetlights immediately failed. Soviet tests with HEMPs were executed above land, where a 1000 km power line was shut down, and all telephone lines within 500 km were damaged.[^cite_note-\:0-31]

HEMPs cause banana-shaped areas of effect, due to the pulse's interaction with the Earth's magnetic field.[^cite_note-47] A nuclear weapon detonated at 400 km creates an EMP 2,200 km in radius, large enough to cover the continental United States. However, any nuclear device detonated above 30 km will create an EMP of at least 600 km in radius.[^cite_note-48]

## Space warfare

Main article: [Space warfare](956982.md)



A [USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF) [F-15 Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15_Eagle) launching an [ASM-135 ASAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ASM-135_ASAT) [anti-satellite missile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-satellite_missile)

Space warfare is combat that takes place in [outer space](177602.md), i.e. outside the [atmosphere](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmosphere). Technically, it refers to [battles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle) where the targets themselves are in space. Space warfare therefore includes *ground-to-space warfare*, such as attacking [satellites](27683.md) from the Earth, as well as *space-to-space warfare*, such as satellites attacking satellites.

A film was produced by the [U.S. Military](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Military) in the early 1960s called *Space and National Security* which depicted space warfare.[^cite_note-49] From 1985 to 2002 there was a [United States Space Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Space_Command), which in 2002 merged with the [United States Strategic Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Strategic_Command). There is a [Russian Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_Space_Forces_\(VKS\)), which was established on August 10, 1992 and was the first independent space force in the world.[^cite_note-nwcr_flightops-50]

Only a few incidents of space warfare have occurred in world history, and all were training missions, as opposed to actions against real opposing forces. In the mid-1980s a [USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF) pilot in an [F-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-15_Eagle) successfully shot down the [P78-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/P78-1), a communications satellite in a 345-mile (555 km) orbit.

In 2007, the [People's Republic of China](5405.md) used a missile system to destroy one of its obsolete satellites (see [2007 Chinese anti-satellite missile test](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2007_Chinese_anti-satellite_missile_test)), in 2008 the [United States](3434750.md) similarly destroyed its malfunctioning satellite [USA 193](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USA_193). In 2019, [India](14533.md) destroyed a live satellite.[^cite_note-51][^cite_note-52] and on 15 November 2021, [Kosmos 1408](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosmos_1408), an old Soviet satellite was destroyed by the Russian military using a ground based missile. To date, there have been no human casualties resulting from conflict in space, nor has any ground target been successfully neutralised from orbit.

International treaties governing space limit or regulate conflicts in space and limit the installation of weapon systems, especially [nuclear weapons](21785.md).

The [Air Force Secretary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_Force_Secretary), [Troy Meink](78903908.md), disclosed at an [Air & Space Forces Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_&_Space_Forces_Association) conference on September 14, 2026 that the [United States](3434750.md) had already deployed via the [U.S. Space Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Space_Force) active "[on-orbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_orbits#Common_abbreviations) space control [weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_weapon) capable of defending the [joint force](32212.md) against hostile adversary action".[^cite_note-AFA_Meink_2026-09-14-53]

## Space treaties

Treaties are agreed to when all parties perceive a benefit from becoming a signatory participant in the treaty. As [mutually assured destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mutually_assured_destruction) (MAD) became the deterrent strategy between the two superpowers in the Cold War, many countries worked together to avoid extending the threat of nuclear weapons to space based launchers.

### Outer Space Treaty

Main article: [Outer Space Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Outer_Space_Treaty)

The Outer Space Treaty was considered by the Legal Subcommittee of the [United Nations Committee on the Peaceful Uses of Outer Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Committee_on_the_Peaceful_Uses_of_Outer_Space) in 1966. Later that year, agreement was reached in the [United Nations General Assembly](31957.md). The treaty included the following principles:

- the exploration and use of outer space shall be carried out for the benefit and in the interests of all countries and shall be the province of all mankind;

- outer space shall be free for exploration and use by all States;

- outer space is not subject to national appropriation by claim of sovereignty, by means of use or occupation, or by any other means;

- States shall not place nuclear weapons or other weapons of mass destruction in orbit or on celestial bodies or station them in outer space in any other manner;

- the Moon and other celestial bodies shall be used exclusively for peaceful purposes;

- Astronauts shall be regarded as the envoys of mankind;

- States shall be responsible for national space activities whether carried out by governmental or non-governmental activities;

- States shall be liable for damage caused by their space objects; and

- States shall avoid harmful contamination of space and celestial bodies.

In summary, the treaty initiated the banning of signatories' placing of [nuclear weapons](21785.md) or any other [weapons of mass destruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapons_of_mass_destruction) in orbit of [Earth](9228.md), installing them on the [Moon](19331.md) or any other [celestial body](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celestial_body), or to otherwise station them in [outer space](177602.md). The United States, the [United Kingdom](31717.md), and the Soviet Union signed the treaty and it entered into effect on October 10, 1967. As of January 1, 2005, 98 States have ratified, and an additional 27 have signed the Outer Space Treaty.

Note that this treaty does not ban the placement of weapons in space in general, only nuclear weapons and WMD.

### Moon Treaty

Main article: [Moon Treaty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moon_Treaty)

The Moon Treaty (not ratified by any space capable state, though signed by some) bans any military use of celestial bodies, including weapon testing, nuclear weapons in orbit, or military bases. The use of military personnel for scientific research or for any other peaceful purposes shall not be prohibited. (Article 3.4)

### Limited Test Ban Treaty

In 1963 the Limited Test Ban Treaty was signed by the United States, the United Kingdom, and the Soviet Union. The treaty was a response to growing concerns over the rapidly increasing power of nuclear weapons as well as damage from radioactive fallout. The treaty banned underwater tests and atmospheric tests, and effectively banned underground nuclear tests. The treaty put an end to further testing of high-altitude nuclear tests, and by extension HEMPs.[^cite_note-54]

### PAROS & PPTW

See also: [Conference on Disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_on_Disarmament)

The [Prevention of an Arms Race in Outer Space](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prevention_of_an_Arms_Race_in_Outer_Space) (PAROS) is a committee subsidiary to the [Conference on Disarmament](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conference_on_Disarmament). The PAROS Cttee, which meets at the [Palais des Nations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palais_des_Nations) in [Geneva](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geneva), has been the forum for discussion of these issues since 1985.[^cite_note-NTI-55]

In February 2008, China and Russia together submitted a draft to the UN known as the [Treaty on Prevention of the Placement of Weapons in Outer Space and of the Threat or Use of Force against Outer Space Objects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_on_Prevention_of_the_Placement_of_Weapons_in_Outer_Space_and_of_the_Threat_or_Use_of_Force_against_Outer_Space_Objects) (PPWT).[^cite_note-dd14-56][^cite_note-ntiparos-57] The US opposed the draft treaty due to security concerns over its space assets despite the treaty explicitly affirming a State's inherent right of self-defence.[^cite_note-58]

On December 4, 2014, the General Assembly of the UN passed two resolutions on preventing an arms race in outer space:[^cite_note-un.org-59]

- The first resolution, *Prevention of an arms race in outer space*, "call\[s] on all States, in particular those with major space capabilities, to contribute actively to the peaceful use of outer space, prevent an arms race there, and refrain from actions contrary to that objective."

  [^cite_note-un.org-59]

  &#x20;There were 178 countries that voted in favour to none against, with 2 abstentions (Israel, United States).

  [^cite_note-un.org-59]

- The second resolution, *No first placement of weapons in outer space*, emphasises the prevention of an arms race in space and states that "other measures could contribute to ensuring that weapons were not placed in outer space."

  [^cite_note-un.org-59]

  &#x20;126 countries voted in favour to 4 against (Georgia, Israel, Ukraine, United States), with 46 abstentions (EU member States abstained on the resolution).

  [^cite_note-un.org-59]

## National Missile Defense (NMD)

Main article: [National Missile Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Missile_Defense)



The logo of the [Missile Defense Agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Missile_Defense_Agency)

With the fall of the Soviet Union and the end of the Cold War defense spending was reduced and space research was chiefly focused on peaceful research. American military research is focused on a more modest goal of preventing the United States from being subject to [nuclear blackmail](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuclear_blackmail) or nuclear terrorism by a [rogue state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rogue_state). This overlapped with militarization of space in the form of ballistic missile defense. Missile defense does not *station* weapons in space, but is designed to intercept incoming warheads at a very high altitude, which requires the interceptor to travel into space to achieve the intercept. These missiles can be land-based or sea-based, and most proposed programs use a mix of the two.

On 16 December 2002, US President [George W. Bush](3414021.md) signed National Security Presidential Directive which outlined a plan to begin deployment of operational ballistic missile defense systems by 2004. The following day, the US formally requested from the UK and [Denmark](76972.md) use of facilities in [RAF Fylingdales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Fylingdales), [England](9316.md) and [Thule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thule_Air_Base), [Greenland](12118.md), respectively, as a part of the NMD Program.[^cite_note-60] The administration continued to push the program, but received pushback from multiple fronts. Firstly, some scientists opposed the program and raised ethical objections. Secondly, some trial-and-error technical failures during development became highly publicized, though from a technical standpoint they were unsurprising and even expected. The projected cost of the program for the years 2004 to 2009 was 53 billion US dollars, making it the largest single line in [The Pentagon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Pentagon)'s budget.
