Parliamentary elections were held in [Kosovo](17391.md) on 7 June 2026. The elections follow a constitutional crisis triggered by the Assembly's [failure to elect a president](67301945.md).[^cite_note-3]

After an unsuccessful vote on 5 March 2026, President [Vjosa Osmani](61979540.md) issued a decree dissolving the Assembly.[^cite_note-4] The decree was challenged by [Vetëvendosje](3225595.md) before the [Constitutional Court of Kosovo](26764195.md), which suspended it on 9 March.[^cite_note-5]

On 25 March, the Court ruled that the dissolution had no legal effect and gave the [Assembly](1068656.md) 34 days to elect a president.[^cite_note-6] After the Assembly failed to do so by 28 April, it was dissolved in accordance with the Court's decision, and acting president [Albulena Haxhiu](43297113.md) set 7 June 2026 as the date for new parliamentary elections.[^cite_note-7]

## Electoral system

The 120 members of the Assembly are elected by [open list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Open_list) [proportional representation](23298.md) for a four-year term, with 20 reserved for national minorities.[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-9] Seats are allocated using the [D'Hondt method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D'Hondt_method) with an [electoral threshold](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_threshold) of 5%.[^cite_note-10]

## Results

[Vetëvendosje](3225595.md) finished first in the parliamentary elections, securing its fifth consecutive victory and remaining the largest party by a significant margin. However, the party lost support compared to the [December 2025 election](81646979.md), while opposition parties made gains. With the backing of nine minority representatives, Vetëvendosje subsequently formed the [Fourth cabinet of Albin Kurti](84217976.md). Voter turnout was 37.03%, the lowest recorded in Kosovo’s electoral history, reflecting widespread political fatigue and frustration with repeated elections and ongoing political instability.
