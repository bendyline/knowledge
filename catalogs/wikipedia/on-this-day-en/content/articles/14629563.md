**Armando Alberto Benedetti Villaneda** (born 29 August 1967) is a Colombian social communicator, journalist, politician, and [diplomat](82433.md) who has served as [Minister of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_the_Interior_\(Colombia\)) since 2025. A member of the [Humane Colombia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humane_Colombia) party, he has also served as [Ambassador of Colombia to Venezuela](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_ambassadors_of_Colombia_to_Venezuela), Colombia's [Ambassador](68651.md) to the [FAO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FAO), and [Chief of Staff of the Casa de Nariño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_Casa_de_Nari%C3%B1o).

Born in [Barranquilla, Atlántico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla,_Atl%C3%A1ntico). He holds a degree in [Social Communication](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Communication) from the [Pontifical Xavierian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Xavierian_University). After serving as a [Councilor of Bogotá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bogot%C3%A1_City_Council) from 1998 to 2000, he was elected to the [Chamber of Representatives](645347.md) for Bogotá and later elected [Senator](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Senator_of_Colombia) in [2006](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Colombian_parliamentary_election), a position he held for sixteen years.

In 2022, he joined [Gustavo Petro 2022 presidential campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gustavo_Petro_2022_presidential_campaign). Due to his political influence within the [Petro administration](71877268.md), he is one of its most controversial members. In February 2024, he was confirmed as [Chief of Staff of the Casa de Nariño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chief_of_Staff_of_the_Casa_de_Nari%C3%B1o) and a week later as [Minister of the Interior](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_the_Interior_\(Colombia\)), replacing [Juan Fernando Cristo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Fernando_Cristo).

## Career

Benedetti attended the [Pontifical Xavierian University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontifical_Xavierian_University), and graduated with a degree in [communication studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Communication_studies).[^cite_note-visible-1] He briefly worked as a reporter for *Noticiero QAP* between 1990 and 1991,[^cite_note-terra-2] and later served as political adviser to [Eduardo Verano de la Rosa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eduardo_Verano_de_la_Rosa) during the [1991 Constituent Assembly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constituent_Assembly_of_Colombia).[^cite_note-terra-2] He served as Secretary General of Public Establishments of the National Traffic and Transport Institute (Intra) between 1992 and 1993,[^cite_note-terra-2] and was later appointed deputy director of the Colombia Health Resource Company (Ecosalud), serving from 1996 to 1997.[^cite_note-terra-2]

In 1998, Benedetti was elected to the Bogotá City Council as a [Liberal party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colombian_Liberal_Party) candidate, and head of his [electoral list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_list).[^cite_note-visible-1] He served as Council Member until 2000, when he decided not to seek re-election in order to run for Congress.

### Congressman

During the [2002 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2002_Colombian_parliamentary_election), Benedetti ran for a seat in the [Chamber of Representatives](645347.md) for the [circumscription](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electoral_district) of the [Capital District](211271.md) as a Liberal candidate and head of his electoral list.[^cite_note-camaraeleccion-3] He won with 31,855 votes.[^cite_note-camaraeleccion-3]

### Senator

In 2006, Benedetti decided to run in the [2006 parliamentary elections](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2006_Colombian_parliamentary_election), this time for a seat in the [Senate](645242.md), and this time as a [Social Party of National Unity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_Party_of_National_Unity) candidate.[^cite_note-senadorseis-4] He won a seat in the national elections with 50,356 votes.[^cite_note-senadorseis-4]

In 2010, Benedetti was re-elected to the Senate with 81,029 votes,[^cite_note-senadorseis2010-5] On 20 July 2010, Benedetti was elected by his peers [President of the Senate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/President_of_the_Senate_of_Colombia). As President of the Senate, Benedetti administered the [oath of office](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oath_of_office) to President [Juan Manuel Santos Calderón](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Manuel_Santos_Calder%C3%B3n) when he took office the following month on 7 August.[^cite_note-6]

### Ambassador

In August 2022, President [Petro](13391962.md) appointed Benedetti as Ambassador to Venezuela. On June 2, 2023, Ambassador Benedetti submitted a letter of resignation to President Petro after recordings of phone conversations surfaced in the media. In these, Benedetti threatened President Petro's Chief of Staff, Laura Sarabia, with releasing information regarding Petro's campaign financing that could, according to him, topple the government and send people to prison.[^cite_note-7] Sarabia, who got her start in politics as an aide to Benedetti, resigned as Chief of Staff on June 2, 2023.

## Personal life

Armando Alberto was born on 29 August 1967 in [Barranquilla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barranquilla), Colombia, to [Armando Benedetti Jimeno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armando_Benedetti_Jimeno?action=edit\&redlink=1) and Genoveva Villaneda Jiménez.[^cite_note-visible-1][^cite_note-terra-2][^cite_note-8][^cite_note-kienyke-9] His sister [Ángela María](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%81ngela_Mar%C3%ADa_Benedetti_Villaneda?action=edit\&redlink=1) is the current [Ambassador of Colombia to Panama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ambassador_of_Colombia_to_Panama?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-10]

## Sanctions

On October 24, 2025, the United States imposed sanctions against Benedetti, accusing him of involvement in drug-trafficking.[^cite_note-11]
