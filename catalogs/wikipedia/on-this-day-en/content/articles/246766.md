**Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol**[^cite_note-5] ([Italian](14708.md): *Trentino-Alto Adige* [\[trenˈtiːno ˈalto ˈaːdidʒe\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Italian); [Austrian German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_German_language): *Trentino-Südtirol*;[^cite_note-6] [Ladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladin_language): *Trentin-Südtirol*[^cite_note-7]), often known in English as **Trentino-South Tyrol**[^cite_note-8] or by its Italian name **Trentino-Alto Adige**,[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-13] is an [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy#Autonomous_regions_with_special_statute) of [Italy](14532.md), located in the [northern part](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Italy) of the country. The region has a population of nearly 1.1 million,[^cite_note-population-2] of whom 62% speak Italian as their mother tongue (in areas where the local languages are transition dialects between [Eastern Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Lombard) and [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_language)), 30% speak [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) (around 93% of whom are fluent in the local [South Tyrolean dialect](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tyrolean_dialect) of [Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_language)), and the remaining are minority speakers of [Ladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladin_language), [Mòcheno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B2cheno) or [Cimbrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimbrian_language) and immigrant communities speaking several foreign languages.[^cite_note-14] Since the 1970s, most legislative and administrative powers have been transferred to the two self-governing [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Italy) that make up the region: the province of [Trento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trento), commonly known as [Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino), and the province of [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano), commonly known as [South Tyrol](169611.md) (Italian: *Alto Adige*; German: *Südtirol*). In South Tyrol, [German remains the sizeable majority language](169611.md#Languages).

From the 9th century until 1806, the region was part of the [Holy Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire). After briefly being part of the newly-formed [Kingdom of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria) and the short-lived [Napoleonic Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(Napoleonic\)), the region was part of the [County of Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Tyrol) within the [Austrian Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austrian_Empire) and its successor [Austria-Hungary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria-Hungary) from 1815 until its 1919 transfer to Italy in the [Treaty of Saint-Germain-en-Laye](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint-Germain-en-Laye_\(1919\)) at the end of [World War I](4764461.md). Together with the Austrian state of [Tyrol](148994.md), it is part of the [Euroregion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euroregion) of [Tyrol-South Tyrol-Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrol-South_Tyrol-Trentino).

## History

The Romans conquered the region in 15 BC. After the end of the [Western Roman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Roman_Empire), it was divided between the [invading Germanic tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Migration_Period) in the [Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombards) [Duchy of Tridentum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Tridentum) (today's [Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino)), the [Alamannic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alamanni) [Vinschgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinschgau), and the [Bavarians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarians) (who took the remaining part). After the creation of the [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(medieval\)) under [Charlemagne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charlemagne), the [Marquisate of Verona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marquisate_of_Verona) included the areas south of [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano), while the [Duchy of Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duchy_of_Bavaria) received the remaining part.[^cite_note-15]

From the 11th century onwards, part of the region was governed by the [prince-bishops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Prince-Bishops_of_Trent) of [Trent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prince-Bishopric_of_Trent) and [Brixen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bishopric_of_Brixen), to whom the [Holy Roman Emperors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holy_Roman_Empire) had given extensive temporal powers over their bishoprics. Soon, they were overruled by the [Counts of Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Tyrol) and [Counts of Görz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Gorizia), who also controlled the [Puster Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puster_Valley): in 1363 its last titular countess, [Margarete of Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Margarete,_Countess_of_Tyrol), ceded the region to the [House of Habsburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_of_Habsburg). The regions north of [Salorno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salorno) were largely [Germanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germanisation) in the early [Middle Ages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_Ages), and important [German](152735.md) poets like [Arbeo of Freising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arbeo_of_Freising) and [Oswald von Wolkenstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oswald_von_Wolkenstein) were born and lived in the southern part of [Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_of_Tyrol).[^cite_note-16]

The two bishoprics were secularized ([mediatized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_mediatisation)) to the Habsburgs by the *[Reichsdeputationshauptschluss](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Reichsdeputationshauptschluss)* in 1803 and incorporated into Tyrol. Two years later, following the Austrian [defeat at Austerlitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Austerlitz), the region was given to Napoleon's ally [Bavaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Bavaria) ([Treaty of Pressburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Pressburg_\(1805\)), 1805). Under Bavarian rule the county was abolished and most of the modern region became part of the *[Eisack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisackkreis?action=edit\&redlink=1)* and *[Etsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etschkreis?action=edit\&redlink=1) Kreise* ("[circles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circle_\(administrative_division\))"), with a small part within the *[Innkreis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innkreis_\(Bavaria\)?action=edit\&redlink=1)*. The new rulers provoked a popular rebellion in 1809, led by [Andreas Hofer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andreas_Hofer), a landlord from [St. Leonhard in Passeier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Leonhard_in_Passeier); this rebellion was crushed the same year. At the resulting Treaty of Paris (28 February 1810), Bavaria ceded the southern part of Tyrol (Trentino and the city of Bolzano) to the Napoleonic [Kingdom of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy_\(Napoleonic\)).[^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18] Under French control this mostly became the *[Haut Adige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Alto_Adige)* department (Italian: "*Alto Adige*"; German: "*Hochetsch*"), literally "High [Adige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adige)", in order to avoid any reference to the former County of Tyrol.[^cite_note-contemporary1-19] After Napoleon's defeat in 1815, the region returned to Austria.

Under Austrian rule, the territory of today's province of South Tyrol was called *südliches Tirol* or *Deutschsüdtirol*,[^cite_note-20] but was occasionally also referred to as *Mitteltirol*, i.e., Middle Tyrol, due to its geographic position,[^cite_note-21] while *Südtirol* ([Italian](14708.md): *Tirolo meridionale*), i.e., South Tyrol, indicated mostly today's province of Trentino.[^cite_note-22] Trentino was also called *Welschtirol* ("[Romance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romance_languages) Tyrol", Italian: *Tirolo italiano*) or *Welschsüdtirol* ("Romance South Tyrol", Italian: *Tirolo meridionale italiano*). Sometimes *Südtirol* also indicated the whole of the Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol region.

During the First World War, major battles were fought high in the Alps and Dolomites between Austro-Hungarian [Kaiserjäger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaiserj%C3%A4ger) and Italian [Alpini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alpini), for whom control of the region was a key strategic objective. The collapse of the Austro-Hungarian war effort enabled Italian troops to occupy the region in 1918 and its annexation was confirmed in the post-war treaties, which awarded the region to Italy under the terms of the [Treaty of Saint-Germain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Saint-Germain).

Under the dictatorship of [Benito Mussolini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Benito_Mussolini), the Fascist dictator of Italy (ruled 1922–1943), the German population was subjected to an increased forced programme of [Italianization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italianization): all references to old Tyrol were banned and the region was referred to as *Venezia Tridentina* between 1919 and 1947, in an attempt to justify the Italian claims to the area by historically linking the region to one of the [Roman Regions of Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italia_\(Roman_Empire\)) (Regio X *[Venetia et Histria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetia_et_Histria)*).[^cite_note-23] [Hitler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hitler) and Mussolini agreed in 1938 that the German-speaking population would be transferred to German-ruled territory or dispersed around Italy, but the outbreak of the Second World War prevented them from fully carrying out the relocation. Nevertheless, thousands of people were relocated to [Nazi Germany](21212.md) and only with great difficulty managed to return to their ancestral land after the end of the war.[^cite_note-24]

In 1943, when the Italian government signed an [armistice with the Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Cassibile), the region was occupied by Germany, which reorganised it as the [Operation Zone of the Alpine Foothills](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Zone_of_the_Alpine_Foothills) and put it under the administration of [Gauleiter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gauleiter) [Franz Hofer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franz_Hofer). The region was *de facto* annexed to the [German Reich](21212.md) (with the addition of the [province of Belluno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Province_of_Belluno)) until the end of the war. This status ended along with the [Nazi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nazi) regime and Italian rule was restored in 1945.

Italy and Austria negotiated the [Gruber-De Gasperi Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruber-De_Gasperi_Agreement) in 1946, put into effect in 1947 when the new republican [Italian constitution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constitution_of_Italy) was promulgated, that the region would be granted considerable autonomy. This region was to be a combination of the two provinces (majority-Italian Trentino and the majority-German province, to be called Alto Adige rather than South Tyrol). In the region, German and Italian were both made official languages, and German-language education was permitted once more.

However, the implementation of the agreement was not seen as satisfactory by either the German-speaking population or the Austrian government. The issue became the cause of significant friction between the two countries and was taken up by the United Nations in 1960. A fresh round of negotiations took place in 1961 but proved unsuccessful, partly because of popular discontent and a campaign of terrorism and bombings by German-speaking autonomists and separatists led by the [South Tyrolean Liberation Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tyrolean_Liberation_Committee).[^cite_note-25][^cite_note-26][^cite_note-27]

The issue began to be resolved in 1971, when a new Austro-Italian treaty was signed and ratified. It stipulated that disputes in South Tyrol would be submitted for settlement to the [International Court of Justice](14918.md) in [The Hague](30269.md); that while staying in the two-province region, South Tyrol would receive greater autonomy within Italy (and apart from Trentino); and that Austria would not interfere in South Tyrol's internal affairs. The new agreement proved broadly satisfactory to the parties involved and the separatist tensions eased.

Thus, the official name of the province had transitioned from 1927/Fascist-era "Bozen", after the capital (Bolzano in Italian), to 1947/Post-WW2 "Alto Adige/Tiroler Etschland", after the river valley, to "Autonomous Province Bozen – South Tyrol" in 1972.[^cite_note-28] Technically, the dispute between Italy and Austria was not solved until much later, with the UN declaring the dispute over only in 1992.[^cite_note-29] Matters were helped further by Austria's accession to the [European Union](9317.md) in 1995, which has helped to improve cross-border cooperation.[^cite_note-contemporary1-19]

Moves toward further self-determination continue to grab headlines from time to time. In May 2006, former Italian president and then-senator-for-life [Francesco Cossiga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francesco_Cossiga) introduced a bill that would allow the region to hold a referendum, in which the local electorate could decide whether to stay within the Italian Republic, become fully independent or return to Austria.[^cite_note-30] Other, less official, votes have been proposed since then, including a 2013 "vote" of the public by the political party, South Tyrol Freedom, asking if voters support "exercising the right of self-determination in order to freely decide the future of South Tyrol," without specifying if that meant reunification with Austria or striking out as an independent nation.[^cite_note-nationalia-31] With about 15% of the electorate responding, the result was about 92% in favor of leaving Italy (which its constitution forbids).[^cite_note-32] In 2016, Italy held a referendum on constitutional changes in general, but the changes would have led to more centralization, not less, so separatists urged a "no" vote. At the same time, South Tyrol's moderates, who were in power, encouraged support, since "safeguards" allowing for future devolution also would have been part of the changes. In the end, the national referendum failed, but not because of South Tyrol, which voted 64% in favor.[^cite_note-nationalia-31]

## Geography



[Alpine](981.md) landscape near the village of [Stilfs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stilfs), South Tyrol



[Lake Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Garda) promenade in [Riva del Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riva_del_Garda), Trentino

The region is bordered by East and North [Tyrol](148994.md) (Austria) to the north-east and north respectively, by [Graubünden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graub%C3%BCnden) (Switzerland) to the north-west, and by the Italian regions of [Lombardy](43807.md) to the west and [Veneto](43780.md) to the south and southeast. It covers 13,607 km<sup>2</sup> (5,254 sq mi). It is extremely mountainous, covering a large part of the [Dolomites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomites) and the southern [Alps](981.md).

The region is composed of two provinces, Trentino in the south and South Tyrol in the north.

[Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino) has an area of 6,207 km<sup>2</sup> (2,397 sq mi), most of it mountainous land (20% is over 2,000 m (6,600 ft) and 70% over 1,000 m) and covered by vast forests (50% of the territory). The climate is various through the province, from an alpine climate to a subcontinental one, with warm and variable summers and cold and quite snowy winters. The region has always been a favourite destination for tourists, both in winter for skiing in the high mountains and in summer to visit the wide valleys and many lakes (the largest being [Lake Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Garda)).[^cite_note-33]

[South Tyrol](169611.md) has an area of 7,398 square kilometres (2,856 square miles), all of it mountainous land and covered by vast forests. The climate is of the continental type, owing to the influence of the many mountain ranges which stand at well over 3,000 metres (9,800 feet) [above sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Above_sea_level) and the wide valleys through which flow the main river, the [Adige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adige), from north to south and its numerous tributaries. In the city of [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano), capital of the province, the average air temperature stands at 12.2 °C (54.0 °F) and the average rainfall at 717.7 mm (28.3 in). The lowest pass across the Alps, the [Brenner Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenner_Pass), is located at the far north of the region on the [border with Austria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria%E2%80%93Italy_border).[^cite_note-regportraits1-34]

## Politics

Main article: [Politics of Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Politics_of_Trentino-Alto_Adige/S%C3%BCdtirol)



The provincial assembly building of [South Tyrol](169611.md)

The region is divided into two autonomous [provinces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_Italy): Trentino (Autonomous Province of Trento) and South Tyrol (Autonomous Province of Bolzano). The Italian Republic recognised a certain degree of autonomy for the region and its two constituent provinces, which was the result of the [Gruber–De Gasperi Agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gruber%E2%80%93De_Gasperi_Agreement) of 1946, as well as of the special status of autonomy approved by constitutional law in 1948. This statute gave the region the right to initiate its own laws on a wide range of subjects and to carry out respective administrative functions.

In 1972, the introduction of the second Statute of Autonomy, which was in the centre of the discussions between the Italian and Austrian governments, meant the transfer of the main competencies from the region to the two provinces. The autonomy recognized by the special statute covers the political, legislative, administrative, and fiscal institutions. The second statute turned the region *de facto* into a loose [commonwealth](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commonwealth) with [devolved](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Devolution) powers to the two autonomous provinces, with very limited legislative or executive competencies left.

&#x20;The capital city is Trento, although the two provincial capitals alternate biennially (the other being [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano)) as the site of the regional assembly.[^cite_note-regportraits1-34]

As of 2026, the population is 1,090,818, of which 49.6% are male, and 50.4% are female. Minors make up 16.8% of the population, and seniors make up 23.0%.[^cite_note-population-2]

The population density in the region is low compared to Italy as a whole, with 80.2 inhabitants per square kilometer, whereas the national density is 195.1 per square kilometer. The population density in the province of Trento was 88.4 per square kilometer, higher than the figure of South Tyrol at 73.3.[^cite_note-population-2][^cite_note-area-1]

The main language groups are Italian and German, with small minorities speaking [Ladin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladin_language), [Lombard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lombard_language) (namely the [Eastern subgroup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Lombard_dialects)), [Venetian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venetian_language), [Mòcheno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B2cheno_language) and [Cimbrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimbrian_language).[^cite_note-37] The latter two of which are varieties of the [Upper German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upper_German) [Bavarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bavarian_language) dialect.[^cite_note-38]

In Trentino, the majority language is Italian, although there are Cimbrian minorities in the municipality of [Luserna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luserna) and four Mòcheno municipalities in the [Mòcheni Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valle_dei_M%C3%B2cheni). There are also Ladin-speaking minorities living in the [Fassa Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fassa_Valley) and in [Non Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non_Valley) (3.5% of the population). While in the Fassa Valley, Ladin already enjoys official status, in the Non Valley, it still does not, despite there being more Ladin speakers in the latter than in the former. [Sole Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sole_Valley) also historically belongs to the Ladin area.

In South Tyrol, the majority language is German (62% of the population). Many of the German speakers use [South Tyrolean dialects](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Tyrolean_dialect) in informal settings. In South Tyrol's capital city [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano), 73% of the population speaks Italian as their maternal language due to internal immigration from other regions of Italy.[^cite_note-39] Italian speakers are also a significant component in other major urban centres of the province, such as in [Merano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merano) (49% Italian as the mother language) and [Brixen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brixen) (26% mother language).[^cite_note-40] More than 90% of the 120,000 Italian speakers live in Bozen/Bolzano, Merano, Leifers and Brixen, and the greater part of the rest in the small towns south of the capital, just north of the border with Trentino or scattered about in very small numbers throughout the rest of the province. The Italian language is the majority in 5 of 116 municipalities. Italian is the first language of 26% of the population (down from 35% in 1960) of the population of 453,000 recorded in the 2011 census, not counting the 51,000 who listed Language as "Other" who are immigrants. Ladin is the additional official language in some municipalities and the majority in 8. According to the census of 2001, 103 out of 116 communes have a majority of German native speakers, eight of Ladin speakers and five of Italian. Today both German and Italian have the status of co-[official languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Official_languages) in South Tyrol.

### Immigration

As of 2025, immigrants make up 14.2% of the population, the 6th-highest proportion out of the Italian regions. The 5 largest foreign countries of birth are [Albania](738.md), [Romania](25445.md), [Morocco](19291.md), [Germany](11867.md), and [Pakistan](23235.md).[^cite_note-foreignborn-41]

## Economy

The region's fertile valleys produce [wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Italian_DOC_wines), fruit, dairy products, and timber, while its industries include paper, chemical and metal production. The region is a major exporter of [hydroelectric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hydroelectric) power. The most important features of the region's economic structure are the strength of tourism and the special system of co-operation between agriculture and industry. In the last decade, tourism became a very important component of the province's economy. The region, which is a staging-post between the countries of northern Europe and central and southern Italy, has found its true vocation in this leading branch of the services sector with all its spin-offs. The region has a higher concentration of hotels than any other region (6,178 establishments in 2001 with 236,864 hotel beds). The total accommodation capacity of the region counts for 651,426 beds available in hotels and other establishments.[^cite_note-42]

Because of its unique history and location within the southern [Alps](981.md) and [Dolomites](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dolomites), in this region grows a wide range of [grape varieties](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grape_varieties) that are not usually seen in other parts of Italy. These include [Müller-Thurgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%BCller-Thurgau), [Vernatsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vernatsch), [Lagrein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrein), [Sylvaner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sylvaner), [Riesling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riesling) (known in Italian as *Riesling Renano*), [Gewürztraminer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gew%C3%BCrztraminer) (known in Italian as *Traminer Aromatico*)[^cite_note-Saunders-43] and [Blatterle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blatterle).[^cite_note-44] [Winemaking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Winemaking) in [Tyrol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tyrol) has a long tradition: the first evidence dates back to the period before the Romans. The South Tyrolean winegrowing area is highly influenced by the [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate), which in the Adige Valley ([Überetsch-Unterland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cberetsch-Unterland), [Überetsch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C3%9Cberetsch), [Bozen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bozen), [Terlan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terlan), [Burggrafenamt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burggrafenamt)) arrives up to [Meran](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Meran). This allows a very versatile winemaking, which includes almost all the red grape varieties and a lot of white grape wines. The [Vinschgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinschgau) and the [Eisacktal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisacktal) have a harsher climate and thus they're specialized in white wines. In South Tyrol there are three [indigenous](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indigenous_\(ecology\)) varieties: [Schiava](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schiava), [Gewürztraminer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gew%C3%BCrztraminer) and [Lagrein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lagrein). A similar winegrowing region is [Trentino wine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino_wine) in the south.

[Christmas in Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_in_Italy) begins on 8 December with the [Feast of the Immaculate Conception](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Feast_of_the_Immaculate_Conception), the day on which traditionally Christmas trees are erected, and ends on 6 January of the following year with [Epiphany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Epiphany_\(holiday\)).[^cite_note-italy-45] In Italy, the oldest Christmas market is considered to be that of [Bologna](21069333.md), held for the first time in the 18th century and linked to the feast of Saint Lucia.[^cite_note-Mercatini_Italia-46] The tradition of the markets has however spread in Italy predominantly especially since the 1990s, with the birth of the first modern markets: among these, the first ever was that of [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano), born in 1991, which was followed by others in South Tyrol,[^cite_note-Mercatino_Bolzano-47] in particular in [Merano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Merano), [Brixen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brixen), [Sterzing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sterzing) and [Bruneck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bruneck).[^cite_note-48] The [Trento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trento) Christmas market, established in 1993, is renowned in [Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino).[^cite_note-49]

## Transport



[Autostrada A22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A22_\(Italy\)) from [Verona](263991.md) to [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano)

[Strada statale 12 dell'Abetone e del Brennero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strada_statale_12_dell'Abetone_e_del_Brennero) in [Brenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenner,_South_Tyrol)



[Trento-Mattarello Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trento-Mattarello_Airport)

The infrastructure system of Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol is made up of railway, airport, motorway, road and lake lines.

The main railway line consists of the [Brenner Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenner_Railway) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Brennerbahn*; [Italian](14708.md): *Ferrovia del Brennero*), which runs along the [Adige Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adige_Valley) from [Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino) to [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano), and through the [Isarco Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisacktal) at the end of the border with [Austria](26964606.md) ([Brenner Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenner_Pass)). The [Trento–Venice railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trento%E2%80%93Venice_railway), the [Trento–Malè–Mezzana railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trento%E2%80%93Mal%C3%A8%E2%80%93Mezzana_railway), the [Puster Valley Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puster_Valley_Railway) are minor. The Brenner Railway is a major line connecting the [Austrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rail_transport_in_Austria) and [Italian railways](1273377.md) from [Innsbruck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Innsbruck) to [Verona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Verona_Porta_Nuova_railway_station), climbing up the [Wipptal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wipptal) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) for "Wipp Valley"), passing over the [Brenner Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenner_Pass), descending down the [Eisacktal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eisacktal) ([German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language) for "Eisack Valley") to [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano), then further down the [Adige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Adige) Valley to [Rovereto](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rovereto), and along the section of the Adige Valley, called in [Italian](14708.md) the "Vallagarina", to Verona. This railway line is part of the [Line 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berlin%E2%80%93Palermo_railway_axis) of [Trans-European Transport Networks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trans-European_Transport_Networks) (TEN-T). It is considered a "fundamental" line by the state railways [Ferrovie dello Stato](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrovie_dello_Stato) (FS).[^cite_note-53]

The Trentino-Alto Adige airport service is divided into the 3 large airports: [Trento-Mattarello Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trento-Mattarello_Airport), [Bolzano Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano_Airport) and [Dobbiaco Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toblach_Airport). Trento-Mattarello Airport ([Italian](14708.md): *Aeroporto di Trento-Mattarello*,[^cite_note-54] also known as *Aeroporto G.Caproni*, is an [airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airfield) located at *[Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mattarello)*, 3.4 [NM](21289.md) (6.3 km; 3.9 mi) south[^cite_note-AIP-55] of [Trento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trento), Italy. The airport is at an elevation of 610 feet (186 m) above [mean sea level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mean_sea_level). It has one [runway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Runway) designated 18/36 with an [asphalt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asphalt_concrete) surface measuring 1,130 by 30 metres (3,707 ft × 98 ft).[^cite_note-AIP-55] Bolzano Airport ([Italian](14708.md): *Aeroporto di Bolzano — Dolomiti*, [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Flughafen Bozen — Dolomiten*) is a [regional airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regional_airport) near [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano) in the province of [South Tyrol](169611.md) in northern [Italy](14532.md). The airport was established in October 1926 with a 1,300-metre (4,300 ft) landing runway.[^cite_note-56] The Dobbiaco Airport lies approximately 1 km to the South of the village of [Dobbiaco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dobbiaco) and is Italy's northernmost and highest airport. The military airfield has a 700 m long and 50 m wide grass runway and is managed by the [Italian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Air_Force)'s Airport Detachment Toblach. On weekends and holidays from May to October, the airport is open for civilian traffic.

The region is crossed by a single motorway, on the [Autostrada A22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A22_\(Italy\)). The Autostrada A22 or Autobrennero or Autostrada del Brennero ("Brenner motorway"; [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Brennerautobahn*) is one of the most important *[autostrada](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrade_of_Italy)* ([Italian](14708.md) for "[motorway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorway)") in [Italy](14532.md), as it connects [Po Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Po_Valley), the city of [Modena](62418.md) and the [Autostrada A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_A1_\(Italy\)) to [Austria](26964606.md) through the [Brenner Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenner_Pass), located in the municipality of [Brenner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenner,_South_Tyrol). The Autostrada A22 is 315 kilometres (196 mi) long. It is a part of the [E45](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_route_E45) European route. The operator of the road is [Autostrada del Brennero S.p.A.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autostrada_del_Brennero_\(company\)). The Autostrada A22 is located in the [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) of [Emilia-Romagna](162715.md), [Lombardy](43807.md), [Veneto](43780.md) and Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol.

Another important artery of the Trentino-Alto Adige road is the [Strada statale 12 dell'Abetone e del Brennero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strada_statale_12_dell'Abetone_e_del_Brennero), which runs parallel to the Autostrada A22, and the [Strada statale 38 dello Stelvio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Strada_statale_38_dello_Stelvio), which links Bolzano to Merano. Strada statale 12 dell'Abetone e del Brennero (SS 12) is an [Italian state highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_highway_\(Italy\)) 523.6 kilometres (325.3 mi) long in [Italy](14532.md) located in the [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) of [Tuscany](21967242.md), [Emilia-Romagna](162715.md), [Lombardy](43807.md), [Veneto](43780.md) and Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol that connects [Pisa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pisa) to the [Austrian border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Austria%E2%80%93Italy_border) at [Brenner Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brenner_Pass). Strada statale 38 dello Stelvio (SS 38, [German](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_language): *Stilfserjochstraße*[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58]) is an [Italian state highway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_highway_\(Italy\)) 224.29 kilometres (139.37 mi) long in [Italy](14532.md) located in the [regions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regions_of_Italy) of [Lombardy](43807.md) and Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol that connects [Valtellina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valtellina) with the [South Tyrolean](169611.md) [Vinschgau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinschgau) via the [Stelvio Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stelvio_Pass) (2,758 metres (9,049 ft)), to continue in [Etschtal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Etschtal) until it reaches [Bolzano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bolzano).

[Riva del Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riva_del_Garda) is connected to the other towns on [Lake Garda](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Garda) via a regulated bank line.

## Media



The seat of Italy's national public broadcasting company [Rai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAI) in Bozen/Bolzano, South Tyrol. The building houses the editorial offices of Rai Südtirol, Rai Ladinia and Rai Alto Adige.

*[L'Adige](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L'Adige_\(newspaper\))* is the regional daily newspaper of Trentino-Alto Adige but is mainly distributed in the province of [Trento](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino). With an average circulation of 28,511 copies, it is the second most-read newspaper in the region, preceded by *Dolomiten* in German with 50,711 copies, mostly sold in South Tyrol. The third regional newspaper is *Alto Adige/Trentino* with an average circulation of 27,736 copies (as of January 2013).

RAI maintains two separate offices in Trento and Bolzano. The Bolzano office includes *RAI Alto Adige* in Italian, *[RAI Südtirol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_S%C3%BCdtirol_\(radio_station\))* in German, and *[RAI Ladinia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rai_Ladinia)* in Ladin. *RAI Alto Adige* works in collaboration with the provincial office in Trento. Therefore, there is no single *RAI Trentino-Alto Adige* at the regional level.

The *[Radiotelevisione Azienda Speciale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rundfunk_Anstalt_S%C3%BCdtirol)* (Special Broadcasting Company) for the autonomous province of Bolzano broadcasts German, Austrian, and Swiss radio and TV programs in the South Tyrol area, especially for the German-speaking population.

Regional private networks include *Radio Tele Trentino Regionale (RTTR)*, *Trentino TV* (which offers television content for the [Cimbrian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cimbrian_language), [Mòcheno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M%C3%B2cheno_language), and Ladin linguistic minorities), and *[Video 33](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/VB33)*.

## Food and wine

Many dishes, such as *[canederli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kn%C3%B6del)* (bread dumplings), *strudel*, and *sauerkraut*, show the strong influence of Austrian cuisine in Trentino-Alto Adige.

A typical agri-food product of the region is [speck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Speck) (smoked cured ham).

The region produces various types of wine, including Alto Adige Pinot Bianco, Alto Adige Val Venosta aromatic Traminer, Teroldego Rotaliano, Lagrein, Trentino Müller-Thurgau, Trento white sparkling wine, and Alto Adige Valle Isarco Sylvaner Bressanone.

A typical bread from Trentino-Alto Adige is the *spaccata*.

## Symbols



Coat of arms of Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol

The [coat of arms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coat_of_arms) quarters the arms of [Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino) (black eagle) and the arms of [South Tyrol](169611.md) (red eagle). The [Flag of Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Trentino-Alto_Adige/S%C3%BCdtirol) consists of a coat of arms, containing two eagles of [San Venceslao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenceslaus_I,_Duke_of_Bohemia) ([Trentino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trentino)) and two Tyrolean red eagles ([Alto Adige](169611.md)), historical symbols of the two provinces, which stand out against a white and blue background. The shape of the flag is a rectangle with a framed heraldic shield on it. Like other flags, the flag of Trentino-Alto Adige is also inspired, albeit differently, by the [French flag](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_France) introduced with the [revolution of 1789](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Revolution). When Napoleon's army crossed Italy, starting from March 1796, flags of tricolour style were adopted both by the various newborn [Jacobin republics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sister_republic) and by the military units that supported the French army. In the Alpine region, however, sketches of the two-tone known today began to emerge. Trentino-Alto Adige has been a region with a special statute since 1948. The two parts that make it up, the province of Trento and the province of Bolzano, in turn, constitute two provinces with particular prerogatives of autonomy defined in 1972. The white-blue flag, in use (limited) since 1995, takes up the characteristics of the banner, including the shield with quartered eagles from the province of Trento (1st and 4th) and that of Bolzano (2nd and 3rd). White and blue are the colours on which the coats of arms of Trento and Bolzano, respectively, were worn in ancient times. It has never been legally defined, unlike the coat of arms and the banner, approved on 17 September 1982 and approved by presidential decree of 21 March 1983.



The [flag of Trentino-Alto Adige/Südtirol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flag_of_Trentino-Alto_Adige/S%C3%BCdtirol)

The flag has no ancient origins and most likely derives from the French tricolour, with the colours in the flag and in the coat of arms. With the Austrian reoccupation of the area, completed in 1814 by [Bellegarde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heinrich_von_Bellegarde) against [Beauharnais](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eug%C3%A8ne_de_Beauharnais), and confirmed by the [Congress of Vienna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congress_of_Vienna), the Trentino-Altoatesino bicolour was completely abandoned, defined as a symbol of the past Napoleonic regime. With regard to Trentino, the first public signal of Austrian intentions to dissolve the army of the Kingdom of Italy consisted of the prohibition, issued by Bellegarde on 13 June 1814, of wearing tricolour cockades, which evidently were very widespread. Certainly, [Francis II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Francis_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor) and Bellegarde were convinced that no other ties had matured in the meantime. There are various versions of the exact origin of the flag. One claims that the flag was created after [World War I](4764461.md), while other says that it was all a coincidence, mainly because it was thought that such different ethnic groups, like the Austrians and the Italians, had never shared a territory. The flag of Trentino-Alto Adige has ancient origins in terms of its coat of arms. The eagle of [St. Wenceslas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/St._Wenceslas), making up the coat of arms, was granted by [John of Bohemia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_of_Bohemia) on 9 August 1339. The rest of the flag consists of two horizontal bands of blue and white, with the coat of arms in the centre.[^cite_note-FOTW-59][^cite_note-60]
