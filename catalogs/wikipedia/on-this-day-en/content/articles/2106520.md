The **Romanian Air Force** (abbr.  **RoAF**, [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_language): *Forțele Aeriene Române*) is the [air force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_force) branch of the [Romanian Armed Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Armed_Forces). It has an air force headquarters, an operational command, five air bases, a logistics base, an air defense brigade, an air defense regiment and an [ISR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ISR_\(Intelligence,_surveillance_and_reconnaissance\)) brigade. Reserve forces include one air base and two airfields. In 2022, the Romanian Air Force employed 11,700 personnel.[^cite_note-balance_2022-4] The current chief of the Romanian Air Force Staff is [Lieutenant general](201916.md) Leonard-Gabriel Baraboi, who succeeded [Lieutenant general](201916.md) [Viorel Pană](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Viorel_Pan%C4%83) on 29 November 2023.[^cite_note-chief_of_staff-3][^cite_note-5]

The Romanian Air Force was first formed as the Military Aeronautics Service on 1 April 1913, transformed into the [Romanian Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Air_Corps) in 1915. The [Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Land_Forces)-subordinated Air Corps was reorganized as the independent [Royal Romanian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Romanian_Air_Force) on 1 January 1924, then converted to the present-day Air Force in 1949. The Air Force went through a modernization plan in the 1990s and early 2000s, dropping the [communist-era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Socialist_Republic_of_Romania) organization system in 1995 and adopting a [NATO](21133.md)-compatible one instead as the country was preparing to [join the Alliance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_in_NATO).

## Current state



Romanian F-16 at the 86th Air Base [Fetești](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fete%C8%99ti) in October 2016

[^cite_note-7]

The Romanian Air Force operates [F-16 Fighting Falcon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16_Fighting_Falcon) [multirole combat aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multirole_combat_aircraft). Three fighter [squadrons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Squadron_\(aviation\))[^cite_note-8] maintaining the [Permanent Combat Service - Air Policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quick_Reaction_Alert#Romania_2) and one training squadron are equipped with the F-16s.[^cite_note-Frontul-9] The Romanian Air Force also operates [C-130 Hercules](7697.md), [C-27J Spartan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alenia_C-27J_Spartan) and [An-26](908173.md) transport airplanes and [IAR 330](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAR_330) helicopters. IAR 330 Puma SOCAT helicopters, modernized by the [Romanian Aviation Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industria_Aeronautic%C4%83_Rom%C3%A2n%C4%83) (IAR) in cooperation with [Elbit Systems](1341669.md) (from [Israel](9282173.md)), are used in an [attack role](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_helicopter). The Romanian Air Force also includes locally built [IAR 99 Șoim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAR_99) jets, mostly used for [training missions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trainer_aircraft#Advanced_training).

In July 2023, the [Supreme Council of National Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supreme_Council_of_National_Defense_\(Romania\)) (CSAT) approved the plan to host an F-16 training center in Romania. The center was planned as a regional training hub for Romanian and other allied fighter pilots, including [Ukrainians](1135769.md).[^cite_note-10] To support this decision, [an international coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16_training_coalition) of 11 nations,[^cite_note-11] as well as Ukraine was formed during the [2023 Vilnius summit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2023_Vilnius_summit).[^cite_note-12] In November 2023, the [European F-16 Training Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RoAF_86th_Air_Base#European_F-16_Training_Center) became operational at the Romanian 86th Air Base. The Netherlands provided the F-16s, and [Lockheed Martin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin) with its subcontractor [Draken International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Draken_International) provided the instructors and technicians.[^cite_note-13][^cite_note-14] The first Ukrainian pilots started training at the center in September 2024 after a first group of Romanian pilots.[^cite_note-15][^cite_note-16] The 18 Dutch F-16s were formally transferred to Romania on 3 November for a symbolic price of [€](9472.md)1.[^cite_note-DutchF-16-17]

To replace the aging [MiG-21 LanceRs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21_LanceR), the Romanian Air Force [procured F-16 fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Dynamics_F-16_Fighting_Falcon_operators#Romania) from partner states. In 2013, Romania signed a contract with [Portugal](23033.md) for 12 [F-16AM/BM MLU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-16_MLU) fighters.[^cite_note-18] Under the *Peace Carpathian* program, the first six fighters entered service with the Romanian Air Force in September 2016, followed by another three in November and the last three in 2017. Romania ordered another five F-16s from Portugal in 2019, and the fighters were delivered by March 2021.[^cite_note-19][^cite_note-20]

Another contract for an additional 32 F-16s was signed with [Norway](21241.md) in 2022, and the first Norwegian F-16s arrived in 2023.[^cite_note-Norwegian_F-16-21] Under the contract, the F-16s were modified to the Romanian M6.5.2 configuration. [Kongsberg Aviation Maintenance Services](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kongsberg_Gruppen) provided support, maintenance, and training for Romanian technical personnel.[^cite_note-auto-22][^cite_note-23] As of 2025, maintenance for the Romanian F-16 fleet is carried out in the country at [Aerostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerostar_\(Romanian_company\)) with a capacity of servicing six jets at the same time.[^cite_note-24]

On 11 April 2023, CSAT approved the [acquisition plan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lockheed_Martin_F-35_Lightning_II_procurement#Romania) for the [fifth-generation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fifth-generation_fighter) [F-35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/F-35) [joint strike fighter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joint_Strike_Fighter_program).[^cite_note-25] The contract was signed on 21 November 2024.[^cite_note-26] The F-35 program is divided in two stages, first of 32 aircraft and the second of 16 aircraft for a total of 48 F-35s which will replace the F-16s between 2034 and 2040.[^cite_note-27] The first batch of eight F-35s is scheduled to arrive in the country in 2032.[^cite_note-Frontul-9]

## History



*[A. Vlaicu Nr. I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Vlaicu_I)* at military exercises, 27 September 1910

### Beginnings

In 1818, during the reign of [John Caradja](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Caradja), the prince of [Wallachia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wallachia), an unmanned hot air balloon was flown off [Dealul Spirii](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dealul_Spirii) in [Bucharest](36877.md).[^cite_note-28] On 2 July \[[O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style) 20 June] 1874, Marius Willemot, the owner of the hydrogen balloon named [Mihai Bravul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihai_Bravul?action=edit\&redlink=1) flew together with [Majors](201920.md) [Iacob Lahovary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iacob_Lahovary), [Constantin Poenaru](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Poenaru) and Dumitrescu over [Bucharest](36877.md). The last flight took place on 19 \[[O.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Style_and_New_Style_dates) 7 July] 1874, Willemot flying together with Colonel [Nicolae Haralambie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nicolae_Haralambie), [Ion Ghica](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ion_Ghica) and a third person. The balloon had made its first flight in [Paris](22989.md) on 27 March of the same year.[^cite_note-29]

The first aeronautical unit of Romania was established in 1893 under the [1st Telegraph Company](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Communications_and_Informatics_Command#Early_telegraphy_sections) in the 1st Engineer Regiment. The role of this unit was to operate the [observation balloons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Observation_balloon) for aerial surveillance in support of the artillery units defending the [fortifications of Bucharest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortifications_of_Bucharest). In 1913, the unit was reorganized into the "[Aerostation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerostat) Company" which became part of the Military Aeronautics Service.[^cite_note-aviation-30][^cite_note-31]

On 20 November 1909, the [Chitila Piloting School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chitila_Piloting_School) was formed as a [joint venture](538782.md) by Mihail Cerchez. The school, conducted by French flight instructors, had five hangars, [bleachers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bleacher) for spectators and workshops where the [Farman airplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farman_III) were built under license. It was opened on 9 July 1910, led by the chief flight instructor and director of the school René Guillemin who was soon replaced by Michel-Paul Molla after an accident.[^cite_note-32][^cite_note-33] The first pilot licensed at Chitila was *Sublocotenent* [Ștefan Protopopescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C8%98tefan_Protopopescu) on 9 July 1911.[^cite_note-34] The school closed in late 1912 due to financial difficulties, having trained six officers, but only licensed two.[^cite_note-35]

In November 1909, the Romanian Minister of War commissioned [Aurel Vlaicu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aurel_Vlaicu) to build the [A Vlaicu I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Vlaicu_I) airplane at the [Bucharest Army Arsenal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bucharest_Army_Arsenal) which first flew on 17 June 1910. On 28 September during the fall [military exercise](1225714.md), Vlaicu flew his airplane from [Slatina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slatina,_Romania) to [Piatra Olt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piatra_Olt) carrying a message, Romania thus becoming one of the first countries to use airplanes for military purposes.[^cite_note-36] Along with other Romanian pilots, Vlaicu flew reconnaissance missions during the [Second Balkan War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Balkan_War).[^cite_note-37][^cite_note-38][^cite_note-39] [Vlaicu III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Vlaicu_III), the first metal aircraft in the world, was completed after his death, in May 1914.[^cite_note-40]

On 1 April 1913, the first law on the organization of military aeronautics was issued, thus forming the Military Aeronautics Service (*Serviciul de Aeronautică Militară*), later reorganized as the Romanian Air Corps (*Corpul de Aviație Român*) in 1915.[^cite_note-aviation-30] Organized as such, the Romanian aviation participated in the Second Balkan War, becoming the first air arm to perform operations over an enemy [capital city](181337.md), with [leaflet drop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Airborne_leaflet_propaganda) missions flown over [Sofia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sofia).[^cite_note-41]

### World War I



A Romanian [Nieuport 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuport_11)

[^cite_note-42]

Main article: [Romanian Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Air_Corps)

On the eve of Romania's [entrance into the war](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romania_in_World_War_I) in August 1916, only 24 out of the 44 aircraft that had been imported and assembled at *[Rezerva generală a aviației](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rezerva_general%C4%83_a_avia%C8%9Biei)* (RGA) were available. Another 20 aircraft were provided by the flight schools. These aircraft included [Bristol T.B.8s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_T.B.8), [Bristol Coanda Monoplanes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bristol_Coanda_Monoplanes), [Blériot XIs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bl%C3%A9riot_XI), and other [Farman aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farman_Aviation_Works).[^cite_note-43][^cite_note-44][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvramArmă20185-45] One of the Vlaicu monoplanes, [A Vlaicu II](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Vlaicu_II), crashed in 1913, while the [A Vlaicu I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Vlaicu_I) was retired in 1914, leaving [A Vlaicu III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A_Vlaicu_III) as the sole Romanian-made aircraft in the [Romanian Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Air_Corps).[^cite_note-46] Also in 1916, the Anti-aircraft Defence Corps was formed and equipped with [searchlights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Searchlight) and locally converted [anti-aircraft guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-aircraft_gun), which included the "Negrei" and "[Burileanu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C8%98tefan_Burileanu)" systems.[^cite_note-aviation-30]

During [World War I](4764461.md), Romania acquired 322 aircraft from [France](5843419.md) and ex-[RNAS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Naval_Air_Service) aircraft from [Great Britain](31717.md) including [Nieuport 11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuport_11) and [17](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuport_17) single seat fighters, [Morane-Saulnier LA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Morane-Saulnier_LA) and [Nieuport 12](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nieuport_12) two seat fighters, [Caudron G.3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudron_G.3), [Henry Farman HF.20](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Henry_Farman_HF.20), [Farman MF.11](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farman_MF.11) and [Farman F.40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Farman_F.40) artillery observation and reconnaissance aircraft, as well as [Caudron G.4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caudron_G.4), [Breguet-Michelin BLM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Breguet-Michelin_BLM) and [Voisin LA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Voisin_LA) bombers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGreen197125–26-47] On 16 September 1916, a Romanian Farman F.40 downed an [Imperial German Air Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luftstreitkr%C3%A4fte) aircraft near [Slobozia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slobozia); this was the first Romanian aerial victory. By the end of World War I, Romanian pilots had flown about 11,000 hours and 750 missions; however, this was unable to prevent the December 1916 Romanian offensive at the [Battle of the Argeș](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Arge%C8%99) from being defeated, which resulted in the occupation of southern Romania, and the [armistice on 6 December 1917](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Armistice_of_Foc%C8%99ani) following the [Russian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_Revolution).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGreen197126-48] Still, the air corps played a significant role in the [1917 summer campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_campaign_\(1917\)) by maintaining [air supremacy](537478.md) over the battlefield and providing reconnaissance for the ground troops, helping secure the victories in the three battles.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvramArmă201830–32-49] The Romanian Air Corps ended the war with 106–146 enemy aircraft shot down in total.[^cite_note-50] Other victories were achieved in the [Hungarian–Romanian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian%E2%80%93Romanian_War) of 1919. According to the [Romanian aerial victory standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerial_victory_standards_of_World_War_I#Romania), one pilot achieved the status of [flying ace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flying_ace), [Second lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_lieutenant) [Dumitru Bădulescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumitru_B%C4%83dulescu) who scored eight victories, of which three unconfirmed.[^cite_note-aviation-30][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAvramArmă201851-51]

### Interwar



[IAR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industria_Aeronautic%C4%83_Rom%C3%A2n%C4%83)-produced [Potez 25](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Potez_25) airplanes in 1931

The beginning of the [interwar period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period) saw a new organization for the Air Corps with units deployed throughout the country. The process of acquiring new aircraft to replace the old World War I ones also continued.[^cite_note-52] On 1 January 1924, the Army-subordinated Air Corps was reorganized as a separate [military branch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_branch). It was named *Aeronautica Regală Română* (ARR, lit. 'Romanian Royal Aeronautics') and placed under the command of the General Inspectorate of Aeronautics.[^cite_note-An1920-1] The number of [aviators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviator) and aircraft built either under license or locally designed also continued to grow.[^cite_note-20_iulie-53] This led to the formation of the aviation flotillas ([air wings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wing_\(military_unit\))) in 1929 from the previous [aviation groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Group_\(military_unit\)).[^cite_note-54] By 1936, the Air Force's aircraft were largely obsolete and the mounting political and military tensions in Europe led to calls for a restructuring plan of the ARR. The drafted plan proposed equipping 36 new squadrons with modern combat aircraft within two years. Due to continued delays, delegations for acquiring new aircraft were only sent in April 1939. The aircraft inventory quickly grew in size with deliveries from Germany and the United Kingdom. Also in 1939, a large number of Polish aircraft arrived following the [invasion of Poland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Poland) and were brought into Romanian service.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBernád19994-55][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBernád20038–9-56]

### World War II



A pair of [IAR 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAR_80) fighters on patrol during [World War II](32927.md)

Main article: [Royal Romanian Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Romanian_Air_Force)

When Romania, allied with [Nazi Germany](21212.md), went to war against the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) on 22 June 1941, the ARR had 621 airplanes, including its locally made [IAR 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAR_80) fighters.[^cite_note-20_iulie-53] The air force accomplished hundreds of missions, contributing to Romania's recapture of [Northern Bucovina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bukovina) and [Bessarabia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bessarabia), which had been [occupied by the Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_occupation_of_Bessarabia_and_Northern_Bukovina) a year earlier. By the end of the [Siege of Odessa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Odessa), the Romanian military fighters claimed about 600 air victories.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBernád200322-57] The Royal Romanian Air Force fought on the [Eastern Front](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Front_\(World_War_II\)), and in the defense of the country against [Allied air raids](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Western_Allied_campaign_in_Romania), until 23 August 1944, bringing an important contribution to the great battles at [Stalingrad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Stalingrad), in [Crimea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crimean_campaign), and the Ukrainian fronts. Between 1941 and 1944, Romanian aircraft won 2,000 air victories.[^cite_note-20_iulie-53] The most famous flying aces were Captain Prince [Constantin Cantacuzino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constantin_Cantacuzino_\(aviator\)), who gained 69 certified victories by the [Romanian air victory standards](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_World_War_II_flying_aces_from_Romania#Romanian_WW2_victory_system), Captain [Alexandru Șerbănescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandru_%C8%98erb%C4%83nescu), with 55 victories, and Captain [Horia Agarici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horia_Agarici) with 13 victories.

In the aftermath of [King Michael's Coup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/King_Michael's_Coup) of 23 August 1944, Romania turned against Germany and joined the [Allies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allies_of_World_War_II). Between 24 and 31 August the ARR fought against the [Luftwaffe](17885.md), destroying 59 German aircraft.[^cite_note-58] Continuing the campaign against the [Axis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Axis_powers), the Royal Romanian Air Force, organized in the [1st Air Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Air_Corps_\(Romania\)), aided the [Soviet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Red_Army) and [Romanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Land_Forces) armies until 18 May 1945. The last victory scored by a Romanian pilot, although not officially registered, happened on 4 May when a Soviet [Yak-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yak-3) was shot down in a clash with two Romanian [Bf 109s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bf_109).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBernád200368-59] In the 1944-1945 campaign, the Air Force destroyed 126 enemy aircraft in battle[^cite_note-60] while losing 176 aircraft itself, of which 30 in combat and the rest mainly in accidents.[^cite_note-20_iulie-53][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEBernád200368-59]

### Cold War



A pair of Romanian [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-21) fighters, late 1970s

See also: [Army of the Socialist Republic of Romania § Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Socialist_Republic_of_Romania#Air_Force)

Starting in 1948, Romania tailored its military to Soviet concepts and doctrine. On 15 February 1949, the Aviation Command was established based on the Soviet model, [regiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aviation_regiment_\(Soviet_Union\)) replacing the flotillas. A year later, on 1 April 1950, the military aviation was named *Forțele Aeriene Militare* (lit. 'Military Air Forces'). In the following years, new Soviet aircraft, such as [Yakovlev Yak-18](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakovlev_Yak-18), [Polikarpov Po-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Polikarpov_Po-2), [Lavochkin La-9](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lavochkin_La-9), [Tupolev Tu-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tupolev_Tu-2), and [Ilyushin Il-10](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ilyushin_Il-10) entered service. The first [jet fighters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jet_fighter) – [Yakovlev Yak-17s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakovlev_Yak-17) and [Yak-23s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yakovlev_Yak-23) entered service in 1951, followed in 1952 by another 88 [MiG-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-15) aircraft. In 1958, the first supersonic fighter [MiG-19](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-19) entered the inventory. Three years later, in February 1962, a new fighter was added to the inventory, the [MiG-21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-21), which represented one of the most effective fighters of that time.[^cite_note-61][^cite_note-avia-ia-62]

Starting in 1974, Romanian-made aircraft supplemented the already existing jets. The Romanian [IAR-93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAR-93) [attack aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Attack_aircraft) flew its first flight on 31 October 1974. It represented a great step forward taking into account that it was the only jet fighter not made by the Soviets, the only one ever manufactured and operated by a [Warsaw Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warsaw_Pact) country.[^cite_note-avia-ia-62][^cite_note-63]

In February 1956, the first helicopter squadron was established, followed by the reception of the first helicopter, a Soviet [Mi-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mil_Mi-4), a few months later. In 1961, the squadron received other Mi-4s, while [PZL SM-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PZL_SM-1) and [SM-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PZL_SM-2) helicopters equipped another squadron.[^cite_note-64] From 1968, [Mi-8](957233.md) helicopters also entered service. In 1971, the first Romanian-manufactured helicopters entered service – the [SA 316 Alouette III](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/A%C3%A9rospatiale_Alouette_III) produced under license by IAR as the [IAR 316](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAR_316).[^cite_note-65] Renewing the aircraft fleet process went on with the first [MiG-23s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mikoyan-Gurevich_MiG-23) arriving on 23 January 1979.[^cite_note-66]

On 14 May 1981, at 20\:16, the Soviet [spaceship](37910.md) [Soyuz 40](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soyuz_40) was launched from [Baikonur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baikonur_Cosmodrome) to perform a common Romanian-Soviet flight, with [Lieutenant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lieutenant) [Dumitru Prunariu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dumitru_Prunariu) and [Colonel](199023.md) [Leonid Popov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leonid_Popov) as commander on board.[^cite_note-avia-ia-62] During the early 1980s, the [67th Fighter-Bomber Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craiova_Air_Base) and the [49th Fighter-Bomber Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ianca_Air_Base) from [Craiova](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Craiova) and [Ianca](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ianca) were equipped with new IAR-93s, which replaced old MiG-15s and MiG-17s. In December 1989, just a few days before the [Romanian revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanian_Revolution_of_1989) against communism began, [MiG-29](185653.md) aircraft entered the Air Force inventory. Initially, 45 MiG-29s were ordered but only 21 were delivered, with the rest of the order being cancelled. The MiG-29s were assigned to the 2nd and 3rd Squadrons of the [57th Fighter Regiment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RoAF_57th_Air_Base) located at the [Mihail Kogălniceanu Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mihail_Kog%C4%83lniceanu_International_Airport).[^cite_note-67]

### Post-1989



The [MiG-29](185653.md) was withdrawn in 2003

In 1990, the last [MiG-15](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-15) fighters were retired from the 49th Aviation Regiment, located in Ianca. In 1992, production of the [IAR-93](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAR-93) was stopped following the start of the [Yugoslav Civil Wars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yugoslav_Civil_Wars). The last IAR-93s were retired in 1998. By 1 June 1995 the Air Force dropped the communist era regimental system in favor of a system consisting of Air Bases, Groups and Squadrons.[^cite_note-scramble-roaf-68]

Due to financial constraints, being unable to purchase new fighters, the Romanian Air Force decided to invest in the upgrade of the MiG-21 fighters. Following a competition between several companies, the [Israeli](9282173.md) company [Elbit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Elbit) was chosen, and the MiG-21M and MF/MF-75 versions were selected. The program was originally called the "DD program" as a tribute to the fighter pilot and writer [Doru Davidovici](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Doru_Davidovici), who died in a MiG-21 crash in 1989. The upgrade program was later renamed to "Lancer", designated as "LanceR" with capital "R" in Romania.[^cite_note-scramble-roaf-68] The first flight of an upgraded MiG, a LanceR 'A' ground attack variant, took place on 22 August 1995.[^cite_note-69]

The Romanian MiG-29 fleet was also intended to undergo modernization under a project named "[Sniper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-29#Upgraded_variants_based_on_original_airframe)" done by [DASA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/DaimlerChrysler_Aerospace), [Aerostar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aerostar_\(Romanian_company\)) and Elbit. The first flight took place on 5 May 2000 and the prototype was presented at [ILA 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ILA_Berlin_Air_Show).[^cite_note-70][^cite_note-71] However, the modernization project was canceled due to various reasons and the MiG-29s were retired.[^cite_note-72]

Following its entry into the [Partnership for Peace](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partnership_for_Peace) program in 1994, Romania started cooperating with other countries with the goal of eventually joining NATO. For this, Romanian Air Force aircraft participated in many local or abroad exercises and airshows. Before joining NATO, the 86th Group at [Borcea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Borcea) started to convert into a NATO compatible unit by making improvements to the base's infrastructure, allowing its pilots to fly a fair amount of training hours and work according to NATO procedures.[^cite_note-scramble-roaf-68]

During the period of reorganization, starting from the year 2000, other aircraft models such as the [MiG-23](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-23) fighters, the [Harbin H-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harbin_H-5) bombers and the [IAR 823](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/IAR_823) and [L-39 Albatros](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L-39_Albatros) trainers were retired, the latter of which was retired in 2007.[^cite_note-scramble-roaf-68][^cite_note-73] Along with the retiring of airplanes, several bases were disbanded as well, including the [Alexeni Airfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexeni_Airfield), the 49th Air Base from Ianca and the [91st Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deveselu_Military_Base#History) from [Deveselu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deveselu). The [93rd Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RoAF_93rd_Air_Base) from [Timișoara](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timi%C8%99oara) was also disbanded and currently serves as an annex to the 71st Air Base.[^cite_note-scramble-roaf-68]

In the spring of 2009, the Romanian government decided to purchase [VSHORAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Close-in_weapon_system)/[SHORAD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SHORAD) systems from France.[^cite_note-74] The deal included [Mistral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mistral_\(missile\)) [MANPADS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MANPADS) and [MICA VL](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MICA_\(missile\)) [surface-to-air missiles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Surface-to-air_missiles).[^cite_note-75] However, after preliminary talks with [MBDA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MBDA) in August, the deal was put on hold and canceled afterwards because of the defense cuts.[^cite_note-76]

In February 2010, CSAT signed an agreement with the United States for missile defence under whose terms land-based [SM-3](15787389.md) systems would be installed in Romania. On 3 May 2011, the president of Romania [Traian Băsescu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traian_B%C4%83sescu) announced the location for the SM-3 systems: former Air Force base Deveselu in the [Olt County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Olt_County).[^cite_note-gandul2012-77] The system includes 3 batteries with 24 SM-3 Block I rockets, manned by approximately 200 US soldiers (with a maximum of 500) initially under Romanian Air Force overall command.[^cite_note-gandul2012-77] The Deveselu [Aegis Ashore](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aegis_Ashore) site was declared operational on 13 May 2016.[^cite_note-78]

On 23 May 2022, the MiG-21 LanceRs resumed flights after being suspended on 15 April 2022. The LanceR continued to fly for a period of one year, until 15 May 2023 when they were retired.[^cite_note-79][^cite_note-80]

#### Russian invasion of Ukraine



F-16 no. 1616, the first Romanian aircraft to score an aerial victory since WW2

[^cite_note-81]

See also: [Violations of non-combatant airspace during the Russo-Ukrainian war (2022–present)](76430324.md) and [2026 drone incidents in Romania](83821982.md)

On the starting day of the [Russian invasion of Ukraine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Russian_invasion_of_Ukraine), two F-16s from the [53rd Fighter Squadron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/53rd_Fighter_Squadron_%22Warhawks%22) were sent to intercept a [Sukhoi Su-27](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sukhoi_Su-27) of the [Ukrainian Air Force](1135769.md) that was approaching Romanian airspace. The Su-27 was escorted to the [95th Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RoAF_95th_Air_Base) where the pilot was taken by Romanian authorities.[^cite_note-82] The [Ukrainian Minister of Defence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister_of_Defence_of_Ukraine), [Oleksiy Reznikov](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oleksiy_Reznikov), quickly apologized for this event and requested the return of the airplane and its pilot. After a maintenance team from Ukraine fixed the technical issues of the fighter,[^cite_note-83] the aircraft was returned without its weapons on 1 March, being escorted by two [MiG-21 LanceRs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MiG-21_LanceR) to the border where other Ukrainian airplanes took over.[^cite_note-84][^cite_note-85]

On 2 March 2022, a MiG-21 LanceR was lost while on an air patrol inside Romanian airspace near [Cogealac](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cogealac), 60 miles from the Ukrainian border. This "occurred amid increased air police missions in Romania after the Russian invasion of Ukraine." An IAR 330 on a search and rescue mission for the missing MiG-21 crashed with seven fatalities.[^cite_note-86][^cite_note-87] The eight servicemen who died in the two accidents were posthumously promoted and decorated by the [president of Romania](273659.md).[^cite_note-88] Shortly after, [fake news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fake_news) claiming that the Romanian MiG was shot down by Ukrainian [S-300 missile systems](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S-300_missile_system) appeared. These claims were officially refuted.[^cite_note-89] The preliminary analysis published on 23 March showed that the crashes occurred due to human and environmental factors.[^cite_note-90]

As of 13 December 2022, Romanian Air Force and allied aircraft took part in more than 150 [air policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_policing) missions since the start of Russia's invasion. The majority of the missions involved Russian aircraft approaching Romanian airspace. Other missions involved coordinating civilian aircraft which had reported bomb threats or after their communications were interrupted.[^cite_note-91]

On 19 May 2026, two Romanian F-16 fighter jets part of the Carpathian Vipers detachment on the [Baltic Air Policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Air_Policing) mission were scrambled by the [Combined Air Operations Centre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_Air_Operations_Centre) Uedem to intercept a [drone](58900.md) which entered [Estonian](28222445.md) airspace during a Ukrainian attack on the [Saint Petersburg](24320051.md) region. The decision was taken to destroy the drone and the unmanned aircraft was shot down with an [air-to-air missile](320568.md) around noon, marking the first Romanian aerial victory since the end of World War 2.[^cite_note-92] The Ukrainian Minister of Defence issued an apology following this incident.[^cite_note-93][^cite_note-94] The pilot who shot down the drone, *Căpitan-Comandor* Costel-Alexandru Pavelescu from the 86th Air Base, was decorated in the aftermath by [Radu Miruță](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radu_Miru%C8%9B%C4%83), the Romanian Minister of Defence.[^cite_note-95]

Another drone was shot down over Romania on 24 July 2026, marking the first such incident over the country. The drone, identified by the pilots as a [Shahed](75153890.md) ([Geran-2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geran-2)) type, was destroyed by a Romanian F-16 with an air-to-air missile over [Padina, Buzău](11136427.md).[^cite_note-96] A day later, a second drone was shot down over Romania near [Sfântu Gheorghe, Tulcea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sf%C3%A2ntu_Gheorghe,_Tulcea) by the same pilot who shot down the previous drone.[^cite_note-97] On 26 July, a third drone was shot down in Romania. It was destroyed by an F-16 over the [Black Sea](3386.md) in Romania's [territorial waters](496983.md), 12 km North-East off [Sulina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sulina).[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

In the morning on 20 August 2026, a Romanian F-16 destroyed a [naval drone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_drone) near the [Neptun Deep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neptun_Deep) [offshore platform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Offshore_platform) using its [onboard cannon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M61_Vulcan).[^cite_note-100] The drone was confirmed to not be Ukrainian through the direct communications channel established following the 5 June Ukrainian naval drone incident in [Constanța](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Constan%C8%9Ba).[^cite_note-101] President [Nicușor Dan](35543359.md) attributed the incident to the Russian Federation.[^cite_note-102]

## International deployments



Romanian MiG-21 of the Baltica 07 Detachment ready for a night mission

### Baltic Air Policing

Romania began participating in the [Baltic Air Policing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baltic_Air_Policing) in August 2007 with the deployment of four MiG-21 LanceR Cs at [Šiauliai Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/%C5%A0iauliai_Air_Base) in [Lithuania](17675.md). The Romanian detachment succeeded the [French Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Air_Force) [Mirage 2000](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_2000)Cs of *Escadron de Chasse 01.012* from [Cambrai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrai). The aircraft and most of the staff came from the [71st Air Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RoAF_71st_Air_Base). A total of 67 personnel, among them nine pilots, were part of the detachment: 63 served at Šiauliai, while other four served at the [air traffic control](48563.md) centre in [Kaunas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kaunas). Once the RoAF finished its three-month stint, a [Portuguese Air Force](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Portuguese_Air_Force) detachment took over the mission. The Romanian detachment attracted attention from the local media, not least from the fact that it was only the second time a [Soviet-era](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union) jet fighter was deployed to Šiauliai ([Polish Air Force](602835.md) MiG-29s had been deployed there first in 2006).[^cite_note-103]

In November 2022, it was announced that Romanian F-16s were to participate in the Baltic Air Policing missions between April and July 2023.[^cite_note-104] The *Carpathian Vipers* detachment formed by four F-16s and 100 servicemen primarily from the 53rd Fighter Squadron and led by Commander Cosmin Vlad was established 14 March 2023.[^cite_note-105][^cite_note-106] The Romanian detachment was deployed together with a Portuguese detachment, taking over the Baltic Air Policing mission from the previous French and Polish detachments.[^cite_note-107] The deployment ended on 31 July and was succeeded by a detachment of [Italian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Italian_Air_Force) [Eurofighter Typhoons](167667.md).[^cite_note-108] Between April and July, the Romanian fighters intercepted over 60 [Russian Air Force](1526308.md) aircraft and participated in various multinational exercises totaling 600 fight hours.[^cite_note-109]

The *Carpathian Vipers* detachment was deployed again in the Baltic Air Policing between April and August 2025. Working alongside the Polish Air Force *Orlik* contingent, the detachment totaled over 400 [flight hours](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_hours) in operational missions as well as exercises such as Ramstein Alloy, Hedgehog, and [BALTOPS](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BALTOPS). The two units were succeeded by [Hungarian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hungarian_Air_Force) [Gripens](87577.md) and [Spanish](3853398.md) Typhoons detachments.[^cite_note-110] Starting in March 2026, the *Carpathian Vipers* detachment was deployed on the *Baltica 2026* mission at Šiauliai with six F-16s.[^cite_note-111] The detachment ended its 2026 deployment in August, accumulating over 800 flight hours in over 20 [alpha scrambles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scrambling_\(military\)) and other operations.[^cite_note-112]



Romanian and American instructor pilots flying T-38Cs in formation

### Euro-NATO Joint Jet Pilot Training

After previously stating the intent of joining the [Euro-NATO Joint Jet Pilot Training Program](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Euro-NATO_Joint_Jet_Pilot_Training_Program) (ENJJPT) in 2015, Romania became a member of the program in 2017.[^cite_note-113] The first Romanian [instructor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Flight_instructor) was sent to the [Sheppard Air Force Base](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sheppard_Air_Force_Base) in 2020.[^cite_note-114] As of 2023, Romania had 15 pilots at the American base, of which nine instructor pilots. The Romanian instructors participate in the training of all partner nation pilots on the [T-6A Texan II](412707.md) and [T-38C Talon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-38C_Talon) aircraft.[^cite_note-115][^cite_note-116]

### 2019–2020 mission to Mali

From October 2019 to November 2020, the *Carpathian Pumas* detachment of the 572nd Helicopter Squadron was deployed to [Mali](19127.md) as part of [MINUSMA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MINUSMA). The detachment consisted of four IAR 330L helicopters and 120 personnel deployed on two rotations which took part in [MEDEVAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MEDEVAC), [CASEVAC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CASEVAC), troop and materiel transport, air patrol, and reconnaissance missions.[^cite_note-117] By the end of the mission to Mali, the detachment flew over 380 missions, of which approximately 100 transport missions, 200 reconnaissance missions, and 18 medical missions during which more than 40 patients were evacuated. It operated in the [UN](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UN) sectors in the [Mopti](1064867.md) and [Kidal](2214311.md) regions.[^cite_note-118]

One IAR 330 was damaged in August 2020. It was located at the [Douentza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Douentza) base when the helicopter was overturned by a storm. No injuries occurred as a result of the accident.[^cite_note-119]

## Structure

See also: [List of Romanian Air Force units](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Romanian_Air_Force_units)

### Air Force General Staff

The Romanian Air Force General Staff represents the military concept-developing, command and executive structure providing Air Forces peacetime, crisis and wartime leadership in order to reach, maintain and increase, as required, the [operational level](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operational_art) of the military subordinated structures so that to be able to operate under authorized commands responsible for military operations planning and conduct.[^cite_note-general_staff-120]

The main task is to generate, mobilize, structure, equip, operationalize and regenerate the required forces, provide the logistic support necessary to conduct [military operations](666301.md) and based on higher orders, take over both the Air Component Command and independent air operations command and control, through the Air Operations Centre.[^cite_note-general_staff-120] Starting with 1 July 2010, the Romanian Air Force bases were renamed to Air Flotillas.[^cite_note-121] The names were kept until 1 December 2013, when they were changed back to Air Bases.[^cite_note-122]

The first aviator badges of Romania were issued in 1912. The current pilot badges were introduced in 1996 and use the same style as the World War I and World War II badges.
