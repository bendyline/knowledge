**Ludovico Scarfiotti** (18 October 1933 – 8 June 1968) was an Italian [racing driver](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Racing_driver), who competed in [Formula One](10854.md) from [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Formula_One_season) to [1968](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Formula_One_season). Scarfiotti won the [1966 Italian Grand Prix](1122358.md) with [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari). In [endurance racing](699018.md), Scarfiotti won the [24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) and the [12 Hours of Sebring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_Hours_of_Sebring), both in 1963 with Ferrari.

Born in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin), Scarfiotti was the grandson of Lodovico Scarfiotti, co-founder and former president of [Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat). He initially competed in [sportscar racing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportscar_racing), racing for [Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scuderia_Ferrari) from 1960 onwards. He finished third in the 1962 [1000 km of Paris](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1000_km_of_Paris) before winning the [24 Hours of Le Mans](1401596.md) in [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) alongside [Lorenzo Bandini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Bandini), driving the [Ferrari 250 P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_250_P). His victory at Le Mans—along with his win at the [12 Hours of Sebring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12_Hours_of_Sebring)—prompted his Formula One debut with Ferrari at the [1963 Dutch Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Dutch_Grand_Prix). Scarfiotti made sporadic appearances for the team over the next five seasons, including his sole win at the [Italian Grand Prix](1122358.md) in [1966](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_Formula_One_season). He added to his sportscar success with a runner-up finish at Le Mans in [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans), as well as at the [24 Hours of Daytona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_24_Hours_of_Daytona). After a one-off appearance for [Eagle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo_American_Racers) in [1967](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Formula_One_season), Scarfiotti signed for [Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Car_Company) the [following season](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Formula_One_season).

A [motorsports](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorsports) competitor for a decade, Scarfiotti had also won the [sportscar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sportscar) class of the [European Hillclimb Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_Hillclimb_Championship) in 1962 and 1965. In June 1968, Scarfiotti died at a [hillclimbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillclimbing) event in the [German Alps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_Alps) when he crashed his [Porsche 910](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_910).

## Early life

Scarfiotti was born in [Turin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turin). Scarfiotti was associated with cars from his youth. His grandfather was the first president and one of the nine founders of the [Fiat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiat) automobile company.[^cite_note-lud-1]

## Sports car competition



Scarfiotti driving [Ferrari 275 P](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_275_P) at the Nürburgring in 1964

Scarfiotti competed in the 1,000 Kilometres de Paris sports car race in October 1962. He finished third with teammate [Colin Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Colin_Davis_\(driver\)). The event was won by [Pedro Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Rodr%C3%ADguez_\(racing_driver\)) and [Ricardo Rodríguez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ricardo_Rodr%C3%ADguez_\(Formula_One\)) driving a Ferrari.[^cite_note-2] Partnered with [Lorenzo Bandini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lorenzo_Bandini), Scarfiotti was victorious in the [24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) in June 1963. Their factory Ferrari achieved an average speed of 117.99 miles per hour over a distance of 2,832 miles (4,558 km). The victory was worth almost \$20,000 in various prize money along with prestige, and gave Ferrari its fourth consecutive Le Mans victory.[^cite_note-3]

In 1965, [John Surtees](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Surtees) and Scarfiotti shared a Ferrari 330 P2 Spyder which gave the [marque](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marque) a fourth consecutive victory at the [1000km Nürburgring](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1000km_N%C3%BCrburgring) race. They led throughout the 44 laps, posting a winning time of 6 hours, 53 minutes, and 5 seconds, for an average speed of 90.46 mph (145.58 km/h).[^cite_note-4] Scarfiotti and Bandini drove a 2-litre [Dino 206 S](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dino_206_S) to second place in the 1966 running of the 1,000 kilometre Nürburgring in which first place went to [Phil Hill](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Hill) and [Joakim Bonnier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joakim_Bonnier) driving a 5.4-litre [Chevrolet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chevrolet)-powered [Chaparral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaparral_Cars). The Dino was 90 seconds behind the Chaparral that debuted the automatic transmission in European competition.[^cite_note-5]

Surtees severed relations with the Ferrari racing team following their decision to replace him with Scarfiotti at the [1966 24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1966_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans). Scarfiotti would go on to finish 31st, retiring after 123 laps. Scarfiotti joined [Mike Parkes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Parkes) in a Ferrari P4 for the [1000 km Spa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1000_km_Spa) in May 1967. They finished a lap behind [Jacky Ickx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacky_Ickx) and Richard Thompson, who drove a [Ford](30433662.md) [Mirage (race car)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mirage_\(race_car\)). The winning team averaged 120.5 mph (193.9 km/h) and posted a time of 5 hours, 9 minutes, 46.5 seconds.[^cite_note-6]

Teamed with Mike Parkes, Scarfiotti took the new Ferrari P4 coupe to second place behind the sister car (a P4 spyder) driven by Lorenzo Bandini and Chris Amon at the 24 Hours of Daytona, with Ferrari taking the first three positions. The same result took place at the Monza 1,000 km in April. Scarfiotti, again teamed with Parkes finished second at the 24 Hours of Le Mans, this time behind the Ford Mark IV driven by A.J. Foyt and Dan Gurney. Scarfiotti raced a Ferrari factory car in the September 1967 200-mile [Canadian-American Challenge Cup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canadian-American_Challenge_Cup) race held on a 2.85-mile (4.59 km) course near [Bridgehampton, New York](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridgehampton,_New_York). His sponsor was the [North American Racing Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_American_Racing_Team) of [Luigi Chinetti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luigi_Chinetti).[^cite_note-7]

After [Günter Klass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCnter_Klass) was killed in July 1967, Ferrari retired the two-litre Dino 206S prototypes that were also used in hillclimbing. After a fast [1967 24 Hours of Le Mans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_24_Hours_of_Le_Mans) race won by a 7-litre V8-powered Ford ahead of Scarfiotti/Parkes, the big engines were banned for the 1968 WSC season in which prototypes were limited to max 3-litre engines, same size as in F1, but those were designed to last for 300 km, not 1000 km or 24 hours. Ferrari was forced to retire the 4-litre V12 Ferrari P series, and in protest to the rule change, did not enter the [1968 World Sportscar Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_World_Sportscar_Championship). With the Germans also being active in hillclimbing, Scarfiotti joined Porsche.

Scarfiotti entered the 1968 [Targa Florio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Targa_Florio), but wrecked his [Porsche 907](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_907) (#230) on the first day of qualifying and was forced to race with Porsche's T-car[^cite_note-8] which did not last the 720-kilometre-long (450 mi) road race.

## Formula One



Scarfiotti driving [Ferrari 312 F1-66](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_312_F1-66) at Monza in 1966

[Enzo Ferrari](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Enzo_Ferrari) signed Scarfiotti to the Ferrari Formula One team of drivers for [1963](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Formula_One_season) along with Surtees, [Willy Mairesse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Willy_Mairesse), Bandini, and [Nino Vaccarella](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nino_Vaccarella).[^cite_note-9] Scarfiotti placed sixth in the second Ferrari in the [1963 Dutch Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_Dutch_Grand_Prix) at [Zandvoort](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_Park_Zandvoort). He was a lap behind victor [Jim Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Clark) in a [Lotus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Team_Lotus). John Surtees piloted the first Ferrari to third place behind [Dan Gurney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dan_Gurney) in a [Brabham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brabham). Scarfiotti finished fifth in the non-championship [1965 Syracuse Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1965_Syracuse_Grand_Prix) on the island of Sicily.[^cite_note-10]

Scarfiotti became the first Italian in fifteen years to win the [Italian Grand Prix](511451.md) when he drove his Ferrari to a track record speed of 136.7 mph (220.0 km/h) at the [1966 event](1122358.md). Scarfiotti was also the last Italian to win it until [Kimi Antonelli](70530918.md) won the [2026 event](81006624.md), sixty years later.

Following the death of Bandini from burns sustained during the [1967 Monaco Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Monaco_Grand_Prix), Ferrari entered two F1 cars for Scarfiotti and Parkes in the non-championship [1967 Syracuse Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1967_Syracuse_Grand_Prix). Scarfiotti drove a 1966 3-litre [Ferrari 312](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ferrari_312) whereas Parkes drove a 1967 with the 1966 nose to accommodate his long frame.[^cite_note-11] They shared the victory when they crossed the finish line in an unusual dead heat. They were clocked at 113.65 mph (182.90 km/h), recording an official time of 1 hour 40 minutes 58 seconds for the 191.2-mile (307.7 km) race.[^cite_note-12] At the GPs of Zandvoort and Spa, both Parkes and Scarfiotti were part of a three-car Scuderia, with a 4-5-6 place finish at the Dutch GP suggesting equal speed of these three drivers, but both Parkes and Scarfiotti were one lap behind Amon, despite Parkes qualifying only 0.1 sec slower than Amon, with Scarfiotti being a full second off pace. At Spa, Parkes had a career-ending accident, while Scarfiotti was 4 laps behind and not classified. For the rest of the 1967 F1 WC Season, Ferrari entered only one car, for Chris Amon. For his home GP at Monza, the winner of the 1966 event secured a drive in the second [All American Racers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/All_American_Racers) [Eagle Mk1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eagle_Mk1), a quite promising move, as Dan Gurney had won the race at the very fast Spa circuit. However, both Weslake V12 failed early in the race.

With Ferrari hiring [Jacky Ickx](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacky_Ickx) and Amon for 1968, there was no place for Scarfiotti, who entered F1 races for Cooper instead. [Brian Redman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Redman) and Scarfiotti came in third and fourth respectively at the [1968 Spanish Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Spanish_Grand_Prix) in [Jarama](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuito_Permanente_Del_Jarama), both driving for [Cooper](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cooper_Car_Company). In his final Formula One appearance, Scarfiotti placed fourth in the [1968 Monaco Grand Prix](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1968_Monaco_Grand_Prix), an event marked by mechanical breakdowns that eliminated 11 of 16 starters before the race was completed.[^cite_note-13]

## Death

Scarfiotti died in 1968 at a [hillclimbing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillclimbing) event on the Roßfeldhöhenringstraße near [Berchtesgaden](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berchtesgaden), Germany, in the German Alps. He became the third Grand Prix driver to die in 1968, following [Jim Clark](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jim_Clark) and [Mike Spence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mike_Spence). Scarfiotti wrecked his [Porsche 910](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Porsche_910) during trials when the car veered abruptly off the Rossfeldstrasse track and catapulted ten yards down a tree-covered slope. The Porsche hung in the trees and Scarfiotti was thrown from the cockpit. He was discovered, badly injured, fifty yards away. He died in an ambulance of numerous fractures. [Huschke von Hanstein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huschke_von_Hanstein), the team manager of Porsche, stated that he had never been associated with a fatal accident during the eighteen years he had been in charge of the team. 60 yards (55 m) of burned rubber braking indicated that Scarfiotti had slammed on his brakes at the final moment.

Scarfiotti was married to Ida Benignetti and had two children from a previous relationship.[^cite_note-lud-1]
