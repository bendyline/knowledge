**Guangxi**,[^cite_note-5] officially the **Guangxi Zhuang Autonomous Region** (**GZAR**), is an [autonomous region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Autonomous_regions_of_China) of the [People's Republic of China](5405.md), located in [South China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_China) and bordering [Vietnam](202354.md) ([Tuyên Quang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuy%C3%AAn_Quang_Province), [Cao Bằng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cao_B%E1%BA%B1ng_Province), [Lạng Sơn](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L%E1%BA%A1ng_S%C6%A1n_Province) provinces, and [Quảng Ninh City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qu%E1%BA%A3ng_Ninh)) and the [Gulf of Tonkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tonkin). Formerly a [province](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Provinces_of_China), Guangxi became an autonomous region in 1958. Its current capital is [Nanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning).[^cite_note-6]

Guangxi's location, in mountainous terrain in the far south of China, has placed it on the frontier of Chinese civilization throughout much of [Chinese history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/History_of_China). The current name "Guang" means "expanse" and has been associated with the region since the creation of Guang Prefecture in 226 AD. It was given [provincial level status](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Administrative_divisions_of_the_Yuan_dynasty) during the [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty), but even into the 20th century, it was considered an open, wild territory. The abbreviation of the region (Chinese: [桂](https://en.wiktionary.org/wiki/%E6%A1%82); [Hanyu pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanyu_pinyin): *Guì*; Zhuang: *Gvei*) comes from the name of the city of [Guilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilin), the provincial capital during both the [Ming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming) and [Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing) dynasties.

Guangxi has the largest ethnic minority population in China. In 2021, a report stated the [Zhuang people](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people) accounted for 31.36% of the region's population. Various regional languages and dialects such as [Pinghua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinghua), [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_languages), [Kam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kam_language), [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese), [Hakka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hakka_Chinese), and [Min](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Chinese) are spoken alongside [Mandarin Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese).[^cite_note-7]

## Name

"*Guǎng*" ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 广; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 廣) means 'expanse' or 'vast', and has been associated with the region since the creation of Guang Prefecture in AD 226.[^cite_note-8] Guangxi and neighboring [Guangdong](65109.md) literally mean 'expanse west' and 'expanse east'. Together, Guangxi and Guangdong are called *[Liangguang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liangguang)* (*Liangkwang*; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 兩廣; [simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 两广; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *liǎng guǎng*; [Cantonese Yale](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yale_romanization_of_Cantonese): *léuhng gwóng*; lit. 'Two Expanses', [Vietnamese](32511.md): *Lưỡng Quảng*). During the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty), the Two Guangs were formally separated as *Guǎngnán Xīlù* (廣南西路; 广南西路; 'vast south west region') and *Guǎngnán Dōnglù* (廣南東路; 广南东路; 'vast south east region'), which became abbreviated as *Guǎngxī Lù* (廣西路; 广西路) and *Guǎngdōng Lù* (廣東路; 广东路).

Guangxi was also previously spelled as *Kwangsi* in [postal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_postal_romanization) and [Wade–Giles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wade%E2%80%93Giles) romanizations. The spelling of the province was replaced by the pinyin spelling of *Guangxi* in 1958 and has been widely used internationally after 1986. The official name was also known as *Kwangsi Chuang Autonomous Region* in a number of Western publications outside of China published in the 1950s to 1970s.[^cite_note-9]

Originally inhabited by a mixture of tribal groups known to the Chinese as the [Baiyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baiyue) ("Hundred Yue", [Vietnamese](32511.md): *Bách Việt*), the region first became part of China during the [Qin dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_dynasty). In 214 BC, the [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) general [Zhao Tuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhao_Tuo) ([Vietnamese](32511.md): *Triệu Đà*) claimed most of southern China for [Qin Shi Huang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_Shi_Huang) before the emperor's death. The ensuing civil war permitted Zhao to establish a separate kingdom at [Panyu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panyu_District) known as [Nanyue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanyue) ("Southern Yue"). Alternatively submissive to and independent of [Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_dynasty) control, Southern Yue expanded colonization and [sinicization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinicization) under its policy of "Harmonizing and Gathering the Hundred Yue" (和集百越) until [its collapse in 111 BC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han%E2%80%93Nanyue_War) during the [southward expansion of the Han dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southward_expansion_of_the_Han_dynasty).[^cite_note-10]

The name "Guangxi" can be traced to the "Expansive" or "Wide" province (廣州) of the [Eastern Wu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eastern_Wu), which controlled southeastern China during the [Three Kingdoms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Three_Kingdoms) period. [Guilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilin_Commandery) formed one of its commanderies.

Under the [Tang dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_dynasty), the Zhuang moved to support [Piluoge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Piluoge)'s kingdom of [Nanzhao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanzhao) in [Yunnan](166410.md), which successfully repulsed imperial armies in 751 and 754. Guangxi was then divided into an area of Zhuang ascendancy west of Nanning and an area of Han ascendancy east of Nanning.

After the collapse of the Southern Zhao, [Liu Yan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Liu_Yan_\(emperor\)) established the [Southern Han](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Han) (Nanhan) in Xingwangfu (modern [Guangdong](65109.md)). Although this state gained minimal control over Guangxi, it was plagued by instability and annexed by the [Song dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Song_dynasty) in 971. The name "Guangxi" itself can be traced to the Song, who administered the area as the Guangnanxi ("West Southern Expanse") [Circuit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Circuit_\(administrative_division\)). Harassed by both Song and the [Jiaozhi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozhi) in modern [Vietnam](202354.md), the Zhuang leader [Nong Zhigao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nong_Zhigao) led a revolt in 1052 for which he is still remembered by the Zhuang people. His independent kingdom was short-lived, however, and the tattooed Song general [Di Qing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Di_Qing) returned Guangxi to China.

The [Yuan dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_dynasty) established control over Yunnan during its conquest of the [Dali Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dali_Kingdom) in 1253 and eliminated the [Southern Song](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Song) following the [Battle of Yamen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Yamen) in 1279. Rather than ruling [Lingnan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingnan) as a subject territory or military district, the Mongolians then established Guangxi ("Western Expanse") as a proper province. The area nonetheless continued to be unruly, leading the [Ming dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ming_dynasty) to employ the different local groups against one another. At the [Battle of Big Rattan Gorge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Big_Rattan_Gorge?action=edit\&redlink=1) between the Zhuang and the [Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao_people_\(Asia\)) in 1465, 20,000 deaths were reported.

During the Ming and Qing dynasties, parts of Guangxi were ruled by the powerful [Cen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cen_\(surname\)) (岑) clan. The Cen were of Zhuang ethnicity and were recognized as *[tusi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tusi)* or local rulers by the Chinese emperors.

The [Qing dynasty](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qing_dynasty) left the region alone until the imposition of direct rule in 1726, but the 19th century was one of constant unrest. A [Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao_people_\(Asia\)) revolt in 1831 was followed by the [Jintian Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jintian_Uprising), the beginning of the [Taiping Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taiping_Rebellion), in January 1851 and the [Da Cheng Rebellion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Da_Cheng_Rebellion) in April 1854. The execution of St. [Auguste Chapdelaine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Auguste_Chapdelaine) by local officials in Guangxi provoked the [Second Opium War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Opium_War) in 1858 and the legalization of foreign interference in the interior. Although [Louis Brière de l'Isle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Louis_Bri%C3%A8re_de_l'Isle) was unable to invade its depot at [Longzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longzhou_County), the [Guangxi Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangxi_Army) saw a great deal of action in the 1884 [Sino-French War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-French_War). Largely ineffective within Vietnam, it was still able to repulse the French from China itself at the [Battle of Zhennan Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Bang_Bo_\(Zhennan_Pass\)) (modern [Friendship Pass](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Friendship_Pass)) on 23 March 1885.

Following the [Wuchang Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuchang_Uprising), Guangxi seceded from the Qing Empire on 6 November 1911. The Qing governor, [Shen Bingdan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shen_Bingdan?action=edit\&redlink=1), initially remained in place but was subsequently removed by a mutiny commanded by General [Lu Rongting](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lu_Rongting). General Lu's [Old Guangxi clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Guangxi_clique) overran [Hunan](166409.md) and [Guangdong](65109.md) as well and helped lead the [National Protection War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Protection_War) against [Yuan Shikai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuan_Shikai)'s attempt to re-establish an imperial government. Zhuang's loyalty made his [Self-Government Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-Government_Army) cohesive but reluctant to move far beyond its own provinces. Subsequent feuding with [Sun Yat-sen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sun_Yat-sen) led to defeat in the 1920 and 1921 [Guangdong–Guangxi War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangdong%E2%80%93Guangxi_War). After a brief occupation by [Chen Jiongming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Jiongming)'s Cantonese forces, Guangxi fell into disunity and profound banditry for several years[^cite_note-11] until [Li Zongren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_Zongren)'s [Guangxi Pacification Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangxi_Pacification_Army?action=edit\&redlink=1) established the [New Guangxi clique](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Guangxi_clique) dominated by Li, [Huang Shaohong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Huang_Shaohong), and [Bai Chongxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bai_Chongxi).

Successful action in Hunan against [Wu Peifu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wu_Peifu) led to the Zhuang GPA becoming known as the "Flying Army" and the "Army of Steel". After the death of Sun Yat-sen, Li also repulsed [Tang Jiyao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tang_Jiyao)'s [revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Guangxi_War) and joined the [Northern Expedition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Expedition) establishing control over other warlords by the [Republic of China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Republic_of_China_\(1912%E2%80%931949\)). His was one of the few [Kuomintang](16903.md) units free from serious [Chinese Communist Party](7175.md) (CCP) influence and was therefore employed by [Chiang Kai-shek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chiang_Kai-shek) for the [Shanghai massacre of 1927](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shanghai_massacre_of_1927). Within the People's Republic of China, Guangxi is also noted for the [Baise Uprising](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baise_Uprising), a failed CCP revolt led by [Chen Zhaoli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chen_Zhaoli?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Deng Xiaoping](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deng_Xiaoping) in 1929.

In 1937, the [Guangxi Women's Battalion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangxi_Women's_Battalion) was founded as a response to [Soong Mei-ling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soong_Mei-ling)'s appeal for women to support the [Sino-Japanese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Sino-Japanese_War).[^cite_note-\:12-12][^cite_note-13] Reports on the size of the battalion vary from 130 students,[^cite_note-\:3-14] to 500,[^cite_note-15] to 800.[^cite_note-\:12-12]

Being in the far south, Guangxi did not fall during the [Chinese Civil War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_Civil_War), but joined the People's Republic in December 1949, two months after its founding.

In 1952, a small section of Guangdong's coastline ([Qinzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinzhou), Lianzhou (now [Hepu County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hepu_County)), [Fangchenggang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangchenggang) and [Beihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beihai)) was given to Guangxi, giving it access to the sea. This was reversed in 1955, and then restored in 1965. This made Guangxi the only autonomous region which is not [landlocked](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landlocked).[^cite_note-16]

The [Guangxi Massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangxi_Massacre), during the [Cultural Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cultural_Revolution), involved the killing of 100,000 to 150,000 in the region in 1967 and 1968.[^cite_note-\:0-17][^cite_note-\:4-18]

While some development of [heavy industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Heavy_industry) occurred in the 1960s and 1970s, the region remained largely a scenic tourist destination. Even the economic growth of the 1990s seemed to leave Guangxi behind. However, in recent years, there has been a growing amount of industrialization and increasing concentration on cash crops. Per capita GDP has risen as industries in Guangdong transfer production to comparatively lower-wage areas in Guangxi.

During the [1979 Sino-Vietnamese War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sino-Vietnamese_War), Guangxi communities were important to the Chinese war effort.[^cite_note-\:Wang2-19]<sup>: 101 </sup> They supplied logistical support to the [People's Liberation Army](66890.md), including food and housing.[^cite_note-\:Wang2-19]<sup>: 101 </sup> [Militia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Militia_\(China\)) members from Guangxi performed tasks including building roads, bridges, trenches, other logistical efforts, and caring for the wounded.[^cite_note-\:Wang2-19]<sup>: 101 </sup>

Located in the southern part of the country, Guangxi is bordered by [Yunnan](166410.md) to the west, [Guizhou](173815.md) to the north, [Hunan](166409.md) to the northeast, and [Guangdong](65109.md) to the east and southeast.[^cite_note-\:1-20][^cite_note-\:2-21] It is also bordered by [Vietnam](202354.md) in the southwest and the [Gulf of Tonkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tonkin) in the south.[^cite_note-\:1-20][^cite_note-\:2-21] Its proximity to Guangdong is reflected in its name, with "Guang" ([simplified Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 广; [traditional Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Traditional_Chinese_characters): 廣; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *Guǎng*) being used in both names.[^cite_note-\:1-20]

Large portions of Guangxi are hilly and mountainous.[^cite_note-\:1-20][^cite_note-\:2-21] The northwest portion of Guangxi includes part of the [Yunnan-Guizhou Plateau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunnan%E2%80%93Guizhou_Plateau),[^cite_note-\:1-20] the [Jiuwan Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiuwan_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the [Fenghuang Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fenghuang_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1) both run through the north,[^cite_note-\:1-20] the [Nanling Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanling_Mountains) form the region's north-east border, and the [Yuecheng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yuecheng_Mountains)[^cite_note-\:1-20] and [Haiyang Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haiyang_Mountains) both branch from the Nanling Mountains. Also in the north are the [Duyao Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duyao_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1). The [Duyang Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duyang_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1) run through the west of Guangxi.[^cite_note-\:1-20] Near the center of the region are the [Da Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dayao_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Da Ming Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daming_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1). On the southeastern border are the [Yunkai Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yunkai_Mountains?action=edit\&redlink=1). Guangxi's highest point is [Kitten Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kitten_Mountain), in the Yuecheng Mountains, at 2,141 metres (7,024 ft).[^cite_note-\:2-21]

[Karst landforms](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst),[^cite_note-22] characterized by steep mountains and large caverns,[^cite_note-\:1-20] are common in Guangxi, accounting for 37.8 percent of its total land area.[^cite_note-\:2-21]

Guangxi is also home to several river systems, which flow into several different bodies of water: the [Qin River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin_River_\(Guangxi\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) and the [Nanliu River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanliu_River?action=edit\&redlink=1) both flow into the Gulf of Tonkin, several tributary rivers flow into the larger [Xiang River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_River) in neighbouring Hunan province, and the [Xi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_River) system flows southeast through the autonomous region into the [South China Sea](74209.md).[^cite_note-\:1-20]

### Human geography

Major cities in Guangxi include [Nanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning), [Liuzhou](1124071.md), [Guilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilin), and [Beihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beihai). Notable towns include [Longmen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longmen,_Pubei_County?action=edit\&redlink=1), [Sanjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjiang_Dong_Autonomous_County), and [Yangshuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangshuo). The [Xi River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xi_River) system provides waterways which connect to the [Pearl River Delta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River_Delta). Important seaports along Guangxi's short coastline on the [Gulf of Tonkin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_of_Tonkin) include Beihai, [Qinzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinzhou), and [Fangchenggang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangchenggang). [Pinglu Canal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinglu_Canal) was constructed to connect Xi River system and coastal Guangxi.[^cite_note-23]

### Climate

Guangxi has a [subtropical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subtropical) climate.[^cite_note-\:2-21] Summers are generally long, hot, and humid, lasting from April to October.[^cite_note-\:1-20] Winters are mild, and snow is rare.[^cite_note-\:1-20] The autonomous region's average annual temperature ranges from 17.5 °C (63.5 °F) to 23.5 °C (74.3 °F),[^cite_note-\:2-21] with January temperatures typically ranging from 4 °C (39 °F) to 16 °C (61 °F),[^cite_note-\:1-20] and July temperatures typically ranging from 27 °C (81 °F) to 32 °C (90 °F).[^cite_note-\:1-20]

Due to frequent rain-bearing [monsoon](57630.md) winds, average annual precipitation is quite high in Guangxi, ranging from 1,080 millimetres (43 in) in drier zones to 1,730 millimetres (68 in) in wetter zones.[^cite_note-\:1-20] The region also experiences monsoons, blowing from south-southwest from late April to the beginning of October. Most of the precipitation occurs between May and August.[^cite_note-\:1-20] [Microbursts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microbursts) can also occasionally occur in the extreme south of the region, from July to September.[^cite_note-\:1-20] This is caused by [typhoons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Typhoon) blowing from the [South China Sea](74209.md).[^cite_note-\:1-20]

### Ethnic groups

The [Han Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Chinese) are the largest ethnic group in Guangxi. Han Chinese populations in Guangxi largely live along the autonomous region's southern coast and eastern portions.[^cite_note-\:2-21] Of these, the main subgroups are those that speak [Yue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yue_Chinese) and [Southwestern Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Mandarin) varieties of [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language_family). Qinzhou and Goulou Yue are spoken in the southern and eastern regions, respectively. [Pinghua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinghua) is spoken in Nanning and Guilin. There are Hakka-speaking regions in [Luchuan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luchuan_County), [Bobai County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bobai_County) and in some areas bordering Vietnam.

Guangxi has over 16 million [Zhuangs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people), the largest minority ethnicity in China. Over 90 percent of Zhuang in China live in Guangxi, especially in the central and western regions. High concentrations of Zhuang people can be found in [Nanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning), [Liuzhou](1124071.md), [Chongzuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongzuo), [Baise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baise), [Hechi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hechi), and [Laibin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laibin).[^cite_note-\:2-21] The highest concentration of ethnic Zhuang people is found in the [county-level city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County-level_city) of [Jingxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingxi,_Guangxi), with a 2021 publication by the People's Government of Guangxi stating that Jingxi's population is 99.7% Zhuang.[^cite_note-\:2-21]

The autonomous region also has sizable populations of indigenous [Yao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yao_people_\(Asia\)), [Miao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miao_people), [Kam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kam_people), [Mulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulao_people), [Maonan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maonan_people), [Hui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hui_people), [Bouyei](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bouyei_people), [Gin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gin_people), [Yi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yi_people), [Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_people) and [Gelao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelao_people) peoples.[^cite_note-\:2-21][^cite_note-53] Other [ethnic minorities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_minorities_in_China) in Guangxi include the [Manchu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manchu_people), [Mongol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mongols), [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Koreans), [Tibetan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tibetan_people), [Hlai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hlai_people), and [Tujia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tujia_people) people.[^cite_note-\:2-21]

## Economy



View of [Nanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning), the capital and economic center of Guangxi

Important crops in Guangxi include [rice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rice), [maize](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maize) and [sweet potatoes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweet_potato). Cash crops include [sugar cane](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sugar_cane), [peanuts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peanut), [tobacco](30942.md), and [kenaf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenaf).

85 percent of the world's [star anise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_anise) is grown in Guangxi. It is a major ingredient in the antiviral [oseltamivir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oseltamivir).[^cite_note-60]

Guangxi is one of China's key production centers for nonferrous metals. The region holds approximately 1/3 of all [tin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tin) and [manganese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Manganese) deposits in China.[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-61]

Liuzhou is the main industrial center and a major motor vehicle manufacturing center. [General Motors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Motors) have a manufacturing base here in a joint venture as [SAIC-GM-Wuling Automobile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SAIC-GM-Wuling_Automobile). The city also has a large steel factory and several related industries. The local government of Guangxi hopes to expand the region's manufacturing sector, and during the drafting of China's Five Year Plan in 2011, earmarked 2.6 trillion RMB for investment in the region's Beibu Gulf Economic Zone(See Below).[^cite_note-thechinaperspective.com-61]

In recent years Guangxi's economy has languished behind that of its wealthy neighbor and twin, [Guangdong](65109.md). Guangxi's 2017 [nominal GDP](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nominal_GDP) was about 2039.63 billion yuan (US\$302.09 billion) and ranked 17th in China. Its [per capita GDP](12594.md) was 38,102 yuan (US\$5,770).[^cite_note-62]

Due to its lack of a major manufacturing industry in comparison to other provincial-level regions, Guangxi is the fourth most energy efficient provincial-level region in China, helping to further boost its green image.[^cite_note-63]

As the only coastal region in China with close proximity to [Southeast Asia](28741.md), Guangxi holds a strategic position in China's trade with the [Association of Southeast Asian Nations](106539.md) (ASEAN).[^cite_note-64]

### Investment

Seventy-one Taiwanese ventures started up in Guangxi in 2007, with contracts bringing up to US\$149 million of investment, while gross exports surpassed US\$1 billion. There are a total of 1182 Taiwan ventures in Guangxi, and by the end of 2006, they have brought a total of US\$4.27 billion of investment into the autonomous region. During the first half of 2007, 43 projects worthy of RMB2.6 billion (US\$342 million) have already been contracted between Guangxi and Taiwan investors. Cooperation between Guangxi and Taiwan companies mainly relates to manufacturing, high-tech electronic industries, agriculture, energy resources, and tourism.

### Power

Guangxi Power Grid invested 180 million yuan in 2007 in projects to bring power to areas that still lacked access to [electricity](9550.md). The areas affected include [Nanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning), [Hechi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hechi), [Bose](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bose,_Guangxi) and [Guigang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guigang). Around 125,000 people have gained access to electricity. The money has been used to build or alter 738 10-kilovolt distribution units with a total length of wire reaching 1,831.8 kilometers.

Due to a lack of investment in construction in the [power grid](20344155.md) net in rural areas, more than 400 villages in Guangxi Province were not included in the projects. Around 500,000 cannot participate in the policy known as "The Same Grid, the Same Price". Guangxi Power Grid will invest 4.6 billion yuan in improving the power grid during the 11th Five Year Plan.

Guangxi Power Grid invested 2.5 billion yuan in building an electric power system in the first half of 2007. Of the total investment, 2.3 billion yuan has been put into the project of the main power grid. So far, four new transformer substations in Guangxi are in various stages of completion. Wenfu substation went into operation in the city of Hechi in January 2007, and since then it has become a major hub of the electrical power system of the surrounding three counties. When the Cangwu substation was completed, it doubled the local transformer capacity. In June 2007, the new substation in Chongzuo passed its operation tests. And in the same month, Qiulong commenced production too. This shall support the power supply system of [Qiulong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qiulong) City, as well as the northern part of the Guangxi region, and facilitate the nationwide project to transmit power from west to east.

### Beibu Gulf Economic Zone

See also: [Beibu Gulf Economic Rim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beibu_Gulf_Economic_Rim)

In late February 2008, the central government approved China's first international and regional economic cooperation zone in Guangxi. The construction of the [Beibu Gulf Economic Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beibu_Gulf_Economic_Zone) began in 2006. With the approval, the Beibu Gulf Economic Zone will be formally incorporated into national development strategies.

The Beibu Gulf Economic Zone covers six coastal cities along the [Beibu Gulf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beibu_Gulf). It integrates the cities of [Nanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning), the region's capital, [Beihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beihai), [Qinzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinzhou), [Fangchenggang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangchenggang), [Chongzuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongzuo) and [Yulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulin,_Guangxi). The state will adopt policies and measures to support mechanism innovation, rational industry layout, and infrastructure construction in the Beibu Gulf Economic Zone.

Guangxi has pledged a 100 billion yuan (US\$14 billion) investment over the next five years for building and repairing 2,500 km (1,600 mi) railways to form a network hub in the area. Beibu Gulf Zone will serve as the [logistics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Logistics) base, business base, processing and manufacturing base, and information exchange center for China-[ASEAN](106539.md) cooperation. Beibu Gulf Zone promises broad prospects for further development and its growth potential is rapidly released. But the shortage of talent and professionals in [petrochemicals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petrochemicals), [iron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iron) and [steel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steel), [electricity](9550.md), [finance](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Finance), [tourism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tourism), [port planning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_planning?action=edit\&redlink=1), logistics and [marine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_\(ocean\)) industries are bottlenecks.

The regional government is also working on speeding up key cooperation projects including [transportation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transport_in_the_People's_Republic_of_China), the marine industry, agriculture, forestry, fisheries, energy development, cross-border tourism, and environmental protection. Beibu Gulf has already attracted several major projects such as Qinzhou [oil refinery](195137.md) projects and [Stora Enso](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stora_Enso), a [Fortune 500](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortune_500) forest products company based in [Finland](10577.md). In January 2008 trade import and export in the Beibu Gulf zone exceeded US\$1.3 billion, a record high.

### Bauxite reserves

In September 2007, China's [Ministry of Commerce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Commerce_of_the_People's_Republic_of_China) said that it has found 120 million tons of new [bauxite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bauxite) reserves in Guangxi. The ministry said that the new reserves, which are located in [Chongzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chongzhou) in the southern region of [Youjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Youjiang), have very high-quality bauxite, a raw material for making [aluminum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aluminum). Currently, the proven reserves of bauxite in Guangxi are about 1 billion tons, making the province one of the country's biggest bauxite sources.

### Rail



The [Guizhou–Guangxi Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guizhou%E2%80%93Guangxi_Railway) near the Layi Station in [Nandan County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nandan_County), [Hechi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hechi)

The [Hunan–Guangxi Railway (Xianggui Line)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan%E2%80%93Guangxi_Railway), which bisects the autonomous region diagonally from [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou_County) in the northeast on the border with [Hunan](166409.md) to [Pingxiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pingxiang,_Guangxi) in the southwest on the border with [Vietnam](202354.md), passes through Guangxi's three principal cities, [Nanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning), [Liuzhou](1124071.md) and [Guilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilin). Most other railways in Guangxi are connected to the Xianggui Line.

From Nanning, the [Nanning–Kunming Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning%E2%80%93Kunming_Railway) heads west through [Baise](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baise) to [Kunming](197181.md), [Yunnan](166410.md) and the Nanning–Fangchenggang Railway runs south to [Qinzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qinzhou), [Fangchenggang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fangchenggang) and [Beihai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beihai) on the coast. From Liuzhou, the [Guizhou–Guangxi Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guizhou%E2%80%93Guangxi_Railway) extends northwestward through [Hechi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hechi) to [Guizhou](173815.md) and the [Jiaozuo–Liuzhou Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiaozuo%E2%80%93Liuzhou_Railway) runs due north to [Hunan](166409.md), and eventually [Hubei](83319.md) and [Henan](166408.md) in [central China](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_Central_China). From [Litang Township](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litang,_Guangxi) on the Xianggui Line between Nanning and Liuzhou, the [Litang–Qinzhou Railway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litang%E2%80%93Qinzhou_Railway) runs south to Qinzhou on the coast and the [Litang–Zhanjiang Railway (Lizhan Line)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Litang%E2%80%93Zhanjiang_Railway) extends southeastward through [Guigang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guigang) and [Yulin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yulin,_Guangxi) to [Zhanjiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhanjiang), [Guangdong](65109.md).

The [Luoyang–Zhanjiang Railway (Luozhan Line)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luoyang%E2%80%93Zhanjiang_Railway), which intersects with the Xianggui Line on the Hunan side of the border at [Yongzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yongzhou), runs south through [Hezhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hezhou) and [Wuzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuzhou) in eastern Guangxi and joins the Lizhan Line at Yulin. At [Cenxi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenxi), a branch of the Luozhan Line heads east to [Maoming](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maoming), Guangdong, forming a second rail outlet from Guangxi to Guangdong.

### Aviation

Guangxi has 7 airports in different cities: Nanning, Guilin, Beihai, Liuzhou, Wuzhou, Baise, and Hechi.

## Culture

Main articles: [Lingnan culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lingnan_culture) and [Zhuang customs and culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_customs_and_culture)



Pagodas in [Guilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilin)

"Guangxi" and neighbouring [Guangdong](65109.md) literally mean "Western Expanse" and "Eastern Expanse". Together, Guangdong and Guangxi are called the "Two Expanses" (simplified Chinese: 两广; traditional Chinese: 兩廣; pinyin: *[Liǎngguǎng](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li%C7%8Enggu%C7%8Eng)*).

Its culture and language are reflected in this. Though now associated with the [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people) ethnic minority, Guangxi's culture traditionally has had a close connection with Cantonese. Cantonese culture and language followed the Xi River valley from Guangdong and are still predominant in the eastern half of Guangxi today. Outside of this area, there is a huge variety of ethnicities and language groups represented.

Guangxi is known for its ethnolinguistic diversity. In the capital of [Nanning](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nanning), for example, three varieties of Chinese are spoken locally: [Southwestern Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Mandarin), [Yue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yue_Chinese) (specifically [Cantonese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_language)), and [Pinghua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinghua), in addition to various [Zhuang languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_languages) and others.

### Cuisine

Main article: [Guangxi cuisine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guangxi_cuisine)

Guangxi cuisine is known as *guìcài* ([Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 桂菜); however, it is not as affluent as that of its more known neighbours such as [Sichuan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sichuan_cuisine), [Hunan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hunan_cuisine) or [Guangdong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese_cuisine). Much of Guangxi's local cuisine is centered around the cuisine of its ethnic minorities. This includes the more well known [Luosifen soup](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luosifen), [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simplified_Chinese_characters): 桂林米粉; [pinyin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinyin): *guìlín mǐfěn* and a variety of pickled dishes.

### Languages

Guangxi is highly linguistically diverse. Aside from [Chinese languages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_languages) such as [Guiliu Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiliu_Mandarin) and [Yue Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yue_Chinese), Guangxi is also home to significant [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_language), [Kam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kam_language), [Mulam](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mulam_language), [Maonan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maonan_language), [Hmong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hmong_language), [Mien](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mien_language), [Bunu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bunu_language), [Lajia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lajia_language), [Sui](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sui_language), [Gelao](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gelao_language), [Nuosu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuosu_language), and [Vietnamese](32511.md) (Kinh) speaking populations.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELi2012166-70]

#### Chinese languages

Significant populations of almost all top-level [Chinese language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_language) families can be found in Guangxi.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELi2012166–176-71]

11.6 million [Yue Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yue_Chinese) speakers live in Guangxi, with the majority of them speaking either [Goulou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Goulou_Yue) (6.8 million) or [Yongxun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yong-Xun_Yue) (4.7 million) varieties. Smaller populations of [Guangfu](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cantonese) (720 thousand) and [Qinlian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qin-Lian_Yue) (3.9 million) speakers can also be found, primarily distributed along the [Pearl River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pearl_River) and the southern coast of the province respectively.

Of the 5.4 million [dialectal Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandarin_Chinese) speakers who call Guangxi home, 5.2 million speak [Guiliu Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guiliu_Mandarin), a subgroup of [Southwestern Mandarin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southwestern_Mandarin). Smaller populations speak varieties closely related to that of southern [Guizhou](173815.md) (265 thousand) and [Yunnan](166410.md) (123 thousand). These speakers of dialectal Mandarin are primarily distributed in the north and west of the province, in prefectures such as [Guilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilin) and [Liuzhou](1124071.md).

Populations of what is typically called [Pinghua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pinghua) can also be found. 1.8 million speak [Northern Pinghua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Northern_Pinghua), and 2.4 million speak [Southern Pinghua](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southern_Pinghua); the latter of which is more closely related to Yue. There are also around 1.3 million [Xiang Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Xiang_Chinese) speakers, primarily found in and around [Quanzhou](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quanzhou,_Guangxi) county, and 140 thousand [Min Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Min_Chinese) speakers.

Almost all counties in Guangxi have multiple varieties of Chinese natively spoken.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELi2012B2-2-72]

## Tourism



[Cormorant fisherman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cormorant_fishing) on the [Li River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_River) in [Yangshuo County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangshuo_County)

The major tourist attraction of Guangxi is [Guilin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guilin), a city famed across China and the world for its spectacular setting by the [Li Jiang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Li_River_\(Guangxi\)) (Li River) among [karst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karst) peaks. It also used to be the capital of Guangxi and [Jingjiang Princes' City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jingjiang_Princes'_City), the old princes' residence, is open to the public. South of Guilin down the river is the town of [Yangshuo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yangshuo), which has become a favourite destination for foreign tourists.

The variety of visible cultures in Guangxi, such as the [Zhuang](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zhuang_people) and [Dong](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dong_people), are also a draw for tourists. The northern part of the region, bordering [Guizhou](173815.md), is home to the [Longsheng Rice Terraces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Longsheng_Rice_Terrace), some of the steepest in the world. Nearby is [Sanjiang Dong Autonomous County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanjiang_Dong_Autonomous_County).

Many Chinese tourists visiting Nanning also visit [Ban Gioc–Detian Falls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ban_Gioc%E2%80%93Detian_Falls) on the China-Vietnam border.
