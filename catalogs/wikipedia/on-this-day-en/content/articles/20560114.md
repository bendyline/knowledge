**Law enforcement in North Macedonia** is the responsibility of the Police of the Republic of North Macedonia ([Macedonian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Macedonian_language): Полиција на Република Северна Македонија, [romanized](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Romanization_of_Macedonian): *Policija na Republika Severna Makedonija*).[^cite_note-4]

The police headquarters are located in [Skopje](29618.md) at the [Ministry of Internal Affairs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ministry_of_Internal_Affairs_\(North_Macedonia\)), and also maintain an air base in nearby Idrizovo. Taiwan, Canada, and Italy have all contributed [Bell Helicopter Textron](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bell_Helicopter_Textron) helicopters to the police force's Macedonian Police Aviation Unit in cooperation with the [Air Force of North Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_of_the_Republic_of_North_Macedonia) to help combat insurgencies the country.[^cite_note-5]

The law enforcement agencies of North Macedonia are regulated by the Constitution of 1991, the Law on Internal Affairs of 1995, the Criminal Procedure Code of 1997 and the Draft Law on Changes and Additions to the Criminal Procedure Code.[^cite_note-6] With police actions and behavior controlled by the Criminal Procedure Code, and the Law on Internal Affairs controlling the use of firearms by the police force. On July 1, 2003, the Law on the Police Academy was enacted, creating a police academy to train civil and border police officers.[^cite_note-7]

The force has been the subject of a number of recent reforms regarding both the Albanian insurgency,[^cite_note-article-8] and possible violations of [human rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_rights_in_North_Macedonia),[^cite_note-9][^cite_note-10] with [NATO](21133.md) officials stating that the force was "not really up to European standards" as policemen lacked in skills and weapons.[^cite_note-11][^cite_note-report-12]

## History

The Police were subject to scrutiny during Albanian riots on 9 July 1997 when protesters collected in the western town of [Gostivar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gostivar). Over 200 were wounded and three killed (two shot, one beaten to death)[^cite_note-report-12] in a resulting clash with police riot squads, and the Humans Rights Watch investigated allegations of police brutality.[^cite_note-report-12] These events underlined a continuing friction between the Macedonian Police force and [Albanians living in North Macedonia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albanians_in_North_Macedonia).[^cite_note-report-12] The [International Helsinki Federation for Human Rights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Helsinki_Federation_for_Human_Rights) reported that police abuse of suspects, particularly during initial arrest and detention, and police harassment of ethnic minorities is ongoing.[^cite_note-Helsinki-13]

The Police of North Macedonia work closely with the NATO peacekeepers in patrolling areas with high numbers of ethnic Albanians ever since the [2001 Macedonia conflict](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2001_Macedonia_conflict) ended, and have been receiving weapons from surrendering Albanian insurgents.[^cite_note-article-8] Violent attacks, however, still occur as in 2001 three officers were killed by Albanian gunmen.[^cite_note-14]

A notable incident was when the Macedonian Police arrested a [Horse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horse) in 2009 used by a crime smuggling ring to smuggle [air conditioners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_conditioning) from [Serbia](29265.md). At that time the Police were not sure whether to press charges or not, but ultimately decided to not press charges against the Horse.[^cite_note-15]

On the 2nd of December, 2022, during a routine traffic stop, а driver was asked to show his documents, to which the driver didn't comply and proceeded to call an armed group. Said group proceeded to chase the police officers out of [Aračinovo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ara%C4%8Dinovo) and into the Gazi Baba police station.[^cite_note-16][^cite_note-17][^cite_note-18]
