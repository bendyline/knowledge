# World Press Freedom Index



**2026 World Press Freedom Index[^cite_note-\:3-1]**&#x20;

&#x20; Good: >85 points

&#x20; Satisfactory: 70–85 points

&#x20; Problematic: 55–70 points

&#x20; Difficult: 40–55 points

&#x20; Very serious: <40 points

&#x20; Not classified

The **World Press Freedom Index** (**WPFI**) is an annual ranking of 180 [countries](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Country) compiled and published by [Reporters Without Borders](526054.md) (RSF) since 2002 based upon the assessment of the [non-governmental organization](46539.md) as well as surveys of professionals around the world, of the countries' [press freedom](105323.md) records in the previous year.[^cite_note-RWBPFIHow-2][^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-\:2-4] It intends to reflect the degree of [freedom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom) that [journalists](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalist), [news organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News_media), and [netizens](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netizen) have in each country, and the efforts made by authorities to respect this freedom.[^cite_note-\:0-3]

## Methodology

The WPFI is based on a [questionnaire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Questionnaire) for all categories except safety, which RSF monitors.[^cite_note-\:0-3] The questionnaire is sent to partner organizations, correspondents, researchers and human rights experts who are all evaluating the country in which they live.[^cite_note-\:0-3] As of 2013, that included 18 [freedom of expression](21401843.md) non-governmental organizations located in five continents, its 150 [correspondents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Correspondent) around the world and journalists, researchers, jurists and human rights activists.[^cite_note-RWBPFIHow-2]

The methodology from 2013 to 2021[^cite_note-5] used seven general criteria: [pluralism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_pluralism) (measures the degree of [representation of opinions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Public_opinion) in the media space), [media independence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Independent_media), environment and [self-censorship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-censorship), legislative framework, [transparency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_transparency), infrastructure, and [abuses](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Political_repression).[^cite_note-methodology-2022-onwards-6] In 2013, Reporters Without Borders said that the WPFI only deals with press freedom and does not measure the quality of [journalism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Journalism) in the countries it assesses.[^cite_note-RWBPFIHow-2]

Starting in 2022, the qualitative survey was updated to reflect needs in a more digital era and was first combined with a quantitative score press freedom incidents.[^cite_note-7] The [scores](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Test_score) are evaluated against five distinct categories: political context, legal framework, economic context, sociocultural context and [safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety_of_journalists).[^cite_note-8][^cite_note-\:2-4]

### Political context

This category aims to evaluate the [autonomy](191145.md) of media and the degree of support to the media to keep [government](12229.md) and officials accountable.

### Legal framework

The questionnaire takes account of the [legal framework for the media](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Media_policy) (including [penalties](870351.md) for press offences, the existence of a [state monopoly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_monopoly) for certain kinds of media and how the media are regulated) and the level of independence of the [public media](178808.md). It also includes [violations of the free flow of information on the Internet](11056386.md).

### Economic context

This category aims to evaluate economic constraints put on the press by carrying out its mission. This includes loss of public financing, [concentration of media ownership](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Concentration_of_media_ownership), and pressure from advertisers, donors or owners.[^cite_note-\:1-9][^cite_note-10]

### Sociocultural context

This category aims to evaluate [the social and cultural constraints](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Social_environment) put on journalists to [self-censor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Self-censorship) against covering specific issues because it would be in opposition to the [predominant culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dominant_culture) of a country.[^cite_note-11]

### Safety

RSF uses its global monitoring of the safety of journalists to score this category.[^cite_note-\:0-3][^cite_note-12][^cite_note-13] This category aims to evaluate journalists' [safety](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safety) in disseminating [news](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/News) without the risk of [bodily harm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bodily_harm), [psychological or emotional distress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mental_distress) or [professional harm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Psychosocial_hazard).

[Violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violence) against journalists, netizens, and media assistants, including abuses attributable to the [state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_\(polity\)), [armed](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Weapon) [militias](20622.md), [clandestine organizations](14558092.md) or [pressure groups](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Advocacy_group), are monitored by RSF staff during the year and are also part of the final score. A higher score on the report corresponds to greater freedom of the press as reported by the organization.

## Analysis

Between 2002–2014, a study found that the ratings became more similar to the press freedom ratings by [Freedom House](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Freedom_House) and that more data became available to make ratings more precise. The authors also found a significant correlation with the [United Nations Human Development Index](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Nations_Human_Development_Index) scores.[^cite_note-24] As of 2026, [South Africa](17416221.md) had been outperforming peer countries due to its robust [civil society](209443.md).[^cite_note-25]
