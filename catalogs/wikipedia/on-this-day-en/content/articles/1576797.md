The **Iraqi Ground Forces** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic): القوات البرية العراقية), also referred to as the **Iraqi Army** ([Arabic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arabic): الجيش العراقي), is the ground force component of the [Iraqi Armed Forces](4627429.md). The current commander is Lt. Gen. Qassim Muhammad Salih.[^cite_note-3]

The Iraqi Army in its modern form was first created by the [United Kingdom](31717.md) during the [inter-war period](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interwar_period) of *de facto* British control of [Mandatory Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Iraq). It was formerly known as the **Royal Iraqi Army** up until the [coup of July 1958](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14_July_Revolution). Following the [invasion of Iraq by U.S. forces](201936.md) in 2003, the Iraqi Army was rebuilt along U.S. lines with large amounts of U.S. military assistance at every level. Because of the [Iraqi insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_insurgency_\(2003%E2%80%932011\)) that began shortly after the invasion, the Iraqi Army was later refocused as a counter-insurgency force.[^cite_note-mssiaug06-4][^cite_note-5] After the [withdrawal of U.S. troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Withdrawal_of_U.S._troops_from_Iraq_\(2007%E2%80%932011\)) in 2010, Iraqi forces assumed full responsibility for security.[^cite_note-6] A *[New York Times](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_York_Times)* article suggested that, between 2004 and 2014, the U.S. had provided the Iraqi Army with \$25 billion in training and equipment in addition to an even larger sum from the Iraqi treasury.[^cite_note-The_New_York_Times-7]

The Army extensively collaborated with the [Popular Mobilization Forces](45671374.md) during anti-[ISIL](9087364.md) operations.

## History



An Iraqi soldier assigned to the Security Battalion, Nineveh Operations Command, fires his [AK-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47) rifle during night range training at Camp Taji, Iraq, April 3, 2016.

The modern Iraqi armed forces were established by the [United Kingdom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_Kingdom_of_Great_Britain_and_Ireland) during their [mandate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandate_for_Mesopotamia) over [Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ottoman_Iraq) after [World War I](4764461.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002148-8] Before that, from 1533 to 1918, Iraq was under the rule of the [Ottoman Empire](22278.md) and fought as part of the [Military of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_of_the_Ottoman_Empire). At first, the British created the [Iraq Levies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Levies), comprising several battalions of troops whose main mission was to garrison the [bases](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Air_base) of the [Royal Air Force](25679.md) (RAF) with which [London](17867.md) controlled [Iraq](7515928.md). The Levies were adequate for their intended mission of defending airfields of [RAF Iraq Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Iraq_Command), but the threat of war with the newly forming [Republic of Turkey](11125639.md) forced the British to expand Iraq's indigenous military forces.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002148-8]

[Ankara](802.md) claimed the Ottoman [*vilayet* of Mosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mosul_Vilayet) as part of their country, during their resistance to the [partitioning of the Ottoman Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Partition_of_the_Ottoman_Empire). This province corresponds to the northern third of modern Iraq, mainly [Iraqi Kurdistan](679693.md), and includes the rich oilfields of [Kirkuk](199292.md).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002148-8] In 1920, Turkish troops penetrated into Iraqi Kurdistan and forced the small British garrisons out of [as-Sulaymaniyyah](653537.md) and [Rawanduz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rawandiz) in eastern Kurdistan. This led the British to form the Iraqi Army on 6 January 1921 (later to be marked as Iraqi Army Day),[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAl-MarashiSalama2008206-9] followed by the [Iraqi Air Force](1817852.md) in 1927. The British recruited former Ottoman officers to man junior and middle ranks of the new Iraqi officer corps, with senior commands, as well as most training positions, being manned by British officers.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002148-8]

The [Musa al-Kadhim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Musa_al-Kadhim) Brigade consisted of ex-Iraqi-Ottoman officers, whose barracks were located in [Kadhimyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kadhimiya). The United Kingdom provided support and training to the Iraqi Army and the Iraqi Air Force through a small military mission based in [Baghdad](4492.md);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyman200525-10] providing weapons and training to defeat the anticipated Turkish invasion of northern Iraq.

### Royal Iraqi Army

In August 1921, the British installed [Hashemite](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashemites) King [Faisal I](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_I_of_Iraq) as the client ruler of [Mandatory Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mandatory_Iraq). Faisal had been forced out as the [King of Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_Syrian_monarchs#Hashemite_dynasty) by the [French](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/French_Third_Republic) in the aftermath of the [Franco-Syrian War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franco-Syrian_War) in 1920. Likewise, British authorities selected [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) [Arab](2185.md) elites from the region for appointments to government and ministry offices in Iraq. The British and the Iraqis formalized the relationship between the two nations with the [Anglo-Iraqi Treaty of 1922](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iraqi_Treaty_of_1922). With Faisal's ascension to the throne, the Iraqi Army became the Royal Iraqi Army (RIrA).

In 1922, the army totalled 3,618 men. This was well below the 6,000 men requested by the Iraqi monarchy and even less than the limit set by the British of 4,500. Unattractive salaries hindered early recruiting efforts. At this time, the United Kingdom maintained the right to levy local forces like the British-officered [Iraq Levies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Levies) which were under direct British control. With a strength of 4,984 men, the Iraq Levies outnumbered the army.

In 1924, the army grew to 5,772 men and, by the following year, had grown even more to reach 7,500 men - maintaining this size until 1933. The force consisted of:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAl-MarashiSalama200823–24-11]

- Six [infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infantry) [battalions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battalion),

- Three [cavalry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cavalry) [regiments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regiment),

- Two [mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mountain_warfare) regiments,

- One field [artillery](2508.md) [battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artillery_battery).

By the late 1920s, the threat of Turkish attack diminished, with the Iraqi army refocusing on new, internal missions. While the British command still worried about both Turkish and [Persian](14653.md) encroachment on the Iraqi territory - as both of these states were considerably more cohesive and with superior armies -, the new focus shifted towards internal security against [centrifugal forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Centrifugal_force) menacing to breakdown the country.

Those threats to the integrity of the nascent Iraqi state were separatist revolts by the [Kurds](17068.md) and by the powerful [tribes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_tribes_in_Iraq) of western and southern Iraq.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002148-8] The British concluded the Iraqi army was not capable of handling either the Turks or the Persians, with the RAF (supported by the [Iraq Levies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Levies)) shouldering the full responsibility for external defense.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002149-12] Henceforth, the Iraqi army was increasingly relegated to [internal security](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Internal_security) duties. Nevertheless, the army enjoyed considerable prestige, with the country's elites seeing the army as a national consolidating force:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002149-12]

- A strong army ensured [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni_Islam) dominance over the [Shia](26961.md) majority;

- Said strong army would allow [Baghdad](4492.md) to control the independent tribes who resisted centralization;

- The army would create a [national identity](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_identity).



Mountain gun of the Iraqi Army column, 'Dicol', shelling Shirwan-A-Mazin from a hillside at Kani-Ling during the [anti-Barzani operations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Barzani_revolt), June 1932.

The army served as a modernizing and socializing force that helped combine the Ottoman *[vilayets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vilayet)* into a unified [Iraqi nation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_nationalism).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESimon1986116–119-13]

There were doubts about the army's actual capabilities, however. In 1928, the number of British officers commanding Iraqi units was increased because Iraqi officers were slow to adapt to modern warfare.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESimon1986116-14] The army's first real test occurred in 1931, when Kurdish leader [Ahmed Barzani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Barzani) unified a number of [Kurdish](3979351.md) tribes and rose up in [open revolt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Barzani_revolt). Iraqi army units were badly mauled by tribesmen under Shaykhs Mahmud and [Mustafa Barzani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Barzani). The Iraqi army's dismal performance did not impress, and the situation required the intervention of British troops to restore order.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002149-12]

In 1932, the [Kingdom of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Iraq) was granted official independence.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002149-12] This was in accordance with the [Anglo-Iraqi Treaty of 1930](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iraqi_Treaty_of_1930), whereby the [United Kingdom](31717.md) would end its official mandate on the condition that the Iraqi government would allow British advisers to take part in government affairs, allow British military bases to remain, and a requirement that Iraq assist the United Kingdom in wartime.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhareebDougherty2004-15]

The new state was weak and the regime survived for only four years, when it was toppled in a *[coup d'état](25914154.md)* in 1936. Upon achieving independence in 1932, political tensions arose over the continued British presence in Iraq, with Iraq's government and politicians split between those considered pro-British and those who were considered anti-British. The pro-British faction was represented by politicians such as [Nuri as-Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuri_al-Said) who did not oppose a continued British presence. The anti-British faction was represented by politicians such as [Rashid Ali al-Gaylani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Ali_al-Gaylani) who demanded that remaining British influence in the country be removed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhareebDougherty2004-15] In 1936, General [Bakr Sidqi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bakr_Sidqi), who had won a reputation from suppressing [tribal revolts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1935%E2%80%931936_Iraqi_Shia_revolts) (and also responsible for the ruthless [Simele massacre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simele_massacre)), was named Chief of the General [Staff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staff_\(military\)) and successfully pressured King [Ghazi bin Faisal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ghazi_of_Iraq) to demand that the Cabinet resign.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFiner1962157–158-16] From that year to 1941, five army coups occurred during each year led by the chief officers of the army against the government to pressure the government to concede to Army demands.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhareebDougherty2004-15]

### 1941 coup

Main article: [1941 Iraqi coup d'état](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1941_Iraqi_coup_d'%C3%A9tat)

In early April 1941, during [World War II](32927.md), [Rashid Ali al-Gaylani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rashid_Ali_al-Gaylani) and members of the anti-British "[Golden Square](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Square_\(Iraq\))" launched a [*coup d'état* against the current government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1941_Iraqi_coup_d'%C3%A9tat). [Prime Minister](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_prime_ministers_of_Iraq) [Taha al-Hashimi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taha_al-Hashimi) resigned and Rashid Ali al-Gaylani took his place as Prime Minister. Rashid Ali also proclaimed himself chief of a "National Defence Government." He did not overthrow the monarchy, but installed a more compliant [regent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regent). He also attempted to restrict the rights of the British which were granted them under the 1930 treaty.

The Golden Square was commanded by the "Four Colonels":[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyman200521-17]

- Colonel [Salah ed-Din es-Sabbagh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salah_al-Din_al-Sabbagh), commander of the [3rd Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Division_\(Iraq\));

- Colonel [Kamal Shahib](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamil_Shabib), commander of the [1st Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Division_\(Iraq\));

- Colonel [Fahmi Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahmi_Said), commander of the Independent Mechanized Brigade;

- Colonel [Mahmud Salman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahmud_Salman), chief of the Air Force.

Although Iraq was nominally independent, Britain *de facto* still governed the country, exercising veto over Iraqi foreign and national security policy. The Iraqi high command saw the opportunity to rid themselves of their colonial master when Britain saw itself in a vulnerable position against [Nazi Germany](21212.md). The *golpistas* were supported by the pro-Nazi and anti-Jewish [Grand Mufti of Jerusalem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grand_Mufti_of_Jerusalem) [Haj Amin al-Husseini](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amin_al-Husseini), the German ambassador [Fritz Grobba](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fritz_Grobba) and Arab guerrilla leader [Fawzi al-Qawuqji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fawzi_al-Qawuqji).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyman200521-17]

On April 30, Iraqi Army units took the high ground to the south of [RAF Habbaniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Habbaniya). An Iraqi envoy was sent to demand that no movements, either ground or air, were to take place from the base. The British refused the demand and then themselves demanded that the Iraqi units leave the area at once. In addition, the British landed forces at [Basra](57580.md) and the Iraqis demanded that these forces be removed.

At 0500 hours on 2 May 1941, the [Anglo-Iraqi War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iraqi_War) broke out between the British and Rashid Ali's new government when the British at [RAF Habbaniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Habbaniya) launched [air strikes](323167.md) against the Iraqis. By this time, the army had grown significantly. It had four infantry divisions with some 60,000 men.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPlayfairFlynnMolonyToomer2004182-18][^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyman200525-10] At full strength, each division had three infantry brigades (3 battalions each) plus supporting units - including artillery brigades.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyman200525-10] The Iraqi [1st](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Division_\(Iraq\)) and [3rd Divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Division_\(Iraq\)) were stationed in Baghdad. The [2nd Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2nd_Division_\(Iraq\)) was stationed in [Kirkuk](199292.md), and the 4th Division was in [Al Diwaniyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Al_Diwaniyah), on the main rail line from Baghdad to Basra.

Also based within Baghdad was the Independent Mechanized Brigade composed of:[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyman200526-19]

- Light Tank Company (Fiat [L3/35](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/L3/35) tankettes);

- Armoured car company (14 [Vickers Crossley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_Crossley_Armoured_Car));

- Two battalions of ["mechanized" infantry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Motorized_infantry);

- One "mechanized" machine-gun company;

- One "mechanized" artillery brigade.

All these "mechanized" infantry units were transported by trucks. The authorized manpower of the Iraqi Infantry Brigades at full strength were of 26 officers and 820 other ranks, 46 [Bren](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bren_light_machine_gun) light machine guns; 8 [Vickers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vickers_machine_gun) heavy machine guns (in two platoons of 4 MGs each) and 4 anti-air [Lewis guns](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lewis_gun).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELyman200526-19]

Hostilities between the British and the Iraqis lasted from 2 May to 30 May 1941. The German government dispatched an aviation unit, [Fliegerführer Irak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fliegerf%C3%BChrer_Irak), and [Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingdom_of_Italy) send limited assistance, but both were too late and far from adequate. Britain pulled together a small force from its armies in the Levant, [Habforce](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Habforce) and its advance element [Kingcol](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kingcol). The British handily defeated the much larger but thoroughly incompetent Iraqi army and air force, marched on [Baghdad](4492.md) and ousted the military commanders.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002149-12] The military commanders were sentenced to death by [hanging](155019.md), and their prime minister, Rashid Ali al-Gaylani, deposed. In their place the British re-installed [Nuri al-Said](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nuri_al-Said), which dominated the politics of Iraq until the [overthrow of the monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14_July_Revolution) and his assassination in 1958. Nuri al-Said pursued a largely pro-[western policy](21208200.md) during this period.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGhareebDougherty2004-15] The army was not disbanded, however. Instead, it was maintained to hinder possible German offensive actions via the southern parts of the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).

### 1948 Arab–Israeli War



Iraqi army troops led by Taher Abdel Ghafour and with a [Humber Armoured Car](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Humber_Armoured_Car) in the city of [Jenin](50997.md), 1948.

The [1948 Arab–Israeli War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1948_Arab%E2%80%93Israeli_War) was the first combat experience of independent Iraqi forces after the Second World War, and its first war outside its territory. Baghdad joined the [Arab states](52625.md) in their opposition to the creation of the [Jewish national homeland](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homeland_for_the_Jewish_people) in Palestine, and in May 1948 sent a sizeable force to help crush the recently [independent](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Israeli_Declaration_of_Independence) [state of Israel](9282173.md). The Iraqi Army by then boasted 21,000 men in 12 brigades, with the [Royal Iraqi Air Force](1817852.md) having a force of 100 aircraft (mostly British);[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002150-20] sending initially 5,000 men in four infantry brigades and an armoured battalion with corresponding support personnel. Iraq continuously sent reinforcements to its expeditionary force, peaking at 15–18,000 men.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002150-20] Iraq also contributed 2,500 volunteers to the [Arab Liberation Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Liberation_Army) (ALA), an irregular force commanded by the former Ottoman officer [Fawzi al-Qawuqji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fawzi_al-Qawuqji).[^cite_note-21][^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDupuy197818-22]

Before the Arab League resolution to attack Israel, the ALA was used to fight the [Jewish settlements](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yishuv), launching its first offensive in February 1948.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002448-23] With a force around 6,000 men it was mainly organized by [Syria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Syrian_Republic), with 2,500 Syrian volunteers providing a third of the force,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002448-23] with another third provided by the Iraqis; the rest being Arab Palestinians, Lebanese and other Muslims. Its commander Fawzi was also Syrian, with the costs being paid by members of the Arab League.

Iraqi forces received their [baptism of fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baptism_by_fire) with the ALA defending [Zefat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed) in April and May 1948.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002150-20] A force of 600 Syrian and Iraqi ALA irregulars were sent to defend this key town, which controlled access between the [Huleh Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hula_Valley) and the [Sea of Galilee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sea_of_Galilee) (Lake Kinneret). [Zefat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Safed) was protected by two police forts built into the rock of the hills, forming a formidable position; and also a priority target for the [Haganah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haganah).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002150-20] The strength of the natural position allowed the ALA, together with some local Arab militiamen, to defeat two Israeli attacks by elements of the [Golani Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golani_Brigade) in April.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002150-20] The Israelis brought a new battalion in May and immediately took one of the forts. With the arrival of another battalion, the Israelis assaulted the town itself under cover of mortar fire but the Arabs succeeded in forcing back repeated assaults. Four days after the first attack in the town, the Israelis attacked at night under cover of a rainstorm and surprised the defenders.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002150-20] The Arabs resisted fiercely and forced the Israelis to fight house to house but ultimately were ejected from the town. After this defeat, the Arab force gave up the last police fort without a fight and withdrew.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002150-20]

On 25 April, the Israeli [Irgun Zvi Leumi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irgun) assaulted the Arab town of Jaffa with 600 men,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002150-20] initiating [Operation Hametz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Hametz), but were stopped cold by a similar-sized force of Iraqi ALA irregulars in [house to house combat](319027.md); forcing the Irgun to ask for help from the Haganah after two days of fighting.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002150-20] Heavy fighting continued with British units intervening on behalf of the Arabs and losing a number of tanks against Irgun ambushes. Jaffa would fall to the Israelis on 13 May.

On 29 April, units of the élite [Palmach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palmach) assaulted positions on the [Katamon Ridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katamon) south of [Jerusalem](16043.md) held by Iraqi ALA irregulars.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002150–151-24] The Palmach secured a foothold with a surprise night attack that took the monastery dominating the ridge. In the morning the Iraqis launched a furious counterattack that evolved into an extremely tough fight, but eventually the Iraqis called off their attack to regroup; at noon the Israelis were reinforced by another battalion.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002151-25] This new balance of combat power lead the exhausted and bloodied Iraqis to decide they did not possess the strength to dislodge the Israelis, and they retired from the field. After these defeats, the ALA took several months to resume operations, but by then most of its Iraqi contingent had joined the main Iraqi expeditionary force that had arrived in northern [Samaria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samaria).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002151-25]



Arab offensive, 15 May – 10 June 1948.

The first Iraqi forces of the expeditionary force reached [Transjordan](7515964.md) in early April 1948, with one infantry brigade and a supporting armoured battalion under the command of General [Nur ad-Din Mahmud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nur_ad-Din_Mahmud_\(Iraq\)?action=edit\&redlink=1).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002151-25] On 15 May, Iraqi [engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combat_engineer) built a [pontoon bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pontoon_bridge) across the [Jordan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_River), allowing the combat units to cross into Palestine. Over 3,000 Iraqi soldiers with armor and air support were unable to defeat less than 50 lightly armed Jewish defenders. After the crossing, the Iraqis immediately launched a frontal assault against the Israeli settlement of [Gesher](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gesher,_Israel), only to be quickly driven back.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002151-25] The Iraq army tried again the next day, with their armour attacking from the south and their infantry from the north. The [double envelopment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pincer_movement) was poorly implemented - lacking [infantry-tank](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Combined_arms) coordination - which left the Israelis with the breathing space to redeploy their small force along internal lines and defeat each attack in turn.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002151-25] The Iraqis launched clumsy [frontal assaults](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontal_assault), with the unprotected tanks and armoured cars being easily destroyed by [AT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anti-tank_warfare) hunter-killer teams. Several days later, Mahmud tried to attack another Jewish settlement in the same area, but the troops did not [scout](146911.md) their route properly and got [ambushed](1905.md) before they could even reach the target settlement. These defeats convinced the Iraqi army to abandon this sector of the front and try their luck elsewhere.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002151-25]

The expeditionary force moved into the [Nablus](233369.md)–[Jenin](50997.md)–[Tulkarm](851834.md) strategic triangle in May,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002151-25] that being the [West Bank](33209.md) region of northern Samaria. That was a key sector for the Arab war effort because it was the ideal jumping point for an attack westward against [Haifa](55606.md) to split the narrow Israeli corridor along the [Mediterranean](19006.md) coast (which was only 15 km wide) and break the country in half; it would also guard the right flank of the [Transjordanian Arab Legion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Legion), which was concentrated to the south, around the [Jerusalem corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jerusalem_corridor). Previously, this sector had been held by elements of the [ALA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arab_Liberation_Army) that were too weak to pose much of a threat to the Israelis, but the arrival of the powerful Iraqi force led the Arabs to believe they would be able to cut [Israel](9282173.md) in two. While setting down the Iraqis were reinforced by another infantry brigade and another armoured battalion. The build-up continued steadily, with the expeditionary force reaching seven or eight infantry brigades, an armoured brigade and three air force squadrons.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEDupuy197851-26][^cite_note-27]

In late May, the Haganah launched a major assault against the Arab Legion's positions in the [Latrun](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battles_of_Latrun_\(1948\)) police fort on the [Jerusalem](16043.md)-[Tel Aviv](31453.md) road.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002153-28] The Israeli attacks were extremely heavy, prompting the Jordanians to plead with the Iraqis to attack to draw off Israeli forces from Latrum; either northwest toward Haifa or north into Galilee. The Iraqi army was slow to respond and only launched two half-hearted attacks that were easily defeated by local Israeli forces.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002153-28] Nevertheless, Haganah commanders pinpointed the Iraqi presence, by its size and location, to be a dangerous threat in a possible offensive. The Israelis decided to launch a preemptive attack south from Galilee to take [Jenin](50997.md), and possibly [Nablus](233369.md), and cut the Iraqi supply lines across the Jordan River. To achieve that the Israelis would employ three brigades: [Alexandroni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alexandroni_Brigade), [Carmeli](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmeli_Brigade) and [Golani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golani_Brigade).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002153-28]

At the same time, the Iraqis were planning the exact offensive the Israelis feared. As the first truce was approaching, the general headquarters of the Arab forces in [Zarqaa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zarqa) ordered the commander of the Iraqi forces in [Shechem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shechem) to take control of a number of Israeli settlements in order to strengthen their position at the ceasefire talks. It was decided to take control of the port of [Netanya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Netanya), as it was considered an essential target and an important commercial center, and it would split Israeli communications between north and south - thus denying the Israelis movement between their internal lines.

The Israeli preemptive offensive began on the night of 28 May and caught the Iraqis by surprise. The plan called for the Alexandroni Brigade to make a diversionary attack against Tulkarm, while the Golanis would drive south toward Jenin; holding the high ground to the north. Then, the Carmeli Brigade would exploit the success passing through the Golani's lines and seize the town itself. The Golani attack to the north made good progress - despite the Alexandronis failing to execute their feint - and took a series of hills, villages and police posts en route to Nablus. The Iraqi defenders responded slowly and Israeli infantry repeatedly occupied key positions before Iraqi armoured car battalions arrived. The Golanis outmaneuvered the Iraqi forces in a series of skirmishes, outflanking and mauling them before they could retreat on multiple occasions. The Iraqis kept launching determined attacks against positions already occupied by the Israelis who, by then dug in, easily threw them back. The Israelis were now in a good position to assault Jenin.[^cite_note-29]

Iraqi reinforcements kept arriving north and when the [Carmeli Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmeli_Brigade) took over the spearhead of the Israeli attack, it began to run into them. An Iraqi brigade had fortified itself in the city by the time the Israelis reached Jenin on 3 June, and on the two hills dominating the city from the south. The [Carmeli Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmeli_Brigade) launched a clumsy frontal night assault but still managed to push off the Iraqis off both hills in a protracted battle. The next morning the Iraqis brought up fresh forces and counterattacked with a reinforced battalion, with artillery support and inaccurate (albeit helpful) airstrikes, that eventually retook the southwestern hill from the exhausted Israelis. A fierce battle developed for control of Jenin itself, and although in a continuous stalemate, the Iraqi commander kept feeding fresh troops into the fight until the Israelis concluded that holding the town was not worth the price in casualties and pulled back to the hills north of Jenin.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalovany201741–43-30] They suffered heavy casualties in the [Israeli attack on Jenin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Jenin_\(1948\)), but they managed to hold on to their positions and could absorb the losses. Overall, the Iraqi troops distinguished themselves at Jenin, even impressing their Israeli opponents.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEMalovany201743-31] Active Iraqi involvement in the war effectively ended at this point.

By the beginning of 1951, British General [Sir Brian Robertson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brian_Robertson,_1st_Baron_Robertson_of_Oakridge), Commander-in-Chief, [Middle East Land Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Middle_East_Land_Forces), was keen to upgrade the Iraqi Army as part of a wider effort to defend against a feared Soviet invasion in the event of war. A British MELF advisory team was dispatched there in November–December 1950. The team estimated that Iraqi's forces of the time, two divisions and a mechanized brigade, but deemed ill-equipped and 'not up to establishment' \[full strength] would have to be increased, and a total of four divisions, three additional brigades, and more artillery units would be needed. The shortage of trained technical personnel was 'grave,' and the Iraqis were 'incapable of maintaining even the limited equipment already in their possession.'[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevey200463-32] In January 1951 the British [Military Attaché](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_attach%C3%A9) wrote that the Iraqi Army's ability '..to wage modern warfare against a first class enemy is practically nil ... in its present state, the Iraqi army would be entirely incapable of remaining an effective force for more than ten hours of battle ... \[it] must be used in war in cooperation with a field force of efficiency and stamina' which would have to do most of the fighting.'[^cite_note-FOOTNOTELevey200464-33]

In May 1955 the British finally withdrew from Iraq. The Iraqi authorities said during the withdrawal negotiations that a motorised infantry brigade was to be formed, based at the previous [RAF Habbaniya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF_Habbaniya), a location that had been occupied by the British [Iraq Levies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraq_Levies).[^cite_note-34]

### Republic declared

Main article: [Iraqi Republic (1958–68)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Republic_\(1958%E2%80%9368\))



A group of Iraqi soldiers, 1958.

The [Hashemite monarchy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hashemite_monarchy) lasted until 1958, when it was overthrown through a [coup d'état](25914154.md) by the Iraqi Army, known as the [14 July Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14_July_Revolution). King [Faisal II of Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Faisal_II_of_Iraq) along with members of the royal family were murdered. The coup brought [Abd al-Karim Qasim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abd_al-Karim_Qasim) to power. He withdrew from the [Baghdad Pact](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baghdad_Pact) and established friendly relations with the [Soviet Union](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Soviet_Union).

When [Qāsim](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul-Karim_Qasim) distanced himself from [Abd an-Nāsir](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gamal_Abdel_Nasser), he faced growing opposition from pro-Egypt officers in the Iraqi army. \`Arif, who wanted closer cooperation with Egypt, was stripped of his responsibilities and thrown in prison. When the garrison in [Mosul](200475.md) rebelled against Qāsim's policies, he allowed the Kurdish leader [Barzānī](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustafa_Barzani) to return from exile in the Soviet Union to help suppress the pro-Nāsir rebels.

The creation of the new Fifth Division, consisting of mechanized infantry, was announced on 6 January 1959, [Army Day](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Army_Day).[^cite_note-35] Qāsim was also promoted to the rank of general.

In 1961, an Army build up close to [Kuwait](7515890.md) in conjunction with Iraqi claims over the small neighbouring state, led to a crisis with British military forces ([land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_army), [sea](26061.md), and [air](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Air_force)) deployed to Kuwait for a period. In 1961, [Kuwait](7515890.md) gained independence from Britain and Iraq claimed sovereignty over Kuwait. As in the 1930s, Qasim based Iraq's claim on the assertion that Kuwait had been a district of the Ottoman province of Basra, unjustly severed by the British from the main body of Iraqi state when it had been created in the 1920s.[^cite_note-36] Britain reacted strongly to Iraq's claim and sent troops to Kuwait to deter Iraq. Qāsim was forced to back down and in October 1963, Iraq recognized the sovereignty of Kuwait.

Qāsim was assassinated in February 1963, when the [Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'ath_Party) [took power](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_1963_Iraqi_coup_d'%C3%A9tat) under the leadership of General [Ahmed Hasan al-Bakr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ahmed_Hasan_al-Bakr) ([prime minister](208879.md)) and Colonel [Abdul Salam Arif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Salam_Arif) ([president](209174.md)). Nine months later \`Abd as-Salam Muhammad \`Arif led a successful coup against the Ba'ath government. On 13 April 1966, President Abdul Salam Arif died in a helicopter crash and was succeeded by his brother, General [Abdul Rahman Arif](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abdul_Rahman_Arif). Following the [Six-Day War](29328.md) of 1967, the Ba'ath Party felt strong enough to retake power (17 July 1968). Ahmad Hasan al-Bakr became president and chairman of the Revolutionary Command Council (RCC).

### Six-Day War



Iraqi Army [Panhard AML-60](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panhard_AML) armored car, 1970s. Iraq ordered about 250 of these vehicles between 1968 and 1976.

During the Six-Day War, the Iraqi [3rd Armoured Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/3rd_Division_\(Iraq\)) was deployed in eastern Jordan.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002167-37] However, the Israeli attack against the West Bank unfolded so quickly that the Iraqi force could not organise itself and reach the front before Jordan ceased fighting. Repeated Israeli airstrikes also held them up so that by the time they did reach the [Jordan River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordan_River) the entire [West Bank](33209.md) was in Israeli hands. During the course of the [Jordanian Campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jordanian_campaign_\(1967\)) ten Iraqis were killed and 30 Iraqis were wounded, especially as the main battle was in Jerusalem. Fighting also raged in other areas of the West Bank, where Iraqi commandos and Jordanian soldiers defended their positions.[^cite_note-38]

Barzānī and the Kurds who had begun a rebellion in 1961 were still causing problems in 1969. The secretary-general of the Ba\`th party, [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein), was given responsibility to find a solution. It was clear that it was impossible to defeat the Kurds by military means and in 1970 a political agreement was reached between the rebels and the Iraqi government.

Following the Arab defeat in 1967, Jordan became a hotbed of Palestinian activity. During this time PLO elements attempted to create a Palestinian state within Jordan caused the Jordanians to launch their full military force against the PLO. As they were doing this Syria invaded Jordan and Iraq moved a brigade in Rihab, Jordan. Otherwise the only Iraqi activity was that they fired upon some Jordanian aircraft.

### Yom Kippur War

Iraq sent a 60,000 man expeditionary force to the Syrian front during the [Yom Kippur War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yom_Kippur_War). It consisted of the 3rd and [6th Armoured Divisions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Division_\(Iraq\)), two infantry brigades, twelve artillery battalions, and a special forces brigade. The two armoured divisions were, Pollack says, 'unquestionably the best formations of the Iraqi Army.'[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002167-37] Yet during their operations on the Golan Heights, their performance was awful in virtually every category of military effectiveness. Military intelligence, initiative, and small unit independent action was virtually absent.[^cite_note-39]

After the war, Iraq started a major military build-up. Active duty manpower doubled, and so did number of divisions, from six to twelve, of which four were now armoured and two mechanised infantry.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002182-40]

### Iran–Iraq war

See also: [Order of battle during the Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Order_of_battle_during_the_Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War)



Iraqi commanders discussing strategy on the battlefront, 1986. [Wafiq Al-Samarrai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wafiq_al-Samarrai) is center-left.

Later, Saddam Hussein, looking to build fighting power against Iran soon after the outbreak of the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War) doubled the size of the Iraqi Army. In 1981, Pollack writes it numbered 200,000 soldiers in 12 divisions and 3 independent brigades, but by 1985, it reached 500,000 men in 23 divisions and nine brigades. An April 1983 CIA estimate suggests that Iraq had at that time five armoured; seven infantry; and two mechanised infantry divisions with ten more forming ("several are probably already operational").[^cite_note-41] The first new divisions were created in 1981 when the 11th and 12th Border Guard Divisions were converted into infantry formations and the 14th Infantry Division was formed.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002207-42] Yet the rise in number of divisions is misleading, because during the war Iraqi divisions abandoned a standard organisation with permanent ('organic') brigades assigned to each division. Instead division headquarters were assigned a mission or sector and then assigned brigades to carry out the task - up to eight to ten brigades on some occasions.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEPollack2002208-43]



Destroyed Iraqi tanks, 1980s.

The war came at a great cost in lives and economic damage - a half a million Iraqi and Iranian soldiers as well as civilians are believed to have died in the war with many more injured and wounded - but brought neither reparations nor change in borders. The conflict is often compared to [World War I](4764461.md),[^cite_note-44] in that the tactics used closely mirrored those of the 1914–1918 war, including large scale [trench warfare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trench_warfare), manned machine-gun posts, bayonet charges, use of [barbed wire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barbed_wire) across trenches and on [no-mans land](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/No-mans_land), [human wave attacks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Human_wave_attacks) by Iran, and Iraq's extensive use of [chemical weapons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chemical_weapons) (such as [mustard gas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mustard_gas)) against Iranian [troops](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Troops) and [civilians](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Civilians) as well as [Iraqi Kurds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halabja_chemical_attack).

### Invasion of Kuwait and the Persian Gulf War



Iraqi tanks in [Kuwait City](57021.md) of Iraqi-occupied Kuwait on 2 August 1990 during the [Iraqi invasion of Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_invasion_of_Kuwait) that began the Gulf War.

By the eve of the [Invasion of Kuwait](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Invasion_of_Kuwait) which led to the 1991 [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_War), the army was estimated to number 1,000,000 men.[^cite_note-45] Just before the [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Persian_Gulf_War) began, the force comprised 47 infantry divisions plus 9 armoured and mechanised divisions, grouped in 7 corps.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEisenstadt1993124-46] This gave a total of about 56 army divisions, and total land force divisions reached 68 when the 12 [Iraqi Republican Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Republican_Guard) divisions were included. Eisenstadt notes that four Republican Guards security divisions were formed between the invasion of Kuwait and the outbreak of war. They remained in Iraq during the war.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEEisenstadt1993124-46] Although the coalition ground forces believed they faced approximately 545,000 Iraqi troops at the beginning of the ground campaign,[^cite_note-47] the quantitative descriptions of the Iraqi army at the time were exaggerated, for a variety of reasons.[^cite_note-48] Many of the Iraqi troops were also young, under-resourced and poorly trained [conscripts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conscript). Saddam did not trust the army; among counterbalancing security forces was the [Iraqi Popular Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Popular_Army_\(Iraq\)).

The wide range of suppliers of Iraqi equipment resulted in a lack of standardization. It additionally suffered from poor training and poor motivation. The majority of Iraqi armoured forces still used old Chinese [Type 59s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_59_tank) and [Type 69s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_69_tank), Soviet-made [T-55s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-55) and [T-62s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-62) from the 1950s and 1960s, and some [T-72s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72) from the 1970s in 1991. These vehicles were not equipped with up-to-date equipment, such as [thermal sights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thermal_sight) or [laser rangefinders](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laser_rangefinder), and their effectiveness in modern combat was very limited. The Iraqis failed to find effective countermeasures to the thermal sights and the [sabot rounds](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy_round) used by [M1 Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1_Abrams), [Challenger 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Challenger_1_tank) and other tanks of the Allied forces. U.S. M1A1s could effectively engage and destroy Iraqi tanks from well outside the distance (e.g. 8,200 ft to Iraqi ranges of 6,600 ft) that Iraqi tanks could engage.



Abandoned Iraqi 2S1 "Gvozdika" artillery system, 1991.

The Iraqi tank guns were supplied with older generation steel core [penetrators](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kinetic_energy_penetrator) which, while perfectly well suited to older Iranian tanks, against the advanced [Chobham Armour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chobham_Armour) of the latest US and British tanks of the coalition the results were disastrous. The Iraqi forces also failed to utilize the advantage that could be gained from using [urban warfare](319027.md) — fighting within [Kuwait City](57021.md) — which could have inflicted significant casualties on the attacking forces. Urban combat reduces the range at which fighting occurs and can negate some of the technological advantage that well equipped forces enjoy. Iraqis also tried to use [Soviet military doctrine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_doctrine#Russia_and_the_Soviet_Union), but the implementation failed due to the lack of skill of their commanders and the preventive air strikes of the [USAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USAF) and [RAF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RAF) on communication centers and bunkers.



Destroyed Iraqi MRLS system, 1991.

While the exact number of Iraqi combat casualties has yet to be firmly determined, sources agree that the losses were substantial. Immediate estimates said up to 100,000 Iraqis were killed. More recent estimates indicate that Iraq probably sustained between 20,000 and 35,000 fatalities, though other figures still maintain fatalities could have been as high as 200,000.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEFisk2005853-49] A report commissioned by the U.S. Air Force, estimated 10,000-12,000 Iraqi combat deaths in the air campaign and as many as 10,000 casualties in the ground war.[^cite_note-50] This analysis is based on Iraqi prisoner of war reports. It is known that between 20,000 and 200,000 Iraqi soldiers were killed. According to the Project on Defense Alternatives study,[^cite_note-51] 3,664 Iraqi civilians and between 20,000 and 26,000 military personnel were killed in the conflict. 75,000 Iraqi soldiers were wounded in the fighting.



Destroyed Iraqi T-72 tank, 1991.

The [International Institute for Strategic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Strategic_Studies) (IISS) estimated the army's composition immediately after the 1991 war as six 'armoured'/'mechanised' divisions, 23 infantry divisions, eight Republican Guard divisions and four Republican Guard internal security divisions.[^cite_note-52] [Jane's Defence Weekly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jane's_Defence_Weekly) for 18 July 1992 stated that 10,000 troops from five divisions were fighting against Shia Muslims in the southern marshlands.

The IISS gave the Iraqi Army's force structure as of 1 July 1997 as seven Corps headquarters, six armoured or mechanised divisions, 12 infantry divisions, six RGF divisions, four [Special Republican Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Republican_Guard) Brigades, 10 commando, and two Special Forces Brigades.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIISS1997-53] It was estimated to number 350,000 personnel, including 100,000 recently recalled reservists.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEIISS1997-53]

### U.S. invasion in 2003

In the days leading up to the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md) and the following [Iraq War](5043324.md), the army consisted of 375,000 troops, organized into five [corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corps). During the [2003 invasion of Iraq](201936.md) the Iraqi Army was defeated in a number of battles, including by [Task Force Viking](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Task_Force_Viking) in the north, and the [Battle of Nasiriyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Nasiriyah) and the [Battle of Baghdad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Baghdad_\(2003\)). The Iraqi Army was disbanded by [Coalition Provisional Authority Order Number 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_Provisional_Authority_Order_Number_2) issued by U.S. Administrator of Iraq [Paul Bremer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Bremer) on May 23, 2003, after its decisive defeat.[^cite_note-ISMFD-61] Bremer said that it was not feasible to reconstitute the armed forces. His justifications for the disbandment included postwar looting, which had destroyed all the bases; that the largely Shiite draftees of the army would not respond to a recall plea from their former commanders, who were primarily Sunnis, and that recalling the army "would be a political disaster because to the vast majority of Iraqis it was a symbol of the old Baathist-led Sunni ascendancy".[^cite_note-62]

### Rebuilding an Army from 2003



[BMP-3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-3) in a Military parade celebrating 100th Anniversary of Army's Founding.



soldiers from 3rd Battalion, 2nd Brigade Combat Team, 9th Iraqi Army Division, conduct final checks before leaving on a mission from Combat Outpost Inchon, Jan. 22, 2008.

Based on Bush administration expectations that coalition forces would be welcomed as liberators after the overthrow of the Hussein regime,[^cite_note-FOOTNOTESpearin2008229-73] prewar planners had only been expecting little if any resistance from the Iraqi people. Thus the new army was initially focused on external defence operations. The new Army was originally intended to comprise 27 battalions in three divisions numbering 40,000 soldiers in three years time.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWrightReese2008433–434-74] [Vinnell Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnell_Corporation) was engaged to train the first nine battalions.[^cite_note-75]

The [Coalition Military Assistance Training Team](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_Military_Assistance_Training_Team) (CMATT), headed by Major General [Paul Eaton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paul_Eaton), was organized by the [Department of Defense](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Defense_\(United_States\)) with the responsibility of training and development of the new army. On August 2, 2003, the first battalion of new Iraqi Army recruits started a nine-week training course at a training base in [Qaraqosh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qaraqosh). They graduated on October 4, 2003.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWrightReese2008433–436-76] In the interim, the new army had been formally established by [Coalition Provisional Authority Order 22](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coalition_Provisional_Authority_Order_22?action=edit\&redlink=1) of August 18, 2003.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWrightReese2008435-77]

In April 2004, several Iraqi battalions refused to fight as part of the force engaged in the [First Battle of Fallujah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Battle_of_Fallujah).[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWrightReese2008448–449-78] The Fifth Battalion was among the new Iraqi units that fought in Fallujah.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEZaccheaKemp2017-79] In June 2004, the CMATT was dissolved, and passed on its responsibilities to the [Multi-National Security Transition Command – Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-National_Security_Transition_Command_%E2%80%93_Iraq) (MNSTC-I) (initially headed by Lt. Gen. [David Petraeus](3223944.md)) with the new focus on providing security for the Iraqi people from the emerging threat posed by the [Iraqi insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_insurgency_\(Iraq_War\)).[^cite_note-80]

While the regular army was being formed, U.S. commanders around the country needed additional troops more quickly, and thus the [Iraqi Civil Defense Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Civil_Defense_Corps) (which became the [Iraqi National Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Guard_\(Iraq\)) by July 2004)[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEAl-MarashiSalama2008\[httpsarchiveorgdetailsiraqsarmedforces0000almapage212_212\]-81] was formed. Coalition commanders formed these militia-type units separately in each area; only later were they gradually brought together as one force. There were several instances where they have refused to take military action against fellow Iraqis, such as in [Fallujah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fallujah), deserted, or allegedly aided the resistance. It is alleged that most guardsmen were drawn from the [Shia](26961.md) majority in Southern Iraq or the [Kurdish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurd) majority in northern Iraq, rather than from the [Sunni](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sunni) area which they were ordered to attack. In September 2004, a senior member of the National Guard, General Talib al-Lahibi was arrested on suspicion of having links with insurgent groups.[^cite_note-82] In December 2004, it was announced that the Iraqi National Guard would be dissolved.[^cite_note-83] At this time its strength was officially over 40,000 men. Its units became part of the army. The absorption of the ING by the regular army appears to have taken place on January 6, 2005, Iraqi Army Day.[^cite_note-84]



Iraqi [commandos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commandos) training under the supervision of soldiers of the US [82nd Airborne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/82nd_Airborne).

On August 14, 2004, the [NATO Training Mission - Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Training_Mission_-_Iraq) was established to assist the Iraqi military, including the army. On September 20 the provisional Fallujah Brigade dissolved after being sent in to secure the city. The Fallujah Brigade experiment of using former insurgents to secure a city was not repeated.

Army training was transferred from [Vinnell Corporation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vinnell_Corporation) to the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) supported by U.S. allies. It was impeded by domestic instability, infiltration by insurgents, and high desertion rates. By June 2005, the number of battalions in the new army had grown to around 115. Out of this number, it was deemed that 80 were able to carry out operations in the field with Coalition support limited to logistics and strategic planning, whilst another 20-30 battalions still needed major Coalition support to carry out their operations. As of October 5, 2005 the Iraqi Army had 90 battalions trained well enough to be "deployed independently", without United States help.[^cite_note-85]

On May 3, 2006, a significant command-and-control development took place. The Iraqi Army command and control center opened in a ceremony at the [Iraqi Ground Forces Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Ground_Forces_Command) (IFGC) headquarters at [Camp Victory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Victory).[^cite_note-86] The IGFC was established to exercise command and control of assigned Iraqi Army forces and, upon assuming Operational Control, to plan and direct operations to defeat the [Iraqi insurgency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_insurgency_\(Iraq_War\)). At the time, the IFGC was commanded by Lt. Gen. Abdul-Qadar. In 2006 the ten planned divisions began to be certified and assume battlespace responsibility: the 6th and 8th before June 26, 2006, the 9th on June 26, 2006, the 5th on July 3, 2006, the 4th on August 8, 2006, and the 2nd on December 21, 2006. After divisions were certified, they began to be transferred from U.S. operational control to Iraqi control of the IGFC. The 8th Division was transferred on September 7, 2006,[^cite_note-87][^cite_note-88] and the 3rd Division on December 1, 2006. Another unspecified division also was transferred to IGFC control.[^cite_note-89] Also transferred to the Iraqi [chain of command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chain_of_command) were smaller logistics units: on November 1, 2006, the 5th Motor Transport Regiment (MTR) was the fifth of nine MTRs to be transferred to the Iraqi Army divisions. 2007 plans included, MNF-I said, great efforts to make the Iraqi Army able to sustain itself logistically.[^cite_note-90]

As of June 26, 2006, three Iraqi divisions, 18 brigades and 69 battalions were responsible for their own areas of operations (including two police commando battalions).[^cite_note-91]

### 2008



Iraqi army battalion trains for urban operations

On March 25, 2008, the Iraqi Army launched its first solely planned and executed high-profile division-level operation, [Operation Charge of the Knights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_Basra_\(2008\)) in Basra. They received [Multi-National Force – Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-National_Force_%E2%80%93_Iraq) support only in air support, logistics and via embedded advisors. Also, a British infantry brigade, part of [Multi-National Division South-East](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-National_Division_\(South-East\)_\(Iraq\)), and stationed in Basra, were ready in a tactical overwatch role. Their participation was limited to the provision of embedded training teams.

In April–June 2008, two brigades of the Iraqi Army 11th Division, supported by US forces, moved into the southern third of [Sadr City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sadr_City). They were tasked to stop rocket and mortar attacks on US bases and the [Green Zone](856542.md). Following the [Siege of Sadr City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Sadr_City)—a month of fighting—the [Mahdi Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mahdi_Army) agreed to let Iraqi forces into the remaining portion of the city. On May 20, troops from the Iraqi Army 3rd Brigade of the [1st (Iraqi Reaction Force) Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Division_\(Iraq\)) and a brigade from the 9th Division moved into the northern districts of Sadr City and began clearing operations.

In May, Iraqi army forces launched [Operation Lion's Roar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Lion's_Roar) (later renamed to Operation Mother of Two Springs) in Mosul and surrounding areas of Nineveh Governorate. Iraq became one of the top purchasers of U.S. military equipment with the Iraqi army trading its [AK-47](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AK-47) assault rifles for the more accurate U.S. [M-16](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M16_rifle) and [M-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M4_Carbine) rifles, among other equipment.[^cite_note-92]

In June 2008 the army moved troops to the southern [Maysan Governorate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maysan_Governorate). Following a four-day amnesty for insurgents to turn over weapons, the Iraqi Army moved into the provincial capital Amarah.

In late 2008, United States personnel were worried by Prime Minister Maliki's attempt to exert control over the Iraqi Army and police by proliferating regional operations commands. "Using the Baghdad Operations Command as his precedent, Maliki created other regional commands in Basrah, Diyala, the mid-Euphrates region, and Ninawa, and others would follow. Initially coalition leaders welcomed the idea of regional commands that could create unity of Iraqi effort, but their enthusiasm faded as Maliki began to use the new headquarters to bypass the formal chain of command,"[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGodfroyMortonPowellZais2019b282–83-93] which came to resemble the operating mode of the [Saddam Hussein](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saddam_Hussein) regime.

Divisions are forming engineer, logistics, mortar, and other units by identifying over-strength units, such as the Intelligence, Surveillance, and Reconnaissance (ISR) battalions and other headquarters elements, and then transferring them as needed.

Problems include infiltration and an insufficient US advisory effort. Former members of the [Special Republican Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Republican_Guard_\(Iraq\)), members of the intelligence services, senior level officials of the [Ba'ath Party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ba'ath_Party_\(Iraqi-dominated_faction\)), people affiliated with terrorist organizations, and anyone with human rights violations or crimes against humanity were prohibited from entering the new army.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEWrightReese2008435-77] However the army was infiltrated by a multitude of groups ranging from local militias to foreign insurgents. This has led to highly publicized deaths and compromised operations (perhaps the most prominent being the attack on a US military base near Mosul in December 2004. More than 20 people, including 13 American servicemen, were killed when a suicide bomber wearing an Iraqi military uniform detonated his vest inside a dining tent).[^cite_note-94]

### 2011

In 2011 Lieutenant General [Michael Barbero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Barbero), Commanding General, MNSTC-I, made an assessment of the Iraqi Armed Forces' shortcomings. [Michael Gordon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_R._Gordon) summarized Barbero's findings as:

> "..for all the U.S. efforts, Iraq’s special operations forces continued being flown to their targets on American helicopters and relied heavily on U.S. intelligence to plan their missions. Iraqi tank crews, artillery batteries, and infantry battalions had been trained separately and were not practiced in combined arms warfare.
>
> &#x20;Logistics remained a challenge, and the Iraqi Army had an enormous and costly maintenance backlog. The Iraqis had no counter-battery radar system to pinpoint the location of rocket attacks on the Green Zone—the fortified sanctuary that served as the seat of the Iraqi government—or, as yet, an air force that could protect the nation’s skies. In short, Barbero concluded, Iraq had a “checkpoint army” that was very much a work in progress."[^cite_note-95]

The response from Iraqi officials was that U.S. troops would have to stay longer.

### U.S. Military Transition Teams

Up until 2010–2011, all Iraqi Army battalions were supposed to have had embedded U.S. [Military transition teams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_transition_team). The MiTTs provided intelligence, communications, fire support, logistics and infantry tactics advice. Larger scale operations were often done jointly with U.S. forces. The training aimed to make the battalion self-sustainable tactically, operationally and logistically so that the battalion would have been prepared to take over responsibility for a particular area.

As of March 2007, the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) reported that 6000 advisors in 480+ teams were embedded with Iraqi units.[^cite_note-DOD_03/07-108] However, in April, the [Congressional Research Service](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congressional_Research_Service) reported that only around 4000 U.S. forces were embedded with Iraqi units at a rate of 10 per battalion.[^cite_note-CRS_2007-04-27-109] Former U.S. Army analyst [Andrew Krepinevich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andrew_Krepinevich) argued that the roughly twelve advisors per Iraqi battalion (approximately 500 troops) was less than half the sufficient amount needed to efficiently implement the combat advisory effort.[^cite_note-110] Krepinevich argues that officers try to avoid taking on advisory tasks due to the US Army's practice of prioritising the promotion of officers that have served with a U.S. unit over ones that have served with foreign forces.[^cite_note-FirstVN-111]

Advisors remained after all U.S. combat brigades left Iraq in August 2010.[^cite_note-112] These troops were required to leave Iraq by 31 December 2011 under an [agreement](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S.%E2%80%93Iraq_Status_of_Forces_Agreement) between the U.S. and Iraqi governments.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEGodfroyMortonPowellZais2019b-113]

### 2014–2016



Iraqi [special forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_forces) wearing masks and armed with [M4A1 carbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M4_carbine), March 2020.

In the summer of 2014, large elements of the Iraqi army were routed by a much smaller and less well-equipped force from the [Islamic State](9087364.md). Nineteen Iraqi Army brigades and six Federal Police brigades disintegrated, a quarter of Iraq's security forces. These losses comprised all of the Ninawa-based 2nd and 3rd Iraqi Army divisions; most of the Salah al-Din-based [4th Iraqi Army division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Division_\(Iraq\)); all of the Kirkuk-based 12th Iraqi Army division; plus at least five southern Iraqi Army brigades that had previously been redeployed to the Syrian border."[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnights20157-114] Islamic State managed to [conquer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anbar_campaign_\(2013%E2%80%932014\)) large swaths of [Al Anbar Governorate](467036.md) and Iraq's second largest city, [Mosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Mosul).

Budget problems continued to hinder the manning of combat support and combat service support units. The lack of soldiers entering boot camp is forcing Iraqi leaders at all levels to face the dual challenge of manning and training enabler units out of existing manpower. In the 2015 Pentagon budget, a further \$1.3 billion has been requested to provide weapons for the Iraqi Army.[^cite_note-The_New_York_Times-7] However, the New York Times reported that "some of the weaponry recently supplied by the army has already ended up on the black market and in the hands of [Islamic State](9087364.md) fighters". The same November 2014 article contended that corruption is endemic in the Iraqi Army. It quoted Col. Shaaban al-Obeidi of the internal security forces, who told the paper: "Corruption is everywhere." The article claimed that one Iraqi general is known as "chicken guy" because of his reputation for selling the soldiers' poultry provisions.[^cite_note-The_New_York_Times-7]



Iraqi soldiers in the aftermath of the [Third Battle of Fallujah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Third_Battle_of_Fallujah) against [ISIL](9087364.md), 2016

In late June 2014, after the large-scale [Islamic State of Iraq and the Levant](9087364.md) offensive in the north of Iraq, it was reported that ISIL ""took the weapons stores of the 2nd and 3rd Iraqi army divisions in [Mosul](200475.md), the [4th division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/4th_Division_\(Iraq\)) in Salah al Din, the [12th division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/12th_Division_\(Iraq\)) in the areas near Kirkuk, and another division in Diyala (the [5th Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Division_\(Iraq\)))," said Jabbar Yawar, secretary-general of the Kurkish Ministry of Peshmerga Affairs.[^cite_note-115]

Reuters reported that the [5th Division (Iraq)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5th_Division_\(Iraq\)), located in [Diyala Governorate](377806.md), was by October 2014 reporting to informal "militias' chain of command," not to the Iraqi Army, according to several U.S. and coalition military officials.[^cite_note-116]

A much later report from Small Wars Journal said that in "..2013 and 2014 the 7th Division of the Iraqi Army, 99% Sunni, fought IS virtually alone, until it was almost completely destroyed."[^cite_note-117]

With the combat losses and desertions, 60 of the Iraqi Army’s 243 combat battalions were gone, along with their equipment, which likely ended up in the hands of the Islamic State. Five of Iraq’s 14 Army divisions were rated as combat ineffective.[^cite_note-118]

The October 2014 Reuters report quoted Lieutenant General [Mick Bednarek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Michael_Bednarek), Chief of the [Office of Security Cooperation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Office_of_Security_Cooperation), in Iraq from 2013 until July 2014, as estimating that "the army has only five functioning divisions ... whose fighting readiness ranges between 60 and 65 percent."

The new government under [Haider al-Abadi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Haider_al-Abadi) dismissed the [Iraqi Ground Forces Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Ground_Forces_Command) commander General [Ali Ghaidan Majid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ali_Ghaidan_Majid) (circa September 2014) and the Iraqi Army chief of operations, General Aboud Kanbar. Lieutenant General Riyadh Tawfiq, the former head of the [Ninewa Operational Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ninewa_Operational_Command), was appointed as the new head of the IGFC and the Mosul Liberation Command.[^cite_note-FOOTNOTEKnights201518-119]

The Institute for the Study of War said in their 29 December 2014 situation report that "..The [19th Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/19th_Division_\(Iraq\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) is a new military formation intended to include members from the 2nd, 3rd, 4th, and 12th IA divisions that melted away during the rapid advance of ISIS in June 2014. This formation will almost certainly include volunteer fighters, most likely displaced persons from Mosul, who reside in refugee camps. The.. sectarian composition of the unit will be important to watch. The formation of the division was initially proposed by Defense Minister [Khaled al-Obaidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khaled_al-Obaidi) on November 4, 2014 during a visit to Iraqi Kurdistan. During that visit he requested assistance from the [Kurdistan Regional Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kurdistan_Regional_Government) \[with] basing the new division in Iraqi Kurdistan and giving the force responsibility for clearing Mosul."[^cite_note-120] The 19th Division was not listed in either the institute's 2017 listing of units, or the IISS Military Balance 2022.

Michael Knights wrote in 2016 that the rebuilding from the mid-2014 disaster had been steady but "very slow". "By January 2015 a fair number of brigades had been salvaged and a couple of new brigades were built but the overall frontline combat strength of the ISF was halved due to attrition in the manning of each brigade. \[U]nits were weaker and many were too demoralized or lightly equipped to do more than hold in place. A year later, by January 2016, significant progress has been made in terms of available forces albeit largely by shuffling around personnel and raising around a dozen new and very small 1,000-strong brigades."[^cite_note-121] The new 15th and 16th Divisions have been identified, which appear to comprise some of the new brigades that Knights mentions, including the 71st, 72nd, 73rd, 75th, and 76th.

## Structure



Iraqi Special Operations Forces training April 2011

The Iraqi Army began the [Anglo-Iraqi War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anglo-Iraqi_War) with a force of four divisions. A fifth was formed in 1959. By the outbreak of the [Iran–Iraq War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iran%E2%80%93Iraq_War), the force had grown to nine divisions. By 1990, with wartime expansion, the force had grown greatly to at least 56 divisions, making the Iraqi army the fourth largest army in the world and one of the strongest in the Middle East. After the defeat in the [Persian Gulf War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gulf_War) in 1991, force size dropped to around 23 divisions, as well as [Republican Guard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Republican_Guard) formations. The new army formed after 2003 was initially planned to be three divisions strong, but was then raised to ten divisions, and more later.

The U.S. [House Armed Services Committee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/House_Armed_Services_Committee) commented in 2007: "in the initial fielding plan, five army divisions would be tied to the regions from where they were recruited and the other five would be deployable throughout Iraq. This was partially due to the legacy of some army divisions being formed from the National Guard units and has caused some complications in terms of making these forces available for operations in all areas of Iraq, and the military becoming a truly national, non-sectarian force."[^cite_note-122]

According to the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) *Measuring Safety and Security in Iraq* report of August 2006, plans at that time called for the Iraqi Army to be built up to an approximately 137,500-person force. This was based around an Army with 9 infantry divisions and one mechanised division consisting of 36 brigades and 112 battalions. Nine Motorized Transportation Regiments, 5 logistics battalions, 2 support battalions, 5 Regional Support Units, and 91 Garrison Support Units were intended to provide logistics and support for each division, with Taji National Depot providing depot-level maintenance and resupply. Each battalion, brigade, and division headquarters was to be supported by a Headquarters and Service Company (HSC) providing logistical and maintenance support. The army was also planned to include 17 Strategic Infrastructure Battalions and an [Iraqi Special Operations Forces](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Special_Operations_Forces) brigade of two battalions.[^cite_note-MSSIAug06-123]

After the [Fall of Mosul](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fall_of_Mosul) to [ISIS](9087364.md), a number of new Iraqi formations and units were created. Among them was the [16th Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/16th_Division_\(Iraq\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) which was new when the [82nd Airborne Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/82nd_Airborne_Division)'s 3rd Brigade arrived in 2015, and operational by November 2015 after fighting in [Ramadi](245655.md).[^cite_note-124] In November 2014 the first units of the new 15th and 16th Divisions held graduation ceremonies.[^cite_note-125]

In late 2020, the [International Institute for Strategic Studies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/International_Institute_for_Strategic_Studies) estimated that the Army was about 180,000 strong, with three SF brigades, a ranger brigade HQ which supervised one ranger battalion; the 9th Armoured Division (2 armoured brigades, 2 mech bdes); the 5th, 8th & 10th Divisions with four mechanised infantry brigades each; the [7th Mechanised Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/7th_Division_\(Iraq\)) with 2 mech inf bde and 1 inf bde; the [6th Motorised Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/6th_Division_\(Iraq\)) with three motorised infantry brigades and an infantry brigade; the [14th Motorised Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/14th_Division_\(Iraq\)?action=edit\&redlink=1) with five motorised and infantry brigades; the [1st Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Division_\(Iraq\)) with two infantry brigades; the 11th Infantry Division with three light inf brigades; the [15th Infantry Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/15th_Infantry_Division_\(Iraq\)) with five infantry brigades; the 16th Infantry Division with two infantry brigades; the [17th Commando Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_Division_\(Iraq\)) with four infantry brigades; the independent [17th Infantry Brigade](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/17th_Infantry_Brigade_\(Iraq\)?action=edit\&redlink=1); and the Prime Minister's Security Force division of three infantry brigades.[^cite_note-126]

The Iraqi Army consists of nine regional commands. The Joint Operations Command (JOC) was composed of numerous branches including the Iraqi army, which oversaw all military operations in the country.[^cite_note-127] On 22 July 2026, the JOC was dissolved after it was merged with the Office of the Commander in Chief, which also includes the National Operations Center and the Office of the Personal Secretary of the Commander in Chief.[^cite_note-128][^cite_note-129]

## Training



An Iraqi Army T-72 tank performs a live-fire training exercise at the [Besmaya Gunnery Range](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Besmaya_Range_Complex), in Besmaya, Baghdad, 28 October 2008.

There are three levels of troop capability in the new army: one, two, and three. Level three refers to troops that have just completed basic training, level two refers to troops that are able to work with soldiers, and level one refers to troops that can work by themselves.

Members of [NATO Training Mission – Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NATO_Training_Mission_%E2%80%93_Iraq) (NTM-I) opened a Joint Staff College in [ar Rustamiyah](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ar_Rustamiyah) in Baghdad on September 27, 2005, with 300 trainers. Training at bases in Norway, Italy, Jordan, Germany, and Egypt has also taken place and 16 NATO countries have allocated forces to the training effort.[^cite_note-130]

The [Multi-National Force Iraq](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Multi-National_Force_Iraq) has also conducted a variety of training programs for both enlisted soldiers and officers including training as [medics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Medic), [engineers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Engineer), [quartermasters](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Quartermaster), and [military police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_police). Beyond the various courses and programs being held in-country, both American [staff colleges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Staff_college) and [military academies](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Military_academy) have begun taking Iraqi applicants, with Iraqi cadets being enrolled at both the [United States Military Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy) and the [US Air Force Academy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Air_Force_Academy).[^cite_note-131]

### Recruits and enlisted soldiers



Iraqi soldiers assigned to 71st Iraqi Army Brigade move closer to an objective, seeking a new location for cover during a simulated exercise under fire at [Camp Taji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Taji), Iraq, Nov. 18, 2015

Iraqi Army recruits undergo a standard eight-week[^cite_note-CRS_2007-04-27-109] basic training course that includes basic soldiering skills, weapons marksmanship and individual tactics. Former soldiers are eligible for an abbreviated three-week "Direct Recruit Replacement Training" course designed to replace regular basic training to be followed by more training once they have been assigned to a unit.

Soldiers later go on to enroll in more specific advanced courses targeted for their respective fields. This could involve going to the Military Intelligence School, the Signal School, the Bomb Disposal School, the Combat Arms Branch School, the Engineer School, and the Military Police School.

### Officers

The Iraqi Armed Service and Supply Institute located in [Taji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taji) plays a significant role in training aspiring Iraqi [non-commissioned officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Non-commissioned_officer) and [commissioned officers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Commissioned_officers). The training is based on the [Royal Military Academy Sandhurst](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Royal_Military_Academy_Sandhurst) model, chosen in part due to its shorter graduation time compared to [West Point](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Military_Academy). Much of the Iraqi officer training programme is copied directly from the Sandhurst course.

## Equipment

Main article: [List of current equipment of the Iraqi Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_current_equipment_of_the_Iraqi_Army)

See also: [List of former equipment of the Iraqi Army](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_former_equipment_of_the_Iraqi_Army)



The 2nd Brigade, 1st Iraqi Division took delivery of 10 armored HMMWVs

Virtually all of the equipment used by the former Iraqi Army was either destroyed by the U.S. and British forces during [the 2003 invasion](201936.md), or was looted during the chaotic aftermath shortly after the fall of the Hussein regime. Among these were more than 20,000 sets of body armor.[^cite_note-132] Four [T-55](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-55) tanks however have been recovered from an old army base in al-Muqdadiyah and are now in service with the [1st Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1st_Division_\(Iraq\)).

In February 2004 the U.S. government announced that Nour USA was awarded a \$327,485,798 contract to procure equipment for both the Iraqi Army and the Iraqi National Guard; however, this contract was canceled in March 2004 when an internal Army investigation (initiated due to complaints from losing bidders) revealed that Army procurement officers in Iraq were violating procedures with sloppy contract language and incomplete paperwork. On May of that same year the [U.S. Army Tank-automotive and Armaments Command](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Army_Tank-automotive_and_Armaments_Command) (TACOM) stated that they would award a contract worth \$259,321,656 to ANHAM Joint Venture in exchange for procuring the necessary equipment (and providing its required training) for a minimum of 15 and a maximum of 35 battalions. The minimum bid would begin to be delivered immediately and further orders could be placed until the maximum of 35 battalion sets or September 2006 after the first order was fully delivered.

In May 2005, [Hungary](13275.md) agreed to donate 77 [T-72s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-72) to the Iraqi Army, with the refurbishment contract going to Defense Solutions to bring the tanks up to operational status for an estimated 4.5 million dollars US.[^cite_note-T72-133] After a delay in the payment of funds from the Iraqi government,[^cite_note-134] the [9th Mechanised Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Division_\(Iraq\)) received the tanks at its headquarters in [Taji](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Taji) over a three-day period starting on November 8, 2005.[^cite_note-T72-133]



A convoy of 1st Motor Transport Regiment, 1st Iraqi Army Division [KrAZ-6322](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KrAZ-6322)

On July 29, 2005, the [United Arab Emirates](69328.md) gained approval to purchase 180 [M113A1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M113_Armored_Personnel_Carrier) APCs in good condition from [Switzerland](26748.md), with the intent to transfer them to Iraq as a gift. Domestic political opposition in Switzerland successfully froze the sale, fearing that the export would violate the country's longstanding tradition of neutrality as well as perhaps make Switzerland a target for terrorism.[^cite_note-135]

173 [M113s](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M113), 44 [APC Talhas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/APC_Talha), and 100 [FV103 Spartans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FV103_Spartan) were donated by [Jordan](7515964.md), [Pakistan](23235.md) and UAE. 600 [AMZ Dzik](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AMZ_Dzik)-3 (*Ain Jaria*) APCs were ordered in [Poland](22936.md) (option for 1,200) for delivery by Jan 2007. 573 [Otokar Akrep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otokar_Akrep) APCs for delivery by Jan 2007. 756 [Iraqi Light Armored Vehicles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Iraqi_Light_Armored_Vehicle) (option for 1,050) for delivery by November 2008.[^cite_note-136][^cite_note-137] Greece donated 100 [BMP-1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BMP-1) to the Iraqi Army.

713 [M1114](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1114) and 400 [M1151](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1151) HMMWVs purchased for IA with delivery complete by end July 2006.

[Serbia](29265.md) has signed a US\$230m deal with [Iraq](7515928.md) to sell weapons and military equipment, the defence ministry said in March 2008. It did not specify the weapons but Serbian military experts believe they include Serbian-made [CZ-99](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/CZ-99) hand guns, [Zastava M21](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zastava_M21) 5.56 mm assault rifles, [Zastava M84](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zastava_M84) machine guns, anti-tank weapons ([M79 "Osa"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M79_Rocket_Launcher), [Bumbar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumbar), and [M90 "Strsljen"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M90_Rocket_Launcher)), ammunition and explosives and about 20 [Lasta 95](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utva_Lasta) basic [trainer aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Trainer_aircraft). Iraq's defence Minister [Abdul-Qadir al-Obaidi](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qadir_Obeidi) visited [Belgrade](55904.md) in September and November to discuss boosting military ties with Serbia.[^cite_note-138][^cite_note-139]

In August 2008, the United States proposed military sales to Iraq, which will include the latest upgraded [M1A1 Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1A1_Abrams) battle tanks, attack helicopters, [Stryker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stryker) armored vehicles, modern radios, all to be valued at an estimated \$2.16 billion.[^cite_note-140]

In December 2008, the United States approved a \$6 billion arms deal with Iraq that included 140 [M1A1 Abrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M1A1_Abrams) tanks and 400 [Stryker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stryker) combat vehicles for elite Iraqi army units.[^cite_note-141]

In December 2009, Ukraine has signed a deal to deliver \$550 million worth of arms to Iraq, the agreement with the Iraqi ministry of defense calls for Ukraine to produce and deliver 420 [BTR-4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/BTR-4) armored personnel carriers, six AN-32B military transport planes and other military hardware to Iraq.[^cite_note-142]

In February 2009, the [United States Department of Defense](7279897.md) announced it had struck deals with Iraq that would see Baghdad spend \$5 billion on U.S.-made weapons, equipment and training.[^cite_note-143]

In 2016, Iraq finalized an order with [Uralvagonzavod](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uralvagonzavod) for 73 [T-90S and SK tanks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-90). The T-90SK is a command variant equipped additional radios and navigation equipment. As of 2018, 36 had been delivered and have been assigned to 35th Brigade of the [9th Armored Division](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/9th_Division_\(Iraq\)).

### Uniforms and personal weapons

The average Iraqi soldier is equipped with an assortment of uniforms ranging from the [Desert Camouflage Uniform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Camouflage_Uniform), the 6 color "Chocolate Chip" [DBDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Desert_Battle_Dress_Uniform), the woodland-pattern [BDU](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_Dress_Uniform), the U.S. Marine Corps [MARPAT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/MARPAT), or Jordanian [KA7](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/KA7?action=edit\&redlink=1). Nearly all have a [PASGT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PASGT) ballistic helmet, Generation I OTV [ballistic vest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ballistic_vest), and radio. Their light weapons consist of stocks of [Cold War](325329.md)-era arms, namely the Tabuk series of [Zastava M-70](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zastava_M-70) copies and derivatives like the [Tabuk Sniper Rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tabuk_Sniper_Rifle), the Soviet [AKM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AKM) and the Chinese [Type 56 assault rifles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Type_56_assault_rifle), the [Zastava M72](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zastava_M72) and [PKM](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PK_machine_gun) machine guns, and [Al-Kadesih sniper rifle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dragunov_sniper_rifle) though they have received assistance from the U.S. in the form of American-made weapons, including [M16A2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M16_rifle) and [M16A4](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M16A4) rifles and [M4 carbines](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/M4_carbine).

However weapons registration is poor. A 2006 report by the [Special Inspector General for Iraq Reconstruction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Special_Inspector_General_for_Iraq_Reconstruction) (SIGIR) notes that out of the 370,000 weapons turned over to the U.S. since the fall of Saddam's regime, only 12,000 serial numbers have been recorded.[^cite_note-144] The lack of proper accounting for these weapons makes the acquisition of small arms by anti-governmental forces such as insurgents or sectarian militias much easier.
