**San Diego** ([/ˌsæn diˈeɪɡoʊ/](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/English) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a0/En-us-san_diego.ogg/En-us-san_diego.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/a/a0/En-us-san_diego.ogg/En-us-san_diego.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup> [*SAN dee-AY-goh*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:Pronunciation_respelling_key); Spanish: [\[san ˈdjeɣo\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Spanish)) is a city on the [Pacific coast](751843.md) of [Southern California](62520.md), next to the [Mexican border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border). It is the [eighth-most populous city in the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_United_States_cities_by_population) and [second-most populous city in California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_largest_cities_in_California_by_population) with a population of over 1.4 million, while the San Diego area with over 3.3 million residents is the [18th-largest metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_statistical_area) in the country. San Diego is the [county seat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/County_seat) of [San Diego County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County,_California).[^cite_note-County_QuickFacts-16] It is known for its mild [Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mediterranean_climate), extensive [beaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_beaches_in_San_Diego_County) and [parks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_parks_in_San_Diego), long association with the [United States Navy](20518076.md), and recent emergence as a [Wireless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wireless_communication), [electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electronics), [healthcare](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_hospitals_in_San_Diego), and [biotechnology](4502.md) development center.

Historically home to the [Kumeyaay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumeyaay) people, San Diego has been referred to as the *Birthplace of California*, as it was the first site visited and settled by Europeans on what is now the [West Coast of the United States](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States).[^cite_note-17] In 1542, [Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Cabrillo) claimed the area for [Spain](26667.md), forming the basis for the settlement of [Alta California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California), 200 years later. The [Presidio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidio_of_San_Diego) and [Mission San Diego de Alcalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Diego_de_Alcal%C3%A1), founded in 1769, formed the first European settlement in what is now California. In 1821, San Diego became part of the newly declared [Mexican Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/First_Mexican_Empire). California was [ceded to the U.S.](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Cession) in 1848 following the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War) and was [admitted as the 31st state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Statehood_Act) in 1850.

The largest sectors of the [economy of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_San_Diego) include military and defense-related activities, tourism, international trade, research, and manufacturing. The city is home to several universities, including [UC San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego), [San Diego State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_State_University), and the [University of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_San_Diego). San Diego is the economic center of the [San Diego–Tijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego%E2%80%93Tijuana) region, the second-most populous [transborder metropolitan area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transborder_agglomeration) in the Western Hemisphere, home to an estimated five million people as of 2022.[^cite_note-18] The primary border crossing between San Diego and [Tijuana](101739.md), the [San Ysidro Port of Entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Ysidro_Port_of_Entry), is the busiest international land border crossing in the world outside of Asia ([fourth-busiest](288329.md#Busiest_checkpoints_in_the_world) overall). [San Diego International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_International_Airport) (SAN) is the busiest single-runway airport in the United States.[^cite_note-19]

## Name

San Diego's name can be traced back to the 17th century when Spanish explorer [Sebastián Vizcaíno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Vizca%C3%ADno) bestowed it upon the area in 1602. He named the bay and the surrounding area "San Diego de Alcalá" in honor of [Saint Didacus of Alcalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1).[^cite_note-\:4-20]

Prior to the Spanish establishment of San Diego, the [Kumeyaay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumeyaay) town was called [Kosa'aay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosa'aay), meaning "drying out place" in the [Kumeyaay language](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumeyaay_language).[^cite_note-\:1-21] After the establishment of San Diego, the Kumeyaay called town and city *Tepacul Watai*, meaning "Stacked Big".[^cite_note-22] [Luiseño](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Luise%C3%B1o_language) speakers in the [North County region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_County_\(San_Diego_area\)) called it *Pushuyi*.[^cite_note-23]

## History

For a chronological guide, see [Timeline of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Timeline_of_San_Diego).

### Pre-colonial period

Full length portrait of a man in his thirties wearing a long robe, woman and child visible behind him and dog to his left

The [Kumeyaay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumeyaay_people), referred to by the Spanish as *Diegueños*, have inhabited the area for thousands of years.

What has been referred to as the [San Dieguito complex](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Dieguito_complex) was established in the area at least 9,000 years ago.[^cite_note-24] The [Kumeyaay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumeyaay) may have culturally evolved from this complex or migrated into the area around 1000 C.E.[^cite_note-25] Archaeologist [Malcolm Rogers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Malcolm_Jennings_Rogers) hypothesized that the early cultures of San Diego were separate from the Kumeyaay, but this claim is disputed.[^cite_note-\:3-26] Rogers later reevaluated his claims, yet they were influential in shaping historical tellings of early San Diego history.[^cite_note-\:3-26]

The Kumeyaay established villages scattered across the region, including the village of [Kosa'aay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kosa'aay) which was the Kumeyaay village that the future settlement of San Diego would stem from in today's [Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town,_San_Diego).[^cite_note-\:1-21][^cite_note-\:2-27] The village of Kosa'aay was made up of thirty to forty families living in pyramid-shaped housing structures and was supported by a freshwater spring from the hillsides.[^cite_note-\:1-21]

### Spanish period



Portuguese explorer [Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Cabrillo) landing in [San Diego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Bay) in 1542, claiming California for the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire)

The first European to visit the region was explorer [Juan Rodríguez Cabrillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Rodr%C3%ADguez_Cabrillo). Sailing his flagship *San Salvador* from [Navidad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barra_de_Navidad), New Spain, Cabrillo claimed the bay for the [Spanish Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_Empire) in 1542, and named the site "San Miguel".[^cite_note-28] In November 1602, [Sebastián Vizcaíno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sebasti%C3%A1n_Vizca%C3%ADno) surveyed the harbor and what are now [Mission Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Bay,_San_Diego,_California) and [Point Loma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Loma) and named the area for the Catholic [Saint Didacus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Didacus_of_Alcal%C3%A1), a [Spaniard](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spaniard) more commonly known as *San Diego de Alcalá*.[^cite_note-\:4-20]

The permanent [European colonization](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/European_colonization_of_the_Americas) of both California and San Diego began in 1769 with the arrival of four contingents of Spaniards from New Spain and the [Baja California](199793.md) peninsula. Two seaborne parties reached San Diego Bay: the *San Carlos*, under Vicente Vila and including as notable members the engineer and cartographer [Miguel Costansó](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miguel_Costans%C3%B3) and the soldier and future governor [Pedro Fages](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pedro_Fages), and the *San Antonio*, under [Juan Pérez](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Jos%C3%A9_P%C3%A9rez_Hern%C3%A1ndez). An initial overland expedition to San Diego from the south was led by the soldier [Fernando Rivera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernando_Rivera_y_Moncada) and included the [Franciscan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Franciscan) missionary, explorer, and chronicler [Juan Crespí](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Cresp%C3%AD), followed by a second party led by the designated governor [Gaspar de Portolá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaspar_de_Portol%C3%A1) and including the mission president [Junípero Serra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jun%C3%ADpero_Serra).[^cite_note-29]



[Mission San Diego de Alcalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Diego_de_Alcal%C3%A1) was founded in 1769 by [Saint Junípero Serra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Saint_Jun%C3%ADpero_Serra), making it the oldest of the [Spanish missions in California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_missions_in_California).

In May 1769, Portolà established the [Presidio of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidio_of_San_Diego) on a hill near the [San Diego River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_River) above the Kumeyaay village of Cosoy,[^cite_note-\:1-21] which would later become incorporated into the Spanish settlement,[^cite_note-\:2-27] making it the first settlement by Europeans in what is now the state of California. In July of the same year, [Mission San Diego de Alcalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Diego_de_Alcal%C3%A1) was founded by Franciscan friars under Serra.[^cite_note-30][^cite_note-31] The mission became a site for a Kumeyaay revolt in 1775, which forced the mission to relocate six miles (10 km) up the San Diego River.[^cite_note-32] By 1797, the mission boasted the largest native population in Alta California, with over 1,400 neophytes living in and around the mission proper.[^cite_note-33] Mission San Diego was the southern anchor in Alta California of the historic mission trail [El Camino Real](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Camino_Real_\(California\)). Both the Presidio and the Mission are [National Historic Landmarks](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Register_of_Historic_Places_listings_in_San_Diego_County,_California).[^cite_note-34][^cite_note-35]

### Mexican period



[José María Estudillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jos%C3%A9_Mar%C3%ADa_Estudillo) served as commandant of the [Presidio of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidio_of_San_Diego) and founded the [Estudillo family](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Estudillo_family_of_California), a powerful clan of [Californios](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Californio).

In 1821, Mexico [won its independence from Spain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_War_of_Independence), and San Diego became part of the Mexican territory of [Alta California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alta_California). In 1822, Mexico began its attempt to extend its authority over the coastal territory of Alta California. The fort on Presidio Hill was gradually abandoned, while the town of San Diego grew up on the level land below Presidio Hill. The Mission was [secularized by the Mexican government in 1834](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_secularization_act_of_1833), and most of the Mission lands were granted to former soldiers. The 432 [residents](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vecino) of the town petitioned the governor to form a [pueblo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabildo_\(council\)), and [Juan María Osuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juan_Mar%C3%ADa_Osuna) was elected the first *[alcalde](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alcalde)* ("municipal magistrate"). Beyond the town, Mexican [land grants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Land_grant) expanded the number of [California ranchos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchos_of_California) that modestly added to the local economy.

However, San Diego had been losing population throughout the 1830s due to increasing tension between the settlers and the indigenous [Kumeyaay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kumeyaay). In 1838 the town lost its pueblo status because its size dropped to an estimated 100 to 150 residents.[^cite_note-36] The [ranchos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ranchos_of_California) in the San Diego region faced Kumeyaay raids in the late 1830s and the town itself faced raids in the 1840s.[^cite_note-37]

Americans gained an increased awareness of California and its commercial possibilities from the writings of two countrymen involved in the hide and tallow trade, which was often officially forbidden to foreigners but economically significant, with San Diego serving as a major port and the only one with an adequate harbor: [William Shaler](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Shaler)'s "Journal of a Voyage Between China and the North-Western Coast of America, Made in 1804" and [Richard Henry Dana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Richard_Henry_Dana_Jr.)'s more substantial and convincing account, of his 1834–36 voyage, *[Two Years Before the Mast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Two_Years_Before_the_Mast)*.[^cite_note-38]



[Casa de Estudillo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_de_Estudillo), built 1827, is one of San Diego's oldest buildings and served as inspiration for [Helen Hunt Jackson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Helen_Hunt_Jackson)'s 1884 novel *[Ramona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramona)*.

In 1846, the United States went to war against Mexico and sent a naval and land [expedition to conquer Alta California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_California). At first, they had an easy time of it, capturing the major ports including San Diego, but the Californios in southern Alta California struck back. Following the successful revolt in [Los Angeles](18110.md), the American garrison at San Diego was driven out without firing a shot in early October 1846. Mexican partisans held San Diego for three weeks until October 24, 1846, when the Americans recaptured it. For the next several months the Americans were blockaded inside the pueblo. Skirmishes occurred daily and snipers shot into the town every night. The Californios drove cattle away from the pueblo hoping to starve the Americans and their Californio supporters out. On December 1, the American garrison learned that the dragoons of General [Stephen W. Kearney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stephen_W._Kearney) were at [Warner's Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Warner's_Ranch). Commodore [Robert F. Stockton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Robert_F._Stockton) sent a mounted force of fifty under Captain [Archibald Gillespie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Archibald_Gillespie) to march north to meet him. Their joint command of 150 men, returning to San Diego, encountered about 93 Californios under [Andrés Pico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Andr%C3%A9s_Pico).&#x20;



The 1846 [Battle of San Pasqual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Pasqual) was a decisive battle between American and [Californio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Californio) forces.

In the ensuing [Battle of San Pasqual](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_San_Pasqual), fought in the [San Pasqual Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pasqual_Valley) which is now part of the city of San Diego, the Americans suffered their worst losses in the campaign. Subsequently, a column led by Lieutenant Gray arrived from San Diego, rescuing Kearny's command.[^cite_note-39] Stockton and Kearny went on to recover Los Angeles and force the capitulation of Alta California with the "[Treaty of Cahuenga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Cahuenga)" on January 13, 1847. As a result of the [Mexican–American War](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican%E2%80%93American_War) of 1846–48, the territory of Alta California, including San Diego, was ceded to the United States by Mexico, under the terms of the [Treaty of Guadalupe Hidalgo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Treaty_of_Guadalupe_Hidalgo) in 1848. The Mexican negotiators of that treaty tried to retain San Diego as part of Mexico, but the Americans insisted that San Diego was "for every commercial purpose of nearly equal importance to us with that of San Francisco", and the Mexican–American border was eventually established to be one league south of the southernmost point of [San Diego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Bay).[^cite_note-40]

### American period



View of [San Diego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Bay) in 1873 following the [U.S. conquest of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conquest_of_California)

The state of California was admitted to the United States in 1850. That same year San Diego was designated the seat of the newly established County of San Diego and was incorporated as a city. [Joshua H. Bean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joshua_H._Bean), the last alcalde of San Diego, was elected the first mayor. Two years later the city was bankrupt;[^cite_note-41] the California legislature revoked the city's charter and placed it under control of a board of trustees, where it remained until 1889. A city charter was reestablished in 1889, and today's city charter was adopted in 1931.[^cite_note-42]

The original town of San Diego was located at the foot of Presidio Hill, in the area which is now [Old Town San Diego State Historic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_San_Diego_State_Historic_Park). The location was not ideal, being several miles away from navigable water at its port at [La Playa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Playa,_San_Diego). In 1850, [William Heath Davis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/William_Heath_Davis) promoted a new development by the bay shore called "New San Diego", several miles south of the original settlement; however, for several decades the new development consisted only of a pier, a few houses and an [Army depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Barracks) for the support of [Fort Yuma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Yuma). After 1854, the fort became supplied by sea and by [steamboats on the Colorado River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Steamboats_of_the_Colorado_River) and the depot fell into disuse. From 1857 to 1860, San Diego became the western terminus of the [San Antonio-San Diego Mail Line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Antonio-San_Diego_Mail_Line), the earliest overland [stagecoach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stagecoach) and mail operation from the [Eastern United States](509486.md) to California, coming from [Texas](29810.md) through [New Mexico Territory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/New_Mexico_Territory) in less than 30 days.[^cite_note-Pierce-43]

Oval, black and white shoulder-height portrait of a man in his forties or fifties, slightly balding wearing a suit

[Horton Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Horton_Plaza_and_Broadway_Fountain) honors [Alonzo Horton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonzo_Horton), who helped develop [Downtown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Diego).

In the late 1860s, [Alonzo Horton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alonzo_Horton) promoted a move to the bayside area, which he called "New Town" and which became [downtown San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Diego). Horton promoted the area heavily, and people and businesses began to relocate to New Town because its location on [San Diego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Bay) was convenient to shipping. New Town soon eclipsed the original settlement, known to this day as [Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town,_San_Diego,_California), and became the economic and governmental heart of the city.[^cite_note-Cornerstone-44] Still, San Diego remained a relatively backwater town until the arrival of a railroad connection in 1878.

In 1912, San Diego was the site of a [free speech fight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_free_speech_fight) between the [Industrial Workers of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Workers_of_the_World) and the city government who passed an ordinance forbidding the [freedom of speech](21401843.md) along an area of "Soapbox Row" that led to civil disobedience, [vigilantism](58325.md), [police violence](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Police_violence), the abduction of [Emma Goldman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emma_Goldman)'s husband [Ben Reitman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Reitman) and [multiple riots](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_free_speech_fight).[^cite_note-45][^cite_note-46] San Diego's proximity to Tijuana during the [Mexican Revolution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_Revolution) made this one of the most significant [free speech fights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Free_speech_fights) during the [Wobbly](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wobbly) era.[^cite_note-47]

In 1916, the neighborhood of [Stingaree](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stingaree,_San_Diego), the original home of San Diego's first [Chinatown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinatown) and "Soapbox Row", was demolished by anti-[vice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vice) campaigners to make way for the [Gaslamp Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaslamp_Quarter,_San_Diego).[^cite_note-48]

Hand drawn illustration of Balboa Park

[Balboa Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balboa_Park_\(San_Diego\)) was built for the [Panama-California Exposition of 1915](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama%E2%80%93California_Exposition).

In the early part of the 20th century, San Diego hosted the [World's Fair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World's_Fair) twice: the [Panama–California Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Panama%E2%80%93California_Exposition) in 1915 and the [California Pacific International Exposition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Pacific_International_Exposition) in 1935. Both expositions were held in [Balboa Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balboa_Park_\(San_Diego\)), and many of the Spanish/Baroque-style buildings that were built for those expositions remain to this day as central features of the park.[^cite_note-49] The menagerie of exotic animals featured at the 1915 exposition provided the basis for the [San Diego Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Zoo).[^cite_note-50] During the 1950s there was a citywide festival called [Fiesta del Pacifico](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fiesta_del_Pacifico) highlighting the area's Spanish and Mexican past.[^cite_note-51]

The southern portion of the [Point Loma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Loma,_San_Diego) peninsula was set aside for military purposes as early as 1852. Over the next several decades the [Army](32087.md) set up a series of coastal artillery batteries and named the area [Fort Rosecrans](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fort_Rosecrans).[^cite_note-52] Significant U.S. Navy presence began in 1901 with the establishment of the Navy Coaling Station in Point Loma, and expanded greatly during the 1920s.[^cite_note-53] By 1930, the city was host to [Naval Base San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_San_Diego), [Naval Training Center San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Training_Center_San_Diego), [San Diego Naval Hospital](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Naval_Hospital), [Camp Matthews](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Matthews), and [Camp Kearny](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Camp_Kearny) (now [Marine Corps Air Station Miramar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Miramar)). The city was also an early center for aviation: as early as World War I, San Diego was proclaiming itself "The Air Capital of the West".[^cite_note-Shepherd-54] The city was home to important airplane developers and manufacturers like Ryan Airlines (later [Ryan Aeronautical](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ryan_Aeronautical)), founded in 1925, and [Consolidated Aircraft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Consolidated_Aircraft) (later [Convair](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Convair)), founded in 1923.[^cite_note-55] [Charles A. Lindbergh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_A._Lindbergh)'s plane, the *[Spirit of St. Louis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spirit_of_St._Louis)*, was built in San Diego in 1927 by Ryan Airlines.[^cite_note-Shepherd-54]



[Downtown San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Diego), c. 1903

During [World War II](32927.md), San Diego became a major hub of military and defense activity, due to the presence of so many military installations and defense manufacturers. The city's population grew rapidly during and after World War II, more than doubling between 1930 (147,995) and 1950 (333,865).[^cite_note-RM_54-56] During the final months of the war, the Japanese had a plan to target multiple U.S. cities for [biological attack](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Biological_attack), starting with San Diego. The plan was called "[Operation Cherry Blossoms at Night](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Cherry_Blossoms_at_Night)" and called for [kamikaze](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamikaze) planes filled with fleas infected with plague (*[Yersinia pestis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yersinia_pestis)*) to crash into civilian population centers in the city, hoping to spread plague in the city and effectively kill tens of thousands of civilians. The plan was scheduled to launch on September 22, 1945, but the plan was not carried out because [Japan surrendered](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_surrender) five weeks earlier.[^cite_note-57][^cite_note-58][^cite_note-59]

After World War II, the military continued to play a major role in the local economy, but post–[Cold War](325329.md) cutbacks took a heavy toll on the local defense and aerospace industries. The resulting downturn led San Diego leaders to seek to diversify the city's economy by focusing on research and science, as well as tourism.[^cite_note-60]

In the early 1960s, [Tom Hom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tom_Hom) would become the first Asian American member of the San Diego City Council.[^cite_note-61] He would be succeeded by [Leon Williams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Leon_Williams_\(politician\)), the first Black member of the city council.[^cite_note-62]



Starting in the 1980s, many areas of Downtown, such as the [Marina District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marina,_San_Diego), underwent [redevelopment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Urban_renewal).

From the start of the 20th century through the 1970s, the American [tuna](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tuna) fishing fleet and tuna canning industry were based in San Diego, "the tuna capital of the world".[^cite_note-gala-63] San Diego's first tuna cannery was founded in 1911, and by the mid-1930s the canneries employed more than 1,000 people. A large fishing fleet supported the canneries, mostly staffed by immigrant fishermen from [Japan](15573.md), and later from the [Azores](3226.md) and [Italy](14532.md) whose influence is still felt in neighborhoods like [Little Italy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Little_Italy,_San_Diego) and [Point Loma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Loma,_San_Diego).[^cite_note-64][^cite_note-65] Due to rising costs and foreign competition, the last of the canneries closed in the early 1980s.[^cite_note-66]

Downtown San Diego was in decline in the 1960s and 1970s, but experienced some urban renewal since the early 1980s, including the opening of [Horton Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Westfield_Horton_Plaza), the revival of the Gaslamp Quarter, and the construction of the [San Diego Convention Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Convention_Center); [Petco Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petco_Park) opened in 2004.[^cite_note-Erie-67] Outside of downtown, San Diego annexed large swaths of land for suburban expansion to the north and control of the [San Ysidro Port of Entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Ysidro_Port_of_Entry).

As the [Cold War](325329.md) ended, the military shrank and so did defense spending. San Diego has since become a center of the emerging biotech industry and is home to telecommunications giant [Qualcomm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualcomm). San Diego had also grown in the tourism industry with the popularity of attractions such as the [San Diego Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Zoo), [SeaWorld San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaWorld_San_Diego), and [Legoland California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Legoland_California) in [Carlsbad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carlsbad,_California).[^cite_note-68]

## Geography

See also: [List of beaches in San Diego, California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_beaches_in_San_Diego,_California) and [Parks in San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parks_in_San_Diego)



Satellite view of the [San Diego–Tijuana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego%E2%80%93Tijuana) area, a [transborder agglomeration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transborder_agglomeration) straddling the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border) in [the Californias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Californias)

According to SDSU professor emeritus Monte Marshall, [San Diego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Bay) is "the surface expression of a north-south-trending, nested [graben](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Graben)". The [Rose Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rose_Canyon_Fault) and [Point Loma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Loma_Formation) [fault zones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fault_\(geology\)) are part of the [San Andreas Fault](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Andreas_Fault) system. About 40 miles (64 km) east of the bay are the [Laguna Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laguna_Mountains) in the [Peninsular Ranges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Peninsular_Ranges), which are part of the [American Cordillera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Cordillera).[^cite_note-Marshall-69]

The city lies on approximately 200 deep canyons and hills separating its [mesas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mesa), creating small pockets of natural open space scattered throughout the city and giving it a hilly geography.[^cite_note-70] Traditionally, San Diegans have built their homes and businesses on the mesas, while leaving the urban canyons relatively wild.[^cite_note-71] Thus, the canyons give parts of the city a segmented feel, creating gaps between otherwise proximate neighborhoods and contributing to a low-density, car-centered environment. The [San Diego River](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_River) runs through the middle of San Diego from east to west, creating a river valley that serves to divide the city into northern and southern segments. Several reservoirs and [Mission Trails Regional Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Trails_Regional_Park) also lie between and separate developed areas of the city.



[Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrey_Pines_State_Natural_Reserve)

Notable peaks within the city limits include [Cowles Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cowles_Mountain), the highest point in the city at 1,591 feet (485 m);[^cite_note-city_data-9] [Black Mountain](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Black_Mountain_Open_Space_Park) at 1,558 feet (475 m); and [Mount Soledad](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mount_Soledad) at 824 feet (251 m). The [Cuyamaca Mountains](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cuyamaca_Mountains) and Laguna Mountains rise to the east of the city, and beyond the mountains are desert areas. [Cleveland National Forest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cleveland_National_Forest) is a half-hour drive from downtown San Diego. Numerous farms are found in the valleys northeast and southeast of the city.

Under the [Köppen–Geiger climate classification system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/K%C3%B6ppen%E2%80%93Geiger_climate_classification_system), the San Diego area has been variously categorized as having either a [hot semi-arid climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate) (*[BSh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot_semi-arid_climate)* in the original classification[^cite_note-73] and *BSkn* in modified Köppen classification with the n denoting summer fog)[^cite_note-74] or a [hot-summer Mediterranean climate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hot-summer_Mediterranean_climate)[^cite_note-75] (*Csa*).[^cite_note-76] San Diego's climate is characterized by warm, dry summers and mild winters, with most of the annual precipitation falling between December and March. The city has a mild climate year-round,[^cite_note-77] with an average of 201 days above 70 °F (21 °C) and low rainfall (9–13 inches \[230–330 mm] annually).

The climate in San Diego, like most of Southern California, often varies significantly over short geographical distances, resulting in [microclimates](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microclimate). In San Diego, this is mostly because of the city's topography (the Bay, and the numerous hills, mountains, and canyons). Frequently, particularly during the "May gray/[June gloom](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/June_Gloom)" period, a thick "[marine layer](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_layer)" cloud cover keeps the air cool and damp within a few miles of the coast, but yields to bright cloudless sunshine approximately 5–10 miles (8–16 km) inland.[^cite_note-78] Sometimes the June gloom lasts into July, causing cloudy skies over most of San Diego for the entire day.[^cite_note-weather1-79][^cite_note-weather_el_cajon-80] Even in the absence of June gloom, inland areas experience much more significant temperature variations than coastal areas, where the ocean serves as a moderating influence. Thus, for example, downtown San Diego averages January lows of 50 °F (10 °C) and August highs of 78 °F (26 °C). The city of [El Cajon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cajon,_California), just 12 miles (19 km) inland from downtown San Diego, averages January lows of 42 °F (6 °C) and August highs of 88 °F (31 °C).

The average surface temperature of the water at Scripps Pier in the [California Current](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Current) has increased by almost 3 °F (1.7 °C) since 1950, according to scientists at [Scripps Institution of Oceanography](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Institution_of_Oceanography).[^cite_note-81] Additionally, the mean minimum is now above 40 °F (4 °C), putting San Diego in [hardiness zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hardiness_zone) 11, with the last freeze having occurred many decades ago.



Surfers at [Pacific Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Beach,_San_Diego)

Annual rainfall along the coast averages 10.65 inches (271 mm) and the median is 9.6 inches (240 mm).[^cite_note-82] The months of December through March supply most of the rain, with February the only month averaging 2 inches (51 mm) or more. The months of May through September tend to be almost completely dry. Although there are few wet days per month during the rainy period, rainfall can be heavy when it does fall. Rainfall is usually greater in the higher elevations of San Diego; some of the higher areas can receive 11–15 inches (280–380 mm) per year. Variability from year to year can be dramatic: in the wettest years of 1883/1884 and 1940/1941, more than 24 inches (610 mm) fell, whilst in the driest years there was as little as 3.2 inches (80 mm). The wettest month on record is December 1921 with 9.21 inches (234 mm).

Snow in the city is rare, having been observed only six times in the century and a half that records have been kept.[^cite_note-83] On February 21, 2019, snow fell and accumulated in residential areas of the city, but none fell in the downtown area.[^cite_note-84]

### Ecology

See also: [California coastal sage and chaparral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_coastal_sage_and_chaparral)



View of [Coronado](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coronado,_California) from [Cabrillo National Monument](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cabrillo_National_Monument)

Like much of [Southern California](62520.md), the majority of San Diego's current area was originally occupied on the west by [coastal sage scrub](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coastal_sage_scrub) and on the east by [chaparral](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaparral), plant communities made up mostly of drought-resistant shrubs.[^cite_note-91] The steep and varied topography and proximity to the ocean create a number of different habitats within the city limits, including [tidal marsh](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidal_marsh) and [canyons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Canyons). The chaparral and coastal sage scrub habitats in low elevations along the coast are prone to [wildfire](56106.md), and the rates of fire increased in the 20th century, due primarily to fires starting near the borders of urban and wild areas.[^cite_note-FireVegetation-92]

San Diego's broad city limits encompass a number of large nature preserves, including [Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrey_Pines_State_Natural_Reserve), [Los Peñasquitos Canyon Preserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Pe%C3%B1asquitos_Canyon_Preserve), and [Mission Trails Regional Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Trails_Regional_Park). Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve and a coastal strip continuing to the north constitute one of only two locations where the rare species of Torrey Pine, *[Pinus torreyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrey_pine)*, is found.[^cite_note-TorreyPine-93] Due to the steep topography that prevents or discourages building, along with some efforts for preservation, there are also a large number of canyons within the city limits that serve as nature preserves, including [Switzer Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Switzer_Canyon), Tecolote Canyon Natural Park,[^cite_note-TecoloteCanyon-94] and Marian Bear Memorial Park in [San Clemente Canyon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Clemente_Canyon),[^cite_note-MarianBear-95] as well as a number of small parks and preserves.

Serra Museum at [Presidio Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Presidio_Park)

San Diego County has one of the highest counts of animal and plant species that appear on the [endangered list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Fish_and_Wildlife_Service_list_of_endangered_species) of counties in the United States.[^cite_note-legacy.utsandiego.com-96] Because of its diversity of habitat and its position on the [Pacific Flyway](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Flyway), San Diego County has recorded 492 different bird species, more than any other region in the country.[^cite_note-97] San Diego always scores high in the number of bird species observed in the annual [Christmas Bird Count](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christmas_Bird_Count), sponsored by the [Audubon Society](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Audubon_Society), and it is known as one of the "birdiest" areas in the United States.[^cite_note-98][^cite_note-99]

San Diego and its backcountry suffer from periodic wildfires. In October 2003, San Diego was the site of the [Cedar Fire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cedar_Fire_\(2003\)), at that time the largest wildfire in California over the past century.[^cite_note-CedarLargeFire-100] The fire burned 280,000 acres (1,100 km<sup>2</sup>), killed 15 people, and destroyed more than 2,200 homes.[^cite_note-101] In addition to damage caused by the fire, smoke resulted in a significant increase in emergency room visits; the poor air quality caused San Diego County schools to close for a week.[^cite_note-102] The [October 2007 California wildfires](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/October_2007_California_wildfires) destroyed some areas, particularly within [Rancho Bernardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Bernardo,_San_Diego), as well as the nearby communities of [Rancho Santa Fe](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Santa_Fe,_California) and [Ramona](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ramona,_California).[^cite_note-legacy.utsandiego.com-96]

### Neighborhoods

Main article: [List of communities and neighborhoods of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_communities_and_neighborhoods_of_San_Diego)

The City of San Diego recognizes 52 individual areas as Community Planning Areas.[^cite_note-103] Within a given planning area there may be several distinct neighborhoods. Altogether the city contains more than 100 identified [neighborhoods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neighborhoods_of_San_Diego,_California).

[Downtown San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Diego) is located on [San Diego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Bay). [Balboa Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balboa_Park_\(San_Diego\)) encompasses several mesas and canyons to the northeast, surrounded by older, dense urban communities including [Hillcrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillcrest,_San_Diego) and [North Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/North_Park,_San_Diego). To the east and southeast lie [City Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_Heights,_San_Diego), the [College Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_Area), and [Southeast San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Southeast_San_Diego). To the north lies [Mission Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Valley) and [Interstate 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_8). The communities north of the valley and freeway, and south of [Marine Corps Air Station Miramar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Air_Station_Miramar), include [Clairemont](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Clairemont,_San_Diego), [Kearny Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kearny_Mesa), [Tierrasanta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tierrasanta), and [Navajo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Navajo,_San_Diego). Stretching north from Miramar are the northern suburbs of [Mira Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mira_Mesa), [Scripps Ranch](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Scripps_Ranch), [Rancho Peñasquitos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Pe%C3%B1asquitos), and [Rancho Bernardo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Bernardo). The far northeast portion of the city encompasses [Lake Hodges](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lake_Hodges) and the [San Pasqual Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Pasqual_Valley), which holds an agricultural preserve. [Carmel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmel_Valley,_San_Diego) and [Del Mar Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Mar_Heights,_San_Diego) occupy the northwest corner of the city. To their south are [Torrey Pines State Natural Reserve](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrey_Pines_State_Natural_Reserve) and the business center of the [Golden Triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Golden_Triangle,_San_Diego). Further south are the beach and coastal communities of [La Jolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla), [Pacific Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Beach,_San_Diego), [Mission Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Beach,_San_Diego), and [Ocean Beach](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ocean_Beach,_San_Diego). [Point Loma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Point_Loma) occupies the peninsula across San Diego Bay from downtown. The communities of [South San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_San_Diego) (an [Exclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Exclave)), such as [San Ysidro](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Ysidro,_San_Diego) and [Otay Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otay_Mesa), are located next to the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border), and are physically separated from the rest of the city by the cities of [National City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_City,_California) and [Chula Vista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chula_Vista). A narrow strip of land at the bottom of San Diego Bay connects these southern neighborhoods with the rest of the city.[^cite_note-104]

### Cityscape

Main article: [List of tallest buildings in San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_San_Diego)



Aerial view of central San Diego

San Diego was originally centered on the [Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town,_San_Diego) district, but by the late 1860s the focus had shifted to the bayfront, in the belief that this new location would increase trade. As the "New Town" – present-day Downtown – waterfront location quickly developed, it eclipsed Old Town as the center of San Diego.[^cite_note-Cornerstone-44]

The first skyscraper over 300 feet (91 m) in San Diego was the [El Cortez Hotel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/El_Cortez_\(San_Diego\)), built in 1927; it was the tallest building in the city until 1963.[^cite_note-107] As time went on, multiple buildings claimed the title of San Diego's tallest skyscraper, including the [530 B Street](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/530_B_Street) and [Symphony Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_Towers). Currently the tallest building in San Diego is [One America Plaza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/One_America_Plaza), standing 500 feet (150 m) tall, which was completed in 1991.[^cite_note-EmpOneAmer-108] The downtown skyline contains no [supertall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Supertall) buildings due to a regulation put in place by the [Federal Aviation Administration](11186.md) (FAA) in the 1970s, which set a 500 feet (152 m) limit on the height of buildings within a one-mile (1.6 km) radius of [San Diego International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_International_Airport).[^cite_note-500feetAirport-109] An iconic description of the skyline includes its skyscrapers being compared to the tools of a toolbox.[^cite_note-SkyGrowsUp-110]

There are [several new high-rises under construction](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_tallest_buildings_in_San_Diego), including two that exceed 400 feet (122 m) in height.

### 2010

The city had a population of 1,307,402 according to the 2010 census, distributed over a land area of 372.1 square miles (963.7 km<sup>2</sup>).[^cite_note-135] The urban area of San Diego had a total population of 2,956,746, making it the [third-largest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_urbanized_areas_in_California_\(by_population\)) in the state, after those of [Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_metropolitan_area) and [San Francisco](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_metropolitan_area).

The 2010 population represented an increase of just under 7% from the 1,223,400 people reported in 2000.[^cite_note-Census_SD_quickfacts-130] The population density was 3,771.9 inhabitants per square mile (1,456.3/km<sup>2</sup>). The racial makeup of San Diego was 58.9% [White](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/White_American), 6.7% Black, 0.6% Native American, 15.9% [Asian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asian_American) (5.9% [Filipino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Filipino_American), 2.7% [Chinese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chinese_American), 2.5% [Vietnamese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vietnamese_American), 1.3% [Indian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Indian_American), 1.0% [Korean](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Korean_American), 0.7% [Japanese](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_American), 0.4% [Laotian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laotian_American), 0.3% [Cambodian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambodian_American), 0.1% [Thai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Thai_American)). 0.5% [Pacific Islander](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Islander_American) (0.2% [Guamanian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guamanian), 0.1% [Samoan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Samoan_American), 0.1% [Native Hawaiian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Native_Hawaiian)), 12.3% from [other races](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Race_\(United_States_Census\)), and 5.1% from two or more races. 28.8% of the population was [Hispanic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_and_Latino_Americans) or [Latino](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Latino_\(U.S._Census\)) (of any race);[^cite_note-Census_SD_quickfacts-130][^cite_note-census2010-136] 24.9% of the total population was of [Mexican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_American) heritage, 1.4% [Spanish](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Spanish_American) and 0.6% [Puerto Rican](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Puerto_Rican_people). The median age of Hispanic residents was 27.5 years, compared to 35.1 years overall and 41.6 years among non-Hispanic whites; Hispanic San Diegans were the largest group under the age of 18, while non-Hispanic whites constituted 63.1% of population 55 and older.



Map of racial distribution in San Diego, 2010 U.S. Census. Each dot is 25 people: ⬤ Non-Hispanic White ⬤ Black ⬤ Asian ⬤ Hispanic ⬤ Other

As of January 2019, the San Diego City and County had the fifth-largest [homeless](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Homelessness_in_the_United_States) population among major cities in the United States, with 8,102 people experiencing homelessness.[^cite_note-137] In the city of San Diego, 4,887 individuals were experiencing homelessness according to the 2020 count.[^cite_note-138] A December 11, 2023, article in *[The San Diego Union-Tribune](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_San_Diego_Union-Tribune)* by Blake Nelson reports a notable decline in the homeless population in downtown San Diego, specifically in the urban core. According to data from the Downtown San Diego Partnership, the number of individuals living outside or in vehicles has reached a two-year low, standing at approximately 1,200 as of last month. The decrease is attributed to the implementation of the city's camping ban and the concerted efforts to establish new shelters. While enforcement has led to relatively few individuals being punished, the threat of legal consequences appears to have played a role in the reduction.[^cite_note-139]



[Barrio Logan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barrio_Logan) is a [Chicano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicano) cultural hub and [ethnic enclave](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ethnic_enclave).

In 2000 there were 451,126 households, out of which 30.2% had children under the age of 18 living with them, 44.6% were married couples living together, 11.4% had a female householder with no husband present, and 39.8% were non-families. Households made up of individuals account for 28.0%, and 7.4% had someone living alone who was 65 years of age or older. The average household size was 2.61, and the average family size was 3.30.

The U.S. Census Bureau reported that in 2000, 24.0% of San Diego residents were under 18, and 10.5% were 65 and over.[^cite_note-Census_SD_quickfacts-130] As of 2011 the median age was 35.6; more than a quarter of residents were under age 20 and 11% were over age 65.[^cite_note-140] [Millennials](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Millennials) (ages 26 through 42) constitute 27.1% of San Diego's population, the second-highest percentage in a major U.S. city.[^cite_note-141] The San Diego County regional planning agency, SANDAG, provides tables and graphs breaking down the city population into five-year age groups.[^cite_note-142]

In 2000, the [median income for a household](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_household_income) in the city was \$45,733, and the median income for a family was \$53,060. Males had a median income of \$36,984 versus \$31,076 for females. The per capita income for the city was \$35,199.[^cite_note-2000SDCensus-143] According to *[Forbes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Forbes)* in 2005, San Diego was the fifth wealthiest U.S. city,[^cite_note-ForbesRichestCities-144] but about 10.6% of families and 14.6% of the population were below the poverty line, including 20.0% of those under age 18 and 7.6% of those age 65 or over.[^cite_note-2000SDCensus-143] As of January 1, 2008, estimates by the [San Diego Association of Governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Association_of_Governments) revealed that the household median income for San Diego rose to \$66,715, up from \$45,733 in 2000.[^cite_note-sandag-145]

San Diego was named the ninth-most [LGBT](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LGBT_rights_in_the_United_States)-friendly city in the U.S. in 2013.[^cite_note-146] The city also has the [seventh-highest population](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Top_US_Gay_Populations) of gay residents in the U.S. Additionally in 2013, [San Diego State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_State_University#LGBT-Friendly_campus) (SDSU), one of the city's prominent universities, was named one of the top LGBT-friendly campuses in the nation.[^cite_note-147]

### Religion



Hsi Fang Buddhist temple in [University Heights](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_Heights,_San_Diego).&#x20;

According to a 2023-24 study by the [Pew Research Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pew_Research_Center), 53 percent of the city identified themselves as [Christians](18337522.md). Among Christians, 25% professed adherence to various [Protestant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Protestant) churches, 21% professed [Roman Catholic](606848.md) beliefs, and 3% identified as [Latter-day Saint (Mormon)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Church_of_Jesus_Christ_of_Latter-day_Saints)[^cite_note-148] In the same study, 40% of San Diegans claimed [no religious affiliation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Irreligion). The same study found that followers of other religions (including [Islam](6037917.md), [Judaism](15624.md), [Hinduism](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hinduism), and [Buddhism](3267529.md)) collectively made up about 6% of the population.

## Economy

Main article: [Economy of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Economy_of_San_Diego)

### Defense and military



View of [Naval Base San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Naval_Base_San_Diego)

The economy of San Diego is influenced by [its deepwater port](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_San_Diego), which includes the only major submarine and shipbuilding yards on the [West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States).[^cite_note-154] Several major national [defense contractors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defense_contractor) were started and are headquartered in San Diego, including [General Atomics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/General_Atomics), [Cubic](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cubic_Corporation), and [NASSCO](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Steel_and_Shipbuilding_Company).[^cite_note-155][^cite_note-156]

San Diego hosts the largest naval fleet in the world:[^cite_note-157] In 2008 it was home to 53 ships, over 120 tenant commands, and more than 35,000 sailors, marines, [Department of Defense](7279897.md) civilian employees and contractors.[^cite_note-Navy.mil-158] About 5 percent of all civilian jobs in the county are military-related, and 15,000 businesses in San Diego County rely on Department of Defense contracts.[^cite_note-Navy.mil-158]



[Marine Corps Recruit Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Marine_Corps_Recruit_Depot_San_Diego)

Military bases in San Diego include [US Navy](20518076.md) facilities, [Marine Corps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USMC) bases, and [Coast Guard](32223.md) stations. The city is "home to the majority of the U.S. Pacific Fleet's surface combatants, all of the Navy's West Coast amphibious ships and a variety of Coast Guard and Military Sealift Command vessels".[^cite_note-Navy.mil-158][^cite_note-159]

The military infrastructure in San Diego is still growing and developing, with numerous military personnel stationed there, numbers of which are expected to rise. This plays a significant role in the city's economy, as of 2020, it provides roughly 25% of the GDP and provides 23% of the total jobs in San Diego.[^cite_note-160][^cite_note-161][^cite_note-162]

### Tourism



[Casa de Balboa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Casa_de_Balboa) at [Balboa Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balboa_Park_\(San_Diego\)) is home to the [San Diego History Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_History_Center).

Tourism is a major industry owing to the city's climate, [beaches](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beaches_in_San_Diego,_California),[^cite_note-163] and tourist attractions such as [Balboa Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Balboa_Park_\(San_Diego\)), [Belmont Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Belmont_Park_\(San_Diego\)), [San Diego Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Zoo), [San Diego Zoo Safari Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Zoo_Safari_Park), and [SeaWorld San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/SeaWorld_San_Diego). San Diego's Spanish and Mexican heritage is reflected in many historic sites across the city, such as [Mission San Diego de Alcalá](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_San_Diego_de_Alcal%C3%A1) and [Old Town San Diego State Historic Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_San_Diego_State_Historic_Park). Also, the [local craft brewing industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beer_in_San_Diego_County,_California) attracts an increasing number of visitors[^cite_note-164] for "beer tours" and the annual San Diego Beer Week in November;[^cite_note-165] San Diego has been called "America's Craft Beer Capital".[^cite_note-166]

San Diego County hosted more than 32 million visitors in 2012; collectively they spent an estimated \$8 billion. The visitor industry provides employment for more than 160,000 people.[^cite_note-sandiego.org-167]

San Diego's cruise ship industry used to be the second-largest in California. Numerous cruise lines operate out of San Diego. However, cruise ship business has been in decline since 2008, when the Port hosted over 250 ship calls and more than 900,000 passengers. By 2016–2017, the number of ship calls had fallen to 90.[^cite_note-MexicanRivieraLikelyEnd-168]

Local sightseeing cruises are offered in San Diego Bay and Mission Bay, as well as whale-watching cruises to observe the migration of [gray whales](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gray_whale), peaking in mid-January.[^cite_note-InTheCitySanDiego.com-169] [Sport fishing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sport_fishing) is another popular tourist attraction; San Diego is home to southern California's biggest sport fishing fleet.[^cite_note-170]

### International trade



The [Port of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_San_Diego) is the third-busiest port in [California](5407.md).

San Diego's commercial port and its location on the [United States–Mexico border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States%E2%80%93Mexico_border) make international trade an important factor in the city's economy. The city is authorized by the United States government to operate as a [foreign-trade zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Foreign-trade_zone).[^cite_note-171]

The city shares a 15-mile (24 km) border with Mexico that includes two border crossings. San Diego hosts the busiest international land border crossing outside of Asia in the San Ysidro neighborhood at the [San Ysidro Port of Entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Ysidro_Land_Port_of_Entry_Expansion_Project).[^cite_note-172] A second, primarily commercial border crossing operates in the [Otay Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otay_Mesa,_San_Diego) area; it is the largest commercial crossing on the California–[Baja California](199793.md) border and handles the third-highest volume of trucks and dollar value of trade among all United States-Mexico land crossings.[^cite_note-173]



[San Ysidro Port of Entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Ysidro_Port_of_Entry) is the [4th-busiest border crossing in the world](288329.md#Busiest_checkpoints_in_the_world) and the busiest in the [Western Hemisphere](73162.md).

The [Port of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_San_Diego) is the third-busiest port in California and one of the busiest on the [West Coast](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/West_Coast_of_the_United_States). One of the Port of San Diego's two [cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cargo) facilities is located in [downtown San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Downtown_San_Diego) at the Tenth Avenue Marine Terminal. This terminal has facilities for [containers](22849124.md), [bulk cargo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bulk_cargo), and [refrigerated](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Refrigerated) and frozen storage, so that it can handle the import and export of many commodities.[^cite_note-174] In 2009 the Port of San Diego handled 1,137,054 short tons of total trade; foreign trade accounted for 956,637 short tons while domestic trade amounted to 180,417 short tons.[^cite_note-175]

Historically tuna fishing and canning was one of San Diego's major industries,[^cite_note-176] although the American tuna fishing fleet is no longer based in San Diego. Seafood company [Bumble Bee Foods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bumble_Bee_Foods) is headquartered in San Diego, as was [Chicken of the Sea](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicken_of_the_Sea) until 2018.[^cite_note-177][^cite_note-178]

### Companies



The [AT\&T Building](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AT\&T_Building_\(San_Diego\))

San Diego hosts several major producers of wireless cellular technology. [Qualcomm](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Qualcomm) was founded and is headquartered in San Diego, and is one of the largest private-sector employers in San Diego.[^cite_note-SDLargeEmployers-179] Other wireless industry manufacturers with U.S. headquarters here include [Nokia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nokia), [LG Electronics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/LG_Electronics),[^cite_note-180] [Kyocera International](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kyocera_International),[^cite_note-181] [Cricket Communications](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cricket_Communications) and Novatel Wireless.[^cite_note-182] San Diego also has the U.S. headquarters for the Slovak security company [ESET](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ESET).[^cite_note-183] San Diego has been designated as an iHub Innovation Center for potential collaboration between wireless and the life sciences.[^cite_note-184]

The [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego) and other research institutions have helped to fuel the growth of [biotechnology](4502.md).[^cite_note-UCSDIsrael-185] In 2013, San Diego had the second-largest biotech cluster in the United States, below [Greater Boston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Boston) and above the [San Francisco Bay Area](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Francisco_Bay_Area).[^cite_note-186] There are more than 400 biotechnology companies in the area.[^cite_note-187] In particular, the [La Jolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla,_San_Diego,_California) and nearby [Sorrento Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorrento_Valley,_San_Diego,_California) areas are home to offices and research facilities for numerous biotechnology companies.[^cite_note-Walcott-188] Major biotechnology companies like [Illumina](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Illumina_\(company\)) and [Neurocrine Biosciences](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Neurocrine_Biosciences) are headquartered in San Diego, while many other biotech and pharmaceutical companies have offices or research facilities in San Diego. San Diego is also home to more than 140 [contract research organizations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Contract_research_organization) (CROs) that provide contract services for pharmaceutical and biotechnology companies.[^cite_note-189]

### Real estate



[La Jolla](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla) is a highly valued [real estate](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Real_estate) market in San Diego.

San Diego has high real estate prices. San Diego home prices peaked in 2005, and then declined along with the national trend. As of December 2010, prices were down 36 percent from the peak,[^cite_note-190] [median price of homes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Median_home_price) having declined by more than \$200,000 between 2005 and 2010.[^cite_note-191] As of May 2015, the median price of a house was \$520,000.[^cite_note-192] In November 2018 the median home price was \$558,000. The San Diego metropolitan area had one of the worst housing affordability rankings of all metropolitan areas in the United States in 2009.[^cite_note-193] The San Diego Housing Market experienced a decline in the median sold price of existing single-family homes between December 2022 and January 2023, with a 2.9% decrease from \$850,000 to \$824,950.[^cite_note-194] As of 2023, the majority of homes (nearly 60%) in San Diego are listed above \$1 million, with the city's median home price at \$910,000, ranking it fourth highest among the 30 largest U.S. cities.[^cite_note-195][^cite_note-196]

Consequently, San Diego has experienced negative net migration since 2004. A significant number of people have moved to adjacent [Riverside County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Riverside_County,_California), commuting daily to jobs in San Diego, while others are leaving the area altogether and moving to more affordable regions.[^cite_note-SDGreenerPastures-197]

## Government

### Local government

See also: [Mayor of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_San_Diego), [San Diego City Council](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_City_Council), and [Government of San Diego County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_San_Diego_County,_California)



[Todd Gloria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Gloria) is the current [mayor of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor_of_San_Diego).

The city is governed by a mayor and a nine-member city council. In 2006, its government changed from a [council–manager government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Council%E2%80%93manager_government) to a [strong mayor government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mayor%E2%80%93council_government), as decided by a citywide vote in 2004. The mayor is in effect the chief executive officer of the city, while the council is the legislative body.[^cite_note-198] The City of San Diego is responsible for [police](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Police_Department), public safety, streets, water and sewer service, planning and zoning, and similar services within its borders. San Diego is a [sanctuary city](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sanctuary_city),[^cite_note-199] however, San Diego County is a participant of the [Secure Communities](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secure_Communities_and_administrative_immigration_policies) program.[^cite_note-200][^cite_note-201] As of 2011, the city had one employee for every 137 residents, with a payroll greater than \$733 million.[^cite_note-202]

The members of the city council are each elected from single-member districts within the city. The mayor and city attorney are elected directly by the voters of the entire city. The mayor, city attorney, and council members are elected to four-year terms, with a two-term limit.[^cite_note-203] Elections are held on a non-partisan basis per California state law; nevertheless, most officeholders do identify themselves as either Democrats or Republicans. In 2007, registered Democrats outnumbered Republicans by about 7 to 6 in the city,[^cite_note-RegDEMREP-204] and Democrats currently (as of 2025) hold all nine seats on the city council. The current mayor, [Todd Gloria](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Todd_Gloria), is a member of the [Democratic Party](5043544.md).



[County Administration Center](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County_Administration_Center), seat of [San Diego County Government](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_San_Diego_County,_California)

San Diego is part of [San Diego County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County), and includes all or part of the 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 4th supervisorial districts of the [San Diego County Board of Supervisors](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Government_of_San_Diego_County,_California#Board_of_Supervisors),[^cite_note-205] Other county officers elected in part by city residents include the [Sheriff](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County_Sheriff), [District Attorney](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County_District_Attorney), Assessor/Recorder/County Clerk, and Treasurer/Tax Collector.

Areas of the city immediately adjacent to [San Diego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Bay) ("[tidelands](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tidelands)") are administered by the [Port of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Port_of_San_Diego), a quasi-governmental agency which owns all the property in the tidelands and is responsible for its land use planning, policing, and similar functions. San Diego is a member of the regional planning agency [San Diego Association of Governments](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Association_of_Governments) (SANDAG). Public schools within the city are managed and funded by independent school districts (see [below](#Education)).

After narrowly supporting [Lyndon B. Johnson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lyndon_B._Johnson) in 1964, San Diego provided majorities to all six Republican presidential candidates from 1968 to 1988. However, in more recent decades, San Diego has trended in favor of [Democratic](5043544.md) presidential candidates for president. [George H. W. Bush](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/George_H._W._Bush) in 1988 is the last Republican candidate to carry San Diego in a presidential election.

### Scandals



[Weinberger U.S. Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacob_Weinberger_United_States_Courthouse)

San Diego was the site of the 1912 [San Diego free speech fight](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_free_speech_fight), in which the city restricted speech, vigilantes brutalized and tortured anarchists, and the [San Diego Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Police_Department) killed a member of the [Industrial Workers of the World](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Industrial_Workers_of_the_World) (IWW).

In 1916, [rainmaker](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rainmaking) [Charles Hatfield](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_Hatfield) was blamed for \$4 million in damages and accused of causing San Diego's worst [flood](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floods_in_the_United_States:_1901%E2%80%932000#Southern_California_floods_-_January_1916), during which about 20 [Japanese American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Japanese_American) farmers died.[^cite_note-209]

Then-mayor [Roger Hedgecock](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roger_Hedgecock) was forced to resign his post in 1985, after he was found guilty of one count of [conspiracy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_\(criminal\)) and 12 counts of [perjury](23688.md), related to the alleged failure to report all [campaign contributions](339302.md).[^cite_note-Horstman-210][^cite_note-Abrahamson-211] After a series of appeals, the 12 perjury counts were dismissed in 1990 based on claims of [juror misconduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Juror_misconduct); the remaining conspiracy count was reduced to a [misdemeanor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Misdemeanor) and then dismissed.[^cite_note-212]

A 2002 scheme to underfund pensions for city employees led to the [San Diego pension scandal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_pension_scandal). This resulted in the resignation of newly re-elected Mayor [Dick Murphy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dick_Murphy)[^cite_note-213] and the criminal indictment of six pension board members.[^cite_note-214] Those charges were finally dismissed by a federal judge in 2010.[^cite_note-215]



[Carter-Keep U.S. Courthouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/James_M._Carter_and_Judith_N._Keep_United_States_Courthouse)

On November 28, 2005, U.S. Congressman [Randy "Duke" Cunningham](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Duke_Cunningham) resigned after being convicted on federal [bribery](260979.md) charges. He had represented [California's 50th congressional district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives,_California_District_50), which includes much of the northern portion of the city of San Diego. In 2006, Cunningham was sentenced to a 100-month prison sentence.[^cite_note-216]

In 2005 two city council members, [Ralph Inzunza](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ralph_Inzunza) and Deputy Mayor [Michael Zucchet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Michael_Zucchet), were convicted of [extortion](162449.md), [wire fraud](905072.md), and [conspiracy to commit wire fraud](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conspiracy_\(criminal\)) for taking campaign contributions from a [strip club](1380550.md) owner and his associates, allegedly in exchange for trying to repeal the city's "no touch" laws at strip clubs.[^cite_note-217] Both subsequently resigned. Inzunza was sentenced to 21 months in prison.[^cite_note-218] In 2009, a judge acquitted Zucchet on seven out of the nine counts against him, and granted his petition for a new trial on the other two charges;[^cite_note-219] the remaining charges were eventually dropped.[^cite_note-220]

In July 2013, three former supporters of Mayor [Bob Filner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bob_Filner) asked him to resign because of allegations of repeated [sexual harassment](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sexual_harassment).[^cite_note-221] Over the ensuing six weeks, 18 women came forward to publicly claim that Filner had sexually harassed them,[^cite_note-222] and multiple individuals and groups called for him to resign. Filner agreed to resign effective August 30, 2013, subsequently pleading guilty to one felony count of [false imprisonment](627536.md) and two misdemeanor [battery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battery_\(crime\)) charges.[^cite_note-223][^cite_note-autogenerated1-224]

### Crime

Main article: [Crime in San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Crime_in_San_Diego)



[San Diego Police Department](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Police_Department)

Like most major cities, San Diego had a declining crime rate from 1990 to 2000. 1991 would mark the city's deadliest year, registering 179 homicides[^cite_note-225] within city limits (while the [region](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County) as a whole peaked at 278 homicides),[^cite_note-226] capping off an unabated, eight-year climb in murders, rapes, robberies, and assault dating back to 1983. At the time, the city was ranked last among the 10 most populous U.S. cities in homicides per 1,000 population, and ninth in crimes per 1,000.[^cite_note-227] From 1980 to 1994, San Diego surpassed 100 murders ten times before tapering off to 91 homicides in 1995. That number would not exceed 79 for the next 15 years.[^cite_note-228] Crime in San Diego increased in the early 2000s.[^cite_note-sdpdcrimeactuals-229][^cite_note-sdpdcrime1000-230][^cite_note-sdtribunecrime-231] In 2004, San Diego had the sixth lowest crime rate of any U.S. city with over half a million residents.[^cite_note-sdtribunecrime-231] From 2002 to 2006, the crime rate overall dropped 0.8%, though not evenly by category. While [violent crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Violent_crime) decreased 12.4% during this period, [property crime](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Property_crime) increased 1.1%. Total property crimes per 100,000 people were lower than the national average in 2008.[^cite_note-232]

According to [Uniform Crime Report](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uniform_Crime_Report) statistics compiled by the [Federal Bureau of Investigation](11127.md) (FBI) in 2010, there were 5,616 violent crimes and 30,753 property crimes. Of these, the violent crimes consisted of forcible rapes, 73 robberies and 170 aggravated assaults, while 6,387 burglaries, 17,977 larceny-thefts, 6,389 motor vehicle thefts and 155 acts of arson defined the property offenses.[^cite_note-233] In 2013, San Diego had the lowest murder rate of the ten largest cities in the United States.[^cite_note-234]

## Education

### Primary and secondary schools

Main article: [Primary and secondary schools in San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Primary_and_secondary_schools_in_San_Diego)



[The Bishop's School](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Bishop's_School_\(La_Jolla\)) in La Jolla

[Public schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/State_schools) in San Diego are operated by independent [school districts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/School_district). The majority of the public schools in the city are served by [San Diego Unified School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Unified_School_District), the second-largest school district in California, which includes 11 K–8 schools, 107 elementary schools, 24 middle schools, 13 atypical and alternative schools, 28 high schools, and 45 [charter schools](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charter_school).[^cite_note-235]

Several adjacent school districts which are headquartered outside the city limits serve some schools within the city; these include [Poway Unified School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Poway_Unified_School_District), [Del Mar Union School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Del_Mar_Union_School_District), [San Dieguito Union High School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Dieguito_Union_High_School_District), and [Sweetwater Union High School District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sweetwater_Union_High_School_District). In addition, there are a number of private schools in the city.

### Colleges and universities



[San Diego State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_State_University)

According to education rankings released by the [U.S. Census Bureau](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/United_States_Census_Bureau) in 2017, 44.4% of San Diegans (city, not county) ages 25 and older hold [bachelor's degrees](188874.md), compared to 30.9% in the United States as a whole.[^cite_note-236]

The largest university in the area is the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego) (UC San Diego). The university is the southernmost campus of the [University of California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California) system and is the second largest employer in the city. It has the seventh largest research expenditure in the country.[^cite_note-237]

Other public colleges and universities in the city include [San Diego State University](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_State_University) (SDSU) and the [San Diego Community College District](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Community_College_District), which includes [San Diego City College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_City_College), [San Diego Mesa College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Mesa_College), and [San Diego Miramar College](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Miramar_College).



[University of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_San_Diego)

There is one medical school in the city, the [UC San Diego School of Medicine](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/UC_San_Diego_School_of_Medicine). There are two [ABA](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Bar_Association) accredited law schools in the city, which include [California Western School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_Western_School_of_Law) and [University of San Diego School of Law](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_San_Diego_School_of_Law). ## Culture

Main article: [Culture of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Culture_of_San_Diego)

See also: [City of San Diego Commission for Arts and Culture](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/City_of_San_Diego_Commission_for_Arts_and_Culture)



The [Museum of Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Us)

The culture of San Diego is influenced heavily by the mixing of [American](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_culture) and [Mexican cultures](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexican_culture), due to the city's position on the [Mexico–United States border](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mexico%E2%80%93United_States_border), its large [Chicano](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chicano) population, and its history as part of [Hispanic America](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hispanic_America) and Mexico. San Diego's longtime association with the [U.S. military](32212.md) also contributes to its culture. There are many [Greek](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greek_cuisine) and Mediterranean restaurants in San Diego.&#x20;

Many popular museums, such as [the San Diego Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_San_Diego_Museum_of_Art), the [San Diego Natural History Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Natural_History_Museum), the [Museum of Us](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Us), the [Museum of Photographic Arts](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Photographic_Arts), and the [San Diego Air & Space Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Air_&_Space_Museum), are located in Balboa Park, which is also the location of the [San Diego Zoo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Zoo). The [Museum of Contemporary Art San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Museum_of_Contemporary_Art_San_Diego) (MCASD) is located in La Jolla and has a branch located at [Santa Fe Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Depot_\(San_Diego\)) downtown.



[The San Diego Museum of Art](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_San_Diego_Museum_of_Art)

The [Columbia district](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Columbia,_San_Diego,_California) downtown is home to historic ship exhibits belonging to the [Maritime Museum of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maritime_Museum_of_San_Diego), headlined by [*Star of India*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Star_of_India_\(ship\)), as well as the unrelated [USS *Midway* Museum](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Midway_Museum) featuring the [USS *Midway*](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/USS_Midway_\(CV-41\)) aircraft carrier.

The [San Diego Symphony](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Symphony) at [Symphony Towers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Symphony_Towers) performs on a regular basis; from 2004 to 2017, its director was [Jahja Ling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jahja_Ling). The [San Diego Opera](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Opera) at Civic Center Plaza, directed by David Bennett. [Old Globe Theatre](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Globe_Theatre) at Balboa Park produces about 15 plays and musicals annually. [La Jolla Playhouse](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/La_Jolla_Playhouse) at UC San Diego is directed by [Christopher Ashley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Christopher_Ashley). Both the Old Globe Theatre and La Jolla Playhouse have produced the world premieres of plays and musicals that have gone on to win [Tony Awards](54741.md)[^cite_note-243] or nominations[^cite_note-244] on [Broadway](725252.md). The [Joan B. Kroc](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joan_Kroc) Theatre at Kroc Center's Performing Arts Center is a 600-seat state-of-the-art theater that hosts music, dance, and theater performances. Hundreds of movies and a dozen TV shows have been [filmed in San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_media_set_in_San_Diego#Films), a tradition going back as far as 1898.[^cite_note-245]

### LGBTQIA+ community

According to a 2021 report by the UCLA School of Law, San Diego has the 16th largest LGBT population amongst U.S. Metropolitan areas.[^cite_note-246] San Diego's [Hillcrest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hillcrest,_San_Diego) neighborhood has long served as the city's [gay village](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gay_village) and is home to The San Diego LGBT Community Center, as well as many gay bars, clubs, and queer-owned businesses. The restaurant and nightclub Gossip Grill, located in Hillcrest, is San Diego's only queer and women-focused venue and is notable as one of the country's last remaining "[lesbian bars](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lesbian_bar)."[^cite_note-247][^cite_note-248] The nearby neighborhood of Northpark is the location of Lips, a drag club and home base of RuPaul's Drag Race All Stars (Season 1) winner [Chad Michaels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chad_Michaels).[^cite_note-249]

The Hillcrest neighborhood is also home to the Lambda Archives of San Diego, a non-profit, publicly accessible archive that collects, preserves, and shares the history of lesbian, gay, bisexual, transgender and all queer people in the region.[^cite_note-250]

The city hosts a [Pride Parade and Pride Festival](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Pride) each year. Unlike most other cities, San Diego celebrates Pride in July, a tradition dating back to the early 1990s, when the city's "June gloom" weather led to the 1990 parade getting rained out.[^cite_note-251]

## Sports

Main article: [Sports in San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sports_in_San_Diego)



[Petco Park](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Petco_Park), home of the [San Diego Padres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Padres) (MLB)

Sports in San Diego includes [major professional league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Major_professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States_and_Canada) teams, other [highest-level professional league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Professional_sports_leagues_in_the_United_States#Other_highest-level_professional_leagues) teams, [minor league](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minor_league) teams, and [college athletics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/College_athletics). San Diego hosts three teams of major professional leagues, the [San Diego Padres](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Padres) of [Major League Baseball](38776.md) (MLB), [San Diego FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_FC) of [Major League Soccer](71802.md) (MLS), and [San Diego Wave FC](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Wave_FC) of [National Women's Soccer League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Women's_Soccer_League) (NWSL).[^cite_note-MLS_Announcement2-252] The city is home to several universities whose teams compete in various [NCAA Division I](669931.md) sports, most notably the [San Diego State Aztecs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_State_Aztecs). [The Sentry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Sentry_\(California\)), a professional golf tournament on the [PGA Tour](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/PGA_Tour), is played annually at [Torrey Pines Golf Course](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Torrey_Pines_Golf_Course).

San Diego hosted the [National Football League](21211.md)'s (NFL) [San Diego Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Chargers) from 1961 to 2017, when the team relocated to the [Greater Los Angeles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Greater_Los_Angeles) area (now the [Los Angeles Chargers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Los_Angeles_Chargers)). The city also hosted the [National Basketball Association](22093.md)'s (NBA) [San Diego Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Rockets) from 1967 to 1971 (now the [Houston Rockets](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Houston_Rockets)) and [San Diego Clippers](72892.md#1978%E2%80%931984:_San_Diego_Clippers) from 1978 to 1984 (now the [Los Angeles Clippers](72892.md)). San Diego has never hosted a [National Hockey League](21809.md) (NHL) franchise, though it hosted the [San Diego Mariners](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Mariners) of the now-defunct [World Hockey Association](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_Hockey_Association) (WHA) from 1974 to 1977.

Currently, there is no NBA, NFL, or NHL team in the city. San Diego is the largest American city not to have won a championship in a major professional league. The city does have one major league title to its name: the [1963 American Football League (AFL) Championship](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/1963_American_Football_League_Championship_Game) won by the San Diego Chargers, when the AFL was an independent entity prior to the [AFL–NFL merger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/AFL%E2%80%93NFL_merger) in 1970. Some San Diego sports fans believe there is [a curse](11347008.md#San_Diego,_California) on professional sports in the city.

The [San Diego Clippers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Clippers_\(NBA_G_League\)) of the [NBA G League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NBA_G_League) and the [San Diego Seals](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Seals) of the [National Lacrosse League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Lacrosse_League) play at [Frontwave Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frontwave_Arena) in the suburb of [Oceanside](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oceanside,_California).[^cite_note-253] [Pechanga Arena](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pechanga_Arena) hosts the [San Diego Gulls](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Gulls) of the [American Hockey League](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Hockey_League).&#x20;

In March 2026, it was announced that San Diego's [Snapdragon Stadium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Snapdragon_Stadium) will host 11 matches during the [LA28](12695048.md) Olympic Football (Soccer) Tournament in July 2028.[^cite_note-254]

## Infrastructure

### Transportation

Main articles: [Transportation in San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Transportation_in_San_Diego) and [Streets and highways of San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Streets_and_highways_of_San_Diego)



[Santa Fe Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Depot_\(San_Diego\)) is served by [Amtrak California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amtrak_California) and [Coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaster_\(rail_service\)) trains.

With the automobile being the primary means of transportation for over 80 percent of residents, San Diego is served by a network of freeways and highways. This includes [I-5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5_in_California), which runs south to [Tijuana](101739.md) and north to Los Angeles; [Interstate 8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_8), which runs east to [Imperial County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Imperial_County,_California) and the [Arizona Sun Corridor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arizona_Sun_Corridor); Interstate 15, which runs northeast through the [Inland Empire](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Inland_Empire) to [Las Vegas](47737.md) and [Salt Lake City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Salt_Lake_City); and [I-805](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_805), which splits from I-5 near the Mexican border and rejoins I-5 at [Sorrento Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sorrento_Valley,_San_Diego).

Major state highways include [SR 94](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_94), which connects downtown with I-805, I-15 and [East County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_County,_San_Diego); [SR 163](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_163), which connects downtown with the northeast part of the city, intersects I-805 and merges with I-15 at [Miramar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Miramar,_San_Diego); [SR 52](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_52), which connects La Jolla with [East County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_County,_San_Diego) through [Santee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santee,_California) and [SR 125](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_125); [SR 56](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_56), which connects I-5 with I-15 through [Carmel Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmel_Valley,_San_Diego) and [Rancho Peñasquitos](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rancho_Pe%C3%B1asquitos,_San_Diego); [SR 75](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_75), which spans [San Diego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Bay) as the [San Diego–Coronado Bridge](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego%E2%80%93Coronado_Bridge), and also passes through [South San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/South_San_Diego) as Palm Avenue; and [SR 905](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/California_State_Route_905), which connects I-5 and I-805 to the [Otay Mesa Port of Entry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otay_Mesa_Port_of_Entry).



[San Diego Trolley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Trolley) is operated by the [S.D. Metropolitan Transit System](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Metropolitan_Transit_System).

The stretch of SR 163 that passes through Balboa Park is San Diego's oldest freeway, dating back to 1948 when it was part of [US 80](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_80_in_California) and [US 395](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/U.S._Route_395_in_California). It has been called one of America's most beautiful parkways.[^cite_note-260]

San Diego's roadway system provides an extensive network of cycle routes. Its dry and mild climate makes cycling a convenient year-round option; however, the city's hilly terrain and long average trip distances make cycling less practicable. Older and denser neighborhoods around the downtown tend to be oriented to [utility cycling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Utility_cycling). This is partly because the grid street patterns are now absent in newer developments farther from the urban core, where suburban-style arterial roads are much more common. As a result, the majority of cycling is recreational.



The [Cross Border Xpress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Border_Xpress), also known as the *Puerta de [las Californias](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Las_Californias)*, connects San Diego to [Tijuana International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuana_International_Airport) in [Baja California](199793.md).

San Diego is served by the [San Diego Trolley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Trolley) light rail system,[^cite_note-SD-Trolley-261] by the [MTS bus system](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_MTS_bus_system),[^cite_note-262] the bus rapid transit system [Rapid](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rapid_\(San_Diego\)), private [jitneys](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Share_taxi#United_States) in some neighborhoods,[^cite_note-263] and by [Coaster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Coaster_\(rail_service\))[^cite_note-Coaster-264] and *[Pacific Surfliner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pacific_Surfliner)*[^cite_note-Surfliner-265] commuter rail; northern [San Diego County](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_County,_California) is also served by the [Sprinter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sprinter_\(rail_service\)) hybrid rail service.[^cite_note-266] The trolley primarily serves downtown and surrounding urban communities, [Mission Valley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mission_Valley,_San_Diego,_California), east county, and coastal south bay. A mid-coast extension of the trolley operates from [Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town,_San_Diego,_California) to [University City](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_City,_San_Diego,_California) and the [University of California, San Diego](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/University_of_California,_San_Diego) along [Interstate 5](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Interstate_5) since November 2021. The Amtrak and Coaster trains currently run along the coastline and connect San Diego with Los Angeles, Orange County, Riverside, San Bernardino, and Ventura via [Metrolink](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metrolink_\(California\)) and the Pacific Surfliner. There are two Amtrak stations in San Diego, in [Old Town](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Old_Town_Transit_Center) and [Santa Fe Depot](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santa_Fe_Depot_\(San_Diego\)) downtown. San Diego transit information about public transportation and commuting is available on the Web and by dialing "[511](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/5-1-1)" from any phone in the area.[^cite_note-267]



[San Diego International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_International_Airport)

The city has two major commercial airports within or near its city limits. [San Diego International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_International_Airport) is the busiest single-runway airport in the United States.[^cite_note-FAAresign-268][^cite_note-269] It served over 24 million passengers in 2018 and is dealing with larger numbers every year.[^cite_note-270] [Tijuana International Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tijuana_International_Airport) has a terminal within the city limits in the [Otay Mesa](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Otay_Mesa) district connected to the rest of the airport in [Tijuana](101739.md), via the [Cross Border Xpress](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cross_Border_Xpress) cross-border footbridge. In addition, the city has two general-aviation airports, [Montgomery-Gibbs Executive Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Montgomery-Gibbs_Executive_Airport) and [Brown Field Municipal Airport](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_Field_Municipal_Airport).[^cite_note-271]



[San Diego Bay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Bay) Festival of Sail

Recent regional transportation projects have sought to mitigate congestion, including improvements to local freeways, expansion of San Diego Airport, and doubling the capacity of the cruise ship terminal. Freeway projects included expansion of Interstates 5 and 805 around "The Merge" where these two freeways meet, as well as expansion of Interstate 15 through North County, which includes new [HOV "managed lanes"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/High-occupancy_vehicle_lane). A tollway (the southern portion of SR 125, known as the South Bay Expressway) connects SR 54 and Otay Mesa, near the Mexican border. According to an assessment in 2007, 37 percent of city streets were in acceptable condition. However, the proposed budget fell \$84.6 million short of bringing streets up to an acceptable level.[^cite_note-37perStreets-272] Expansion at the port has included a second cruise terminal on [Broadway Pier](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Broadway_Pier,_San_Diego), opened in 2010. Airport projects include the expansion of Terminal Two.[^cite_note-273]

### Utilities

Water is supplied to residents by the Water Department of the City of San Diego. The city receives most of its water from the [Metropolitan Water District of Southern California](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Metropolitan_Water_District_of_Southern_California), which brings water to the region from the Sacramento and San Joaquin rivers, via the state project and the Colorado River, via the Colorado Aqueduct.[^cite_note-274] 80–90% of the water supply is imported.[^cite_note-275]

Gas and electric utilities are provided by [San Diego Gas & Electric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/San_Diego_Gas_&_Electric), a division of [Sempra Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sempra_Energy). The company provides energy service to 3.7 million people through 1.5 million electric meters and 900,000 natural gas meters in San Diego and southern Orange counties.[^cite_note-276]

#### Street lights



Street lights in the [Gaslamp Quarter](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gaslamp_Quarter)

In the mid-20th century the city had [mercury vapor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mercury_vapor) street lamps. In 1978, the city decided to replace them with more efficient [sodium vapor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sodium_vapor) lamps. This triggered an outcry from [astronomers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Astronomer) at [Palomar Observatory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Palomar_Observatory) 60 miles (100 km) north of the city, concerned that the new lamps would increase [light pollution](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_pollution) and hinder astronomical observation.[^cite_note-277] The city altered its lighting regulations to limit light pollution within 30 miles (50 km) of Palomar.[^cite_note-278]

In 2011, the city announced plans to upgrade 80% of its street lighting to new energy-efficient lights that use [induction technology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Electrodeless_lamp), a modified form of [fluorescent lamp](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fluorescent_lamp) producing a broader spectrum than sodium vapor lamps. The new system is predicted to save \$2.2 million per year in energy and maintenance.[^cite_note-install-279] In 2014, San Diego announced plans to become the first U.S. city to install cyber-controlled street lighting.[^cite_note-280]
