**Norddeutscher Rundfunk** (pronounced [\[ˈnɔʁtˌdɔʏtʃɐ ˈʁʊntfʊŋk\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German); "North German Broadcasting"), commonly shortened to **NDR** (pronounced [\[ˌɛndeːˈʔɛʁ\]](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Help\:IPA/Standard_German) [//upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/7d/De-NDR.ogg/De-NDR.ogg.mp3](https://upload.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/transcoded/7/7d/De-NDR.ogg/De-NDR.ogg.mp3)<sup>ⓘ</sup>), is a [public radio and television broadcaster](178808.md), based in [Hamburg](13467.md). In addition to the city-state of Hamburg, NDR broadcasts for the German states of [Lower Saxony](18435.md), [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](38168.md) and [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md). NDR is a member of [ARD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARD_\(broadcaster\)), the joint organisation of German public broadcasters.

## History

See also: [Nordwestdeutscher Rundfunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordwestdeutscher_Rundfunk)

### Pre-war

In 1924 broadcasting began in Hamburg, when *Norddeutsche Rundfunk AG* (NORAG) was created. In 1934 it was incorporated into the *Großdeutscher Rundfunk*, the national broadcaster controlled by [Joseph Goebbels](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Joseph_Goebbels)'s [Propagandaministerium](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Propagandaministerium), as *Reichssender Hamburg*.

In 1930, NORAG commissioned the Welte-Funkorgel – a large [theatre organ](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theatre_organ) custom-built by the firm of [M. Welte & Sons](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Welte-Mignon) to meet the specific acoustic requirements of radio broadcasting – and installed it in their radio studio (today the world's oldest such facility still in use) on Rothenbaumchaussee 132, Hamburg, where it continues to be played, now maintained by volunteers.[^cite_note-1]

### Post-war

In the [British Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_Occupation_Zones_in_Germany#British_Zone) of occupied Germany, the military authorities quickly established *Radio Hamburg* to provide information to the population of the area.

The [British Control Commission](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/British_Control_Commission) appointed [Hugh Greene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hugh_Greene) to manage the creation of public service broadcasting in their Zone. On 22 September 1945, *Radio Hamburg* became *Nordwestdeutscher Rundfunk* (North-Western German Broadcasting), the single broadcasting organisation of the British Zone.

The state of [Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bremen_\(state\)), while surrounded by the British Zone, was given to the United States as part of the [American Zone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/American_Zone). A separate broadcaster was established for this state, [Radio Bremen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Bremen). However, Radio Bremen and NDR cooperate in certain programmes and stations.

### Länder control

In 1948, the Control Commission transferred the [Nordwestdeutscher Rundfunk](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nordwestdeutscher_Rundfunk) (NWDR) to the control of the constituent Länder ([Hamburg](13467.md), [Lower Saxony](18435.md), [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md) and [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md)). At first, NWDR had just one radio station, later known as NWDR1. In 1950, it introduced a regional station for the north, *NWDR Nord* (later to become NDR2), and a regional station for the west, *NWDR West* (later WDR2).

That same year, NWDR became a founding member of [ARD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARD_\(broadcaster\)), a joint organisation of all German regional broadcasters. The NWDR also played a founding role in launching [625-line](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Standard-definition_television) television in Germany, starting broadcasts on 25 December 1952.

### NWDR split

In February 1955, [North Rhine-Westphalia](38414.md) decided to establish its broadcaster, whilst [Hamburg](13467.md), [Lower Saxony](18435.md) and [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md) continued with the existing joint system. To this end, the NWDR was split into two broadcasters, Norddeutscher Rundfunk (NDR) in the north and [Westdeutscher Rundfunk](808025.md) (WDR) in North Rhine-Westphalia.

NDR continued to operate out of Hamburg. The split was effective from 1 January 1956, although the radio station NWDR1 remained a joint operation with regional opt-outs.

The NWDR television service also remained a joint operation, from 1 April 1956 under the name *Nord- und Westdeutsche Rundfunkverband* (North and West German Broadcasting Federation – NWRV). NDR and WDR launched separate television services for their respective areas in 1961.

### NDR history



NDR's first logo, used from 1956 to 1980



NDR's previous corporate logo, was used from 1980 to 2001. Its old corporate logo featured [Antje the Walrus](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Antje_\(walrus\)), the mascot of the broadcaster for the north.

[^cite_note-Antje-2]

On 1 December 1956 NDR started its third radio channel, NDR3 (from 1962 to 1973, it was operated jointly with [Sender Freies Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sender_Freies_Berlin)).

In 1958 [Han Koller](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Han_Koller?action=edit\&redlink=1) became the musical director of Hamburg's [NDR Jazz Workshop](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_Jazz_Workshop?action=edit\&redlink=1), which became a popular radio broadcast. Numerous names in [Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz) performed on these broadcasts including; [Dave Brubeck](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dave_Brubeck), [Kenny Clarke](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kenny_Clarke), [Lucky Thompson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lucky_Thompson), [Wes Montgomery](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wes_Montgomery), [Johnny Griffin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnny_Griffin), [Oscar Peterson](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oscar_Peterson), [Ben Webster](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ben_Webster), [Sahib Shihab](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sahib_Shihab), [Carmell Jones](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carmell_Jones), [Lee Konitz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lee_Konitz), [Cecil Payne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cecil_Payne), [Slide Hampton](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Slide_Hampton), [Phil Woods](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phil_Woods), [Jazz Composers Orchestra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz_Composers_Orchestra), [Howard Riley](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Howard_Riley_\(musician\)), [Barry Guy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barry_Guy), [John Surman](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Surman), the [Kuhn Brothers](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kuhn_Brothers?action=edit\&redlink=1) and [Barney Wilen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barney_Wilen). Some of these have been released since 1987, while the older ones only exist as rare [bootlegs](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bootleg_recording), sought after by many [Jazz](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jazz) aficionados.

On 4 January 1965 NDR, Radio Bremen and Sender Freies Berlin (SFB) began a joint "third channel" television service, *Norddeutsches Fernsehen*, later *Nord 3* and *N3*. Since December 2001, this service is called *NDR Fernsehen*. [SFB](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sender_Freies_Berlin) started a separate TV channel for Berlin in 1992, called B1, later SFB1, now [RBB Fernsehen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/RBB_Fernsehen).

In 1977, [Gerhard Stoltenberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gerhard_Stoltenberg), the [minister-president](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Minister-president) of [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md) unilaterally cancelled the *NDR-Staatsvertrag*, the governing contract of NDR. This caused a discussion on how to organise broadcasting in the North German region.

In 1980, NDR signed a new contract with the three Länder, changing the pattern of broadcasting and creating new regional services. NDR1 was divided into three independent radio stations from 2 January 1981:

- *NDR 1 Radio Niedersachsen* (from 2002, *NDR 1 Niedersachsen*) for [Lower Saxony](18435.md)

- *NDR 1 Welle Nord* for [Schleswig-Holstein](27126.md)

- *NDR Hamburg-Welle 90.3* (from 2 December 2001, *NDR 90.3*) for [Hamburg](13467.md)

NDR2 and NDR3 (now NDR Kultur) continued as regional stations.

These regional services were further subdivided with opt-outs for specific areas. *NDR 1 Niedersachsen* established regions based around Oldenburg-Ostfriesland-Bremen-Cuxhaven, Osnabrück-Emsland, greater Hanover, Braunschweig-southern Lower Saxony and northern Lower Saxony. *NDR 1 Welle Nord* was subdivided with studio centres in Flensburg, Heide, Norderstedt, Lübeck and Kiel.

Roughly around 1983–1984, "[Subways of Your Mind](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Subways_of_Your_Mind)" was recorded from NDR2, with the title of the song remaining completely unknown until forty years after it was played on NDR2.

On 30 September 1988 NDR introduced a [Teletext](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Teletext) service on its *N3* television channel. Originally called *Nordtext*, it became *NDR Text* on 2 December 2001. The Teletext service also offers information for viewers in the Radio Bremen area under the title *Radio Bremen Text*.

On 1 April 1989, NDR introduced its fourth radio service, NDR4. This service was later renamed *NDR4 Info* and since 2 June 2002 has been known as *NDR Info*. The station is a news and information service for the whole NDR region.

On 1 January 1992, [Mecklenburg-Vorpommern](38168.md) in former [East Germany](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/East_Germany) joined NDR as the fourth state in the organisation, where it replaced [Fernsehen der DDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fernsehen_der_DDR) and [Rundfunk der DDR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rundfunk_der_DDR). The area receives the main NDR radio and television stations, plus the regional *NDR 1 Radio mV*, which has subregions based in Schwerin, Rostock, Neubrandenburg and Greifswald. In October of the same year, SFB in Berlin stopped relaying the *Nord 3* television service in favour of its own *Berlin 1* TV channel.

On 4 April 1994, NDR introduced *N-Joy Radio* (known simply as *N-Joy* since 2001), a radio station aimed at 14 to 29-year-old listeners.

On 3 October 1997, NDR3 was relaunched as *Radio 3*, produced in co-operation with Ostdeutscher Rundfunk Brandenburg. At the end of 2000, SFB joined Radio 3. This arrangement lasted until ORB and SFB merged on 1 January 2003 and started its own classical and cultural network. NDR3 became *NDR Kultur* on 1 January 2003.

On 1 November 2001, NDR and Radio Bremen launched a joint radio station, *Nordwestradio*, to serve Bremen and northwestern Lower Saxony. This service replaced *[Radio Bremen 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Radio_Bremen_2)* and control of the service remains with Radio Bremen.

NDR is the organization within ARD which oversaw [Germany's participation in the Eurovision Song Contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Germany_in_the_Eurovision_Song_Contest) from 1996 to 2025. [SWR](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/S%C3%BCdwestrundfunk) took over that function from 2026 onwards. Following Germany's victory in the [55th annual contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2010), NDR organised the [56th annual contest](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eurovision_Song_Contest_2011) on behalf of ARD. The contest was held in [Düsseldorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/D%C3%BCsseldorf) on 10–14 May 2011 despite the city being located outside NDR's broadcasting area.

## Studios



NDR television buildings in Hamburg (2023)

NDR's studios in Hamburg are in two locations, both within the borough of [Eimsbüttel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eimsb%C3%BCttel): the television studios are in the quarter of [Lokstedt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lokstedt) while the radio studios are in the quarter of [Harvestehude](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Harvestehude) (though they are called "Funkhaus am Rothenbaum"), a little closer to the city centre. There are also regional studios, having both radio and television production facilities, in the state capitals [Hanover](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hanover), [Kiel](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kiel) and [Schwerin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schwerin). The facility in Hanover is now called the [Landesfunkhaus Niedersachsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Landesfunkhaus_Niedersachsen). In addition, NDR maintains facilities at [ARD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARD_\(broadcaster\))'s national studios in [Berlin](3354.md).

### Funding

NDR is in part funded by the limited sale of on-air commercial advertising time; however, its principal source of income is the revenue derived from viewer and listener [licence fees](48551.md). As of August 2021, the monthly fee due from each household for radio and television reception was €18.36.[^cite_note-3] These fees are collected not directly by NDR but by a [joint agency](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beitragsservice_von_ARD,_ZDF_und_Deutschlandradio) of [ARD](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ARD_\(broadcaster\)) (and its member institutions), [ZDF](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/ZDF), and [Deutschlandradio](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Deutschlandradio).

### Radio

- *[NDR 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_1)* network consists of four independent radio stations:&#x20;

  - *[NDR 90,3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_90,3)* – Local station for Hamburg, music for older listeners.

  - *[NDR 1 Niedersachsen](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_1_Niedersachsen)* – Local station for Lower Saxony, run from Hanover with some regional opt-outs. Music for older listeners.

  - *[NDR 1 Welle Nord](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_1_Welle_Nord)* – Local station for Schleswig-Holstein, run from Kiel with some regional opt-outs. Music for older listeners.

  - *[NDR 1 Radio MV](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_1_Radio_MV)* – Local station for Mecklenburg-Vorpommern, run from Schwerin with some regional opt-outs. Music for older listeners.

- *[NDR 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_2)* – Popular music station for middle-aged listeners. A commercial public service station.

- *[NDR Kultur](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_Kultur)* – Arts and culture station (formerly NDR 3). Classical music.

- *[NDR Info](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_Info)* – News and information station (formerly NDR 4 or NDR 4 Info).

- *[NDR Info Spezial](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_Info_Spezial)* - Same programming as NDR Info with opt-outs for sports, sessions of parliament, maritime forecasts, multicultural programming and *ARD Infonacht*. (planned to close by 31 December 2026)

- *[N-Joy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/N-Joy_\(German_radio_station\))* – youth station.

- *[NDR Blue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_Blue?action=edit\&redlink=1)* – Music "away from the charts". (planned to close by 31 December 2026)

- *[NDR Schlager](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_Schlager?action=edit\&redlink=1)* – a music station with the programming format *schlager* music and easy listening (planned to close by 31 December 2026)

## Musical organizations

NDR has four musical organizations, including two orchestras, a chorus and a "big band":

- [NDR Elbphilharmonie Orchester](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_Elbphilharmonie_Orchestra) – the *North German Radio Elbphilharmonie Orchestra*; created in 1945 as the Symphony Orchestra of the NWDR and continued by NDR under the name *NDR Sinfonieorchester* between 1955 – 2016. It was renamed in 2016 to its current name. Principal conductors have included [Günter Wand](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/G%C3%BCnter_Wand) and [John Eliot Gardiner](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/John_Eliot_Gardiner). Currently it is [Alan Gilbert](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Alan_Gilbert_\(conductor\)).

- [NDR Radiophilharmonie](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_Radiophilharmonie) – the *NDR Radio Philharmonic*; created in 1950 as the Hanover Radio Orchestra of the NWDR and continued by NDR under the name Radiophilharmonie Hannover; it received its current name in 2003. Principal conductors have included Willy Steiner, [Bernhard Klee](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bernhard_Klee) (twice), and [Eiji Oue](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eiji_Oue). The orchestra also plays light classical or "concert hall" music.

- [NDR Chor](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/NDR_Chor) or *Chor des Norddeutschen Rundfunks*—created in 1946 by the NWDR and continued under that name until 2022 when it became NDR Vokalensemble.

  [^cite_note-4]

  &#x20;It consists of 21 professional singers on fixed contracts. The is Klaas Stok. The choir specializes in Early music, but a broad repertory also includes contemporary music.

- NDR Bigband; created by the NWDR and continued by NDR in 1955 as the *NDR Studioband*. Renamed *NDR Bigband* in 1971.
