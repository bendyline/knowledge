The **Secretary of State for Energy Security and Net Zero** is a [secretary of state](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_\(United_Kingdom\)) in the [Government of the United Kingdom](25318118.md), with responsibility for the [Department for Energy Security and Net Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Energy_Security_and_Net_Zero). The incumbent is a member of the [Cabinet of the United Kingdom](162790.md).[^cite_note-3]

## History

Between 1974 and 1992, the post was known as **Secretary of State for Energy**.

Under the [Conservative](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conservative_Party_\(UK\)) government of [Sir John Major](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sir_John_Major) in 1992 the [Department of Energy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Energy_\(United_Kingdom\)) was merged into the [Department of Trade and Industry](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_of_Trade_and_Industry_\(United_Kingdom\)).

The position of **Secretary of State for Energy and Climate Change** was created on 3 October 2008 when then-[Prime Minister](24150.md) [Gordon Brown](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_Brown) reshuffled his [Cabinet](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Brown_ministry). Immediately prior to the creation of the new department, [energy policy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Energy_policy) was the responsibility of the [Department for Business, Enterprise and Regulatory Reform](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Business,_Enterprise_and_Regulatory_Reform).

Former Labour leader [Ed Miliband](1290018.md) was the inaugural secretary of state at DECC. After Labour lost the 2010 general election and the [Cameron–Clegg coalition](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cameron%E2%80%93Clegg_coalition) was formed, [Chris Huhne](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chris_Huhne) was appointed as his successor. On 3 February 2012, Huhne resigned from the post after it was announced that he would be [prosecuted for perverting the course of justice](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/R_v_Huhne), in relation to accusations that he passed on speeding penalties to his ex-wife to avoid losing his own licence. The post was taken over by [Ed Davey](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ed_Davey) on the same day, and served until the Liberal Democrats left government, and Davey lost his seat, in 2015.[^cite_note-4]

[Amber Rudd](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Amber_Rudd) was the final secretary of state at DECC, until she became [Home Secretary](149104.md). The post was formed into the new [Department for Business, Energy and Industrial Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Business,_Energy_and_Industrial_Strategy) by new prime minister [Theresa May](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Theresa_May) in July 2016.

On 7 February 2023, a [government reshuffle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/February_2023_British_cabinet_reshuffle) meant that the [Department for Business, Energy and Industrial Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Business,_Energy_and_Industrial_Strategy) was split up into separate departments.[^cite_note-5][^cite_note-\:0-6] The [Department for Energy Security and Net Zero](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Department_for_Energy_Security_and_Net_Zero) took on the energy portfolio and policy functions from the Department for Business, Energy and Industrial Strategy.[^cite_note-\:0-6]

[Grant Shapps](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Grant_Shapps) was appointed the first Secretary of State for the department, having previously been the last holder of the office of [Secretary of State for Business, Energy and Industrial Strategy](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Secretary_of_State_for_Business,_Energy_and_Industrial_Strategy) from 2022 to 2023.[^cite_note-7] The department was tasked by the Prime Minister, [Rishi Sunak](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rishi_Sunak), with "securing our long-term energy supply, bringing down bills and halving inflation".[^cite_note-\:0-6]

In May 2023 the position was incorporated as a [corporation sole](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporation_sole).[^cite_note-8]
